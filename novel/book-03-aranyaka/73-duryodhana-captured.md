# Duryodhana Captured

Then King Duryodhana, staying here and there in the forest, went round the
cattle stations and made his camp there. His men made his dwelling in a lovely,
well-known place with water and good trees, a place with every virtue. In the
same way they made many separate dwellings near it for Karna, for Shakuni and
for all his brothers.

Then the king saw the cows in their hundreds and thousands, and he had them all
marked with brands and signs. He had the calves branded, and he learned which
cows had been covered, and he had the cows with young calves counted too. When
he had done the count and marked the three-year-olds, the joy of the Kurus went
about happily, surrounded by the cowherds. All the townspeople and the soldiers
in their thousands played in that forest as they pleased, like immortals.

Then cowherds came to wait on the son of Dhritarashtra, singers skilled in dance
and music, and girls in fine ornaments. Surrounded by crowds of women, the king
was delighted and gave them wealth, food and drink of many kinds, as each
deserved. Then all of them together drove hyenas, buffaloes and deer, gayals,
bears and boars from every side. Piercing them with arrows and catching
elephants in the great forest, he had deer caught in lovely places.

Enjoying the milk and the other pleasures, Bharata, and seeing the lovely
forests in flower, loud with drunken bees and the cries of peacocks, he came by
stages to the holy Dvaitavana lake. He was filled with the highest splendour,
like great Indra who holds the thunderbolt.

---

That same day, as it happened, Dharma's son Yudhishthira was holding a sacrifice
of the royal seers, one that is finished in a single day, lord of the people.
The king, best of the Kurus, held it with the divine rite and with the things of
the forest. The wise Kaurava had made his dwelling near that lake, with
Draupadi, his wife in dharma.

Then Duryodhana and his younger brothers ordered their servants, "Let pleasure
houses be made at once." "So be it," said the servants to the Kauravya, and they
did his bidding, and went to the Dvaitavana lake to make the pleasure houses.
The van of the son of Dhritarashtra's army reached the Dvaitavana lake. As it
was entering at the edge of the forest, gandharvas stopped it.

For the king of the gandharvas had come there before them from Kubera's palace,
surrounded by his hosts, lord of the people. He had come with hosts of apsarases
and with sons of the gods. He loved his pleasure, and he had closed that lake
off for his play. When the king's servants saw that the lake was closed off,
they went back to where King Duryodhana was.

When he heard what they said, the Kauravya sent soldiers who were wild for
battle, saying, "Drive them off." Hearing the king's order, the men of the van
went to the Dvaitavana lake and said to the gandharvas, "The mighty king named
Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, is coming here to take his pleasure. So get
out of the way."

At this the gandharvas laughed, lord of the people, and gave those men a very
harsh answer. "Your king, dull-witted Suyodhana, has no sense, if he gives
orders like this to us who live in heaven, as though we were his subjects. And
you are about to die, no doubt of it, you fools, since you talk to us like this
on his word, without a thought in your heads. Go back quickly, all of you, to
where that Kaurava king is, or you will go this very day to the hated house of
Yama, the King of Dharma."

When the gandharvas had said this, the men of the king's van ran back to where
the son of Dhritarashtra was.

---

They all came to Duryodhana together, great king, and told him what the
gandharvas had said. His army had been stopped by the gandharvas, and the mighty
son of Dhritarashtra was filled with rage, Bharata. He said to his troops,
"These creatures do not know dharma, and they do what displeases me. Punish
them, even if Shatakratu himself is taking his pleasure there with all the
gods!"

When they heard Duryodhana's words, the mighty sons of Dhritarashtra all armed
themselves, and so did the warriors in their thousands. Then they crushed the
gandharvas and forced their way into that forest, filling the ten directions
with a great lion's roar. Other gandharvas tried to stop the Kuru soldiers, lord
of the earth, and spoke to them only gently. But the soldiers paid them no heed
and went into that great forest.

When the sons of Dhritarashtra and their king would not stop at words, all those
who move in the sky told Chitrasena. The king of the gandharvas, Chitrasena,
most unforgiving, said of all those Kauravas, "Punish these low creatures."

With Chitrasena's leave, Bharata, all the gandharvas took up their weapons and
rushed at the sons of Dhritarashtra. When they saw the gandharvas swooping
swiftly on them with weapons raised, the Kauravas all ran from the fight while
the son of Dhritarashtra looked on.

Seeing all the sons of Dhritarashtra running with their backs turned, the hero
Vaikartana alone did not turn his back. When he saw the great army of the
gandharvas bearing down, Radheya held it back with a great rain of arrows. With
razor-headed arrows and feathered shafts, with broad-headed arrows and iron ones
with calf-tooth heads, the suta's son struck down gandharvas in hundreds, for
his hands were quick. Striking off the heads of the gandharvas, the great
chariot fighter scattered the whole of Chitrasena's army in a moment. Though the
wise suta's son was killing them, the gandharvas came on again in hundreds and
thousands. In a moment the earth was made all of gandharvas, as Chitrasena's
soldiers swooped down at great speed.

Then King Duryodhana, Shakuni son of Subala, Duhshasana, Vikarna and the other
sons of Dhritarashtra struck at that army with chariots that roared like Garuda.
They fought again, putting Karna in front, and with a great din of chariots and
the charge of horses, trying to protect Vaikartana, they held the gandharvas
back. Then all the gandharvas closed with the Kauravas, and there was a wild
battle that made the hair stand on end. Then the gandharvas, pierced by arrows,
grew weak, and the Kauravyas shouted when they saw them hurt.

When he saw the gandharvas frightened, Chitrasena, most unforgiving, leapt up
from his seat in anger, set on killing them. He knew many strange ways of
fighting. He fought with a weapon of illusion, and the Kauravyas were bewildered
by Chitrasena's illusion. Each single warrior of the son of Dhritarashtra found
himself surrounded by ten gandharvas, Bharata. Crushed by that great force, they
ran in fear from the battle to where King Yudhishthira was.

As the ranks of the sons of Dhritarashtra broke on every side, Karna Vaikartana
stood like an unmoving mountain, king. Duryodhana, Karna and Shakuni son of
Subala fought the gandharvas in the battle, badly wounded. Then all the
gandharvas together rushed at Karna in hundreds and thousands, wanting to kill
him. The mighty ones surrounded the suta's son on every side with swords,
spears, pikes and maces, eager to kill him.

Some cut his yoke, others brought down his banner. Others struck down the pole,
others the horses, others the driver. Some cut his parasol, some the fender, and
others the frame. The gandharvas in their many thousands struck the chariot to
pieces. Then the suta's son leapt down from the chariot with sword and shield,
mounted Vikarna's chariot, and drove the horses on to escape.

---

When the great chariot fighter Karna had been broken by the gandharvas, great
king, the whole army ran while the son of Dhritarashtra looked on. Seeing all
the sons of Dhritarashtra running with their backs turned, Duryodhana did not
turn his back, great king.

When he saw the great army of the gandharvas bearing down, the tamer of enemies
rained a great shower of arrows on it. Paying no heed to his rain of arrows, the
gandharvas surrounded his chariot on every side, eager to kill Duryodhana. The
yoke, the pole and the fender, the banner and the driver, the horses, the
three-bamboo frame and the seat: they struck the chariot into pieces as small as
sesame seeds. Then strong-armed Chitrasena rushed at Duryodhana as he lay fallen
on the ground without a chariot, and took him alive.

When he had been taken, king of kings, the gandharvas surrounded Duhshasana
where he stood in his chariot and seized him on every side. Others took
Vivimshati and Chitrasena and ran off with them. Others took Vinda and Anuvinda,
and all the king's women.

The soldiers of the son of Dhritarashtra, routed by the gandharvas, went with
those who had broken earlier to the Pandavas. The carts, the shops, the
courtesans, all the carriages and beasts of burden went to the Pandavas for
shelter as the king was carried off.

"The strong-armed son of Dhritarashtra, handsome and mighty, the king, is being
carried off by the gandharvas! Parthas, run after him! Duhshasana, Durvishaha,
Durmukha and Durjaya are being bound and carried off by the gandharvas, and all
the king's women!"

Crying out like this, all Duryodhana's counsellors came to Yudhishthira,
distressed and wretched, in voices full of misery, eager for their king.

Seeing Duryodhana's old counsellors so troubled and wretched, begging
Yudhishthira, Bhimasena said to them, "What we meant has turned out one way, and
what we were doing another. What we should have done, the gandharvas have done
for us. This was a bad plan of the king's, my friends, who plays at crooked
dice. We have heard that others bring down the enemy of a weakling. Now the
gandharvas have done it before our eyes, a deed beyond men. By good fortune
there is someone in the world who stands for what is dear to us, who has taken
our burden and brought us ease while we sit still. We bear cold, wind and heat,
and are worn thin by austerity, and the wicked man wanted to see us in our
trouble while he was at his ease. Those who follow the ways of that wicked
Kauravya who walks against dharma are seeing his fall. For whoever taught him
this did wrong. But the sons of Kunti are not cruel. I say it to you, his
overseers."

As Bhimasena, son of Kunti, spoke like this in his anger, the king said to him,
"This is no time for harsh words."

**Divergences.**

* **Duryodhana bound.** A rejected passage (\*1114) has Chitrasena tie Duryodhana's arms with a rope, set him on his own chariot and roar. In the CE, Chitrasena takes him alive (3.231.6). That he is bound comes later, in Chitrasena's own words (3.235.5–7). See L-147.
* **The Pandavas beside the lake.** A rejected line (\*1105) places Bhima, Arjuna and the twins with Yudhishthira by the lake, and another (\*1104) the forest brahmins. Not narrated.
* **Chitrasena's arrows.** A rejected passage (\*1110) describes Chitrasena's arrows darkening the sky and killing elephant riders and horsemen. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*1103, \*1106–\*1109, \*1111–\*1113, \*1115–\*1117) add descriptive detail or repeat what is said. Not narrated.

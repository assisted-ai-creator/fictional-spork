# Draupadi and Satyabhama

While the brahmins and the great Pandavas sat together, Draupadi and Satyabhama
went in together, laughing and glad, and sat down at their ease. Having seen
each other for the first time in a long while, king, the two sweet-spoken women
told each other bright stories of the Kurus and the Yadus. Then Satyabhama,
Krishna's dear queen, the daughter of Satrajit, slender-waisted, said this to
Yajnaseni in private.

"By what conduct, Draupadi, do you keep the Pandavas at your service? They are
heroes like the guardians of the world, young, held in the highest honour. How
are they in your power, and never angry with you, fair one? The Pandavas are
always in your power, lovely one, and they all watch your face. Tell me the
truth of it. Is it the practice of a vow, or austerity, or bathing, charms and
herbs, the power of some knowledge or the power of roots, prayers and fire
offerings, or drugs? Tell me, Panchali, the charm that brings fame and good
fortune, Krishna, by which my Krishna would always follow my will."

When she had said this, glorious Satyabhama fell silent. Draupadi, faithful to
her husbands and greatly blessed, answered her.

"You ask me, Satya, about the conduct of bad women. How could one speak of a
road that bad women travel? This question, or this doubt, is not fitting for
you, for you have understanding and you are Krishna's dear queen. The moment a
husband knows that his wife is given to charms and roots, he is as frightened of
her as of a snake in the house. How can a frightened man have peace, and how can
a man without peace be happy? A husband never comes into a wife's power because
of charms. People who want to kill send terrible drugs by enemies' hands, and
give poison under the name of roots. Powders given in what a man touches with
his tongue or his skin would kill him quickly, no doubt of it. Women have made
their husbands dropsical, leprous, grey, impotent, dull, blind and deaf. Evil
women who follow evil hurt their husbands. A wife must never do anything that
her husband dislikes.

"Hear from me, glorious Satyabhama, the whole truth of the conduct I keep
towards the great Pandavas. I put away pride, and desire and anger, always, and
I serve the Pandavas and their other wives with care. Holding back my love,
keeping myself to myself, I serve my husbands without pride, guarding their
hearts. I am afraid of speaking badly, of standing badly, of looking badly, of
sitting badly, of walking badly, and of showing my feelings. I serve the
Parthas, who are like the sun and fire, like the moon, great chariot fighters,
fierce in splendour and heat, who can kill with a look. A god, a man or a
gandharva, young and well adorned, rich or handsome: no other man means anything
to me.

"I do not eat or bathe or lie down until my husband has eaten and bathed and
lain down, and it is the same with our servants. When my husband comes home from
the field, the forest or the village, I get up and greet him and give him a seat
and water. I keep the vessels clean and the food well cooked, and serve the
meals on time. I am restrained, I keep the grain stored and the house well
swept. I speak without scorn, I do not keep company with bad women, I am always
agreeable and never lazy. I avoid laughing when there is no joke, standing at
the door again and again, and staying long in the privy or in the gardens. I
avoid loud laughter and great anger, and whatever brings on anger. I am always
devoted to truth, Satya, and to serving my husbands. Anything at all without my
husband I do not want in any way. When my husband is away from home on some
family business, I give up flowers and ointments and keep a vow. What my husband
does not drink, what he does not eat, what he does not taste, I give up all of
it.

"I keep to what I have been taught, fine lady, well adorned, careful, devoted to
my husband's pleasure and good. My mother-in-law told me long ago the duties of
the household: the alms, the offerings and rites for the dead, the pots of rice
cooked at the joints of the moon, and the honour and welcome due to those who
deserve honour. These and whatever else I have learned I follow day and night
without tiring. I hold to discipline and restraint always, with my whole self. I
wait on my gentle husbands, good, true in conduct, keepers of true dharma, as if
they were angry venomous snakes. For I think the dharma that rests on the
husband is the eternal dharma of women. He is her god, he is her way, there is
no other. What wife would do what he dislikes?

"I do not outdo my husbands in sleeping, eating or dressing, and I never speak
against my mother-in-law. I am always held in check. It is by attentiveness,
fortunate one, by always being up and ready, and by serving my elders, that my
husbands are in my power. Every day I myself serve noble Kunti, the mother of
heroes, who speaks the truth, with bathing, clothes and food. I never outdo her
in clothes, ornaments or food, and I never speak against Pritha, who is like the
earth.

"Eight thousand brahmins always ate first from golden plates in Yudhishthira's
house. Yudhishthira kept eighty-eight thousand snataka householders, each with
thirty slave women. The well-cooked food of ten thousand more, ascetics who had
drawn up their seed, was carried to them on golden plates. I used to honour all
those brahmins, speakers of brahman, with the first share, as each deserved,
with drink, clothing and food. The great son of Kunti had a hundred thousand
slave women. They wore shell bracelets and armlets and gold coins at their
necks, well adorned, with costly garlands and ornaments, fair, sprinkled with
sandal, wearing jewels and gold, skilled in dance and song. I knew the names and
faces of all of them, their food and clothing, and what work they had done and
not done. The wise son of Kunti had a hundred thousand slave women who fed
guests day and night with plates in their hands. A hundred thousand horses and a
hundred thousand elephants followed Yudhishthira when he lived in Indraprastha.

"That was the king's when he ruled the earth. I laid down their numbers and
their ordering, and I heard their reports. Of all the women's quarters and all
the servants, down to the cowherds and shepherds, I knew what was done and not
done. I alone knew all the king's income, revenue and spending, fair one, for
the glorious Pandavas. The bulls of the Bharatas put the whole household on me
and busied themselves with their worship, fair-faced one. I carried that burden
laid on me, which no wicked person could touch, giving up all ease, and worked
at it night and day. I alone knew the treasury of my righteous husbands, as
unassailable as Varuna's, like the ocean full of wealth. Serving the Kauravyas,
bearing hunger and thirst, night and day were the same to me. I wake first and I
lie down last, always, Satya. That is my charm. That is the great charm for
husbands that I know how to work. The conduct of bad women I would not follow
and do not want."

When she heard the words Krishna spoke then, full of dharma, Satya honoured the
Panchali, who walked in dharma, and said, "I am at fault, Panchali, Yajnaseni.
Forgive me. Friends are free to say things in jest."

---

"I will tell you this faultless way," said Draupadi, "to win your husband's
heart. If you keep to it as you should, friend, you will draw your husband away
from other women. There is no god like the husband, Satya, in all the worlds
with their gods. When he is pleased, all your wishes can be had. When he is
angry, he could destroy you. From him come children and pleasures of many kinds,
beds and seats wonderful to see, clothes, garlands and perfumes, the world of
heaven and great fame. Happiness is never won here by happiness. A good woman
wins happiness through hardship.

"So worship Krishna always with affection and love, and by returning his
kindness. With fine foods, the best garlands, with courtesy and scents of many
kinds, act so that he knows, 'I am dear to her,' and he will cling to you with
all his heart. When you hear the voice of your husband at the door, stand up and
wait in the middle of the house. When you see him come in, honour him quickly
with a seat and water for his feet. Even when you have sent a maid, get up and
do it all yourself. Let Krishna know this feeling of yours, Satya: 'She loves me
with all her heart.'

"Whatever your husband says in your presence, even if it is no secret, you must
keep it. Some fellow wife of yours might tell Vasudeva, and that would make him
cold. Feed those who are dear to your husband, who love him and wish him well,
in every way you can. Always keep apart from those he hates, those against him,
who wish him harm, and the tricksters. Put away pride and carelessness among
men, hold yourself in, and keep silent. Never wait on even the young princes
Pradyumna and Samba when you are alone. Be friends with women of great family,
without sin, good women. Avoid the violent, the drunken, the gluttonous, the
thieving, the wicked and the fickle. This brings fame and good fortune and
heaven, and destroys enemies. With costly garlands, ornaments and ointments,
fragrant, worship your husband."

---

When Janardana had sat with Markandeya and the other brahmins and the great
Pandavas, talking pleasantly, the slayer of Madhu took leave of them as was
proper. As he was about to mount his chariot, Keshava called Satya. Then
Satyabhama embraced Drupada's daughter and spoke to her kindly, as she felt.

"Krishna, do not long, do not grieve, do not lie awake. You will win the earth,
conquered by your husbands, who are like gods. Women with such good conduct and
such honoured marks as yours, black-eyed one, do not suffer long. I have heard
that you will surely enjoy this earth with your husbands, its thorns destroyed
and its troubles gone. When they have killed the sons of Dhritarashtra and paid
back the enmity, you will see the earth in Yudhishthira's hands, Drupada's
daughter. The Kuru women who laughed at you in their pride as you went out into
exile, you will soon see their hopes destroyed. Know, Krishna, that all those
who did you wrong when you were in sorrow have already set out for Yama's house.

"Your sons, Prativindhya and mighty Sutasoma, Arjuna's son Shrutakarman,
Nakula's son Shatanika, and your son born of Sahadeva, Shrutasena, all your sons
are well, heroes, and have learned their weapons. Like Abhimanyu, they are happy
and delight in Dvaravati. Subhadra is devoted to them with her whole heart, as
you are, and she loves them without distinction and free of care. The mother of
Pradyumna is devoted to them with her whole heart too. Keshava values them above
Bhanu and the rest. My father-in-law always sees to their food and clothing, and
all the Andhakas and Vrishnis, led by Rama, are devoted to them. Their love for
them is equal to their love for Pradyumna, fine lady."

When she had said these and other kind, loving and pleasing words, she turned
her mind to going to Vasudeva's chariot. Krishna's queen walked round Krishna,
keeping her on her right, and the lovely Satyabhama mounted Shauri's chariot.
The best of the Yadus smiled, comforted Draupadi, turned back, and went off with
his swift horses, the scorcher of enemies.

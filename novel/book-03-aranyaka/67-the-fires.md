# The Fires

When he had heard that fine tale full of dharma, the Dharma King questioned the
seer Markandeya, rich in austerity, again. "How did Agni go to the forest, and
how did Angiras, the great seer, become the fire long ago and carry the
offerings when the fire was lost? Agni is one, and yet in his work he is seen to
be many. Blessed one, I want to know all this: how Kumara was born, how he
became the son of Agni, and how he was born of Rudra, of the Ganga and of the
Krittikas. I want to hear it from you truly, joy of the Bhargavas, for I am full
of curiosity, great sage."

"On this they tell this ancient story," said Markandeya. "It tells how the eater
of offerings grew angry and went to the forest to practise austerity, and how
the blessed Angiras himself became the fire, scorching with his own light and
destroying the darkness. The greatly blessed one, living in his hermitage,
surpassed the carrier of offerings, and so became the fire and lit up the whole
world. As the eater of offerings practised austerity, he was scorched by the
other's splendour and grew very faint, and the splendid one knew nothing. Then
the blessed carrier of offerings thought, 'Brahma has set another fire going for
the worlds here, for my fire-nature was lost while I practised austerity.'
Thinking, 'How can I be the fire again?', he saw the great sage scorching the
worlds like a fire. He crept up to him slowly and in fear.

"Then Angiras said to him, 'Be the fire again quickly, you who give life to the
worlds. You are known in the three worlds, which move and stand still. You were
created first by Brahma, Agni, to drive away the darkness. Take your own place
again quickly, you who dispel darkness.'

"'My fame is lost in the world,' said Agni. 'You have become the eater of
offerings. People will know you as the fire, and not me. I lay down my
fire-nature. You be the first fire. I will be the second, the one called
Prajapatya.'

"'Do the holy work that brings creatures to heaven,' said Angiras. 'Be the fire
that drives away darkness. And, god, Agni, make me your first son straight
away.'

"When he heard these words of Angiras, Jatavedas did so, king. Angiras too had a
son named Brihaspati. When the gods learned that he was the first-born son of
the fire, a son of Angiras, they went to him and asked the reason, Bharata.
Asked by the gods, he told them the reason, and the gods accepted the words of
Angiras. Now I will tell you of the fires of many kinds and great splendour,
known by their many works in the brahmins' rites here.

---

"Hear from me, upholder of the Kuru line, about the third son of Brahma, his
wife and his offspring. His sons were Brihajjyotis, Brihatkirti, Brihadbrahman,
Brihanmanas, Brihanmantra, Brihadbhasa and Brihaspati, king. Among all those
children, the goddess named Bhanumati, the first daughter of Angiras, had no
equal in beauty. The second daughter of Angiras was the one in whom all
creatures took delight, whom they called Raga, from that delight. The third
daughter of Angiras, whom embodied beings call the daughter of Kapardin, seen
and unseen, is Sinivali, because she is so slender. Archishmati sees by her
rays, and Havishmati by the offerings: they call the sixth daughter of Angiras
the holy Havishmati. The seventh daughter, known as Mahamati, is the daughter of
Angiras in great and blazing sacrifices. And the daughter of Angiras whom people
greet with a cry of "kuhu" when they see her, the blessed one, they call Kuhu,
the single part.

"Brihaspati's glorious wife was Chandramasi, and she bore six holy fires and one
daughter. The fire whose offering in the oblations is clarified butter is
Brihaspati's son, the fire of great splendour named Shamyu. It is he whose
sacrifice is in the four-monthly rites, and the first victim in the horse
sacrifice, blazing with flames of many colours, one fire of great power.
Shamyu's matchless wife was Satya, true and born of Dharma. His son was a
blazing fire, and he had three daughters of good vows. The fire honoured with
the first share of butter in the sacrifice is called his first son, Bharadvaja.
The butter raised in the ladle at all the full-moon rites is for the fire named
Bharata, the second son of Shamyu. There are three other daughters, whose
husband is that Bharata. Bharata had a son, Bharata, and one daughter, Bharati.
Bharata was the son of the fire Bharata, and the great fire Pavaka, set down
with great care, is Prajapati's, best of the Bharatas.

"Bharadvaja's wife was Vira, and Vira the giver of the rice ball was his son.
The twice-born say that his worship is with butter, like Soma's, softly spoken.
The one who is joined with Soma in the second offering is called Rathaprabhu,
Rathadhvana and Kumbharetas. On the Sarayu he begot Siddhi, and he covered the
sun with his rays. He is called the one who brings the fire at the invocations.
The fire that never falls from fame, splendour and glory is named Nishchyavana,
and he praises only the earth. His son is the fire Vipapa, free of sin and
stain, pure, blazing with flame, true in the rites of agreement. The fire that
makes amends for creatures who cry out is named Nishkriti, and he shines when he
is served. His son is the fire named Svana, which brings sickness, by which
people moan here when they are struck by pain. The fire that stands pervading
the understanding of the whole universe those who know the self call Vishvajit.
The fire within that cooks the food eaten by the embodied is called Vishvabhuj
at the sacrifice in all the worlds, Bharata. Celibate, self-restrained, always
keeping great vows, brahmins worship him in the cooked-food sacrifices. The one
named Gopati, whose beloved was a river, is famous. All rites are performed in
him by those who do them. The most terrible one with the mare's mouth, who
drinks the water, the upward-going, named Urdhvabhaj, is a poet who rests in the
breath. The one whose offering is always given at the northern door of the
house, so that the butter is well offered, is held to be the highest,
Svishtakrit.

"The fire that becomes wrath when creatures are calm is Manyu. From the essence
of anger a daughter was born, Manyati, and Svaha, terrible and cruel, who dwells
in all creatures. The fire for whom there is no equal in beauty in heaven was
named Kama by the gods because he was beyond compare. The fire who holds his
anger in joy, bow in hand, garlanded, standing in his chariot, and who destroys
enemies in battle, is named Amogha. The greatly blessed one named Uktha, praised
with three hymns, begot Mahavach, whom they know as Sakamashva.

---

"Kashyapa, Vasishtha, Prana and Pranaputra, Agni Angirasa and Chyavana
Trishuvarchaka practised fierce austerity for many years for a son. 'May we have
a son most righteous, equal to Brahma in fame.' Meditated on by those five with
the great utterances, there was born one made of splendour, flaming, of five
colours, giving life. Blazing fire was his head, his arms were like the sun, his
skin and eyes like gold, and his shanks black, Bharata. Made of five colours by
the austerity of those five men, he is known in the Veda as Panchajanya, the
maker of five lines. When he had practised austerity for ten thousand years, the
great ascetic begot the terrible Pavaka, creating offspring of the Fathers. From
his head he created the Brihat and Rathantara chants, and from his mouth quickly
the two Haris. From his navel he created Shiva, from his strength Indra, from
his breath Vayu and Agni, from his arms the two Anudattas, and all creatures.
When he had created these, he created the five sons of the Fathers: Pranidhi,
son of Brihadurja, Brihattara, son of Kashyapa, the hero Bhanu, son of Angiras,
Saubhara, son of Varcha, and Anudatta, son of Prana. These are the five of the
lines, as told.

"Then he created fifteen more gods who steal the sacrifice. Tapas created these
five gods who steal the sacrifice: Abhima, Atibhima, Bhima, Bhimabala and Abala.
Tapas created these gods: Sumitra, Mitravant, Mitrajna, Mitravardhana and
Mitradharman. Tapas created these five: Surapravira, Vira, Sukesha, Suvarchas,
and the slayer of the gods. These stand in three groups of five, each apart.
Standing here, they steal from those who sacrifice, as those go to heaven. They
carry off their offerings and do great harm on earth. In rivalry with the
carriers of offerings, they harm and carry off. The offering on the altar is set
going by the skilled, and they do not come near where a fire is standing. The
fire that is laid, carrying the sacrifice, drives them off with its two wings,
and calmed by mantras they do not steal the sacrificial offering.

"The son of Tapas, Brihaduktha, has gone to the earth, and when the fire
offering is made he is worshipped on earth by good men. The fire Rathantara is
said to be a son of Tapas too, and the priests know the offering to him as
Mitravinda's. The glorious one rejoiced, very pleased, with his sons.

---

"The fire named Bharata, joined to great restraints, is the fire named
Pushtimati. When he is pleased he gives increase. He bears all creatures, and so
he is called Bharata. The fire named Shiva is given to the worship of power. He
always does good to all who are in pain, and so he is Shiva. Seeing that the
fruit of austerity had grown great, a wise son, Purandara, was born, wanting to
take it up. From Ushma was born Ushma, the fire that is seen in creatures. And
the fire named Manu performed the rite of Prajapati. The brahmins who have
mastered the Veda call a fire Shambhu. The twice-born call the blazing fire of
great splendour Avasathya. Tapas begot five sons of the sacrifice, carriers of
offerings who give strength, bright as gold. When the fire was calmed, greatly
blessed one, the tired Gavampati begot terrible asuras and mortals of many
kinds.

"Angiras created Manu, the son of Tapas, and Bhanu. The brahmins who have
mastered the Veda call him Brihadbhanu. Bhanu's wives were Supraja and
Brihadbhasa, the daughter of Soma, and they bore six sons. Hear how their
offspring were made. The fire that gives a body to weak creatures they call
Balada, the first son of Bhanu. The terrible fire that becomes wrath when
creatures are calm is named Manyumant, the second son of Bhanu. The fire whose
offering is at the new and full moon, named Vishnu here, is the Angiras named
Dhritimant. The fire whose offering, with Indra, is held to be the first-fruits
offering is named Agrayana, and he too is of Bhanu's line. He who is the one
without grasping among the constant offerings in the four-monthly rites, with
his four sons, is also of Bhanu's line. She begot a daughter, Nisha, and the
two, Agni and Soma. She became Manu's wife and bore five fires.

"The fire honoured with the first offering in the four-monthly rites, glorious,
together with Parjanya, is Vaishvanara. The one who is called lord of this whole
world is the fire named Vishvapati, the second son of Manu. Through him the
butter is well offered, and he is held to be the highest, Svishtakrit. The girl
named Rohini was the daughter of Hiranyakashipu. By her deed she became the wife
of that fire, who is Prajapati. The fire that rests in the breath and sets the
body of the embodied going is named Samnihita, the means of sound and form. The
god whose way is white and black, who bears the eater of offerings, is himself
without stain but makes stains, and rests in anger. The one the ascetics always
call the great seer Kapila is the fire named Kapila, who set Samkhya yoga going.
The fire by which creatures are always led in their many works here is called
Agrani, the leader.

"He created these other fires, famous on earth, powerful, for the atonement of a
spoiled fire offering. If fires should somehow touch one another through the
wind, a sacrifice on eight potsherds must be made to Agni Shuchi. If the
southern fire mixes with the other two, a sacrifice on eight potsherds must be
made to Agni Viti. If fires in a dwelling are touched by a forest fire, a
sacrifice on eight potsherds must be made to Agni Shuchi. If a woman in her
courses touches the fire of the fire offering, a sacrifice on eight potsherds
must be made to Agni Dasyumant. If one is heard to be dead while living, as when
cattle stray, a sacrifice on eight potsherds must be made to Agni Abhimata. If a
brahmin in distress does not offer to the fire for three nights, a sacrifice on
eight potsherds must be made to Agni Uttara. If a man's new-moon and full-moon
rites are left undone, a sacrifice on eight potsherds must be made to Agni
Pathikrit. If the fire of the birth chamber touches the fire of the fire
offering, a sacrifice on eight potsherds must be made to Agni Agnimant.

---

"Mudita was the dear wife of Saha, the fire of the waters. Bhupati, Bhuvabhartr,
begot the highest fire. Among the twice-born of good line they call him the
fire, lord of all creatures, the self, the bearer of the world. He is lord of
all the great creatures here, the blessed one of great splendour, and he is
always moving, the fire. The fire named Grihapati is always worshipped at
sacrifices. He is the fire who carries the offering that is offered for this
world. The greatly blessed child of the waters, most wonderful, with his son
Saha, Bhupati, Bhuvabhartr, is called the lord of the great. His fire was
Bharata, who burns dead creatures, and Niyata in the Agnishtoma, the finest of
rites, is Bhara's.

"Seeing Niyata coming, he entered the ocean in fear. The gods, searching every
quarter, could not find him. Then Agni saw Atharvan and said, 'Carry the
offerings to the gods, hero. I am very weak. Atharvan, go to Madhvaksha and do
this kindness for me.' Having sent Atharvan off, Agni went to another place. The
fish told of him, and Agni, angry, said to them, 'You will be eaten by embodied
beings in many ways.' And the carrier of offerings spoke to Atharvan too. Though
he was earnestly entreated at the gods' word, he would not carry all the
offering, and he gave up his body. Leaving that body, he entered the earth.
Touching the earth, he created the minerals, each separately. From his mouth
came fragrance and brightness, from his bones the deodar tree, from his phlegm
crystal, from his bile emerald, from his liver black iron. By these three things
creatures shone. His nails became mica and his network of veins coral. From his
body other minerals of many kinds came, king.

"So, having left his body, he stood in the highest austerity, and he was raised
up again by Bhrigu, Angiras and the others with their austerity. Strengthened by
austerity, the splendid flame blazed high. But seeing the seers, he entered the
great ocean again in fear. When he was lost, the world was afraid and turned to
Atharvan, and the divine seers honoured Atharvan. Atharvan, seeing the fire
himself, created the worlds, and as all creatures watched he churned the great
ocean. So the fire, lost long ago, was called back by the blessed Atharvan, and
he always carries the offerings of all creatures.

"So, wandering and roaming through many countries there, he begot many gods, the
fires of the altar told in the Veda. These rivers are called the mothers of the
altar fires. There are the five rivers besides the Sindhu, the Devika and
Sarasvati, the Ganga and Shatakumbha, the Sarayu and the one called Ganda. There
are the Charmanvati and Mahi, the Medhya and Medhatithi, and the three rivers
Tamravati, Vetravati and Kaushiki. There are the Tamasa and Narmada and the
river Godavari, the Venna, Praveni, Bhima and Medratha, Bharata. There are the
Bharati, Suprayoga, Kaveri and Murmura, the Krishna, the Krishnavenna, the
Kapila and the Shona.

"Adbhuta's beloved wife had a son, Vidurata. As many fires as have been told, so
many are the Somas. The mind-born children of Brahma were born in Atri's line.
Atri, wishing to create sons, held them in himself, and the eaters of offerings
carry them out from that brahmin's body. So I have told you of these great
fires, beyond measure, as they were born, glorious, dispelling darkness. Know
that the greatness of all of them is like the greatness of Adbhuta as told in
the Vedas, for this eater of offerings is one. This blessed one is to be known
as one, the first Angiras, who came forth from the body in many ways, like the
Jyotishtoma sacrifice. So I have told you this very great line of the fires.
Made pure by many mantras, it carries the offerings of embodied beings."

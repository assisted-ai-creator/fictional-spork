# Prayaga

"'Then,'" said Narada, telling Pulastya's words, "'a man should come at twilight
to the fine ford of Samvedya. A wise man who touches the water there will become
learned, no doubt of it. Long ago, by Rama's grace, a ford was made, king.
Coming to that Lohitya, he gains much gold. Coming to the Karatoya and fasting
three nights, a man wins the horse sacrifice, by the Grandsire's ordinance.
Where the Ganga meets the sea, king of kings, the wise say he wins ten times the
horse sacrifice. Reaching the island on the far side of the Ganga, Bharata, the
man who bathes there and fasts three nights gains every wish, king.

"'Then going to the Vaitarani, the river that frees a man from sin, and coming
to the ford of Viraja, he shines like the moon. He is born into a holy family
and puts away every sin. He gains the reward of a thousand cows and purifies his
family. Living pure where the Shona meets the Jyotirathya, and offering water to
the Fathers and the gods, he gains the reward of the agnishtoma. At the source
of the Shona and the Narmada, joy of the Kurus, touching the water at
Vamshagulma, he gains the reward of the horse sacrifice.

"'Coming to the ford of Rishabha in Kosala, lord of men, fasting three nights,
a man wins the vajapeya. Coming to Kosala, he should touch the water at
Kalatirtha. He gains the reward of eleven bulls, no doubt of it. Touching the
water of the Pushpavati and fasting three nights, he gains the reward of a
thousand cows and raises up his family. Then bathing at Badarikatirtha with a
controlled mind, he gains long life and goes to the world of heaven.

"'Then coming to Mahendra, where Jamadagnya lives, and bathing at Ramatirtha, a
man gains the reward of the horse sacrifice. The Kedara of Matanga is there too,
joy of the Kurus. Bathing there, king, a man gains the reward of a thousand
cows. Coming to Shriparvata, he should touch the water on the river bank. He
wins the horse sacrifice and goes to the world of heaven. On Shriparvata,
Mahadeva lived with the goddess, in great splendour and great joy, and Brahma
with the thirty gods around him. Bathing there in the gods' lake, pure and with
a controlled mind, he wins the horse sacrifice and reaches the highest
perfection. Going to the mountain Rishabha among the Pandyas, honoured by the
gods, he wins the vajapeya and rejoices on the vault of heaven.

"'Then he should go to the Kaveri, where hosts of apsarases go. Bathing there,
king, a man gains the reward of a thousand cows. Then on the shore of the sea he
should touch the water at Kanyatirtha. Touching the water there, king of kings,
he is freed from every sin.

"'Then he comes to Gokarna, famous in the three worlds, in the middle of the
sea, king of kings, honoured by all the worlds. There the gods with Brahma at
their head attend the husband of Uma. So do the seers rich in austerity,
spirits, yakshas and pishachas, kinnaras and great snakes, siddhas, charanas and
gandharvas, men and serpents, rivers, seas and mountains. Worshipping the Lord
there and fasting three nights, a man wins ten horse sacrifices and gains the
lordship of the god's hosts. Staying twelve nights, a man becomes perfect in
spirit. From there, the place of Gayatri, famous in the three worlds: staying
three nights there, he gains the reward of a thousand cows. There is a proof
there, plain to see, for brahmins, lord of men. When one of mixed birth recites
the Gayatri there, it comes out of him as a ballad or a song, king.

"'Coming to the rare pool of the brahmin seer Samvarta, a man shares in beauty
and is born fortunate. Then coming to the Venna, he should offer water to the
Fathers and the gods. A man gains a chariot of the air drawn by peacocks and
geese. Then reaching the Godavari, where siddhas always go, he wins the
gavamayana and reaches the world of Vasuki. Bathing where the Venna meets it, he
gains the reward of the vajapeya. Bathing where the Varada meets it, he gains
the reward of a thousand cows. Coming to Brahmasthana and staying three nights,
a man gains the reward of a thousand cows and goes to the world of heaven.
Coming to Kushaplavana, chaste and with a gathered mind, staying three nights
and bathing, he gains the reward of the horse sacrifice.

"'Then there is the lovely Devahrada, born of the water of the Krishnavenna, and
Jatimatrahrada, and Kanyashrama, king, where the king of the gods sacrificed
with a hundred rites and went to heaven. Merely by going there he gains a
hundred agnishtomas, Bharata. Bathing at Sarvadevahrada, he gains the reward of
a thousand cows. Bathing at Jatimatrahrada, a man remembers his former births.
Then reaching the most holy Payoshni, the best of rivers, devoted to worship of
the Fathers and the gods, he gains the reward of a thousand cows. Coming to the
Dandaka forest, great king, he should touch the water. There is the reward of a
thousand cows as soon as he bathes, Bharata. Going to the hermitage of
Sharabhanga and of great Shuka, a man does not come to a bad end and purifies
his family.

"'Then he should go to Shurparaka, where Jamadagnya lives. Bathing at
Ramatirtha, a man gains much gold. Bathing at Saptagodavara, restrained and
sparing in his food, he wins great merit and goes to the world of the gods. Then
he should go to Devapatha, restrained and sparing in his food. A man wins the
merit of the gods' session of sacrifice.

"'Coming to the Tungaka forest, chaste and master of his senses: there the seer
Sarasvata once taught the Vedas. When the Vedas had been lost, the son of the
sage Angiras sat on the upper cloths of the great seers, Bharata. By the
syllable om, pronounced rightly and properly, whatever each had learned before
came back to him. The seers there and the gods, Varuna, Agni and Prajapati, the
god Hari Narayana, and Mahadeva too, and the blessed Grandsire in his splendour
with the gods, appointed splendid Bhrigu to perform the sacrifice. Then the
blessed one set up the fires again for all the seers, as the rules require, with
the rites the rules prescribe. Satisfied as the rules require with their share
of the ghee, the gods went to the three worlds, and the seers went as they
pleased. When a woman or a man enters that Tungaka forest, best of kings, all
their sin is destroyed. A steady man should live there a month, restrained and
sparing in his food. He goes to Brahma's world, king, and purifies his family.

"'Coming to Medhavika, he should offer water to the Fathers and the gods. He
wins the agnishtoma and gains memory and wisdom. Then going to Kalanjara, the
mountain famous in the world, and bathing there in the gods' lake, he gains the
reward of a thousand cows. He should perfect himself there, on Mount Kalanjara,
king. A man is honoured in the world of heaven, no doubt of it.

"'Then, lord of the people, on Chitrakuta, the best of fine mountains, he comes
to the Mandakini, the river that frees a man from sin. Bathing there, devoted to
worship of the Fathers and the gods, he wins the horse sacrifice and goes the
highest way. Then, king of kings, he should go to the matchless Bhartristhana,
where the god Mahasena is always present, king. A man succeeds there merely by
going, best of men. Bathing at Kotitirtha, a man gains the reward of a thousand
cows. Going round it, a man should go to Jyeshthasthana. Going to Mahadeva, he
shines like the moon. There is a well there, great king, famous, bull of the
Bharatas, where the four seas live, Yudhishthira. Touching the water there, king
of kings, and going round it, a man with a controlled self is made pure and goes
the highest way.

"'Then, best of the Kurus, he should go to the great city of Shringaverapura,
where Rama Dasharathi once crossed over, great king. Bathing in the Ganga,
chaste and with a gathered mind, a man shakes off his sin and wins the vajapeya.
Going to Mahadeva and worshipping him, lord of men, and going round him, he
gains the lordship of the god's hosts.

---

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to Prayaga, praised by the seers. The gods
live there with Brahma at their head, and the quarters with their lords, the
guardians of the world, the sadhyas, the nairritas and the Fathers. The great
seers live there with Sanatkumara at their head, and the brahmin seers with
Angiras at their head, and others. The snakes and the suparnas are there, the
siddhas and those who move in circles, the rivers and seas, the gandharvas and
apsarases, and the blessed Hari, with Prajapati before him. There are three pits
of fire, and between them the Jahnavi flows out from Prayaga, with all the fords
before her. There the daughter of the Sun, famous in the three worlds, the
Yamuna, which purifies the world, meets the Ganga. The land between the Ganga
and the Yamuna is known as the loins of the earth, and the seers know Prayaga as
the part within the loins.

"'Prayaga with Pratishthana, Kambala and Ashvatara, and the ford Bhogavati, are
called the altar of Prajapati. There, Yudhishthira, the Vedas and the sacrifices
in bodily form attend Prajapati, and the seers of great vows. The gods and those
who move in circles sacrifice there with their rites, king. There is nothing
holier than that in the three worlds, Bharata. Prayaga surpasses all the fords,
lord. By hearing of that ford, by speaking its name, or by touching its earth, a
man is freed from sin. The man of strict vows who bathes at the meeting there
gains the holy reward of the royal consecration and the horse sacrifice. This is
the place of sacrifice honoured even by the gods. What is given there, Bharata,
even a little, becomes great. Let no word of the Veda, my son, nor any word of
the world, turn your mind from dying at Prayaga. Ten thousand fords and six
hundred million more are said to be present here, joy of the Kurus. Whatever
merit lies in the four Vedas and in those who speak the truth, a man gains by
bathing where the Ganga and the Yamuna meet.

"'There is Bhogavati, the fine ford of Vasuki. Whoever bathes there wins the
horse sacrifice. There is Hamsaprapatana, a ford famous in the three worlds, and
Dashashvamedhika on the Ganga, joy of the Kurus. Wherever the Ganga is, great
king, that land is a forest of austerity. The land along the bank of the Ganga
should be known as a field of perfection.

"'This truth one should whisper in the ear of the twice-born, of good men, of
one's son and friends, and of a devoted pupil. This is lawful, this is holy,
this is pure, this is happiness. This leads to heaven, this is lovely, this is
the highest purifier. This is the secret of the great seers, which frees a man
from every sin. Reciting it among the twice-born, one becomes spotless. The man
who always hears, pure, of the merit of the fords remembers many births and
rejoices on the vault of heaven. The fords that can be reached, and those told
of that cannot, one should visit in the mind, longing to see all the fords.
Vasus, sadhyas, Adityas, Maruts and Ashvins, and seers like gods, seeking good
deeds, have gone to them.

"'So you too, Kauravya, you of good vows, go to the fords by this rule, with
restraint. Merit grows by merit. Those fords are reached by good men who follow
the learned, through causes cultivated before, through faith and through knowing
the scriptures. No one without vows, no one of unformed self, no one impure, no
thief and no man of crooked mind bathes in the fords, Kauravya. But by you,
always well behaved, who see dharma and wealth, your fathers have been carried
across, my son, and all your great-grandfathers. The gods with the Grandsire at
their head, with the hosts of seers, king, are always pleased by your dharma,
you who know dharma. You will reach the worlds of the Vasus, you who are like
Vasava, and win great and lasting fame on the earth, Bhishma.'

"When he had said this and taken his leave, the blessed seer Pulastya, pleased
and glad at heart, vanished there and then. And Bhishma, tiger of the Kurus, who
saw into the truth and meaning of the texts, went round the earth at Pulastya's
word.

"Whoever goes round the earth by this rule will enjoy after death a reward above
that of a hundred horse sacrifices. And you, Partha, will gain eight times the
highest dharma, because you will lead the seers. That is why your reward will be
eightfold. These fords are overrun by hosts of rakshasas, Bharata. No one but
you can go there, joy of the Kurus. Whoever rises at dawn and recites this
account of the divine seer, which rests on the meaning of all the fords, is
freed from every sin.

"The chief seers are always there. Valmiki is there, and Kashyapa, Atreya and
Kaundinya, Vishvamitra and Gautama, Asita Devala, Markandeya and Galava. So are
Bharadvaja and Vasishtha, the sage Uddalaka, Shaunaka with his son, and Vyasa,
the best of those who pray, Durvasas, the best of sages, and Galava of great
austerity. All these fine seers, rich in austerity, are waiting for you. Go to
these fords with them, great king. This divine seer of boundless splendour named
Lomasha will meet you. Go with him. And go to these fords with me too, you who
know dharma. You will win great fame, like King Mahabhisha. As righteous Yayati,
as King Pururavas, so you, tiger of the Kurus, shine by your own dharma. As King
Bhagiratha, as famous Rama, so you shine above all kings like the sun. As Manu,
as Ikshvaku, as glorious Puru, as splendid Vainya, so you are famous. As the
killer of Vritra once burned up all his rivals, so you will destroy your enemies
and protect your people. When you have won the earth by your own dharma,
lotus-eyed one, you will be famed for your dharma, like Kartavirya Arjuna."

When the blessed seer Narada had comforted the king like this, he took leave of
the great man and vanished there and then. And righteous Yudhishthira, thinking
over that same matter, told the seers of his holy plan of a pilgrimage to the
fords.

**Divergences.**

* **The bridge.** A rejected passage (\*438, after 3.83.20) adds Setutirtha, "the ford of the bridge",
  which removes the sin of killing a brahmin. Not in the CE.
* **The Ganga's water.** An appendix passage (@13, after 3.83.82) says Ganga water burns up a hundred
  wrong deeds as fire burns fuel, and praises Kanakhala and Prayaga above all. Not in the CE.
* **The merit of hearing.** A rejected passage (\*448) promises a son to the sonless and wealth to the
  poor who hear the list of fords. The CE's promise is 3.83.84–87.

**Choices.**

* 3.83.11 *vṛṣabhaikādaśaphalam*: "the reward of eleven bulls".
* 3.83.22–25: the text's long list of those who attend Shiva at Gokarna is kept whole.

* 3.83.27: the "proof" at the place of Gayatri is obscure; it is given as the text seems to give it.
* 3.83.44 *muner aṅgirasaḥ sutaḥ*: "the son of the sage Angiras", unnamed. 3.83.43 *ṛṣiḥ sārasvataḥ*:
  the seer Sarasvata.
* 3.83.57 *mahāsenaḥ*: Mahasena, Skanda.
* 3.83.65–68: the long list of those who live at Prayaga is kept whole.

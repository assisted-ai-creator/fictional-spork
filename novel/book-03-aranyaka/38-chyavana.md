# Chyavana

"The great seer Bhrigu had a son named Chyavana, the Bhargava," said Lomasha.
"He practised austerity near this lake, in great splendour. Standing like a post
in the posture of the hero, the mighty one stayed in one place for a very long
time, lord of the people. The seer became an ant hill, covered with creepers,
and in a long time, king, he was overrun with ants. Covered like that on every
side, like a lump of earth, the wise man practised austerity, king, wrapped in
the ant hill.

"After a long time a king named Sharyati came to this lovely, fine lake to take
his pleasure. He had four thousand women in his household, and a single bright
daughter named Sukanya, Bharata. Surrounded by her friends, adorned with every
jewel, walking about, she came to the Bhargava's ant hill. The girl with the
lovely teeth walked there with her friends, looking at the charming trees,
choosing among them. Full of beauty and youth, of love and high spirits, she
broke the flowering branches of the forest trees.

"The wise Bhargava saw her there alone, without her friends, in a single
garment, adorned, moving like lightning. Looking at her in that lonely place,
the splendid one was delighted, and the brahmin seer, his throat dry, strong
with the power of austerity, called to the lovely girl. But she did not hear
him. Then Sukanya saw the Bhargava's eyes in the ant hill. Out of curiosity, her
mind carried away, she said, 'What is this?' and pierced his eyes with a thorn.
With his eyes pierced by her, he was furious, and he stopped the dung and urine
of Sharyati's army.

"When their dung and urine were stopped, the army suffered from swollen bellies.
Seeing it like that, the king asked, 'Who has wronged the great Bhargava here
today, an old man always given to austerity, and very quick to anger? Whether it
was done knowingly or not, tell the truth quickly.' All the soldiers said to
him, 'We do not know of any wrong. Find it out as you will, by every means.'
Then the lord of the earth questioned his friends himself, gently and harshly,
but they did not know.

"When Sukanya saw the army suffering from swollen bellies and her father in
distress, she said, 'As I was walking here, I saw in an ant hill a creature
shining like a firefly. I did not know what it was, and I pierced it close by.'
When he heard this, Sharyati ran at once to the ant hill. There he saw the
Bhargava, old in austerity and old in years. The lord of the earth joined his
hands and begged him for the sake of his army. 'You should forgive what my girl
did in ignorance.'

"Then Chyavana the Bhargava said to the king, 'She is full of beauty and grace,
and greed and folly carried her away. If I take your daughter, king, I will
forgive her, lord of the earth. I tell you the truth.' Hearing the seer's words,
Sharyati did not hesitate. He gave his daughter to the great Chyavana. When he
had taken the girl, Chyavana was appeased, and the king, forgiven, went home
with his army.

"And blameless Sukanya, when she had an ascetic for her husband, always served
him with love, with austerity and discipline. The lovely-faced woman, eager to
serve the fires and the guests, free of envy, quickly won Chyavana's favour.

---

"Then after some time, king, the Ashvins of the gods saw Sukanya after she had
bathed, uncovered. She was lovely in every limb, like the daughter of the king
of the gods. When they saw her, the Nasatyas, the Ashvins, ran up to her. 'Whose
are you, lovely-thighed one, and what are you doing in the forest? We want to
know you, good lady. Tell us, lovely one.'

"Then Sukanya covered herself and said to those best of the gods, 'Know that I
am Sharyati's daughter and Chyavana's wife.' Then the Ashvins laughed and said
to her again, 'How is it, lovely one, that your father gave you to a man far
gone on his road? You shine in the middle of the forest like lightning in the
clouds. We do not see your equal even among the gods, beautiful one. With every
ornament and the finest clothes you would shine, flawless one, but not like
this, covered in dirt and mud. Why, being what you are, do you serve an old
husband broken by age, shut out of love and pleasure, lovely one, who cannot
protect you or feed you, you of the bright smile? Leave Chyavana and choose one
of us for your husband, you who are like a child of the gods. Do not waste your
youth.'

"When they said this, Sukanya said to the gods, 'I love my husband Chyavana. Do
not think otherwise of me.' They said to her again, 'We are the best physicians
of the gods. We will make your husband young and handsome. Then choose one of us
or him for your husband. Tell him of this agreement, lovely-faced one.'

"At their word she went to the Bhargava, king, and told the son of Bhrigu what
they had said. When Chyavana heard it, he said to his wife, 'Let it be done.'
With her husband's leave she said, 'Let it be done.' When the Ashvins heard her
words, 'Let it be done,' they said to the princess, 'Let your husband go into
the water.' Then Chyavana, wanting beauty, quickly went into the water, king,
and the Ashvins too went into the lake, lord.

"A moment later they all came out of the lake, all of them divinely beautiful,
young, with polished earrings, all looking the same, delighting the heart. All
together they said, 'Choose one of us, good lady, the one you want for your
husband, lovely one. Choose the one you love, beautiful one.' She looked at all
of them standing there looking the same, and when she had made up her mind with
her heart and her understanding, the lady chose her own husband.

"When Chyavana had his wife, and the youth and beauty he wanted, the splendid
one spoke gladly to the Nasatyas. 'You have made me, an old man, handsome and
young, and I have won this wife. So I will gladly make you drinkers of soma
before the eyes of the king of the gods. I tell you the truth.' When they heard
this, they went back to heaven, glad at heart. And Chyavana and Sukanya took
their pleasure like gods.

---

"When Sharyati heard that Chyavana had been made young, he came with his army to
the Bhargava's hermitage, overjoyed. Seeing Chyavana and Sukanya like the
children of gods, King Sharyati was as delighted as if he had won the whole
earth. The seer honoured the lord of the earth and his wife, and the
great-hearted king sat near him and talked of good things. Then the Bhargava
said to him, comforting him, 'I will perform a sacrifice for you, king. Get the
things ready.'

"King Sharyati, overjoyed, welcomed Chyavana's word, great king. On an
auspicious day fit for sacrifice, Sharyati had a fine place of sacrifice made,
rich in everything one could want. There Chyavana the Bhargava performed his
sacrifice, king, and there were wonders there. Hear them from me.

"Chyavana took up the soma for the two gods, the Ashvins. As he took up their
cup, Indra tried to stop him. 'I do not think these two Nasatyas are fit for
soma,' said Indra. 'They are the physicians of the sons of the gods, and by
their work they do not deserve it.'

"'Do not despise these great ones, so rich in beauty,' said Chyavana, 'who have
made me ageless like a god, Maghavan. Apart from you and the other gods, why
should they not have a share of the pressing? Know that the Ashvins too are
gods, lord of the gods, Purandara.'

"'They are doctors and workmen, taking what forms they please, going about in
the world of mortals,' said Indra. 'How can they deserve soma here?'

"When Vasava repeated these same words, the Bhargava paid no heed to Shakra and
took up the cup. As he was about to take up the finest soma for the Ashvins, the
god who split Bala saw him. 'If you take up the soma for them yourself, I will
strike you with my thunderbolt, terrible and matchless.' When he said this, the
Bhargava smiled, looked at Indra, and took up the finest cup of soma for the
Ashvins as the rules require. Then the husband of Shachi hurled at him the
terrible thunderbolt. As he hurled it, the Bhargava paralysed his arm.

"When he had paralysed him, Chyavana poured an offering into the fire with
mantras. Wanting a creature of magic, the splendid one set out to harm the god.
Then by the power of the seer's austerity the creature came into being, a great
asura named Mada, very mighty, of huge body, whose body neither gods nor asuras
could describe. His mouth was terrible, with sharp-pointed teeth, and huge. One
jaw was on the ground, the other reached to heaven. He had four long fangs, each
a hundred leagues, and his other teeth were ten leagues each, like ramparts,
looking like the points of spears. His arms were like mountains, ten thousand
leagues long and equal. His eyes were like the sun and moon, his mouth like
Death. He licked his mouth with a tongue that flickered like lightning. His jaws
gaping, his look terrible, as if he were swallowing the world by force, he
rushed in fury at Shatakratu to eat him, filling the worlds with a great and
terrible roar.

---

"The god Shatakratu saw Mada with his terrible face coming to eat him, jaws
gaping like Death. His arm was paralysed, and he licked the corners of his mouth
again and again in fear. Tormented by fear, the king of the gods said to
Chyavana, 'From this day these two Ashvins will be fit for soma, Bhargava. I
tell you this truly, brahmin. Let your undertaking not be in vain. Let this be
the highest rule. And I know, brahmin seer, that you would not do anything in
vain. As you have made the Ashvins fit for soma today, so let your power shine
forth still more, Bhargava. Let the fame of Sukanya and her father spread in the
world. That is why I did this, to show your power. So be gracious to me. Let it
be as you wish.'

"When Shakra said this, great Chyavana's anger quickly left him, and he set
Purandara free. And the mighty one divided Mada, whom he had made, among drink,
women, dice and hunting, again and again. So he cast Mada away and satisfied
Shakra with soma, and performed the sacrifice for the gods with the Ashvins and
for the king. Then the best of speakers made his power known in all the worlds
and took his pleasure in the forest with Sukanya, who loved him.

"This is his lake, king, loud with the twice-born, shining. Offer water here to
the Fathers and the gods with your brothers. When you have seen this, lord of
the earth, and Sikataksha, Bharata, go to the Saindhava forest and see the
channels. Touch the water in all the Pushkaras, great king. Here is the Archika
mountain, the home of the wise, always in fruit, always flowing, the finest
place of the Maruts. And here are the hundreds of shrines of the thirty gods,
Yudhishthira. This is the ford of the Moon, which the seers attend, the
Vaikhanasas and the Valakhilyas. There are three holy peaks and three springs.
Go round them all and touch the water as you please.

"Shantanu came here, son of Kunti, and Shunaka, lord of men, and Nara and
Narayana both, and reached the eternal place. The gods who always lie here, and
the Fathers, with the great seers, practised austerity on the Archika mountain.
Worship them, Yudhishthira. Here they ate the offerings of rice, and the seers
too, lord of the people. And here is the Yamuna, whose stream never fails, and
Krishna, given to austerity. The twins and Bhimasena and Krishna, tamer of
enemies, and all of us will go there, lean and rich in austerity. This is the
holy spring of Indra, lord of men, where Dhatar and Vidhatar and Varuna went
upward. Here they lived, king, patient and supremely righteous. This fine
mountain belongs to those who are friendly and straight of mind. This is the
Yamuna, king, where hosts of royal seers go, piled with sacrifices of every
kind, holy, taking away the fear of sin. Here the great archer King Mandhatar
sacrificed himself, son of Kunti, and Sahadeva, and Somaka, the best of givers."

**Divergences.**

* **Sukanya's truth-oath.** Rejected passages (\*601–\*603) have Sukanya swear by her truth, as Damayanti did,
  and the gods show her Chyavana. In the CE she simply decides "with her heart and her understanding" and
  chooses her own husband (3.123.19).
* **A verse for the eyes.** A rejected passage (\*608) adds a verse sung of Chyavana that saves the eyes of those
  who hear it. Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 3.122.2 *vīrasthānena*: "in the posture of the hero".
* 3.122.11 *kṣāmakaṇṭhaḥ*: "his throat dry"; the sense is uncertain.
* 3.122.13: the stopping of the army's dung and urine is the text's.
* 3.123.5 *gatādhvane*: "a man far gone on his road", old.

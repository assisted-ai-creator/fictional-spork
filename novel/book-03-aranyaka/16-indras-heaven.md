# Indra's Heaven

As Arjuna watched, the bearer of Pinaka, whose banner is a bull, vanished, like
the sun setting on the world. Arjuna, the killer of enemy heroes, was filled
with wonder, Bharata. "I have seen the great god with my own eyes. I am blessed,
I am favoured, for I have seen Hara, the three-eyed, bearer of Pinaka, giver of
boons, in bodily form, and touched him with my hand. I know myself fulfilled, my
enemies all beaten, my purpose done."

Then the lord of the waters came, splendid, the colour of beryl, lighting up all
the quarters, with the creatures of the waters around him. Varuna, the master of
the water beasts, the controller, came to that place with snakes, rivers male
and female, daityas, sadhyas and gods. Then Kubera came, the lord, with a body
of river gold, in a blazing chariot of the air, with yakshas following him. The
splendid lord of wealth, wonderful to see, came to see Arjuna, lighting up the
sky. And Yama came in person, splendid and mighty, who makes an end of the
worlds. With him came the Fathers, who bring the worlds into being, some with
bodies and some without. The rod was in his hand, the son of Vivasvat, the king
of dharma, whose self is past thought, who destroys all beings. He lit up the
three worlds from his chariot, the guhyakas, the gandharvas and the snakes, like
a second sun when the end of an age has come. They took their places on the
bright, many-coloured peaks of the great mountain, and there they saw Arjuna in
his austerity.

A moment later the blessed Shakra came, riding on the head of Airavata, with
Indrani and the hosts of the gods around him. With a white parasol held over his
head, he shone like the moon, the king of the stars, resting on a white cloud.
Praised by gandharvas and by seers rich in austerity, he came to the peak of the
mountain and stood there like the risen sun.

Then wise Yama, who knows the highest dharma, standing in the southern quarter,
spoke in a voice like thunder, and his words were good.

"Arjuna, Arjuna, see us, the guardians of the world, gathered here. We grant you
sight today, for you deserve to see us. You are an ancient seer of boundless
spirit, the mighty one called Nara, who has become mortal at Brahma's command,
my son. You were born of Vasava, great in strength and courage. The kshatriya
order, fierce as fire to the touch, guarded by Bharadvaja's son, must be
overcome by you, joy of the Kurus. So must the mighty danavas who have been born
as men, and the Nivatakavachas too. Karna, a portion of my father, the god who
warms all the worlds, is to be killed by you, Dhananjaya, mighty as he is. The
portions of gods, gandharvas and rakshasas who have come down to earth, felled
by you in battle, will reach each the end their own deeds have won, son of
Kunti, grinder of enemies. Your fame will stand in the world undying, Phalguna.
For you have pleased the great god himself in a great fight. And the earth must
be lightened by you, with Vishnu. Take this weapon, strong-armed one, the Rod,
that cannot be turned aside. With this weapon you will do a very great deed."

Partha, the joy of the Kurus, received it as the rules require, with its
mantras, with the rites, with how to loose it and how to call it back.

Then Varuna, the lord of the water beasts, dark as a rain cloud, standing in the
western quarter, raised his voice. "Partha, you are the first of kshatriyas,
firm in a kshatriya's dharma. Look at me, you with the wide copper-red eyes. I
am Varuna, lord of the waters. Receive from me my nooses, Varuna's own, that
cannot be warded off, with their secrets and the way to call them back, son of
Kunti. With these, hero, in the war over Tara, I bound thousands of the great
sons of Diti. Take them, then, great-hearted one, risen by my favour. Not even
Death could escape you, if he came against you with them. When you range the
battle with this weapon, the earth will be empty of kshatriyas, beyond doubt."

When Varuna and Yama had given their divine weapons, the keeper of wealth, whose
home is Kailasa, spoke. "Strong-armed Savyasachin, ancient god, eternal, in ages
past you have always laboured beside us. Take a weapon from me too, killer of
enemies, the Vanishing, which I love. It takes away strength, splendour and
light, and puts to sleep." Then strong-armed, mighty Arjuna, joy of the Kurus,
took Kubera's divine weapon too, as the rules require.

Then the king of the gods spoke to Partha, whose deeds are never strained, and
comforted him with gentle words, in a voice like thunder and drums.
"Strong-armed son of Kunti, you are an ancient lord. You have reached the
highest perfection, you have gone the way of the gods themselves. You have a
very great work of the gods to do, scourge of enemies. You must climb to heaven.
Make ready, splendid one. A chariot yoked by Matali will come down to earth for
you. There I will give you divine weapons, Kaurava."

When wise Dhananjaya, the son of Kunti, saw the guardians of the world gathered
on the mountaintop, he was filled with wonder. Then Arjuna, full of splendour,
honoured the guardians of the world as the rules require, with words, with water
and with fruits. And the gods honoured Dhananjaya in return and went back the
way they had come, all of them, going wherever they wished as fast as thought.
Then Arjuna, bull among men, rejoiced, for he had the weapons. He thought
himself fulfilled, and his heart was full.

---

When the guardians of the world had gone, Partha, the destroyer of enemies,
thought of the coming of the king of the gods' chariot, king of kings. As wise
Gudakesha was thinking of it, the chariot yoked by Matali came in great
splendour. It drove the darkness from the sky, as if tearing the clouds apart,
and filled the quarters with a noise like the roar of great clouds. On it were
swords, terrible spears and maces fearful to see, darts of divine power and
lightnings of great brilliance. There were thunderbolts, and wheeled engines of
war driven by the wind, with a crash like thunder, sounding like clouds when
peacocks call. There were huge snakes with blazing mouths, very fierce, like
peaks of white cloud, packed close together like stones. Ten thousand bay
horses, swift as the wind, drew that divine chariot, made of illusion, that
dazzled the eye. On it he saw the great banner Vaijayanta, of deep blue,
blazing, dark as a blue lotus, its staff adorned with gold. When strong-armed
Partha saw the charioteer standing in that chariot, adorned with burnished gold,
he took him for a god.

As Phalguna was thinking this, Matali bowed low and spoke to Arjuna with
courtesy. "Splendid son of Shakra, Shakra wishes to see you. Climb quickly into
Indra's chariot, which he has sent. The best of the immortals, your father of
the hundred sacrifices, told me, 'Let the dwellers in heaven see the son of
Kunti come here.' Shakra waits to see you, with the gods around him, and the
hosts of seers, gandharvas and apsarases. At the command of the chastiser of
Paka, climb with me from this world to the world of the gods. When you have the
weapons you will come back."

"Matali," said Arjuna, "go quickly. Climb into the chariot first. It is hard to
win, this best of chariots, even with hundreds of royal consecrations and horse
sacrifices, for great and fortunate kings who sacrifice and give rich fees, for
gods or for danavas. This divine great chariot cannot be seen by one who has not
done austerity, or touched, let alone climbed. When you are standing in the
chariot, good friend, and the horses are steady, I will climb in after you, as a
man of good deeds climbs onto the good road."

When he heard these words, Matali, Shakra's charioteer, quickly climbed into the
chariot and held the horses with the reins. Then Arjuna, the son of Kunti, glad
at heart, bathed in the Ganga and made himself pure. The joy of the Kurus said
the prayers that must be said, as the rules require. Then he offered water to
the Fathers, as is right and proper, and began to take leave of Mandara, the
king of mountains.

"Mountain, you are always the refuge of good men who keep dharma, of sages of
holy deeds, who long for the road to heaven. By your favour, mountain, brahmins,
kshatriyas and vaishyas have always reached heaven and walked with the gods,
free from pain. King of hills, great mountain, refuge of sages, rich in holy
fords, I am going. I take my leave of you. I have lived happily on you. I have
seen your slopes and thickets, your rivers and springs and your holiest fords,
again and again."

When he had said this, Arjuna, the killer of enemy heroes, took leave of the
mountain and climbed into the divine chariot, lighting it up like the sun. In
that divine chariot, bright as the sun, whose deeds were wonderful, the wise joy
of the Kurus rose upwards, full of joy.

When he had gone out of sight of mortals who walk the earth, he saw chariots of
the air in their thousands, wonderful to see. No sun shines there, no moon and
no fire. They shine there by their own light, won by merit. The lights that are
seen from here as stars look small as lamps because they are so far away, though
they are very great. The Pandava saw them there, shining and beautiful, each in
its own place, blazing with its own fire. There royal seers and siddhas, and
heroes killed in battle, and those who had won heaven by austerity, came
together in hundreds of throngs. There were gandharvas in their thousands, with
the splendour of the sun and of fire, and hosts of guhyakas, seers and
apsarases.

Seeing those worlds, lit by their own light, Phalguna was filled with wonder and
gladly questioned Matali. And Matali told him, "These are men of good deeds,
Partha, each in his own place. It is they whom you have seen from the earth as
stars, lord."

Then he saw standing at the gate the white elephant Airavata, bringer of
victory, with four tusks, like Kailasa with its peaks. Taking the road of the
siddhas, the best of the Kurus and the Pandavas shone, as Mandhatar, the best of
kings, shone of old. The lotus-eyed one passed beyond the worlds of the kings,
and then he saw Shakra's city, Amaravati.

---

He saw the lovely city, where siddhas and charanas lived, adorned with holy
trees that flower in every season. A breeze fanned him there, mixed with the
holy scent of the fragrant saugandhika trees. He saw the divine forest of
Nandana, where hosts of apsarases lived, and its trees with their divine flowers
seemed to call him in.

That world of the doers of merit cannot be seen by one who has not done
austerity, or who has not kept the sacred fire, nor by those who turn their
backs on battle. It cannot be seen by those who do not sacrifice, by liars, or
by those who have not heard the Veda. Nor can it be seen by those who have not
bathed in the holy fords, or by those shut out from sacrifice and gifts. It
cannot be seen by those who wreck sacrifices, by the base, by drinkers, by those
who defile their teacher's bed, by meat eaters or by the wicked.

Seeing that divine forest, loud with divine song, the strong-armed one entered
Shakra's beloved city. There he saw the gods' chariots of the air that go where
they wish, in their thousands, some standing, some moving, in tens of thousands.
Gandharvas and apsarases sang the Pandava's praises, and holy winds, carrying
the scent of flowers, fanned him. Then the gods, with the gandharvas, the
siddhas and the great seers, gladly honoured Partha, whose deeds are never
strained. Praised with blessings, to the sound of divine instruments, the
strong-armed one went on, where conches and drums were sounding.

At Indra's command Partha went along the wide road of the stars, known as the
Street of the Gods, praised on every side. There were the sadhyas, the
Vishvedevas, the Maruts and the two Ashvins, the Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras
and the spotless brahmin seers. There were many royal seers, kings with Dilipa
at their head, and Tumburu and Narada, and the two gandharvas Haha and Huhu. The
joy of the Kurus met them all as was proper, and then he saw the king of the
gods, of the hundred sacrifices, the tamer of enemies.

Then strong-armed Partha stepped down from the fine chariot and saw with his own
eyes the lord of the gods, his father, the chastiser of Paka. A white parasol
with a lovely golden staff was held over him, and he was fanned with a whisk
scented with divine perfume. Gandharvas led by Vishvavasu praised him with hymns
and salutations, and the best of the twice-born with the praises of the Rig, the
Yajur and the Sama. The mighty son of Kunti went up to him and bowed his head to
the ground. Indra took him in his round arms. Then Shakra took him by the hand
and sat him near him, on Shakra's own holy seat, which the gods and royal seers
honour. The lord of the gods, the killer of enemy heroes, kissed him on the head
and took him onto his lap, as he bent low in courtesy. At the command of the
thousand-eyed god, Partha of boundless spirit took his place on Shakra's seat
then, like a second Vasava.

Then, lovingly, the enemy of Vritra touched Arjuna's fair face with his fragrant
hand, comforting him. The wielder of the thunderbolt, the killer of Bala, gently
stroked his long, handsome arms, hardened by drawing the bowstring and loosing
arrows, like golden pillars. With his hand marked by the grip of the
thunderbolt, he patted them gently, again and again. The thousand-eyed killer of
Vritra gazed at Gudakesha as if smiling, his eyes wide with joy, and could not
look at him enough. Sitting on one seat, the two of them made the hall
beautiful, like the sun and the moon risen in the sky on the fourteenth day of
the fortnight.

There the gandharvas, led by Tumburu, skilled in song and in the Sama chants,
sang verses to the loveliest Sama tunes. Ghritachi, Menaka, Rambha, Purvachitti,
Svayamprabha, Urvashi, Mishrakeshi, Dundu, Gauri, Varuthini, Gopali, Sahajanya,
Kumbhayoni, Prajagara, Chitrasena, Chitralekha and sweet-voiced Saha, these and
other lovely women danced here and there. They had lotus eyes, and their
business was to trouble the minds of siddhas. With broad hips and swaying
breasts, with sidelong glances, gestures and sweetness, they stole hearts, minds
and wits.

---

Then the gods and the gandharvas, knowing Shakra's mind, took the finest guest
water and at once honoured Partha. They had the prince accept water for his feet
and water to sip, and then they led him into Purandara's palace.

Honoured so, Jishnu lived in his father's house. The Pandava studied the great
weapons, with the ways of calling them back. From Shakra's own hand he took the
thunderbolt, his beloved weapon, hard to withstand, and the thunderbolts that
roar, whose signs are clouds and peacocks. When he had the weapons, the son of
Kunti remembered his brothers. But at Purandara's command he lived there happily
for five years.

Then, when the time came, Shakra said to Partha, who had mastered the weapons,
"Son of Kunti, learn dance and song from Chitrasena. Learn the music the gods
have made, which does not exist in the world of men, son of Kunti. It will be
good for you." And Purandara gave him Chitrasena as a friend. Partha met with
him and took his pleasure there, free from all ill.

---

Once the great seer Lomasha, as he wandered, came to Shakra's palace, wishing to
see Purandara. The great sage came up and bowed to the king of the gods, and saw
the Pandava sitting on half of Vasava's seat. Then, at Shakra's leave, the best
of the twice-born sat down on a seat covered with a mat of grass, honoured by
the great seers.

When he saw Partha sitting on Indra's seat, a thought came to him. "How has
Partha, a kshatriya, come to Shakra's seat? What good deed has he done, what
worlds has he won, that he has reached a place the gods bow to?"

Shakra, the killer of Vritra, the husband of Shachi, knew his thought, and
laughing, he said this to Lomasha.

"Brahmin seer, hear what it is that you have in your mind to ask. This is no
mere mortal who has become a kshatriya. Great seer, he is my son, the
strong-armed one, born of Kunti. He has come here for weapons, for a particular
reason. Ah, you do not know him, the ancient, the best of seers. Hear me as I
tell you, brahmin, who he is and what his purpose is.

"Know that those two ancient seers, the best of seers, Nara and Narayana, are
these two, Hrishikesha and Dhananjaya. There is a holy hermitage called Badari,
famed in the world, which the gods and the great seers cannot see. There the
Ganga first flows, which the siddhas and charanas love. It was the home of
Vishnu and of Jishnu, brahmin. At my command, brahmin seer, those two splendid
ones have been born on earth. Mighty as they are, they will take away the burden
of the earth.

"For there are certain asuras, grown overbearing, called the Nivatakavachas.
Blinded by the boon they were given, they are set on harming us. Swollen with
pride in their strength, they plot to kill the gods, and they count the gods as
nothing, so great was the boon given to them. They live in Patala, terrible, the
mighty sons of Danu. All the hosts of the gods together are not able to fight
them. The splendid Vishnu, the killer of Madhu, who has come down to earth, is
the god called Kapila, the blessed, unconquered Hari. It was he who long ago,
with a single look, killed the great sons of Sagara as they were digging down to
Rasatala, lord. There is great work for us to be done by him and by Partha
together in a great war, best of brahmins, beyond doubt. This one is able to
crush them all. When the hero has killed them in battle, he will go back to men.

"Go down to the earth now, at my command. In the Kamyaka forest you will see the
hero Yudhishthira living. Tell him, from me, the righteous one, true to his
word: 'Do not pine for Phalguna. He will come soon, with the weapons mastered.
Bhishma, Drona and the rest cannot be met in battle by one whose strength of arm
is untried, or who has not mastered the weapons. Gudakesha, strong-armed and
high-minded, has taken the weapons, and he has gone to the far shore of divine
dance, music and song. You too, lord of men, tamer of enemies, should see the
lonely holy fords, with all your brothers. When you have bathed in the holy
fords, free from sin and free from fever, you will enjoy your kingdom in
happiness, king of kings, cleansed of all stain.'

"And you, best of brahmins, with the strength of your austerity, should guard
him as he wanders the earth, first of the twice-born. For in the mountain
fastnesses and the rough country fierce rakshasas always live. You must always
protect him from them."

"So be it," promised Lomasha, the great ascetic, and he went down to the earth,
making for the Kamyaka forest. There he saw the son of Kunti, the Dharma King,
the tamer of enemies, with his brothers and the ascetics all around him.

**Divergences.**

* **Urvashi's curse.** Arjuna's meeting with Urvashi, her advances and her curse that he
  become a eunuch, follow here in the vulgate. It is App. I No. 6 (after 3.45.8) and not in
  the CE. See L-25.
* **Arjuna's unrest in heaven.** A rejected passage (\*192, after 3.45.8) says he could find no
  peace, remembering the dicing and his anger against Duhshasana and Shakuni. Not in the CE.
* **Kubera's weapon.** The vulgate adds that it was the weapon with which Shiva burnt Tripura
  (\*187). Not in the CE; in the CE Kubera's gift is the Vanishing (3.42.33).
* **Yama speaks first.** In the CE it is Yama who speaks first and names Arjuna as Nara and Karna
  as his to kill (3.42.16–23). Yama calls the Sun "my father" (3.42.20); Yama is the son of
  Vivasvat (3.42.10).

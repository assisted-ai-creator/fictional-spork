summary: The Pandavas and Draupadi miss Arjuna in Kamyaka and each speaks of him; Narada comes, and when Yudhishthira asks the merit of going round the earth to its holy fords, he retells what the seer Pulastya once told Bhishma at Gangadvara: who wins the fruit of the fords, why the fords are open even to the poor, and the first stages of the pilgrimage, from Pushkara round the west to the Sarasvati.

# The Merit of the Fords

"Blessed one," said Janamejaya, "when Partha, my great-grandfather, had gone
from Kamyaka, what did the Pandavas do without Savyasachin? He was their refuge,
the great archer, the conqueror of armies. He seems to me to have been to them
what Vishnu is to the Adityas. How did my heroic great-grandfathers live in the
forest without him, equal to Indra in courage, who never turned back in battle?"

When the Pandava Savyasachin had gone from Kamyaka, my son, the Kauravas were
lost in sorrow and grief. The Pandavas were all unhappy, like jewels fallen from
their string, like birds with their wings cut. Without him, whose deeds were
never strained, the forest was like the Chaitraratha forest without Kubera.
Without that tiger among men, Janamejaya, the Pandavas lived in Kamyaka and
found no joy. For the brahmins' sake the great chariot fighters, bold, killed
with clean arrows the many kinds of beasts fit for sacrifice. Every day those
tigers among men, tamers of enemies, went out and gathered forest food and gave
it to the brahmins. So those bulls among men lived there, longing, all of them
joyless, king, now that Dhananjaya was gone.

Then the Panchali, remembering her middle husband, the hero far away, the best
of the Pandavas, said this.

"That Arjuna who, with two arms, was the equal of Arjuna of the thousand arms,
without that best of the Pandavas the forest does not please me. I see this
earth as if empty wherever I look. This wonderful forest, with its flowering
trees, is not so lovely to me without Savyasachin. He is like a dark blue cloud,
he walks like a maddened elephant. Without the lotus-eyed one Kamyaka does not
shine for me. I remember Savyasachin, king, whose bow sounded like the
thunderbolt, and I find no peace."

When he heard her lamenting like this, great king, Bhimasena, killer of enemy
heroes, said to Draupadi, "What you say, good lady, slim-waisted one, gladdens
my heart, like a taste of nectar. His arms are long and even and thick, like
iron bars, round, calloused by the bowstring, bearing sword and weapon and mace.
Adorned with gold armlets, they are like five-headed snakes. Without that tiger
among men this forest is as if the sun had gone. Relying on that strong-armed
one, the Panchalas and the Kurus do not fear even the armies of the gods in
arms. Relying on his arms, all of us great-hearted men think our enemies beaten
in battle and the earth won. Without the hero Phalguna I find no steadiness in
Kamyaka. I see this earth as if empty wherever I look."

"He went to the north, beat the chief gandharvas in battle in their hundreds,
and won horses," said Nakula. "The son of Vasava, the splendid, gave his
brother, with love, horses speckled like partridges and swift as the wind, at
the great royal consecration, king. Without him, the younger brother of Bhima,
with his terrible bow, like an immortal, I have no wish to live in the Kamyaka
forest now."

"He won wealth and maidens from great chariot fighters in battle," said
Sahadeva, "and brought them to the king long ago, at the great royal
consecration. Alone, of boundless splendour, he beat the gathered Yadavas in
battle and carried off Subhadra, with Vasudeva's consent. When I see Jishnu's
mat empty in our dwelling, great king, my heart is never at peace. I would like
us to leave this forest, tamer of enemies. Without that hero this forest is not
lovely to us."

---

Longing for Dhananjaya, the great chariot fighters, the blessed Pandavas, lived
in that forest with Draupadi. Then they saw the great divine seer Narada there,
blazing with the splendour of brahman, bright as a blazing fire. Surrounded by
his brothers, the splendid best of the Kurus shone, full of blazing power, like
Shatakratu with the gods. And as Savitri does not leave the Vedas, as the sun's
light does not leave Meru, so the good Yajnaseni, by dharma, did not leave the
Parthas.

When the blessed seer Narada had received their honour, he comforted the son of
Dharma fittingly, sinless one. Then he said to the great Dharma King
Yudhishthira, "Tell me, best of those who uphold dharma, what you need. What
shall I give you?"

Then the king, the son of Dharma, bowed with his brothers and joined his hands.
He said to Narada, who was like a god, "When you are pleased, blessed one, whom
all the worlds honour, I think everything done, by your grace, you of good vows.
But if I and my brothers are to be favoured, sinless one, best of sages, you
should cut through a doubt that is in my heart. The man who goes round the
earth, devoted to the holy fords, what is his reward, in full? Tell me,
brahmin."

"Listen closely, king, Bharata," said Narada, "to how Bhishma heard all this
from Pulastya.

"Long ago, on the bank of the Bhagirathi, Bhishma, the best of those who uphold
dharma, kept the vow of the Fathers and lived like a sage, great king. He lived
in a lovely holy place at Gangadvara, where divine seers, gods and gandharvas
live. The splendid man satisfied the Fathers and the gods, and pleased the
seers, with the rites the rules prescribe. Then, after some time, as the great
ascetic was praying, he saw Pulastya, the best of seers, wonderful to see. When
he saw him, fierce in austerity, as if blazing with splendour, he felt a joy
beyond measure and was filled with wonder. Bhishma, the best of those who uphold
dharma, honoured him as he stood there, great king, Bharata, with the rites the
rules prescribe. Pure and with his mind controlled, he held the guest water to
his head and spoke his name to that best of brahmin seers. 'I am Bhishma, bless
you. I am your servant, you of good vows. By the mere sight of you I am freed
from all my sins.' When he had said this, great king, Bhishma, the best of those
who uphold dharma, held his tongue, joined his hands, and was silent,
Yudhishthira.

"When the sage saw Bhishma, the best of the Kuru line, worn thin by his
discipline, his study and his recitation, he was pleased at heart. 'I am wholly
pleased with you, you who know dharma,' said Pulastya, 'with your courtesy, your
self-control and your truth, blessed one. Because your dharma is like this,
resting on devotion to your fathers, sinless one, you see me, my son, and I love
you. I am one whose sight is never fruitless, Bhishma. Tell me what I can do for
you. Whatever you ask, best of the Kurus, I will give you, sinless one.'

"'When you are pleased, blessed one, whom all the worlds honour,' said Bhishma,
'I think everything done, since I have seen the lord. But if I am to be
favoured, best of those who uphold dharma, I will tell you a doubt in my heart,
and you should answer it. I have a doubt about dharma, blessed one, concerning
the holy fords. I want to hear it from you, told one by one. The man who goes
round the earth, you of boundless courage, what is his reward, brahmin seer?
Tell me, you who are rich in austerity.'

"'Well, then, I will tell you what is the last refuge of the seers,' said
Pulastya. 'Listen with a single mind, my son, to the reward there is in the holy
fords.

"'The man whose hands and feet and mind are well controlled, who has learning,
austerity and good name, wins the reward of the fords. The man who turns away
from taking gifts, who is content, restrained and pure, and free of pride, wins
the reward of the fords. The man without deceit, who begins no ventures, who
eats little and has mastered his senses, free of every fault, wins the reward of
the fords. The man who is free of anger, king of kings, true in his character,
firm in his vows, who sees himself in all creatures, wins the reward of the
fords.

"'The seers have taught the sacrifices in the Vedas, in their order, and their
reward, truly, after death and here, in full. But a poor man cannot reach those
sacrifices, lord of the earth. Sacrifices need many things, with every kind of
preparation. They are within reach of kings, or of rich men here and there, not
of those short of wealth and means, alone, without helpers. Learn, best of
fighters, the way that even the poor can reach, lord of men, holy and equal to
the rewards of sacrifice. This is the highest secret of the seers, best of the
Bharatas: going to the holy fords is holy, and it is better even than
sacrifices. A man who has not fasted three nights, who has not gone to the holy
fords, who has not given gold and cows, is born poor. A man who has sacrificed
with the agnishtoma and other sacrifices, with rich fees, does not win the
reward that he wins by going to the holy fords.

---

"'In the world of men there is a ford of the god of gods, famous in the three
worlds, called Pushkara. A blessed man should enter it. Ten thousand million
holy fords are present at Pushkara at the three twilights, joy of the Kurus. The
Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the sadhyas with the hosts of the Maruts, the
gandharvas and apsarases are always present there, lord. There gods, daityas and
brahmin seers practised austerity and won divine powers, great king, and great
merit. Even the wise man who only longs in his mind for Pushkara has all his
sins cleansed, and he is honoured on the vault of heaven. In that ford, blessed
one, the Grandsire always lived, full of joy, honoured by gods and danavas. At
Pushkara, blessed one, the gods with the seers at their head won perfection and
great merit. The wise say that the man who bathes there, devoted to worship of
the Fathers and the gods, wins ten times the horse sacrifice. Even if he feeds a
single brahmin, living in the Pushkara forest, Bhishma, by that deed he rejoices
after death and here. Whatever vegetables, roots or fruits a man lives on
himself, if he gives them to a brahmin, with faith and without envy, by that
alone the wise man wins the reward of the horse sacrifice.

"'Brahmin, kshatriya, vaishya or shudra, best of kings, these great-hearted
ones, when they have bathed in the ford, do not go to a lower birth. The man who
goes to Pushkara above all at the full moon of Karttika, his reward there grows
without end, bull of the Bharatas. The man who remembers Pushkara at evening and
at dawn, with joined hands, has bathed in all the holy fords, Bharata, and wins
the imperishable worlds in Brahma's house. Whatever sin a woman or a man has
done since birth is all destroyed as soon as they bathe at Pushkara. As
Madhusudana is the first of all the gods, so Pushkara is called the first of the
fords, king. The man who lives at Pushkara for twelve years, restrained and
pure, wins all the sacrifices and goes to Brahma's world. The man who keeps the
fire offering for a full hundred years and the man who lives one Karttika at
Pushkara are equal. It is hard to go to Pushkara, hard to practise austerity at
Pushkara, hard to give gifts at Pushkara, and very hard to live there.

"'When he has stayed twelve nights, restrained and sparing in his food, and gone
round it keeping it on his right, a man should go to Jambumarga. When he enters
Jambumarga, where divine seers and Fathers go, he wins the horse sacrifice and
goes to Vishnu's world. The man who stays there five nights, eating only at
every sixth meal time, does not come to a bad end and wins the highest
perfection. Coming back from Jambumarga, he should go to the hermitage of
Tandulika. He does not come to a bad end, and he is honoured in the world of
heaven. At Agastya's lake, devoted to worship of the Fathers and the gods,
fasting three nights, king, he wins the reward of the agnishtoma. Living on
vegetables or fruit there, he reaches the state of Kumara.

"'He comes to Kanva's hermitage, full of fortune, honoured by the world. That is
the holy Dharmaranya, the first forest, bull of the Bharatas. As soon as he
enters it, a man is freed from his sins. Restrained and sparing in his food,
when he has worshipped the Fathers and the gods, he wins the reward of a
sacrifice rich in every desire. Then, going round it, he should go to
Yayatipatana, the place of Yayati's fall. There he wins the reward of the horse
sacrifice. Then he should go to Mahakala, restrained and sparing in his food.
Touching the water at Kotitirtha, he wins the reward of the horse sacrifice.
Then, you who know dharma, he should go to the holy place of Uma's husband,
called Bhadravata, famous in the three worlds. When he goes there to the Lord,
he wins the reward of a thousand cows, and by the grace of Mahadeva he wins the
lordship of his hosts.

"'Coming to the Narmada, the river famous in the three worlds, and offering
water to the Fathers and the gods, he wins the reward of the agnishtoma. Coming
to the southern Sindhu, chaste and master of his senses, he wins the agnishtoma
and rides in a chariot of the air. Coming to the Charmanvati, restrained and
sparing in his food, with Rantideva's leave, he wins the reward of the
agnishtoma.

"'Then, you who know dharma, he should go to Arbuda, the son of Himavat, where
there was once a cleft in the earth, Yudhishthira. There is Vasishtha's
hermitage, famous in the three worlds. Staying there one night, he wins the
reward of a thousand cows. Touching the water at Pingatirtha, chaste and master
of his senses, he wins the reward of a hundred tawny cows, tiger among men.

"'Then, you who know dharma, he should go to Prabhasa, famous in the world,
where Fire himself is always present, the mouth of the gods, hero, whose
charioteer is the Wind. Bathing in that finest of fords, pure and with his mind
controlled, a man wins the reward of the agnishtoma and the atiratra. Then going
to where the Sarasvati meets the sea, he wins the reward of a thousand cows and
is honoured in the world of heaven, always blazing with light like fire, bull of
the Bharatas. Staying there three nights, he should offer water to the Fathers
and the gods. He shines like the moon and wins the horse sacrifice.

"'Then, best of the Bharatas, he should go to the ford of Varadana, where
Durvasas gave Vishnu a boon, Yudhishthira. Bathing at Varadana, a man wins the
reward of a thousand cows. Then he should go to Dvaravati, restrained and
sparing in his food. Bathing at Pindaraka, a man wins much gold. At that ford,
blessed one, seals marked with the sign of the lotus are still seen today. That
is a wonder, tamer of enemies. Lotuses marked with the trident are seen there,
joy of the Kurus, and Mahadeva is present there, bull of the Bharatas.

"'He should come to where the sea meets the Sindhu, Bharata, and bathe with a
controlled mind in the ford of the king of the waters. Offering water there to
the Fathers, the gods and the seers, he reaches Varuna's world, blazing with his
own splendour. The wise say that worshipping the god Shankukarneshvara is worth
ten times the horse sacrifice, Yudhishthira. Going round it, best of the Kurus,
bull of the Bharatas, he should go to the ford famous in the three worlds, known
by the name of Drimi, which frees a man from every sin. There the gods, with
Brahma at their head, worship Maheshvara. Bathing there and worshipping Rudra,
surrounded by the hosts of gods, a man drives away the sins he has done since
birth. And Drimi is praised there by all the gods, best of men. Bathing there,
tiger among men, he wins the horse sacrifice. There long ago, wise one, the
mighty Vishnu, when he had conquered and killed the thorns in the gods' side,
cleansed himself, king.

"'Then, you who know dharma, he should go to the praised Vasordhara. Merely by
going there he wins the horse sacrifice. Bathing there, best of the Kurus, with
a controlled mind, and offering water to the gods and the Fathers, a man is
honoured in Vishnu's world. And there is a most holy ford of the Vasus there,
bull of the Bharatas. Bathing there and drinking, he becomes dear to the Vasus.
The ford called Sindhuttama destroys every sin. Bathing there, best of men, he
wins much gold. Coming to Brahmatunga, pure and with a controlled mind, the good
man, free of passion, reaches Brahma's world. The ford of Shakra's maidens,
where siddhas go: bathing there, a man quickly reaches Shakra's world. The ford
of Renuka is there too, where the gods go. Bathing there, a brahmin becomes
spotless as the moon.

"'Then going to Panchanada, restrained and sparing in his food, he wins the five
sacrifices, in the order in which they are taught. Then, you who know dharma, he
should go to the fine place of the goddess Bhima. Bathing there in the yoni,
best of the Bharatas, a man becomes the goddess's son, king, with a body bright
with earrings of burnished gold, and wins the great reward of a hundred thousand
cows. Coming to Girimunja, famous in the three worlds, and bowing to the
Grandsire, he wins the reward of a thousand cows. Then, you who know dharma, he
should go to the fine ford of Vimala, where fish of gold and silver are still
seen today. Bathing there, best of men, he wins the vajapeya, and with his whole
self cleansed of sin he reaches the highest goal.

"'Then he should go to Malada, famous in the three worlds. Touching the water at
the western twilight as the rules require, he should offer a pot of rice to the
seven-flamed fire, as he is able, lord of men. The wise call it an imperishable
gift to the Fathers. The rice offering to the seven-flamed fire is better than a
hundred thousand cows, a hundred royal consecrations and a thousand horse
sacrifices. Turning back from there, king of kings, he should go to Vastrapada.
Going to Mahadeva there, he wins the reward of the horse sacrifice. Coming to
Manimant, chaste and with a gathered mind, staying one night, king, he wins the
reward of the agnishtoma.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the Devika, famous in the world, where,
we hear, the brahmins were born, bull of the Bharatas. The place of the bearer
of the trident is there, famous in the three worlds. Bathing in the Devika and
worshipping Maheshvara, and offering a pot of rice there as he is able, bull of
the Bharatas, he wins the reward of a sacrifice rich in every desire. Rudra's
ford called Kama is there, where divine seers go. Bathing there, a man quickly
wins perfection, Bharata. Going to Yajana and Yajana, and to Brahmavaluka, and
touching the water at Pushpanyasa, he need not grieve over death. They say that
the holy Devika, where the divine seers go, is half a league wide and five
leagues long.

"'Then, you who know dharma, he should go in due order to Dirghasatra. There the
gods with Brahma at their head, the siddhas and the great seers, firm in their
vows, attend the long sacrificial session with rich fees. Merely by going to
Dirghasatra, king of kings, tamer of enemies, a man wins the reward of the royal
consecration and the horse sacrifice. Then he should go to Vinashana, restrained
and sparing in his food, where the Sarasvati goes underground in the desert and
appears again at Chamasa, Shivodbheda and Nagodbheda. Bathing at Chamasodbheda
he wins the reward of the agnishtoma. Bathing at Shivodbheda, a man wins the
reward of a thousand cows. Bathing at Nagodbheda, a man reaches the world of the
snakes.

"'Then he comes to the rare ford of Shashayana, king of kings. There the
Pushkaras, hidden in the form of hares, bathe in the Sarasvati every year, best
of the Bharatas, always at the full moon of Karttika. Bathing there, tiger among
men, a man always shines like the moon, and wins the reward of a thousand cows,
bull of the Bharatas. Coming to Kumarakoti, restrained, joy of the Kurus, he
should bathe there, devoted to worship of the Fathers and the gods. He wins the
gavamayana and raises up his family.

"'Then, you who know dharma, he should go to Rudrakoti with a gathered mind.
Long ago, great king, ten million seers gathered there, and sat down in joy,
longing to see the god. "I will see the god whose banner is a bull first! I
first!" So the seers set out, they say, king, Bharata. Then the lord of yoga,
taking to yoga, lord of the earth, to put an end to the anger of those seers of
perfected spirit, created ten million Rudras, who stood before the seers. Each
of them thought, "I saw him first." Mahadeva was pleased with the highest
devotion of those seers of fierce power, king, and gave them a boon: "From today
your dharma will grow." Bathing at Rudrakoti, a pure man, tiger among men, wins
the horse sacrifice and raises up his family.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the famous confluence of the Sarasvati,
most holy, where they worship Janardana. There the gods with Brahma at their
head, the seers, the siddhas and the charanas come, king of kings, on the
fourteenth day of the bright half of Chaitra. Bathing there, tiger among men, he
gains much gold, and with his whole self cleansed of sin he goes to Brahma's
world. Coming to Satravasana, where the seers' sessions of sacrifice ended, lord
of men, he wins the reward of a thousand cows.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Narada's pilgrimage.** A rejected passage (\*368, before 3.79) opens with Narada on a pilgrimage
  of the fords. In the CE he simply comes to the Pandavas at Kamyaka (3.80.2).
* **Arjuna's gift to Drona.** A rejected line (\*373) has Sahadeva recall that Arjuna won half of
  Drupada's kingdom for Drona. Not here in the CE.
* **Kashmir.** A rejected passage (\*387, after 3.80.104) adds the Vitasta and the dwelling of the
  snake Takshaka in Kashmir. Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* The fords' merits are kept one by one, as Pulastya gives them, in plain words: "the reward of a
  thousand cows" for *gosahasraphalam*, and so on. The sacrifices keep their names: the
  *agniṣṭoma*, the *atirātra*, the *vājapeya*, the *gavāmayana*, the royal consecration
  (*rājasūya*) and the horse sacrifice (*aśvamedha*).
* 3.79.5 *ākṣiptasūtrā maṇayaḥ*: "like jewels fallen from their string".
* 3.79.12 *arjunena … bahubāhunā*: "Arjuna of the thousand arms", Kartavirya Arjuna.
* 3.79.28 *bṛsīm*: Arjuna's "mat", the seat of grass he used.
* 3.80.4: Draupadi as Savitri to the Vedas is the narrator's comparison.
* 3.80.61 *ṣaṣṭhakālakṣamī*: "eating only at every sixth meal time".
* 3.80.64 *kaumāraṃ padam*: "the state of Kumara"; the sense is uncertain.
* 3.80.67 *yayātipatanam*: Yayatipatana, "the place of Yayati's fall".
* 3.80.70 *gāṇapatyam*: "the lordship of his hosts".

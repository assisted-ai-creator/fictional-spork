# Kunti's Boon

"What was the secret that the hot-rayed one did not tell Karna here?" said
Janamejaya. "What were those earrings like, and what was the armour like? And
where did his armour and earrings come from, good man? I want to hear this. Tell
me, you who are rich in austerity."

I shall tell you, king, what that secret of the shining one was, and what the
earrings and the armour were like.

Long ago, king, a brahmin came to Kuntibhoja, fierce in his energy, very tall,
with a beard, a staff and matted hair. He was handsome, faultless in every limb,
and seemed to blaze with his splendour, tawny as honey, sweet-spoken, adorned
with austerity and study. That great ascetic said to King Kuntibhoja, "I want to
eat alms in your house, you who are free of envy. You and your people must never
do anything that displeases me. On these terms I shall live in your house, if it
pleases you, sinless one. I shall go and come as I please, king, and no one must
wrong me in my bed or my seat."

Kuntibhoja said to him, gladly, "So be it, and more." And again he said to him,
"I have a daughter, great brahmin, the glorious Pritha. She is good, devoted and
restrained, full of good conduct and without pride. She will wait on you,
honouring you and never slighting you, and you will be pleased by her conduct
and character."

When he had said this to the brahmin and honoured him as the rules require, he
went to his daughter, wide-eyed Pritha, and said, "My child, this greatly
blessed brahmin wants to live in my house, and I have promised him, 'So be it.'
I trust you, my child, to please the brahmin. You must never make my word false.
This lord is an ascetic, a brahmin given to study. Whatever that one of great
energy asks, you must give him without grudging. For brahmins are the highest
energy, and brahmins are the highest austerity. It is by the homage of brahmins
that the sun shines in the sky. The great asura Vatapi, and Talajangha too, were
struck down by the brahmin's staff for failing to honour those who deserved
honour. Now this great burden is laid on you, my child. You must always, with
restraint, please the brahmin.

"I know your devotion, daughter, since your childhood, to all the brahmins here,
and to your elders and kin, and to all the servants, friends, relations and
mothers. And you behave as you should to me, respecting everything. There is no
one here, in the city or the women's quarters, even among your servants, who is
not pleased with your good conduct, faultless one. But I think I must instruct
you about this quick-tempered brahmin, Pritha, since you are a girl and my
daughter. You were born in the family of the Vrishnis, Shura's beloved daughter,
given to me by your loving father himself long ago when you were a child. You
are Vasudeva's sister, the first of my children. He promised me his firstborn
long before, and so you are my daughter. Born in such a family and raised in
such a family, you have come from happiness to happiness, like one who goes from
lake to lake.

"Women of low family especially, when they are somehow taken in hand, mostly go
astray out of childishness, lovely one. Pritha, you have birth in a royal family
and wonderful beauty, and you have all these things, lady. So give up pride,
deceit and vanity, and please the brahmin who gives boons, and you will be
joined to good fortune, Pritha. So, lovely, sinless one, you will surely win
good fortune. But if the best of brahmins is angered, my whole family will be
burned."

---

"I shall wait on the brahmin with honour, king, keeping myself in check, as you
have promised," said Kunti. "I do not speak falsely, king of kings. It is my
nature to honour brahmins. And I must do what pleases you, and this is my
highest good. Whether the lord comes in the evening, or in the morning, or at
night, or at midnight, he will not make me angry. It is a gain for me, king of
kings, to honour brahmins and to do good by keeping to your command, best of
men. Trust me, king of kings. The best of brahmins will meet with nothing he
dislikes while he lives in your house. I tell you the truth. I shall try to do
what pleases this brahmin and what is good for you, sinless one. Let the fever
of your mind pass, king.

"For brahmins, greatly blessed, when honoured, lord of the earth, can save, and
when it is the other way, they can destroy. Knowing this, I shall please the
best of brahmins. You will meet no pain from the best of brahmins through me,
king. For when kings do wrong, brahmins bring them harm, as Chyavana did long
ago over Sukanya. I shall wait on the best of brahmins with the greatest
discipline, as you have told me, lord of men."

"You must do this, good woman, without hesitating," said the king, "for my good,
for the family's, and for your own, daughter."

When he had said this to his daughter, glorious Kuntibhoja, who loved his child,
gave Pritha into the brahmin's charge. "This daughter of mine, brahmin, is a
young girl, raised in comfort. If she does anything wrong, you must not take it
to heart. Greatly blessed brahmins are mostly without anger towards the old, the
young and ascetics, even when they are crossed. Even for a very great offence,
brahmins should show patience. Accept her honour as far as she is able, and as
far as she has the will, best of brahmins."

When the brahmin said, "So be it," the king was glad at heart and gave him a
house like a goose or a moonbeam. There in the fire-room a shining seat was made
ready for him, and the king provided food and everything else in the same way.
The king's daughter put away laziness and pride and made the greatest effort to
please the brahmin. Going to the brahmin, Pritha, devoted to purity, good woman,
pleased him as was right, as if he were a god who deserved service.

---

The girl pleased the brahmin of strict vows with a pure mind, king, keeping
strict vows herself. Sometimes the best of brahmins would say, "I shall come in
the morning," and then come in the evening, or at night. At all hours the girl
honoured him with food to eat and to chew and with lodging, always more and
more. His food and his bed and seat were attended to better day by day, never
worse. Even with his threats, his reproaches and his unkind words, Pritha did
nothing to displease the brahmin, king. Often the brahmin came back at odd
times, or did not come at all, and said, "Let food be given me," even food that
was hard to get. Pritha told him it was all done, like a pupil, like a son, like
a sister, restrained. The faultless girl pleased the best of brahmins as he
wished, by her efforts. The best of brahmins was pleased with her conduct and
character, and she made an even greater effort in her care of him.

Morning and evening her father asked her, Bharata, "Is the brahmin pleased with
your service, daughter?" The glorious girl answered him, "Very much so." Then
great-minded Kuntibhoja was filled with the greatest joy.

When a year was complete and that best of those who pray, who delighted in her
kindness, had seen no fault in Pritha, the brahmin was pleased at heart and said
to her, "I am highly pleased with your service, good and lovely one. Choose
boons, fair one, hard for men to win here, by which you will surpass all women
in fame."

"Everything is done for me, best of those who know the Veda," said Kunti, "since
you and my father are pleased. The boons are done for me, brahmin."

"If you do not want a boon from me, good woman of the bright smile," said the
brahmin, "take this spell for calling the dwellers in heaven. Whatever god you
call with this spell will come into your power, good woman. Willing or
unwilling, he will come into your power, the god calmed by the spell, bowing to
your word like a servant."

The faultless girl could not refuse the best of brahmins a second time, king,
for fear of his curse. Then the brahmin taught the faultless one that set of
spells, heard in the Atharvashiras, king. When he had given it, king of kings,
he said to Kuntibhoja, "I have lived happily, king, pleased by the girl, well
cared for in your house and always well honoured. Now I shall go." And when he
had said this, he vanished. When the king saw the brahmin vanish right there, he
was filled with wonder, and honoured Pritha.

---

When that best of brahmins had gone, after some time the girl thought about the
power of the set of spells. "What kind of set of spells is this that the great
one has given me? I shall know its power before long." As she was thinking this,
by chance she saw her season. The young girl was ashamed at being in her season
while still a maiden.

Then Pritha saw the thousand-rayed sun rising, blazing, and she could not see
enough of the beauty of the sun at dawn. Her sight became divine, and she saw
the god divine to look at, wearing armour and adorned with earrings. She was
curious about the spell, king, and the lovely girl made the call to that god.
She touched water and called the maker of day. Then the maker of day came
quickly, king, tawny as honey, strong-armed, with a neck like a conch, as if
smiling, wearing armlets and a crown, as if setting the directions alight. By
yoga he made himself two, and came, and went on shining too.

He spoke to Kunti with the gentlest courtesy. "I have come into your power, good
woman, drawn by the force of the spell. What shall I do, in your power, queen?
Tell me, and I shall do it."

"Go back, lord, where you came from," said Kunti. "I called you out of
curiosity. Be gracious, lord."

"I shall go, as you tell me, slender one," said the Sun. "But it is not right to
call a god and send him away for nothing. Your intention, fortunate one, was
that a son should be born to you from the Sun, unmatched in valour in the world,
with armour and earrings. So give yourself to me, you who walk like an elephant.
A son will be born to you as you intended, lady. If I go without having been
with you, lovely-smiling one, I shall curse you in my anger, and the brahmin,
and your father. Because of you I shall burn them all, no doubt of it, and your
foolish father, who does not know your misconduct. And on that brahmin who gave
you the spell without knowing your character and conduct, I shall inflict the
greatest punishment today. For all the gods in heaven, led by Purandara, see
that you have tricked me, lady, and seem to smile. Look at these hosts of the
gods, for you have this divine eye, which I gave you before, and with which you
saw me."

Then the king's daughter saw all the thirty gods, each in his own place in the
sky, shining, and the great radiant one, bright as the sun itself. When she saw
them, the young girl, as if ashamed, spoke to the Sun in fear. "Go back to your
flying chariot, lord of rays. Because I am a maiden, this is a grief to me. My
father, my mother and my elders, and the others who have the right to give this
body away, I will not destroy dharma in the world. Guarding the body is honoured
as the conduct of women. I called you to learn the power of the spell, shining
one. Forgive it, lord, as the act of a child."

"Because you are a child, I grant you my kindness," said the Sun. "Another would
not get it. Give yourself, daughter of Kunti, for so you will have peace, timid
one. It is not right for me to go, called by the spell and made a thing of
falsehood. I would go, faultless one, a laughing-stock in the world, and be
talked of by all the gods, lovely one. So come to me, and you will have a son
like me, and you will be the most honoured in all the worlds, lady."

---

Though she spoke many sweet words, the high-minded girl could not win over the
thousand-rayed one. When the young girl could not refuse the dispeller of
darkness, she was afraid of the curse, king, and thought for a long time. "How
can my innocent father, and the brahmin too, not be cursed because of me by this
angry shining one? Even with good intentions, one should not in folly provoke
energy and austerity too far, even with things that can be taken back. Now I am
very frightened, and held fast by the hand. How can I do the wrong thing, and
give myself away?"

So, terrified of the curse, thinking a great deal, her limbs overcome with
confusion, smiling again and again, she spoke to the god in fear for her kin,
king of kings, in a voice shaken with shame, trembling at the curse. "My father
is alive, god, and my mother and my other kin. While they are alive, this breach
of the rules must not happen. If I come together with you, god, outside the
rules, then because of me the fame of this family would be destroyed in the
world. Or if you think this is dharma, best of those who shine, I shall do what
you wish without being given by my kin. When I have given myself to you,
unassailable one, I shall still be a good woman. For in you are the dharma, the
glory, the fame and the life of embodied beings."

"Not your father, nor your mother, nor your elders have power over you,
lovely-hipped one," said the Sun. "Bless you. Listen to what I say. Since she
desires all, from the root *kam*, lady, a maiden, *kanya*, is independent here,
lovely-hipped one. You will have done no wrong at all, lady. How could I, who
wish the good of the world, do what is against dharma? All women and men are
unrestrained, lovely one. That is the nature of creatures. Anything else is held
to be a change from nature. When you have been with me, you will be a maiden
again, and your son will be strong-armed and very glorious."

"If I have a son by you, dispeller of all darkness," said Kunti, "may he have
earrings and armour, and be brave, strong-armed and very mighty."

"He will be strong-armed, lady, with earrings and divine armour," said the Sun,
"and both will be made of amrita."

"If the earrings and the fine armour of my son, whom you will beget in me, are
from the amrita," said Kunti, "then let me come together with you, god, as you
have said, lord. And may he be like you in valour, beauty, spirit and energy,
and joined to dharma."

"Aditi gave me these earrings, proud queen," said the Sun. "I shall give them to
him, timid one, and this fine armour too."

"Very well, lord god," said Pritha. "I shall come together with you, if my son
will be as you say, lord of rays."

"So be it," said the rover of the sky, the enemy of Svarbhanu, whose self is
yoga, and entered Kunti, and touched her at the navel. Then the girl was as if
overcome by the Sun's energy, and the queen fell on her bed, her mind dazed.

"I shall accomplish it, lovely-hipped one," said the Sun. "You will bear a son,
the best of all who bear weapons, and you will remain a maiden."

Then, ashamed, the young girl said to the Sun as he was going, the one of great
splendour, "So be it," king of kings. When she had said this, the daughter of
the king of the Kuntis, begging Vivasvat, ashamed, fell on that holy bed,
overcome with confusion, like a creeper breaking. The hot-rayed one dazed her
with his energy, entered her by yoga and set himself within her. But the Sun did
not defile her, and the girl came to herself again.

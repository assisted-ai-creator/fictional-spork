# Krishna in the Forest

When they heard that the Pandavas had gone into exile, the Bhojas, the Vrishnis
and the Andhakas came to them in the great forest, burning with grief. So did
the heirs of the king of Panchala, and Dhrishtaketu, lord of the Chedis, and the
Kekaya brothers, men of great courage, famous throughout the world. They came to
the Parthas in the forest, full of anger and resentment, cursing the sons of
Dhritarashtra and saying, "What shall we do?" With Vasudeva at their head, all
those bulls of the kshatriyas sat down around Yudhishthira, the Dharma King.

"The earth will drink the blood of Duryodhana, of Karna, of wicked Shakuni, and
of Duhshasana, the fourth," said Vasudeva. "Then we will all consecrate
Yudhishthira, the Dharma King. A man who acts by trickery deserves to be killed.
That is the eternal dharma."

Janardana was so angry at the wrong done to the Parthas that he seemed ready to
burn up all creatures, and Arjuna calmed him. When he saw Keshava so angry,
Phalguna recounted the deeds Krishna had done in his former bodies. He was the
great one of true fame, the boundless Person, the true one of unmeasured
splendour, Vishnu, lord of the lord of creatures, the wise lord of the world.

"Long ago, Krishna, you wandered on Mount Gandhamadana for ten thousand years, a
sage who made his home wherever evening found him. For ten thousand years and
ten hundred more, Krishna, you lived at Pushkara, living on water. At broad
Badari, Madhusudana, you stood on one foot with your arms raised for a hundred
years, living on air. At the twelve-year sacrifice on the Sarasvati, Krishna,
you sat with your upper garment thrown off, thin, your veins standing out. Then,
Krishna, you went to Prabhasa, the holy ford beloved of the pious, and stood in
austerity for a thousand heavenly years on one foot, keeping your vow, you of
great splendour. You are the knower of the field in all beings, the beginning
and the end, Keshava. You are the treasure-house of austerity, Krishna, and the
eternal sacrifice.

"You killed Naraka, son of the earth, brought back the jewelled earrings, and
let loose the first-born sacrificial horse, Krishna. When you had done that
deed, bull of the worlds, conqueror of all the worlds, you killed in battle all
the daityas and danavas who had gathered. Then you gave the lordship of all
things to Shachi's husband, strong-armed Keshava, and were born among men. You
became Narayana, you were Hari, scourge of enemies. You are Brahma, Soma and
Surya, Dharma, the Placer, Yama and Fire, Wind, Vaishravana and Rudra, Time,
space, earth and the quarters. You are the Unborn, the teacher of all that moves
and does not move, the creator, highest of beings.

"In the Chaitraratha forest, god, you sacrificed with the Turayana and other
rites with rich fees, Krishna, you of great splendour. At each of those
sacrifices, Janardana, you paid out in full a hundred times a hundred thousand
pieces of gold. You became the son of Aditi too, joy of the Yadavas, and were
known on earth as Vishnu, Indra's younger brother. As a child, Krishna, scourge
of enemies, you strode across heaven, sky and earth by your power in three
strides. You reached heaven and the sky, and stood in the house of the sun, and
outshone the sun with your own splendour, soul of beings.

"You cut the nooses of Muru, you killed Nisunda and Naraka, and made the road to
the city of Pragjyotisha safe again. At Jaruthi you beat Ahuti and Kratha,
Shishupala with his people, Bhimasena, Shaibya and Shatadhanvan. With a chariot
that thundered like the rain cloud and shone like the sun, you carried off your
queen, the daughter of the Bhoja, after beating Rukmin in battle. In your anger
you killed Indradyumna and the Yavana Kasheruman. You killed Shalva, lord of
Saubha, and brought Saubha down. On the Iravati you killed the Bhoja, a match
for Kartavirya in battle, and both Gopati and Talaketu. And you will take the
holy city of Dvaraka, rich in pleasures and loved by the sages, into yourself
and let the sea have it, Janardana.

"There is no anger in you, Madhusudana, no envy, no falsehood, Dasharha, no
cruelty. How could there be crookedness? All the seers came to you, Achyuta, as
you sat in the midst of the mind, blazing with your own splendour, and begged
you for safety. At the end of the age, Madhusudana, you draw in all creatures,
take the world into yourself, and rest, scourge of enemies. What you did as a
mere child, god, with Baladeva's help, lotus-eyed one, no one before or after
will do. And you lived with Brahma in the house of Viraj."

When the Pandava, the self of Krishna, had said this to Krishna, he fell silent.
Then Janardana said to Partha, "You are mine and I am yours, and those who are
mine are yours. Whoever hates you hates me. Whoever follows you follows me. You
are Nara, unconquerable one, and I am Hari Narayana. We two seers, Nara and
Narayana, have come from our world to this world. You are no other than I,
Partha, and I am no other than you, Bharata. No one can tell the difference
between us, bull of the Bharatas."

---

In that gathering of heroes, among the enraged kings, the princess of Panchala
stood surrounded by her brothers, the heroes led by Dhrishtadyumna. She went up
to lotus-eyed Krishna as he sat with the Yadavas. Seeking refuge, she spoke to
him who gives refuge.

"At the first creation of beings, they say, you were the one lord of creatures,
the creator of all beings. So said Asita Devala. You are Vishnu, the
unconquerable. You are the sacrifice, Madhusudana. You are the sacrificer and
the one to whom sacrifice is made, as Jamadagnya said. The seers call you
patience and truth, highest of beings. You are the sacrifice born of truth, as
Kashyapa said of you. You are the lord of lords of the Sadhyas and of the gods,
of the Vasus, maker of the worlds, lord of the worlds, as Narada said of you.
Heaven is filled by your head, lord, and the earth by your feet. These worlds
are your belly. You are the eternal Person. You are the goal of the seers who
are tempered by learning and austerity, whose selves are formed by austerity,
who are perfected in the vision of the self, best of seers. You are the goal of
the royal seers of holy deeds, who never turn back in battle and have every
virtue, highest of beings. You are the lord, you are the all-pervading, you are
the earth, the self-born, the eternal. The guardians of the worlds and the
worlds, the stars, the ten quarters, the sky, the moon and the sun: all rest in
you. The mortality of creatures and the immortality of the gods, strong-armed
one, all the business of the world, rests in you.

"So I will tell you my sorrow, Madhusudana, out of love. You are lord of all
beings, divine and human. How could a woman like me, the wife of the Parthas,
your friend, Krishna, my lord, the sister of Dhrishtadyumna, be dragged into the
hall? I was in my period, trembling, stained with blood, in a single garment,
and I was dragged, in my misery, into the assembly of the Kurus. When they saw
me in the middle of the hall among the kings, stained by my period, the
evil-minded sons of Dhritarashtra laughed. They wanted to enjoy me as a slave,
Madhusudana, while the sons of Pandu lived, and the Panchalas, and the Vrishnis.
Am I not, Krishna, by dharma the daughter-in-law of both Bhishma and
Dhritarashtra? And I was made a slave by force.

"I blame the Pandavas, great and mighty, the best in battle, who watched their
glorious lawful wife being tormented. Shame on Bhimasena's strength, and shame
on Partha's archery, Janardana, that they could bear to see me wronged by low
men! This is the eternal path of dharma, always followed by the good, that
husbands protect their wife, even when they are weak. When the wife is
protected, the children are protected. When the children are protected, a man's
own self is protected. For a man's self is born in her, and that is why she is
called his wife. And a husband must be protected by his wife, so that he may be
born in her womb.

"Surely these men never abandon anyone who comes to them for refuge. Yet when I
came to them for refuge, the Pandavas did not come to me. These five boys of
boundless power were born to me by the five of them. For their sake too I should
have been saved, Janardana: Prativindhya by Yudhishthira, Sutasoma by Vrikodara,
Shrutakirti by Arjuna, Shatanika, Nakula's son, and Shrutakarman by the
youngest, all of true courage. They are great chariot fighters, Krishna, like
Pradyumna. Are these men not the best of archers, unconquerable in battle by
their enemies? Why do they put up with the far weaker sons of Dhritarashtra? The
kingdom was taken by adharma, all of them were made slaves, and I was dragged
about the hall in a single garment, in my period.

"No one but Arjuna, Bhima or you, Madhusudana, can even string Gandiva. Shame on
Bhimasena's strength, and shame on Partha's Gandiva, when Duryodhana lives even
a moment longer, Krishna! He drove them out of the kingdom with their mother,
when they were harmless boys, studying and keeping their vows, Madhusudana. The
wicked man had poison put in Bhimasena's food, fresh, sharp, gathered kalakuta
poison, that makes the hair stand on end. It was digested with the food and did
him no harm, Janardana, because Bhima's time was not yet over, strong-armed one,
highest of beings. At Pramanakoti, when Vrikodara was asleep and trusting,
Krishna, he tied him up, threw him into the Ganga, and came back. When the son
of Kunti woke, he tore off his bonds, and strong-armed, mighty Bhimasena rose
up. He had black snakes full of poison bite him all over his body as he slept,
and the killer of enemies did not die. When the son of Kunti woke, he crushed
all the snakes, and with the back of his hand he killed Duryodhana's dear
charioteer.

"Then again, at Varanavata, he tried to burn the boys as they slept with their
mother. Who could do such a thing? There their mother, weeping and afraid,
surrounded by fire, fallen into great danger, said to the Pandavas, 'Alas, I am
lost! How can we escape this fire now? I will die helpless, with my little
boys.' Then strong-armed Vrikodara, with the speed and strength of the wind,
comforted his mother and his brothers. 'Like Vinata's son, Garuda, the best of
birds, I will fly out. You have nothing to fear here.' He took his mother on his
left hip, and the king on his right, the twins on his shoulders and Bibhatsu on
his back. The mighty man leapt up suddenly with all of them, with his brothers
and his mother, and saved them from the fire.

"The glorious ones set out by night with their mother, and came to the great
forest near Hidimba's wood. There they fell asleep with their mother, worn out
and in great sorrow, and as they slept a rakshasi named Hidimba came to them.
The lovely woman took Bhima's feet into her lap and gladly pressed them with her
soft hand. Strong Bhima, of boundless spirit and true courage, woke and saw her,
and asked her, 'What do you want here, blameless one?' When he heard them
talking, the vilest of rakshasas came, terrible in form and to look at, roaring
loudly. 'Who are you talking to? Bring him to me. We will eat him, Hidimba. Do
not delay.' But the proud, blameless woman, her heart seized by pity, would not
tell him, out of compassion. Then the rakshasa, the man-eater, roaring terribly,
rushed at Bhimasena, they say. In his fury the mighty rakshasa rushed at him
with great speed, and seized Bhimasena's hand with his own. He clenched his
hand, which struck like Indra's thunderbolt, as hard and firm as the diamond,
and suddenly swung it at Bhimasena. Strong-armed Vrikodara could not bear it
that the rakshasa had seized his hand, and he grew angry. There was a terrible,
tumultuous fight between Bhimasena and Hidimba, both masters of every weapon,
like Vritra and Vasava.

"When Bhima had killed Hidimba, he set out with his brothers, taking Hidimba
with them, in whom Ghatotkacha was born. Then the glorious ones all hurried on
with their mother towards Ekachakra, surrounded by crowds of brahmins. On that
journey Vyasa was their counsellor, who loved them and wished them well. Then
the Pandavas, strict in their vows, went to Ekachakra. There too they came upon
a mighty man-eater called Baka, fearsome, the equal of Hidimba. Bhima, the best
of fighters, killed that fierce one too, and went with all his brothers to
Drupada's city. I was won there by Savyasachin, as he lived there, as you won
Rukmini, Bhishmaka's daughter, Krishna. So I was won by Partha in a great fight,
Madhusudana, at my bridegroom choice, by a great deed that others could not
easily do.

"So, tormented by many hardships, in great sorrow, we live without our mother,
Krishna, with Dhaumya at our head. These men, with the courage of lions, greater
in strength than their enemies, how can they ignore me as I am tormented by
lesser men? They bear such sufferings from weaker men, wicked men of mean deeds,
burning for so long. I was born in a great family, by divine ordinance, they
say, the dear wife of the Pandavas, the daughter-in-law of great Pandu. I, the
best of good women, was seized by the hair, Krishna, while the five men like
Indra looked on, Madhusudana."

With these words, soft-spoken Krishna covered her face with her soft hand, which
was like a lotus bud, and wept. The princess of Panchala wet her full, shapely,
well-marked breasts with the drops of tears born of her sorrow. Wiping her eyes,
sighing again and again, she spoke in anger, her throat full of tears.

"I have no husbands, Madhusudana, no sons, no brothers, no father. Nor do I have
you, nor any kin. For you all looked on unmoved, as if you felt no grief, while
I was wronged by low men. My sorrow will not be calmed, for Karna laughed at me
then."

Then Krishna said to her, in that gathering of heroes, "The women of those you
are angry with will weep like this, lovely lady. They will see them covered in
Bibhatsu's arrows, drenched in floods of blood, killed, their lives thrown away,
lying on the ground. Do not grieve. I will do what is fitting for the Pandavas.
I promise you truly: you will be queen of kings. The sky may fall, the Himalaya
split, the earth break into pieces, the ocean dry up, Krishna, but my word will
not be in vain."

"I will kill Drona," said Dhrishtadyumna, "and Shikhandi the grandfather.
Bhimasena will kill Duryodhana, and Dhananjaya will kill Karna. With Rama and
Krishna to rely on, we cannot be beaten in battle, bright-smiling one, even by
the killer of Vritra, let alone by the sons of Dhritarashtra."

When this had been said, the heroes turned to Vasudeva, and in their midst the
strong-armed Keshava spoke.

**Divergences.**

* **"I have no husbands, no sons, no brothers … nor you."** In the CE Draupadi tells Krishna he too
  looked on (3.13.112–113). The vulgate adds four reasons why Krishna must always protect her,
  by kinship, respect, friendship and power (\*66). Not in the CE.
* **Krishna's promise.** "You will be queen of kings" is in the CE (3.13.116). Arjuna's comforting
  words to her after it are \*70. Not in the CE.
* **Draupadi's account of the past.** Her story of the poison, the Ganga, the snakes, the fire at
  Varanavata, Hidimba and Baka (3.13.71–101) is her own telling, and differs in details from Book 1. In Book 1 Bhima is awake, keeping watch, when Hidimba comes
  (1.139.12). In her telling he is asleep, and Hidimba presses his feet (3.13.86–88). Both tellings
  are kept.
* The vulgate adds the story of Madhu and Kaitabha (\*50), and has Dhrishtadyumna add that Sahadeva
  will kill Shakuni (\*71). Not in the CE.

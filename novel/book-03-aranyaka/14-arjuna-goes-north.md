# Arjuna Goes North

After some time Yudhishthira, the Dharma King, remembered the sage's instruction
and spoke to Arjuna in private. He smiled at Arjuna, the bull of the Bharatas,
whose wisdom was known, and touched him with his hand, speaking kindly. The
scourge of enemies thought for a moment about their life in the forest, and then
the Dharma King said this to Dhananjaya in secret.

"The fourfold science of the bow now rests in Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna and
Drona's son, Bharata. They know in full the weapons of Brahma, of the gods and
of the asuras, with how to use them and how to counter them, and how to use
every weapon. The son of Dhritarashtra has won them all over. He has shared his
wealth with them and they are content, and he treats them as his elders. His
conduct towards all his fighting men is always the best. Honoured as they are,
they will not hold back their strength when the time comes. And now this whole
earth obeys Duryodhana. You are our refuge. The burden rests on you. I see what
must be done now that the time has come, scourge of enemies.

"My son, I have received from Krishna Dvaipayana a secret teaching. When it is
used rightly, the whole world lies open. Take that sacred knowledge, my son, and
with your mind fixed, wait for the gods' favour in its time. Join yourself to
fierce austerity, bull of the Bharatas. Go north, my son, armed with bow, armour
and sword, a sage full of strength, and make way for no one. For Indra has all
the divine weapons, Dhananjaya. The gods, afraid of Vritra, gave their strength
to Indra then, and you will find them all there in one place. Go to Shakra
himself. He will give you the weapons. Go today, consecrated, to see the god
Purandara."

When he had said this, the lord, the Dharma King, taught him, when he had been
consecrated in the proper way, restrained in speech, body and mind. Then the
elder brother gave his hero brother leave to go. At the Dharma King's command,
to see the god Purandara, strong-armed Arjuna took up the bow Gandiva and the
two great quivers that never run out. He wore his armour and his arm guards, and
tied on his finger guards of lizard skin. He made an offering to the fire and
had the brahmins speak blessings, giving them gold coins. Then, bow in hand, the
strong-armed one set out to bring about the death of the sons of Dhritarashtra,
sighing and looking upwards.

When they saw the son of Kunti there, bow in hand, the brahmins, the siddhas and
the unseen beings said, "Son of Kunti, may you quickly win whatever you wish in
your mind."

As Arjuna set off like a lion, with thighs like shala trunks, carrying all their
hearts with him, Draupadi said this.

"Strong-armed Dhananjaya, may everything Kunti wished for you when you were born
be yours, son of Kunti, and everything you wish for yourself. May none of us
ever be born again in a family of kshatriyas. I bow always to the brahmins, who
do not live by war. Surely all your brothers will lie awake and talk of you,
telling your heroic deeds again and again. We will find no content or thought in
pleasures, in wealth or in life, Partha, while you are gone so long. On you,
Partha, rests the happiness and sorrow of us all, life and death, kingdom and
power. I have said farewell to you, son of Kunti. Go safely, Pandava. I bow to
the Placer and the Ordainer. Go safely, free from harm. May you be safe from
beings of the sky and of the earth, Bharata, and from beings of heaven, and from
any others who may stand in your way."

Then the Pandava walked round his brothers and Dhaumya, keeping them on his
right, took up his fine bow, and the strong-armed one set out. As he went,
joined to the yoga of Indra, bold and full of force, all creatures moved out of
his way. The high-minded man, joined in yoga, went as fast as thought, like the
wind, and reached the holy mountain in a single day. He crossed the Himalaya and
Gandhamadana too, and went past the hard places day and night without tiring.

When he came to Indrakila, Dhananjaya stopped. For he heard a voice in the sky.
"Stop!" Then Savyasachin saw an ascetic at the foot of a tree, shining with the
splendour of brahman, tawny, with matted hair, and thin. When the great ascetic
saw Arjuna standing there, he said to him, "Who are you, my son, who have come
here with bow and armour and arrows, with sword and arm guards, following the
dharma of the kshatriyas? There is no work for weapons here. This is the home of
calm brahmins, ascetics who have put away anger and joy. There is no use here
for a bow, and never for battle. Put down that bow, my son. You have reached the
highest goal."

So the brahmin spoke again and again to the hero of boundless power, as if to
any common man. But he could not shake him from his steadfastness, so firm was
his resolve. Then the brahmin, pleased, said to him, as if laughing, "Choose a
boon, bless you. I am Shakra, killer of enemies."

When he was told this, Dhananjaya, the brave pride of the Kuru line, bowed with
joined hands and answered the thousand-eyed god. "This is what I want. Give me
this as my boon. I want to learn all your weapons today from you, blessed one."

Great Indra, well pleased, answered him, as if laughing. "Now that you have come
here, Dhananjaya, what use are weapons to you? Choose pleasures, choose worlds.
You have reached the highest goal."

When he was told this, Dhananjaya answered the thousand-eyed god. "I do not want
worlds, or pleasures, or godhead, let alone happiness, nor the lordship of all
the immortals, lord of the thirty gods. If I left my brothers in the forest and
did not pay back the enmity, I would be disgraced in every world for ever."

When he said this, the killer of Vritra, whom all the worlds honour, answered
the son of Pandu, comforting him with gentle words. "When you see Shiva, the
lord of beings, the three-eyed bearer of the trident, then, my son, I will give
you all the divine weapons. Strive to see the highest god. When you have seen
him, son of Kunti, you will be fulfilled and go to heaven."

With these words to Phalguna, Shakra vanished, and Arjuna stayed there, joined
in yoga.

---

"Blessed one," said Janamejaya, "I want to hear in full how Partha, whose deeds
are never strained, won the weapons. How did the long-armed tiger among men,
Dhananjaya, full of splendour, go without fear into the forest empty of men?
What did he do while he lived there, best of those who know brahman? And how did
he please the blessed Sthanu and the king of the gods? I want to hear this, by
your favour, best of the twice-born, for you know everything, divine and human.
They say that Arjuna, the best of fighters, never beaten in battle, once fought
a matchless battle with Bhava, wonderful and hair-raising. When they heard of
it, the hearts of the Parthas, lions among men, brave as they were, shook from
sorrow, joy and wonder. Tell me all the other things Partha did. I see nothing
blameworthy in Jishnu, not the smallest thing. Tell me all the deeds of that
hero."

"I will tell you this great, divine story of the great man, my son, tiger of the
Kauravas, a wonder," said Vaishampayana. "Hear properly of Partha's meeting with
the three-eyed god of gods, sinless one, and how their bodies touched."

At Yudhishthira's command, Arjuna of boundless courage went to see Shakra, lord
of the gods, and Shankara, god of gods. Mighty, strong-armed Arjuna took his
divine bow and his sword, to achieve his purpose, and the Kauravya went north
towards the peak of the Himalaya. The son of Indra, steady of mind, the great
chariot fighter of all the world, went in the greatest haste, determined on
austerity, alone into the terrible thorny forest. It was full of flowers and
fruit of every kind, full of birds of every kind, crowded with herds of every
kind of beast, and siddhas and charanas lived there.

When the son of Kunti had gone into the forest empty of men, a sound of conches
and drums rose in the sky. A great rain of flowers fell on the earth, and a net
of clouds spread out and covered everything. He passed the rough places of the
forest, and near the great mountain Arjuna shone as he lived on the slope of the
Himalaya.

There he saw trees in flower, loud with the sweet songs of birds, and rivers
full of whirlpools, the colour of blue beryl, ringing with the calls of geese
and karandava ducks, of cranes, of male cuckoos, of curlews and peacocks, with
lovely woods along them. When Arjuna, the great chariot fighter, saw those holy,
cool, pure waters, his heart was glad. Delighting in that lovely stretch of
forest, the high-minded Arjuna, fierce in his splendour, took up fierce
austerity.

He put on clothes of darbha grass, with a staff and a deerskin. For one month he
ate fruit every third night. He passed the second month eating at twice that
interval. In the third month he ate once a fortnight, and lived on withered
leaves that had fallen to the ground. When the fourth month had come and passed,
strong-armed, the son of Pandu lived on air. He stood with his arms raised,
without support, on the tips of his great toes. From his constant bathing, the
matted locks of the great man of boundless power turned the colour of lightning
and of the lotus.

Then all the great seers went to the god who bears the bow Pinaka, the
blue-throated, the fortunate. They bowed to him, won his favour, and told him
all of Phalguna's deeds. "This Partha, of great splendour, has gone to the slope
of the Himalaya. He stands in fierce austerity, hard to endure, and makes the
quarters smoke. Lord of the gods, none of us knows what he means to do. He is
burning us all. It would be well to stop him."

"Go quickly and gladly, back the way you came, without fear," said the great
lord. "I know the purpose that is in his mind. He has no longing for heaven, nor
for power, nor for long life. All that he desires I will do today."

When they heard Sharva's words, the seers, who speak the truth, went back gladly
to their own hermitages.

**Divergences.**

* **The order of events.** In the CE Indra meets Arjuna first, disguised as an ascetic, at
  Indrakila, and tells him to see Shiva before he will give him the weapons (3.38.30–45). Arjuna's
  austerities come after (3.39.16–24).
* **Draupadi's farewell.** Her farewell is in the CE (3.38.19–25). The vulgate adds several
  more blessings from her, and her grief that Duryodhana had called her "cow" (\*146). Not in
  the CE. In the CE it was Duhshasana who cried "Cow!", at Bhima (2.68.19). See L-121. Also rejected: Draupadi's glance as Arjuna's provision for the road (\*155), and a six-day journey to Indrakila (\*159); in the CE he reaches the mountain in a single day (3.38.28).

* 3.38.5 *brāhmaṃ daivam āsuram*: the weapons "of Brahma, of the gods and of the asuras".
* 3.38.9 *upaniṣat*: "a secret teaching", the Pratismriti of 3.37.27.
* 3.38.11 *na kasya cid dadan mārgam*: "make way for no one".

# Ravana's Birth

"When Krishna had been carried off like this," said Janamejaya, "and they had
suffered that terrible hardship, what did the tigers among men, the Pandavas, do
after that?"

When he had freed Krishna like this and beaten Jayadratha, Yudhishthira the
Dharma King sat down with the hosts of sages. In the midst of those great seers,
who listened and grieved with him, the joy of the Pandavas said this to
Markandeya.

"I think Time is strong, and fate is ordained by the Maker, and what is to be
for living beings cannot be avoided. How else could such a thing touch our wife,
who knows dharma and walks in dharma, as a false charge of theft might touch an
honest man? Draupadi has never done anything wicked, nor any deed that is
blamed, anywhere. She has kept great dharma well towards the brahmins. Yet King
Jayadratha, fool that he is, carried her off by force. For carrying her off he
had his head shaved, and he and his allies met defeat in battle. We have brought
her back by killing that Saindhava army. But this carrying off of our wife came
on us without our dreaming of it. And this life in the forest is hard, living by
hunting, killing the beasts of the forest, we who live in the forest ourselves,
and this exile from our kin, who have acted falsely. Is there any man less
fortunate than I am? Have you seen or heard of one before?"

---

"Rama suffered matchless sorrow, bull among the Bharatas," said Markandeya. "His
wife, Janaki, was carried off by a stronger rakshasa, Ravana, lord of the
rakshasas, who took her through the sky from the hermitage by using magic, after
swiftly killing the vulture Jatayu. Rama brought her back with the help of
Sugriva's army, after building a bridge across the sea and burning Lanka with
sharp arrows."

"In what family was Rama born?" said Yudhishthira. "What was his strength, what
was his courage? Whose son was Ravana, and what was his quarrel with him? You
must tell me all of it properly, lord. I want to hear the story of Rama, whose
deeds were untiring."

"There was a great king named Aja, born in the line of Ikshvaku," said
Markandeya. "His son was Dasharatha, always given to study, and pure. He had
four sons skilled in dharma and wealth: Rama, Lakshmana and Shatrughna, and
mighty Bharata. Rama's mother was Kausalya, Bharata's was Kaikeyi, and Lakshmana
and Shatrughna, scorchers of enemies, were the sons of Sumitra. Janaka was king
of Videha, and Sita was his daughter, lord, whom Tvashtri himself made to be
Rama's beloved queen. That is the birth of Rama and of Sita. Now I shall tell
you Ravana's birth too, lord of the people.

"Ravana's grandfather was the god Prajapati himself, the self-born, the lord and
creator of all the worlds, rich in austerity. He had a dear son born from his
mind, named Pulastya. Pulastya had a son named Vaishravana, a lord, born of a
cow. He left his father and went to wait on his grandfather. In anger at this,
king, his father made another self from himself. The brahmin born from half of
his self was named Vishravas, full of anger, to take revenge on Vaishravana. But
the grandfather was pleased with Vaishravana, and gave him immortality, the
lordship of wealth, and the guardianship of a quarter of the world, friendship
with Ishana, a son, Nalakubara, and Lanka, full of hosts of rakshasas, for his
royal seat."

---

"The sage who was born in anger from half of Pulastya's body, Vishravas, full of
rage, looked on Vaishravana with anger," said Markandeya. "The lord of the
rakshasas knew that his father was angry, king, and Kubera always tried to win
his favour. The king of kings, who lived in Lanka and was carried by men, gave
his father three rakshasa women to wait on him. They set out to please that
great seer, bull of the Bharatas, skilled in dance and song: Pushpotkata, Raka
and Malini, lord of the people, slender-waisted, rivalling one another, each
wanting the most for herself, king.

"The great lord was pleased with them and gave them boons: sons like the
guardians of the world, as each one wished. Pushpotkata bore two sons, lords of
the rakshasas, Kumbhakarna and Dashagriva, unmatched on earth in strength.
Malini bore one son, Vibhishana. Raka bore twins, Khara and Shurpanakha.
Vibhishana was the handsomest of them all. He was greatly blessed, a guardian of
dharma, who delighted in the rites. Dashagriva, the eldest of them all, the bull
of the rakshasas, had great energy, great strength, and great spirit and
courage. Kumbhakarna was the strongest of them all, a master of magic, wild for
battle, a fierce rover of the night. Khara was valiant with the bow, a hater of
brahmins and an eater of flesh, and Shurpanakha was fierce, a troubler of the
siddhas. All of them knew the Veda and were brave, and all kept their vows well.
They lived happily with their father on Mount Gandhamadana.

"Then they saw Vaishravana there, carried by men, sitting with their father,
filled with the highest splendour. Rivalry was born in them, and they made up
their minds to practise austerity. They pleased Brahma with terrible
austerities. Dashagriva stood on one foot for a thousand years, living on air,
with the five fires about him, his mind wholly fixed. Kumbhakarna lay on the
ground, restrained in his food and his vows. Wise Vibhishana ate a single
withered leaf. The noble-minded one delighted in fasting and was always intent
on prayer. He kept up fierce austerity for the same time. Khara and Shurpanakha
gladly served them and guarded them while they practised austerity.

"When the thousand years were full, Dashagriva, the unassailable, cut off his
head and offered it into the fire, and with this the lord of the world was
pleased. Then Brahma went himself and stopped them from their austerities,
tempting each of them separately with the granting of boons.

"'I am pleased with you,' said Brahma. 'Stop, and choose boons, my sons.
Whatever you wish shall be, all but one thing, immortality. All the heads you
offered into the fire, wanting greatness, shall be on your body again as you
wish. There will be no deformity on your body, and you will take any form you
please. You will beat your enemies in battle, no doubt of it.'

"'May I never be defeated by gandharvas, gods or asuras,' said Ravana, 'by
yakshas or rakshasas, by snakes, kinnaras or spirits.'

"'You will have nothing to fear from any of those you have named,' said Brahma,
'except from man. Bless you, so I have ordained it.'

"When this was said to him, Dashagriva was pleased," said Markandeya. "For the
fool, an eater of men, despised men. Then the great-grandfather spoke to
Kumbhakarna in the same way, and he, his mind swallowed by darkness, chose a
great sleep. 'So it shall be,' said Brahma, and he said to Vibhishana again and
again, 'Choose a boon, my son. I am pleased.'

"'Even in the greatest distress,' said Vibhishana, 'may my mind never turn to
what is against dharma. And, lord, though I have not been taught it, may the
Brahma weapon come to me.'

"'Since your mind does not delight in what is against dharma,' said Brahma,
'though you were born in the womb of a rakshasa, tamer of enemies, I give you
immortality.'

"When he had won his boon, lord of the people, the rakshasa Dashagriva beat the
lord of wealth in battle and drove him out of Lanka. The lord left Lanka and
went to Gandhamadana, followed by gandharvas and yakshas, with rakshasas and
kimpurushas. Ravana attacked him and took his flying chariot, the Pushpaka.
Vaishravana cursed him: 'This will not carry you. It will carry only the one who
kills you in battle. Because you have scorned me, your elder, you will soon be
no more.'

"But Vibhishana, whose self was dharma, remembering the dharma of the good,
followed Kubera, great king, filled with the highest splendour. The wise lord of
wealth, his brother, was pleased with his brother and gave him command of the
armies of the yakshas and rakshasas. The rakshasas, the eaters of men and the
mighty pishachas all came together and consecrated ten-faced Dashagriva as their
king.

"Fierce in his strength, taking any form he pleased and moving through the sky,
Dashagriva attacked the daityas and the gods and carried off their jewels.
Because he made the worlds cry out, he is called Ravana. Dashagriva, strong as
he pleased, filled the gods with fear.

---

"Then the brahmin seers, the siddhas and the royal seers of the gods went to
Brahma for shelter, with Agni, the carrier of offerings, at their head.

"'Dashagriva, the mighty son of Vishravas,' said Agni, 'was made unkillable long
ago by your boon, lord. The mighty one torments all creatures with outrages.
Save us from him, lord, for there is no other saviour.'

"'He cannot be beaten in battle by gods or asuras, Agni,' said Brahma. 'What
must be done to hold him down has been arranged. For that purpose the four-armed
Vishnu, best of strikers, has gone down to earth at my command. He will do this
deed.'

"Then the grandfather said in their presence, 'Be born on earth with all the
hosts of the gods. Beget heroic sons, able to take any form and full of
strength, in female bears and monkeys everywhere, to help Vishnu.' Then the
gods, gandharvas and danavas all quickly set about going down to earth, each
with his share and part. In their presence the god who gives boons gave orders
to a gandharvi named Dundubhi, to bring about the gods' purpose. At the
grandfather's words the gandharvi Dundubhi was born in the world of men as the
hunchback Manthara.

"Shakra and all the other best of the gods begot sons in the finest of female
monkeys and bears, and all of them took after their fathers in fame and
strength. They could split mountain peaks, and their weapons were sal and palm
trees and rocks. All of them had bodies as hard as diamond, and all had the
strength of a flood. They had all the power they wished and were all skilled in
war. Each had the strength of ten thousand elephants and the speed of the wind.
Some of them lived wherever they liked, and some lived in the forests here.

"When the maker of the worlds had arranged all this, he told Manthara what she
was to do, and how. She understood his words, and, swift as thought, she did as
he said, going here and there, intent on kindling enmity."

**Divergences.**

* **Sita's birth.** In the Mahabharata's telling, Sita is Janaka's daughter, "whom Tvashtri himself made" for Rama (3.258.9). The story of her being found in a furrow is not told here. See L-154.
* **Ravana's mother.** Here Ravana and Kumbhakarna are sons of Pushpotkata, one of three rakshasa women Kubera gave his father (3.259.3–8). The names familiar from other tellings, such as Kaikasi, do not appear.
* **Kumbhakarna's boon.** He chooses "a great sleep" because his mind is "swallowed by darkness" (3.259.28). The popular story that he meant to ask for Indra's seat and the goddess of speech twisted his tongue is not in the Mahabharata.
* **The austerities.** A rejected passage (\*1236) adds details of the brothers' austerities at Gokarna. Not narrated.
* **The monkeys' birth.** Rejected passages (\*1240–\*1241) add that the seers, siddhas and charanas also created monkeys. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*1229–\*1235, \*1237–\*1239) add phrases, including Draupadi's birth from the altar and Kubera's gift of the Pushpaka from Brahma. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 3.257.5 *śuciṃ stainyam ivānṛtam*: "as a false charge of theft might touch an honest man".
* 3.258.12 *gavi putraḥ*: "born of a cow". The text says no more.
* 3.258.13 *sasarjātmānam ātmanā*: "made another self from himself".
* 3.259.3 *naravāhanaḥ*: "carried by men", Kubera's title.
* 3.259.16 *pañcāgniḥ*: "with the five fires about him", four fires and the sun.
* 3.259.23: Brahma restores the heads Ravana offered. The text does not say how many.
* 3.259.40 *rāvayām āsa lokān … tasmād rāvaṇa ucyate*: "Because he made the worlds cry out, he is called Ravana". The name is explained from *rāvaya*, "to make cry out".
* 3.260.10 *mantharā … kubjā*: "the hunchback Manthara".

# The Search

"When Nala had lost his kingdom and gone into service with his wife," said
Brihadashva, "Bhima sent out brahmins, longing to see Nala. Bhima gave them
plenty of wealth and told them, 'Search for Nala and for my daughter Damayanti.
When this work is done, and the lord of the Nishadhas is found, I will give a
thousand cows to the one who brings them back. I will give him land held free as
well, a village the size of a town. If Damayanti or Nala cannot be brought here,
I will give a thousand cows as a reward even for finding out where they are.'

"When he said this, the brahmins set out gladly in every direction, searching
cities and kingdoms for the Naishadha and his wife. Then a brahmin named Sudeva,
searching for the princess of Vidarbha in the lovely city of the Chedis, saw her
in the king's palace, standing with Sunanda at the king's ceremony of the holy
day. Her matchless beauty was dimmed, like the light of the fire wrapped in a
net of smoke. When he looked at her, long-eyed, very dirty and thin, he guessed
from the signs that she was Bhima's daughter.

"'This woman is just as I saw her before,' said Sudeva. 'I have what I came for
today, now that I have seen her, like Shri, whom the world loves. She has a face
like the full moon, she is dark and lovely, with fine round breasts, and she
makes all the quarters bright with her splendour, the queen. Her eyes are like
lovely lotus petals, she is like Rati, the wife of the god of love, loved by the
whole world, like the light of the full moon. She is like a lotus stalk torn by
some evil fate from the lake of Vidarbha, her limbs smeared with dirt and mud.
She is like the night of the full moon when Rahu has swallowed the moon.
Troubled by grief for her husband and wretched, she is like a river whose
streams have dried. She is like a lotus pool troubled by an elephant's trunk,
its leaves and flowers torn, its birds frightened away.

"'She is tender, finely made, fit for a jewelled inner room, and she seems to
burn like a lotus stalk just pulled from the water in the heat. She has beauty,
nobility and virtue, she deserves ornaments and has none, like the new crescent
moon in the sky, covered by dark clouds. She has lost the pleasures she loved,
she has lost her kin, and she keeps her body alive, wretched, in the hope of
seeing her husband. A husband is a woman's greatest ornament, even without
ornaments. Parted from him, lovely as she is, she is not lovely. Nala does
something very hard when he keeps his body alive without her and does not sink
with grief. When I see her, with her black hair and her long eyes like a
hundred-petalled lotus, suffering, who deserves happiness, my own heart aches.

"'When will this good woman reach the far shore of her sorrow, by meeting her
husband, as Rohini meets the moon? Surely the Naishadha will be glad to have her
back, as a king who has lost his kingdom is glad to win back the earth. The
Naishadha deserves the princess of Vidarbha, equal to him in character, in age
and in birth, and this black-eyed woman deserves him. It is right for me to
comfort the wife of that man of boundless courage and spirit, who longs to see
her husband. I will comfort her, with her face like the full moon, who never
knew sorrow before and is now lost in it and in thought.'

"When he had thought it over by many signs and reasons, the brahmin Sudeva went
up to Bhima's daughter and said, 'I am Sudeva, princess of Vidarbha, your
brother's dear friend. I have come here to search for you, at King Bhima's word.
Your father is well, queen, and your mother and your brothers. Your two children
are there, alive and well. Your kin sit as if their life had gone out of them,
because of you.'

"When Damayanti recognised Sudeva, Yudhishthira, she asked after all her
friends, one by one. And the princess of Vidarbha wept bitterly, king, wasted
with grief, when she suddenly saw Sudeva, the best of brahmins, her brother's
friend.

"When Sunanda saw her weeping, wasted with grief, and talking alone with Sudeva,
Bharata, she sent word to her mother. 'The sairandhri is weeping bitterly with a
brahmin she has met. Find out about her, if you think fit.' Then the mother of
the lord of the Chedis came out of the king's inner rooms to where the girl was
with the brahmin. The king's mother sent for Sudeva, lord of the people, and
asked him, 'Whose wife is this lovely woman, or whose daughter? How was this
beautiful-eyed woman lost to her kin, or to her husband? And how did you know
her, brahmin, when she has come to this? I want to know all this from you, in
full. Tell me truly, for I ask you about her, who looks like a goddess.'

"When she asked him this, king, Sudeva, the best of brahmins, sat down at his
ease and told the story of Damayanti as it was.

"'The king of Vidarbha is the righteous Bhima, of terrible courage,' said
Sudeva. 'This good woman is his daughter, famed as Damayanti. The king of the
Nishadhas is Nala, son of Virasena, and this good woman is the wife of that wise
king of good name. He was beaten at dice by his brother, and the lord of the
earth lost his kingdom. He went away with Damayanti, and no one knows where he
is. We wander this earth for Damayanti's sake, and here I have found the girl in
your son's house.

"'There is no woman here like her in beauty. Between the brows of this dark
woman there is a fine mark she was born with, like a lotus. I saw that it was
hidden. For it is covered with dirt, like the moon by a thin cloud. The Ordainer
made it as a sign of her good fortune. It does not shine much, like the line of
the moon on the first day, dimmed. But her beauty is not lost. Her body, covered
in dirt and unadorned, still shines plainly like gold. By this body and by this
mark I knew the queen, like fire hidden by its heat.'

"When she heard Sudeva's words, lord of the people, Sunanda washed away the dirt
that covered the mark. With the dirt washed away, Damayanti's mark shone like
the moon in a cloudless sky. When Sunanda and the king's mother saw the mark,
Bharata, they held her and wept, and stood so for a while. Then the king's
mother, slowly letting her tears fall, said this.

"'You are my sister's daughter. This mark shows it. Your mother and I are
daughters of the great Sudama, lord of the Dasharnas, lovely one. She was given
to King Bhima, and I to Virabahu. I saw you born in the Dasharnas, in my
father's house. This house is yours as much as your father's house, lovely one.
My wealth is yours as much as mine, Damayanti.'

"Damayanti, glad at heart, lord of the people, bowed to her mother's sister and
said, 'Even unknown, I have lived happily here, with everything I wished for,
always protected by you. My stay would be happier still, no doubt. But I have
been away so long, mother. Let me go. My two children have been taken there, and
they are living there, little ones, without their father, grieving, and without
me too. How must they be? If you want to do anything to please me, I want to go
to the Vidarbhas. Order a carriage for me quickly.'

"'Very well,' said her mother's sister, gladly, king. With her son's consent,
the king's mother sent her off, guarded by a great army, in a splendid
palanquin, best of the Bharatas, with good food, drink and clothes. Before long
the lovely woman came to the Vidarbhas, and all her kin welcomed her gladly and
honoured her. The glorious Damayanti saw all her kin well, and the two children,
her mother and her father and all her friends. Then she worshipped the gods and
the brahmins in the finest way, lord of the people. The king was pleased to see
his daughter, and he rewarded Sudeva with a thousand cows, a village and wealth.

"When she had spent the night in her father's house and rested, king, the lovely
woman spoke to her mother. 'If you want me to live, mother, I tell you truly,
try to bring back that hero among men, Nala.'

"When Damayanti said this to her, the queen, in great sorrow, choked with tears,
could not say a word in answer, king. When they saw her in that state, the whole
inner palace cried out and wept bitterly.

"Then the queen said to Bhima, 'Great king, your daughter Damayanti mourns for
her husband. She put aside her shame and told me herself, king. Let your
servants try to find the king of good name.'

"Urged on by her, the king sent out in every direction brahmins who were under
his command. 'Strive to find Nala.' Then at the command of the lord of Vidarbha,
the bulls among brahmins went to Damayanti and said, 'We are setting out.'

"Then Bhima's daughter said to them, 'Say these words in every kingdom, in the
gatherings of people, wherever you go, again and again.'

"'"Where have you gone, gambler, my love, after cutting half my cloth and
leaving me, your loving dear wife, asleep in the forest? She waits for you as
you told her, burning bitterly, the girl, covered by half a cloth. She weeps all
the time with that grief, king. Be kind, hero, and give her an answer."

"'Say this and more, so that he will take pity on me. For the fire, fanned by
the wind, burns the forest. "A wife must always be supported and protected by
her husband. How have both these gone, in you who know dharma? You were known to
be wise, well born and always kind. You have become unkind, I fear, because my
luck has run out. Take pity on me, great archer, bull among men. For I heard
from you yourself that kindness is the highest dharma."

"'If anyone answers you when you say this, you must find out in every way who
that man is and where he lives. And whatever answer a man gives when he hears
your words, take his words and bring them to me quickly, best of brahmins. Take
care, and do it tirelessly, that he does not know you are going about at Bhima's
command, or that you will come back. Whether he is rich, or whether he is poor,
or whether he wants wealth, you must find out what he means to do.'

"When she said this, the brahmins went out in every direction, king, to search
for Nala, who had met with such misfortune. The brahmins searched cities and
kingdoms, villages, herdsmen's camps and hermitages for Nala, king, and did not
find him. And everywhere, lord of the people, the brahmins made that speech
heard, just as Damayanti had spoken it.

---

"Then, after a long time, a brahmin named Parnada came back to the city and said
this to Bhima's daughter. 'Searching for the Naishadha day and night, Damayanti,
I went to the city of Ayodhya and came before Bhangasvari. I made him hear your
words, the blessed Rituparna, in a great gathering, just as you said them,
lovely one. When he heard them, King Rituparna said nothing, nor did anyone in
the assembly, though I said them again and again.

"'But when the king had let me go, a man of Rituparna's spoke to me in private,
named Bahuka. He is that king's charioteer, misshapen, with short arms, very
skilled at driving fast, and he makes delicious food. He sighed many times and
wept again and again, asked after my welfare, and then said this:

"'"Women of good family, even when they meet misfortune, guard themselves by
themselves. Beyond doubt, the true ones win heaven. Even when their husbands
leave them, they are never angry. She should not be angry that he left her, a
man in misfortune, bewildered, fallen from happiness. The dark woman should not
be angry with him, when he wanted only to stay alive, when the birds took his
clothes and worries burned him. Whether he treated her well or badly, when she
sees her husband come to this, fallen from his kingdom and robbed of fortune,
the dark woman should not be angry."

"'When I heard his words, I came here quickly. Now that you have heard, you must
decide, and tell the king too.'

"When she heard this from Parnada, lord of the people, Damayanti, her eyes full
of tears, went to her mother in private and said, 'Mother, this must never be
told to Bhima. I will instruct Sudeva, the best of brahmins, in your presence.
If you want to please me, you must take care that King Bhima does not learn what
I intend. Let Sudeva go quickly, without delay, from here to the city of Ayodhya
to bring Nala, mother, with the same good fortune with which he quickly brought
me back to my kin.'

"Then, when Parnada, the best of brahmins, had rested, the lovely princess of
Vidarbha honoured him with a great deal of wealth. 'When Nala has come here,
brahmin, I will give you more wealth. You have done much for me, as no one else
will, brahmin, by which I will soon be joined with my husband, best of the
twice-born.' When she said this, he blessed her with good blessings and went
home, great-hearted, his purpose achieved.

"Then Damayanti sent for Sudeva, Yudhishthira. In her mother's presence, full of
sorrow and grief, she said, 'Go to the city, Sudeva, and tell King Rituparna,
who lives in Ayodhya, "Bhima's daughter Damayanti will hold a bridegroom choice
again. She wants another husband. Kings and princes are going there from
everywhere. As the time has been counted, it will be tomorrow. If you can manage
it, go quickly, tamer of enemies. At sunrise she will choose a second husband.
For no one knows whether the hero Nala is alive or dead."'

"So Sudeva went, great king, and said to King Rituparna just what she had told
him."

**Divergences.**

* **The second bridegroom choice.** In the CE the announcement is Damayanti's plan to bring Nala to
  Vidarbha. She tells her mother to keep it from Bhima (3.68.14–15) and sends Sudeva with the message
  (3.68.21–23).
* **The queen mother.** The CE makes the queen mother of the Chedis Damayanti's mother's sister, both
  daughters of Sudama of the Dasharnas (3.66.12–13). The queen mother's husband is named Virabahu;
  her son, the reigning king, is Subahu (3.61.125).

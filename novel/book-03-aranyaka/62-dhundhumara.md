# Dhundhumara

The Dharma King Yudhishthira questioned Markandeya, bull of the Bharatas, who
was great in austerity, long-lived and without stain. "You know the gods,
danavas and rakshasas, you who know dharma, and the many lines of kings and the
eternal lines of seers. There is nothing in this world you do not know, best of
the twice-born. You know the divine stories of men, snakes and rakshasas, sage.
I want to hear this, told truly, brahmin. The Ikshvaku famed as Kuvalashva, the
unconquered: how did he come by a changed name, and become Dhundhumara? I want
to know truly, best of the Bhargavas, how the wise Kuvalashva's name was
changed."

"Yes, I will tell you," said Markandeya. "Listen, King Yudhishthira. This is the
most righteous story of Dhundhumara. Hear how that king, the Ikshvaku
Kuvalashva, lord of the earth, came to be Dhundhumara.

"There was a famous great seer named Uttanka, my son, Bharata, and his hermitage
was in the lovely desert lands, Kaurava. Uttanka, great king, practised very
hard austerity for many years, lord, wanting to win the favour of Vishnu. The
blessed one was pleased with him and showed himself to him. As soon as he saw
him the seer bowed low and praised him with many hymns.

"'By you, god, all creatures were made, with gods, asuras and men, all things
that do not move and all that move, brahman and the Vedas and what is to be
known, you of great splendour. The sky is your head, god, the moon and the sun
your eyes, the wind your breath, and fire your splendour, unfallen one. All the
quarters are your arms and the great ocean your belly. The mountains are your
thighs, god, the air your navel, slayer of Madhu, the goddess earth your feet
and the plants your hair. Indra, Soma, Agni and Varuna, the gods, the asuras and
the great snakes wait on you, bowing and praising you with many hymns. All
creatures are pervaded by you, lord of the worlds. Yogis of great power and
great seers praise you. When you are pleased the world is well. When you are
angry there is great fear. You alone take fears away, highest Person. You bring
happiness to gods and men and all creatures. With three strides, god, you took
the three worlds, and you destroyed the thriving asuras. By your strides the
gods reached the highest peace, and the lords of the daityas were overthrown
when you were angry, you of great splendour. You are the maker and the unmaker
of all creatures here. Worshipping you, the gods thrive in happiness
everywhere.'

"When great Uttanka had praised Hrishikesha like this, Vishnu said to Uttanka,
'I am pleased with you. Choose a boon.'

"'This is boon enough for me,' said Uttanka, 'that I have seen Hari, the eternal
divine Person, the creator of the world, the lord.'

"'I am pleased with you for your lack of greed and your devotion, best of the
twice-born,' said Vishnu. 'You must take a boon from me, brahmin.'

"So pressed by Hari with the offer of a boon, Uttanka joined his hands and chose
his boon, best of the Bharatas. 'If the blessed lotus-eyed one is pleased with
me, let my mind always be on dharma, truth and self-control, and let me always
practise devotion to you, great lord.'

"'All this will be yours by my grace, brahmin,' said Vishnu. 'And the yoga will
come to you by which, joined with it, you will do a great work for the dwellers
in heaven and for the three worlds. A great asura named Dhundhu is practising
terrible austerity to destroy the worlds. Hear who will kill him. There will be
a king famed as Brihadashva. His son, pure and self-controlled, will be known as
Kuvalashva. That best of kings, taking on my power of yoga, will become
Dhundhumara, Dhundhu's Killer, at your command, brahmin seer.' When he had said
this to Uttanka, Vishnu vanished.

---

"When Ikshvaku had passed away, king, the most righteous Shashada came to this
earth and became king in Ayodhya. Shashada's heir was the mighty Kakutstha, and
Anenas was Kakutstha's son, and Prithu the son of Anenas. Vishvagashva was
Prithu's son, and from him Ardra was born. Ardra's son was Yuvanashva, and his
son was Shravasta. King Shravastaka was born, who built Shravasti. Shravasta's
heir was the mighty Brihadashva, and Brihadashva's son was known as Kuvalashva.
Kuvalashva had twenty-one thousand sons, all skilled in learning, strong and
hard to withstand. Kuvalashva surpassed his father in his virtues, and in time
Brihadashva consecrated him king, great king, the brave and most righteous
Kuvalashva. When he had passed his fortune to his son, wise King Brihadashva,
slayer of enemies, went to the forest of austerity to practise austerity.

"Then Uttanka, best of the twice-born, heard that the royal seer Brihadashva had
set out for the forest, Yudhishthira. Uttanka of great splendour, of boundless
spirit, went to that best of men, the finest of all masters of weapons, and held
him back.

"'Your task is to protect,' said Uttanka. 'Do that first. Let us live free of
care by your grace, king. With you, great-spirited, to protect it, the earth
will be free of care, king. You should not go to the forest. There is great
dharma to be seen here in protecting the people. It is not seen so in the
forest. Do not think of this. Nowhere, king of kings, is such dharma seen as the
protection of the people that the royal seers of old practised. A king must
protect his people, and you should protect them. I cannot practise my austerity
free of care, king.

"'Near my hermitage, in the level desert, there is a sea filled with sand called
Ujjanaka, many leagues wide and many leagues long. There lives a fierce lord of
the danavas, of great courage and might, the son of Madhu and Kaitabha, the most
terrible Dhundhu. He lives under the earth, king, of boundless courage. Kill
him, great king, and then you may go to the forest. He lies there practising
terrible austerity to destroy the worlds, to destroy the thirty gods and the
worlds, king. He has won a boon from the Grandfather of all the worlds, king,
that he cannot be killed by gods, daityas or rakshasas, by snakes, yakshas or
gandharvas. Destroy him, and good fortune be yours. Do not think of anything
else. You will win great fame, eternal, unfailing and sure.

"'When that cruel one sleeping under the sand breathes out, at the end of each
year, the earth shakes with its mountains, woods and forests. The wind of his
breath raises a great dust that covers the path of the sun, and the earth quakes
for seven days, with sparks, flames and smoke, most terrible. So I cannot stay
in my own hermitage, king. Destroy him, king of kings, for the good of the
worlds. Let the worlds be at ease today when that asura is killed. I think you
are able to destroy him. Vishnu will swell your splendour with his own. Long ago
Vishnu gave me a boon for his killing: the king who kills that terrible great
asura, the unassailable splendour of Vishnu will enter him. Take that splendour,
king of kings, unbearable on earth, and kill that wicked daitya of terrible
courage. For Dhundhu of great splendour cannot be burned by a little splendour,
lord of the earth, not even in hundreds of years.'

"At Uttanka's words the unconquered royal seer joined his hands and said to him,
best of the Kauravas, 'Your coming will not be in vain, brahmin. This son of
mine, blessed one, is known as Kuvalashva. He is steady and quick to act, and
has no equal on earth in courage. He will do all this that you wish, without
doubt, surrounded by all his brave sons with arms like iron bars. Let me go,
brahmin. I have laid down my weapons now.' The sage of boundless splendour said
to him, 'So be it.' The royal seer gave his son to the great Uttanka, saying,
'Let it be done,' and went to the finest of forests."

"Who is this daitya of great courage, blessed one, rich in austerity?" said
Yudhishthira. "Whose son or grandson is he? I want to know. I have never heard
of so mighty a daitya, rich in austerity. I want to know it truly, blessed one,
all of it and at length, wise one, rich in austerity."

---

"Hear all this, king, just as it happened, lord of men," said Markandeya. "Once
there was one terrible ocean. All that moves and does not move was destroyed,
and all creatures had perished, bull of the Bharatas. Then the source of all
creatures, the eternal unchanging Person, the blessed Vishnu, slept alone on a
bed in the waters, on the great coil of the snake Shesha of boundless splendour.
The maker of the world, the blessed unfallen Hari, embraced this earth with the
great coil of the snake. As the god slept, a lotus bright as the sun came out of
his navel, and there the Grandfather was born, Brahma himself, teacher of the
worlds, in the lotus bright as the sun and moon. He had four Vedas, four forms
and four faces, and by his own power he could not be assailed, of great strength
and courage.

"After some time two danavas of great courage, Madhu and Kaitabha, saw the lord
Hari. He lay on his divine bed, the coil of the snake, many leagues wide and
many leagues long. He was of great splendour, wearing the diadem and the
Kaustubha jewel, dressed in yellow silk, blazing with glory, splendour and
beauty, king, like a thousand suns, wonderful to see. Madhu and Kaitabha were
filled with great wonder, and they saw the Grandfather too in the lotus, with
his lotus eyes. Then they began to terrify Brahma of boundless power. Terrified
again and again by the two of them, glorious Brahma shook the lotus stalk, and
then Keshava woke.

"Then Govinda saw the two danavas of great courage. When he saw them the god
said, 'Welcome, mighty ones. I will give you a fine boon, for I am pleased with
you.' The two great asuras of great courage laughed at Hrishikesha and answered
the slayer of Madhu together, great king. 'Ask us for a boon, god. We are the
givers of boons, best of the gods. We will give you a boon. Tell us, and do not
hesitate.'

"'I accept a boon, heroes,' said the blessed one, 'and I want one. You are full
of courage, and no man is your equal. May you become killable by me, you whose
courage is true. That is the wish I want to gain, for the good of the worlds.'

"'We have never spoken an untruth, not even in jest,' said Madhu and Kaitabha,
'let alone otherwise. Know that we are devoted to truth and dharma, highest
Person. In strength, beauty and courage and in calm there is no one to equal us,
nor in dharma, austerity and giving, in character, spirit and self-control. A
great calamity has come upon us, Keshava. Do what you have said, for time is
hard to pass. We want one thing done by you, god, lord: death in a place in the
sky that is not covered, best of the gods. And we would become your sons,
fair-eyed one. That is the boon we choose, god. Know it, best of the gods.'

"'Very well,' said the blessed one. 'I will do this. All of it will be.'

"Then Govinda thought, and the slayer of Madhu could see no place that was not
covered, on the earth or in the sky. Then the best of the gods saw his own
thighs, which were not covered, king, and there the glorious slayer of Madhu cut
off the heads of Madhu and Kaitabha with his sharp-edged discus.

---

"Their son was Dhundhu, of great splendour and brilliance. He practised great
austerity, of great courage and might. He stood on one foot, lean, his veins
standing out. Brahma, pleased, gave him a boon, and he chose it, lord. 'May I be
unkillable by gods, danavas and yakshas, by snakes, gandharvas and rakshasas.
That is the boon I choose.' 'So be it. Go,' said the Grandfather to him. At
these words he touched the Grandfather's feet with his head and went.

"When Dhundhu, of great courage and might, had won the boon, he remembered the
killing of his father and rushed at Vishnu. Unforgiving, Dhundhu conquered the
gods with the gandharvas, and he harassed all the gods and Vishnu again and
again. The wicked one came to the sea filled with sand known as Ujjanaka, bull
of the Bharatas, and harassed Uttanka's hermitage with all his power, lord.
There Dhundhu, son of Madhu and Kaitabha, of terrible courage, lay under the
earth, hidden by the sand, relying on the strength of his austerity, to destroy
the worlds, breathing out flames of fire near Uttanka's hermitage.

"At that very time King Kuvalashva, slayer of enemies, with his servants, his
army and his mounts, followed by his mighty ones and his twenty-one thousand
sons, set out with Uttanka for Dhundhu's dwelling. Then the blessed Vishnu, the
lord, entered him with his splendour at Uttanka's bidding, for the good of the
worlds. As the unassailable one set out, a great voice was heard in the sky:
'This glorious prince will be Dhundhumara.' The gods scattered divine flowers
all round him, and the drums of the gods sounded of themselves. A cool wind blew
as the wise one set out, and the lord of the gods rained, laying the dust on the
earth. The aerial chariots of the gods were seen in the sky, Yudhishthira, right
over the place where the great asura Dhundhu lay. The great seers, with the gods
and gandharvas, watched, eager to see the fight between Kuvalashva and Dhundhu.

"Filled then with splendour by Narayana, the king went quickly with his sons in
every direction, Kauravya. King Kuvalashva had his sons dig in that sea of sand.
When they had dug for seven days, the mighty Dhundhu was seen. His terrible
body, huge, hidden in the sand, blazed with splendour like the sun, bull of the
Bharatas. Dhundhu lay asleep there, great king, turned to the west, tiger among
kings, bright as the fire at the end of time.

"Surrounded on every side by Kuvalashva's sons, he was attacked with sharp
arrows, with maces and clubs, with axes, iron bars, spears and bright sharp
swords. As they struck him the mighty one rose up in anger, and in his anger he
ate their many weapons. Vomiting from his mouth a fire like the fire of the
dissolution, he burned all the king's sons with his own splendour. In his anger
he burned them in a moment with the fire from his mouth, as if overturning the
worlds, tiger among kings, as the lord Kapila in his anger burned the sons of
Sagara long ago. It was a kind of wonder.

"When they had been burned by the fire of his anger, best of the Bharatas, King
Kuvalashva of great splendour went up to the great one, now awake, like a second
Kumbhakarna. Water poured from the king's body in floods, great king. With it
the king drank up that watery splendour, king, and the yogi quenched the fire
with water by his yoga. Then the king burned the daitya of cruel courage with
the Brahma weapon, best of the Bharatas, to free all the worlds from fear. When
the royal seer Kuvalashva, slayer of enemies, had burned that great asura, the
enemy of the gods, with the weapon, like a second lord of the three worlds, he
became known by the name Dhundhumara.

"Then all the thirty gods, pleased, with the great seers, told him, 'Choose a
boon.' He joined his hands and bowed, and, overjoyed, king, said, 'May I give
wealth to the best of the brahmins, and may my enemies never conquer me. May I
have friendship with Vishnu and bear no malice to any creature. May I always
delight in dharma, and may I dwell in heaven for ever.' The gods, pleased, said
to the king, 'So be it,' and so did the seers with the gandharvas and wise
Uttanka. When they had honoured the king with many blessings, the gods and the
great seers went back to their own places.

"Three of his sons were left then, Yudhishthira: Dridhashva, Kapilashva and
Chandrashva, Bharata. From them comes the line of the great Ikshvakus, king.

"So the daitya Dhundhu, of great courage, the son of Madhu and Kaitabha, was
killed by Kuvalashva, good man. And King Kuvalashva was called Dhundhumara, and
from that time his name matched his deeds. I have told you all that you asked
me, the story of Dhundhumara, made famous by his deed. Whoever hears this holy
story, the praise of Vishnu, becomes righteous and has sons. Hearing it at the
joints of the moon he becomes long-lived and steady, and free of fever he meets
no fear of sickness."

**Divergences.**

* **Born of Vishnu's ear.** A rejected line (\*980) says Madhu and Kaitabha were born of the wax of Vishnu's ear. The CE says only that they came and saw Hari (3.194.13). See L-142.
* **Four arms.** A rejected line (\*979) has Markandeya say he saw Vishnu four-armed. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 3.192.17 *tribhir vikramaṇaiḥ*: "With three strides".
* 3.193.4: Shravasta and Shravastaka are both named; the text gives both forms.
* 3.193.15 *ujjānakaḥ*: Ujjanaka, the sea of sand.
* 3.194.26 *anāvṛte 'sminn ākāśe*: "in a place in the sky that is not covered". Vishnu's bare thighs are that place (3.194.30).
* 3.195.25 *kapilaḥ*: Kapila, who burned Sagara's sons (3.105–106).

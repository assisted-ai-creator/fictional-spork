# The Nivatakavachas

"Then, praised here and there by the great seers," said Arjuna, "I saw the
ocean, terrible, the undying lord of the waters. Its waves could be seen,
foaming, scattered and heaped, towering, moving like mountains. There were ships
in thousands all round, full of jewels. Timingilas, tortoises, timitimingilas
and makaras could be seen there, like mountains sunk in the water. Thousands of
conches could be seen sunk in the water all round, like stars at night thinly
veiled by cloud. Heaps of jewels in thousands floated there, and a terrible wind
whirled about. It was a kind of wonder.

"When I had passed that finest of all waters, of great speed, I saw near me the
city of the daityas, crowded with danavas. Matali swooped down there quickly to
the ground and rushed at the city, filling it with the thunder of the chariot.
When the danavas heard the sound of the chariot, like thunder in the sky, they
thought I was the king of the gods and were alarmed. All of them, bewildered,
stood holding bows and arrows, and spears, swords, axes, maces and clubs. Then
the danavas, in terror, shut the gates. They set a guard in the city, and no one
could be seen.

"Then I took the conch Devadatta, of great sound, and close to the asura city I
blew it slowly. The sound stiffened the sky and raised an echo, and creatures,
even very great ones, were terrified and hid. Then the Nivatakavachas, the sons
of Diti, appeared on every side in thousands. They were clad in armour of many
kinds, with weapons of many kinds in their hands: great iron spears, maces and
clubs, axes and swords, chariot wheels, shataghnis and bhushundis, and bright,
well-adorned swords, Bharata.

"Then Matali, after thinking it over many ways, drove those horses on level
ground along the chariot paths, bull of the Bharatas. Those swift horses went so
fast when he urged them on that I could see nothing. It was a kind of wonder.
Then the danavas there urged on their many troops of warriors, all misshapen in
voice and form. At that great sound, fish like mountains floated up lifeless in
the ocean in hundreds of thousands. Then the danavas rushed at me with great
speed, loosing sharp arrows in hundreds and thousands. There was a tumult of
blows between them and me, Bharata, most terrible, the end of the
Nivatakavachas. Then the divine seers and the hosts of danava seers, the brahmin
seers and the siddhas came to that great battle. Wishing for victory, the sages
praised me with sweet and fitting words, as they praised Indra in the war over
Tara.

---

"Then all the Nivatakavachas ran at me together in the battle with great speed,
their weapons raised, Bharata. Shouting, the great chariot fighters cut off my
chariot's road, surrounded me on every side and covered me with rains of arrows.
Then other danavas of great courage, with spears and axes in their hands, loosed
spears and bhushundis at me. That great rain of spears, thick with maces and
lances, loosed by them without pause, fell on my chariot. Other Nivatakavachas
ran at me in the battle, fierce, with sharp weapons, in the form of Time,
striking.

"I struck each of them in the battle with ten swift, straight arrows of many
kinds, loosed from Gandiva. My arrows, sharpened on stone, turned them all back.
Then Matali quickly urged on the horses, and they ran as fast as the wind, many
ways from the chariot road. Well held by Matali, they crushed the sons of Diti.
There were a hundred hundred of those bay horses yoked to that great chariot,
but held in check by Matali they moved as if they were few. By the trampling of
their feet, the sound of the chariot wheels and the fall of my arrows, the
asuras were killed in hundreds. Others, lifeless, still holding their bows,
their charioteers killed, were dragged about by their horses.

"Blocking all the quarters and the spaces between, they struck with weapons of
many kinds, and my mind was troubled. Then I saw Matali's most wonderful
strength, how he held those swift horses without effort. Then in the battle,
with light and wonderful weapons, I cut down those asuras with their weapons in
hundreds and thousands, king. As I ranged there with all my might, slayer of
enemies, the hero Matali, Shakra's charioteer, was pleased with me. Struck down
by the horses and the chariot, some of them were destroyed, and others turned
back.

"As if competing with us, the Nivatakavachas held me back on every side in the
battle with great rains of arrows. Then with light, bright arrows charged with
the Brahma weapon I quickly struck them down in hundreds and thousands. Then,
hard pressed and filled with rage, the great asuras pressed me all together with
rains of arrows, spears and swords. Then I took up the supreme weapon of fierce
splendour, dear to the king of the gods, called the Madhava, Bharata. By the
power of that weapon I cut the swords, tridents and lances they loosed in
thousands into a hundred pieces. When I had cut their weapons, I pierced them
all in return, in anger, with ten arrows each. The arrows flew from Gandiva in
that battle like lines of bees, and Matali praised it.

"Their arrows too, as many as locusts, covered me hard, and I struck them down
with my arrows. Struck down, the Nivatakavachas again surrounded me on every
side with great rains of arrows. I destroyed the force of their arrows with
weapons that destroy arrows, and pierced them in thousands with blazing, supreme
and swift ones. Their severed limbs poured out blood, like mountain peaks in the
rainy season when it rains hard. Struck by my swift, straight arrows, whose
touch was like Indra's thunderbolt, the danavas were in turmoil. Their bodies
and entrails broken in a hundred pieces, their weapons and strength failing, the
Nivatakavachas fought me with magic.

---

"Then a great rain of rocks broke out on every side, most terrible, each as big
as a mountain, and it pressed me hard. With swift arrows like thunderbolts,
driven by Indra's weapon, I ground each rock in the battle into a hundred
pieces. As the rain of rocks was ground to dust, fire was born, and the rock
dust fell like heaps of flame. When the rain of rocks was destroyed, a greater
rain of water broke out near me, in streams as thick as axles. Streams of fierce
power poured from the sky in thousands and covered the sky on every side, and
the quarters and the spaces between. With the fall of the streams, the roaring
of the wind and the bellowing of the daityas, nothing could be seen. The
streams, joined to the sky and to the earth on every side, falling on the ground
without pause, bewildered me there. Then I loosed the divine weapon Indra had
taught me, the Drier, blazing and terrible, and it dried up that water.

"When I had destroyed the rain of rocks and dried up the rain of water, the
danavas let loose magic fire and wind, you who give honour to others. Then I put
out the fire everywhere with the Water weapon, and held back the force of the
wind with the great Mountain weapon. When that was turned back, the danavas, mad
for battle, made many kinds of magic all at once, Bharata. Then a very great
rain broke out that made the hair stand on end, a rain of terrible weapons, of
fire, wind and rocks. That magic rain pressed me in the battle.

"Then a terrible, thick darkness came on every side. With the world covered in
that terrible, harsh darkness, the horses turned away and Matali stumbled. The
golden goad fell from his hand to the ground, and in his fear he said to me
again and again, 'Where are you?' bull of the Bharatas. A sharp fear came over
me when he lost his wits. And he, his senses gone, said this to me in terror.
'There was a great battle between the gods and the asuras long ago for the drink
of immortality, Partha, and I saw it, sinless one. There was a great battle too
when Shambara was killed, and there too I was the king of the gods' charioteer.
So too I held the horses when Vritra was killed, and I saw the most terrible
battle with the son of Virochana. I have been through these most terrible
battles, Pandava, and never before have I lost my senses. The Grandfather has
surely ordained the destruction of creatures. Such a battle is not possible
except at the end of the world.'

"When I heard his words I steadied myself by my own will, and to bewilder the
danavas' army of magic, I said to frightened Matali, 'See the strength of my
arms, and the power of my weapons and of the bow Gandiva. Today, by the magic of
my weapons, I will destroy their most terrible magic and this fierce darkness.
Do not be afraid, charioteer. Stand firm.' When I had said this, lord of men, I
loosed the magic of weapons that bewilders all enemies, for the good of the
dwellers in heaven.

"As each of their kinds of magic was crushed, the lords of the asuras, of
boundless power, made many kinds of magic again. Now it was light, now it was
swallowed by darkness again. Now the world went out of sight, now it sank in the
water. When it was light, Matali, holding the bay horses well in hand, ranged
the hair-raising battlefield in that fine chariot. Then the fierce
Nivatakavachas fell on me, and seeing my chance I sent them to Yama's house. As
that battle went on, the end of the Nivatakavachas, I suddenly could not see any
of the danavas, for they were covered by magic.

---

"The daityas, unseen, fought with magic, and I fought them, unseen as they were,
by the power of my weapons. The arrows loosed from Gandiva, rightly driven by my
weapons, cut off their heads wherever they were. Then the Nivatakavachas, as I
struck them down in the battle, suddenly drew in their magic and went into their
city. When the daityas had gone and I could see again, I saw danavas lying dead
there in hundreds of thousands. Their weapons and ornaments, their limbs and
armour could be seen there, crushed, in heaps. There was not room for the horses
to move one step from another. They suddenly leapt up and stood in the sky.

"Then the Nivatakavachas, unseen, covering the whole sky, came at me again,
hurling mountains. Other terrible danavas, gone under the earth, seized the
horses' feet and the chariot wheels, Bharata. Holding my bay horses and my
chariot as I fought, they heaped mountains all round me and my chariot. With
mountains piling up and others falling, the place where we were became like a
cave. Covered by mountains, my horses held fast, I came into the greatest
distress, and Matali saw it.

"Seeing my fear he said, 'Arjuna, Arjuna, do not be afraid. Raise the
thunderbolt weapon.' When I heard his words I raised the thunderbolt, lord of
men, the weapon dear to the king of the gods. Taking a firm stand and charging
Gandiva with mantras, I loosed sharp iron arrows whose touch was like the
thunderbolt. Driven by the thunderbolt, those arrows became thunderbolts and
went into all those kinds of magic and all those Nivatakavachas. Struck by the
force of the thunderbolt, the danavas, like mountains, clung to one another and
fell to the ground. The arrows went into the danavas who had seized the chariot
and horses from under the earth and sent them to Yama's house. The place was
covered with the fallen Nivatakavachas, like mountains, as if mountains had been
scattered there. No harm could be seen to the horses, or the chariot, or Matali,
or me. It was a kind of wonder.

"Then Matali laughed and said to me, king, 'The courage seen in you, Arjuna, is
not found among the gods.' When the hosts of asuras were killed, their wives all
wailed in that city like cranes in autumn. Then with Matali I went to that city,
terrifying the wives of the Nivatakavachas with the sound of the chariot. Seeing
those ten thousand horses like peacocks, and the chariot like the sun, the women
fled in crowds. The sound their ornaments made as they fled in fear was like the
sound of rocks falling on mountains. The frightened wives of the daityas went
into their own houses, bright with many jewels and made of gold.

"When I saw that city, wonderful in shape and finer than the city of the gods, I
asked Matali, 'Why do the gods not live in a city like this? I think it is finer
than Purandara's city.'

"'This was our city long ago, Partha, the city of the king of the gods,' said
Matali. 'Then the Nivatakavachas drove the gods out of it. They practised great
and fierce austerity and won the favour of the Grandfather, and they chose this
for their dwelling, and to be safe from the gods in battle. Then Shakra urged
the blessed Self-born, "Blessed one, do what is right here," for his own good.
Then the blessed one said to Vasava, "It is fated here. You yourself will be
their end, slayer of Vritra, in another body." So Shakra gave you weapons to
kill them. These whom you have killed could not be killed by the gods. In the
ripening of time you have come here, Bharata, to be their end, and you have done
it. Mahendra made you master the great strength of the great weapons, lord of
men, to destroy the danavas, and that is the best of it.'

"Then I entered the city, and when I had killed those danavas, I went back again
with Matali to the dwelling of the gods.

**Divergences.**

  weapon (3.168.2).

* The chapter is Arjuna's speech to Yudhishthira; Matali's words are quoted within it.

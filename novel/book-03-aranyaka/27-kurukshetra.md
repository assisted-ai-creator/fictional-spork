summary: Pulastya's account of the holy fords continues with Kurukshetra, the holiest land in the three worlds: its gatekeepers, Rama Jamadagnya's five lakes of blood and the Fathers' boon, the sage Mankanaka who danced for joy until Shiva humbled him, Prithudaka, Sannihiti, and the bounds of the Samantapanchaka between Tarantuka, Arantuka, the Lakes of Rama and Machakruka.

# Kurukshetra

"'Then, king of kings,'" Narada went on, telling Pulastya's words, "'a man
should go to praised Kurukshetra. All creatures who go there are freed from
their sins. The man who says always, "I will go to Kurukshetra, I will live in
Kurukshetra," is freed from sin too. He should live there a month on the
Sarasvati, hero, Yudhishthira. The gods with Brahma at their head come there,
with the seers, siddhas and charanas, the gandharvas and apsarases, the yakshas
and snakes, lord of the earth, to the most holy field of Brahma, Bharata. Even
the man who only longs for Kurukshetra in his mind, Yudhishthira, has his sins
destroyed and goes to Brahma's world. Going to Kurukshetra with faith, upholder
of the Kurus, a man wins the reward of the royal consecration and the horse
sacrifice.

"'Then, greeting the mighty yaksha Machakruka, the gatekeeper, he wins the
reward of a thousand cows. Then, you who know dharma, he should go to the
matchless place of Vishnu called Satata, king of kings, where Hari is always
present. Bathing there and worshipping Hari, the source of the three worlds, he
wins the horse sacrifice and goes to Vishnu's world. Then he should go to
Pariplava, a ford famous in the three worlds. A man wins the reward of the
agnishtoma and the atiratra. Coming to the ford of the Earth, he wins the reward
of a thousand cows. Then, lord of men, going to Shalukini as a pilgrim, and
bathing at Dashashvamedhika, he wins that same reward. Coming to Sarpadarvi, the
fine ford of the snakes, he wins the agnishtoma and finds the world of the
snakes. Then, you who know dharma, he should go to the gatekeeper Tarantuka.
Staying there one night, he wins the reward of a thousand cows.

"'Then going to Panchanada, restrained and sparing in his food, and touching the
water at Kotitirtha, he wins the reward of the horse sacrifice. Coming to the
ford of the Ashvins, he is born handsome. Then, you who know dharma, he should
go to the fine ford of the Boar, where Vishnu once stood in the form of a boar.
Bathing there, tiger among men, he wins the reward of the agnishtoma. Then, king
of kings, he should go to Somatirtha at Jayanti. Bathing there, a man wins the
reward of the royal consecration. Bathing at Ekahamsa, a man wins the reward of
a thousand cows. Coming to Kritashaucha as a pilgrim, upholder of the Kurus, he
wins the pundarika sacrifice and is made pure.

"'Then there is Munjavata, the place of wise Mahadeva. Staying there one night,
he wins the lordship of the god's hosts. There too, great king, is a yakshi
famous in the world. Going to her, king of kings, he reaches holy worlds. That
is the famous gate of Kurukshetra, bull of the Bharatas. Going round it, the
pilgrim with a gathered mind should bathe at the place that is the measure of
the Pushkaras, brought there by great Rama Jamadagnya, and worship the Fathers
and the gods. Then he has done all he need do, king, and he wins the horse
sacrifice.

"'Then the pilgrim should go to the Lakes of Rama, lord of men, where Rama,
blazing in splendour, destroyed the kshatriyas by his force and courage, king of
kings, and made five lakes. He filled them with blood, tiger among men, so we
have heard, and with it he offered water to all his fathers and grandfathers.
Then those Fathers, pleased, said to Rama, lord of the earth, "Rama, Rama,
blessed one, we are pleased with you, Bhargava, with this devotion to your
fathers and with your courage, lord. Choose a boon, bless you. What do you wish,
splendid one?"

"'When they said this, king of kings, Rama, the best of fighters, joined his
hands and spoke to the Fathers standing in the sky. "If you are pleased with me,
and if I am to be favoured, I would wish, by my fathers' grace, to be made
strong again by austerity. And I destroyed the kshatriyas when anger overwhelmed
me. By your power let me be freed from that sin. And let my lakes become holy
fords, famous on the earth."

"'When they heard these good words of Rama's, the Fathers, full of joy, answered
him. "Let your austerity grow again, above all by your devotion to your fathers.
As for the kshatriyas you destroyed when anger overwhelmed you, you are freed
from that sin. They fell by their own deeds. And your lakes will become holy
fords, no doubt of it. Whoever bathes in these lakes and offers water to his
fathers, his fathers, pleased, will give him the wish of his heart, hard to win
on earth, and the eternal world of heaven." When they had given Rama these
boons, king, the Fathers took leave of Bhargava, pleased, and vanished there and
then. So the Lakes of great Rama Bhargava are holy. Bathing in Rama's lakes,
chaste and of good vows, and worshipping Rama, king of kings, a man wins much
gold.

"'Coming to Vamshamulaka, the pilgrim, bathing there, raises up his own line,
king. Then he comes to the ford of Kayashodhana, best of the Bharatas. The body
of the man who bathes there is made pure, no doubt of it, and with a pure body
he goes to the matchless worlds of the good. Then, king of kings, he should go
to the ford famous in the three worlds, where mighty Vishnu once raised up the
worlds. Coming to Lokoddhara and bathing in that finest of fords, he raises up
his own worlds, king. Coming to Shritirtha he gains the highest fortune.

"'Coming to Kapilatirtha, chaste and with a gathered mind, bathing and
worshipping the gods and the Fathers there, a man wins the reward of a thousand
tawny cows. Coming to Suryatirtha and bathing with a controlled mind,
worshipping the Fathers and the gods and fasting, he wins the agnishtoma and
goes to the world of the sun. Coming in turn to Gavambhavana and bathing there,
the pilgrim wins the reward of a thousand cows. Coming to Shankhini, upholder of
the Kurus, and bathing in the goddess's ford, a man gains the finest beauty.
Then, king of kings, he should go to the gatekeeper Arantuka. His ford, the ford
of the great lord of the yakshas, is on the Sarasvati. Bathing there, king, a
man wins the reward of the agnishtoma.

"'Then, you who know dharma, he should go to Brahmavarta, lord of men. Bathing
at Brahmavarta, a man reaches Brahma's world. Then, you who know dharma, he
should go to the matchless Sutirthaka, where the Fathers are always present with
the gods. He should bathe there, devoted to worship of the Fathers and the gods.
He wins the horse sacrifice and goes to the world of the Fathers. Then, you who
know dharma, coming in turn to Ambuvashya and bathing in the fords of
Kosheshvara, best of the Bharatas, he is freed from every sickness and honoured
in Brahma's world. Matritirtha is there too. For the man who bathes there,
Bharata, his children increase, king, and he enjoys endless fortune.

"'Then he should go to Shitavana, restrained and sparing in his food. There is a
great ford there, great king, hard to find elsewhere. The mere sight of it makes
a man pure in a moment, lord of men. Sprinkling his hair there, he is made pure,
Bharata. There is a ford there, great king, known as the remover of dog's hair.
There learned brahmins, tiger among men, devoted to the fords, the best of the
twice-born, rid themselves of dog's hair by controlling the breath. Pure in
spirit, king of kings, they go to the highest goal. Dashashvamedhika is at that
ford too, lord of the earth. Bathing there, tiger among men, one should reach
the highest goal.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to Manusha, famous in the world, where black
antelopes, wounded by a hunter, plunged into the lake and became men. Bathing at
that ford, chaste and master of his senses, a man is cleansed of every sin and
honoured in the world of heaven. A kos to the east of Manusha, lord of the
earth, is the famous river called Apaga, where siddhas go. The man who gives a
meal of shyamaka millet there, for the gods and the Fathers, wins a great reward
of dharma. When one brahmin is fed there, ten million are fed. Bathing there and
worshipping the gods and the Fathers, and staying one night, he wins the reward
of the agnishtoma.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the fine place of Brahma known on earth
as Brahmodumbara, Bharata. He should bathe in the pools of the Seven Seers
there, bull of the Kurus, and at the Kedara of great Kapishthala, king of kings.
Going to Brahma, pure and with a controlled mind, he is cleansed of every sin
and reaches Brahma's world. Coming to the Kedara of Kapishthala, very hard to
reach, his sins burned away by austerity, he gains the power to vanish.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to Saraka, famous in the world. Going to the
god whose banner is a bull on the fourteenth day of the dark half of the month,
he gains every wish and goes to the world of heaven. There are thirty million
fords at Saraka, joy of the Kurus, and Rudrakoti in the well and in the lakes,
lord of the earth, and the ford Ilaspada is there too, best of the Bharatas.
Bathing there and worshipping the Fathers and the gods, Bharata, he does not
come to a bad end, and he wins the vajapeya. Bathing at Kimdana and at Kimjapya,
lord of the earth, a man wins gifts and prayers beyond measure, Bharata.
Touching the water at Kalashi too, with faith and mastery of his senses, a man
wins the reward of the agnishtoma sacrifice.

"'To the east of Saraka, best of the Kurus, is the ford of great Narada known as
Anajanma. The man who bathes at that ford and gives up his life there, Bharata,
reaches worlds hard to win, with Narada's leave. On the tenth day of the bright
half of the month one should go to Pundarika. Bathing there, king, a man wins
the reward of the pundarika sacrifice. Then one should go to Trivishtapa, famous
in the three worlds. The holy river Vaitarani is there, which frees a man from
sin. Bathing there and worshipping the god with the trident in his hand and the
bull on his banner, he is cleansed of every sin and goes to the highest goal.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the fine Phalakivana, where the gods
always live, king, practising great austerity for many thousands of years.
Bathing in the Drishadvati and offering water to the gods, a man wins the reward
of the agnishtoma and the atiratra, Bharata. Bathing at the ford of all the
gods, best of the Bharatas, a man wins the reward of a thousand cows, king of
kings. Bathing at Panikhata and offering water to the gods, a man wins the royal
consecration and goes to the world of the seers.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the fine ford of Mishraka. There, tiger
among kings, we have heard, great Vyasa mixed the fords together for the sake of
the twice-born. The man who bathes at Mishraka has bathed in all the fords. Then
he should go to Vyasavana, restrained and sparing in his food. Bathing at
Manojava, a man wins the reward of a thousand cows. Going to Madhuvati, the
goddess's ford, a pure man should bathe there and worship the gods and the
Fathers with a controlled mind. With the goddess's leave, he wins the reward of
a thousand cows. Whoever bathes, sparing in food, where the Kaushiki meets the
Drishadvati, Bharata, is freed from every sin.

"'Then there is the place called Vyasasthali, where wise Vyasa, burning with
grief for his son, resolved to give up his body, king of kings, and was raised
up again by the gods. Going to his place, a man wins the reward of a thousand
cows. Coming to the well of Kimdatta and giving a measure of sesame there, one
reaches the highest perfection, upholder of the Kurus, freed from his debts. The
two fords Ahan and Sudina are very hard to reach. Bathing in them, tiger among
men, one reaches the world of the sun.

"'Then he should go to Mrigadhuma, famous in the three worlds. Bathing there in
the lake of the Ganga and worshipping Mahadeva, the trident in his hand, a man
wins the reward of the horse sacrifice. Bathing at Devatirtha, a man wins the
reward of a thousand cows. Then he should go to Vamanaka, famous in the three
worlds. Bathing there at Vishnupada and worshipping the Dwarf, he is cleansed of
every sin and reaches Vishnu's world. Bathing at Kulampuna, a man purifies his
own family. Going to the lake of the Wind, the fine ford of the Maruts, and
bathing there, tiger among men, he is honoured in the world of the Wind. Bathing
in the lake of the immortals, lord of men, he is honoured in the world of heaven
among the immortals, by their power. Bathing as the rules require at
Shalishurpa, the ford of Shalihotra, best of men, he wins the reward of a
thousand cows. Shrikunja is a ford on the Sarasvati, best of the Bharatas.
Bathing there, king, a man wins the reward of the agnishtoma.

"'Then one comes to Naimishakunja, upholder of the Kurus. Long ago, they say,
king of kings, the seers of the Naimisha forest, rich in austerity, set out on a
pilgrimage to the fords and went to Kurukshetra. Then a grove was made on the
Sarasvati, best of the Bharatas, so that there would be room for the seers, a
great source of content. Bathing in that grove, a man wins the reward of a
thousand cows. Bathing at Kanyatirtha, a man wins the reward of the agnishtoma.
Then, tiger among men, he should go to the fine place of Brahma. Bathing there,
a man of lower class gains the rank of a brahmin, and a brahmin, pure in spirit,
goes to the highest goal. Then, best of men, he should go to the matchless
Somatirtha. Bathing there, king, a man reaches the world of Soma.

---

"'Then, lord of men, he should go to the ford of the Seven Sarasvatas, where the
perfected Mankanaka lived, a great seer famous in the world. Long ago, king, we
have heard, Mankanaka cut his hand, they say, on the tip of a blade of kusha
grass, and vegetable juice flowed from it. When the great ascetic saw the
vegetable juice, the brahmin seer was filled with joy and began to dance, they
say, his eyes wide with wonder. As he danced, everything moving and unmoving
danced too, bewildered by his power, hero.

"'Then the gods with Brahma at their head, and the seers rich in austerity,
appealed to Mahadeva about the seer, lord of men. "You must see to it, god, that
he does not dance." So the god came to him as he danced with a heart full of
joy, and for the good of the gods he spoke to the seer. "Ah, great seer, you who
know dharma, why are you dancing? What cause of joy have you today, bull among
sages?"

"'"Do you not see, god," said the seer, "the vegetable juice flowing from my
hand? I saw it, and I danced, filled with great joy."

"'The god laughed and said to the sage, blinded by his passion, "I feel no
wonder, brahmin. Look at me." When he had said this, best of men, wise Mahadeva
struck his own thumb with the tip of his finger, sinless one, king of kings.
Then ash like snow came out of the wound, king. When he saw it, king, the sage
was ashamed and fell at his feet.

"'"I know no god higher or greater than Rudra. You are the way of the world, of
gods and asuras, you who bear the trident. You made this universe, the three
worlds with all that moves and does not move. At the end of the age, blessed
one, all things enter you. Even the gods cannot know you wholly, let alone I. In
you all the gods are seen, with Brahma at their head, sinless one. You are
everything, the maker of the worlds and the one who makes them made. By your
grace all the gods rejoice here, fearing nothing." When he had praised Mahadeva
like this, the seer bowed low. "By your grace, Mahadeva, let my austerity not
drain away."

"'Then the god, glad at heart, said to the brahmin seer, "Let your austerity
grow a thousandfold, brahmin, by my grace. And I will live here in this
hermitage with you, great sage. Those who bathe at the Seven Sarasvatas and
worship me will find nothing hard to win in this world or the next, and they
will go to the world of Sarasvati, no doubt of it."

---

"'Then he should go to Aushanasa, famous in the three worlds. There the gods
with Brahma at their head and the seers rich in austerity, and the blessed
Karttikeya, were present at the three twilights, they say, Bharata, to please
Bhargava. The ford Kapalamochana frees a man from every sin. Bathing there,
tiger among men, he is freed from every sin. Then he should go to Agnitirtha.
Bathing there, bull among men, he reaches the world of Fire and raises up his
family. The ford of Vishvamitra is there too, best of the Bharatas. Bathing
there, great king, one is born to the rank of a brahmin. Coming to Brahmayoni,
pure and with a controlled mind, and bathing there, tiger among men, he reaches
Brahma's world and purifies his family to the seventh generation, no doubt of
it.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the ford famous in the three worlds known
as Prithudaka, the ford of Karttikeya, king. He should bathe there, devoted to
worship of the Fathers and the gods. Whatever evil deed a woman or a man has
done, in ignorance or knowingly, with a human mind, all of it is destroyed as
soon as they bathe there, Bharata. They win the reward of the horse sacrifice
and go to heaven. They call Kurukshetra holy, the Sarasvati holier than
Kurukshetra, the fords holier than the Sarasvati, and Prithudaka holier than the
fords. The man who gives up his body at Prithudaka, the best of all the fords,
intent on prayer, will not be troubled by death tomorrow. Sanatkumara and great
Vyasa have sung it, and it is fixed in the Veda, king: one should go to
Prithudaka. There is no ford holier than Prithudaka, best of men. It is pure,
holy and purifying, no doubt of it. Bathing there, even evildoers go to heaven.
So the wise speak of Prithudaka, best of men. Madhusrava is a ford there too,
best of the Bharatas. Bathing there, king, a man wins the reward of a thousand
cows.

"'Then, best of men, he should go in turn to the goddess's ford, the famous
meeting of the Sarasvati and the Aruna. Fasting three nights and bathing there,
he is freed from the killing of a brahmin. A man wins the reward of the
agnishtoma and the atiratra, and purifies his family to the seventh generation,
bull of the Bharatas. Avatirna is a ford there too, upholder of the Kuru line,
made long ago by Darbhin out of kindness to brahmins. By vows and initiation, or
by fasting, a twice-born man joined to rites and mantras becomes a brahmin, no
doubt of it. But even without rites and mantras, bull among men, bathing there a
brahmin becomes one who has kept his vows. This was seen in ancient times.
Darbhin also brought the four seas there. Bathing in them, tiger among men, a
man does not come to a bad end, and he wins the reward of four thousand cows.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the ford Shatasahasraka, and Sahasraka is
there too, two fords famous in the world. Bathing in both, a man wins the reward
of a thousand cows, and a gift or a fast there is multiplied a thousand times.
Then, king of kings, he should go to the fine ford of Renuka. He should bathe
there, devoted to worship of the Fathers and the gods, and cleansed of every
sin, he wins the reward of the agnishtoma. Touching the water at Vimochana, a
man who has mastered his anger and his senses is freed from all the faults of
taking gifts. Then going to Panchavata, chaste and master of his senses, he is
joined to great merit and honoured in the world of the good. There the lord of
yoga, Sthanu himself, whose banner is a bull, is present. Worshipping the lord
of the gods there, one succeeds by merely going.

"'The ford of Aujasa of Varuna blazes with its own splendour. There the gods
with Brahma at their head, and the seers rich in austerity, consecrated Guha as
general of the gods. To the east of Aujasa, upholder of the Kurus, is
Kurutirtha. Bathing at Kurutirtha, chaste and master of his senses, a man is
cleansed of every sin and reaches the world of Kuru. Then he should go to
Svargadvara, restrained and sparing in his food. He reaches the world of heaven
and goes to Brahma's world.

"'Then, lord of men, the pilgrim should go to Anaraka. Bathing there, king, a
man does not come to a bad end. There Brahma himself, lord of the earth, is
always attended by the gods, best of men, with Narayana at their head. And the
wife of Rudra is present there, king of kings, upholder of the Kurus. Going to
that goddess, one does not come to a bad end. There too, great king, going to
Mahadeva, the lord of all, the husband of Uma, a man is freed from every sin.
Going to Narayana, the lotus-navelled tamer of enemies, he shines, great king,
and reaches Vishnu's world. Bathing at the ford of all the gods, bull among men,
he is free of every sorrow and always shines like the moon.

"'Then, lord of men, the pilgrim should go to Svastipura. Coming to the ford of
Pavana, he should offer water to the Fathers and the gods. A man wins the reward
of the agnishtoma sacrifice. The lake of the Ganga is there too, and a well,
bull of the Bharatas. There are thirty million fords in that well, lord of the
earth. Bathing there, king, a man reaches the world of heaven. Bathing in the
Apaga and worshipping Maheshvara, he gains the lordship of the god's hosts and
raises up his own family. Then he should go to Sthanuvata, famous in the three
worlds. Bathing there and staying the night, he reaches the world of Rudra.

"'Then he should go to Badaripachana, the hermitage of Vasishtha. A man should
eat jujube fruit there, fasting three nights. Whoever eats jujube fruit properly
for twelve years, and whoever fasts three nights there, are equal, lord of men.
Coming to Indramarga, the pilgrim, fasting a day and a night, is honoured in
Shakra's world, lord of men. Coming to Ekaratra and staying one night, a man
restrained and truthful is honoured in Brahma's world.

"'Then, you who know dharma, he should go to the ford famous in the three worlds
where the hermitage of the great Aditya is, the mass of splendour. Bathing at
that ford and worshipping the Sun, a man goes to the world of the Adityas and
raises up his family. Bathing at Somatirtha, upholder of the Kurus, the pilgrim
reaches the world of Soma, no doubt of it. Then, you who know dharma, he should
go to the ford of great Dadhicha, the holiest and most purifying, famous in the
world, king, where Angiras Sarasvata lived, a treasure of austerity. Bathing at
that ford, a man wins the reward of the vajapeya and reaches the state of
Sarasvata, no doubt of it.

"'Then he should go to Kanyashrama, restrained and chaste. Fasting three nights,
king, given to fasting, he gains a hundred divine maidens and goes to Brahma's
world. Then, you who know dharma, he should go to the ford of Sannihiti, where
the gods with Brahma at their head, and the seers rich in austerity, gather
month by month, full of great merit. Touching the water at Sannihiti when Rahu
has swallowed the sun, a man has made a hundred horse sacrifices, for ever. All
the fords on the earth and those that move in the sky gather at Sannihiti month
by month, no doubt of it, with the rivers, streams and lakes, all the springs,
wells and pools and holy shrines. Whatever evil deed a woman or a man has done
is all destroyed as soon as they bathe there, no doubt of it. Such a man goes to
Brahma's world in a chariot the colour of a lotus.

"'Then, greeting the yaksha Arantuka, the gatekeeper, and touching the water at
Kotirupa, he gains much gold. The lake of the Ganga is a ford there too, best of
the Bharatas. Bathing there, you who know dharma, chaste and with a gathered
mind, one wins for ever the reward of the royal consecration and the horse
sacrifice.

"'On the earth Naimisha is holy, in the sky Pushkara, but Kurukshetra is above
all three worlds. Even the dust of Kurukshetra, carried by the wind, leads an
evildoer to the highest goal. Those who live in Kurukshetra, south of the
Sarasvati and north of the Drishadvati, live in heaven. By speaking even one
word, "I will go to Kurukshetra, I will live in Kurukshetra," a man is freed
from every sin. Kurukshetra is the altar of Brahma, holy, where the brahmin
seers go. Those who live there, king, are never to be pitied.

"'What lies between Tarantuka and Arantuka, and between the Lakes of Rama and
Machakruka, this is Kurukshetra, the Samantapanchaka, and it is called the
northern altar of the Grandsire.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Kurukshetra's dust.** The verses in praise of Kurukshetra, its dust that saves the evildoer, and
  its bounds between the Sarasvati and the Drishadvati, are placed near the start of the chapter in the
  vulgate (\*389). The CE keeps them at the end (3.81.173–178).
* **The new moon at Sannihiti.** Rejected lines (\*403, \*404) add that the fords gather at Sannihiti at
  the new moon. The CE says "month by month" (3.81.166, 169).

**Choices.**

* Narada tells the chapter, retelling Pulastya: Narada's words are in double quotes and Pulastya's inside
  them in single quotes.
* 3.81.7 *macakrukam*: Machakruka, a yaksha gatekeeper; 3.81.13 *tarantukam* and 3.81.42 *arantukam*:
  Tarantuka and Arantuka, the other gatekeepers. The same three mark the bounds at 3.81.178.
* 3.81.17 *puṇḍarīkam*: the *puṇḍarīka* sacrifice.
* 3.81.21 *saṃmite puṣkarāṇām*: "the place that is the measure of the Pushkaras"; the sense is uncertain.
* 3.81.22–23: Rama Jamadagnya's lakes of blood are in the CE and are kept as the text gives them.
* 3.81.50–51 *śvānalomāpaha*: "the remover of dog's hair"; the text does not explain the name.
* 3.81.55 *krośamātre*: "a kos", about two miles.
* 3.81.56 *śyāmākabhojanam*: "a meal of shyamaka millet".
* 3.81.61 *antardhānam*: "the power to vanish".
* 3.81.81–82: Vyasa's grief for his son is mentioned here and not told.
* 3.81.98 *śākarasaḥ*: "vegetable juice", which flowed instead of blood.
* 3.81.133 *darbhiṇā*: Darbhin, a seer.
* 3.81.143 *guhaḥ*: Guha, Skanda, "consecrated as general of the gods".
* 3.81.178 *samantapañcaka*: the Samantapanchaka, the name of this land in the frame of Book 1, where the five lakes of blood are also told (1.2.1–7).

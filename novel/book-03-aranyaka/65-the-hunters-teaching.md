summary: The hunter of Mithila goes on teaching Kaushika; dharma is subtle, and a lie may be truth when it serves the good of creatures; deeds bear fruit, the self does not die with the body but passes on bound by its deeds; the senses, mind and understanding lead a man to greed or to dharma; he explains the five great elements and their qualities, the three strands of nature, the breaths and fire within the body, and how the wise, calm in mind, see the self and are freed.

# The Hunter's Teaching

"Then the righteous hunter, Yudhishthira," said Markandeya, "best of all who
uphold dharma, spoke skilfully again to that bull among brahmins.

"'Dharma has the Veda as its authority, the old say. But the way of dharma is
subtle, many-branched and endless. When life is at stake, and at a wedding, a
lie may be spoken. A lie may be truth, and truth may be a lie. What is most for
the good of creatures, that is truth: that is the rule. The opposite is adharma.
See how subtle dharma is. Whatever deed a man does, bad or good, best of the
twice-born, he surely reaps it. There is no doubt of it. When he falls into a
hard state, the fool blames the gods bitterly, and does not know the faults of
his own deeds. When the turn of happiness and sorrow comes on the fool, the
deceitful man and the fickle one, best of the twice-born, neither wisdom, nor
good counsel, nor manliness saves him.

"'If the fruit of a man's work did not depend on another, whoever wanted
anything would get it as he wished. But restrained men, skilled and wise, are
seen to fail, stripped of the fruit of all their work. Another man, always busy
harming creatures and cheating the world, lives here in comfort. Fortune comes
to one who sits and does nothing, and another, doing his work, does not get what
he should. Wretched people, greedy for sons, worship the gods and practise
austerity, and children carried ten months in the womb are born to disgrace the
family. Others are born into great wealth, grain and pleasures heaped up by
their fathers, won by those same blessings.

"'Men's diseases are born of their deeds, no doubt of it. They are tormented by
troubles of the mind as small beasts are by hunters. And their diseases are kept
off by skilled and clever physicians who have gathered their herbs, as beasts
are kept off by hunters, brahmin. Look, best of those who uphold dharma: those
who have food to eat, suffering from disorders of the belly, cannot eat it. Many
others, strong in their arms, suffer, and win their food with pain, best of the
twice-born. So the world, helpless, flooded with delusion and grief, is thrown
again and again by the current and carried off by one stronger. If there were
mastery, none would die or grow old. All would have all they wished and would
see nothing unpleasant.

"'Everyone strives to rise above the world. He tries as hard as he can, and it
does not turn out so. Many are seen with the same stars and the same omens, and
a great difference in the fruit is seen when the deeds come together. No one is
master of what he would take for himself, brahmin, good man. Success here is
seen to come from deeds done before. As the scripture says, brahmin, the living
self is eternal, and the body in this world is not lasting for any creature
here. When the body is struck down, the body perishes. But the living self
passes elsewhere, tied by the bonds of its deeds.'

"'How is the living self eternal, best of those who uphold dharma?' said the
brahmin. 'I want to know this truly, best of speakers.'

"'The living self does not perish when the body breaks,' said the hunter. 'Fools
say falsely that it dies. The living self goes on into another body. The
breaking of the body is only its going back to the five. No one else eats the
fruit of a deed done. The doer alone shares in its joy and sorrow. Whatever deed
he has done, he eats its fruit. What is done does not perish. Those of good
conduct grow in merit, and those who do evil go on doing evil. A man is followed
here by his own deeds, and shaped by them he is born again.'

"'How is he born in a womb?' said the brahmin. 'And how does he go to holy
births or unholy ones, through merit and evil, good man?'

"'This work is seen to go with the placing of the seed in the womb,' said the
hunter. 'I will tell you briefly and quickly, best of the twice-born. As he has
gathered his store, so he is born again: the doer of good in good births, the
doer of evil in evil births. By good works he becomes a god, by mixed ones a
man, by those that bewilder he goes to the births of beasts, and by sins he goes
down below. Always beset by the sorrows of birth, death and old age, a man is
cooked in the turning of the world by the faults he has made himself. The living
selves turn about through thousands of animal births and through hell, tied by
the bonds of their deeds.

"'A creature made wretched after death by the deeds he has done himself takes an
unholy birth to wear out that sorrow. Then he takes on again many other new
deeds, and is cooked again by them, like a sick man who has eaten what does not
suit him. He is always tormented by sorrow, and when he is not in sorrow he
calls it happiness. Then, because his bonds are not undone and his deeds keep
rising, he goes round and round in the world like a wheel, full of pain. But if
his bonds are undone and he is made pure by his deeds, he reaches the worlds of
those who do good, where those who go grieve no more. The man who does evil and
lives by evil does not reach the end of evil. So one should strive to do good
and avoid wrongdoing.

"'A man free of envy and grateful, who does only what is good, wins happiness,
dharma, wealth and heaven. The wise man, disciplined, self-controlled,
restrained, with his self held in, goes straight on here and in the next world.
One should live by the dharma of the good and act as the cultured do. One should
seek one's livelihood without harming the world, brahmin. There are cultured men
who have come to knowledge and are skilled in the scriptures. When work in the
world is done by one's own dharma, there is no mixing of works. The wise man
delights in dharma and lives by dharma. Of the wealth he wins by dharma, best of
the twice-born, he waters the root where he sees virtues. So he becomes
righteous, and his mind grows calm. He is content with his friends and rejoices
here and hereafter.

"'Sound and touch, form, and the scents he likes, good man, and lordship too he
gains. They know that as the fruit of dharma. But having won the fruit of
dharma, great brahmin, he is not satisfied. Not satisfied, he grows weary of it,
with the eye of knowledge. With the eye of wisdom, a man here does not cling to
fault. He turns away from desire as he will, and does not let go of dharma.
Seeing that the world is made to perish, he strives to give up everything. Then
he strives for release, by the right means and not by wrong ones. So he grows
weary of the world and gives up evil deeds. He becomes righteous and wins the
highest release. Austerity is the highest good for a creature, and its root is
calm and self-control. By it he wins all the wishes he desires in his mind. By
restraining the senses, by truth and self-control, he reaches the highest place
of brahman, best of the twice-born.'

"'Which are the senses they speak of, you of restrained vows?' said the brahmin.
'How are they to be restrained, and what is the fruit of restraining them? And
how does one win their fruit, best of those who uphold dharma? I want to know
this dharma truly, most righteous one.'

---

"When the brahmin had said this, Yudhishthira," said Markandeya, "the righteous
hunter answered him. Hear what he said, lord of men.

"'The mind first goes out to know,' said the hunter. 'When it has reached a
thing, it turns to desire and to anger, best of the twice-born. Then it strives
for that, and begins great undertakings, and practises over and over the forms
and scents it likes. Then passion arises, and hatred after it. Then greed
arises, and delusion after it. The understanding of one overcome by greed and
struck by passion and hatred does not turn to dharma, and he practises dharma
only as a pretence. He follows dharma as a pretence and likes wealth won by
pretence, and when wealth comes to him by pretence, best of the twice-born, his
understanding delights in it, and then he wants to do evil. When his friends and
the learned hold him back, best of the twice-born, he gives an answer tied to
scripture and yoked to scripture. His threefold adharma grows through the fault
of passion. He thinks evil, speaks it and does it. As he goes on in adharma his
good qualities perish, and evildoers of like character make friends with him. So
he comes to unhappiness, and hereafter he is struck down. So he becomes evil in
himself.

"'Now hear from me how dharma is gained. The one who sees these faults
beforehand by his wisdom, who is skilled in joys and sorrows and waits on the
good: by his dealings with the good, his understanding turns to dharma.'

"'You speak true and pleasant dharma, such as no one else speaks,' said the
brahmin. 'I think you are a seer of divine power, very great.'

"'Brahmins are greatly blessed,' said the hunter, 'Fathers who always eat first.
A wise man in the world must do what pleases them with his whole self. I will
tell you what pleases them, best of the twice-born. Bowing to the brahmins,
learn from me the knowledge of brahman. This whole universe, the world,
unconquerable in every way, is made of the great elements, brahmin. There is
nothing beyond this. The great elements are space, wind, fire, water and earth,
and their qualities are sound, touch, form, taste and smell. They have all their
qualities, and the qualities work on one another. In the three that have
qualities, the qualities of each before are all present in turn. The sixth is
awareness, called the mind. The seventh is the understanding, and after it the
sense of self. The five senses, and passion, goodness and darkness: this group
of seventeen is called the unmanifest. Wrapped all round by all the objects of
the senses, manifest and unmanifest, this makes twenty-four. That is the quality
made of the manifest and the unmanifest. I have told you all this. What more do
you want to hear?'

---

"At the righteous hunter's words, Bharata," said Markandeya, "the brahmin took
up the talk again, which gladdened his heart.

"'The five great elements they speak of, best of those who know dharma,' said
the brahmin. 'Tell me well the qualities of each of the five.'

"'Earth, water, fire, wind and space,' said the hunter, 'each has more qualities
than the one after. I will tell you their qualities. Earth has five qualities,
brahmin, water four, fire three, and wind and space three together. Sound,
touch, form and taste, and smell as the fifth: these five qualities are earth's,
richer in qualities than all the rest. Sound, touch, form and taste, best of the
twice-born, are told as the qualities of water, brahmin of good vows. Sound,
touch and form are the three qualities of fire. Sound and touch are in wind, and
sound in space. These fifteen qualities, brahmin, are in the five elements. They
are in all creatures, in which the worlds are founded. They do not go beyond one
another, and there is harmony, brahmin. But when things moving and unmoving fall
out of balance, then in time the embodied one takes another body. They perish in
order and are born in order. Here and there the elements made of the five are
seen, by which this whole world, moving and unmoving, is covered.

"'Whatever is put forth by the senses is called the manifest. What is beyond the
senses, grasped only by its signs, is to be known as the unmanifest. These
senses grasp sound and the rest, each its own. When the embodied one, holding
the senses here, burns with austerity, he sees the self spread through the world
and the world in the self. Knowing the high and the low, though still attached,
he sees all creatures. For one who has become brahman, who sees all creatures in
every state and always, there is no joining with evil. When he has passed beyond
the suffering born of delusion, whose root is knowledge, the world is seen by
the light of understanding, on the road of what is to be known. The wise blessed
one has said that the creature is without beginning or end, born of itself,
always unchanging, beyond compare and without form. All this you ask me about,
brahmin, has austerity as its root.

"'The senses are all of it, heaven and hell both. Restrained, they lead to
heaven. Let loose, they lead to hell. This is the whole method of yoga: the
holding of the senses. This is the root of all austerity, and of hell too.
Through attachment to the senses a man comes to fault, no doubt of it. But
restraining them, he wins success. The man who wins lordship over the six that
are always in the self, who has conquered his senses, is not joined to sins, let
alone to misfortunes. The body of a man is seen as a chariot, the self as the
driver, and the senses, they say, as the horses. With those good horses well
tamed, the watchful, skilled and steady man goes happily, like a charioteer. The
steady man who holds the reins of the six senses always in the self, those
tormentors, is the best of charioteers. When the senses are let loose, like
horses on the roads, one should use steadiness as the charioteer. By steadiness
he will surely conquer them. For the mind that follows the wandering senses
carries off his understanding, as the wind carries off a boat on the water. When
men are bewildered by the six as they reach their fruit, the one who has settled
his mind on them and meditates wins the fruit born of meditation.'

---

"When the subtle matter had been told by the righteous hunter, Bharata," said
Markandeya, "the brahmin, very attentive, asked again about subtle things.

"'Tell me truly the qualities of goodness, of passion and of darkness,' said the
brahmin, 'as they really are, since I ask.'

"'Yes, I will tell you what you ask,' said the hunter. 'Learn their qualities
one by one as I tell them. Darkness is the one made of delusion. Passion is the
one that sets things going. Because light is greatest in it, goodness is called
the highest here. The dark man is full of ignorance, deluded, given to sleep,
without sense, hard to look at, overcome by darkness, angry and lazy. The man of
passion is ready of speech, a counsellor, full of attachment, envious, eager to
know, brahmin seer, stiff and proud. The man of goodness is full of light,
steady, not eager to know, free of envy, free of anger, wise and
self-controlled.

"'The man of goodness, when he has woken up, is troubled by the ways of the
world. When he understands what is to be understood, he loathes the ways of the
world. The form of dispassion comes first in him. His sense of self grows soft,
and his straightness becomes clear. Then all the pairs of opposites in him quiet
one another, and there is no such thing as restraint for him anywhere. One born
of a shudra womb who attends to good qualities comes to be a vaishya, brahmin,
and a kshatriya too. And one who lives in straightness is born a brahmin. I have
told you all the qualities. What more do you want to hear?'

"'How does the fire in the body come to be, when it has reached the earthly
element?' said the brahmin. 'And how does the wind move according to the space
it has?'

"This question the brahmin put, Yudhishthira," said Markandeya, "the hunter
explained to the great brahmin.

"'The fire rests in the head, guarding the body,' said the hunter. 'The breath
called prana moves, dwelling in the head and in the fire. Past, present and
future all rest on prana. We worship that best of creatures, the light of
brahman. That creature is the self of all creatures, that Person, the eternal.
It is mind, understanding and the sense of self, and the objects of the
elements. So here it is guarded everywhere by prana, and behind, by samana, each
keeps to its own way. At the root of the bladder and in the anus the fire rests,
and the breath apana moves, carrying urine and faeces. The one that works in
three, in effort, in action and in strength, those who know the self call udana.
The wind that is set in every joint of the bodies of men is taught as vyana. The
fire is spread through the elements of the body, and driven by the wind it runs
about, working the juices, the elements and the humours.

"'From the meeting of the breaths comes a union. The heat is to be known as the
fire that cooks the food of the embodied. Prana and vyana are set between apana
and udana. Joined with them, the fire cooks its seat well. Its extent reaches to
the anus and is called by that name. From it the channels arise in all the
breaths of the embodied. Prana, carrying the force of the fire, strikes against
the end of the anus, and rising again it throws the fire upwards. The place of
cooked food is below the navel, and the place of raw food above. All the breaths
are set in the middle of the body at the navel. Starting from the heart, the ten
channels, driven by the breaths, run sideways, upwards and downwards, carrying
the juices of food.

"'This is the road of yogis by which they go to the highest. The steady ones,
who have conquered weariness and their seats, placed the self in the head. So
prana and apana are spread through all embodied beings. Know that the self, made
of the eleven changes and gathered from the stores of the parts, has a body,
always conquered by deeds. The fire that stands in it, always set there as in a
pot, know as the self, always conquered by yoga. The god who stands in it like a
drop of water on a lotus leaf, know as the knower of the field, always conquered
by giving up. Know that passion, goodness and darkness belong to the living
self, that the living self is a quality of the self, and the self belongs to the
supreme self. They say the conscious is a quality of the living self. It moves
and makes all things move. Beyond that, the knowers of the field say, is the one
who made the seven worlds.

"'So in all creatures the self of beings does not shine out. But it is seen by
the knowers of knowledge with the highest, subtle understanding. By the calm of
the mind a man destroys good and evil deeds. With his self calm, standing in the
self, he wins endless happiness. The mark of calm is this: as one who is
satisfied sleeps happily, or as a lamp lit by a skilled hand burns where there
is no wind. In the first part of the night and the last, always yoking his mind,
eating little, pure in himself, seeing the self in the self, he sees with the
lamp of the mind as with a blazing lamp. Seeing the self without self, he is
then set free.

"'By every means greed and anger must be restrained. This is the purification of
sacrifices, and austerity is held to be the crossing. One should always guard
austerity from anger, fortune from envy, learning from honour and dishonour, and
the self from carelessness. Gentleness is the highest dharma and patience the
highest strength. Knowledge of the self is the highest knowledge, and the vow of
truth the highest vow. Speaking the truth is best, and knowing the truth is
good. What is most for the good of creatures is held to be the highest truth.
The man whose undertakings are always free of the bonds of hope, who has offered
everything in the fire of giving up, is the one who gives up, and he is wise.
When not even a teacher can move him from it, though he tries, know that as the
yoga of brahman, the parting called yoga. One should harm no creature, and
should live in friendship. Having come into this life, one should make no enmity
with anyone. Having nothing, full contentment, freedom from hope and from
restlessness: this is the highest knowledge, the best knowledge of the self.

"'Give up possessions and be restrained in your vows by understanding. Stand in
the place without grief, unmoving, hereafter and here. The sage who is always
given to austerity, self-controlled, with his self restrained, wanting to
conquer the unconquered, must be unattached among attachments. Unattached to
quality or lack of quality, with one thing to do and nothing between: this,
brahmin, they call your conduct, the happy single step. The man who gives up
both sorrow and joy reaches brahman for ever, and goes by being unattached. I
have told you all this briefly, best of the twice-born, as I have heard it. What
more do you want to hear?'"

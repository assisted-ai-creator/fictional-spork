# Into the Mountains

"You have passed Ushirabija, Mainaka, the White Mountain and the Black Mountain,
son of Kunti, lord of the earth," said Lomasha. "Here the Ganga shines in seven
streams, bull of the Bharatas. This is the lovely, spotless place where fire is
always kindled. No man can even see it today. Be still and calm, and you will
see these holy places. We will enter the White Mountain and the mountain
Mandara, where the yaksha Manichara lives, and Kubera, king of the yakshas.

"Eighty-eight thousand swift gandharvas, and as many kimpurushas and four times
as many yakshas, of many shapes and forms, with weapons of every kind, attend
Manibhadra, lord of the yakshas, best of men. Their power is very great, and
they are as swift as the wind. They could surely throw even the king of the gods
from his place. These mountains are guarded by those mighty ones and protected
by sorcerers, and they are hard to cross, son of Pritha. Be very watchful. There
are other rakshasas too, Kubera's counsellors, some fierce and some friendly. We
shall meet them, son of Kunti. Be ready to show courage. The mountain Kailasa is
six hundred leagues high, king, where the gods gather, and where Vishala is,
Bharata. The yakshas, rakshasas and kinnaras, the snakes, suparnas and
gandharvas going to Kubera's palace are beyond counting, son of Kunti. Pass
through them today, son of Pritha, by austerity and self-control, protected by
me, king, and by Bhimasena's strength.

"May King Varuna give you good fortune, and Yama, conqueror in battle, and the
Ganga, and the Yamuna, and the mountain. Goddess Ganga, I hear your roar on the
golden peak of Indra's mountain. Guard from the mountains, fortunate one, this
king, honoured by all the Ajamidhas. Be a shelter to him as he goes into these
peaks."

"This is a fear I have not seen in Lomasha before," said Yudhishthira. "All of
you, guard Krishna. Do not be careless. He thinks this country is very hard to
cross. So practise the greatest purity here."

Then he said to Bhima of noble courage, "Guard Krishna carefully, Bhimasena.
With Arjuna away and not with us, my son, you are the one Krishna turns to in
hardship." Then the great king went to the twins, kissed their heads, stroked
their bodies, and said to them, his voice thick with tears, "Do not be afraid.
Come, and take care."

---

"Hidden beings and mighty rakshasas can be passed only by fire and austerity,
Vrikodara," said Yudhishthira. "Hold back your hunger and thirst, son of Kunti,
by strength of will. Then rely on strength and skill, upholder of the Kurus. You
heard the seer's words about Mount Kailasa. Think it over, son of Kunti. How
will Krishna go? Or else, Bhima, go back with Sahadeva and Dhaumya, lord, with
the cooks and stewards and all the servants, with the chariots and horses, and
the brahmins who cannot bear the hardships of the road, long-eyed one. Three of
us will go on, eating little, keeping our vows: I, Nakula and the great ascetic
Lomasha. Wait here at Gangadvara, calm, waiting for me to come back, guarding
Draupadi until I return."

"The princess is worn with weariness, Bharata, and with sorrow," said Bhima.
"Yet the good woman keeps going, longing to see the rider of white horses. Your
own unhappiness grows sharper, not seeing him, and how much more Sahadeva's, and
mine, and Krishna's, Bharata. Let the chariots go back if you wish, and all the
servants, the cooks and stewards, as you think best. I will never leave you
here, on this mountain full of rakshasas, in its rough and dangerous places. And
this blessed princess, firm in her vows, would not want to turn back without
you, tiger among men. And Sahadeva here, who always follows you, would never
turn back. I know his mind. And, great king, we are all longing to see
Savyasachin. So we will go together. If this mountain, with its many caves,
cannot be crossed by chariots, we will go on foot. Do not be downcast, king. I
will carry the Panchali wherever she cannot go. That is my resolve. Do not be
downcast, king. And if the two heroes, tender as they are, the joy of Madri,
cannot manage, I will carry them over the rough places."

"May your strength grow, Bhima, as you speak like this," said Yudhishthira, "you
who dare to carry Draupadi on the long road, and the twins too, bless you. No
one else could do it. May your strength, your fame, your dharma and your glory
grow, since you dare to bring your two brothers and Krishna. Let no weariness
come to you, strong-armed one, and no defeat."

Then Krishna laughed, and said lightly, "I will go. Do not worry about me,
Bharata."

"The mountain Gandhamadana can be reached by austerity," said Lomasha. "We will
all give ourselves to austerity, son of Kunti. Nakula, Sahadeva and Bhimasena,
king, and I and you, son of Kunti, will see the rider of white horses."

As they talked like this, king, they came gladly to the great country of Subahu
on the Himalaya, rich in elephants and horses. It was crowded with Kiratas and
Tanganas, full of hundreds of Kunindas, loved by the immortals, full of many
wonders. Subahu, lord of the Kunindas, saw them and welcomed them with honour
and affection at the border of his kingdom. Honoured by him, they all stayed
there happily, and when the sun was clear they set out towards the Himalaya. The
great chariot fighters left with the king of the Kunindas the servants led by
Indrasena, the stewards and cooks, and all Draupadi's things, king. The mighty
joy of the Kauravas went on foot. Slowly all the Pandavas set out from that
country with Krishna, full of joy, longing to see Dhananjaya.

---

"Bhimasena and the twins, and Panchali, listen," said Yudhishthira. "What a
creature has done is not lost. See us, wanderers in the forest. We say to one
another, 'We are weak, we are worn out,' and yet we go where we cannot go,
longing to see Dhananjaya. It burns my limbs as fire burns a heap of cotton,
that I do not see the hero Dhananjaya near me. As I live in the forest with my
younger brothers, thirsting to see him, the memory of the insult to Yajnaseni
burns me too, hero. I do not see Partha, the elder brother of Nakula, of
boundless power, unconquered, the fierce bowman, and so I suffer, Vrikodara.

"I wander with you through lovely fords and forests and lakes, longing to see
him. For five years I have not seen the hero Dhananjaya, true to his word,
Bibhatsu, and so I suffer, Vrikodara. I do not see the dark, strong-armed
Gudakesha, who walks like a lion, and so I suffer, Vrikodara. I do not see that
best of men, master of weapons, skilled in battle, the model for all bowmen, and
so I suffer, Vrikodara. Dhananjaya ranges among the hosts of enemies like Time,
angry, like the Ender, like an elephant in rut, with the shoulders of a lion.
Partha, the elder brother of the twins, rider of white horses, of boundless
courage, is no less than Shakra in courage and in wealth. Overwhelmed by a great
sorrow that I brought on myself and cannot undo, I do not see Phalguna, the
unconquered, the fierce bowman.

"He is always patient, even when a lesser man insults him. To one who walks the
straight road he gives shelter and freedom from fear. But to one who acts
crookedly and tries to kill by trickery, even to the wielder of the thunderbolt,
he would be like a deadly poison. Even to an enemy who comes to him for refuge,
that mighty one, Bibhatsu of boundless spirit, is kind, and gives him freedom
from fear. He is the refuge of us all, the crusher of enemies in battle, the
bringer of every jewel, the bringer of all our happiness. By his courage, hero,
I had many divine jewels of every kind long ago, which Suyodhana has now taken.
By the strength of his arms, hero, I had the hall, made of every jewel, famous
in the three worlds, Pandava. He is equal to Vasudeva in courage, equal to
Kartavirya in battle, unconquered and unbeaten in war, and I do not see
Phalguna. The slayer of enemies follows mighty Samkarshana, and you, Bhima the
unconquered, and Vasudeva in courage. In strength of arm and in power he is
equal to Purandara, in speed to the wind, in face to the moon, in anger to
eternal Death.

"All of us long to see that tiger among men, and we will enter the mountain
Gandhamadana, strong-armed one, where wide Badari is, the hermitage of Nara and
Narayana. We will see that fine mountain where yakshas always live. We will go
on foot to Kubera's lovely lotus pool, guarded by rakshasas, practising great
austerity. No one who has not practised austerity can go to that country,
Vrikodara, nor anyone cruel, greedy or restless, Bharata. We will all go there,
Bhima, seeking Arjuna's footsteps, with our weapons and our swords bound on,
with the brahmins of great vows. A man who is not restrained meets flies, gnats,
gadflies, tigers, lions and snakes, Partha. One who is restrained does not see
them. So, restrained, eating sparingly, we will enter the mountain Gandhamadana,
longing to see Dhananjaya."

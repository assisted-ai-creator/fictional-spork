# The Bridegroom Choice

"Then, when the good time came, on a holy day and at a holy hour," said
Brihadashva, "King Bhima called the lords of the earth to the bridegroom choice.
When they heard it, all the lords of the earth, tormented by love and longing
for Damayanti, came quickly. The kings entered the great arena, bright with
golden pillars and a shining gateway, like lions entering a mountain. The lords
of the earth sat there on seats of every kind, all wearing fragrant garlands and
polished jewelled earrings. That gathering of kings was full of tigers among
men, like Bhogavati full of snakes, like a mountain cave full of tigers. Their
arms could be seen there, thick as iron bars, well shaped and very smooth, like
five-headed snakes. The kings' faces shone, with their fine hair and fine noses,
lovely and handsome, like the stars in the sky.

"Then Damayanti, with her lovely face, came into the arena, stealing the eyes
and the hearts of the kings with her beauty. The eyes of those great men fell on
her limbs, and wherever they fell they stuck there and did not move. Then, as
the names of the kings were proclaimed, Bharata, Bhima's daughter saw five men
who all looked the same. When the princess of Vidarbha looked at them all
standing there, not one different from the others, she was in doubt and could
not tell which was King Nala. Whichever of them she looked at, she thought was
King Nala.

"The lovely woman thought hard, and reasoned with herself. 'How can I know the
gods? How can I tell King Nala?' Thinking like this, in great distress, the
princess of Vidarbha remembered the signs of the gods she had heard of, Bharata.
'The signs of the gods that I heard of from the old people, I cannot see in a
single one of those standing here on the ground.'

"She decided, turning it over in her mind again and again, and she saw that the
time had come to go to the gods for refuge. She bowed to the gods in word and in
thought, joined her hands, and trembling, said this. 'I heard the geese, and I
chose the Naishadha for my husband. By that truth, let the gods show him to me.
In word and in thought I have never strayed from him. By that truth, gods, let
them show him to me. The gods themselves made the lord of the Nishadhas my
husband. By that truth, let the gods show him to me. Let the guardians of the
world, with their lords, take on their own forms, so that I may know the king of
good name.'

"When the gods heard Damayanti's pitiful lament, Bharata, they saw her firm
resolve, her truthfulness, her love for the Naishadha, the purity of her heart,
her understanding, her devotion and her passion. They did as she had asked, and
showed their signs as well as they could. She saw all the gods without sweat,
their eyes unblinking, their garlands fresh and free of dust, standing without
touching the ground. And the Naishadha was shown by his shadow, his wilting
garland, the dust and sweat on him, his standing on the ground, and the blinking
of his eyes.

"When she had looked at the gods and at the king of good name, Bharata, Bhima's
daughter chose the Naishadha, keeping to dharma. The long-eyed girl, lovely,
shyly took hold of the hem of his garment and laid a very beautiful garland on
his shoulders, and so chose him for her husband. At that the kings suddenly
cried out, 'Alas! Alas!' And the gods and the great seers, amazed, cried 'Well
done! Well done!', Bharata, praising King Nala.

"When Bhima's daughter had chosen the Naishadha, the guardians of the world, of
great power, were all glad at heart, and they gave Nala eight boons. Shakra, the
husband of Shachi, pleased, gave the Naishadha the power to see him in person at
sacrifices, and the highest and happiest way hereafter. Agni gave him his own
presence wherever the Naishadha wished, and the fire-eater gave him worlds
shining with their own light. Yama gave him the taste of food, and the highest
firmness in dharma. The lord of the waters gave him water wherever the Naishadha
wished, and a garland rich with the finest scent. So each of them gave him two
boons. When they had given him these boons, the gods went to heaven.

"The kings, when they had seen Damayanti's marriage, went back the way they had
come, amazed and glad. And when he had won that jewel of a woman, the king of
good name took his pleasure with her, as the killer of Bala and Vritra does with
Shachi. The king, full of joy, shining like the sun, delighted his people, the
hero, and protected them by dharma. The wise king sacrificed with the horse
sacrifice, like Yayati, son of Nahusha, and with many other rites with rich
fees. And Nala, like an immortal, took his pleasure with Damayanti in lovely
forests and groves. So, sacrificing and taking his pleasure, the lord of the
earth guarded the earth, full of wealth.

---

"When Bhima's daughter had chosen the Naishadha, the guardians of the world, of
great power, as they went away, saw Dvapara coming, with Kali. Then Shakra, the
killer of Bala and Vritra, looked at Kali and said, 'Kali, where are you going
with Dvapara as your companion? Tell me.'

"Kali said to Shakra, 'I am going to Damayanti's bridegroom choice, and I will
choose her. For my heart has gone to her.'

"Indra laughed and said to him, 'That bridegroom choice is over. She chose King
Nala for her husband, in our presence.'

"When Shakra said this, Kali was filled with anger. He called to all the gods
and said, 'Since she has found a man for her husband in the midst of the gods,
surely it is right that she be punished heavily.'

"When Kali said this, the dwellers in heaven answered, 'Nala was chosen by
Damayanti with our consent. What woman would not turn to King Nala, who has
every virtue? He knows all the laws of dharma, just as they are, and he keeps
his vows. In him, tiger among men, a king equal to the guardians of the world,
truth, steadiness, generosity, austerity, purity, self-control and calm are
firmly fixed. The fool who would wish to curse a Nala such as this, Kali, would
curse himself and kill himself by his own hand. He would sink in hell, hard,
bottomless and wide, where there is no raft.' When they had said this to Kali
and Dvapara, the gods went to heaven.

"When the gods had gone, Kali said to Dvapara, 'I cannot hold back my anger. I
will live in Nala, Dvapara. I will cast him from his kingdom, and he will not
take his pleasure with Bhima's daughter. You too must enter the dice and help
me.'

---

"When Kali had made this agreement with Dvapara, he came to where the Naishadha
king was. He lived among the Nishadhas for a long time, always watching for a
lapse. Then, in the twelfth year, Kali saw his chance. The Naishadha had passed
water, touched water, and sat down to his twilight prayers without washing his
feet. There Kali entered him.

"When he had entered Nala, he went to Nala's brother Pushkara and said, 'Come
and play dice with Nala. With my help you will beat Nala at the dice. Beat King
Nala, king, and take the Nishadhas.'

"When Kali said this, Pushkara went to Nala. And Kali became the bull, the best
of the dice, and went with Pushkara. Pushkara, the killer of enemy heroes, came
to the hero Nala, and his brother said to him again and again, 'Let us play,
with the bull!' The great-hearted king could not bear the challenge, with the
princess of Vidarbha looking on, and he thought it the time for staking.
Possessed by Kali, Nala lost at the game his gold and silver, his carriages and
teams, and his clothes. He was mad with the frenzy of the dice, and none of his
friends could stop him as he played, out of his mind.

"Then all the people of the city, with the ministers, came to see the king,
Bharata, to stop him in his distress. The charioteer came to Damayanti and told
her, 'All the people of the city are standing at the door, with business to do.
Let the Naishadha be told that all his subjects are here. They cannot bear the
disaster that has come to the king who sees into dharma and wealth.'

"Then Bhima's daughter, worn with grief, her mind struck by sorrow, spoke to the
Naishadha in a voice thick with tears. 'King, the people of the city are
standing at the door, wanting to see you, with all your ministers, out of their
loyalty to the king. You should see them.' She said it again and again. But the
king, possessed by Kali, said nothing to her, lovely-eyed and slim-waisted as
she was, as she wept. Then all the ministers and the people of the city,
grieving and ashamed, said, 'He is not himself,' and went home.

"So the game between Pushkara and Nala went on, Yudhishthira, for many months,
and the king of good name kept losing.

---

"Then Damayanti saw the king of good name like a madman, though he was not mad,
his mind gone into the game. Bhima's daughter was filled with fear and sorrow,
king, and thought hard about what she must do for the king. Fearing the evil
that might come to him, and wanting to do what was good for him, and seeing that
Nala had lost everything, she said this. 'Brihatsena, go and bring the
ministers, at Nala's command. Tell them what wealth has been lost and what is
left.'

"When all the ministers heard it was Nala's command, they said, 'Perhaps our
luck has turned,' and came back. Bhima's daughter told him that all his subjects
were there a second time, but he took no pleasure in it. When she saw that her
husband took no pleasure in her words, Damayanti went back into the house,
ashamed.

"She heard that the dice went always against the king of good name, and that
Nala had lost everything, and she spoke to her nurse again. 'Brihatsena, go
again, at Nala's command, and bring the charioteer Varshneya, good woman.
Something very grave is at hand.'

"When Brihatsena heard Damayanti's words, she had Varshneya brought by trusted
men. Then Bhima's daughter, blameless, who knew what suited the place and the
time, spoke gently to Varshneya, soothing him, as the moment required.

"'You know how the king has always treated you well. Now that he is in trouble,
you should help him. The more the king is beaten here by Pushkara, the more his
passion for the game grows. The dice are in Pushkara's power, and I see that
they go against Nala. He does not listen properly to the words of his friends
and his own people. Surely, I think, nothing will be left of the great
Naishadha, since the king, in his madness, takes no pleasure in my words. I come
to you for refuge, charioteer. Do as I say. My heart has no peace. He may be
ruined at any time. Yoke Nala's favourite horses, the swift ones, put the two
children in the chariot, and go to Kundina. Leave the children with my kin, and
the chariot and these horses, and then stay there if you wish, or go somewhere
else.'

"Varshneya, Nala's charioteer, told Damayanti's words in full to Nala's chief
ministers. When he had met with them and decided, lord of the earth, and had
their leave, he put the children in the chariot and drove with it to the
Vidarbhas. There the charioteer left the horses and that fine chariot, and the
girl Indrasena and the boy Indrasena. He took his leave of King Bhima, and in
sorrow, grieving for King Nala, he wandered until he came to the city of
Ayodhya. There, very unhappy, he went to King Rituparna and took service with
him as his charioteer, lord of the earth."

**Divergences.**

* **The children's birth.** The vulgate says that Nala had a son Indrasena and a daughter
  Indrasena by Damayanti (\*229, after 3.54.37). The CE does not narrate the birth. The
  children appear first as "the two children" (*mithunam*, 3.57.17) and are named when they
  reach Vidarbha (3.57.21).
* **Kali's look.** An appendix passage (@9, after 3.55.1) describes Kali with a pot of wine,
  meat and dogs. Not in the CE.
* **The bull.** In the CE it is Kali who becomes "the bull", the best of the dice (3.56.6).
  Ganguli's vulgate gives it to Dvapara (G 3.59).
* **The marriage and the return home.** Rejected lines (\*226–\*228) describe the wedding rites
  and Nala's return to his own city. The CE passes straight to his reign (3.54.33–38).

**Choices.**

* 3.54.5 *bhogavatīm*: Bhogavati, the city of the snakes.
* 3.54.17–19: Damayanti's three oaths "by that truth" are kept as three.
* 3.54.24 *nimeṣeṇa ca sūcitaḥ*: "the blinking of his eyes".
* 3.54.27 *sahasā*: "suddenly".
* 3.54.29 *pratyakṣadarśanaṃ yajñe*: "to see him in person at sacrifices".
* 3.54.30 *ātmabhavam*: "his own presence", fire appearing wherever Nala wished.
* 3.54.31 *annarasam*: "the taste of food".
* 3.54.32 *sarve ca mithunaṃ daduḥ*: "each of them gave him two boons", with Ganguli.
* 3.56.3: Kali's lapse is kept as the text gives it: Nala sat to his twilight prayers without
  washing his feet.
* 3.56.6 *vṛṣo … gavām*: "the bull, the best of the dice"; literally "the bull among cows".
* 3.56.17 *nāyam asti*: "He is not himself"; literally "he is not".
* 3.57.4 *bṛhatsene*: Brihatsena, Damayanti's nurse (3.57.8 *dhātrīm*).
* 3.57.9 *vārṣṇeyam*: Varshneya, Nala's charioteer.
* 3.57.17 *kuṇḍinam*: Kundina, the city of the Vidarbhas.

# The Sun's Warning

"Great brahmin," said Janamejaya, "Lomasha came to Yudhishthira son of Pandu at
Indra's bidding and spoke these words. 'And the sharp fear you have and never
speak of anywhere, I shall take that away too, when Savyasachin has come back
here.' What was that great fear of Karna, best of the learned, which the Dharma
King, whose self was dharma, never told anyone?"

I shall tell you this story, tiger among kings, since you ask, best of the
Bharatas. Listen to my words.

When the twelfth year had passed and the thirteenth had come, Shakra, who worked
for the good of the Pandavas, made ready to beg from Karna. Knowing great
Indra's purpose, great king, the Sun, the shining one, came to Karna for the
sake of the earrings. The hero was lying on a costly bed spread with fine
coverings, sleeping trustfully, kind to brahmins and truthful. At night, at the
end of a dream, lord of kings, the rayed one showed himself, filled with the
greatest pity, and with a father's love, Bharata. Having taken by yoga the form
of a handsome brahmin who knew the Veda, the Sun spoke to Karna gently, for his
good.

"Karna, my son, best of those who keep the truth, listen to what I tell you
today out of friendship, strong-armed one, for your highest good. Shakra will
come to you, for the good of the Pandavas, disguised as a brahmin, Karna,
wanting to take your earrings. He knows your nature, and so does the whole
world: that when the good beg from you, you give, and do not beg yourself. For
you give, my son, to brahmins when they ask, wealth or anything else they speak
of, and you never refuse. Knowing you to be like this, the chastiser of Paka
himself will come to beg your earrings and your armour. When he begs, you must
not give him your earrings. You must win him over as best you can, for this is
your highest good.

"When he speaks of the earrings, my son, you must turn him back again and again
with many reasons, and with wealth of many other kinds. When Purandara wants
your earrings, offer him jewels, women, pleasures and riches of many kinds, and
give him many examples. If you give away your beautiful earrings that were born
with you, Karna, your life will run out and you will come under the power of
death. With your armour and your earrings, giver of honour, you cannot be killed
by enemies in battle. Know that this is my word. Both of them came out of the
amrita, from the source of jewels. So you must guard them, Karna, if your life
is dear to you."

"Who are you, who speak to me like this, showing me such friendship?" said
Karna. "If you please, lord, tell me. Who are you in a brahmin's dress?"

"I am the thousand-rayed one, my son," said the brahmin. "I point this out to
you out of friendship. Do as I say, for this is your highest good."

"It is the greatest good for me," said Karna, "that the lord of rays should
speak to me today, wanting my good. But hear what I say. I beg your favour,
giver of boons, and I speak to you with love. If I am dear to you, I must not be
turned from this vow. The whole world knows my vow, shining one, that I would
surely give even my life to the chief brahmins. Shakra may come to me disguised
as a brahmin, best of sky-rovers, to beg for the good of the Pandavas. If he
does, I shall give him my earrings and my fine armour, best of the gods, so that
my fame, known through the three worlds, is not destroyed. For one like me, it
is not right to save his life with disgrace. A death with fame, honoured by the
world, is what is right.

"So I shall give Indra my earrings with my armour, if the slayer of Bala and
Vritra comes to me to beg, to ask for my earrings for the good of the sons of
Pandu. That will bring me fame in the world, and disgrace to him. For I choose
fame in the world, even at the cost of my life, radiant one. The man with fame
wins heaven, but the man without it perishes. For fame keeps a man alive in the
world like a mother, and ill fame kills the life even of a man who still lives
in his body. There is an old verse, shining one, sung by the Maker himself, lord
of the world, that fame is a man's life. In the other world fame is a man's
final refuge, and in this world pure fame lengthens life.

"So when I have given away the things born with my body, I shall win lasting
fame. When I have given gifts to brahmins as the rules require, when I have
offered my body in battle and done a very hard deed, or beaten my enemies in the
fight, I shall win glory alone. When I have given safety in battle to the
frightened who want to live, and saved the old, the young and the brahmins from
great danger, I shall win the highest glory in the world, slayer of Svarbhanu.
My fame must be guarded even with my life. Know that this is my vow. So I shall
give this matchless alms to Maghavan, disguised as a brahmin, god, and go to the
highest state in the world."

---

"Do not do harm to yourself, Karna," said the Sun, "or to your friends, your
sons, your wives, your mother and your father. It is without harming the body,
best of living beings, that one should seek to win glory and lasting fame in
heaven. If you want lasting fame at the cost of your life, it will take your
life and go, no doubt of it. It is for the living that father, mother and sons
do their work, and any other kin in this world, bull among men, and kings too,
by their manhood. Know that. Fame is good for a man while he lives, splendid
one. What use is fame to a dead man, whose body has turned to ashes? The dead do
not know their fame. The living enjoy it. The fame of a dead mortal is like a
garland on a corpse.

"I tell you this for your good because you are devoted to me. For those who are
devoted to me must be protected, and for this reason too. 'He is devoted to me
with the highest devotion,' I think, strong-armed one, and devotion to you has
been born in me too. So do as I say. And there is something beyond this, a
secret of the self made by the gods. That is another reason I tell you this. Do
it without doubting. You cannot know the gods' secret, bull among men. So I do
not tell you the secret. You will know it in time.

"I tell you again, Radheya. Listen. Do not give your earrings to this beggar
with the thunderbolt in his hand. You shine with your lovely earrings, splendid
one, like the spotless moon in the sky between the two stars of Vishakha. Know
that fame is good for a man while he lives. You must refuse Purandara when he
asks for the earrings, my son. With many words, sinless one, full of reasons,
you can defeat again and again the king of the gods' wish for your earrings.
With arguments whose meaning is sound, adorned with sweetness, drive this
thought out of Purandara's mind, Karna. For you always rival Savyasachin, tiger
among men, and Savyasachin will meet you in battle, the hero. But Arjuna cannot
beat you in battle while you have your earrings, even if Indra himself were his
arrow. So you must not give these lovely earrings to Shakra, Karna, if you want
to beat Arjuna in battle."

"You know that I am devoted to you, lord of rays," said Karna, "as to no other
god, fiercest of the fierce. Not my wives, not my sons, not myself, not my
friends, are as dear to me in my devotion as you, lord of rays. The great surely
show the love their dear ones and devotees want. You know that, maker of light.
'Karna is dear to me and devoted to me, and he has no other god in heaven,'
knowing this, the lord has told me what is for my good.

"But again I bow my head and beg you, pleading again and again. I say this,
fierce-rayed one, but you should forgive me. I do not fear death as much as I
fear falsehood, above all towards the twice-born, all of them, always, and the
good. In giving even my life, I have no hesitation. And what you tell me, god,
about Phalguna the Pandava, let the pain in your mind that comes of your
distress go, maker of light. I shall beat Arjuna in battle. You know, god, that
I too have great power in weapons, which I won from Jamadagni's son and from the
great Drona. Give your leave for this vow of mine, best of the gods. I would
give even my life to the thunderbolt-wielder if he begs."

"If you are giving these lovely earrings to the thunderbolt-wielder, my son,"
said the Sun, "then you too must speak to him, mighty one, for the sake of
victory. Give your earrings to Shakra on a condition. For with your earrings you
cannot be killed by any creature. The slayer of danavas wants your destruction
by Arjuna in battle, my son, and so he wants to take your earrings. So you too
must win him over with kind words again and again. Ask the lord of the gods,
Purandara, whose purpose never fails. 'Give me the unfailing spear that destroys
enemies, and I shall give you my earrings and my fine armour, thousand-eyed
one.' Give your earrings to Shakra on this condition, Karna. With it you will
kill your enemies in battle. For that spear of the king of the gods does not
return to the hand, strong-armed one, until it has killed enemies in their
hundreds and thousands."

When he had said this, the thousand-rayed one suddenly vanished. Then, at the
end of his prayers, Karna told the Sun his dream. The bull told him all of it in
order, as he had seen it, truly, as the two of them had spoken in the night.
When he heard it, the blessed god, the Sun, slayer of Svarbhanu, said to Karna,
as if smiling, "It is so."

Then Radheya, slayer of enemy heroes, knowing it was true, waited for Vasava,
wanting only the spear.

**Divergences.**

* **Karna's resolve.** A rejected passage (\*1335) has Karna wake troubled and resolve at once to ask for the spear. In the CE he tells the Sun his dream at the end of his prayers and waits (3.286.17–20).
* **The Sun keeps his secret.** The Sun does not tell Karna that he is his father. He speaks of "a secret made by the gods" that Karna will know in time (3.285.8–9).

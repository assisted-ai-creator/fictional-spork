# Lomasha

As Dhaumya was speaking so, joy of the Kauravas, the seer Lomasha of great
splendour came there. The eldest of the Pandavas, the king, and his company, and
the brahmins, rose to greet the blessed one, as the immortals rise to greet
Shakra in heaven. The Dharma King Yudhishthira honoured him as was proper, and
asked him why he had come and what purpose his wanderings had.

When the son of Pandu asked him, the great-hearted seer was pleased, and he
answered gently, as if to gladden the Pandavas.

"I wander through all the worlds as chance takes me, son of Kunti. I went to
Shakra's palace and there I saw the lord of the gods. And I saw your heroic
brother Savyasachin, sitting on half of Shakra's seat. I was greatly amazed,
tiger among men, to see Partha there like that. The lord of the gods said to me
there, 'Go to the sons of Pandu.' So I have come quickly, wanting to see you and
your younger brothers.

"At the word of Puruhuta and of great Partha, I will tell you great and welcome
news, my son, joy of the Pandavas. Hear it with your brothers and with Krishna,
king. The weapon for which you sent the strong-armed one, bull of the Pandavas,
Partha has won from Rudra, great and matchless. It is called Brahmashiras. It
came to Rudra through austerity, Rudra's own, risen from the nectar, and
Savyasachin has it, with its mantras, the way to call it back, and its
atonements and blessings. Partha of boundless courage has learned the
thunderbolt and other weapons, the Rod and the rest, the divine weapons of Yama,
Kubera, Varuna and Indra, Yudhishthira, joy of the Kurus. From the son of
Vishvavasu he learned song, dance and the Sama chants, and instrumental music,
properly, as the rules require. So, master of the weapons, the son of Kunti has
won the lore of the gandharvas too. Bibhatsu, the younger brother of your
younger brother, lives happily.

"Now I will tell you why the best of the gods gave me this message,
Yudhishthira. Hear me. 'You will go to the world of men, no doubt of it,' he
said. 'Tell Yudhishthira there, best of the twice-born, from me: "Your brother
Arjuna will come soon, master of the weapons. First he will do a great work for
the gods which the dwellers in heaven cannot do. But you, with your brothers,
give yourself to austerity. There is nothing higher than austerity. By austerity
a man wins greatness. And I know Karna as he truly is, bull of the Bharatas. He
is not worth a sixteenth part of Partha in battle. The fear of him that lies in
your mind, tamer of enemies, I will take away too, when Savyasachin has come
back here. And your wish, hero, to make this pilgrimage to the fords, Lomasha
will tell you all about it, no doubt of it. Whatever fruit of austerity there is
in the fords, Bharata, whatever this great seer tells you, believe it, and
nothing else."'

"And hear what Dhananjaya said too, Yudhishthira. 'Join my brother Yudhishthira
to the fortune that comes of dharma. You know the highest laws and the
austerities, you who are rich in austerity, and you know the eternal dharma of
splendid kings. Whatever you know to be most purifying for men, join the Pandava
to it, through the merit of the fords. See to it with all your heart that the
king goes to the fords and gives away cows.' So Vijaya told me. 'Let him travel
to all the fords guarded by you. He must be protected from rakshasas in the
rough and dangerous places. As Dadhicha guarded the king of the gods, as Angiras
guarded the sun, so guard the son of Kunti from the rakshasas, best of the
twice-born. For there are many sorcerers, rakshasas as big as mountains. Let
them not come near the sons of Kunti while you guard them.'

"So, at Indra's word and Arjuna's bidding, I will travel with you, guarding you
from dangers. Twice before I have seen the fords, joy of the Kurus. This third
time I will see them again with you. This pilgrimage, which takes away fear, has
been made by royal seers who did holy deeds, Yudhishthira, by Manu and others,
great king. No man who is not straight, not perfected, unlearned or an evildoer
bathes in the fords, Kauravya, and no man with a crooked mind. But you are
always minded to dharma, you know dharma and keep your promises. You will be
freed from every sin even more. As King Bhagiratha, as the kings Gaya and the
others, as Yayati, son of Kunti, so will you be, Pandava."

"For joy I can find no answer to these words," said Yudhishthira. "What can be
greater than to be remembered by the king of the gods? Who can be greater than a
man who has met you, whose brother is Dhananjaya, whom Vasava remembers? As for
what you say, blessed one, about seeing the fords, I had already made up my mind
to it, at Dhaumya's word. Whenever you think it right to go and see the fords,
brahmin, I will go at once. That is my firm resolve."

When the Pandava had made up his mind to go, Lomasha said to him, "Travel light,
great king. If you are light, you will go where you please."

"Let the brahmins who live on alms turn back," said Yudhishthira, "and the
ascetics, and the people of the city who followed us out of loyalty to the king.
Let them go to the great king Dhritarashtra. He will give each of them in due
time the keep that is fitting. If that lord of men does not give them their
proper living, the Panchala will give it to you, for love of us and for our
good."

Then most of the people of the city, weighed down with a heavy burden, and the
brahmins and the ascetics, went to the City of the Elephant. Ambika's son, the
king, received them all as the rules require, out of love for the Dharma King,
and satisfied them with wealth. Then the king, the son of Kunti, well pleased,
stayed three nights in Kamyaka with a few brahmins and with Lomasha.

---

As the son of Kunti was setting out, the brahmins who lived in the forest came
to him, king, and said this. "King, you will go to the holy fords with your
brothers and with the great divine seer Lomasha. You should take us too, great
king, Pandava. For we cannot reach them without you, Kaurava. The fords are
beset by beasts of prey, rough and hard to get through, and small bands of men
cannot reach them, lord of men. You brothers are heroes, always the best of
bowmen. Guarded by you heroes, let us go too. By your grace we would gain the
good reward of the fords and of our vows, lord of the earth, lord of the people.
Protected by your courage, purified, bathed in the fords, we would have our sins
shaken off by the sight of the fords, king.

"You too, Bharata, bathed in the fords, will surely win the worlds hard to win
of King Kartavirya and the royal seer Ashtaka, lord of the earth. You will win
those of Lomapada, and of heroic Bharata, the lord of all the earth. We want to
see with you, lord of the earth, the fords beginning with Prabhasa, the
mountains beginning with Mahendra, the rivers beginning with the Ganga and the
trees beginning with the plaksha. If you have any love for brahmins, lord of the
people, do as we ask quickly, and you will find good fortune. For the fords are
always overrun by rakshasas, who hinder austerity, strong-armed one. You should
protect us from them. Travel as the rules require, lord of men, to all the fords
that Dhaumya and wise Narada told of, and the great ascetic, the divine seer
Lomasha. Go with us, your sins shaken off, guarded by Lomasha."

Honoured like this by them, the bull of the Pandavas, surrounded by his heroic
brothers with Bhimasena at their head, shed tears of joy and said to all those
seers, "Very well." Taking leave of Lomasha and of Dhaumya, the family priest,
the best of the Pandavas, master of himself, made up his mind to go, with his
brothers and with blameless Draupadi.

Then the blessed Vyasa, and Narada and Parvata, came to Kamyaka, wise men, to
see the Pandava. King Yudhishthira honoured them as the rules require, and the
blessed ones said to him, "Yudhishthira, twins, Bhima, make your minds straight.
Purify yourselves in mind, and go to the fords made pure. The brahmins call the
discipline of the body a human vow. The twice-born call a mind made pure a
divine vow. A mind unspoiled is enough for heroes, lord of men. Hold to
friendliness of mind, and go to the fords made pure. Purified in mind, with your
vows of bodily discipline, keeping the divine vow, you will gain the reward as
it has been told."

"So be it," they promised, the Pandavas with Krishna. Blessings for the road
were said by the sages, divine and human. They clasped the feet of Lomasha and
of Dvaipayana, of Narada, king of kings, and of the divine seer Parvata. Then,
with Dhaumya and other forest dwellers, the heroes set out when the full moon of
Margashirsha had passed, under the star Pushya. Carrying their vessels, wearing
bark and deerskins, with matted hair, and with armour that could not be pierced,
they went round the fords then. They had servants led by Indrasena, fourteen
chariots or so, cooks and other attendants. With their weapons, swords bound on,
quivers and arrows, the heroic Pandavas set out facing east, Janamejaya.

---

"I do not think I am without virtue, best of divine seers," said Yudhishthira.
"And yet I am tormented by sorrow as no other king is. And I think my enemies
are without virtue, nor do they delight in dharma. Yet they prosper in this
world, Lomasha. What is the cause?"

"You must not grieve over this at all, king, Partha," said Lomasha, "that men
who love adharma prosper by adharma. A man grows by adharma, then he sees good
fortune, then he defeats his rivals, and then he is destroyed, root and all. For
I have seen daityas and danavas, lord of the earth, growing by adharma, and then
going to destruction. Long ago, in the age of the gods, I saw it all, lord. The
gods loved dharma, and the asuras gave it up. The gods went to the fords, and
the asuras did not, Bharata. Pride that came from adharma took hold of them
first. From pride came arrogance, from arrogance anger, from anger
shamelessness, and then their conduct was lost. When they were shameless,
without modesty, fallen in conduct, their vows empty, patience, fortune and
dharma soon left them. Fortune went to the gods, and misfortune to the asuras,
king. Then Kali too entered the daityas and danavas, possessed by misfortune,
their minds struck by pride. Possessed by misfortune and by Kali, overcome by
pride, without rites, witless, and overcome by arrogance, the danavas soon met
destruction, son of Kunti. The daityas, their fame gone, perished utterly.

"But the gods, who kept to dharma, went to the seas and rivers and lakes and
holy shrines. By austerity, rites, gifts and blessings, Pandava, they shook off
every sin and gained good fortune. So the gods, generous and faithful to their
rites in every way, went to the fords, and so they won the highest prosperity.
In the same way, king of kings, when you have bathed in the fords with your
younger brothers, you will find that fortune again. This is the eternal road.
Think of King Nriga, of Shibi Aushinara, of Bhagiratha, Vasumanas, Gaya, Puru
and Pururavas. Always practising austerity, they were purified by touching the
water, by going to the fords and by seeing great men, and they won holy fame and
wealth, lord of the people. So you too, king of kings, will win great fortune.
Ikshvaku travelled with his sons, his people and his kin, and so did Muchukunda,
Mandhatar and King Marutta, and they won holy fame, as the gods did by the
strength of their austerity, and the divine seers. So you too will win it in
full. But the sons of Dhritarashtra, mastered by pride and folly, will soon be
destroyed, like the daityas, no doubt of it."

---

So the heroes travelled together, living here and there, and in due course they
came to the Naimisha forest, lord of the earth. There, in the holy fords of the
Gomati, the Pandavas bathed and gave away cows and wealth, Bharata. There the
Kauravas offered water again and again to the gods, the Fathers and the
brahmins, at Kanyatirtha, Ashvatirtha and the ford of the cows. The Pandavas
stayed on the mountain at Valakoti and Vrishaprastha, and all bathed in the
Bahuda, lord of the earth.

At Prayaga, the gods' place of sacrifice, lord of the earth, they bathed and
stayed, and took up the highest austerity. At the meeting of the Ganga and the
Yamuna, true to their promises, free of sin, the great-hearted men gave wealth
to the brahmins. Then, king, the sons of Pandu went with the brahmins to the
altar of Prajapati, where ascetics go, Bharata. The heroes lived there and took
up the highest austerity, always satisfying the twice-born with forest food as
their offering.

Then they went to the mountain honoured by the royal seer Gaya, of matchless
splendour, who knew dharma and did holy deeds. There is the lake Gayashiras and
the holy Mahanadi, and the fine and holy ford of Brahmasaras, loved by the
seers. There the blessed Agastya went to Vaivasvata, and there eternal Dharma
himself lived, king. There all the rivers rise, lord of the people, and
Mahadeva, the bearer of Pinaka, is always present. There the heroic Pandavas
sacrificed with the four-month rite, a great sacrifice of the seers, where the
great undying banyan stands. Brahmins rich in austerity gathered there in
hundreds and sacrificed with the four-month rite by the seers' rule. There
brahmins always given to learning and austerity, masters of the Veda, sitting in
the assembly, told holy stories of great men.

There Shamatha, who had completed his learning and his vows and kept the vow of
celibacy, told of Gaya, son of Amurtarayas, king.

"Gaya, son of Amurtarayas, was the best of royal seers. Hear from me of his holy
deeds, Bharata. His sacrifice was held here, with plenty of food and rich fees.
There were mountains of food in hundreds and thousands, king, streams of ghee
and hundreds of rivers of curds, and floods of costly dishes in thousands. Day
after day this was given to all who asked, and the brahmins ate other food, well
prepared, king. There, at the time of the fees, the sound of the Veda went up to
heaven. Nothing could be heard above the sound of the Veda, Bharata. As the holy
sound went on, king, the earth, the quarters, the air and the sky were filled
with it, and that too was a great wonder.

"Men sang verses there, bull of the Bharatas, bright ones, satisfied with good
food and drink, in land after land. 'Who now, of living things, still wants to
eat at Gaya's sacrifice, where there are twenty-five mountains of leftover food?
What the royal seer Gaya of boundless splendour did at his sacrifice, men before
him did not do, and those after will not. The gods have been so filled by Gaya
with oblations, how will they be able to take what others give?' Many such
verses were sung at the sacrifice of that great man, near this lake, joy of the
Kurus."

**Divergences.**

* **Indra and Karna.** In the CE Indra's message already promises to remove Yudhishthira's fear of
  Karna (3.89.19–20). How he does so, by taking Karna's armour and earrings, comes near the end of

  alms, the ascetics and the loyal citizens (3.90.19).

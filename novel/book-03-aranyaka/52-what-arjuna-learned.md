# What Arjuna Learned

"I spent that night there gladly, Bharata, by the grace of the god of gods, the
great Three-eyed One," said Arjuna. "When the night was over and I had done the
morning rites, I saw that best of brahmins I had seen before. I told him all
that had happened, Bharata: 'I have met the blessed great god.' That best of
brahmins was pleased and said to me, king of kings, 'You have seen the great god
as no one else has. When you have met all the guardians of the world, with
Vaivasvata at their head, you will see the lord of the gods, sinless one, and he
will give you weapons.' When he had said this, king, the brahmin, bright as the
sun, embraced me again and again and went where he wished.

"Then in the afternoon of that day a holy wind blew, slayer of rivals, as if
making this world new again. Divine garlands, fragrant and fresh, appeared near
me at the foot of the snowy mountain. Divine instruments sounded sweetly all
round, and lovely hymns in praise of Indra were heard. Hosts of apsarases and
gandharvas sang songs everywhere before the god of gods. Hosts of the Maruts
came there in the chariots of the gods, and those who follow Mahendra and live
in the house of the gods. Then the lord of the Maruts came there with Shachi, in
a chariot drawn by fine bay horses, well adorned, with all the immortals. At
that same moment, king, Kubera, drawn by men, showed himself to me, in the
highest glory. In the southern quarter I saw Yama standing, and Varuna and the
king of the gods each in his own place.

"The bulls among the gods spoke kindly to me, great king. 'Savyasachin, look at
the guardians of the world standing here. You have seen Shankara, so that the
work of the gods may be done. Take weapons from us too.' So they said on every
side. Then I made myself pure and bowed to the bulls among the gods, and took
those great weapons from them by the rules, lord. When I had taken the weapons,
the gods gave me leave, Bharata, and all the gods went back as they had come,
tamer of enemies.

"Maghavan too, the lord of the gods, the blessed slayer of the enemies of the
gods, mounted his shining chariot. He said to me, as if smiling, 'I knew you,
Dhananjaya, even before you came here. From now on I will show myself to you,
bull of the Bharatas. You bathed often in the fords long ago, and you have
practised this austerity. You will go to heaven, Pandava. But you must practise
austerity again, most terrible.' So the blessed one told me everything, and how
austerity is to be done. 'At my command Matali will bring you to heaven. You are
known to the gods and to the great-spirited seers.'

"Then I said to Shakra, 'Be gracious to me, blessed one. I choose you as my
teacher in weapons, lord of the thirty gods.'

"'When you know the weapons, my son, you will do cruel deeds, scorcher of
enemies,' said Indra. 'Win the wish for which you want the weapons, Pandava.'

"Then I said, 'Slayer of enemies, I will not use the divine weapons against
humans, except to turn back weapons. Give me those divine weapons, lord of the
gods. Afterwards, bull among the gods, may I win the worlds that weapons win.'

"'I said this to test you, Dhananjaya,' said Indra. 'Your words are fitting for
my son. Come to my house and learn all the weapons, Bharata. Learn those of the
Wind, of Fire, of the Vasus, of Varuna with the hosts of the Maruts, the weapon
of the Sadhyas and the Grandfather's, those of the gandharvas, snakes and
rakshasas, all Vishnu's weapons and those of the Nairritas, and all the weapons
that are with me here, upholder of the Kurus.'

"When he had said this to me, Shakra vanished there and then. Then I saw Indra's
chariot standing ready, yoked with bays, divine, made by magic, holy, driven by
Matali, king. When the guardians of the world had gone, Matali said to me,
'Shakra, king of the gods, wants to see you, you of great splendour. You are
made perfect, strong-armed one. Do the highest work. See the worlds of those who
have done holy deeds. Go to heaven in your body.'

"At Matali's words I took leave of the snowy mountain, walked round it keeping
it on my right, and mounted that finest of chariots. Matali, who knew the
science of horses, urged on the horses, swift as mind and wind, as was proper,
you of rich sacrificial gifts. Then the charioteer looked at my face as I stood
there. When the chariot had wheeled, king, he said in wonder, 'This seems to me
most wonderful and strange today, that you have stood in the divine chariot
without moving your feet. For I have always seen even the king of the gods
stagger at the first leap of the horses, bull of the Bharatas. But you stood
firm in the chariot as it wheeled, upholder of the Kurus. This spirit of yours
seems to me greater than Shakra's.'

"When he had said this, Matali went up into the sky and showed me the homes of
the gods and their aerial chariots, king, Bharata. Matali, Shakra's charioteer,
gladly showed me the many forests of the gods, Nandana and the others. Then I
saw Shakra's city, Amaravati, adorned with divine trees that bear whatever fruit
one wishes, and with jewels. The sun does not light it. There is no cold or heat
there, no weariness, no dust, mud or darkness, and no old age, king. No grief or
wretchedness is seen there, great king, and no pallor among the dwellers in
heaven, and no faintness, tamer of enemies. There was no anger or greed there,
lord of the people, and nothing unlovely. The creatures in the house of the gods
are always content and glad. The trees there are always in flower and fruit,
with green leaves, and there are lotus pools of many kinds, full of lotuses and
saugandhikas. A cool wind blew there, fragrant, life-giving and pure. The ground
was bright with every jewel and adorned with flowers. There were many lovely
beasts and birds with sweet voices, and many immortals were seen travelling in
aerial chariots.

"Then I saw the Vasus, the Rudras, the Sadhyas with the hosts of the Maruts, the
Adityas and the two Ashvins, and I honoured them all. They blessed me with
courage, fame, splendour and strength, with weapons and with victory in battle.
I entered that lovely city, where gods and gandharvas go, and stood before the
thousand-eyed king of the gods with joined hands. Shakra, best of givers, gladly
gave me half his seat, and Vasava touched my limbs with great respect.

"There, you of rich sacrificial gifts, I lived in heaven with gods and
gandharvas for the sake of weapons, making them my own, Bharata. Chitrasena, son
of Vishvavasu, became my friend, and he taught me the whole art of the
gandharvas, king. Then I lived happily in Shakra's house, king, with my weapons
won, well honoured, with everything I could wish for. I heard the sound of songs
and the full sound of instruments, and saw the finest apsarases dancing,
scorcher of enemies. I did not scorn any of it, Bharata, and I knew it for what
it was. I took it all in fully, but I held fast to the weapons alone. The
thousand-eyed lord was pleased with me for that wish. So, king, this time went
by as I lived in heaven.

---

"When I had mastered the weapons and he trusted me, the one whose horses are
bays touched my head with both hands. He said, 'Today not even the hosts of the
gods could conquer you in battle, let alone men in the world of men, whose
selves are unformed. You are beyond measure, not to be withstood, without equal
in battle.' Then the god, his hair standing on end with joy, said again, 'No one
will be your equal in the battle of weapons, hero. You are always watchful and
skilled, truthful, master of your senses, devoted to brahmins, a knower of
weapons, and brave, upholder of the Kurus. You have won the weapons, ten and
five, Partha, and in the five methods no one is your equal. You know how to use
them, how to withdraw them, how to use them again, how to atone for them and how
to turn them back in every way, Dhananjaya. Now the time for your teacher's fee
has come, scorcher of enemies. Promise to pay it, and then I will tell you what
it is.'

"Then, king, I said this to the king of the gods: 'If it is something I can do,
know that it is done already.' Then the slayer of Bala and Vritra laughed and
said to me, king, 'There is nothing in the three worlds today that you cannot
do. There are danavas called the Nivatakavachas, enemies of mine, who live in a
stronghold in the belly of the ocean. They are said to number three crores,
alike in form, strength and splendour. Kill them there, son of Kunti. That will
be your teacher's fee.'

"Then he gave me a divine chariot of great splendour, driven by Matali, yoked
with horses whose hair was like a peacock's. He bound this fine diadem on my
head, and gave me ornaments for my limbs to match my form. He gave me this fine
armour, which cannot be pierced, lovely to touch and to see, and he fixed this
unfading string on Gandiva. Then I set out in that shining chariot in which the
lord of the gods once conquered Bali, son of Virochana.

"Then all the gods, woken by its sound, came together, lord of the people,
thinking I was the king of the gods. When they saw me they asked, 'What are you
going to do, Phalguna?' I told them just how it was: 'This is what I will do in
battle. Know that I have set out wanting to kill the Nivatakavachas, blessed
ones. Wish me well, sinless ones.' They were pleased and praised me as they
praise the god Purandara. 'In this chariot Maghavan conquered Shambara in
battle, and Namuchi, Bala and Vritra, Prahlada and Naraka. In this chariot
Maghavan conquered many thousands, millions and hundreds of millions of daityas
in battle. You too, son of Kunti, will conquer the Nivatakavachas in battle with
it, showing your courage, as the self-controlled Maghavan did long ago. And here
is the finest of conches, with which you will conquer the danavas. With it even
the great Shakra conquered the worlds.' So the gods gave it to me, Devadatta,
born of the water. I took it for victory, praised by the immortals. With the
conch and armour and arrows, my bow in my hand, I set out, eager to fight, for
the most terrible home of the danavas.

**Divergences.**

* **Urvashi.** Arjuna's account to Yudhishthira says only that he saw the apsarases dance and held fast to the weapons (3.164.56–57). The episode of
  Urvashi and her curse is not in the CE at all (App. I No. 6); see L-25.
* **"Unconquerable by gods and asuras".** A rejected line (\*846) adds that Arjuna cannot be beaten by gods and asuras together. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* The chapter is Arjuna's speech to Yudhishthira. The speech markers in the text for Indra (3.164.25, 28) are given as quoted speech inside Arjuna's
  account.
* 3.164.5 *vaivasvatādibhiḥ*: "with Vaivasvata at their head", Yama.
* 3.164.22 *uvāca bhagavān sarvaṃ tapasaś copapādanam*: "So the blessed one told me everything, and how austerity is to be done."
* 3.164.30 *nairṛtāni*: "those of the Nairritas".
* 3.164.37 *padā na calito bhavān*: "without moving your feet".
* 3.164.57: Arjuna "did not scorn any of it" and "held fast to the weapons alone".
* 3.165.5 *daśa ca pañca ca*: "ten and five". *pañcabhir vidhibhiḥ*: "the five methods", named in the next verse.
* 3.165.11 *tisraḥ koṭyaḥ*: "three crores".
* 3.165.19 *sahasrāṇi prayutāny arbudāni*: "thousands, millions and hundreds of millions".
* 3.165.22 *devadattaṃ jalodbhavam*: the conch Devadatta, "born of the water". In Book 2 Maya brings Arjuna Varuna's conch Devadatta (2.3); here the gods
  give it. Both are kept as the text has them.

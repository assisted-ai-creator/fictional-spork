# Prabhasa

"When the Vrishnis and the Pandavas had come to the ford of Prabhasa," said
Janamejaya, "what did they do, you who are rich in austerity? What did they talk
about there? For all of them were great, learned in every science, the Vrishnis
and the Pandavas, and friends to one another."

When they had come to Prabhasa, the holy ford of the great sea, the Vrishnis
gathered around the heroic Pandavas and attended them. Then Rama, the bearer of
the plough, wearing his garland of wild flowers, bright as cow's milk, the kunda
flower, the moon, a lotus stalk or silver, spoke to the lotus-eyed Krishna.

"Krishna, dharma practised does not seem to bring a man good fortune, nor
adharma his fall. Great Yudhishthira lives in the forest with matted hair,
suffering, dressed in bark. Duryodhana rules the earth, and the earth does not
open to swallow him. A man of little wit would think adharma better practised
than dharma. With Duryodhana thriving and Yudhishthira robbed of his kingdom,
unhappy, doubt has risen among men and they ask each other, 'What are we to do
now?' This lord of men, born of Dharma, devoted to dharma, steady in truth,
generous, Partha, would sooner fall from his kingdom and from happiness than
grow by leaving dharma.

"How can Bhishma, the brahmin Kripa, Drona and the king, the elder of the
family, find any happiness after banishing the Parthas? Shame on the chiefs of
the Bharatas, with their wicked minds! What will that wicked chief of the earth
say when he meets his fathers in the next world? 'I dealt rightly with my sons,'
when he cast out sons who had done no wrong from the kingdom? He does not see
with his mind why he was born blind among the kings of the earth, when he could
banish the son of Kunti from the kingdom. Surely Vichitravirya's son and his
sons, when they have done this cruel thing, see in the land of the Fathers the
golden trees growing out of the earth in flower. Surely he never asks these men,
broad-shouldered and red-eyed, never listens to them, since he sent Yudhishthira
and his brothers, with their weapons, into the forest without a qualm.

"This long-armed Vrikodara could destroy the rich army of his enemies even
without weapons. At the mere sound of Vrikodara's voice, armies drop their dung
and urine. He is worn thin by hunger, thirst and the road. When the mighty one
comes against them with weapons and arrows of every kind in his hands,
remembering this terrible life in the forest, he will leave no one alive. I am
sure of it. For no man on earth will equal him in courage and strength. His body
worn by cold and heat, wind and sun, he will leave no one of his enemies alive
in battle. Vrikodara, that mighty and daring chariot fighter, who beat the kings
of the east and their followers in battle with a single chariot and came home
safe, now suffers in the forest in bark clothes. Look at Sahadeva today, who
beat the kings of the south who gathered at Dantakura, now an ascetic in an
ascetic's dress. The hero who beat the kings of the west with a single chariot,
bold in battle, now wanders in the forest with matted hair, living on roots and
fruit, his body covered in dirt.

"The daughter who rose up from the floor of the altar at the rich sacrifice of
the king, how does this good woman, who deserves happiness, bear this miserable
life in the forest now? These are the sons of the gods, of Dharma, the first of
the three aims, of the Wind, of the lord of the gods and of the Ashvins. How do
they wander in the forest with little happiness, when they deserve happiness?
The son of Dharma has been beaten with his wife and driven out with his brothers
and his followers, and Duryodhana is thriving. How does the earth with its
mountains not sink?"

---

"This is no time for lamenting, Rama," said Satyaki. "Let us all do what must be
done next, while there is still time, even if Yudhishthira says nothing. Those
who have protectors in the world do not take up the work themselves. Their
protectors see to their affairs, Rama, as Shaibya and the others did for Yayati.
Men whose protectors take up their work by their own judgement, Rama, are heroes
with protectors, and they do not suffer hardship like men without them. How is
it that this man lives in the forest with his brothers, when he has Rama and
Janardana, Pradyumna, Samba and me, the protectors of the three worlds?

"Let the army of the Dasharhas march out today, well armed with weapons of every
kind and bright armour. Let the son of Dhritarashtra go to the house of Yama
with his kin, overwhelmed by the strength of the Vrishnis. You alone in your
anger could surround this whole earth. Let the bearer of the Sharnga bow stay
behind. Kill the son of Dhritarashtra with all his kin, as great Indra, lord of
the gods, killed Vritra. Partha, who is my brother, my friend and my teacher,
and who is like Janardana's own self, is doing the finest of deeds, unmatched,
for the purpose for which he set out.

"I will beat back all his rain of weapons with my fine weapons and overcome him
in battle. With my fine arrows, like snake poison and fire, I will strike his
head from his body, Rama. With a sharp sword in battle I will strike his head
from his body by force, and then I will kill all his followers, and Duryodhana,
and all the Kurus. Let the people of the earth, son of Rohini, see me here with
my weapons, joyful in battle. Let them see me killing alone the chief fighters
of the Kurus, like the fire at the end of time burning a great thicket.

"Kripa, Drona, Vikarna and Karna cannot bear the sharp arrows Pradyumna looses.
I know the courage of your son too, and what the son of Krishna is like when he
stands in battle. Let Samba crush Duhshasana with his arms by force, with his
charioteer and his chariot, and punish him. Nothing in battle is too much for
the son of Jambavati, wild in battle. This boy scattered the army of the daitya
Shambara in a moment. By him, in battle, Ashvachakra was killed, round-thighed,
with long thick arms. What man in battle could get between Samba's arms? As a
man who has gone into the grip of Death when his time has come does not come out
again, so who that has gone between his arms in battle could come back alive?

"Drona and Bhishma, those great chariot fighters, and Somadatta surrounded by
his sons, and all their armies, Vasudeva will burn with the flames of his
arrows. What is there in all the worlds, with all their gods, that Krishna
cannot overcome, when he takes up his weapons, the finest arrows in his hand,
the discus for his weapon, matchless in battle? Then let Aniruddha too, sword
and shield in hand, strew this earth with the sons of Dhritarashtra, senseless,
their heads struck off, killed, like the altar strewn with kusha grass at a
sacrifice. Let Gada and Ulmuka, Bahuka, Bhanu and Nitha, the hero Nishatha, a
boy in battle, and Sarana and Charudeshna, wild in battle, do deeds worthy of
their family. Let the army of the Shurasenas, the kshatriyas with the chief
fighters of the Vrishnis, Bhojas and Andhakas, gather, kill the sons of
Dhritarashtra in battle, and win wide fame in the world.

"Then let Abhimanyu rule the earth, while Yudhishthira, the best of those who
uphold dharma, the great one, fulfils his vow, as he promised the best of the
Kurus at the dicing. Then the Dharma King, his enemies beaten by the arrows we
loose, will enjoy the earth, with no sons of Dhritarashtra and no suta's son
left. This is what we most must do, and it will bring us fame."

"No doubt this is true, Madhava," said Vasudeva. "We accept your word, you of
undaunted spirit. But the bull of the Kurus would never want land that he had
not won with his own arms. Yudhishthira would never give up his own dharma, for
desire, for fear or for greed. Nor would Bhima and Arjuna, those great chariot
fighters, or the twins, or Krishna, the daughter of Drupada. Both of them,
Vrikodara and Dhananjaya, have no match in battle on earth. Why should he not
rule the whole earth, with the sons of Madri before him? When the great lord of
the Panchalas, with the Kekayas, the lord of the Chedis and we ourselves, fight
and press on against the enemy, then Suyodhana will leave the world of the
living."

"What you say is no wonder, Madhava," said Yudhishthira. "But it is truth I must
guard above all, not the kingdom. Krishna alone knows me as I truly am, and I
know Krishna as he truly is. When this hero among men knows the time for courage
has come, Madhava, then you, hero of the Shinis, and Keshava will beat Suyodhana
in battle. Let the heroes of the Dasharhas go back today. I am made strong by my
protectors, the protectors of the world of men. Be steadfast in dharma, you of
boundless power. I will see you all again, happy and together."

They took leave of one another and greeted each other, embraced the elders and
all the children, and the heroes of the Yadus went to their homes. The king went
on round the fords. When the Dharma King had sent Krishna away, he went to the
Payoshni and stayed there. The king of Vidarbha had honoured that river, with
its fine fords and its water mixed with pressed soma.

---

"Here, we hear, king," said Lomasha, "Purandara was satisfied with soma by Nriga
as he sacrificed, and grew drunk with it. Here the gods with Indra, and the
Prajapatis too, sacrificed with many kinds of great sacrifices, with rich fees.
And here King Gaya, son of Amurtarayas, satisfied the lord who bears the
thunderbolt with soma at seven horse sacrifices. At his seven sacrifices,
everything of wood and earth that is used in a sacrifice was made of gold. At
those sacrifices seven famous rites were performed, and on each sacrificial post
were seven rings. At his sacrifices, Yudhishthira, the gods with Indra
themselves raised his shining golden posts. At those finest of rites of King
Gaya, Indra was drunk with soma, and the brahmins with the fees.

"No one can count the grains of sand in the world, the stars in the sky, or the
drops of rain when it pours. So the wealth that Gaya gave to the members of the
assembly at those seven sacrifices could not be counted, great king. What has
been told here might be counted, but the fees of that giver of fees could not be
counted. With golden cows made by Vishvakarman he satisfied the brahmins who
came from every quarter. The earth was left with little room for the sacrificial
shrines of great Gaya as he sacrificed, here and there, lord of the people. By
that deed, Bharata, he reached the worlds of Indra. Whoever touches the water of
the Payoshni will share his world. So bathe here, king of kings, with your
brothers, sinless one. When you have touched the water, lord of the earth, you
will be free of sin."

When the best of men had bathed in the Payoshni with his brothers, the splendid,
sinless one came with his brothers to the Vaidurya mountain and the great river
Narmada. There the blessed seer Lomasha told him of all the lovely fords, here
and there, lord of the people. He went with his brothers as it suited and as he
pleased, giving wealth again and again to brahmins in thousands.

"One reaches the world of the gods and of kings, son of Kunti," said Lomasha,
"by seeing the Vaidurya mountain and going down into the Narmada. This is the
meeting of the Treta and the Dvapara ages, best of men. Coming here, son of
Kunti, one is freed from every sin. This is the place of Sharyati's sacrifice,
my son, shining, where Kaushika drank the soma himself with the Ashvins. And the
great ascetic Chyavana, the Bhargava, was angry with great Indra, and the lord
Chyavana paralysed Vasava. And he won the princess Sukanya for his wife."

"How was the blessed chastiser of Paka paralysed by him?" said Yudhishthira.
"Why was the Bhargava, the great ascetic, angry? And how did he make the
Nasatyas drinkers of soma, brahmin? Tell me all this, blessed one, just as it
happened."

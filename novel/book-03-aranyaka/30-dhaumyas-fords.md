# Dhaumya's Fords

Knowing the mind of his brothers and of wise Narada, King Yudhishthira spoke to
Dhaumya, who was like the Grandsire.

"I sent that tiger among men away for weapons, Jishnu, true in his courage, the
strong-armed one of boundless spirit. He is a hero, devoted, able, and very
skilled in weapons, rich in austerity, a lord like Vasudeva. For I know those
two, brahmin, the two Krishnas, killers of enemies, in their courage, Vasudeva
and Dhananjaya, the lotus-eyed ones of the three ages. So does mighty Vyasa.
Narada knows it too, and he has always told me so. I also know that they are the
seers Nara and Narayana. Because I thought he was able, I sent Arjuna. He is no
less than Indra, a son of the gods, able to see the lord of the gods and to take
the weapons from Indra. That is why I sent him away.

"Bhishma and Drona, the great chariot fighters, Kripa and the unconquerable son
of Drona, all mighty in battle, have been chosen by Dhritarashtra's son. All of
them know the Veda and are brave, and all are skilled in weapons. And mighty
Karna, the suta's son, the great chariot fighter who knows the divine weapons,
always longs to fight Partha. He is a fire at the end of an age, let loose as if
by Time. The force of his horses is its wind, his arrows its flames, the slap of
his bowstring its roar. Dust is its smoke and weapons its heat, and the sons of
Dhritarashtra are the wind that fans it. He will burn up the dry grass of my
army, no doubt of it. But Arjuna is a great cloud, driven by the wind of
Krishna. Divine weapons are its rain, his white horses its line of cranes, and
Gandiva its bright rainbow. That cloud will rise and quench the fire of Karna in
battle with unceasing streams of arrows. Bibhatsu, conqueror of enemy cities,
will take all the divine weapons truly from Shakra himself. I think he will be a
match for all of them. In battle the enemy has no answer to him and no way to
outdo him. We shall all see the Pandava Dhananjaya with his weapons won. For
Bibhatsu does not sink under a burden once he has taken it up.

"But without the hero, best of men, we can find no peace in this forest of
Kamyaka, with Krishna. Tell us of another forest, a good one, with plenty of
food and fruit, pure and lovely, where men of holy deeds go. Let us live there
for a time, waiting for Arjuna, true in his courage, as those who long for rain
wait for the cloud. Tell us of hermitages that brahmins have heard of, lakes and
rivers and lovely mountains. For we do not like living in this Kamyaka forest
without Arjuna, brahmin. Let us go to another quarter."

---

When Dhaumya, who was like Brihaspati, saw all the Pandavas longing and sick at
heart, he comforted them.

"Hear me, bull of the Bharatas, as I tell you of the holy hermitages the
brahmins approve, and of the quarters, the fords and the mountains, king. First
I will tell you, as I remember it, of the lovely eastern quarter, Yudhishthira,
where hosts of royal seers go.

"In that quarter, which the divine seers love, is Naimisha, Bharata, where the
gods have their holy fords, each one apart. There is the holy Gomati, lovely,
where the divine seers go, and the sacrificial ground of the gods, and the
slaughtering place of Vivasvat. In that quarter is the holy mountain Gaya,
honoured by royal seers, where the auspicious Brahmasaras is, which the thirty
seers visit. It is for this, tiger among men, that the ancients say, 'One should
wish for many sons, if even one of them goes to Gaya.' The Mahanadi is there,
and Gayashiras, sinless one, where the brahmins speak of the banyan that makes
things undying. Food given to the Fathers there becomes undying, lord. There is
the great river called Phalgu, with its holy water, and the Kaushiki, rich in
roots and fruit, bull of the Bharatas, where Vishvamitra, rich in austerity,
rose to the rank of a brahmin.

"There is the holy river Ganga, on whose bank Bhagiratha sacrificed with many
rites with rich fees, my son. Among the Panchalas, Kauravya, they speak of
Utpalavata, where Vishvamitra Kaushika sacrificed with Shakra. There the blessed
Jamadagnya, seeing Vishvamitra's more than human greatness, sang a verse about
his line: 'Kaushika drank the soma at Kanyakubja with Indra. Then he left the
kshatriya order and said, "I am a brahmin."' There is the meeting of the Ganga
and the Yamuna, hero, famous in the world, pure, loved by the seers, holy, the
highest purifier, where the Grandsire, the soul of beings, sacrificed long ago.
That is why it is known as Prayaga, best of the Bharatas.

"There too, king of kings, is the great and fine hermitage of Agastya, and
Hiranyabindu, as it is called, on Mount Kalanjara, king. Above all other
mountains, king, is the holy and auspicious Mahendra, Kauravya, the mountain of
great Bhargava. There the Grandsire sacrificed long ago, son of Kunti, and the
holy Bhagirathi sat in his assembly, Yudhishthira. There the holy river famed as
Brahmashala flows, lord of the people, crowded with those whose sins are shaken
off, and the sight of it is holy. The famous Kedara of Matanga is pure and
blessed, eternal in the world, and his great hermitage is the finest. There is
Kundoda, a lovely mountain rich in roots, fruit and water, where the thirsty
Naishadha found water and peace. There is the lovely Devavana, adorned by
ascetics, and the river Bahuda, and the Nanda on the mountain top.

"I have told you, great king, of the fords, rivers, mountains and holy shrines
of the eastern quarter. Now hear from me of the holy fords in the other three
quarters, the rivers, the mountains and the holy shrines.

---

"Hear, Bharata, of the holy fords in the south, told at length as far as I know
them. In that quarter, they say, is the holy river Godavari, rich in gardens and
in water, lovely, where ascetics go. There are the two rivers Venna and
Bhimarathi, which take away sin and fear, full of beasts and birds, adorned with
the homes of ascetics. There, bull of the Bharatas, is the river of the royal
seer Nriga, the Payoshni, with lovely fords and plenty of water, where brahmins
go. There too the great yogi Markandeya, of great austerity, sang a verse on the
line of Nriga, lord of the earth. When Nriga sacrificed, we have heard, Indra
was drunk with soma before men's eyes, and the brahmins with the sacrificial
fees.

"There is the holy forest of Mathara, rich in roots and fruit, auspicious, and
the sacrificial post, best of the Bharatas, on the mountain of Varunasrotasa. On
the northern side of the Praveni, and at the holy hermitage of Kanva, are
forests of ascetics, told as I have heard of them. There is the altar of great
Jamadagni at Shurparaka, my son, and the lovely Pashanatirtha and Purashchandra,
Bharata. Among mortals there is Ashokatirtha, son of Kunti, with many
hermitages. Among the Pandyas there is Agastyatirtha, and Varuna's ford,
Yudhishthira. The holy maidens are told of among the Pandyas too, bull among
men.

"I will tell you of the Tamraparni, son of Kunti. Hear it. There the gods
practised austerity in a hermitage, longing for greatness. It is famous in the
three worlds as Gokarna, Bharata. It is a lake of cool water, deep and holy,
auspicious, my son, very hard to reach for men who have not perfected
themselves. There, on the mountain Devasabha, rich in fruit and roots, is the
holy hermitage of Trinasomagni, a pupil of Agastya. There is the splendid
Vaidurya mountain, made of jewels, auspicious, and the hermitage of Agastya,
rich in roots, fruit and water.

"I will tell you also of the holy shrines among the Surashtras, the hermitages,
rivers, mountains and lakes, lord of men. The brahmins speak of Chamasonmajjana
there, and of Prabhasa, the ford of the thirty gods by the sea, Yudhishthira.
There is the ford called Pindaraka, where ascetics go, auspicious, and the great
peak Ujjayanta, which quickly brings perfection. An ancient verse is heard about
it, told by Narada, the best of the divine seers. Hear it, Yudhishthira: 'On the
holy mountain Ujjayanta among the Surashtras, where beasts and birds live, one
who has burned his body in austerity is honoured on the vault of heaven.'

"There is holy Dvaravati, where Madhusudana lives. He is the ancient god
himself. He is the eternal dharma. The brahmins who know the Veda and the people
who know the self call the great Krishna the eternal dharma. For Govinda is
called the highest purifier of all purifiers, the holiest of the holy, the most
auspicious of the auspicious. The lotus-eyed god of gods, eternal, Hari of
unthinkable self, Madhusudana, lives there, and he is the three worlds.

---

"I will tell you, in the western quarter, among the Avantis, of the pure things
there and the holy shrines. There, Bharata, is the holy river Narmada, flowing
west, bordered with groves of priyangu and mango, garlanded with thickets of
cane. There, they say, is the holy dwelling of the sage Vishravas, where Kubera,
the lord of wealth, who is carried by men, was born. There is the holy and fine
mountain called Vaiduryashikhara, whose trees have green leaves and divine
flowers and fruit. On the peak of that mountain there is a lake, king, with
lotuses in bloom, where gods and gandharvas go. Many wonders are seen on that
mountain, great king, holy, like heaven, divine, where the divine seers always
go.

"There is the river of the royal seer, with deep pools and holy fords, the river
Para of Vishvamitra, holy, conqueror of enemy cities. On its bank, among good
men, Yayati, son of Nahusha, fell, and won again the eternal worlds of dharma.
There is the holy lake, my son, and the mountain Mainaka, and the mountain named
Asita, rich in roots and fruit, hero. There is the holy hermitage of Kakshasena,
Yudhishthira, and the hermitage of Chyavana, famous everywhere, Pandava, where
men reach perfection with little austerity, lord. There is Jambumarga, great
king, the hermitage of the seers of perfected spirit, the best of the calm, full
of beasts and birds. Then, king, there are the holiest Ketumala and Medhya,
always full of ascetics, and Gangaranya, lord of the earth, and the famous
Saindhavaranya, holy, where brahmins go.

"There is the Grandsire's holy lake called Pushkara, Bharata, the dear hermitage
of the Vaikhanasas, the siddhas and the seers. Prajapati himself sang a verse to
praise Pushkara, best of the Kurus, best of those who do good. 'Even the wise
man who only longs in his mind for Pushkara has his sins destroyed, and he
rejoices on the vault of heaven.'

---

"I will tell you of the holy things in the northern quarter, tiger among kings,
and the holy shrines. There is the Sarasvati, which carries merit, with its deep
pools, garlanded with forests, and the swift Yamuna that runs to the sea,
Pandava. There is the holiest and most auspicious ford, Plakshavatarana, where
brahmins, after sacrificing with the rites of the Sarasvati, go for the closing
bath. There is the holy and divine Agnishiras, they say, sinless one, where
Sahadeva sacrificed by throwing the peg, Bharata. On this very matter,
Yudhishthira, a verse that Indra sang goes about the world, sung by the
twice-born. 'The fires that Sahadeva piled along the Yamuna were a hundred
hundred thousand, with fees of a hundred thousand.' There the famous emperor
King Bharata offered thirty-five horse sacrifices.

"I heard long ago, my son, of one who granted the wishes of the twice-born. His
very holy hermitage is the famous Saraka. The river Sarasvati is always honoured
by the good, son of Pritha, where the Valakhilya seers sacrificed long ago,
great king. The Drishadvati is famous there as the holiest, Yudhishthira. There
were the two very holy seers Vaivarnya and Varna, lord of men, knowing the Veda
and known in the Veda, masters of learning. They were always sacrificing with
holy rites, best of the Bharatas. The gods with Indra and Varuna came together
many times and practised austerity at Vishakhayupa, so it is the holiest.

"The great seer, blessed and glorious Jamadagni, the mighty one, sacrificed at
the lovely, holy Palashakas. All the best of rivers came there in person to that
best of seers, each bringing her own water, and stood around him. There, great
king, Vishvavasu himself sang this verse, hero, when he saw the might of that
great man: 'When great Jamadagni sacrificed to the gods, all the rivers came and
satisfied them with honey.'

"The Ganga broke with her force through the mountain at Gangadvara,
Yudhishthira, the best of peaks, adorned by gandharvas, yakshas, rakshasas and
apsarases, the home of Kiratas and kinnaras. That place is famed as holy, king,
where hosts of brahmin seers go. So is Sanatkumara, Kauravya, and holy
Kanakhala, and the mountain named Puru, where Pururavas was born. There Bhrigu
practised austerity, where hosts of great seers go. That hermitage is famed,
king, as Bhrigutunga, the great mountain.

"Narayana is what was, what will be and what is, bull among men, the lord
Vishnu, eternal, the highest person. Along the wide Badari the most glorious one
has a holy hermitage, famous in the three worlds. Along the wide Badari, king,
the Ganga carries hot water, and another stream cold, with sands of gold. There
the blessed seers and gods of great power come always and bow to the god
Narayana, the lord. Where the god Narayana is, the eternal highest self, there
is the whole world, son of Pritha, and all the fords and shrines. That is holy,
that is the highest brahman, that is the ford, that is the forest of austerity.
The divine seers are there, the siddhas and all who are rich in austerity. Where
the first god, the great yogi Madhusudana, lives, that is the holiest of the
holy. Have no doubt of it.

"These, king, are the holy fords and shrines on the earth, lord of the earth,
that I have told you of, best of men. Vasus, sadhyas, Adityas, Maruts and
Ashvins, and seers like Brahma, great-hearted, have gone to them. Travelling to
them, son of Kunti, with the bulls among brahmins and your blessed brothers, you
will be rid of your longing."

**Divergences.**

* **Mathura.** A rejected line (\*461, after 3.86.20) says Narayana left his serpent couch in the
  ocean of milk and came to Mathura. Not in the CE; Dhaumya speaks only of Dvaravati (3.86.21).
* **Gaya.** The vulgate adds to the Gaya verse the horse sacrifice and the freed dark bull (\*456).
  In the CE Dhaumya quotes only the first line (3.85.7); Pulastya gave the whole verse at 3.82.85.

**Choices.**

* 3.84.4 *triyugau*: "of the three ages"; the sense is uncertain.
* 3.84.9–12: Yudhishthira's figure of Karna as a forest fire and Arjuna as the cloud that puts it out
  is kept whole.
* 3.85.5 *śāmitram*: "the slaughtering place", where sacrificial animals are killed.
* 3.85.11–12: Jamadagnya's verse is taken to be 3.85.12, Vishvamitra's leaving the kshatriya order.
* 3.85.20 *naiṣadhaḥ*: the Naishadha, Nala.
* 3.86.6: the verse on Nriga's sacrifice is kept, with Indra "drunk with soma".
* 3.86.11 *kumāryaḥ*: "The holy maidens"; perhaps a ford, the sense is uncertain.
* 3.86.14 *tṛṇasomāgneḥ*: Trinasomagni, Agastya's pupil.
* 3.86.21–24: Dhaumya's praise of Krishna at Dvaravati as the eternal dharma is in the CE.
* 3.87.3 *naravāhanaḥ*: Kubera, "who is carried by men".
* 3.88.4 *sahadevaḥ*: an ancient king Sahadeva, not the Pandava. *śamyākṣepeṇa*: "by throwing the peg",
  a rite in which the sacrificer moves on by the distance of a thrown peg.
* 3.88.7 *viṃśatiṃ sapta cāṣṭau ca*: "thirty-five" horse sacrifices.
* 3.88.8: the one "who granted the wishes of the twice-born" is not named in the text.
* 3.88.10 *vaivarṇyavarṇau*: "the two seers Vaivarnya and Varna"; the reading is uncertain, and Ganguli's
  text differs.
* 3.88.23: the hot and cold streams at Badari are as the text has them.

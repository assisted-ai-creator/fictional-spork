# The Rescue

"My friend," said Yudhishthira, "these Kauravas have come to us in fear and
trouble, seeking shelter. How can you talk like that? There are quarrels among
kinsmen, Vrikodara, and there are feuds, and they go on. But the dharma of
kinsmen is never lost. When some outsider attacks the family, good men do not
bear that attack from outside. This fool knows that we have lived here a long
time. He has shown us contempt in doing this thing that displeases us. By the
gandharva's seizing of Duryodhana in battle, and by the outsider's laying hands
on our women, our family is struck down.

"So stand up, tigers among men, and make ready without delay, to save those who
have come for shelter and to protect our family. Arjuna, and the twins, and you,
Bhima, never defeated: free Suyodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, who is being
carried off. Here are the chariots, tigers among men, with every kind of weapon,
yoked, with golden banners, and Indrasena and the other drivers. Mount them, my
friends, and do your utmost, and do not tire, to fight the gandharvas and set
Suyodhana free.

"Any kshatriya at all would protect with all his strength a stranger who had
come here for shelter. How much more you, Vrikodara? When he is called to with
'Run to help me!', who else could be a refuge here? Surely he would help even an
enemy, seeing him come for shelter with joined palms. Granting a boon, a
kingdom, the birth of a son, and freeing an enemy from trouble, Pandava: the
three and the one are equal. What could be greater than this, that Suyodhana,
fallen into trouble, seeks his life by relying on the strength of your arms?

"I would run there myself, Vrikodara, hero, if this sacrifice were not under
way. I have no doubt of that. Try every means, Bhima, joy of the Kurus, to free
Suyodhana by peaceful words. If that king of the gandharvas will not yield to
peaceful words, free Suyodhana by gentle force. And if he will not let the
Kauravas go for gentle fighting, Bhima, then they must be freed by every means,
and the robbers held down. This is all I can charge you with, Vrikodara,
Bharata, while the rite of the sacred fires is under way and going on."

When he heard those words of Ajatashatru, Dhananjaya promised, at his elder's
word, to free the Kauravas. "If the gandharvas will not let the sons of
Dhritarashtra go for peaceful words," said Arjuna, "the earth will drink the
blood of the king of the gandharvas today."

When they heard that promise of Arjuna, who spoke the truth, the Kauravas'
hearts came back to them, king.

---

When they heard Yudhishthira's words, the bulls among men, with Bhimasena at
their head, all rose with glad faces. Then all the great chariot fighters put on
their armour, which could not be pierced, bright with river gold. Armoured, with
their chariots and banners and bows, the Pandavas looked like blazing fires. The
tigers among chariot fighters mounted those well-furnished chariots, yoked to
swift horses, and went off at once.

Then there was a great noise in the Kaurava army, when they saw the great
chariot fighters, the sons of Pandu, setting out together. In a moment the
rovers of the sky, flushed with victory and swift, great chariot fighters too,
came together in that forest, as if without fear. Then all the gandharvas,
flushed with victory, turned back when they saw the four heroic Pandavas on
their chariots in battle. Seeing them shining like the guardians of the world,
raised up for battle, the dwellers on Gandhamadana stood with their ranks drawn
up.

Having heard the words of the wise king, the Dharma King, they began the battle
in order, and gently, Bharata. But the soldiers of the gandharva king,
dull-witted, could not then be brought to their own good by gentleness. So
Savyasachin, the scorcher of enemies, hard to overcome in battle, spoke to the
rovers of the sky in the field, first in words of peace. "This is not fit for
the king of the gandharvas. It is a deed to be despised, this laying hands on
other men's wives and this fighting with men. Let go these sons of
Dhritarashtra, men of great courage, and set their wives free, at the Dharma
King's command."

At these words of the glorious Pandava, the gandharvas smiled and said this to
Partha. "There is only one on earth whose word we would do, my friend, one whose
command we obey and so go free from care. We do as that one commands, Bharata.
We have no other master than that lord of the gods."

At the gandharvas' words, Dhananjaya son of Kunti spoke to them again. "If you
will not let the son of Dhritarashtra go for peaceful words, gandharvas, I shall
free Suyodhana myself, by force."

When he had said this, Partha Savyasachin Dhananjaya loosed sharp arrows, rovers
of the sky against the rovers of the sky. In the same way the gandharvas, fierce
in their strength, came at the Pandavas with a rain of arrows, and the Pandavas
at the dwellers in heaven. Then there was a wild battle, Bharata, between the
swift gandharvas and the Pandavas, fearsome in their speed.

---

Then the gandharvas, masters of divine weapons, wearing golden garlands,
surrounded them on every side, loosing blazing arrows. The four heroic Pandavas
and the gandharvas in their thousands met in battle, king, and it was a wonder
to see. As the gandharvas had cut to pieces the chariots of Karna and the son of
Dhritarashtra, they tried the same with theirs, in their hundreds. But the
tigers among men met the gandharvas rushing at them in their hundreds, king,
with rain after rain of arrows. Covered on every side by rains of arrows, the
sky-goers could not come near the sons of Pandu.

When he saw the gandharvas in their fury, Arjuna took aim and began to use his
great divine weapons. With the fire weapon, Arjuna, fierce in his strength, sent
a million gandharvas to Yama's house in that battle. So too Bhima, the great
archer, best of the strong, killed gandharvas in hundreds in battle with his
sharp arrows, king. The sons of Madri, fierce in their strength, fought in the
same way, and took on the enemy in front of them and killed them in hundreds.

As they were being killed by the great warriors with divine weapons, the
gandharvas flew up into the sky, taking the sons of Dhritarashtra with them. But
when Dhananjaya son of Kunti saw that they were about to fly up, he surrounded
them on every side with a great net of arrows. Shut in by the net of arrows like
birds in a cage, they rained maces, spears and swords on Arjuna in their fury.
Dhananjaya, who knew the great weapons, struck down those showers of maces,
spears and swords, and struck the gandharvas' bodies with broad-headed arrows.
With heads and feet and arms falling like a shower of stones, fear came on the
enemy.

As they were being killed by the great Pandava, the gandharvas in the sky poured
rains of arrows on him where he stood on the ground. But Savyasachin, the
scorcher of enemies, full of splendour, held back their rains of arrows with his
weapons and struck the gandharvas in turn. The joy of the Kurus loosed the
sthunakarna, the indrajala, the weapon of the sun, the fire weapon and the
weapon of the moon. Burned by the arrows of Kunti's son, the gandharvas fell
into the deepest despair, like the daityas before Shakra. When they tried to
climb upward, they were held back by the net of arrows. When they tried to slip
away on every side, Savyasachin held them back with broad-headed arrows.

When he saw the gandharvas terrified by Kunti's wise son, Chitrasena seized a
mace and rushed at Savyasachin. As he came swiftly on in the battle, mace in
hand, Partha cut his mace, made all of iron, into seven pieces with his arrows.
When the mighty king of the gandharvas saw his mace cut into many pieces by the
swift Pandava's arrows, he hid himself by his magic and fought the Pandava.
Standing in the sky, he fought against his divine weapons, hidden by illusion.
Seeing that he was striking while hidden, Arjuna struck him with sky-going
arrows charged by the spells of divine weapons.

Then Arjuna in his anger, taking many forms, used the art of shooting at sound
and set about destroying Chitrasena's power to hide. As he was being struck by
those weapons of the great Arjuna, his dear friend showed himself to him. When
he saw that it was his friend Chitrasena, weakened in battle, the bull among the
Pandavas withdrew the weapon he had loosed. Seeing Dhananjaya withdraw his
weapon, all the Pandavas held back their galloping horses, the force of their
arrows and their bows. Then Chitrasena, Bhima, Savyasachin and the twins asked
after each other's welfare, and stayed where they were in their chariots.

---

Then the great archer Arjuna, full of splendour, laughed and said to Chitrasena
in the midst of the gandharva armies, "What did you mean by holding the
Kauravas, hero? And why was Suyodhana taken with his wives?"

"The great one knew their purpose while he was there, Dhananjaya," said
Chitrasena, "the purpose of wicked Duryodhana and of Karna. They knew that you
were living in the forest and suffering what you do not deserve, and they came
to laugh at you and at glorious Draupadi. When the lord of the gods knew what
they meant to do, he said to me, 'Go and bind Duryodhana with his counsellors
and bring him here. And you must protect Dhananjaya and his brothers in the
battle, for the Pandava is your dear friend and your pupil.' So at the word of
the king of the gods I came here quickly. This wicked man is bound, and I shall
go to the home of the gods."

"Let Suyodhana go, Chitrasena," said Arjuna. "He is our brother. It is the
Dharma King's order, if you want to please me."

"This wicked man is always spiteful," said Chitrasena. "He does not deserve to
be freed. He has deceived the Dharma King and Krishna, Dhananjaya. The son of
Kunti, the Dharma King of great vows, does not know what he meant to do. Let him
hear it, and then do as you wish."

Then they all went to King Yudhishthira, and when they had come, they told him
all of Duryodhana's wicked plan. When Ajatashatru had heard the gandharva's
words, he had all of them freed, and he praised the gandharvas.

"By good fortune," he said, "you, who are mighty and could have done it, did not
kill this ill-behaved son of Dhritarashtra with his counsellors, kinsmen and
family. You have done me a great kindness, sky-goers, my friends. By the freeing
of this wicked man my family has not been shamed. Tell me what you wish. We are
glad to have seen you. When you have had all you want, go, and do not delay."

With leave from the wise son of Pandu, the gandharvas went away rejoicing with
the apsarases, with Chitrasena at their head. And the king of the gods brought
back to life, with a divine shower of amrita, those gandharvas who had been
killed by the Kauravas in the battle.

When they had freed their kinsmen and all the king's women, and had done that
hard deed, the Pandavas were full of joy. Honoured by the Kurus with their women
and children, the great chariot fighters, those great men, shone in the midst of
the Kurus like fires.

Then, when Duryodhana had been freed with his brothers, Yudhishthira spoke to
him with affection. "My friend, never again do anything so rash as this. Those
who act rashly do not prosper in happiness, Bharata. Go home safely with all
your brothers, as you wish, joy of the Kurus, and do not be downcast."

With the Pandava's leave, King Duryodhana went off towards the city, torn apart
with shame. When the Kaurava had gone, heroic Yudhishthira son of Kunti stayed
with his brothers, honoured by the brahmins. Surrounded by all those rich in
austerity, like Shakra by the immortals, he lived happily in that Dvaitavana
forest.

**Divergences.**

* **"We are a hundred and five."** In several rejected passages (\*1119, \*1121, \*1122) Yudhishthira says that among themselves the cousins are five and a hundred, but against outsiders they are a hundred and five. The line is not in the CE, and not in Ganguli's text either. Not narrated. See L-148.
* **Bhima's grievances and Duryodhana's cries.** A rejected appendix passage (@23) has Bhima answer Yudhishthira by recalling the house of lac, the dice game, and Draupadi dragged by the hair and stripped. In it Duryodhana, as he is carried off, also cries out to Yudhishthira for rescue. Not narrated: in the CE Arjuna answers with his vow, and the brothers rise gladly (3.232.19–3.233.1).
* **The Kauravas' chariots.** A rejected line (\*1120) makes the chariots the sons of Dhritarashtra's. The CE does not say whose they are.
* **Chitrasena reports to Indra.** A rejected passage (\*1132) has Chitrasena go back to heaven and tell Indra everything, and another (\*1133) describes Duryodhana freed from fetters. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*1118, \*1123–\*1131, \*1134) add small details or repeat what is said. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 3.232.3 *bāhyenābhipramarṣaṇam*: "that attack from outside".
* 3.232.8: Indrasena is Yudhishthira's charioteer.
* 3.232.12: "Granting a boon, a kingdom, the birth of a son, and freeing an enemy from trouble: the three and the one are equal." The saying is kept as it stands.
* 3.232.17 *paripanthinaḥ*: "the robbers".
* 3.232.18 *vaitāne karmaṇi*: "the rite of the sacred fires". Yudhishthira's one-day sacrifice (3.229.14).
* 3.233.8 *gandhamādanavāsinaḥ*: "the dwellers on Gandhamadana", the gandharvas.
* 3.233.19 *khacarān khacarān prati*: "rovers of the sky against the rovers of the sky". The arrows and the gandharvas are both *khacara*, "moving in the sky"; the novel keeps the play on the word.
* 3.234.7 *sahasrāṇāṃ sahasram*: "a million", a thousand thousands.
* 3.234.17: the weapons are named as the text names them: *sthūṇākarṇa*, *indrajāla*, *saura*, *āgneya* and *saumya*.
* 3.234.24 *śabdavedhyam*: "the art of shooting at sound". *bahurūpaḥ*: "taking many forms".
* 3.235.3 *tatrasthena mahātmanā*: "the great one knew their purpose while he was there". Indra, in heaven.
* 3.235.9 *pralabdhā*: "he has deceived".
* 3.235.17: Indra brings back only the gandharvas killed by the Kauravas. The text does not mention those Arjuna killed.

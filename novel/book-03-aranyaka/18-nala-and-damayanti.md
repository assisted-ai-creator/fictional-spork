# Nala and Damayanti

"There was a king named Nala," said Brihadashva, "the mighty son of Virasena. He
had every virtue men desire, he was handsome, and he knew horses. He stood at
the head of the lords of men like the lord of the gods, above them all, like the
sun in splendour. The king of the Nishadhas honoured brahmins, knew the Veda,
and was brave. He loved dice, he spoke the truth, and he was a great lord of
armies. The best of women longed for him. He was generous and master of his
senses, a protector, the best of bowmen, like Manu himself.

"In the same way there was among the Vidarbhas a king named Bhima, of terrible
courage, brave and full of every virtue. He wanted children, and he had none.
With his mind fixed, he made great efforts for the sake of children. A brahmin
seer named Damana came to him, Bharata. Bhima, who knew dharma and wanted
children, pleased the glorious seer with his queen, great king, and treated him
with honour. Damana was pleased, and he gave the king and his wife a boon: a
jewel of a daughter and three noble sons. They were Damayanti, Dama, Danta and
glorious Damana, full of every virtue, fierce and of fierce courage.

"Damayanti, slim-waisted, won fame in the worlds for her beauty, her splendour,
her glory, her grace and her good fortune. When she came of age, a hundred maids
and a hundred friends, well adorned, waited on her, as on Shachi. There Bhima's
daughter shone among her friends, adorned with every jewel, flawless in every
limb, like lightning in the clouds, very beautiful, with long eyes, like Shri
herself. Nowhere among the gods or the yakshas had such a beauty been seen or
heard of before, nor among men or any others. The girl troubled the hearts even
of the gods, she was so lovely.

"And Nala, tiger among men, had no equal in beauty on earth. He was like
Kandarpa, the god of love himself, in bodily form. People praised Nala in her
presence out of eagerness, and praised Damayanti again and again in the presence
of the Naishadha. Each heard the other's virtues all the time, and though they
had never seen each other, love grew in them, son of Kunti, each for the other.

"Nala could not hold his longing in his heart. He sat alone in a grove near the
women's quarters. There he saw geese with golden wings wandering in the grove,
and he caught one of the birds. Then the bird spoke to Nala. 'You must not kill
me, king. I will do what you want. I will speak of you to Damayanti, Naishadha,
so that she will never think of any man but you.'

"When he was told this, the king let the goose go. The geese flew up and went to
the Vidarbhas. When they came to the city of Vidarbha, the winged birds came
down near Damayanti, and she saw them. She was surrounded by her friends, and
when she saw those wonderful birds, she ran gladly to catch them. The geese
scattered all over the pleasure garden, and each of the girls ran after one of
them. The goose that Damayanti ran to near her spoke to her in a human voice.

"'Damayanti, there is a king among the Nishadhas named Nala. He is as handsome
as the Ashvins. Men are not his equal. If you were his wife, lovely one, your
birth and this beauty of yours would bear fruit, slim-waisted one. We have seen
gods, gandharvas, men, snakes and rakshasas, and we have never seen one like
him. You are the jewel of women, and Nala is the best of men. The union of the
best woman with the best man would be a fine thing.'

"When the goose said this, lord of the people, Damayanti said to it there,
'Speak like this to Nala too.'

"'So be it,' said the bird to the daughter of the king of Vidarbha, lord of the
people. It went back to the Nishadhas and told Nala everything.

---

"When Damayanti heard the goose's words, Bharata, from that time she was not at
ease, for thinking of Nala. She was lost in thought, miserable, pale-faced and
thin. She sighed and sighed. She gazed upwards, lost in thought, and looked like
one gone mad. She took no pleasure in bed or seat or any comfort. She did not
sleep by night or by day, and kept crying 'Alas! Alas!' Her friends knew from
her looks that she was not well and what was wrong with her.

"Then Damayanti's friends told the lord of Vidarbha, lord of men, that Damayanti
was not well. When King Bhima heard it from her friends, he thought hard about
what he must do for his daughter. The lord of the earth saw that his daughter
had come of age, and he saw that the thing to do was to hold a bridegroom choice
for Damayanti. He called together the lords of the earth, lord of the people.
'Heroes, come and take part in this bridegroom choice.'

"When all the kings heard of Damayanti's bridegroom choice, they came to Bhima
at his command. They filled the earth with the din of elephants, horses and
chariots, with fine armies, splendidly adorned, bright with many-coloured
garlands and ornaments.

"At that very time the two ancient seers, great-hearted, the best of seers, had
gone from here to the world of Indra as they wandered. Narada and Parvata, great
men of great vows, entered the palace of the king of the gods and were greatly
honoured. The thousand-eyed lord honoured them and asked after their lasting
welfare and their health, in every way.

"'All is well with us, god, in every way, lord,' said Narada. 'And in the whole
world, Maghavan, the kings are well, lord.'

"When he heard Narada's words, the killer of Bala and Vritra asked, 'There are
lords of the earth who know dharma and fight without thought for their lives.
They go to their deaths by the sword when their time comes, never turning their
backs. This world is theirs for ever, as it is mine, a cow that yields every
wish. Where are those brave kshatriyas? I do not see them, the lords of the
earth, my dear guests, coming here.'

"When Shakra said this, Narada answered him. 'Hear from me, blessed one, why the
lords of the earth are not to be seen. The daughter of the king of Vidarbha,
famed as Damayanti, has passed all the women on earth in beauty. Her bridegroom
choice will be held soon, Shakra. All the kings and princes are going there. The
lords of the earth desire her, the jewel of the world, and long for her above
all, killer of Bala and Vritra.'

"While this was being said, the guardians of the world, with Agni, the best of
the immortals, came to the king of the gods. They all heard Narada's great news.
When they heard it, they said gladly, 'We will go too.' Then all of them, great
king, with their followers and their mounts, went to the Vidarbhas, where all
the lords of the earth had gone.

"King Nala too, son of Kunti, heard of the gathering of kings, and he came with
a glad heart, devoted to Damayanti. On the way the gods saw Nala standing on the
ground. He was like the god of love standing there in bodily form, so rich was
his beauty. When the guardians of the world saw him, bright as the sun, they
stood still, their plans forgotten, amazed at his beauty. Then the dwellers in
heaven stopped their chariots in the air, came down from the sky, and spoke to
the Naishadha.

"'Naishadha, king of kings, Nala, you are true to your vows. Help us. Be our
messenger, best of men.'

---

"Nala promised them, Bharata, 'I will do it.' Then he stood with joined hands
and asked them, 'Who are you? And who is he whose messenger you want me to be?
And what must I do there? Tell me truly.'

"When the Naishadha said this, Maghavan answered. 'Know that we are immortals,
come for Damayanti. I am Indra, this is Agni, and this is the lord of the
waters. And this, king, is Yama, who brings the bodies of men to their end. Tell
Damayanti that we have come. Say, "The guardians of the world, with Indra, are
coming, wishing to see you. The gods Shakra, Agni, Varuna and Yama want to win
you. Choose one of these gods for your husband."'

"When Shakra said this, Nala joined his hands and said, 'Do not send me. I have
come for the same purpose.'

"'You promised us before, Naishadha, "I will do it,"' said the gods. 'Why will
you not do it? Go, Naishadha, without delay.'

"When the gods said this, the Naishadha spoke again. 'The houses are well
guarded. How can I hope to enter?'

"'You will enter,' Shakra told him again.

"'So be it,' he said, and went to Damayanti's house. There he saw the princess
of Vidarbha, surrounded by her friends, lovely, blazing with beauty and grace.
Her limbs were very tender, her waist slender, her eyes fine, and with her own
splendour she seemed to put the light of the moon to shame. As soon as he saw
her, sweetly smiling, his love grew. But wanting to keep his word, he held back
his desire.

"When they saw the Naishadha, those fine women were flustered. They sprang up
from their seats, overcome by his splendour. Amazed and delighted, they praised
Nala. They did not speak to him, but they thought in their hearts, 'Ah, what
beauty! Ah, what grace! Ah, the steadiness of this great man! Who can he be? A
god, a yaksha, or a gandharva?' But those lovely women could not say a word to
him, all overcome by his splendour and shy.

"Then Damayanti, amazed, with the half smile she always wore when she spoke,
said to the hero Nala, 'Who are you, flawless in every limb, who make my love
grow? You have come here like an immortal, hero. I want to know you, sinless
one. How did you come here, and how were you not seen? My house is well guarded,
and the king's orders are strict.'

"When the princess of Vidarbha said this, Nala answered her. 'Know that I am
Nala, lovely one, come here as the gods' messenger. The gods want to win you,
Shakra, Agni, Varuna and Yama. Choose one of those gods for your husband,
beautiful one. It is by their power that I came in unseen. No one saw me come
in, and no one stopped me. The best of the gods sent me here for this, good
lady. Now that you have heard it, lovely one, make up your mind as you wish.'

---

"She bowed to the gods, laughed, and said to Nala, 'Show me your love as you
truly feel it, king. What can I do for you? I myself, and whatever else I own,
all of it is yours. Trust me. Show me your love, lord. The word the geese
brought burns me, king. It was for you, hero, that I had the kings called
together. If you reject me, giver of honour, when I am yours, I will take
poison, or fire, or water, or a rope, because of you.'

"When the princess of Vidarbha said this, Nala answered her. 'When the guardians
of the world are standing by, how can you want a man? Set your heart on those
great lords, makers of the world. I am not worth the dust of their feet. For a
mortal who does what displeases the gods meets his death. Save me, you who are
flawless in every limb. Choose the best of the gods.'

"Then Damayanti, of the bright smile, spoke slowly to King Nala in a voice thick
with tears. 'I have seen a way, lord of men, a way without harm, by which no
fault will come to you at all, king. You, best of men, and the gods with Agni at
their head, let all of you come together to the place of my bridegroom choice.
Then, in the presence of the guardians of the world, I will choose you, tiger
among men, lord of men. So there will be no fault.'

"When the princess of Vidarbha said this, lord of the people, King Nala went
back to where the gods were gathered. The guardians of the world, with their
lords, saw him coming, and when they saw him they asked him all that had
happened.

"'Did you see Damayanti of the bright smile, king?' said the gods. 'And what did
she say to all of us? Tell us, sinless lord of the earth.'

"'At your command I entered Damayanti's house,' said Nala. 'It has great courts,
guarded by old men with staffs. No one saw me as I went in, by your power,
except the princess herself. I saw her friends, and they saw me too. They were
all amazed when they saw me, lords of the gods. While I was speaking of you, the
lovely-faced one set her mind on me alone, and she chooses me, best of the gods.
And the girl said to me, "Let the gods all come together, with you, best of men,
to the place of my bridegroom choice. In their presence I will choose you, best
of men. So no fault will be yours, strong-armed one." That is all, gods, just as
it happened, and I have told it in full. Now you must decide, lords of the
thirty gods.'

**Divergences.**

* **The geese.** A rejected passage (\*212, after 3.50.31) says the geese were gods and
  gandharvas under a curse of Indra's, freed by Nala's touch. Not in the CE.
* **Nala's plea.** Rejected lines (\*216, \*219) give Nala a fuller protest and have Indra
  promise to hide him by illusion. In the CE Indra says only "You will enter" (3.52.10), and
  Nala tells her he came in unseen "by their power" (3.52.23).
* **Nala's praise of the gods.** An appendix passage (@8, after 3.53.7) has Nala describe the
  gods' gifts to Damayanti at length. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* The story is told by Brihadashva to Yudhishthira, and sits inside his quotation marks, as

  confusion.

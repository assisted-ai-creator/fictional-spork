# Agastya Drinks the Ocean

"I want to hear more, best of the twice-born," said Yudhishthira, "of the deeds
of that wise great seer Agastya, in full."

"Hear, king, a divine story, wonderful and more than human," said Lomasha, "of
the power of Agastya, of boundless spirit, great king.

"In the Krita age there were terrible danavas, wild in battle, in fearsome hosts
known as the Kaleyas. Taking Vritra as their refuge, with weapons of every kind
raised, they chased the gods with great Indra at their head on every side. Then
the thirty gods, set on killing Vritra long ago, put Purandara at their head and
went to Brahma. As they stood with joined hands, the Supreme One said to them
all, 'Gods, I know all that you want to do. I will tell you the way to kill
Vritra. There is a great seer of noble mind, famed as Dadhicha. Go to him all
together and ask him for a boon. He is righteous, and he will give it with a
glad heart. All of you together, longing for victory, must say to him, "Give us
your bones, for the good of the three worlds." He will give up his body and give
you his bones. With his bones make a terrible, strong thunderbolt, great, sharp,
six-edged, with a fearful roar, to kill the enemy. With that thunderbolt
Shatakratu will kill Vritra. I have told you everything. Now do it quickly.'

"When he said this, the gods took leave of the Grandsire, put Narayana at their
head, and went to Dadhicha's hermitage. It was on the far bank of the Sarasvati,
covered with trees and creepers of every kind. It was loud with the humming of
bees, as if with singers of the Sama, mixed with the calls of the male cuckoo
and the chirp of the jivamjivaka. Buffaloes, boars, sharmara deer and yaks
wandered here and there, free of fear of tigers. Elephants and cow elephants,
with their temples running with rut, played in the lake and filled the place
with sound. Lions and tigers roared great roars, and others lay hidden, living
in caves and hollows. Here and there it was lovely and delightful, like heaven
itself. There they came to Dadhicha's hermitage.

"There they saw Dadhicha, as bright as the sun, blazing in body like the
Grandsire in his glory. The gods greeted his feet, king, and bowed, and asked
all together for the boon, as the Supreme One had told them. Then Dadhicha, full
of joy, said to those best of the gods, 'I will do what is good for you today,
gods, and I will give up my own body.' When he had said this, the best of men,
master of himself, gave up his life at once. Then the gods took the bones of the
dead man, as they had been told.

"Glad and sure of victory, the gods went to Tvashtri and told him what they
needed. When Tvashtri heard their words, he was glad, and with great care and
effort he made a thunderbolt, very terrible in form. When he had made it, he
said gladly to Shakra, 'With this finest of thunderbolts, god, burn the fierce
enemy of the gods to ashes today. Then, your enemy dead, rule all of heaven
happily with your hosts, living in the sky.' When Tvashtri said this, Purandara
gladly and carefully took the thunderbolt.

---

"Then the wielder of the thunderbolt, guarded by the mighty gods, came against
Vritra, who stood filling heaven and earth. He was guarded on every side by the
huge Kalakeyas with their weapons raised, like mountains with peaks. Then there
was a great battle of the gods with the danavas for a while, best of the
Bharatas, terrifying the worlds. There was a tremendous noise of swords raised
and crashing, wielded by heroes' arms, falling on bodies. Heads falling from the
sky to the ground looked like palm fruit fallen from their stalks, lord of the
earth. The Kaleyas, in golden armour, with iron bars for weapons, attacked the
thirty gods like mountains burnt by a forest fire. The gods could not bear the
force of that swift host charging together. They broke and fled in fear.

"When the thousand-eyed Purandara saw them fleeing in terror, and Vritra
growing, a great faintness came over him. When eternal Vishnu saw Shakra
overcome by faintness, he put his own power into Shakra and made his strength
grow. When the hosts of the gods saw Shakra strengthened by Vishnu, they each
gave him their own power, and so did the spotless brahmin seers. Strengthened by
Vishnu, by the gods and by the blessed seers, Shakra became mighty.

"When Vritra knew that the lord of the thirty gods was strong, he roared great
roars. At his roaring the earth, the quarters, the air, the sky and the
mountains all shook. Then great Indra, burning with fear when he heard that
great and terrible roar, sank in terror and quickly hurled the great thunderbolt
to kill him, king. Struck by Shakra's thunderbolt, the great asura fell, wearing
his golden garland, as the great mountain Mandara fell long ago when it slipped
from Vishnu's hand. When the chief of the daityas was killed, Shakra ran in
terror to hide in a lake. He did not believe the thunderbolt had left his hand,
and in his fear he did not believe that Vritra was dead.

"All the gods, glad and joyful, and the great seers praised Indra. The gods
quickly gathered and killed all the daityas, who were burning with grief at
Vritra's death. As the thirty gods cut them down, they entered the ocean in
terror. When they had entered the boundless ocean, full of fish and full of
jewels, they held a council together, smiling, to destroy the three worlds. Some
of them, who knew how to decide, described one plan and another. As they
thought, a terrible idea came to them, fitting the time and the order of things.
'Those who have learning and austerity must be destroyed first. For all the
worlds are held up by austerity. So be quick to destroy austerity. Let all the
ascetics on the earth who know dharma and know that, be killed at once. When
they are destroyed, the world will be destroyed.' So they all lost their reason,
and rejoiced at the thought of the world's destruction. They took as their
fortress Varuna's home, the mine of jewels, with its great waves.

---

"Taking shelter in the ocean, the treasure house of the waters, Varuna's own,
the Kaleyas set about destroying the three worlds. At night, in fury, they ate
the sages in the hermitages and the holy shrines. In Vasishtha's hermitage those
wicked ones ate a hundred and eighty-eight brahmins and nine other ascetics.
They went to Chyavana's holy hermitage, where brahmins go, and ate a hundred
sages who lived on fruit and roots. So they did by night, and by day they
entered the sea. At Bharadvaja's hermitage they struck down twenty restrained
and chaste men, who lived on air and water. So in turn the danavas ran through
all the hermitages by night, drunk with the strength of their arms, the Kaleyas,
driven by Time, killing hosts of brahmins.

"Men did not discover the daityas doing this to the ascetics, best of men. In
the morning the sages were seen lying on the ground, their bodies lifeless, thin
from their fasting, their flesh gone, their blood gone, their marrow and
entrails gone, their joints broken apart. The earth was covered with them, like
heaps of conch shells. It was covered with overturned water pots, broken ladles
and scattered sacred fires. The world had no recitation and no call of vashat,
its sacrifices and festivals were lost, and it was without heart, tormented by
fear of the Kaleyas.

"As they were destroyed like this, lord of men, men fled in fear in every
direction, thinking only of saving themselves. Some went into caves, some hid by
waterfalls, and some, in terror of death, gave up their lives out of fear. Some
great archers there, brave and very proud, made great efforts to find the
danavas. But they did not find them, hidden in the ocean, and they were worn
out, and came to destruction themselves.

"When the world had fallen still and its sacrifices, festivals and rites were
lost, the thirty gods fell into great distress, lord of men. With great Indra
they gathered and took counsel in their fear. They put Narayana at their head,
Vaikuntha, the unconquered, and the gathered gods said to Madhusudana:

"'You are our creator, our protector and our upholder, lord of the world. You
made all this, what moves and what does not. Long ago, lotus-eyed one, when the
earth was lost in the ocean, you took the form of a boar and raised it up for
the world's sake. You took the body of the Man-Lion and killed the first daitya,
the mighty Hiranyakashipu, highest of beings. The great asura Bali, whom no
being could kill, you drove from the three worlds, taking the body of a dwarf.
The asura called Jambha, the great archer, the cruel one who disturbed
sacrifices, you struck down. Such deeds as these, beyond counting, are yours.
You are our refuge, Madhusudana, in our fear. So, god, lord of the gods, we tell
you this for the world's sake. Protect the worlds, the gods and Shakra from this
great fear.

"'All creatures of the four kinds live by what is given from here. Nourished,
they nourish the dwellers in heaven with oblations to the gods and the Fathers.
So the worlds live, each depending on the other, free from trouble by your
grace, protected by you alone. Now this great fear has come to the worlds. We do
not know who is killing these brahmins by night. When the brahmins are gone, the
earth will perish. When the earth has perished, heaven will perish. By your
grace, strong-armed lord of the world, let all the worlds not come to
destruction, protected by you.'

"'Gods,' said Vishnu, 'I know the whole cause of the destruction of the
creatures. I will tell you too. Listen, free from fever. There is a terrible
host known as the Kaleyas. Relying on Vritra, they tormented the whole world.
When they saw Vritra killed by the wise thousand-eyed god, they entered the home
of Varuna to save their lives. Having entered the terrible ocean, full of
crocodiles and sharks, they kill the sages here by night to wipe out the worlds.
They cannot be destroyed, for they have taken refuge in the ocean. You must make
up your minds to destroy the ocean. And who but Agastya could dry up the sea?'

"When they heard these words of Vishnu's, the gods took leave of the Supreme One
and went to Agastya's hermitage. There they saw the great son of Varuna, blazing
with splendour, attended by the seers as the Grandsire is by the gods. They went
to the great, unshakable son of Mitra and Varuna, a mass of austerity, in his
hermitage, and praised him for his own deeds.

"'Long ago you were the refuge of the worlds when Nahusha tormented them. The
thorn in the world's side was thrown down from the lordship of the gods for the
world's sake. The Vindhya, best of mountains, suddenly grew in anger against the
sun, and now, not disobeying your word, the Vindhya does not grow. When the
world was covered in darkness and creatures were tormented by death, they came
to you as their lord and found the highest peace. You are always our refuge,
blessed one, when we are afraid. So in our distress we ask you for a boon, for
you are a giver of boons.'

---

"Why did the Vindhya suddenly grow, mad with anger?" said Yudhishthira. "I want
to hear this in full, great sage."

"The sun went round the king of mountains, the great mountain Meru of gold, at
its rising and setting, keeping it on his right," said Lomasha. "When the
Vindhya mountain saw this, it said to the sun, 'As you go round Meru every day
and keep it on your right, do the same for me, sun.'

"When it said this, the sun answered the lord of mountains, 'I do not go round
it by my own wish, mountain. This is the road laid down for me by the one who
made this world.' When he said this, the mountain suddenly grew in anger,
scourge of enemies, wanting to block the road of the sun and the moon. Then all
the gods together, with Indra, came to the great king of mountains and tried to
stop it by every means, but it did not do as they said.

"Then they went to the sage in his hermitage, the ascetic, the best of those who
uphold dharma, Agastya, blazing with wonderful power, and the gods together told
him their business. 'The Vindhya, king of mountains, in the grip of anger, is
blocking the road of the sun and the moon and the course of the stars. No one
else can stop it, best of the twice-born, but you, blessed one. So stop it.'

"When the brahmin heard the gods' words, he went to the mountain. He came to it
with his wife and said to the Vindhya, 'I want you to give me a road, best of
mountains. I am going to the southern quarter on some business. Wait for me
until I come back. When I have returned, lord of mountains, then grow as much as
you like.' When he had made this agreement with the Vindhya, scourge of enemies,
the son of Varuna went to the south, and to this day he has not come back from
the southern country. I have told you all you asked, how the Vindhya does not
grow, by the power of Agastya.

"Now hear from me, king, how the Kaleyas were destroyed by all the gods, when
they had won the boon from Agastya. When he heard the gods' words, the son of
Mitra and Varuna said, 'Why have you come? What boon do you want from me?' When
he said this, the gods said to the sage, 'This is what we want you to do, great
seer, great one. Drink up the great ocean. Then we will kill those enemies of
the gods called the Kaleyas, with all their kin.'

"When he heard the gods' words, the sage said, 'So be it. I will do what you
wish, and bring great happiness to the worlds.' When he had said this, the seer
of good vows went to the ocean, the lord of rivers, with the seers perfected by
austerity and with the gods. Men, snakes, gandharvas, yakshas and kimpurushas
followed the great man, wanting to see that wonder. Then together they came to
the ocean with its terrible roar. It seemed to dance with its waves, to leap
with the wind, and to laugh with its masses of foam, stumbling into its caves,
full of crocodiles of every kind and hosts of birds. The gods with Agastya, with
the gandharvas and great snakes and the blessed seers, came to the great ocean.

"When the blessed seer, the son of Varuna, came to the ocean, he said to the
gods and seers who had gathered, 'I will drink up the home of Varuna for the
good of the world. Quickly get ready what you have to do.' When he had said only
this, the unshakable son of Mitra and Varuna drank up the ocean in anger, as the
whole world watched. When the gods with Vasava saw the ocean being drunk, they
were filled with wonder and worshipped him with praises. 'You are our protector
and our ordainer, who bring the worlds into being. By your grace the world with
its immortals may not be destroyed.'

"Honoured by the thirty gods, while the gandharvas' music sounded on every side
and divine flowers were strewn on him, the great man left the great ocean
without water. When they saw the great ocean left without water, all the gods
were overjoyed. Taking up their fine divine weapons, with undaunted spirit, they
killed those danavas. As the great, mighty, swift gods cut them down, roaring,
the danavas could not bear the force of the swift, great dwellers in heaven. As
the thirty gods cut them down, the danavas, with their terrible roars, fought a
tumultuous battle for a moment, Bharata. Burned already by the austerity of the
sages of perfected spirit, they strove with all their strength and were killed
by the thirty gods. Wearing gold coins, earrings and armlets, killed, they
looked beautiful, like kimshuka trees in flower. A few of the Kaleyas who were
left, best of men, split open the goddess Earth and went down to the floor of
Patala.

"When the thirty gods saw the danavas killed, they praised the bull among sages
with many words. 'By your grace, blessed one, the worlds have found great
happiness, and by your power the Kaleyas of cruel courage have been killed. Now
fill the ocean, strong-armed one, who bring the worlds into being. Pour back
into it the water you drank.'

"When they said this, the blessed bull among sages answered, 'That water has
been digested by me. You must think of another way, and try to fill the ocean.'
When they heard these words of the great seer of perfected spirit, the gods
together were amazed and dismayed. They took leave of each other, bowed to the
bull among sages, and all the creatures went away as they had come, great king.
The thirty gods went with Vishnu to the Grandsire. When they had taken counsel
again and again about filling the ocean, they all joined their hands and spoke
of filling the sea."

**Divergences.**

* **The Vindhya.** In the familiar version the Vindhya bows down before Agastya and stays bowed. In
  the CE Agastya asks it for a road and tells it to wait until he comes back, and it "does not grow"
  (3.102.11–14). See L-127.
* **Vritra's body.** A rejected line (\*499) adds that the gods saw Vritra lying on the earth like a
  mountain struck by the thunderbolt. Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 3.98.13 *jīvaṃjīvaka*: the jivamjivaka, a bird. 3.98.14 *sṛmaraiḥ*: "sharmara deer"; the kind is
  uncertain.
* 3.98.21 *sahasā*: "at once", of Dadhicha giving up his life; the sense is of speed, not surprise.
* 3.99.14: the comparison with Mandara falling from Vishnu's hand is the text's.
* 3.99.15: Indra's flight into a lake, not believing Vritra dead, is in the CE.
* 3.99.3 and 3.99.2: the demons are called both Kaleyas and Kalakeyas, as in the text.
* 3.100.3: the numbers of the dead are given as the text gives them.
* 3.101.12 *vāruṇim*, 3.101.13 *maitrāvaruṇim*: Agastya is "the son of Varuna" and "the son of Mitra
  and Varuna".
* 3.101.15, 3.102.1 and 3.102.5 *sahasā*: "suddenly", of the Vindhya's growing.
* 3.102.13: "to this day he has not come back from the southern country" is Lomasha's word.
* 3.103.16 *jīrṇam*: "digested". The ocean's filling is the next story.

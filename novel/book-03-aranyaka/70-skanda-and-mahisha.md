# Skanda and Mahisha

"When Skanda had done this kindness for the Mothers," said Markandeya, "Svaha
said to him, 'You are my own son. I want a joy from you that is very hard to
win.' Skanda said to her, 'What joy do you want?'

"'I am Daksha's dear daughter, named Svaha, strong-armed one,' said Svaha. 'From
my childhood I have always longed for the eater of offerings. But Pavaka does
not truly know that I love him, son. I want to live with Agni always, son.'

"'Whatever offering to the gods or the Fathers the twice-born pour into the fire
with mantras,' said Skanda, 'from today they will always offer it saying
"Svaha," goddess, those of good conduct who stand on the good road. So Agni will
always live with you, fine lady.'

"At these words Svaha was pleased and honoured by Skanda, and joined with
Pavaka, her husband, she honoured Skanda.

"Then Brahma, Prajapati, said to the great general, 'Go to your father, the
great god, destroyer of Tripura. Rudra entered Agni, and Uma entered Svaha, and
so you were born, unconquered, for the good of all the worlds. The seed that the
great Rudra poured into Uma's womb fell on the mountain, and from it came
Minjika and Minjika. What was left of the seed fell into the red ocean, some
into the rays of the sun, some onto the earth, and some clung to the trees. So
it fell in five ways. The wise should know these hosts of many shapes as your
terrible companions, the eaters of flesh.'

"'So be it,' said the great general, of boundless spirit, loving his father, and
he honoured his father, the great lord. Those who want wealth should worship
those five hosts with arka flowers, and one should worship them to cure
sickness. The pair Minjika and Minjika, born of Rudra, should always be bowed to
here by one who wishes children well. The goddesses called Vriddhikas, women who
eat human flesh, born in trees, should be bowed to by those who want children.
So these countless hosts of pishachas are told of.

"Now hear from me, king, of the birth of his bell and his banner. Airavata's two
bells were called the Vaijayantis. Wise Shakra had them brought and gave them
himself to Guha. One of them belongs to Vishakha and the other to Skanda. The
banner of Karttikeya and of Vishakha is red. The mighty god, the great general,
plays with the toys the gods gave him then.

"Surrounded by hosts of pishachas and hosts of gods, he shone on the golden
mountain, blazing, wrapped in glory. The mountain with its fine forests shone
because of that hero, as lovely-caved Mandara shines with the rayed sun. The
White Mountain shone with its flowering groves of santanaka, karavira and
parijata, of japa and ashoka, with its clumps of kadamba trees, and with its
divine beasts and birds. All the hosts of the gods and all the great seers were
there, and the sounds of clouds and instruments, like the roar of the stirred-up
ocean. Divine gandharvas and apsarases danced there, and a great clamour of glad
creatures was heard. So the whole world, with Indra, stood on the White
Mountain, looking joyfully at Skanda, and it did not tire of looking.

---

"When the son of Pavaka had been consecrated as general, the glorious lord Hara
set out gladly for Bhadravata with Parvati in a chariot the colour of the sun. A
thousand lions were yoked to that finest of chariots, and urged on by time it
flew up into the bright sky. The lions with their fine manes went through the
sky roaring, as if drinking the air and terrifying all that moves and does not
move. Standing in that chariot with Uma, Pashupati shone like the sun with
lightning in a cloud with a rainbow.

"Ahead of him went the blessed Lord of Wealth with the guhyakas, riding the fine
Pushpaka, drawn by men. Shakra too, mounted on Airavata, followed behind the
boon-giver whose banner is the bull, with the gods. The great yaksha Amogha went
on the right wing, adorned with jambhakas, yakshas and rakshasas in garlands. On
his right went the Maruts, the gods who fight in wonderful ways, together with
the Vasus and the Rudras. Yama went with Death, surrounded on every side by
hundreds of terrible diseases, his own body terrible in form. Behind Yama went
Rudra's terrible, sharp, three-pointed spear, named Vijaya, well adorned. The
blessed Varuna, lord of the waters, with his fierce noose, went slowly round it,
surrounded by water creatures of many kinds. Behind Vijaya went Rudra's axe,
surrounded by the finest weapons, maces, clubs, spears and the rest. After the
axe went Rudra's great shining parasol, king, and after it his water pot,
surrounded by hosts of great seers. On its right shone his staff, going with
glory, with the Bhrigus and Angirases, honoured by the gods.

"Behind these went Rudra in a spotless chariot, delighting all the dwellers in
heaven with his splendour. The seers and gods, gandharvas and snakes, rivers,
streams and trees, the hosts of apsarases, the stars and planets, the children
of the gods, and women of many shapes went behind Rudra. They were fine women,
lovely, raining showers of flowers. Parjanya followed too, bowing to the bearer
of Pinaka. Soma held a white parasol over his head, and Vayu and Agni stood
holding the fly whisks. Shakra went behind him, king, wrapped in glory, with all
the royal seers, praising the one whose banner is the bull. Gauri, Vidya,
Gandhari, Keshini and the one called Mitra, with Savitri, all went behind
Parvati. All the hosts of the sciences made by the poets were there. One went
ahead holding the banner, whose bidding Indra and the gods at the head of the
army do. He was the rakshasa Graha, Rudra's constant friend, busy in the burning
ground, the lord of yakshas named Pingala, who gives joy to the world. With
these the god went as he pleased, now ahead and now behind, for his way is never
fixed. Mortals here worship Rudra, the god, with good deeds. They call him
Shiva, the lord, Rudra, bearer of Pinaka, and worship the great lord with
feelings of many kinds. So the general of the gods, surrounded by the armies of
the gods, devoted to brahmins, the son of the Krittikas, followed the lord of
the gods.

"Then the great god said these weighty words to the great general. 'Guard the
seventh wind-course always, without tiring.'

"'I will guard the seventh wind-course, lord,' said Skanda. 'And whatever else I
have to do, god, tell me at once.'

"'In your tasks, son, you must always look to me,' said Rudra. 'By seeing me and
by devotion you will win the highest good.'

---

"When he had said this, the great lord embraced him and sent him away. When
Skanda had been sent away, a great portent suddenly came, great king, and
bewildered all the gods. The sky blazed with its stars, the world was deeply
bewildered, the earth shook and roared, and the world became dark, lord. Seeing
that terrible thing, Shankara was shaken, and so were greatly blessed Uma and
the gods with the great seers. As they stood bewildered, a great, terrible army
appeared, like mountains and clouds, with weapons of many kinds. That terrible,
countless army, roaring with many voices, rushed at the gods and the blessed
Shankara in battle. They loosed on the ranks nets of arrows in many ways,
mountains and shataghnis, lances, iron bars and maces. Under those terrible
great weapons falling on it, the whole army of the gods broke in a moment and
was seen turning away. With its warriors, elephants and horses cut down, its
weapons and great chariots cut to pieces, the army of the gods, crushed by the
danavas, turned its back. Struck down by the asuras, it fell, like a forest of
great trees mostly burned by fires. With their heads and bodies split, the
dwellers in heaven fell. Struck down in that great battle, they found no
protector.

"Then the god Purandara, seeing that army routed and hard pressed by the
danavas, spoke strongly to comfort it. 'Give up fear, and bless you. Take up
your weapons, heroes. Set your minds on courage, and let no fear touch you.
Conquer these wicked danavas, terrible to see. Charge the great asuras with me,
and bless you.'

"At Shakra's words the dwellers in heaven took heart and fought the danavas
back, relying on Shakra. Then all the thirty gods and the mighty Maruts went out
at great speed, and the Sadhyas with the Vasus. The weapons they loosed on the
ranks in anger in the battle, and their arrows, drank the thick blood in the
daityas' bodies. Piercing their bodies, the sharp arrows could be seen coming
out of them like snakes from mountains. The daityas' bodies, split by the
arrows, fell to the ground on every side, king, like torn clouds. Then the army
of the danavas was terrified in battle by all the hosts of the gods with arrows
of many kinds and made to turn its back. Then all the gods raised their weapons
and shouted in joy, and all the instruments were struck together in many ways.

"So the battle between gods and danavas was most terrible, mixed together, with
a mud of flesh and blood. Suddenly the ruin of the world of the gods was seen,
for the terrible danavas were killing the dwellers in heaven. Then came the
sound of instruments and the great noise of kettledrums, and the terrible lion
roars of the lords of the danavas.

"Then out of the terrible army of the daityas rushed a mighty danava named
Mahisha, holding a huge mountain. Seeing him surrounded by them like the sun by
clouds, with the mountain raised, king, the dwellers in heaven fled. Mahisha
rushed at the gods and threw the mountain at them. By the falling mountain, of
terrible form, ten thousand of the army of the gods were killed and fell to the
ground, king. Then Mahisha with those danavas rushed quickly at the gods in
battle, terrifying them, as a lion rushes at small beasts. Seeing Mahisha
coming, the dwellers in heaven with Indra fled from the battle in fear, their
weapons and banners scattered.

"Then Mahisha in anger went quickly to Rudra's chariot, rushed at it and seized
the pole of Rudra's chariot. When Mahisha suddenly reached Rudra's chariot in
anger, heaven and earth groaned deeply and the great seers were bewildered. The
huge daityas, like clouds, roared, and they were sure: 'We have won.' When
things stood so, the blessed one did not kill Mahisha in battle. He remembered
Skanda, who was to be the death of that wicked one. Mahisha too, seeing Rudra's
chariot, roared again and again, terrifying the gods and gladdening the daityas.

"Then, when that terrible fear had come on the gods, the great general came,
blazing with anger like the sun. Dressed in red, adorned with red garlands,
red-faced, strong-armed, in golden armour, the lord stood in a chariot like the
sun, bright as gold. Seeing him, the army of the daityas suddenly fled from the
battle. And the mighty great general, king of kings, loosed his blazing spear
that splits Mahisha. Loosed, the spear struck Mahisha's great head. His head
split, Mahisha fell, his life gone. Thrown again and again, the spear killed
enemies in thousands and came back to Skanda's hand, as gods and danavas
watched.

"Most of them were killed with arrows by the wise great general. The rest of the
terrible hosts of daityas, frightened and terrified, were killed by Skanda's
unassailable companions and eaten in hundreds of crowds. Eating the danavas and
drinking their blood, the overjoyed companions made everything empty of danavas
in a moment. As the sun conquers the darkness, as fire conquers trees and the
bird conquers clouds, so glorious Skanda conquered his enemies by his own
courage. Honoured by the thirty gods, he saluted the great lord, and the son of
the Krittikas shone like the sun with its rays spread out.

"When his enemies were destroyed and Skanda had gone to the great lord,
Purandara embraced the great general. 'Skanda,' he said, 'you have killed this
Mahisha, who had a boon from Brahma, and to whom the gods were like straw, best
of conquerors. You have put down this thorn of the gods, strong-armed one. You
have killed in battle a hundred danavas equal to Mahisha, enemies of the gods,
who troubled us before. Your followers have eaten other danavas in hundreds of
crowds. You cannot be conquered by enemies in battle, lord, like Uma's husband.
This will be your first famous deed, god. Your fame will be unfailing in the
three worlds, and the gods will be in your power, son of the god.'

"When he had said this to the great general, the husband of Shachi turned back
with the gods, with the leave of the blessed Three-eyed One. Rudra went to
Bhadravata, and the dwellers in heaven turned back. Rudra told the gods, 'Look
on Skanda as on me.' Having killed the hosts of danavas, honoured by the great
seers, the son of Agni conquered the whole of the three worlds in a single day.
Whoever reads this birth of Skanda with a collected mind wins increase here and
goes to the world of Skanda."

**Divergences.**

* **Who killed Mahisha.** In the familiar story the buffalo-demon Mahisha is killed by the goddess Durga. In the CE he is the danava Mahisha, killed by Skanda with his spear
  (3.221.52–66). See L-146.
* **Mahisha's head.** A rejected passage (\*1088) says Mahisha's falling head blocked a pass sixteen leagues wide, through which the Northern Kurus now travel. Not narrated.
* **Rudra's words to Skanda.** A rejected passage (\*1087) has Rudra call Skanda and tell him to kill the wicked danava. In the CE Rudra only remembers Skanda (3.221.60).
* **Skanda's names.** An appendix passage (@22, after 3.221.80) has Yudhishthira ask for Skanda's names, with a long list. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 3.220.9–10: Brahma says Rudra entered Agni and Uma entered Svaha, and that Rudra's seed fell on the mountain; kept as the text gives it. See L-145.

* 3.220.16 *vṛddhikā*: the Vriddhikas.
* 3.221.1 *bhadravaṭam*: Bhadravata.

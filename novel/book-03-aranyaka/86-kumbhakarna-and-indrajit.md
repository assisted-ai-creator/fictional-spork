# Kumbhakarna and Indrajit

"Then Prahasta suddenly came at Vibhishana," said Markandeya, "and, harsh in
battle, roared and struck him with his mace. Though he was struck by that mace
of terrible force, the wise, strong-armed one did not tremble, but stood as firm
as the Himalaya. Then Vibhishana took up a great spear with a hundred bells,
charged it with a spell, and hurled it at his head. As it fell on him with
force, with the roar of a thunderbolt, the rakshasa's head was carried off, and
he looked like a tree broken by the wind.

"When he saw the night-rover Prahasta killed in battle, Dhumraksha rushed at the
monkeys with great force. When they saw his army falling on them like a cloud,
terrible to look at, the bulls among the monkeys suddenly broke in the battle.
When he saw the bulls among the monkeys suddenly broken, Hanuman, the tiger
among monkeys, came out and took his stand. Seeing the son of the wind standing
firm in the battle, the monkeys all turned back with great force, king. Then
there was a great uproar, wild and hair-raising, as the armies of Rama and
Ravana rushed at each other.

"In that battle, terrible and deep in the mud of blood, Dhumraksha drove the
monkey army back with his arrows. As that great rakshasa counsellor came on,
Hanuman son of the wind, conqueror of enemies, met him with speed. There was a
terrible battle between the monkey hero and the rakshasa hero, each wanting to
beat the other, like Indra and Prahlada. The rakshasa struck the monkey with
maces and iron bars. The monkey struck the rakshasa with trees, trunks and
branches and all. Then wise Hanuman, son of the wind, with his huge body, killed
Dhumraksha with his horses, his chariot and his driver.

"When they saw Dhumraksha, the best of the rakshasas, killed, the monkeys took
heart, came on, and struck down the soldiers. Killed by the mighty monkeys,
flushed with victory, the rakshasas lost heart and fled in fear to Lanka. The
night-rovers who were left alive fled broken to the city and told King Ravana
all that had happened.

"Ravana heard from them that Prahasta had been killed in battle, and the great
archer Dhumraksha with his army, by the bulls among the monkeys. He sighed long
and deep, leapt up from his fine seat and said, 'The time for Kumbhakarna's work
has come.' When he had said this, he woke Kumbhakarna, who lay asleep, much
given to sleep, with instruments of many kinds, very loud. He woke him with
great effort, and Kumbhakarna sat up, well and calm and awake. Then the lord of
the rakshasas, ten-necked Ravana, whose fear had come upon him, said to mighty
Kumbhakarna.

"'You are fortunate, Kumbhakarna, to have such sleep as this, that you do not
know this cruel time of great danger. This Rama has crossed the ocean by a
bridge with the monkeys, and in scorn of us all he is making a great slaughter
here. For I carried off his wife, Sita Janaki, and he has come to free her,
after building a bridge across the great ocean. And he has killed our great
kinsmen, Prahasta and the rest. There is no one but you to kill him, tormentor
of enemies. Put on your armour and go out today, best of the mighty, and kill
all our enemies in battle, Rama and the rest, tamer of enemies. Dushana's two
younger brothers, Vajravega and Pramathin, will follow you with a great force.'

"When he had said this to mighty Kumbhakarna, the lord of the rakshasas gave
Vajravega and Pramathin their orders. 'So be it,' the two heroes, Dushana's
younger brothers, said to Ravana, and putting Kumbhakarna in front, they went
quickly out of the city.

---

"Then Kumbhakarna came out of the city with his followers and saw the monkey
army standing before him, flushed with victory. The monkeys ran up quickly,
surrounded him on every side, and struck him with many huge trees. Others clawed
at him with their nails, putting away all fear. Fighting in many ways, by all
the ways of war, the monkeys struck the terrible lord of the rakshasas with
weapons of many kinds. Laughing as he was struck, he ate the monkeys: Panasa,
Gavaksha and the monkey Vajrabahu.

"When they saw that frightening deed of the rakshasa Kumbhakarna, Tara and the
others cried out in terror. When Sugriva saw Tara and the other leaders of the
monkey troops crying out loudly, he rushed at Kumbhakarna without fear. The
great-hearted elephant among monkeys fell on Kumbhakarna with force and struck
him on the head with a sal tree with all his strength. The great-hearted, swift
Sugriva broke the sal tree on Kumbhakarna's head. But the monkey did not hurt
him at all.

"Then Kumbhakarna, roused by the touch of the sal tree, roared, and laughing,
took Sugriva in his arms and carried him off by force. When he saw Sugriva being
carried off by the rakshasa Kumbhakarna, the hero Saumitri, who gladdened his
friends, ran at him. Falling on him, Lakshmana, the slayer of enemy heroes,
loosed at Kumbhakarna a great arrow of great speed, with golden feathers. The
arrow split his armour and his body and went on, tearing into the earth,
drenched with blood.

"His heart split open like this, the great archer Kumbhakarna let go of the lord
of the monkeys. Seizing a rock for a weapon, he lifted a great rock and ran at
Saumitri. As he ran at him swiftly, Lakshmana cut off his two raised hands with
two sharp-edged razor arrows, and he became four-armed. Showing how light his
weapon was, Saumitri cut off all those arms too, which held rocks, with razor
arrows. Then he became huge, with many feet, heads and arms. Saumitri burned
him, like a heap of mountains, with the Brahma weapon. Struck by the divine
weapon, that one of great valour fell in battle, like a tree with its shoots
burned by a great thunderbolt.

"When they saw swift Kumbhakarna, like Vritra, fallen lifeless on the ground,
the rakshasas fled in fear. When Dushana's two younger brothers saw their
warriors running, they stood Saumitri off and rushed at him in anger. Saumitri
roared and met the angry pair, Vajravega and Pramathin, with his arrows. Then
there was a wild and hair-raising battle, Partha, between Dushana's younger
brothers and wise Lakshmana. He rained a great shower of arrows on the two
rakshasas, and the angry heroes both rained arrows on him. For a moment the
clash between Vajravega and Pramathin and strong-armed Saumitri was very
terrible.

"Then Hanuman, son of the wind, took a mountain peak, ran at the rakshasa
Vajravega and took his life. And the mighty monkey Nila ran at Pramathin,
Dushana's younger brother, with a great stone and crushed him. Then the battle
began again, bitter in its outcome, as the armies of Rama and Ravana rushed at
each other. The forest-dwellers killed the rakshasas in hundreds, and the
rakshasas the forest-dwellers, but it was mostly rakshasas who were killed
there, not monkeys.

---

"Ravana heard that Kumbhakarna had been killed in battle with his followers, and
the great archer Prahasta, and Dhumraksha of great energy. He said to his heroic
son Indrajit, 'Kill Rama, slayer of enemies, and Sugriva with Lakshmana. For it
was you, my good son, who won me blazing fame, when you beat in battle the
thunderbolt-wielder, the thousand-eyed husband of Shachi. Seen or unseen, with
the arrows given you by the boon of the gods, kill our enemies, slayer of
enemies, best of my weapon-bearers. Rama, Lakshmana and Sugriva cannot bear the
touch of your arrows, sinless one, let alone their followers. Go and pay the
debt for Khara that Prahasta and Kumbhakarna did not pay in battle, strong-armed
one. Kill our enemies with their soldiers today with your sharp arrows, my son,
and delight me, as you did long ago when you bound Vasava.'

"At this, Indrajit said, 'So be it,' put on his armour, mounted his chariot and
went quickly to the battlefield, king. There the bull among the rakshasas
proclaimed his name clearly and challenged Lakshmana, who bore auspicious marks,
to battle. Lakshmana ran at him with his bow and arrow, frightening him with the
slap of his arm-guard, as a lion frightens a small deer.

"A great and fierce battle arose between the two, each eager for victory, both
knowing the divine weapons, rivalling each other. When Ravana's son could not
get the better of him with his arrows, the best of the mighty made a greater
effort. He pressed him with lances of great speed, but Saumitri cut them down
with sharp arrows as they came, and they fell to the earth, cut to pieces by the
sharp arrows.

"Then glorious Angada, Valin's son, raised a tree, ran at him with great speed,
and struck him on the head. Undaunted, mighty Indrajit tried to strike him in
the chest with a lance, but Lakshmana cut his lance. As the hero Angada, bull
among monkeys, came near, Ravana's son struck him on the left side with his
mace. Heedless of the blow, mighty Valin's son, conqueror of enemies, angrily
hurled the trunk of a sal tree at Indrajit. Thrown in anger by Angada to kill
Indrajit, the tree destroyed Indrajit's chariot with its horses and driver,
Partha.

"Then Ravana's son jumped down from his chariot, whose horses and driver were
killed, and vanished right there by his magic, king. Knowing that the rakshasa,
a master of many magics, had vanished, Rama came to that place and guarded his
army. Then, aiming at Rama, he pierced him in every limb with his boon-given
arrows, and great chariot fighter Lakshmana too. The two heroes, Rama and
Lakshmana, fought Ravana's son with arrows, though he was invisible, hidden by
his magic. In his rage he loosed arrows again and again, in hundreds and
thousands, at every limb of those lions among men. The monkeys went up into the
sky with great rocks, looking for the invisible one who loosed arrows without
end. The invisible rakshasa pierced them too, and the two brothers, with his
arrows, striking hard, hidden by his magic. Covered with arrows, the heroic
brothers Rama and Lakshmana fell from the sky to the earth like the sun and
moon.

---

"When he saw the two brothers of boundless energy fallen, Ravana's son bound
them again with his boon-given arrows. Bound in battle by Indrajit with a net of
arrows, the heroes, tigers among men, looked like two birds in a cage. Sugriva
saw them fallen on the ground, covered with arrows in their hundreds. The lord
of the monkeys surrounded them and stood guard, with Sushena, Mainda and
Dvivida, Kumuda and Angada, Hanuman, Nila, Tara and Nala.

"Then Vibhishana, having done his work, came to that place and woke the two
heroes with the weapon of wisdom. And in a moment Sugriva drew out the arrows
from both of them with the great herb vishalya, applied with divine spells. When
they had come to themselves, freed of arrows, the best of men rose up. In a
moment both great chariot fighters were free of weariness and fatigue.

"Then Vibhishana saw Rama, the joy of the Ikshvakus, free of pain. He joined his
palms and said to him, Partha, 'A guhyaka has come to you from the White
Mountain, tamer of enemies, with water, at the command of the king of kings.
Great King Kubera gives you this water, scorcher of enemies, so that you may see
beings who are hidden. When you have touched your eyes with it, you will see
hidden beings, and so will anyone you give it to.'

"'So be it,' said Rama, and took that water, which had been honoured, and bathed
his eyes, and so did great-minded Lakshmana. So did Sugriva and Jambavan,
Hanuman and Angada, Mainda, Dvivida and Nila, and most of the best of the
monkeys. It happened as Vibhishana had said. In a moment their eyes could see
beyond the senses, Yudhishthira.

"Indrajit, having done his work, told his father what he had done, and came back
quickly to the front of the battle. As he came on again in his rage, eager to
fight, Saumitri, following Vibhishana's advice, ran at him. Wanting to kill him
before he had done his daily rites, flushed with victory as he was, Lakshmana,
now given the sign, struck him angrily with arrows. A battle arose between the
two, each wanting to beat the other, very strange and wonderful, like Shakra's
with Prahlada. Indrajit pierced Saumitri with sharp arrows that split the
vitals, and Saumitri pierced Ravana's son with arrows that burned like fire. At
the touch of Saumitri's arrows Ravana's son was beside himself with rage, and
loosed at Lakshmana eight arrows like venomous snakes.

"Hear from me how the hero Saumitri took his life with three arrows that burned
like fire. With one he struck from his body the arm that held the bow. With the
second he brought down to the earth the arm that held the iron arrows. With the
third arrow, broad-bladed and gleaming, he took off his head with its fine nose
and shining earrings. When he had killed him, a trunk terrible to see, its arms
and shoulders cut away, the best of the mighty killed his driver too with his
weapons.

"The horses carried that chariot into Lanka, and Ravana saw the chariot without
his son. When he saw his son killed, Ravana, his eyes rolling in fear, stricken
with grief and confusion, set out to kill Vaidehi. The wicked one took a sword
and ran swiftly at her where she was in the Ashoka grove, longing to see Rama.

"When Avindhya saw the evil intent of that fool, he calmed his rage. Hear by
what reasoning. 'Established in a great and shining kingdom, you should not kill
a woman. This woman is as good as killed already, since she is a woman and a
prisoner in your house. I do not think she would be killed by the destruction of
her body. Kill her husband. When he is killed, she will be killed too. Not even
Shatakratu himself is your equal in valour. Again and again you have terrified
the thirty gods, with Indra, in battle.'

"With many such words Avindhya calmed the angry Ravana, and he took his advice.
The night-rover put down his sword, set his mind on going out to battle, and
gave the order, 'Let my chariot be made ready.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Kumbhakarna's death.** In the Mahabharata's telling Lakshmana kills Kumbhakarna with the Brahma weapon (3.271.16). Rama does not fight him. See L-157.
* **The healing herb.** Sugriva heals the brothers "with the great herb vishalya, applied with divine spells" (3.273.6). Hanuman carrying a mountain of herbs is not in the Mahabharata. See L-157.
* **Garuda.** Rejected passages (\*1288–\*1289) have Garuda come and free the brothers from a noose of snakes. Not narrated. In the CE they are bound by Indrajit's arrows and woken by Vibhishana (3.273.1–5).
* **Lakshmana struck by Ravana's spear.** A rejected passage (\*1290) has Ravana strike Lakshmana with a spear. Not narrated.
* **Sugriva's revenge.** A rejected passage (\*1284) has Sugriva, carried off, bite off Kumbhakarna's ears and nose and escape. In the CE Lakshmana's arrow makes Kumbhakarna let go (3.271.13).
* Other rejected lines (\*1280–\*1283, \*1285–\*1287, \*1291–\*1294) add small details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 3.270.1, 3.270.6–7 *sahasā*: "suddenly came at Vibhishana"; "suddenly broke"; "suddenly broken". "Suddenly" renders *sahasā* each time.
* 3.270.3 *śataghaṇṭām*: "a great spear with a hundred bells".
* 3.270.12: "like Indra and Prahlada", the old enemies.
* 3.270.27 *dūṣaṇāvarajau*: "Dushana's two younger brothers", Vajravega and Pramathin.
* 3.271.14–16: Kumbhakarna grows new arms each time they are cut, and then many heads and feet. Kept as the text says.
* 3.271.21 *pārtha*: Markandeya addresses Yudhishthira as "Partha".
* 3.272.3, 3.272.7: Indrajit's defeat and binding of Indra is only mentioned here.
* 3.273.5 *prajñāstreṇa*: "the weapon of wisdom".
* 3.273.6 *viśalyayā mahauṣadhyā*: "the great herb vishalya", a herb that draws out arrows.
* 3.273.9 *guhyakaḥ*: "a guhyaka", one of Kubera's attendants.
* 3.273.17 *akṛtāhnikam*: "before he had done his daily rites". *kṛtasaṃjñaḥ*: "now given the sign", by Vibhishana.
* 3.273.30: "I do not think she would be killed by the destruction of her body." The sense is uncertain; Avindhya seems to mean that her death would not end the matter.

# The Age of Kali

"'Even the gods do not know me truly, brahmin,' said the god. 'But out of love
for you I will tell you how I create this world. You are devoted to your father,
brahmin seer, and you have come to me for refuge, and so you have seen me face
to face. Your celibacy too is great.

"'The waters are called nara. I gave them that name. So I am called Narayana,
for they have always been my resting place. I am named Narayana, the source,
eternal and unchanging, the ordainer of all creatures and their destroyer, best
of the twice-born. I am Vishnu, I am Brahma, and I am Shakra, lord of the gods.
I am King Vaishravana, and Yama, lord of the dead. I am Shiva and Soma and
Kashyapa Prajapati. I am the Creator and the Ordainer, and I am sacrifice, best
of the twice-born. Fire is my mouth, the earth my feet, the moon and the sun my
eyes. The sky with the quarters is my body, and the wind is in my mind.

"'I have offered hundreds of sacrifices with rich gifts, and those who know the
Veda sacrifice to me, standing in the place of sacrifice to the gods. On earth
the lords of the kshatriyas, kings longing for heaven, sacrifice to me, and so
do vaishyas who want to win the world of heaven. I become Shesha and hold up
this earth, bounded by the four oceans and adorned with Meru and Mandara. Long
ago, taking the form of a boar, I lifted this world out of the water by my
strength as it sank, brahmin. I become the fire with the mare's mouth, best of
the twice-born, and drink the waters that pour into me, and I let them go again.
Brahmins are my mouth, kshatriyas my arms, the vaishyas rest on my thighs, and
shudras serve my feet, in their order and their stride. The Rig Veda, the Sama
Veda, the Yajur Veda and the Atharvan come forth from me and enter into me
again.

"'Ascetics devoted to peace, their selves restrained, seeking release, free of
desire, anger and hatred, unattached, without stain, standing in goodness, free
of egotism, always knowing the self: these brahmins worship me alone, always
thinking of me. I am the fire of the dissolution. I am Yama, who ends all
things. I am the sun of the dissolution. I am the wind of the dissolution. Know,
best of the twice-born, that the forms of the stars seen in the sky are my
forms. Know that the oceans, the mines of jewels, in all four quarters, are my
clothing, my bed and my home. Know, good man, that desire and anger, joy, fear
and delusion are all my forms.

"'What men gain by doing fine deeds, truth, giving, fierce austerity and
non-violence towards creatures, is made by my ordering. They move within my
body, their understanding overcome by me, and they do not act by their own will.
The twice-born who study the Veda well and sacrifice with many kinds of
offering, whose selves are calm and who have conquered anger, reach me. Know me,
wise one, as the place of great fruit for those who do good deeds, which cannot
be reached by men who do evil, overcome by greed, mean, ignoble, their selves
unformed. I am the road hard to reach for the deluded, which the yogis follow.

"'Whenever dharma declines, good man, and adharma rises, then I create myself.
When daityas bent on violence, whom the best of the gods cannot kill, and
terrible rakshasas are born in this world, then I am born in the houses of those
whose deeds are good. Entering a human body, I quiet everything. When I have
created gods and men, gandharvas, snakes and rakshasas, and things that do not
move, I destroy them by my own magic. At the time for action I think of a body
again and create myself, entering a human body to bind the bounds.

"'My colour is white in the Krita age, yellow in the Treta, red when the Dvapara
comes, and black in the Kali age. In that time three parts are adharma. When the
time of the end has come, I become Time, most terrible, and alone I destroy the
three worlds, all that moves and does not move. I have three paths, I am the
self of all, I bring happiness to all the worlds. I am the overcomer, going
everywhere, endless, Hrishikesha, the wide-striding one. I alone turn the wheel
of time, brahmin, without form, the quieter of all creatures, the one who sets
all the worlds striving. So, best of sages, my self is set rightly in all
creatures, lord of brahmins, and no one knows me.

"'Whatever hardship you have met in me, brahmin, was all for your joy and your
good, sinless one. Whatever you have seen in the world, moving and unmoving,
best of sages, is all my self, set out in every way. The Grandfather of all the
worlds is half my body. I am named Narayana, who bears the conch, the discus and
the mace. For as long as a thousand ages turn, brahmin seer, I, the self of all,
sleep, the Grandfather of all the worlds. So I stay here all this time, best of
sages, a child who is no child, until Brahma wakes.

"'I, in the form of Brahma, pleased with you again and again, gave you this
boon, brahmin, honoured by the hosts of brahmin seers. When you saw everything
one ocean and all that moves and does not move destroyed, I knew you were in
despair, and so I showed you the world. When you entered inside my body, and saw
the whole world, you were amazed and did not understand. Then I sent you quickly
out of my mouth, brahmin seer, and I have told you of my self, which even gods
and asuras can hardly know. Until the blessed Brahma of great austerity wakes,
brahmin seer, wander here happily and without fear. Then, when the Grandfather
of all the worlds wakes, I will become one with him and create from my body,
best of the twice-born, the sky, the earth, light, wind and water, and whatever
else there is in the world, moving and unmoving.'

"When he had said this, my son, the most wonderful god vanished, and I saw these
creatures, bright and made in many ways. That is what I saw, king, when the end
of the age had come, a wonder, best of the Bharatas, best of all who uphold
dharma. The god I saw long ago, with eyes like lotuses, is this Janardana, your
kinsman, tiger among men. By his gift my memory does not leave me, son of Kunti,
and I have long life and die only when I choose. This is Krishna the Varshneya,
the ancient Person, the lord, Hari, whose self is beyond thought, sitting here,
strong-armed, as if at play. He is the Creator, the Ordainer and the Destroyer,
the Satvata, with the Shrivatsa on his chest, Govinda, lord of the lords of
creatures, the master. Seeing this tiger of the Vrishnis, this memory has come
back to me: the first god, unborn, Vishnu, the Person in yellow clothes. Madhava
is father and mother to all creatures. Go to him for refuge, bulls of the
Kauravas, the refuge of all."

---

At these words the Parthas, the twin bulls among men and Draupadi Krishna bowed
to Janardana. And he, tiger among men, knowing that they deserved honour,
comforted them with very gentle words, as is proper.

Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, asked the great sage Markandeya again what would
become of the world under its rulers to come. "We have heard from you a wonder,
best of speakers, Bhargava sage, what happened at the beginning of the ages, of
their rise and their ending. But I am curious still about this Kali age. When
the dharmas are thrown into confusion, what will be left? What will be the
strength of men then, what will they eat and how will they live, how long will
they live and what will they wear at the end of the age? And reaching what limit
will the Krita age come again? Tell me at length, sage, for the things you tell
are wonderful."

At these words the best of sages spoke again, delighting the tiger of the
Vrishnis and the Pandavas.

"Hear from me, bull of the Bharatas, the story of what will happen to the whole
world when the stained time comes. In the Krita age long ago, dharma stood among
men whole, on four feet, free of deceit and pretence: the bull of dharma. In the
Treta it is pierced by a foot of adharma and stands on three parts. In the
Dvapara dharma is said to be mixed half and half. Then adharma stands on three
parts, overrunning the worlds, and dharma stays with men on one quarter. Learn
from me that the life, strength, understanding, might and splendour of men grow
less with each age, Pandava.

"Kings, brahmins, vaishyas and shudras, Yudhishthira, will practise dharma with
deceit, men who make a show of dharma. Men who think themselves learned will cut
truth short in the world, and with the loss of truth their lives will be short.
Because their lives run out, they will not be able to master learning, and greed
will overcome them, without learning and without knowledge. Given to greed and
anger, deluded, attached to desire, men will be bound by enmity and want to kill
one another. Brahmins, kshatriyas and vaishyas, mixing with one another, will
become like shudras, without austerity or truth. The lowest will be in the
middle, and those in the middle among the lowest. Such will the world be when
the end of the age has come.

"At the end of the age the best cloth will be hemp and the best grain
koradushaka, and men will make friends only of their wives. When the cows are
gone, men will live on the flesh of fish and milk goats and sheep. Men will rob
one another and do violence, will not recite, will deny the other world and
steal. They will sow their plants with hoes on the river banks, and those will
bear them little fruit. Even men who always keep their vows will eat one
another's food at the rites for the Fathers and the gods, out of greed. The
father will eat the son's food and the son the father's, and forbidden foods
will be eaten. Brahmins will not keep vows. They will revile the Veda, and, led
astray by reasoning, they will not sacrifice or make offerings. Men will plough
low ground, yoke milch cows to the yoke and make yearling calves draw loads. The
son who kills his father, and the father who kills his son, will boast without a
care and meet no blame.

"The whole world will become mleccha, without rites, without sacrifice, without
joy and without festivals. Men will steal the wealth of the helpless, of those
with kin, and of widows. Of little strength and courage, stiff, given to greed
and delusion, pleased with the talk and gifts of the wicked, men will take
gifts, and their possessions will come from evil conduct. At the end of the age
the kings, evil-minded, banding together, bent on killing one another, fools who
think themselves wise, will be kshatriyas who are thorns in the world, son of
Kunti. They will not protect. They will be greedy, puffed up with pride and
self-regard, and care only for punishing. Pitiless, they will seize again and
again the wives and wealth of good men and enjoy them while they weep, Bharata.
No one will ask for a girl in marriage, and no girl will be given. They will
take for themselves. The kings, never content, their minds deluded, will steal
others' wealth by every means.

"The whole world will become mleccha, Bharata. Hand will rob hand. Men who think
themselves wise will cut truth short in the world. The old will have the minds
of children, and children the minds of the old. The cowardly will think
themselves brave, and the brave will lose heart like cowards. They will not
trust one another. The whole world will be of one kind of food, set in greed and
delusion. Adharma will grow great, and dharma will not go on. Brahmins,
kshatriyas and vaishyas will not remain, lord of men, and the world will be of
one class. The father will not bear with the son, nor the son with the father,
and no wife will serve her husband. At the end of the age men will go to the
countries where barley and wheat are eaten. Men and women will eat as they
please and will not bear with one another. The whole world will become mleccha,
Yudhishthira, and men will not satisfy the Fathers with rites for the dead. No
one will listen to anyone, no one will be anyone's teacher, and the world will
be seized by darkness, lord of men.

"The longest life then will be sixteen years, and then they will give up their
breath. A girl will bear children in her fifth or sixth year, and men will beget
them at seven or eight. At the end of the age, tiger among kings, a wife will
not be content with her husband, nor a husband with his wife. Wealth will be
little, the marks of holiness false, and violence will rule. No one will give
anything to anyone. The countries will sell their food, the crossroads will sell
the Veda, and women will sell their hair. Men in that last time will be
mlecchas, cruel, eating everything, harsh in all they do. There is no doubt of
it. At the end of the age everyone will cheat everyone in buying and selling,
out of greed for a living. They will perform rites without understanding them,
and live as they please. By nature cruel in their deeds, all people will suspect
one another. Without a qualm they will destroy gardens and trees. There will be
a wasting away of the life of all embodied beings in the world.

"Overcome by greed, they will roam this earth, and brahmins will eat what
belongs to brahmins. The twice-born, crying out, tormented by fear and harassed
by shudras, will wander over this earth, finding no one to protect them. When
men become fierce, cruel, destroyers of life, killers of creatures, then the age
will be cut short. The twice-born, running in terror, will take refuge in
rivers, mountains and rough places, upholder of the Kuru line. Harassed by
robbers, king, and always crushed by bad kings with the burden of taxes, the
best of the twice-born will be like crows. At the terrible end of the age,
giving up their steadiness, lord of the earth, they will do work not theirs, as
servants of shudras. Shudras will teach dharma, and brahmins will wait on them
and listen, holding them as their authority.

"This world will be turned upside down, the low above. They will worship charnel
houses and leave the gods alone. At the end of the age shudras will not serve
the twice-born. In the hermitages of the great seers, in the dwellings of
brahmins, in the places of the gods and the shrines and the homes of the snakes,
the earth will be marked with charnel houses and not adorned with the houses of
the gods, when the age is worn out. That is the sign of the end of the age. When
men are always fierce, without dharma, eating flesh and drinking, then the age
will be cut short. When flower grows on flower and fruit on fruit, great king,
then the age will be cut short.

"When the age is gone, the rain god will rain out of season. The rites of men
will not follow their order, and the shudras will quarrel with the brahmins.
Soon the earth will be crowded with mlecchas, and for fear of the burden of
taxes the brahmins will flee to the ten quarters. The countries, all alike,
tormented by men and by droughts, will go to the hermitages and live on fruit
and roots. With the world thrown into confusion like this, there will be no
bounds. Pupils will not keep to the teaching, and will do what is unwelcome. The
teacher's store will be gone after that. Friends, relatives and kin will keep
together only for gain. At the end of the age there will be ruin for all
creatures.

"All the quarters will blaze, and the stars will move. The lights of the sky
will be against us, and the winds in turmoil. Many meteors will fall, showing
great fear. The sun will burn with six others. There will be tumultuous roars
and fires in the quarters on every side. At rising and setting the sun will be
hidden by a headless trunk. The thousand-eyed one will rain out of season, and
the crops will not grow, when the end of the age has come. Women will always be
harsh of speech, rough, fond of weeping, and will not keep to their husbands'
words. At the end of the age sons will kill their mothers and fathers, and
wives, relying on their sons, will kill their husbands. Rahu will seize the sun
at the wrong time, great king, and at the end of the age fire will blaze on
every side. Travellers begging for water and food will get none, nor any
lodging, and, turned away, will lie down on the road. Thunderbolts, crows,
snakes and birds, with beasts and fowl, will utter harsh cries when the end of
the age has come. Men will abandon their friends and relatives, their people and
their servants. Then they will go one after another to other countries, other
quarters, towns and cities. "Ah, father! Ah, son!" So, crying out these terrible
words to one another, the people will roam the earth.

---

"Then, when the end of the age has come in that tumult, the world will rise
again little by little, beginning with the twice-born. Then, at another time,
fate will again turn favourable, as it will, for the world to grow again. When
the moon and the sun, Tishya and Brihaspati come together in one house, the
Krita age will begin. The rain god will rain in season, the stars will be
auspicious, and the planets will go their right way round in their proper order.
There will be peace, plenty and health, free of sickness.

"Urged on by Time, a brahmin named Kalki Vishnuyashas will be born, of great
courage, great understanding and might. He will be born in the village of
Sambhala, in the fine dwelling of a brahmin. At a thought of his, all mounts and
weapons, warriors, arms and armour will come to him. He will be a king who
conquers by dharma, a turner of the wheel, and he will bring this troubled world
to peace. That brahmin will rise, blazing, of noble mind, the ender of
destruction. He is the ending of all, and he will turn the age. Surrounded by
brahmins, that twice-born one will root out all the lowly mleccha hosts wherever
they have gone."

# Skanda's Hosts

"Hear of Skanda's terrible companions, wonderful to see," said Markandeya. "From
the blow of the thunderbolt on Skanda boys were born there, the cruel ones who
carry off children newly born and in the womb. Girls of great strength were born
to him too from the thunderbolt's blow, and the boys took Vishakha as their
father. Becoming goat-faced, the blessed one guarded them in battle, surrounded
by all the hosts of girls and by his own sons, as the Mothers watched. He is
Bhadrashakha and Kaushala. So people on earth call Skanda the father of the
Kumaras. Those who want sons, and those who have sons, always worship Rudra,
Agni, Uma and mighty Svaha in their places.

"The girls whom the eater of offerings named Tapas begot came to Skanda and
said, 'What shall we do?'

"'Let us be the highest Mothers of the whole world,' said the Mothers, 'and
worshipped, by your grace. Do this kindness for us.'

"He said, 'Yes,' again and again, the noble-minded one. 'You will be of many
kinds, harmful and kindly.' Then the host of the Mothers, having taken Skanda as
their son, went away: Kaki, Halima, Rudra, Brihali, Arya, Palala and Mitra,
these seven are the Mothers of children. Of these, born by Skanda's grace, was a
son named Shishu, most terrible, of great courage, red-eyed, fearsome. These are
called the eight heroes, born of Skanda's host of Mothers. With the goat-faced
one they are called the nine. Know that the sixth face, the goat's face, is
Skanda's own, king. Within the six heads it is always worshipped by the host of
the Mothers. It is said to be the chief of his six heads, the one by which he
created the divine spear. It is called Bhadrashakha. All these things happened
on the fifth of the bright fortnight, and there the most terrible battle was
fought on the sixth, lord of men.

---

"Then Skanda sat, in golden armour and garland, with a golden crest and diadem.
He was golden-eyed, of great splendour, dressed in red, with sharp teeth,
lovely, with every good mark, very dear to the three worlds. Then lotus-formed
Shri herself, in bodily form, came to that giver of boons, the hero, the youth
with polished earrings. Joined with Shri, of wide fame, that finest of youths
was seen by creatures sitting there like the moon at the full. The great
brahmins honoured the mighty one, and the great seers said this to Skanda there.
'Golden one, bless you. Be the bringer of good to the worlds. Born only six
nights ago, you have brought all the worlds under your power. And you have given
them freedom from fear again, best of the gods. So be Indra, the one who makes
the three worlds free from fear.'

"'What does Indra do for all the worlds here, rich in austerity?' said Skanda.
'How does the lord of the gods always protect the hosts of the gods?'

"'Indra gives creatures strength, splendour, offspring and happiness,' said the
seers. 'When he is pleased, the lord of the gods gives all their shares. The
slayer of Bala destroys the wicked and gives to those who keep to good conduct,
and he directs creatures in their tasks. Where there is no sun he becomes the
sun, where there is no moon the moon, and he becomes fire and wind, earth and
water, for his reasons. This is what Indra must do, for Indra has great
strength. And you, hero, are the best in strength. So be our Indra.'

"'Be Indra, strong-armed one,' said Shakra, 'bringer of happiness to all of us.
Be consecrated today. You are fit for it, good man.'

"'You rule the three worlds, calm, devoted to victory,' said Skanda. 'I am your
servant, Shakra. I do not want to be Indra.'

"'Your strength is wonderful, hero,' said Shakra. 'You kill the enemies of the
gods. The worlds, amazed by your courage, will despise me, weak and beaten, even
if I remain Indra, hero. And they will try tirelessly to set us against each
other. When you are set against me, lord, the world will split in two. When the
worlds are divided and have taken sides between us, war will break out from the
division of creatures, mighty one. Then you will conquer me in battle, my son,
as you believe. So be Indra today. Do not hesitate.'

"'You alone are king, bless you,' said Skanda, 'of the three worlds and of me.
And what command of yours shall I carry out, Shakra? Tell me.'

"'If what you have said is true and spoken with resolve,' said Shakra, 'and if
you wish to carry out my command, Skanda, then listen. Be consecrated as general
of the gods, mighty one. At your word I will be Indra, mighty one.'

"'Consecrate me as general,' said Skanda, 'to destroy the danavas, to win the
cause of the gods, and to protect cows and brahmins.'

"Consecrated by Maghavan with all the hosts of the gods, he shone greatly there,
honoured by the great seers. His golden parasol, held over him, shone like the
disc of a well-kindled fire. The glorious slayer of Tripura himself bound on him
a divine golden garland made by Vishvakarman. The blessed one whose banner is
the bull came, tiger among men, scorcher of enemies, with the goddess, and
gladly honoured him.

"The twice-born call Agni Rudra, and so he is Rudra's son. The seed let fall by
Rudra became the White Mountain, and Pavaka's seed was placed on the White
Mountain by the Krittikas. Seeing him honoured by Rudra, all the dwellers in
heaven then called Guha, the best of the virtuous, Rudra's son. This child was
born when Rudra had entered the fire. So Skanda, born there, became Rudra's son.
Born of the splendour of Rudra, of the fire, of Svaha and of the six women,
Skanda, best of the gods, became Rudra's son.

"Wearing two spotless red garments, the son of Pavaka shone with his blazing
body, glorious, like the sun between two red clouds. The cock given him by Agni
was his adorned banner, raised on his chariot, red, shining like the fire at the
end of time. His armour, born with him, entered his body, and it always appears
when the god fights. Power, armour, strength, splendour, beauty, truth, being
unhurt, devotion to brahmins, freedom from bewilderment, the protection of
devotees, the cutting down of enemies and the guarding of the worlds: all these
were born with Skanda, lord of men.

"So, consecrated by all the hosts of the gods and well adorned, he shone, glad
and of good heart, like the full moon. There were the chosen sounds of Veda
recitation, the music of the gods' instruments, the songs of gods and gandharvas
and all the hosts of apsarases. With these and many others, glad, content and
adorned, the son of Pavaka was consecrated by the gods as if at play. The
dwellers in heaven saw the great general consecrated, like the sun risen after
killing the darkness. Then all the armies of the gods came to him in thousands,
saying on every side, 'You are our lord.' The blessed one, surrounded by all the
hosts of creatures, honoured and praised, met them and comforted them too.

"When Shatakratu had consecrated Skanda as general, he remembered Devasena, whom
he had rescued. Thinking, 'Surely Brahma himself has ordained him as her
husband,' he brought Devasena, well adorned. The breaker of Bala said to Skanda,
'Best of the gods, this girl was named your wife by the Self-born before you
were born. So take the right hand of the goddess, bright as a lotus, in yours,
by the rules, with mantras.' At these words he took her hand as the rules
require, and Brihaspati recited the mantras and made the offering. So the wise
know Devasena as Skanda's queen, whom the brahmins call Shashthi, Lakshmi, Asha,
the giver of happiness, Sinivali and Kuhu, Sadvritti and Aparajita. When Skanda
was won as her eternal husband by Devasena, the goddess Lakshmi herself, in
bodily form, came to him. Skanda was joined with Shri on the fifth, and so it is
remembered as Shri's Fifth. Because he achieved his aim on the sixth, the sixth
is a great day.

---

"When the great general had been made general of the gods, joined with Shri, the
six goddesses, the wives of the seven seers, came to him. Abandoned by the
seers, righteous and keeping great vows, they came quickly and said to the
general of the gods, the lord, 'We have been abandoned, son, by our husbands,
who are like gods. Without cause, in anger, my son, we have fallen from our holy
place. Someone said that you were born of us. Having heard this untruth, you
should save us. By your grace, lord, we may have heaven without end. We want you
for our son too. Do this, and be free of your debt.'

"'You are my mothers, blameless ones,' said Skanda, 'and I am your son. All you
wish will be yours.'

"When he had said this, he said to Shakra, 'What is to be done?' Told by Skanda,
'Speak,' Vasava said, 'Abhijit, the younger sister of Rohini, jealous of her,
wanting to be the eldest, has gone to the forest to practise austerity. I am
bewildered, bless you, for a star has fallen from the sky. Think about this
great matter of time with Brahma, Skanda.' Then Brahma arranged time to begin
with Dhanishtha. Before, it had begun with Rohini, and so the count was even.
When Shakra had said this, the Krittikas went to heaven. That star, whose god is
fire, shines in the shape of a cart.

"Vinata said to Skanda, 'You are my son, who gives me the rice ball. I want to
live with you always, son.'

"'So be it,' said Skanda. 'I bow to you. Command me out of a mother's love for
her son. You will always live honoured by your daughter-in-law, goddess.'

"Then the whole host of the Mothers said to Skanda, 'We are the Mothers of the
whole world, praised by the poets. We want to be mothers to you. Honour us.'

"'You are my mothers,' said Skanda, 'and I am your son. Say what you wish me to
do.'

"'The Mothers who were appointed long ago for this world,' said the Mothers,
'let their place be ours, and let it not be theirs. Let us be worshipped by the
world, and let them not be worshipped, bull among the gods. They have taken our
children because of you. Give them to us.'

"'Children once given cannot be taken back by you,' said Skanda. 'What other
children shall I give you, that you wish for in your minds?'

"'We want to eat the children of those Mothers,' said the Mothers. 'Give them to
us, and those who are their lords, set apart with you.'

"'I give you the children,' said Skanda, 'but you have asked for a hard thing.
Guard the children well, bless you, when they are honoured with bows.'

"'We will guard the children, Skanda, bless you, as you wish,' said the Mothers.
'We would like to live with you a long time, lord.'

"'As long as children are young, until sixteen years,' said Skanda, 'trouble
men's children in forms of many kinds. And I will give you my fierce, unchanging
self. Joined with it, you will live happily, worshipped.'

"Then a man bright as gold, of great strength, sprang out of Skanda's body to
eat the children of mortals. He fell to the ground then, senseless and hungry.
With Skanda's leave he became a Graha, a seizer, of terrible form. The best of
the twice-born call that Graha Skandapasmara.

"The most terrible Vinata is called the Bird Graha. They call the rakshasi
Putana, and she should be known as the Putana Graha. The cruel one of terrible
form, the dreadful night-rover, the pishachi of terrible shape, is called
Shitaputana. That dreadful one carries off the unborn children of women. They
call Aditi Revati, and her Graha is Raivata. That great and terrible Graha too
troubles little children. Diti, the mother of the daityas, they call
Mukhamandika. She delights greatly in children's flesh and is hard to withstand.
The boys and girls said to be born of Skanda are all great Grahas who feed on
the unborn, Kauravya. Their husbands are said to be the husbands of those girls.
Unknown, those of terrible deeds seize children. The one the wise call the
mother of cows, Surabhi, king: a Bird rides on her and eats children on earth.
The goddess named Sarama, the mother of dogs, lord of men, always takes the
unborn children of women. The mother of trees lives in the karanja tree. So men
who want sons bow to her in the karanja. These eighteen other Grahas love flesh
and honey, and they always stay in the birth chamber for ten nights. When Kadru
enters a pregnant woman in a subtle body, she eats the child there, and the
woman gives birth to a snake. The mother of the gandharvas takes the child and
goes, and then the human woman is seen on earth with her child gone. The mother
of the apsarases stays holding the child, and so the wise say the child is
sitting. The daughter of the red ocean is remembered as Skanda's nurse. She is
called Lohitayani and is worshipped in the kadamba tree. As Rudra is among men,
so Arya is among women. Arya, the mother of Kumara, is worshipped for separate
wishes.

"These are the great Grahas of children I have told you of. Until sixteen years
they are harmful, and after that kindly. The hosts of the Mothers I have named,
and the male Grahas, should always be known by embodied beings as Skanda's
Grahas. To quiet them, bathing, incense and ointment should be used, offerings
and gifts, and above all the worship of Skanda. Honoured like this, and
worshipped and bowed to as they should be, all of them give men what is good,
king of kings, life and strength.

"Now, having bowed to the great lord, I will tell you of the Grahas that come to
men after the sixteenth year. The man who sees gods, awake or asleep, quickly
goes mad, and they know that as the God Graha. The man who sees the Fathers,
sitting or lying, quickly goes mad, and that should be known as the Father
Graha. The man who scorns the siddhas, or whom they curse in anger, quickly goes
mad, and that should be known as the Siddha Graha. The one who smells scents and
tastes of many kinds quickly goes mad, and that should be known as the Rakshasa
Graha. The man on earth whom the divine gandharvas touch quickly goes mad, and
that Graha is the Gandharva. The man the yakshas enter in the course of time
quickly goes mad, and that should be known as the Yaksha Graha. The man the
pishachas always ride quickly goes mad, and they know that Graha as the
Pishacha. The embodied one whose mind, stirred up by the humours, is bewildered,
quickly goes mad, and his cure is by the science of medicine. From faintness of
heart, from fear and from seeing terrible things, a man quickly goes mad, and
his cure is courage. One Graha wants to play, another wants to eat, and another
desires: so the Graha is of three kinds. These Grahas come to men until they are
seventy years old. After that, for embodied beings, fever is like a Graha. The
Grahas always avoid the man whose senses are not scattered, who is
self-controlled, pure, always watchful, who believes and has faith. I have told
you of the Grahas of men. The Grahas do not touch men devoted to the god, the
great lord."

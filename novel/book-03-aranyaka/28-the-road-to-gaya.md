# The Road to Gaya

"'Then, you who know dharma,'" said Narada, telling Pulastya's words, "'a man
should go to the ancient Dharmatirtha. Bathing there, king, a man of dharmic
character, with a gathered mind, purifies his family to the seventh generation,
no doubt of it. Then, you who know dharma, he should go to the fine Karapatana.
He wins the agnishtoma and goes to the world of the sages. Then, king, a man
should go to the Saugandhika forest, where the gods with Brahma at their head,
the seers rich in austerity, the siddhas, charanas and gandharvas, the kinnaras
and great snakes go. As soon as he enters that forest, he is freed from every
sin.

"'From there the best of streams, the finest of rivers, the goddess Sarasvati,
most holy, flows out of the plaksha tree, king. He should bathe there in the
water that comes out of the ant hill, and worshipping the Fathers and the gods,
he wins the reward of the horse sacrifice. There is a ford there called
Ishanadhyushita, very hard to reach, six throws of the peg from the ant hill; so
it is settled. Bathing there, tiger among men, he gains a thousand tawny cows
and the horse sacrifice. This was seen in ancient times. Going to Sugandha,
Shatakumbha and Panchayajna, Bharata, best of men, he is honoured in the world
of heaven. Coming there to the ford of Trishulakhata, Bharata, he should bathe,
devoted to worship of the Fathers and the gods. When he leaves his body he gains
the lordship of the god's hosts, no doubt of it.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the rare place of the goddess famous in
the three worlds as Shakambhari. For a thousand divine years, you of good vows,
they say, she ate vegetables once a month, lord of men. Seers rich in austerity
came there out of devotion to the goddess, and she entertained them with
vegetables, they say, Bharata. So the name Shakambhari, "she who bears
vegetables", was fixed on her. Coming to Shakambhari, chaste and with a gathered
mind, staying three nights, restrained and pure, one should eat vegetables.
Whatever reward there is in living rightly on vegetables for twelve years, that
reward is his, Bharata, by the goddess's will.

"'Then he should go to Suvarnaksha, famous in the three worlds, where Vishnu
long ago worshipped Rudra to win his favour. He gained many boons, rare even
among the gods, and the destroyer of Tripura, well pleased, said to him,
Bharata, "Krishna, you will be dearer in the world even than I. The whole world
will have you for its mouth, no doubt of it." Going there, king of kings, and
worshipping the god whose banner is a bull, he wins the horse sacrifice and
gains the lordship of the god's hosts. Then he should go to Dhumavati. Fasting
three nights, a man gains the wishes he prays for in his mind, no doubt of it.
On the southern side of the goddess, lord of men, is Rathavarta. He should climb
it, you who know dharma, with faith and master of his senses, and by Mahadeva's
grace he goes to the highest goal. Going round it, bull of the Bharatas, he
should go to Dhara, wise one, which destroys every sin. Bathing there, tiger
among men, he does not grieve, lord of men.

"'Then, you who know dharma, bowing to the great mountain, he should go to
Gangadvara, which is equal to the gate of heaven, no doubt of it. He should
bathe there at Kotitirtha with a gathered mind. He wins the pundarika and raises
up his family. Offering water as the rules require to the gods and the Fathers
at Saptaganga, Triganga and Shakravarta, he is honoured in the world of the
holy. Then bathing at Kanakhala, fasting three nights, a man wins the horse
sacrifice and goes to the world of heaven.

"'The pilgrim should go to Kapilavata, lord of men. Staying one night there, he
wins the reward of a thousand cows. There is the ford of the great Kapila, king
of the snakes, best of the Kurus, famous in all the worlds. He should bathe
there at Nagatirtha, lord of men. A man wins the reward of a thousand tawny
cows. Then he should go to Lalitika, the fine ford of Shantanu. Bathing there,
king, a man does not come to a bad end. The man who bathes where the two streams
of the Ganga meet wins ten horse sacrifices and raises up his family. Then, king
of kings, he should go to Sugandha, famous in the world. Cleansed of every sin,
he is honoured in Brahma's world. Then the pilgrim should go to Rudravarta, lord
of men. Bathing there, king, a man is honoured in the world of heaven. Bathing
where the Ganga meets the Sarasvati, best of men, he wins the horse sacrifice
and goes to the world of heaven. Going to Bhadrakarneshvara and worshipping the
god as the rules require, he does not come to a bad end and goes to the world of
heaven.

"'Then the pilgrim should go in turn to Kubjamraka. He wins a thousand cows and
goes to the world of heaven. The pilgrim should go to Arundhativata, lord of
men. Touching the water at Samudraka and fasting three nights, a man gains the
reward of a thousand cows and raises up his family. Then he should go to
Brahmavarta, chaste and with a gathered mind. He wins the horse sacrifice and
goes to the world of heaven. Going to the source of the Yamuna and touching the
water of the Yamuna, he gains the reward of the horse sacrifice and is honoured
in the world of heaven. Coming to Darvisamkramana, a ford famous in the three
worlds, he wins the horse sacrifice and goes to the world of heaven. Going to
the source of the Sindhu, where siddhas and gandharvas go, and staying five
nights there, he gains much gold.

"'Then coming to Vedi, very hard to reach, a man wins the horse sacrifice and
goes the way of Ushanas. Coming to Rishikulya and to Vasishtha, Bharata, all the
classes become twice-born by passing beyond Vasishtha. Bathing at Rishikulya, a
man reaches the world of the seers, if he lives there a month on vegetables,
lord of men. Coming to Bhrigutunga, he gains the reward of the horse sacrifice.
Going to Virapramoksha, he is freed from every sin. Coming to the ford of
Krittika and Magha, Bharata, the doer of merit wins the reward of the agnishtoma
and the atiratra. Then coming at twilight to the matchless Vidyatirtha and
touching the water, he masters every kind of learning.

"'He should spend a night at Mahashrama, which frees a man from every sin.
Eating once only, or not at all, he lives in fine worlds. Staying a month at
Mahalaya, fasting to every sixth meal time, he is cleansed of every sin and
gains much gold. Going to Vetasika, where the Grandsire goes, he wins the horse
sacrifice and goes the way of Ushanas. Coming to Sundarikatirtha, where siddhas
go, he shares in beauty. This was seen in ancient times. Then going to Brahmani,
chaste and master of his senses, he reaches Brahma's world in a chariot the
colour of a lotus.

"'Then he should go to Naimisha, holy, where siddhas go. Brahma always lives
there, with the hosts of the gods around him. For the man who longs for
Naimisha, half his sin is destroyed. As soon as he enters it, a man is freed
from every sin. A steady man should live there a month, at Naimisha, devoted to
the fords. Whatever fords there are on the earth, Bharata, are at Naimisha.
Bathing there, restrained and sparing in his food, he wins the reward of the
gavamayana sacrifice, Bharata, and purifies his family to the seventh
generation, best of the Bharatas. The man who gives up his life at Naimisha,
given to fasting, rejoices in the world of heaven; so the wise say. Naimisha is
always holy and pure, best of kings.

"'Coming to Gangodbheda and fasting three nights, a man wins the vajapeya and
becomes one with brahman. Coming to the Sarasvati, he should offer water to the
Fathers and the gods. He rejoices in the worlds of Sarasvati, no doubt of it.
Then he should go to the Bahuda, chaste and with a gathered mind. A man wins the
reward of the sacrifice called the session of the gods. Then he should go to the
holy Chiravati, surrounded by the holiest. Devoted to worship of the Fathers and
the gods, he wins the vajapeya. Coming to Vimalashoka, he shines like the moon.
Staying one night there, he is honoured in the world of heaven.

"'Then he should go to Gopratara, the fine ford on the Sarayu, where Rama went
to heaven with his servants, his army and his mounts. He left his body and went
to heaven by the power of that ford, by Rama's grace, and by his own resolve,
Bharata. Bathing at that ford of Gopratara, lord of men, a man is cleansed of
every sin and honoured in the world of heaven. Bathing at Ramatirtha on the
Gomati, joy of the Kurus, a man wins the horse sacrifice and purifies his
family. Shatasahasrika is a ford there, best of the Bharatas. Touching the water
there, restrained and sparing in his food, he gains the holy reward of a
thousand cows, bull of the Bharatas. Then, king of kings, he should go to the
matchless Bhartristhana. Bathing at Kotitirtha and worshipping Guha, king, a man
gains the reward of a thousand cows and becomes full of splendour.

"'Then going to Varanasi and worshipping the god whose banner is a bull, and
bathing at Kapilahrada, a man gains the reward of the royal consecration. Coming
to the rare ford of Markandeya, king of kings, where the Gomati meets the Ganga,
famous in the world, he wins the agnishtoma and raises up his family.

---

"'Then coming to Gaya, chaste and master of his senses, he wins the horse
sacrifice merely by going there, Bharata. There is the banyan called
Akshayavata, the undying banyan, famous in the three worlds. What is given to
the Fathers there becomes undying, lord. Touching the water of the Mahanadi, he
should offer water to the Fathers and the gods. He reaches undying worlds and
raises up his family. Then he should go to Brahmasaras, adorned by the
Dharmaranya. When the night has turned to dawn, he wins the paundarika. In that
lake, king of kings, Brahma's sacrificial post stands raised. Going round the
post, he gains the reward of the vajapeya.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to Dhenuka, famous in the world. Staying one
night, king, he should give a cow made of sesame. Cleansed of every sin, he
surely goes to the world of Soma. There is a sign there to this day, great king,
no doubt of it: a tawny cow with her calf wanders on the mountain, and the
footprints of the cow and her calf are still seen today, Bharata. Touching the
water in those footprints, king of kings, best of kings, whatever evil deed he
has done is destroyed, Bharata.

"'Then he should go to Gridhravata, the place of the wise god. Going to the god
whose banner is a bull, he should bathe there in ash. For a brahmin it counts as
a vow of twelve years kept; for the other classes every sin is destroyed. Then
he should go to the mountain Udyanta, loud with song. The footprint of Savitri
is seen there, bull of the Bharatas. A brahmin of strict vows should perform his
twilight worship there. By him the twilight worship of twelve years will have
been performed. The famous Yonidvara is there too, bull of the Bharatas. Going
there, a man is freed from the mixing of births.

"'The man who lives at Gaya through both halves of the month, the dark and the
bright, purifies his family to the seventh generation, king, no doubt of it. One
should wish for many sons, if even one of them goes to Gaya, or performs the
horse sacrifice, or sets free a dark bull.

"'Then the pilgrim should go to the Phalgu, lord of men. He wins the horse
sacrifice and reaches great perfection. Then, king of kings, he should go with a
gathered mind to Dharmaprishtha, where Dharma always lives, great king,
Yudhishthira. Going there, he gains the reward of the horse sacrifice. Then,
king of kings, he should go to the fine ford of Brahma. Worshipping Brahma of
boundless power there, king of kings, a man wins the reward of the royal
consecration and the horse sacrifice.

"'Then the pilgrim should go to Rajagriha, lord of men. Touching the water of
the hot springs, he rejoices like Kakshivat. There a pure man should eat the
daily offering of the yakshi. By the yakshi's grace he is freed from the killing
of an embryo. Going to Maninaga, he gains the reward of a thousand cows. The man
who eats the daily offering of Maninaga will not be harmed by poison, even if a
poisonous snake bites him. Staying one night there, he is freed from every sin.

"'Then he should go to the forest of the brahmin seer Gautama, king. Bathing in
Ahalya's lake, he goes to the highest goal. Going to Shri, king, he gains the
highest fortune. There is a well there, you who know dharma, famous in the three
worlds. Bathing there, he wins the horse sacrifice. The well of the royal seer
Janaka is honoured by the thirty gods. Bathing there, he reaches Vishnu's world.
Then he should go to Vinashana, which frees a man from every sin. He wins the
vajapeya and goes to the world of Soma.

---

"'Coming to the Gandaki, born of the water of all the fords, he wins the
vajapeya and goes to the world of the sun. Then, you who know dharma, entering
the ascetics' forest of Adhivamshya, he rejoices among the guhyakas, great king,
no doubt of it. Coming to the river Kampana, where siddhas go, he wins the
pundarika and goes to the world of the sun. Then coming to the Vishala, the
river famous in the three worlds, he wins the agnishtoma and goes to the world
of heaven. Coming to the stream of Maheshvara, lord of men, he wins the horse
sacrifice and raises up his family. Coming to the lotus pool of the dwellers in
heaven, a pure man does not come to a bad end and wins the vajapeya.

"'He should go to Maheshvarapada, chaste and with a gathered mind. Bathing at
Maheshvarapada, he gains the reward of the horse sacrifice. Ten million fords
are famous there, bull of the Bharatas. An evil asura in the form of a tortoise
was carrying them off, king of kings, and the mighty Vishnu took them back,
king. Bathing at the Tirthakoti, Yudhishthira, he wins the pundarika and goes to
Vishnu's world.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the place of Narayana, where Hari always
lives, present, Bharata, known as Shalagrama, the place of Vishnu of wonderful
deeds. Going to Vishnu, the lord of the three worlds, the unchanging giver of
boons, he wins the horse sacrifice and goes to Vishnu's world. There is a well
there, you who know dharma, which frees a man from every sin. The four seas are
always present in that well. Touching the water there, king of kings, he does
not come to a bad end. Going to Mahadeva, and to Vishnu, the unchanging giver of
boons, he shines like the moon, freed from his debts, Yudhishthira. Touching the
water at Jatismara, pure and with a controlled mind, a man bathing there gains
the memory of his former births, no doubt of it. Going to the city of Vateshvara
and worshipping Keshava, he gains the wishes he longs for by fasting, no doubt
of it. Then going to Vamana, which frees a man from every sin, and bowing to the
god Hari, he does not come to a bad end.

"'Going to the hermitage of Bharata, which frees a man from every sin, he should
bathe in the Kaushiki there, which destroys the great sins. A man wins the
reward of the royal consecration sacrifice. Then, you who know dharma, he should
go to the fine Champakaranya. Staying one night there, he gains the reward of a
thousand cows. Then coming to Jyeshthila, the most honoured ford, and fasting
one night, he gains the reward of the agnishtoma. Seeing there the Lord of All,
of great splendour, with the goddess, he reaches the worlds of Mitra and Varuna,
bull among men. Coming to Kanyasamvedya, restrained and sparing in his food, he
reaches the worlds of Manu Prajapati, bull of the Bharatas. The seers of strict
vows say that drink and food given at Kanya are undying, Bharata.

"'Coming to the Nishchira, famous in the three worlds, he wins the horse
sacrifice and goes to Vishnu's world. Men who give gifts where the Nishchira
meets the river go to Brahma's world, tiger among men, no doubt of it. There is
the hermitage of Vasishtha, famous in the three worlds. Bathing there, he wins
the vajapeya. Coming to Devakuta, where hosts of brahmin seers go, he wins the
horse sacrifice and raises up his family.

"'Then, king of kings, he should go to the lake of the sage Kaushika, where
Vishvamitra Kaushika won the highest perfection. He should live there a month,
hero, on the Kaushiki, bull of the Bharatas. What merit there is in the horse
sacrifice he gains in a month. The man who lives at the great lake, the best of
all fords, does not come to a bad end and gains much gold. Going to Kumara, who
lives at Virashrama, a man wins the horse sacrifice, no doubt of it. Coming to
Agnidhara, famous in the three worlds, he wins the agnishtoma and does not
return from heaven.

"'Going to the Grandsire's lake, set on the king of mountains, and bathing
there, he gains the reward of the agnishtoma. From the Grandsire's lake flows
Kumaradhara, which purifies the world, famous in the three worlds. Bathing
there, a man knows that he has done all he need do. Fasting to every sixth meal
time, he is freed from the killing of a brahmin. Climbing the peak of the great
goddess Gauri, famous in the three worlds, a man who makes offerings to the
Fathers should go down into the pools of Stanakunda. Bathing there, devoted to
worship of the Fathers and the gods, he wins the horse sacrifice and goes to
Shakra's world. Coming to Tamraruna, chaste and with a gathered mind, he wins
the horse sacrifice and goes to Shakra's world. Coming to the well at Nandini,
where the thirty gods go, he gains the merit of the human sacrifice, upholder of
the Kurus.

"'Bathing where the Kalika meets the Kaushiki and the Aruna, fasting three
nights, a wise man is freed from every sin. Coming to Urvashitirtha and then to
Somashrama, and bathing at Kumbhakarnashrama, a wise man is honoured on earth.
Bathing at holy Kokamukha, chaste and firm in his vows, he gains the memory of
his former births. This was seen in ancient times. Coming once to the Nanda, a
twice-born man is made perfect in spirit. Cleansed of every sin, he goes to
Shakra's world. Coming to Rishabhadvipa, fit to be sought, which destroys the
curlew, and touching the water of the Sarasvati, he shines, standing in a
chariot of the air. The ford of Auddalaka, great king, is where sages go. He
should bathe there, and he is freed from every sin. Coming to holy Dharmatirtha,
where brahmin seers go, a man wins the vajapeya, no doubt of it.

"'Then coming to Champa and offering water in the Bhagirathi, and going to
Dandarka, he gains the reward of a thousand cows. Then he should go to holy
Lavedika, where the holy go. He wins the vajapeya and is honoured, standing in a
chariot of the air.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Varanasi.** In the CE the pilgrim at Varanasi worships "the god whose banner is a bull" and
  bathes at Kapilahrada (3.82.69). The praise of Avimukta, where death brings freedom, and of
  Vishveshvara bathing in the Ganga with the goddess, are rejected passages (\*419, \*421). See L-125.
* **The distances.** A rejected passage (\*407, after 3.82.23) gives distances in leagues from
  Pushkara, Dharmaprishtha and Prayaga. Not in the CE.
* **Matanga's hermitage at Gaya.** Rejected passages (\*423–\*425, after 3.82.87) add Matanga's
  hermitage and the marks of the dark bull. Not in the CE.

  verse it is. *nīlaṃ vṛṣam utsṛjet*: "sets free a dark bull".

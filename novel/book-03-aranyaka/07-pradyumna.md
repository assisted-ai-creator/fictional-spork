# Pradyumna

"When he had said this to the Yadavas, bull of the Bharatas," said Vasudeva,
"the son of Rukmini mounted his golden chariot, yoked to armoured bay horses. He
raised his banner of the makara, with its jaws wide open, adorned, and with
those horses, which seemed to fly up into the sky, he went against the enemy.
The mighty hero drew and twanged the finest of bows, with a quiver and a sword,
and his finger guards of lizard skin tied on. Whirling his bow, which flickered
like lightning, from one hand to the other, he bewildered all the daityas who
lived in Saubha. As he drew his bow and fitted arrows again and again and killed
his enemies in battle, no one could see a pause.

"The colour of his face did not change, and his limbs did not tremble. The world
heard his wonderful roar, high as a lion's, as he roared at them. The creature
of the waters, set on a golden pole, with gaping jaws, the crusher of all the
timis, shone at the head of his fine horses, the finest of banners, terrifying
the front of Shalva's army.

"Then Pradyumna, the scourge of enemies, sprang forward quickly and ran at
Shalva himself, king, to pick a fight. But Shalva, in his fury, could not bear
the attack of the hero Pradyumna in the great battle, pride of the Kuru line.
Drunk with anger and pride, Shalva, the conqueror of enemy cities, came down
from the chariot that went where he wished and fought Pradyumna. The worlds
gathered and watched the tumultuous fight between those two heroes, of Shalva
and of the Vrishnis, like the fight of Bali and Vasava.

"He had a chariot made by magic, hero, adorned with gold, with banner and flags,
axle support and quivers. The glorious, mighty one mounted that fine chariot,
they say, my lord, and loosed arrows at Pradyumna, Kauravya. Then Pradyumna,
with the speed of his arms, let loose a rain of arrows on Shalva in the battle,
as if to bewilder him. Struck by them in battle, the king of Saubha could not
bear it, and he loosed arrows like blazing fire at my son. Pierced by Shalva's
arrows, king of kings, the joy of Rukmini quickly loosed an arrow in the battle
that pierced the vitals. The feathered arrow my son had sent split his armour at
once and pierced his heart, and he fell and fainted.

"When the hero King Shalva fell senseless, the chiefs of the danavas fled,
tearing up the earth. Shalva's army cried out in dismay, lord of the earth, when
the lord of Saubha fell senseless, king. Then Kauravya, the mighty one rose and
came to his senses, and quickly loosed an arrow at Pradyumna. Pierced by it at
the base of the neck, the strong-armed hero Pradyumna, standing in the battle,
sank down in his chariot. When Shalva had pierced the son of Rukmini, great
king, he roared a lion's roar, filling the earth with the noise. When my son had
fainted, Bharata, he quickly loosed more arrows, hard to bear. Struck by those
many arrows and stunned, Pradyumna lay motionless on the field of battle, best
of the Kauravas.

---

"When Pradyumna, the best of the strong, was hurt by Shalva's arrows, the
Vrishnis lost heart and were shaken, there in the army. The whole army of the
Vrishnis and Andhakas cried out in dismay when Pradyumna fell, king, and the
enemy rejoiced. When the charioteer, the trained son of Daruka, saw him
unconscious like this, he quickly carried him off the field with his swift
horses.

"The chariot had not gone far when the best of chariot fighters came to his
senses. He took up his bow and said to his driver, 'Son of a suta, what are you
doing? Why are you turning away? This is not called the dharma of the Vrishni
heroes in battle. I hope, son of a suta, you are not bewildered at the sight of
Shalva in this great battle, or dismayed at the sight of the fighting. Tell me
the truth.'

"'Son of Janardana,' said the driver, 'I am not bewildered, and fear has not
taken hold of me. But I think Shalva is too much for you, joy of Keshava. So I
am drawing away slowly, hero. This wicked man is strong. A hero who has fainted
in battle must be guarded by his driver. You must always be guarded by me,
long-lived one, and I by you. A chariot fighter must always be guarded, and so I
am drawing away. You are alone, strong-armed one, and the danavas are many. I do
not think the fight is even, son of Rukmini, and so I am drawing away.'

"When the driver said this, Kauravya, the bearer of the makara banner said to
him, 'Turn the chariot round again. Son of Daruka, never do this again, never
turn away from battle, son of a suta, while I live. No one born in the Vrishni
family abandons a fight, or kills a man who has fallen, or who says, "I am
yours." No one born there kills a woman, an old man or a child, a man without
his chariot, one who is scattered, or one whose weapons are broken. And you were
born into a family of charioteers, and trained in a charioteer's work, and you
know the dharma of the Vrishnis in battle, Daruka. So, knowing all the ways of
the Vrishnis at the head of an army, never turn away again, son of a suta.

"'What will Madhava, Gada's elder brother, the unconquerable, say to me, when I
have turned away, been struck in the back, afraid, running from the battle? Or
what will Keshava's elder brother, wild with drink, in his dark blue clothes,
strong-armed Baladeva, say when he comes? What will the grandson of Shini say,
the lion among men, the great archer, or Samba, the conqueror in battle, if I
turn back from the fight, son of a suta? What will unconquerable Charudeshna
say, and Gada and Sarana, and strong-armed Akrura, driver? And the women of the
Vrishni heroes, when they gather, what will they say of me, who have always been
counted brave and good and proud of my manhood? "Here comes Pradyumna, afraid,
having left the great battle. Shame on him!" That is what they will say. They
will not say, "Well done!"

"'For me, or for anyone like me, being mocked, even in words, is worse than
death, son of a suta. So never turn away again. Hari, the slayer of Madhu, left
the burden on me when he went to the sacrifice of Partha, the lion of the
Bharatas, of boundless splendour. I held back the hero Kritavarma as he was
about to go out. I told him, "I will hold off Shalva. You stay here, son of a
suta." And Hridika's son turned back, trusting me. If I leave the battle, what
will I say to that great warrior when I meet him? What will I say to the
lotus-eyed Person, the unconquerable, strong-armed one, who bears the conch, the
discus and the mace, when he comes? What will I say to Satyaki and Baladeva, and
to the other Andhakas and Vrishnis who are always vying with me?

"'If I leave this battle, son of a suta, struck in the back by arrows, carried
off helpless by you, I could not live at all. Turn back quickly with the
chariot, son of Daruka. This must never be done, even in distress. I do not
value my life at all, son of a suta, if I have fled the battle in fear, struck
in the back by arrows. When have you ever known me, son of a suta, to be
stricken with fear, to leave the battle and run like a coward? It is not right
for you to abandon the battle, son of Daruka, when I am so eager to fight. Go
where the fighting is.'

---

"When he was told this, son of Kunti, the son of the suta answered Pradyumna in
the battle, at once, in smooth and sweet words. 'I have no fear, son of Rukmini,
in driving the horses in battle. I know the Vrishnis' way of war. There is
nothing else in it. But, long-lived one, remember the teaching that governs a
charioteer: the chariot fighter must be guarded in every case, and you were
badly hurt. You were badly struck by the arrow Shalva loosed, and you had
fainted, hero, and so I drew away. Now, chief of the Satvatas, you have come to
your senses by good fortune. See my skill in driving horses, joy of Keshava. I
am the son of Daruka, and trained as I should be. I will go without fear into
Shalva's great army.'

"With these words, hero, he urged on the horses in the battle, and gathering the
reins he rushed forward at speed. Wheeling in wonderful circles, in pairs and
singly, to the left and to the right, the fine horses, struck with the goad and
held with the reins, seemed to fly up into the sky, king. They knew the son of
Daruka's light hand, king, and they seemed to be burning as they touched the
ground with their hooves. Without much effort he circled Shalva's army, keeping
it on his left, bull of the Bharatas. It was like a wonder. The king of Saubha
could not bear Pradyumna circling him like this, and suddenly struck his driver
with three arrows. The son of Daruka, the best of horsemen, ignored the force of
the arrows and drove on again, strong-armed one.

"Then the king of Saubha again loosed arrows of many kinds at my heroic son, the
joy of Rukmini. The son of Rukmini, the killer of enemy heroes, cut them with
sharp arrows before they reached him, smiling, showing the lightness of his
hand. When the king of Saubha saw his arrows cut down by Pradyumna, he turned to
the terrible magic of the asuras and let loose arrows. The mighty one saw the
daitya weapon being used, cut it off in mid-air with the weapon of Brahma, and
loosed other arrows. They shook off that weapon at once, and the drinkers of
blood pierced him in the head, the chest and the face, and he fainted and fell.

"When the wretch Shalva had fallen, hurt by the arrows, the son of Rukmini
fitted another arrow, the destroyer of enemies. It was honoured by all the hosts
of the Dasharhas with blessings, and it shone like the sun or fire. When they
saw the arrow being drawn to the bowstring, a cry of alarm went up in the sky.
Then all the hosts of the gods, with Indra and the lord of wealth, sent Narada
and mighty Shvasana, the Wind. The two of them came to the son of Rukmini and
gave him the message of the gods.

"'You must not kill King Shalva in any way, hero. Take back your arrow. He
cannot be killed by you in battle. There is no man anywhere in battle whom this
arrow cannot kill. But the Placer has decided, strong-armed one, that his death
in battle will be Krishna, the son of Devaki. That must not be made false.'

"Then Pradyumna, very gladly, took back the fine arrow from his great bow and
put it in his quiver. Then Shalva rose, king of kings, in the deepest dismay,
and quickly drew off with his army, hurt by Pradyumna's arrows. The cruel man
left Dvaraka, king of kings, harried by the Vrishnis, mounted Saubha, and rose
into the sky."

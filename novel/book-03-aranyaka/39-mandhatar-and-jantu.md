# Mandhatar and Jantu

"Mandhatar, son of Yuvanashva, tiger among kings, best of kings, is famous in
the three worlds," said Yudhishthira. "How was he born, great brahmin? How did
he of boundless splendour reach that highest point, so that the three worlds
were in his power as they are in great Vishnu's? I want to hear the story of
that wise man, how he got the name Mandhatar, he who shone like Shakra, and the
birth of that man of matchless courage. You are skilled at telling it."

"Listen closely, king, to how the name of that great king, Mandhatar, came to be
sung in the worlds," said Lomasha. "Yuvanashva, a lord of the earth born of the
Ikshvaku line, sacrificed with rites with rich fees, protector of the earth. The
best of those who uphold dharma performed a thousand horse sacrifices, and other
chief rites of every kind with rich fees. But the great royal seer, firm in his
vows, had no child. He left his kingdom to his ministers and lived always in the
forest. Joining himself to the rule the texts lay down, his heart dry with
thirst, he entered Bhrigu's hermitage.

"That very night, king of kings, the great son of Bhrigu, the great seer,
performed a rite for the son of Sudyumna, for the sake of a son. A great pitcher
had been filled with water made pure by mantras, and it stood there ready, king
of kings, so that when the king's wife drank it she would bear a son equal to
Shakra. The great seers set the pitcher on the altar and fell asleep, worn out
with keeping watch all night. The son of Sudyumna passed them by, his throat
dry, tormented by thirst, desperate for water. Worn out, the king entered the
hermitage and asked for water. Tired as he was, calling out with his dry throat,
no one heard him, any more than the cry of a bird. Then, seeing that pitcher
full of water, the lord of the earth ran to it quickly, drank the water and left
it. When he had drunk the cool water, the wise lord of the earth, tormented by
thirst, found relief and was very happy.

"Then the seers woke, with the lord of men, and all of them saw the pitcher
empty of water. They gathered and asked, 'Whose doing is this?' 'Mine,'
Yuvanashva said, truthfully. 'That was not right,' the blessed Bhargava said to
him. 'The water was set there for a son, and gathered by austerity. I placed
brahman in it, practising terrible austerity, for your son, royal seer of great
strength and courage. He was to be very mighty, strong, full of the power of
austerity, able to send even Shakra to the house of Yama by his courage. I made
it by this rule, king, and you, king, have done wrong by drinking the water
today. We cannot now undo it. Surely this was the work of fate, that you did
this. The water you drank in your thirst, great king, made holy by the rule and
the mantras and gathered by the power of my austerity, will make you bear a son
of such power yourself. We will perform a most wonderful rite for you there, so
that you will bear a son equal to Shakra, mighty one.'

"Then, when a full hundred years had passed, a son like a second sun split the
left side of the great king and came out, in great splendour, and death did not
come to King Yuvanashva. It was like a wonder. Then splendid Shakra came to see
him. Shakra put his forefinger in the child's mouth. 'He will suck me,' said the
wielder of the thunderbolt, and the gods with Indra named him Mandhatar. Sucking
the forefinger Shakra gave him, the child grew, lord of the earth, thirteen
spans. The Vedas with the science of the bow, and the divine weapons, came to
the lord as soon as he thought of them. The bow called Ajagava, arrows made of
horn, and armour that could not be pierced came to him at once.

"He was consecrated by Maghavan, Shakra himself, Bharata, and by dharma he
conquered the three worlds, as Vishnu did with his strides. The great man's
wheel rolled on unchecked, and jewels came to the royal seer of their own
accord. This earth full of wealth was his, lord of the earth. He sacrificed with
many rites of every kind with rich fees. He of great splendour piled up shrines,
won ample dharma, and gained half of Shakra's seat, king, he of boundless
splendour. In a single day that wise man, always devoted to dharma, conquered
the earth by his command alone, with its mines of jewels and its ports. The
earth to its four ends was covered with the shrines of his rites with rich fees,
great king, and nothing was left uncovered. The great man gave ten thousand
padmas of cows to the brahmins, great king, so they say. In a drought of twelve
years the great man made it rain, before the eyes of the wielder of the
thunderbolt, so that the crops would grow. The great lord of Gandhara, born of
the lunar line, roaring like a great cloud, was crushed and killed by his
arrows. The great man conquered the four kinds of creatures, king, and by his
own austerity and splendour he held up the worlds. This is the place where he
sacrificed to the gods, the man bright as the sun. See it, in the holiest land,
in the middle of Kurukshetra. I have told you all the great story of Mandhatar,
and his fine birth, lord of the earth, as you asked me."

---

"What was the power of King Somaka, best of speakers?" said Yudhishthira. "I
want to hear truly of his deeds and his power."

"Yudhishthira, there was a righteous king named Somaka," said Lomasha. "He had a
hundred wives, king, well matched to him. With great effort, over a long time,
the lord of the earth could not get a son by them. Once, when he was old and
still striving hard, a son named Jantu was born among those hundred women. All
the mothers sat around him when he was born, always, putting the pleasures of
love behind them, lord of the people. Then one day an ant bit Jantu on the
buttock. When he was bitten, the boy cried out in pain, king. Then all the
mothers cried out in great sorrow, surrounding Jantu together, and there was a
great uproar.

"The lord of the earth suddenly heard that cry of pain as he sat among his
ministers and his council with his priests. The king sent to ask what it was,
and the steward told him what had happened to his son. Rising quickly, Somaka
went with his ministers into the women's quarters and comforted his son, the
tamer of enemies. When he had comforted his son, the king came out of the
women's quarters and sat down with his priests and ministers, king.

"'Shame on having only one son here!' said Somaka. 'It would be better to have
none. Because creatures are always in danger, one son is nothing but grief. I
married these hundred wives, brahmin, lord, chosen with care, wanting a son, and
they have no children. Somehow this one son, Jantu, was born to me, though I
tried with them all. What sorrow could be greater than this? My years have
passed, best of the twice-born, and my wives'. Their lives and mine hang on this
one son. Is there any rite by which I might have a hundred sons, whether great
or small, or hard to do?'

"'There is such a rite, by which you would have a hundred sons,' said the
priest. 'If you can do it, Somaka, I will tell you.'

"'Whether it should be done or not,' said Somaka, 'if it gives me a hundred
sons, know that it is as good as done. Let the blessed one tell me.'

"'Sacrifice Jantu, king, in a rite that I will perform,' said the priest. 'Then
you will soon have a hundred splendid sons. When his fat is offered in the fire
and the mothers smell the smoke, they will bear you mighty sons. And your Jantu
will be born again to that same mother as your son, and there will be a golden
mark on his left side.'

---

"'Brahmin, do whatever must be done, in whatever way,' said Somaka. 'Wanting
sons, I will do everything you say.'

"Then he performed the sacrifice for Somaka with Jantu. But the mothers, full of
pity, dragged the son back by force. 'Alas, we are killed!' they cried, in
bitter grief, and the mothers pulled him back, holding his right hand, while the
priest held his left hand and pulled too. He dragged the son away from them as
they cried like ospreys in their pain, cut him up as the rule requires, and
offered his fat. When the fat was offered and the mothers smelled it, they
suddenly fell to the ground in their pain, joy of the Kurus, and all those
queens became pregnant. Then, after ten months, lord of the people, a full
hundred sons were born to Somaka by all of them, Bharata. Jantu was born the
eldest, to his own mother, Bharata, and he was the one they loved, not their own
sons in the same way. And he had that golden mark on his left side. Among those
hundred sons he was the first, with every virtue.

"Then Somaka's priest went to the next world, and after some time Somaka too
went there. He saw him roasting in a terrible hell and asked him, 'Why are you
roasting in hell, brahmin?' The priest, roasting hard in the fire, said to him,
'I performed that sacrifice for you, king. This is the fruit of that deed.'

"When he heard this, the royal seer said to the king of dharma, 'I will go in
here. Let my priest be freed. For it is because of me that the blessed man is
roasting in the fire of hell.'

"'No one ever enjoys the fruit of what another has done, king,' said Dharma.
'The fruits that are yours can be seen here, best of givers.'

"'I do not want the holy worlds without this speaker of brahman,' said Somaka.
'I want to live with him alone, in the dwelling of the gods or in hell, king of
dharma, for my deed is equal to his. Let the fruit of merit and of sin be the
same for both of us, god.'

"'If that is what you want, king,' said Dharma, 'share his fruit with him, for
the same time as he does. Afterwards you will reach the good way.'

"The lotus-eyed king did it all, and afterwards he won back the fine worlds he
had won by his deeds, with that same brahmin, his priest, for he loved his
priest. This is his holy hermitage, shining before us. A patient man who stays
here six nights reaches the good way. We too will stay here, king of kings, free
from fever, for six nights, with our minds controlled. Make ready, upholder of
the Kurus."

---

"Here, they say, king," said Lomasha, "Prajapati himself once performed the
session of sacrifice called Ishtikrita, lasting a thousand years. And Ambarisha,
son of Nabhaga, sacrificed along the Yamuna, and by sacrifices and austerity he
reached the highest perfection. This is the holiest land of the sacrifices of
the son of Nahusha, king, where he sacrificed and gave ten padmas to the members
of the assembly. See here the sacrificial ground of Yayati of boundless power,
lord of all the earth, who rivalled Shakra, son of Kunti. See the earth piled
with fire altars of every shape, as if sinking, pressed down by Yayati's
sacrifices. This is the shami with a single leaf, and this is the fine Sharaka.
See these Lakes of Rama, see the hermitage of Narayana. This is where the son of
Richika, of boundless power, wandering the earth by his yoga, came down to the
Raupya, lord of the earth.

"Hear from me, joy of the Kurus, the verse that a pishachi wearing mortars for
ornaments recited here: 'You ate curds at Yugamdhara, you stayed at
Achyutasthala, and you bathed at Bhutilaya. Do you want to live here with your
sons? If you stay here one night and then stay a second, this is what happens to
you by day. By night it is otherwise.' We will stay here tonight, best of the
Bharatas. For this is the gate of Kurukshetra, son of Kunti, Bharata.

"Here King Nahusha sacrificed with his rites, king, and Yayati, with many
jewels, where Indra was delighted. This is called Plakshavatarana, a ford of the
Yamuna. The wise call it the gate to the vault of heaven. Here the great seers,
sacrificing with the rites of the Sarasvati, with their posts and their mortars,
go for the closing bath, my son. Here King Bharata let loose the sacrificial
horse, the black antelope, again and again, when he had won the earth by dharma.
Here, tiger among men, Marutta holds his fine session of sacrifice, protected by
Samvarta, the chief of divine seers. Touching the water here, king of kings, a
man sees all the worlds, and by touching the water he is cleansed of his evil
deeds, Bharata."

Then the best of the Pandavas bathed there with his brothers, praised by the
great seers, and said to Lomasha, "I see all the worlds by austerity, you whose
courage is true. Standing here, I see the best of the Pandavas, the rider of
white horses."

"It is so, strong-armed one," said Lomasha. "So the great seers see. See this
holy Sarasvati, surrounded by those who have her as their only refuge. Bathing
here, best of men, you will be free of sin. Here the divine seers sacrificed
with the rites of the Sarasvati, and the seers, son of Kunti, and the royal
seers too. This is the altar of Prajapati, five leagues on every side. This is
the field of great Kuru, who loved to sacrifice."

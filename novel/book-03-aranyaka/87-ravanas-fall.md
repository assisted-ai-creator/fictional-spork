# Ravana's Fall

"Then, when his beloved son had been struck down, ten-necked Ravana came out in
anger, mounted on a chariot adorned with gold and jewels," said Markandeya.
"Surrounded by terrible rakshasas with weapons of many kinds in their hands, he
rushed at Rama, crushing the leaders of the monkey troops. As he rushed on in
fury, Mainda, Nila, Nala and Angada, Hanuman and Jambavan surrounded him with
their forces. The leaders of the bears and monkeys destroyed ten-necked Ravana's
army with trees before his eyes.

"When he saw his army being struck down by the enemy, Ravana, lord of the
rakshasas, a master of magic, used his magic. Out of his body came rakshasas in
their hundreds and thousands, with arrows, spears and swords in their hands.
Rama killed all those rakshasas with a divine weapon. Then the lord of the
rakshasas used his magic again. He made forms of Rama and of Lakshmana, Bharata,
and the ten-faced one rushed at Rama and Lakshmana. Those night-rovers, raising
their bows high, fell on Rama and Lakshmana to strike them, king.

"When he saw this magic of the lord of the rakshasas, the joy of the Ikshvakus,
Saumitri, said to Rama calmly and loudly, 'Kill these wicked rakshasas that look
like you.' And Rama killed them, and the others that looked like himself.

"Then Matali, Shakra's charioteer, came up to Rama in the battle with a chariot
yoked to bay horses, bright as the sun.

"'This is Maghavan's finest chariot, yoked to bay horses, the chariot of
victory,' said Matali. 'With this great chariot Shakra killed daityas and
danavas in their hundreds in battle, Kakutstha, tiger among men. So kill Ravana
quickly in battle with this chariot, which I am driving, tiger among men. Do not
delay.'

"When he was told this, Raghava doubted Matali's words, true though they were.
'This is a magic of the rakshasa's,' he thought. But Vibhishana said to him,
'This is no magic of wicked Ravana's, tiger among men. Mount this chariot of
Indra's quickly, you of great splendour.'

"Then Kakutstha was glad. 'So be it,' he said to Vibhishana, and he fell on
ten-necked Ravana with the chariot, full of anger. As Ravana was attacked, all
creatures cried out, and in heaven there were lions' roars with drums, and
heavenly sounds. The night-rover loosed at Rama a most terrible spear, like
Indra's thunderbolt, like Brahma's staff raised to strike. Rama cut that spear
in the air with sharp arrows. When he saw that hard deed, fear came into Ravana.
Then ten-necked Ravana in his anger quickly loosed sharp arrows at Rama by the
tens of thousands, and weapons of many kinds: bhushundis, spears, clubs and
axes, darts of various shapes, and shataghnis with sharp razors. When they saw
that monstrous magic of the ten-necked rakshasa, all the monkeys fled in fear in
every direction.

"Then Kakutstha took from his quiver the best of arrows, well feathered, with a
fine point and golden feathers, and joined it to the Brahma weapon. When they
saw that best of arrows charged by Rama with the Brahma weapon, the gods and
gandharvas, with Shakra at their head, were glad. From the raising of the Brahma
weapon against their enemy, the gods, gandharvas and kinnaras thought the
rakshasa's life nearly spent. Then Rama loosed that arrow of matchless power,
terrible, bringing Ravana's end, like Brahma's staff raised to strike. With it
the best of the rakshasas, with his chariot, his horses and his driver, burst
into flame, wrapped in a fire of great flames.

"Then the thirty gods rejoiced, with the gandharvas and the charanas, when they
saw Ravana killed by Rama, whose deeds were untiring. The five elements left the
great Ravana, for he was cast out of all the worlds by the power of the Brahma
weapon. The elements of his body, his flesh and his blood, burned up by the
Brahma weapon, were destroyed, and not even ash was seen.

---

"When he had killed the mean Ravana, lord of the rakshasas, enemy of the gods,
Rama was glad, with his friends and with Saumitri. When ten-necked Ravana had
been killed, the gods, with the seers at their head, honoured the strong-armed
one with blessings for victory. All the gods praised lotus-eyed Rama, and the
gandharvas and the dwellers of heaven honoured him with showers of flowers and
with words. When they had honoured Rama as was fitting, they went back as they
had come. The sky seemed like a great festival, unfailing one.

"When he had killed ten-necked Ravana, glorious Rama, the lord, conqueror of
enemy cities, gave Lanka to Vibhishana. Then the very wise old counsellor named
Avindhya came out, bringing Sita before him, with Vibhishana in front. He said
to great-hearted Kakutstha, who was downcast, 'Receive the queen, Janaki, whose
conduct is pure, great-hearted one.'

"When he heard this from him, the joy of the Ikshvakus got down from his fine
chariot and saw Sita, her face hidden by tears. He saw her, lovely in every
limb, sitting in a litter, wasted by grief, her whole body caked with dirt, her
hair matted, dressed in black. Fearing that she had been touched, Rama said to
Vaidehi, 'Go, Vaidehi. You are free. I have done what I had to do. That
night-rover has been killed so that you, who had me for a husband, should not
grow old in a rakshasa's house, good woman. How could anyone like me, who knows
the settled truth of dharma, keep for even a moment a woman who has been in
another man's hands? Whether your conduct has been good or not, Maithili, I
cannot take you now for my enjoyment, any more than an offering licked by a
dog.'

"When she heard those cruel words, the young queen suddenly fell in pain, like a
plantain tree cut down. The flush that joy had brought to her face vanished
again in a moment, like breath from a mirror. When they heard Rama's words, all
the monkeys, with Lakshmana, were as if dead, and did not move.

"Then the god whose self is pure, the four-faced grandfather, the creator of the
world, showed himself to Raghava in a flying chariot. So did Shakra, Agni and
Vayu, Yama and Varuna, the lord Kubera, lord of the yakshas, the spotless seven
seers, and King Dasharatha, in a shining, divine form, in a costly flying
chariot drawn by geese. Then all that sky, crowded with gods and gandharvas,
shone like the sky in autumn, bright with stars.

"Then glorious Vaidehi rose up in their midst, and the good woman said to
broad-chested Rama, 'Prince, I am not angry with you, for I know the ways of
women and of men. But hear what I say. The wind, always moving, moves within all
creatures. Let him take my life if I have done wrong. Let fire, water and space,
earth and wind take my life if I have done wrong.'

"Then a voice was heard in the sky, sounding in every direction, holy,
gladdening those great monkeys.

"'Ho, Raghava!' said the Wind. 'It is true. I am the Wind, always moving.
Maithili is without sin, king. Be reunited with your wife.'

"'I am within the bodies of all creatures, joy of the Raghus,' said Fire.
'Maithili has not done wrong in the smallest thing, Kakutstha.'

"'The juices in the bodies of creatures come from me, Raghava,' said Varuna. 'I
tell you, take Maithili back.'

"'My son,' said Brahma, 'this is no wonder in you, who keep the dharma of the
royal seers, good man, who stand on the road of good conduct. But hear what I
say. You have struck down this enemy of the gods, the gandharvas and the snakes,
of the yakshas, the danavas and the great seers, hero. By my grace he could not
be killed by any creature before. For some reason the wicked one was left alone
for a time. He carried off Sita to bring about his own death, and I protected
her by Nalakubara's curse. Long ago he was told, "If you take any other woman
who does not want you, your body will surely burst into a hundred pieces." You
must have no doubt here. Take her back, you of great splendour. You have done a
great deed for the gods, you who are as bright as the immortals.'

"'I am pleased, my child, bless you,' said Dasharatha. 'I am your father
Dasharatha. I give you leave. Rule the kingdom, best of men.'

"'I salute you, king of kings, if you are my father,' said Rama. 'I shall go to
lovely Ayodhya at your command.'

"His father, overjoyed, said to him again, lord of men, 'Go to Ayodhya and rule,
Rama, you whose eyes are red at the corners.' Then Rama bowed to the gods, and,
praised by his friends, was united with his wife, like great Indra with
Pauloma's daughter.

"Then the scorcher of enemies gave a boon to Avindhya, and gave wealth and
honour to the rakshasi Trijata. Brahma, surrounded by the gods led by Shakra,
said to him, 'Son of Kausalya, what boons that you wish shall I give you today?'
Rama chose to stand firm in dharma, never to be beaten by enemies, and that the
monkeys killed by the rakshasas should rise again. When Brahma had said, 'So be
it,' to these words, the monkeys rose up, great king, their senses restored.

"And greatly blessed Sita gave a boon to Hanuman. 'Your life, my son, will last
as long as Rama's fame. Heavenly pleasures will always come to you by my grace,
Hanuman,' she said to the tawny-eyed one.

"Then, as those men of untiring deeds looked on, all the gods, led by Shakra,
vanished. When he saw Rama reunited with Janaki, Shakra's charioteer, full of
joy, said this among his friends. 'You have taken away the sorrow of the gods,
gandharvas and yakshas, of men, asuras and snakes, you whose valour is true. The
worlds, with gods, asuras and gandharvas, yakshas, rakshasas and snakes, will
tell of you as long as the earth endures.' When he had said this, he took leave
of Rama, the best of weapon-bearers, honoured him, and went away in that chariot
bright as the sun.

---

"Then Rama, with Sita before him, with Saumitri, and with all the monkeys led by
Sugriva, set a guard over Lanka. With Vibhishana before him, the master of
himself crossed the home of sea monsters again by that bridge. He went in the
Pushpaka, the shining flying chariot that went through the sky wherever it was
wished, surrounded by his chief counsellors. On the shore of the ocean, where
the king had lain down before, he stayed, whose self was dharma, with all the
monkeys. In time Raghava gathered them, honoured them, pleased them all with
jewels and sent them away.

"When the lords of the monkeys, the gopucchas and the bears had gone, Rama came
back to Kishkindha with Sugriva, followed by Vibhishana, showing Vaidehi the
forest from the Pushpaka. When he reached Kishkindha, Rama, best of strikers,
consecrated Angada, who had done his work, as crown prince. Then with them, and
with Saumitri, Rama set out for his own city by the road he had come.

"When he reached the city of Ayodhya, the lord of the kingdom sent Hanuman as a
messenger to Bharata. When he had read all the signs and given the good news,
and the son of the wind had come back, he went to Nandigrama. There he saw
Bharata, his body caked with dirt, wearing bark, sitting on a seat with the
sandals before him. Mighty Raghava, with Saumitri, met Bharata and Shatrughna
and was glad, bull of the Bharatas. Bharata and Shatrughna, meeting their elder,
were both delighted at the sight of Vaidehi. Bharata, with the greatest joy,
handed back to him, now he had come, the kingdom he had held in trust and
honoured.

"Then Vasishtha and Vamadeva together consecrated the hero on a day chosen as
auspicious, under the constellation of Vishnu. When he had been consecrated, he
gave leave to go home to Sugriva, best of monkeys, with his friends and kin, and
to Vibhishana the Paulastya. He honoured them with jewels of many kinds, and
when he had arranged what was to be done, he sent them off with sorrow, while
they were full of gladness. Raghava honoured the flying chariot Pushpaka and
gave it back with love to Vaishravana. Then, with the divine seers, the joy of
the Raghus performed ten horse sacrifices along the river Gomati, with threefold
fees, open to all.

---

"This was the terrible hardship, strong-armed one, that Rama of boundless energy
met long ago, brought on by life in the forest. Do not grieve, tiger among men.
You are a kshatriya, scorcher of enemies. You walk the road that rests on the
strength of arms, whose outcome is glorious. Not the least wrong is seen in you.
Even the gods and asuras, with Indra, might lose heart on this road. The
thunderbolt-wielder, with the Maruts' help, killed Vritra, and Namuchi, who was
hard to overcome, and the rakshasi Dirghajihva. For one who has helpers, every
purpose here is achieved in every way. What can he not win in battle, who has
Dhananjaya for a brother? And here is Bhima, of terrible valour, strongest of
the strong, and the young twins, sons of Madri, great archers. With such
helpers, why do you lose heart, scorcher of enemies? They could beat the
thunderbolt-wielder's army with its hosts of Maruts. With these great archers,
like gods, as your helpers, you too will beat all your enemies in battle, bull
of the Bharatas.

"And look at this. Draupadi Krishna, carried off by the wicked Saindhava, mighty
and drunk with valour, has been brought back by these great ones after a very
hard deed, and King Jayadratha has been beaten and brought under your power.
Rama brought Vaidehi back without such helpers, after killing in battle the
ten-necked rakshasa of terrible valour. His friends were monkeys and black-faced
bears, creatures of other kinds, king. Think about this. So do not grieve, tiger
of the Kurus, bull of the Bharatas. Great ones like you do not grieve, scorcher
of enemies."

Comforted like this by wise Markandeya, the king, high-minded, let his sorrow
go, and spoke again.

**Divergences.**

* **No fire ordeal.** In the CE Sita does not walk into fire. She calls on the wind and the elements to take her life if she has sinned, and the Wind, Fire, Varuna and Brahma declare her innocent (3.275.22–34). The pyre lit by Lakshmana and Sita's entering the flames are a rejected appendix passage (@28). See L-158.
* **The end of the story.** The Mahabharata's telling ends with Rama's consecration and ten horse sacrifices (3.275.65–69). There is no second banishment of Sita, and no Lava and Kusha. A rejected line (\*1307) gives the length of Rama's reign. See L-158.
* **Yama's word.** A rejected passage (\*1304) adds Yama among the gods who vouch for Sita, and another (\*1303) a hymn to Rama as Vishnu. Not narrated.
* **Fourteen years.** A rejected line (\*1305) has Dasharatha say the fourteen years are complete. Not narrated.
* **The benefits of hearing.** Rejected passages (\*1308, @29, \*1309) add the merit of hearing the Ramayana. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*1295–\*1302, \*1306) add small details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 3.274.8: Ravana makes forms of Rama and Lakshmana; "Bharata" is Markandeya's address to Yudhishthira.
* 3.274.22 *bhuśuṇḍīḥ*: "bhushundis", a kind of missile.
* 3.274.30: "The five elements left the great Ravana." The epithet *mahābhāga*, "great", is the text's.
* 3.275.10 *parāmarśaviśaṅkitaḥ*: "Fearing that she had been touched".
* 3.275.13 *śvāvalīḍhaṃ havir yathā*: "any more than an offering licked by a dog". Rama's words, kept.
* 3.275.14 *sahasā*: "suddenly fell". "Suddenly" renders *sahasā*.
* 3.275.28: Varuna speaks as lord of the waters: "the juices in the bodies of creatures come from me".
* 3.275.31 *kasmāc cit kāraṇāt*: "For some reason". The text does not say what.
* 3.275.38 *paulomyā*: "Pauloma's daughter", Shachi, Indra's wife.
* 3.275.43: Here Sita gives Hanuman life "as long as Rama's fame". When Hanuman told Bhima of it, he said he had asked Rama for life as long as Rama's story is told (3.147.37). Both are kept as the text has them. See L-133.
* 3.275.65 *vaiṣṇave … nakṣatre*: "under the constellation of Vishnu", Shravana.
* 3.275.69 *jārūthyān*: "with threefold fees". Ganguli has "treble presents".
* 3.276.4: Dirghajihva, "long-tongued", a rakshasi killed by Indra.

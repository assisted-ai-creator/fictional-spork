# The Vaishnava Sacrifice

"While the great Parthas were living in that forest," said Janamejaya, "what did
the sons of Dhritarashtra do, those great archers, best of men? And Karna
Vaikartana, and mighty Shakuni, and Bhishma, Drona and Kripa? You must tell me."

So the Parthas had gone their way, and Suyodhana, freed by the sons of Pandu and
let go, had come to Hastinapura. Then Bhishma said this to the son of
Dhritarashtra, great king.

"I told you before, my son, when you were going to the ascetics' forest, that I
did not like your going, and you did not do as I said. So you were taken by
force by your enemies, hero, and freed by the Pandavas, who know dharma, and you
are not ashamed. Before your eyes, son of Gandhari, lord of the people, before
you and your army, the suta's son ran from the gandharvas' battle in fear. He
ran while you and your army cried out, prince. You have seen the courage of the
great Pandavas, strong-armed one, and that of Karna, the wicked suta's son.
Karna is not worth a quarter of the Pandavas, best of kings, in the science of
the bow, in bravery or in dharma, lover of dharma. So I think it fitting for
this family's growth that you make peace with the great Pandavas, best of those
who know how to make peace."

When Bhishma had said this, King Duryodhana son of Dhritarashtra laughed and
suddenly left with the son of Subala, king. When they saw that he had gone,
Karna, Duhshasana and the others, great archers, followed the mighty son of
Dhritarashtra. Seeing them go, Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, was overcome
with shame, king, and went to his own house.

When Bhishma had gone, great king, King Duryodhana son of Dhritarashtra came
back to that place and took counsel with his counsellors. "What would be best
for us? What is left to be done? How may it be done well?" So he took counsel,
Bharata.

"Duryodhana," said Karna, "hear what I shall tell you, Kaurava, and when you
have heard it, do all of it, tamer of enemies. Today the earth is yours, hero,
without a rival, best of kings. Protect it like great-minded Shakra when he had
killed his enemies."

At Karna's words the king said to Karna again, "Nothing is hard to win for the
man who has you, bull among men, as his ally, devoted to him and ready to act
for him. But I have a wish. Hear it as it is. When I saw the Pandava's royal
consecration, the best of sacrifices, a longing arose in me. Fulfil it, son of
the suta."

Then Karna said to the king, "Today all the rulers of the earth are in your
power, best of kings. Let the best of the brahmins be called, and let the things
needed be gathered as the rules say, best of the Kurus, and the tools of
sacrifice. Let priests who have mastered the Veda be called, as it is taught,
and let them do the rite as the treatises say, king, tamer of enemies. Let your
great sacrifice begin too, bull among the Bharatas, with much food and drink,
rich and full of every virtue."

At Karna's words the son of Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, sent for his
household priest. He said to him, "Perform for me the royal consecration, the
best of sacrifices, with the finest fees at its close, in the right way and in
due order."

The bull among brahmins said to the king, "That best of sacrifices cannot be
held in your family while Yudhishthira is alive, best of the Kauravas, best of
kings. And your father Dhritarashtra is alive too, and long-lived. For this
reason as well this sacrifice goes against you, best of kings. But there is
another great sacrifice, lord, equal to the royal consecration. Sacrifice with
that, king of kings, and hear what I say. Let these rulers of the earth who pay
you tribute give you their tribute, king, and gold, worked and unworked. With it
let a plough be made for you today, best of kings, and with it let the ground of
your sacrificial enclosure be ploughed, Bharata. There let the sacrifice go on
in the right way, well prepared, with abundant food, barred to no one. This is
the sacrifice named for Vishnu, fit for good men. No one has offered it except
Vishnu of old. This great sacrifice rivals the royal consecration, the best of
sacrifices. It pleases us, and it is best for you, Bharata. It will be free of
obstacles, and your wish will bear fruit."

When the brahmins had said this, King Duryodhana son of Dhritarashtra said to
Karna, to the son of Subala and to his brothers, "The brahmins' words please me
in every way, no doubt of it. If they please you, tell me at once."

"So be it," they all said to the king. Then the king gave his orders to those in
charge, in due order. All the craftsmen were set to making the plough, best of
kings, and all of it was done as it had been said, in due order.

---

Then all the craftsmen and the chief counsellors, and the very wise Vidura, told
the son of Dhritarashtra, "The great sacrifice is ready, king, and its time has
come, Bharata. A divine golden plough of great price has been made."

When he heard this, King Duryodhana, best of kings, son of Dhritarashtra, lord
of the people, ordered the king of sacrifices to begin. Then the sacrifice
began, well prepared, with abundant food, and the son of Gandhari was
consecrated as the treatises say, in due order. Dhritarashtra was delighted, and
so were the glorious Vidura, Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna and glorious Gandhari.

He sent swift messengers to invite the kings, king of kings, and the brahmins
too. The messengers set off as they had been told, on fast mounts. Duhshasana
said to one messenger as he was setting out, "Go quickly to Dvaitavana, and
invite the Pandavas, those wicked men, as is proper, and the brahmins in that
great forest."

He went to the Pandavas' dwelling, bowed to them and said, "Duryodhana, best of
kings, is sacrificing, great king. The joy of the Kurus has won a flood of
wealth by his own courage. Kings and brahmins are going there from everywhere. I
have been sent, king, by the great Kaurava. King Duryodhana son of
Dhritarashtra, lord of men, invites you. You should come to see the sacrifice
the king has longed for in his heart."

When he heard the messenger's words, King Yudhishthira, tiger among kings, said,
"By good fortune King Suyodhana, who adds to the fame of our forefathers, is
sacrificing with the chief of sacrifices. We too would go to him, but not now,
in any way. We must keep our pact until the thirteenth year."

When he heard the Dharma King's words, Bhima said, "King Yudhishthira, the
Dharma King, will go then, after the thirteenth year. He will go when he casts
him into the fire that blazes with missiles and weapons, in the sacrifice of
battle. When the Pandava pours the offering of his anger on the sons of
Dhritarashtra, then we shall come. So tell that Suyodhana."

The other Pandavas said nothing unpleasant, king. The messenger told the son of
Dhritarashtra all that had happened.

Then the best of men, lords of many countries, came to the city of the son of
Dhritarashtra, and greatly blessed brahmins too. They were honoured as the
treatises say, each by his class and in due order, and they were filled with the
greatest joy and gladness, lord of men.

Dhritarashtra, surrounded by all the Kauravas, was filled with great joy, king
of kings. He said to Vidura, "Steward, see to it quickly that all the people are
happy and well fed and content in the sacrificial hall."

At this order Vidura, wise and knowing dharma, honoured all the classes by their
due measure, tamer of enemies. He gladly gave them things to eat and to chew,
food and drink, fragrant garlands, and clothes of many kinds.

The hero, the king of kings, took the final bath as the treatises say, in due
order. He spoke kindly, gave wealth of many kinds, and sent the kings and the
brahmins away in their thousands. When he had sent the kings away, he entered
Hastinapura, surrounded by his brothers, with Karna and the son of Subala.

---

As he entered, great king, the sutas praised the unfailing one, and the people
praised the great archer, the best of kings. The people scattered parched grain
and sandal powder over him and said, "By good fortune your sacrifice is finished
without hindrance, king."

But some others there, windbags, said to the king, "This sacrifice is not equal
to Yudhishthira's. It is not worth a sixteenth part of that one." So some
windbags said to the king.

But his friends said, "This sacrifice goes beyond all others. Yayati and
Nahusha, Mandhatri and Bharata offered this sacrifice, were made pure, and all
went to heaven."

Hearing these fine words of his friends, bull among the Bharatas, the king
entered the city and his own house, delighted. Then he greeted the feet of his
mother and father, lord of the people, and of Bhishma, Drona and Kripa, and of
wise Vidura. His younger brothers greeted him, and, loving his brothers, he sat
down on the chief seat, surrounded by them.

The suta's son rose and said to him, great king, "By good fortune this great
sacrifice of yours is finished, best of the Bharatas. When the Parthas have been
killed in battle, and you have performed the royal consecration, then, best of
men, I shall honour you again."

The glorious great king, the son of Dhritarashtra, said to him, "This is true,
hero. When the wicked Pandavas have been killed, best of men, and I have held
the great sacrifice, the royal consecration, you will again give me joy, hero."
When he had said this, Bharata, the very wise Kaurava embraced Karna and thought
about the royal consecration, the best of sacrifices. The best of kings said to
the friends at his side, "When shall I kill all the Pandavas, Kauravas, and hold
that best of sacrifices, the rich royal consecration?"

Then Karna said to him, "Hear me, elephant among kings. I will not have my feet
washed until Arjuna is killed."

At this the great archers, the great chariot fighters, the sons of
Dhritarashtra, shouted aloud, now that Karna had vowed to kill Phalguna in
battle. The sons of Dhritarashtra thought the Pandavas already beaten. King
Duryodhana too sent away the bulls among men and went into his house in
splendour, like the lord Kubera into Chaitraratha. All those great archers went
to their houses too, Bharata.

---

The Pandavas, great archers, stirred by the messenger's words, kept thinking
about that same matter and found no ease anywhere. And again, king of kings,
spies brought news of the suta's son's vow to kill Vijaya. When he heard it, the
son of Dharma was deeply troubled, lord of men. He thought of Karna's armour
that could not be pierced, and of his wonderful courage, and remembering their
hardships, he could find no peace. As he was lost in these thoughts, the
great-hearted one made up his mind to leave the Dvaitavana forest, full of many
beasts of prey.

King Duryodhana son of Dhritarashtra ruled the earth with his heroic brothers
and with Bhishma, Drona and Kripa. Joined with Karna the suta's son, who shone
in battle, King Duryodhana always did what pleased him, and honoured the chief
brahmins with sacrifices and rich fees. The scorcher of enemies did what pleased
his brothers, king, for the hero had made up his mind that wealth bears fruit in
giving and enjoyment.

---

"When the mighty sons of Pandu had freed Duryodhana," said Janamejaya, "what did
they do in that forest? You must tell me."

Then one night, as Kunti's son Yudhishthira lay asleep at Dvaitavana, the deer
showed themselves to him at the end of a dream, their throats choked with tears.
The king of kings said to them as they trembled with joined palms, "Say what you
wish to say. Who are you, and what do you want?"

At these words of the glorious Pandava, son of Kunti, the deer that were left
alive said to Yudhishthira, "We are the deer of Dvaitavana, those left alive,
Bharata. Move your dwelling, great king, so that we are not wiped out. You
brothers are all heroes, all skilled in weapons. You have left only a few of the
families of the forest-dwellers. We few who are left are like seed, wise one.
Let us grow again, king of kings, by your grace, Yudhishthira."

When he saw the deer trembling and afraid, left only as seed, Yudhishthira the
Dharma King was deeply grieved. The king, who delighted in the good of all
beings, said to them, "So be it. What you say is true, and I shall do it."

He woke at the end of the night, and the best of kings, full of pity for the
deer, said to his brothers together, "In the night, at the end of a dream, the
deer that are left alive said to me, 'We have grown few. Bless you, have pity on
us.' They spoke the truth. We must have pity on the dwellers of the forest. For
a year and eight months now we have been living off them. Let us go back to the
lovely Kamyaka, the best of forests, full of deer, at the head of the desert,
known far and wide, towards Lake Trinabindu. Let us spend the rest of our time
there happily, roaming about."

Then the Pandavas, who knew dharma, set out at once, king, with the brahmins who
had lived there with them, and followed by Indrasena and their other servants.
They went by roads that others had travelled, with good food and pure water, and
they saw the holy hermitage of Kamyaka, full of ascetics. The Kauravyas, best of
the Bharatas, surrounded by the best of the brahmins, entered that forest as
those who have done good enter heaven.

**Divergences.**

* **Karna's conquest of the world.** A long rejected appendix passage (@24), placed after 3.241.15, has Karna complain that Bhishma always runs him down, and then set out to conquer the earth for Duryodhana: Drupada, Bhagadatta, the Himalayan kings, Anga, Vanga and Kalinga, Rukmin in the south, Shishupala's son, the Vrishnis and the western peoples. It is not in the CE, and not narrated. In the CE Karna says only that the earth is already Duryodhana's, without a rival (3.241.16, 20). See L-150.
* **Karna's other vows.** A rejected passage (\*1167) adds to Karna's vow that he will not eat meat, will keep the "asura vow", and will never say "there is none" to anyone who asks. Not narrated. His vow of giving to brahmins is in the CE later, at 3.284.12–13 and 3.284.25. See L-151.
* **Arjuna's answer.** A rejected passage (\*1173) gives Arjuna a reply to Yudhishthira about the move. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*1160–\*1166, \*1168–\*1172, \*1174) add small details, such as Karna looking back as he ran and Karna giving away wealth at home. Not narrated.

# Kubera

Ajatashatru, son of Kunti, heard the caves of the mountain ring with sounds of
many kinds. So did both the sons of Madri, and Dhaumya and Krishna, and the
brahmins and all their friends. Not seeing Bhimasena, they were all troubled.
The great chariot fighters left Draupadi with Arshtishena, and the heroes
climbed the mountain together, armed. The great chariot fighters reached the top
and looked about them. There the great bowmen saw Bhimasena, the tamer of
enemies, and the huge, mighty, most terrible rakshasas Bhimasena had struck
down, some twitching, some lifeless. The strong-armed one shone, holding mace,
sword and bow, like Maghavan when he has killed all the danavas in battle.

Then the Parthas went up to Vrikodara, embraced him, and sat down there. They
had reached the highest road. The mountain peak shone with those four great
bowmen like heaven with the great, blessed guardians of the world, the best of
the gods. Seeing Kubera's dwelling and the rakshasas struck down, the brother
spoke to his brother, the Pandava, as he sat there. "Whether it was rashness or
folly, Bhima, you have done an evil thing. It is not like you, hero, any more
than a lie is like a sage. Those who know dharma know that one must not do what
the king hates. You have done what the thirty gods hate, Bhimasena. Whoever
ignores wealth and dharma and sets his mind on evil, Partha, will surely reap
the fruit of evil deeds. Do not do such a thing again, if you want to please
me."

So spoke righteous Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, to his unfallen brother. He knew
how to tell the true nature of things. Then he fell silent, full of splendour,
and went on thinking of it.

---

Then the rakshasas whom Bhimasena had left alive went together to Kubera's
dwelling. They reached Vaishravana's home at great speed and, tormented by fear
of Bhimasena, gave a terrible cry of pain. They had thrown down their weapons.
They were worn out, their clothes smeared with blood, their hair loose. They
said to the lord of the yakshas, king, "Lord, all your rakshasas who fought in
the front with maces, iron bars, swords, lances and spears are killed. A single
man, a human, has forced his way onto the mountain, Lord of Wealth, and the
hosts of the Krodhavashas are killed in battle all together. The finest of the
lords of the rakshasas and of the yakshas lie dead, lord, lifeless, their breath
gone. The mountain has been taken. We have been let go. Your friend Maniman is
killed. A human has done this. Do what must be done next."

When the lord of all the hosts of yakshas heard this, his eyes red with anger,
he said, "What?" Hearing that Bhima had offended a second time, the Lord of
Wealth, the lord of the yakshas, was angry and said, "Harness my chariot." Then
they yoked gandharva horses to his fine chariot, which looked like a mass of
dark cloud and rose like a mountain peak. The finest horses, with every good
quality, clear-eyed, full of splendour, strength and speed, adorned with jewels
of every kind, shone when they were yoked to the chariot. Swift, as if they were
about to fly, they cheered one another with signs that promised victory.

The blessed one, the king of kings, of great splendour, mounted that great
chariot and set out, praised by gods and gandharvas. So the great-spirited lord
of the wealth of all the yakshas set out. Thousands upon thousands of yakshas,
red-eyed, golden, huge and mighty, armed, their swords bound on, surrounded him
and went with him at great speed. The Pandavas, their hair on end, saw the great
Lord of Wealth, fair to see, coming close. And Kubera, seeing the sons of Pandu,
the great-spirited chariot fighters, with their bows and swords in their hands,
was pleased. Flying up like birds, at great speed, the yakshas stood on the
mountain peak near them, with the Lord of Wealth at their head. Then the yakshas
and gandharvas, seeing that he was glad at heart towards the Pandavas, stood
there without hostility, Bharata.

The great-spirited Pandavas, Nakula and Sahadeva and the son of Dharma who knew
dharma, bowed to the lord, the Giver of Wealth. Thinking that they had done
wrong, the great chariot fighters all stood round the Lord of Wealth with joined
hands. The lord of wealth sat on the glorious Pushpaka, finest of couches and
seats, made by Vishvakarman, with bright edges. As he sat there, yakshas and
rakshasas in thousands, huge, with ears like spikes, very swift, sat down around
him. Gandharvas in hundreds, and hosts of apsarases, stood round him, as the
gods stand round Shatakratu.

Bhimasena, wearing a fine golden garland on his head, with arrows, sword and bow
in his hands, looked at the Lord of Wealth. Bhima felt no fear and no faintness,
though the rakshasas had wounded him, even in that state, even looking at
Kubera. He saw Bhima standing ready to fight, taking up his sharp arrows. Then
the one drawn by men said to the son of Dharma, "All creatures know you, Partha,
as one who loves the good of creatures. Live without fear on the mountain top
with your kin. And do not be angry with Bhimasena, Pandava. These were killed
before by Time. Your younger brother was only the means. There is no need for
shame at this rash act. The gods foresaw long ago the destruction of the yakshas
and rakshasas. I am not angry with Bhimasena. I am pleased, bull of the
Bharatas. I was pleased long ago with this deed of Bhima's."

When he had said this to the king, he said to Bhimasena, "It does not weigh on
my mind, my son, best of the Kurus. I mean this rash act you have done for
Krishna's sake, Bhima. You ignored me and the gods and destroyed the yakshas and
rakshasas, trusting in the strength of your arms, and so I am pleased with you.
Today I am freed from a terrible curse, Vrikodara. Long ago the great seer
Agastya, in anger, cursed me for some offence. This was its ending. My suffering
was foreseen long ago, joy of the Pandavas. There is no fault of yours in this
at all, slayer of enemies."

"How were you cursed by the great Agastya, lord?" said Yudhishthira. "I want to
hear the cause of the curse, god. And I wonder that you were not burned up there
and then by the wise one's anger, with your army and your followers."

"There was a council of the gods at Kushavati, lord of men," said Vaishravana.
"I went there, surrounded by three hundred mahapadmas of yakshas of terrible
form, bearing weapons of every kind. On the way I saw Agastya, best of seers,
practising fierce austerity on the bank of the Yamuna, in a place full of birds
of every kind and bright with trees in flower. He stood with his arms raised,
facing the sun, a mass of splendour, blazing like a fire fed with fuel. My
friend, the glorious lord of the rakshasas named Maniman, out of stupidity and
ignorance, pride and folly, spat on the great seer's head from the sky, Bharata.

"In his anger the seer said this to me, as if burning all the quarters. 'Lord of
Wealth, because this wicked friend of yours has scorned me and insulted me like
this before your eyes, he will be killed by a human, with these troops of yours.
And you, fool, will suffer when these troops are killed. When you see that same
human you will be freed from the sin. But this terrible curse will not fall on
those of your troops who obey your command, with their sons and grandsons and
their forces.' That was the curse I had long ago from that best of seers, and
your brother Bhima has freed me from it, great king."

---

"Yudhishthira," said Vaishravana, "steadiness, skill, place and time, and
courage: these are the five rules by which the world's affairs are done. In the
Krita age men were steady and skilled each in his own work, and knew the rules
of courage. A kshatriya who is steady, who knows place and time and the rules of
every dharma, rules the earth, best of kshatriyas. A man who acts so in all his
work, Partha, wins fame in the world, hero, and after death the good road.
Watching for the right place and time, Shakra, slayer of Vritra, showed his
courage and won the kingdom of heaven with the Vasus.

"The man who is evil in himself and evil in mind, who follows only evil and does
not know how to tell deeds apart, is lost here and hereafter. A fool who does
not know the time, who cannot tell one task from another, whose undertakings are
empty, is lost here and hereafter. The rash, the deceitful and the wicked, who
want to seize every power, come to an evil purpose. This Bhimasena does not know
dharma. He is proud, childish in mind, impatient and fearless. Govern him, bull
among men.

"Go back to the hermitage of the royal seer Arshtishena and live there through
the first, dark half of the month, free of grief and fear. The people of Alaka,
with the gandharvas, the yakshas and the rakshasas, and all who live on the
mountain, will guard you at my command, strong-armed lord of men. They will
guard you and the best of the brahmins with you. And Vrikodara here, who stands
on rashness on this mountain, must be kept well in check by you, king, best of
the upholders of dharma. From now on, king of kings, the forest dwellers will
see you, wait on you always, and guard you in every way. My servants will always
bring you food and drink, sweet and plentiful, bulls among men.

"Jishnu is Mahendra's own son, born of union, and Vrikodara the Wind's, and you
Dharma's, my son, and the twins, strong in themselves, are the Ashvins' own
sons. As each of you is his father's to guard, so you are all mine to guard here
too, Yudhishthira. Phalguna, the younger brother of Bhimasena, who knows how to
tell the true nature of things and knows the fine points of every dharma, is
well in heaven. Whatever good fortunes are held highest in the worlds, my son,
have all been in Dhananjaya from his birth. Self-control, giving, strength,
wisdom, modesty, steadiness and the highest splendour: these too are in that
great-spirited one of boundless splendour. Jishnu does no shameful deed out of
folly, Pandava. Men do not tell of any lie of the Partha's among men. Honoured
by the gods, the Fathers and the gandharvas, he who increases the fame of the
Kurus is making weapons his own in Shakra's house, Bharata.

"Shantanu of great splendour, your father's grandfather, brought all the kings
of the earth under his power by dharma. He is pleased with the Partha, the
bearer of Gandiva, in heaven, Partha. That mighty and glorious one, who stood as
the bearer of his family's yoke, honoured the Fathers, the gods and the brahmins
and performed seven great horse sacrifices by the Yamuna. Your great-grandfather
Shantanu, the high king, who won heaven and lives in Shakra's world, asks after
your welfare, king."

Then Vrikodara laid down his spear, mace, sword and bow, bull of the Bharatas,
and bowed to Kubera. The lord of wealth, the refuge of those who come for
refuge, said to him, "Be the breaker of your enemies' pride and the increaser of
your friends' joy. Live in your own lovely dwellings, scorchers of enemies. The
yakshas will bring you what you want, bulls of the Bharatas. Soon Gudakesha,
Dhananjaya, bull among men, will come, with his weapons won, sent by Maghavan
himself."

When he had counselled Yudhishthira of excellent deeds like this, the lord of
the guhyakas went off to the Asta, the finest of mountains. Yakshas and
rakshasas in thousands followed him in chariots strewn with coverlets and
adorned with jewels of every kind. As the fine horses went along the road of
Airavata to Kubera's dwelling, there was a sound like the sound of birds. The
horses of the Lord of Wealth went swiftly through the sky, as if dragging the
clouds along, as if drinking the wind. Then, at the command of the Lord of
Wealth, the lifeless bodies of the rakshasas were dragged away from the mountain
top. For wise Agastya had fixed that as the time of their curse, and so they
were all killed in battle, with Maniman. The great-spirited Pandavas spent that
night happily in those dwellings, free of fear, honoured by all the rakshasas.

---

Then at sunrise, when he had performed the morning rites, Dhaumya came to the
Pandavas with Arshtishena, tamer of enemies. They saluted the feet of
Arshtishena and of Dhaumya, and then they all honoured those brahmins with
joined hands. Then Dhaumya, the great seer, took Yudhishthira by the right hand,
looked to the east and spoke.

"There stands the king of mountains, Mandara, shining, covering the earth to the
edge of the ocean, great king. Indra and Vaishravana guard this quarter,
Pandava, adorned with mountains, forests and groves. The wise seers who know
every dharma call this the dwelling of the great Indra and of King Vaishravana,
my son. From here creatures, and the seers who know dharma, the siddhas, the
sadhyas and the gods, worship the rising sun.

"Yama, the righteous king, lord of all that breathes, dwells in this southern
quarter, the road of the dead. This is Samyamana, holy and most wonderful to
see, the dwelling of the king of the dead, full of the highest wealth. The wise
call this king of mountains the Asta, the Setting Mountain, reaching which the
sun stands firm by truth, king. Dwelling on this king of mountains and in the
great ocean, King Varuna guards all creatures.

"Lighting up the northern quarter, great Meru stands in its glory, blessed one,
auspicious, the goal of those who know brahman. On it is the court of Brahma,
and there stands Prajapati, the self of all creatures, creating all that moves
and does not move. Great Meru is the auspicious, untroubled seat too of those
called the mind-born sons of Brahma, with Daksha as the seventh. Here the seven
divine seers, led by Vasishtha, always set, and here they rise again, my son.

"Look at the dustless place, the highest peak of Meru, where the Grandfather
sits with the gods who are content in themselves. Beyond that seat of Brahma
shines the place of the one they call the unchanging nature of the nature of all
creatures, the god without beginning or end, the lord, the supreme Narayana.
Even the gods see that divine, auspicious place, made of splendour, only with
effort. That place of the great Vishnu blazes beyond the sun and fire. By its
own light it is hard for gods and danavas to look at, king. All the lights reach
it and shine no more, for the all-pervading lord, of undaunted spirit, shines
there himself. Ascetics go there to Narayana, Hari, by devotion, joined to the
highest austerity, made pure by good deeds. Perfected by yoga, great-spirited,
free of darkness and delusion, they go there and do not come back to this world,
Bharata. This is the place of the Lord, fixed, unfailing and unchanging,
Yudhishthira. Bow to it always from here.

"The blessed sun, whose work dispels darkness, draws all the lights along and
goes round this, keeping it on his right. Reaching the Setting Mountain, and
passing the twilight, the sun turns to the northern quarter. Having followed
Meru round, Pandava, the god, the sun, given to the good of all creatures, goes
on again facing east. So too the blessed moon goes with the stars, dividing time
into months many ways at the joints of the moon. So he goes round great Meru,
tireless, giving life to all creatures, and comes again to Mandara. So too the
god who kills darkness, giving life to the world with his rays, the sun, turns
along this unhindered road.

"Wanting to make the cold season, he goes to the southern quarter, and then time
brings cold to all creatures. Turning back, the sun takes to himself the heat of
all creatures, moving and unmoving, by his own heat. Then sweat, weariness,
sloth and faintness come on men, and living things seek sleep again and again.
So, having covered this road that cannot be described, the blessed one, the sun,
sends the rains again, giving life to creatures. Making things moving and
unmoving grow by rain, wind and pleasant heat, the one of great splendour turns
back again. So the sun goes round, Partha, tireless, turning the wheel of time
and drawing all creatures along. His going is unbroken. He never stands still,
Pandava. He takes the heat of creatures and gives it back again. Dividing the
life and the work of all creatures, Bharata, the lord always makes the days and
nights, the kalas and the kashthas."

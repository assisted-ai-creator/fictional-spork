# The Hawk and the Dove

"Here mortals practise austerity and go to heaven, Bharata," said Lomasha. "Men
who want to die come here in thousands, king. For Daksha, when he sacrificed
long ago, pronounced this blessing: 'Men who die here win heaven.' This is the
holy, divine Sarasvati, a river full of flood. This is the place called
Vinashana, the vanishing of the Sarasvati, lord of the people. It is the gate of
the land of the Nishadas, and it was out of dislike for them that the Sarasvati
went into the earth, hero, so that the Nishadas would not know her. This is
Chamasodbheda, where the Sarasvati can be seen, where the divine, holy rivers
that run to the sea came to her.

"This is the great ford of the Sindhu, tamer of enemies, where Lopamudra met
Agastya and chose him for her husband. Here shines the ford of Prabhasa, you who
shine like the sun, dear to Indra, holy, purifying, destroying sin. Here is the
fine ford called Vishnupada, and here is the lovely Vipasha, the most purifying
river. Here the blessed seer Vasishtha, in grief for his sons, bound himself and
threw himself in, and rose again unbound.

"This is the land of Kashmir, all holy, tamer of enemies, where great seers
live. See it with your brothers. Here all the northern seers had their
conversation with the son of Nahusha, and here Agni and Kashyapa had theirs,
Bharata. Here shines the gate of Manasa, great king. Glorious Rama made a
passage through the middle of this mountain. This is Vatikashanda, famous, whose
courage is true, which did not pass beyond the gate of the Videhas or the north.
This is the place called Ujjanaka, where Yavakri became calm, and the blessed
seer Vasishtha with Arundhati. And here is the lake Kushavant, where the lotus
Kusheshaya grows, and the hermitage of Rukmini, where she, who was never angry,
became calm.

"You have heard in brief of the places of meditation, Pandava. Now you will see
the great mountain Bhrigutunga, great king, and the Jala and the Upajala, the
rivers on either side of the Yamuna, where Ushinara sacrificed and surpassed
Vasava. To know that king, lord of the people, Vasava and Agni came to his
assembly of the gods, Bharata. Wanting to test the great Ushinara, the two
givers of boons came to his sacrifice, Indra as a hawk and Agni as a dove. The
dove, fleeing from the hawk in fear, came to the king's thigh, king, and hid
there, tormented by fear, seeking refuge.

---

"'All the lords of the earth say that you alone are righteous, king,' said the
hawk. 'Why then do you set out to do a thing against dharma? This is my
appointed food, king, and I am tormented by hunger. Do not break dharma out of
greed for dharma. You have abandoned dharma.'

"'This bird has come to me terrified, looking for protection, great bird, afraid
of you, clinging to life,' said the king. 'When a dove has come to me like this,
seeking safety, not to give it would be the highest adharma. Do you not see it,
hawk? The dove is plainly trembling and frightened, hawk, come to me for its
life. To give it up would be shameful.'

"'All creatures come into being from food, lord of the earth,' said the hawk.
'By food they grow, and by it they live. A man can live long without something
he finds hard to give up. But no one can live long if he gives up food. Robbed
of my food today, lord of the people, my breath will leave my body and go by the
road of no return. When I have died, righteous one, my wife and children will
perish. By protecting the dove you will destroy many lives. A dharma that
obstructs another dharma is no dharma. It is a bad dharma. The dharma that does
not conflict is dharma, you whose courage is true. Where dharmas conflict, lord
of the earth, weigh the greater and the less, and practise that dharma in which
there is no obstruction. Knowing the greater and the less in deciding what is
dharma and what is not, king, decide for dharma where the greater lies.'

"'You speak with great wisdom, best of birds,' said the king. 'Are you Suparna,
king of the birds? Beyond doubt you know dharma, for you say many wonderful
things joined with dharma. I see that nothing is unknown to you. How can you
think it right to abandon one who seeks refuge? Your effort is for the sake of
food, bird. It can be met another way, and with more food. Let a bull, a boar, a
deer or a buffalo be prepared for you today, or whatever else you want.'

"'I do not eat a boar, a bull, or deer of any kind, great king,' said the hawk.
'What use is that food to me? The food that fate has given me, bull among
kshatriyas, give up to me, lord of the earth: this dove. Hawks eat doves. That
is the eternal law. Do not climb a plantain trunk, king, when you know the
road.'

"'Rule the rich kingdom of the Shibis, hawk honoured by the birds,' said the
king. 'Or whatever you want, hawk, I will give you all of it, except this bird
that has come to me for refuge. Tell me by what deed you would let it go, best
of birds, and I will do it. I will not give you the dove.'

"'Ushinara, if you love the dove, lord of men,' said the hawk, 'cut out your own
flesh and weigh it on the scales against the dove. When your flesh is equal to
the dove, king, give it to me. That will satisfy me.'

"'I take it as a kindness, hawk, that you ask me this,' said the king. 'So I
will give you my own flesh today, weighed on the scales.'

"Then the king, who knew the highest dharma, cut out his own flesh and weighed
it against the dove, son of Kunti, lord. Held in the scales, the dove was
heavier. Again the king Ushinara cut out flesh and gave it. When there was no
flesh left that weighed equal to the dove, he whose flesh was cut away climbed
into the scales himself.

"'I am Indra, you who know dharma,' said the hawk, 'and this dove is the fire
that carries the offerings. We came to your place of sacrifice to test you in
dharma. Since you cut the flesh from your limbs, lord of the people, your
shining fame will surpass the worlds. As long as men in the world tell of you,
king, so long your fame and your worlds will last for ever.'

"This is the dwelling of that great king, Pandava. See it with me, holy, freeing
from sin. Here the gods and the eternal sages are always seen by holy and great
brahmins, king."

**Divergences.**

* **Shibi or Ushinara.** In the familiar version the king is Shibi. In the CE he is Ushinara, whose kingdom is
  that of the Shibis (3.130.17; 3.131.20). Later in Book 3 the hunter of Mithila does credit "Shibi Aushinara" with
  giving his own flesh (3.199.6). See L-130.
* **The restored body.** Rejected passages (\*647, \*648) have Indra and Agni make the king whole again and the king
  rise to heaven. In the CE Indra promises only undying fame and worlds (3.131.29–30).
* **The famous line.** The lines that all sacrifices weigh less than saving one frightened creature, and that
  abandoning a refugee equals killing a brahmin or a cow, are rejected (\*642, \*643).

**Choices.**

* 3.130.4: the Sarasvati hides from the Nishadas "out of dislike for them"; kept.
* 3.130.9: Vasishtha's attempt at death in grief for his sons is named here and told elsewhere (1.167).
* 3.130.13–15: Vatikashanda, Ujjanaka, Kushavant and Rukmini's hermitage are named as the text names them; the
  sense of 3.130.13 is uncertain.
* 3.131.10 *dharmaṃ yo bādhate dharmo na sa dharmaḥ kudharma tat*: "A dharma that obstructs another dharma is no
  dharma. It is a bad dharma."
* 3.131.13 *suparṇaḥ*: Suparna, Garuda.
* 3.131.19 *mā … kadalīskandham āruha*: "Do not climb a plantain trunk", something that will not bear you.

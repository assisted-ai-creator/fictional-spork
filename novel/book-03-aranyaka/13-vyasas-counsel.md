# Vyasa's Counsel

"You have made a pact with Time," said Bhimasena, "with the Ender, who flies,
who is endless and immeasurable, a stream that carries everything away. You
think Time is before your eyes, great king, while you are a mortal, bound by
Time. Men are like foam, and like fruit, that falls. When a man's life wastes
away at every blink of an eye, son of Kunti, like eye-black taken away on a
needle, why should he wait? Only a man whose life has no measure, or who knows
its measure, and sees everything before his eyes, should wait for the time. As
we wait, king, Time will waste away our lives for these thirteen years and bring
us to our deaths. Death always lives in the body of every embodied creature. So
let us strive for the kingdom before we die.

"The man who is not counted, who is not plain to see, a burden on the earth, and
does not pay back enmity, sinks like an ox. The man of little spirit and effort
who does not pay back enmity, I think his birth is fruitless, a man born to a
bad birth. A king's arms become golden, and his fame spreads, when he has killed
his enemy in battle and enjoys the wealth his arms have won. If a man kills the
one who tricked him, king, scourge of enemies, and goes straight to hell for it,
that hell is heaven to him. The burning that comes from resentment is brighter
than fire. I burn with it, and I cannot sleep by night or by day.

"And Partha here, Bibhatsu, the best at drawing the bowstring, sits burning
greatly, surely, like a lion in its den. He alone thinks himself above all the
archers in the world, and he holds in the heat of his own spirit like a great
elephant. Nakula and Sahadeva, and our old mother, the mother of heroes, sit
like dumb idiots, wanting only what pleases you. All our kin want to please you,
with the Srinjayas. Only I burn, and the mother of Prativindhya. What I say is
what pleases them all. All of them have come to disaster, and all of them long
for war.

"There could be no worse misfortune than this, king, that our kingdom has been
torn from us and is enjoyed by low men of little strength. From a fault of
character, full of pity, out of kindness, scourge of enemies, you bear these
hardships, king, and no one else praises it. You are merciful, a brahmin in
form. How were you born a kshatriya? For in this womb, mostly, men of cruel
minds are born. You have heard the dharma of kings, as Manu taught it, cruel,
joined with guile, not given to peace. When there is work to be done, tiger
among men, why do you sit like a cripple, you who have wisdom and courage,
learning and birth?

"You want to hide the Himalaya with a handful of grass, son of Kunti, when you
want to hide us. You cannot live unknown, Partha, famous as you are on the
earth, any more than the sun can go about unknown in the sky. How will Jishnu go
about unknown, like a great shala tree in a marsh, with its branches, flowers
and leaves, like a white elephant? How will these two, Nakula and Sahadeva, like
lions, brothers together, boys still, go about, Partha? How will Krishna go
about unknown, Partha, this princess of holy fame, Draupadi, the mother of
heroes, known everywhere? And these people know me, king, down to the children.
I see living unknown as like hiding Mount Meru.

"And we have driven many kings and princes from their kingdoms, who are now
loyal to Dhritarashtra. Those we have humbled will not be at peace with us, whom
they tricked. They must do us harm, to please him. They too will send many men
against us in secret, and tell of us when they know us. That would be a great
danger for us.

"We have lived in the forest for thirteen months, fully. Take them by measure as
so many years. A month can stand in place of a year, as the wise say, as the
putika plant stands for the soma. Let it be done so. Or, king, a man is freed
from sin by giving one good ox, a good bearer of loads, a full meal. So, king,
make up your mind to kill our enemies. For a kshatriya there is no other dharma
than battle."

---

When he heard Bhimasena's words, Yudhishthira, the son of Kunti, sighed, the
tiger among men, and thought deeply. When he had thought for a moment and
decided what was to be done, the scourge of enemies at once said this to
Bhimasena.

"It is just as you say, strong-armed one, Bharata. But take this other word of
mine to heart, you who know words. Great and evil deeds that are begun out of
sheer rashness, Bhimasena, come to grief, Bharata. When things are well planned,
boldly carried out, well done and well thought through, they succeed,
strong-armed one, and fate favours them. But you, out of sheer restlessness,
swollen with pride in your strength, think this deed should be begun. Hear me on
this.

"Think of Bhurishravas and Shala, mighty Jalasamdha, Bhishma, Drona and Karna,
and Drona's mighty son. Think of the sons of Dhritarashtra, hard to overcome,
with Duryodhana at their head, all masters of weapons and always armed for
battle. Think of the kings and lords of the earth whom we have harmed, who have
joined the Kaurava side and now feel affection for it. They are set on
Duryodhana's good, not on ours, Bharata. With full treasuries and strong forces,
they will strive to defend him. All of the Kaurava army, with their sons,
ministers and soldiers, have been given their shares of wealth and pleasures in
every way. Duryodhana has honoured those heroes above all. I am sure they will
give up their lives in battle.

"Though Bhishma, and Drona and great Kripa, strong-armed one, behave equally
towards us and towards them, they must surely repay the king's bread, I think.
So they will give up their lives in battle, hard as they are to give up. All of
them know divine weapons, all are devoted to dharma. I think they cannot be
beaten even by the gods with Vasava. And there is Karna, the great chariot
fighter, always resentful, always eager, who knows every weapon, who cannot be
beaten, covered in armour that cannot be pierced. Unless you beat all these best
of men in battle, you cannot kill Duryodhana alone, without help. I cannot
sleep, Vrikodara, thinking of the suta's son's lightness of hand, beyond all
archers."

When he understood these words, Bhimasena, who could never bear an insult, was
dismayed and afraid, and said nothing.

---

As the two Pandavas were talking like this, the great yogi Vyasa, the son of
Satyavati, came. He came to them as was proper and was honoured by the Pandavas,
and then the best of speakers said this to Yudhishthira.

"Yudhishthira, strong-armed one, I know the thought in your heart, and so I have
come quickly, bull among men, with my wisdom. The fear of Bhishma, Drona, Kripa,
Karna and Drona's son that turns over in your heart, Bharata, killer of enemies,
I will destroy by a means found in the rules. When you have heard it, take heart
and carry it out."

Then Parasharya took Yudhishthira aside, and the master of words said this to
him, full of good sense. "The highest time for your good has come, best of the
Bharatas, by which Partha Dhananjaya will overcome your enemies in battle. Take
this knowledge that I give you, like success made flesh, called Pratismriti. I
tell it to you, who have come to me. When strong-armed Arjuna has it, he will
achieve his aim. Let him go to great Indra for weapons, and to Rudra, to Varuna,
to the lord of wealth and to the king of dharma, Pandava. He has the power to
see the gods, by his austerity and his courage. For he is a seer of great
splendour, the companion of Narayana, an ancient, eternal god, an eternal
portion of Vishnu. When the strong-armed one has taken weapons from Indra and
Rudra and the guardians of the world, he will do a great deed.

"And think of another forest to go to from this one, son of Kunti, one fit for
you to live in, lord of the earth. For a long stay in one place brings no joy.
It troubles the calm ascetics, and uses up the deer and the plants and herbs.
For you support many brahmins, masters of the Veda and its limbs."

When he had said this, the blessed lord, the wise Vyasa, son of Satyavati, who
knew the truth of yoga, spoke again. He taught the highest knowledge of yoga to
the Dharma King, who had come to him pure. Then he took leave of the son of
Kunti and vanished there and then.

The righteous, wise Yudhishthira held that sacred knowledge in his mind, keeping
himself in check, and practised it from time to time. Glad at Vyasa's words, he
went from the forest of Dvaitavana to the forest called Kamyaka, on the bank of
the Sarasvati. Brahmins rich in austerity followed him, great king, who knew the
teachings and the syllables, as the seers follow the king of the gods. When they
came to Kamyaka again, the great bulls of the Bharatas settled there with their
counsellors and followers. There the high-minded heroes lived for some time,
king, devoted to the science of the bow, listening to the Veda. They hunted
every day with clean arrows, seeking game, and made the proper offerings to the
fathers, the gods and the brahmins.

**Divergences.**

* **Bhima's loophole.** In the CE Bhima proposes counting the thirteen months they have spent in
  the forest as thirteen years, "as the putika plant stands for the soma", or buying freedom from
  the sin of breaking the vow by feeding an ox (3.36.31–33). Yudhishthira does not answer the
  proposal directly; he answers with the strength of the enemy.
* **Karna.** Yudhishthira's fear of Karna, who keeps him awake at night, is in the CE (3.37.16–18).

**Choices.**

* 3.36.2 *phenadharmā … phaladharmā*: "Men are like foam, and like fruit, that falls".
* 3.36.3 *sūcyevāñjanacūrṇasya*: "like eye-black taken away on a needle", a little at a time.
* 3.36.7 *aspaṣṭo bhūmivardhanaḥ*: "not plain to see, a burden on the earth". The sense is
  uncertain.
* 3.36.9 *hairaṇyau … bāhū*: "A king's arms become golden".
* 3.36.15 *mātā ca prativindhyataḥ*: "the mother of Prativindhya", Draupadi.
* 3.36.21 *pīṭhasarpavat*: "like a cripple", one who moves on a stool.
* 3.36.32 *pūtikān iva somasya*: the putika plant is a lawful substitute for soma.
* 3.37.7 *jalasaṃdhaḥ*: Jalasamdha, a son of Dhritarashtra. *śalaḥ*: Shala.
* 3.37.14 *rājapiṇḍaḥ*: "the king's bread", what they owe for his keep.
* 3.37.27 *pratismṛtim*: Pratismriti, "recollection", kept as the name of the knowledge.
* 3.37.28 *dharmarājam*: here Yama, the king of dharma, one of the guardians of the world.

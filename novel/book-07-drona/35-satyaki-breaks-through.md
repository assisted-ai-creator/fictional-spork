summary: Dhrishtadyumna and Vasudana rally the Pandavas to clear Satyaki's way, and Satyaki splits the Kaurava army. Drona blocks him and taunts him that his teacher Arjuna ran from battle like a coward. Satyaki answers that he goes at the king's command and must not lose time, and drives round Drona, choosing the gap between the Kaurava divisions and cutting through Karna's troops. Kritavarma stops him and wounds him through the chest. Satyaki kills Kritavarma's charioteer and goes on towards the Kambojas. Drona follows, leaving Kritavarma to hold the Pandavas. Dhritarashtra laments his army, chosen, trained and paid with care, and asks what his men did. Sanjaya tells him the ruin is his own fault, and that Vasudeva, seeing his partiality, brought on the war. Then he tells how Kritavarma alone held back the Pandavas, knocked Bhima reeling, cut Shikhandi's bow, made him faint, and put the Pandava army to flight.

# Satyaki Breaks Through

"When Yuyudhana had gone into your army to fight," said Sanjaya, "the King of
Dharma, great king, surrounded by his own division, went after Yuyudhana,
wanting to reach Drona's chariot. Then the son of the Panchala king, fierce in
battle, and King Vasudana shouted to the Pandava army, 'Come on, strike, run in
quickly, so that Satyaki, fierce in battle, can go through easily. Many great
warriors will try to beat him.' Saying this, they fell on your army at speed.
We, wanting to beat them, rushed at them there.

"Then there was a great noise around Yuyudhana's chariot. Your son's great host,
shaken by the Satvata, great king, was split into a hundred pieces. As it split,
Shini's grandson, the great warrior, crushed seven heroes, great bowmen, in the
front line. Frightened and crushed, wiped away by the long-armed man, the heroes
left the fight when they saw him, more than human.

"The earth was covered, best of men, with chariots, their axles smashed and
their boxes broken, with smashed and broken wheels and fallen banners. Drag
ropes, flags and golden helmets covered it, lord of the people, and arms smeared
with sandal paste and wearing armlets. Men's thighs covered it, like elephants'
trunks, like the coils of snakes. The ground shone, Bharata, with the fallen
faces of bull-eyed men, like the moon, with lovely earrings. It shone brightly
with elephants cut to pieces in many places, lying like mountains, as if strewn
with hills. Horses, their life gone, fell to the ground, wiped out by the
long-armed man, and still looked fine in their golden harness, adorned with nets
of pearls, and their bright breastplates.

"Having killed your armies of many kinds, the Satvata went into your host,
putting your army hard to flight. Then Satyaki wanted to go by the same road
Dhananjaya had taken, and Drona blocked him. When he came up to Bharadvaja's
son, angry Yuyudhana did not go past him, as the sea does not pass the shore.

"Drona held back the great warrior Yuyudhana in battle and pierced him with five
sharp arrows that split the vital places. Satyaki pierced Drona in battle, king,
with seven gold-feathered arrows, whetted on stone and winged with heron and
peacock feathers. Drona struck him with six arrows, and his horses and driver.
The great warrior Yuyudhana could not bear it. He gave a lion roar and pierced
Drona with ten arrows, and with six more, and with eight. Again Yuyudhana
pierced Drona with ten arrows, his charioteer with one, his four horses with
four and his banner with one arrow, sir. Drona quickly covered him, with his
horses and driver, his chariot and his banner, with swift arrows like swarms of
locusts. Yuyudhana too, quite calm, covered Drona with many swift arrows.

"Then Drona said, 'Your teacher left the battle like a coward. While I was
fighting, he left me and walked round me. You will not get away alive from me
today, Madhava, while I am fighting, unless you leave me in battle and go
quickly, like your teacher.'

"Satyaki said, 'I go along Dhananjaya's track at the King of Dharma's command.
Bless you, brahmin. I must not lose time.'

"Having said only this, Shaineya, avoiding the teacher, set off at once, king,
and said to his charioteer, 'Drona will try in every way to stop me. Go
carefully in the battle, Suta, and hear this, which matters most. That is the
army of the men of Avanti, bright and splendid. Next to it is this mighty army
of the south. Next to that is this great army of the Bahlikas, and set close to
the Bahlikas is Karna's great army. These armies are separate from each other,
charioteer, but they rely on each other and will not leave the battlefield. Go
through this gap. Urge the horses on gladly, and carry me there at a middle
pace, charioteer. Go where the Bahlikas are seen with weapons of every kind
raised, and many southerners led by the son of the Suta. Go where you see the
division crowded with elephants, horses and chariots and manned by foot soldiers
from many lands.'

"Having said this to his driver, avoiding the brahmin, he passed through where
Karna's great and fierce army was. Drona followed him in anger, scattering many
arrows at strong-armed Yuyudhana, who went on without turning back. Striking
Karna's great army with sharp arrows, Satyaki went into the endless Bharata
host.

"When Yuyudhana had gone in and the soldiers had fled, Kritavarma, who could not
bear it, surrounded Satyaki. As he came on, the mighty Satyaki quickly struck
him with six arrows, and his four horses with four. Then Satyaki pierced
Kritavarma between the breasts with sixteen smooth-jointed swift arrows. Struck
by the Satvata with many sharp-pointed arrows, great king, Kritavarma could not
bear it. He set on his bow a calf-tooth arrow like a snake or a fire, drew it to
his ear, king, and pierced Satyaki in the chest. The arrow split his armour and
his body and went into the ground, soaked in blood, feathers and all. Then
Kritavarma, master of the highest weapons, cut Satyaki's bow, string, arrow and
all, with many arrows, king. In great anger he pierced Satyaki, whose courage
was true, between the breasts with ten sharp arrows in the battle, king.

"With his bow broken, Satyaki, best of spearmen, struck Kritavarma's right arm
with a spear. Then the hero Satyaki took another very strong bow and quickly
loosed arrows by the hundred and the thousand. Satyaki covered Kritavarma
completely, chariot and all, on every side in the battle. Then with a broad
arrow he cut off the head of Hardikya's charioteer. The charioteer fell dead
from Hardikya's great chariot, and with their driver killed, the horses bolted.

"But the Bhoja, calm, took hold of the horses himself and stood with bow and
arrows in his hand, and the armies honoured him for it. He rested a moment, then
urged on his fine horses. Free of fear, he brought great fear to his enemies.
Satyaki went on from there, and Kritavarma rushed at Bhima.

"Yuyudhana, king of kings, came out of Drona's division and went quickly towards
the great host of the Kambojas. There many brave great warriors blocked him, and
Satyaki, whose courage was true, did not waver, king. Drona set the army in
order, left the burden to the Bhoja, and ran after Yuyudhana in the battle,
ready to fight him. As he ran after Yuyudhana, the greatest men of the Pandava
army blocked him in fury. But the Panchalas, led by Bhimasena, came up to
Hardikya, the best of chariot fighters, and lost heart, king, held back by the
brave Kritavarma as they pressed on. As they strove, a little dazed, he held
them all back on every side with a flood of arrows, and their horses were tired.
Held back by the Bhoja, the heroes, wanting to reach the Bhoja's division, stood
their ground like noble men, seeking great fame."

Dhritarashtra said, "My army had so many kinds of men, so well chosen and so
fine, drawn up so properly and so large, Sanjaya. We always honoured it, and it
always loved us. It was mature, wonderful to look at, and firm in courage from
the start. They were not too old and not boys, not thin and not too fat. They
were light, well built, long-winded, strong of body and healthy. They wore their
armour and carried many weapons. They were grounded in many arts of handling
weapons. They were skilled at mounting and jumping down, at moving forward and
leaping aside, at striking well, at advancing and retreating. They had been
tested many times on elephants, horses and chariots, and were paid their wages
after proper testing. No one was in my army through talk, or through favour, or
because of kinship. No one was there unasked, and no one was there unpaid.

"It was full of noble men of good family, content and well fed, not unruly,
honoured and rewarded, famous and spirited. It was guarded, my son, by ministers
and many other leading men of leading deeds, the best of men, like the guardians
of the world. It was guarded by many kings who wanted to please us, who came to
us of their own will with their armies and their followers. It was like the
great ocean, filled on every side by rivers. It was covered with chariots and
horses like wingless birds.

"It was a dreadful sea. Its fighters were water that never ran out, and its
mounts were its waves and swell. Slings, swords, maces, spears, arrows and
lances were its fish. It was crowded with banners and ornaments and studded with
strings of jewels, and its mounts, running, shook it like the force of the wind.
Drona was its deep underworld, Kritavarma its great pool, Jalasandha its great
crocodile and Karna the rising moon that stirred it up. The bull of the Pandavas
has broken that sea of an army and gone, and Yuyudhana with him in a single
chariot, Sanjaya. Now that the Left-Handed Archer and the Satvata, that fine
chariot fighter, have gone in, I see nothing left of my army.

"When they saw the two fearless, swift men pass, and saw the Sindhu king within
range of Gandiva's arrows, what did the Kurus do then, driven by Time? At that
dreadful time, when there was only one way to go, how did they act? I think the
Kurus, gathered together, have been swallowed by death, my son. Their courage in
battle is not seen now as it was. Krishna and the Pandava went in unharmed in
battle, and there is no one here to stop them, Sanjaya.

"Many fighters and great warriors were hired only after testing, some with wages
as was fitting, and others with kind words. There is no one in my army hired
without reason, my son. Each gets his wage and his food as his work deserves.
There was no fighter in my army, Sanjaya, paid with small gifts, my son, and no
man paid in base metal. I honoured them as far as I could, with gifts and honour
and seats, and so did my sons, my son, and my kin and relations. And yet, as
soon as they met him in battle, they were beaten by the Left-Handed Archer and
overrun by Shaineya. What is this but fate? The one who is guarded in battle,
Sanjaya, and those who guard him, go the same one road, the guarded with the
guards.

"When my son saw Arjuna in battle standing before the Saindhava, deeply
bewildered, what did he think must be done? When he saw Satyaki going into the
battle without fear, what did Duryodhana think was the thing to do in time? When
my men saw the two best of chariot fighters, beyond the reach of all weapons, go
into the army, what courage did they find for battle? When they see Krishna, the
Dasharha, set on Arjuna's cause, and the bull of the Shinis, I think my sons
grieve. When they see the army passed by the Satvata and by Arjuna, and the
Kurus running, I think my sons grieve. When they see the chariot fighters
fleeing, with no heart for beating the enemy and every heart for running, I
think my sons grieve. When they see the chariot boxes emptied by the Satvata and
by Arjuna, and the fighters killed, I think my sons grieve. When they see heroes
by the thousand, without horses, elephants or chariots, running wildly in the
battle, I think my sons grieve. When they see horses without their riders and
men without their chariots there, because of Satyaki and Partha, I think my sons
grieve. When they see the troops of foot soldiers running everywhere in the
battle, all of them without hope of victory, I think my sons grieve. When they
see how those two heroes, never beaten, passed Drona's division in a moment, I
think my sons grieve.

"I am utterly bewildered, my son, to hear that Krishna and Dhananjaya have gone
into my army, unharmed, with the Satvata. When the best chariot fighter of the
Shinis had gone into the army and passed the Bhoja's division, how did the
Kauravas fare? And how did the battle go there, when the Pandavas were held back
by Drona? Tell me, Sanjaya. Drona is strong and brave, skilled in weapons and
firm in courage. How did the Panchalas fight that great bowman in battle? They
are bound in feud with Drona, wanting the King of Dharma's victory, and
Bharadvaja's son, the great warrior, has made them his enemies. And tell me all
that Arjuna did to kill the Sindhu king, Sanjaya, for you tell it well."

Sanjaya said, "This disaster came from your own fault, bull of the Bharatas. Now
that it has come, hero, you should not grieve like a common man. Vasudeva knew
that you had no virtue, that you took your sons' side, that you were
double-minded about dharma and jealous of the Pandavas. He knew your many wild
laments, lord of men. So Vasudeva, who knows the truth of all the world, the
teacher and lord of all the world, brought on this great war of the Kurus. From
your own fault this vast destruction has come to you. Nothing good of yours is
seen, Bharata, at the beginning or in the middle or at the end. Defeat has its
root in you. So be steady now, know how the world is ruled, and hear how the
dreadful battle went, like the battle of the gods and the asuras.

"When Shaineya, whose courage was true, had gone into your army, the Parthas led
by Bhimasena went against your host. As they came on fast with their followers,
looking angry, the great warrior Kritavarma held back the Pandus in battle
alone. As the shore holds back the swollen sea, so Hardikya held back the
Pandava army in battle. People thought Hardikya's courage a wonder, for the
Parthas together could not get past him in battle.

"Then strong-armed Bhima pierced Kritavarma with three iron arrows and blew his
conch, making all the Pandavas glad. Sahadeva pierced Hardikya with twenty
arrows, the King of Dharma with five and Nakula with a hundred. The sons of
Draupadi struck Kritavarma with seventy-three arrows, Ghatotkacha with seven,
Dhrishtadyumna with three, and Virata, Drupada and Yajnaseni with five each.
Shikhandi too pierced Hardikya with five swift arrows, and again with twenty, as
if laughing.

"Then Kritavarma, king, pierced each of those great warriors on every side with
five arrows, and Bhima with seven. With care he brought Bhima's bow and banner
down from his chariot to the ground. Then the great warrior, in his haste and
anger, struck him in the chest with seventy sharp arrows as he stood with his
bow cut. Pierced deep by Hardikya's fine arrows, the mighty Bhima shook as he
stood in his chariot, like a mountain in an earthquake. Seeing Bhimasena like
that, those led by the King of Dharma loosed dreadful arrows and pressed
Kritavarma hard. They shut him in with a line of chariots, sir, and gladly
pierced him with arrows in the battle to protect the son of the Wind.

"Then mighty Bhimasena came round. He took an iron spear with a golden staff in
the battle and hurled it quickly from his chariot at Kritavarma's chariot.
Loosed from Bhima's arm like a snake that has shed its skin, very cruel, it
blazed around Kritavarma. As it came at him fast, as bright as the fire at the
end of an age, Hardikya cut it in two with two arrows. Cut, the spear, adorned
with gold, fell to the ground, lighting up the quarters, king, like a great
meteor fallen from the sky. Seeing the spear destroyed, Bhima grew very angry.

"Then Bhimasena, angry in battle, took another bow, swift and very loud, and
held Hardikya back. Then Bhima, of dreadful strength, struck him between the
breasts with five arrows, king, because of your bad counsel. The Bhoja, wounded
in every limb by Bhimasena, sir, shone on the battlefield like a red ashoka tree
in flower. Then, as if laughing, he struck Bhimasena hard with three arrows in
the battle, and pierced all the others in return, three arrows each for the
great warriors as they strove. They pierced him back with seven arrows each.

"Then the great warrior, in anger, as if laughing, cut Shikhandi's bow in the
battle with a razor arrow, Bharata. With his bow cut, Shikhandi quickly took up
in anger a sword and a bright shield with a hundred moons. Whirling the great
shield adorned with gold, he sent the sword at Kritavarma's chariot. The great
sword cut Kritavarma's bow, arrow and all, in the battle, and went into the
earth, king, like a star fallen from the sky. At that very moment the great
warriors quickly pierced Kritavarma deep with arrows in the battle.

"Then Hardikya, killer of enemy heroes, threw away that broken great bow and
took another, best of the Bharatas. He pierced the Pandavas in the battle with
three straight arrows each, and pierced Shikhandi with three and with five.
Famous Shikhandi took another bow and held back Hridika's son with swift arrows,
tipped like tortoise claws. Then Hridika's son, in anger, king, rushed fast in
the battle at the great warrior Yajnaseni. He was the cause of great-souled
Bhishma's death in battle, king, and Kritavarma, brave man, showed his strength
against him, like a tiger against an elephant.

"Like elephants of the quarters, like two blazing fires, the two tamers of
enemies met each other with hosts of arrows. They shook their fine bows, set
their arrows, and loosed them by the hundred, like two suns their rays.
Scorching each other with sharp arrows, the two great warriors, heroes like the
end of an age, shone like two suns. Then Kritavarma pierced the fierce great
warrior Yajnaseni with seventy-three arrows, and again with seven. Pierced deep
and in pain, he sank down in his chariot box, dropping his bow and arrow,
overcome by a faint. Seeing him fallen in his chariot, bull of the Bharatas,
your men honoured Hardikya and waved their clothes. Knowing that Shikhandi had
been hurt by Hardikya's arrows, his driver quickly carried the great warrior
away from the battle.

"Seeing Shikhandi sunk down in his chariot box, the Parthas quickly surrounded
Kritavarma with their chariots in the battle. There the great warrior Kritavarma
did the greatest wonder, for he alone held back the Parthas and their followers
in battle. Having beaten the Parthas, the great warrior Kritavarma beat the
Chedis, the Panchalas and the Srinjayas, and the mighty Kekayas. As Hardikya
struck them in the battle, the Pandavas ran this way and that and could not
stand firm. Having beaten the sons of Pandu led by Bhimasena in battle, Hardikya
stood in the battle like a fire without smoke. Driven off by Hardikya in battle,
hurt by his rains of arrows, the great warriors turned their backs."

**Divergences.**

* **"Vasudeva brought on this war."** Sanjaya says that Vasudeva, knowing Dhritarashtra's faults, "brought on this great war of the Kurus" (*vāsudevas tato yuddhaṃ kurūṇām akaron mahat*, 7.90.3).
* **Drona's taunt.** In the CE Drona says Arjuna "left the battle like a coward" (7.88.25). Satyaki does not answer the taunt. The longer rejoinder in \*604, that pupils follow the road their teacher took, is rejected.
* **Kritavarma's horses.** Satyaki "struck" (*ājaghāna*) Kritavarma's four horses (7.88.40). They are not killed, for they bolt when the driver falls (7.88.50).
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*598–\*603, \*605–\*626. \*601 has Satyaki kill many more kings; \*618 and \*621 lengthen Sanjaya's rebuke.

# Drona Takes Command

"When Duryodhana saw that tiger among men, Karna, standing in his chariot," said
Sanjaya, "he was glad, king, and said to him, 'This army, guarded by you, now
truly has a protector. But let us decide what is right and good to do.'

"'Tell us, tiger among men,' said Karna, 'for you are the wisest, king. The one
whose business it is sees what must be done better than anyone else. We all want
to hear what you say, lord of men. I do not think you would say anything
unwise.'

"'Bhishma led the army,' said Duryodhana. 'He had age and courage, and learning,
and all the virtues of a warrior. For ten days, Karna, that great and famous man
guarded us, killing my enemies and fighting well. He did what was hard to do,
and now he has gone to heaven. Whom do you think we should have as the leader of
the army after him? Without a leader an army does not last a moment in battle,
best of fighters, any more than a boat in water without a pilot. Like a boat
without a helmsman, like a chariot without a driver, an army without a commander
would drift wherever it liked. So look among all my great men and choose a fit
commander to follow Shantanu's son. Whoever you name as leader of the army in
battle, we will all make him so together, sir.'

"'All these great men, the best of men, deserve to be commander,' said Karna.
'There is no question of that. They have family, strength and knowledge, power,
courage and wisdom. They are grateful and modest, and they never turn back in
battle. But they cannot all be made leaders at once. Only one should be made
leader, the one with outstanding virtues. They all rival one another. If you
honour one of them, the rest will be offended and will not fight, Bharata, that
is plain. But this man is the teacher of all the warriors, old and revered.
Drona, the best of all who bear arms, is fit to be made commander. Who else
could be commander, when invincible Drona is here, the best of those who know
Brahman, who is as wise as Shukra and Angiras? And there is not one warrior
among all your kings, Bharata, who would not follow Drona when he goes into
battle. He is the best of leaders of armies, the best of those who bear arms,
and the best of the wise, and he is your teacher, king. So make the teacher
commander at once, Duryodhana, as the gods made Karttikeya commander when they
wanted to beat the demons in battle.'

"When he heard Karna's words, King Duryodhana spoke to Drona, who stood in the
middle of the army.

"'You excel in every virtue,' he said. 'You have the high rank of your class and
your birth, learning, age and wisdom. You have courage, skill and strength, the
knowledge of wealth and of policy, and victories. You have austerities and
gratitude. There is no other guardian of kings equal to you. So guard us all, as
Vasava guards the gods. With you to lead us, we mean to beat the enemy, best of
brahmins. Kapalin is the best of the Rudras, Pavaka of the Vasus, Kubera of the
yakshas and Vasava of the Maruts. Vasishtha is the best of brahmins and the sun
of lights. Dharma is the best of the ancestors and the lord of the waters of the
Adityas. The moon is the best of the stars and Ushanas of the sons of Diti. So
are you the best of the leaders of armies. Be our commander. Let these eleven
akshauhinis be under your command, blameless one. Draw up your array against the
enemy and kill them, as Indra killed the danavas. Go in front of us, as Pavaki
goes in front of the gods, and we will follow you in battle, as the herd follows
the bull. When Arjuna, fierce archer that he is, sees you in front of us,
twanging your divine bow, he will not stand against us. If you are commander,
tiger among men, I will surely beat Yudhishthira in battle, with his followers
and his kin.'

"When he had said this to Drona, the kings cried 'Victory!' and cheered your son
with a great lion's roar. The soldiers too, full of joy, with Duryodhana at
their head, praised the best of brahmins, hoping for great glory.

"'I know the Veda with its six limbs,' said Drona, 'and the science of wealth
that comes from Manu. I know the Traiyambaka arrow weapon and weapons of many
kinds. You want victory, and you have said that I have these virtues. I will try
to make them true. I will fight the Pandavas.'

"When he had agreed, king, your son made Drona commander with the rite laid down
in the texts. The kings, led by Duryodhana, consecrated Drona as commander, as
the gods led by Shakra once consecrated Skanda. When Drona had been made
commander, joy broke out, with the sound of music and the great shouts of men.
They honoured Drona in the proper way. There was the chanting of the blessing
for an auspicious day and the sound of good wishes. There were praises and songs
from the bards, the heralds and the minstrels, the brahmins' cries of 'Victory!'
and the dancing of fortunate women. And they thought the Pandavas already
beaten.

"When the son of Bharadvaja, the great chariot fighter, had been given the
command, he drew up the army, eager to fight, and marched out with your sons.
The Saindhava, the king of Kalinga and your son Vikarna took their place in
armour on the right flank. Shakuni guarded their wing, with fine Gandhara
horsemen who fought with bright lances. Kripa and Kritavarma, Chitrasena and
Vivimshati, with Duhshasana and the rest, guarded the left flank, ready for
battle. The Kambojas, led by Sudakshina, guarded their wing, riding very fast
horses, and the Shakas with the Yavanas. Behind the charioteer's son, with your
son in front, went the Madras, the Trigartas and the Ambashthas, and the peoples
of the west and the north. With them went the Shibis, the Shurasenas, the
Shudras and the Maladas, the Sauviras, the Kitavas, and the peoples of the east
and the south.

"Vaikartana Karna rode at the head of all the bowmen, cheering all the troops
and putting strength into the army. His great, tall, blazing banner, with its
emblem of an elephant's girth, shone like the sun, and it cheered his ranks.
When they saw Karna, no one gave a thought to the loss of Bhishma. All the kings
and the Kurus were free of grief. Many warriors gathered and talked together
happily. 'When the Pandavas see Karna in battle,' they said, 'they will not
stand. For Karna can beat the gods in battle, and Vasava with them, let alone
the sons of Pandu, whose courage and strength are nothing much. Strong- armed
Bhishma spared the Parthas in battle. But Karna will destroy them with his sharp
arrows, no doubt of it.' So they talked among themselves, lord of the people,
and they marched out gladly, honouring Radheya and praising him.

"Drona drew up our army in the cart array, king. The enemy's array was the
heron. Yudhishthira the King of Dharma drew it up with pleasure, Bharata. At the
front of their array stood the two bulls among men, Vishvaksena and Dhananjaya,
with the monkey banner raised high. The banner of Partha, whose power had no
measure, was the crest of all the armies and the mark of all bowmen, and it flew
in the path of the sun. It lit up the army of the great Pandava as the blazing
sun lights up the earth at the end of an age. Of archers, Arjuna is the best. Of
bows, the Gandiva is the best. Of beings, Vasudeva is the best. Of discuses,
Sudarshana is the best. Carrying these four splendours, the chariot with the
white horses stood in front of the enemy like the wheel of Time raised to
strike. So those two great men stood at the front of the two armies, Karna at
the head of yours and Dhananjaya at the head of the enemy's. Then, full of fury
and each wanting to kill the other, Karna and the Pandava stared at each other
across the field.

"When the great chariot fighter, Bharadvaja's son, set out at speed, the earth
shook with a terrible rumbling from inside it. A thick, harsh dust, stirred up
by the wind and looking like piles of silk, covered the sky and the sun. From a
cloudless sky it rained flesh, bones and blood. Vultures, hawks, cranes, herons
and crows by the thousand flew again and again over your army, king. Jackals
howled with terrible, frightening cries. They went round your army many times,
keeping it on their left, hungry to eat flesh and drink blood. A blazing meteor
fell in the battle with a crash and a quake, its tail spread out over
everything. A great halo, with lightning and thunder, formed round the sun,
king, as the commander set out. These and many other terrible portents appeared,
foretelling the end of the lives of heroes in battle.

"Then the battle began between the armies of the Kurus and the Pandavas, each
eager to kill the other, and the sound filled the world. The Pandavas and the
Kauravas, in a fury, struck at one another with sharp arrows, greedy for
victory. The great archer, in all his splendour, charged the great army of the
Pandavas, scattering hundreds of sharp arrows. When the Pandavas and the
Srinjayas saw Drona attacking, they met him with showers of arrows, each on his
own. Shaken and split open by Drona, the great army broke apart with the
Panchalas, as clouds are broken by the wind. Using many divine weapons in the
battle, Drona crushed the Pandavas and the Srinjayas in a moment. The Panchalas,
led by Dhrishtadyumna, trembled as Drona struck them, as the danavas trembled
when Vasava struck them.

"Then Yajnasena's son, a brave great chariot fighter who knew the divine
weapons, broke Drona's ranks in many places with a shower of arrows. But mighty
Drona met his showers of arrows with his own, one by one, held back the army,
and killed Kurus too. Then strong-armed Drona drew back, steadied his own ranks
in the battle, and rushed at Parshata. He loosed a great shower of arrows at
Parshata, as Maghavan in his fury loosed his arrows on the danavas. Shaken by
Drona's arrows, the Pandavas and the Srinjayas broke again and again, like
smaller beasts before a lion. Then mighty Drona swept round the Pandava army
like a whirling firebrand, king, and it was a marvel to see. His fine chariot
was like a city of the gandharvas in the sky, built by the rules of the science.
Its flags fluttered in the wind, it rattled, its horses leapt, and its banner
was as clear as crystal. Mounted on it, the tormentor of his enemies struck down
the enemy's army.

"When the Pandavas saw Drona striking down horses, charioteers, chariots and
elephants, they were shaken and could not hem him in. Then King Yudhishthira
said to Dhrishtadyumna and Dhananjaya, 'Let the one born from a pot be held back
on every side, with every effort.' Then Arjuna and Parshata with his followers,
and all the great chariot fighters, closed round Drona as he came on. The
Kekayas and Bhimasena, Subhadra's son and Ghatotkacha, Yudhishthira, the twins
and the Matsyas, and the sons of Drupada were there. So were the sons of
Draupadi, full of joy, Dhrishtaketu with Satyaki, angry Chekitana and the great
chariot fighter Yuyutsu. They and all the other kings who followed the Pandava,
king, did many deeds worthy of their families and their courage.

"When Bharadvaja's son saw his army held back by the Pandavas in the battle, he
rolled his eyes in anger and looked on. Then, in fierce anger, that fighter so
mad for battle scattered the Pandava ranks from his chariot, as the wind
scatters the clouds. Rushing here and there at chariots, horses, men and
elephants, old Drona ranged about like a madman, as if he were young. His
horses, red and swift as the wind, well bred and never tiring, were smeared with
blood, king, and looked splendid. When the Pandava's warriors saw him, firm in
his vows, coming on like Death in his anger, they fled in all directions. As
some fled and some turned back, some looked on and some stood their ground, a
terrible sound went up. It gave joy to the brave and deepened the fear of
cowards, and it filled the space between heaven and earth on every side.

"Then Drona shouted his name again in the battle, made himself terrible, and
scattered hundreds of arrows on the enemy. Mighty Drona, old as he was, killed
the Pandava's ranks like Time, as if he were a young man. The fierce, great
chariot fighter cut off heads and arms with their ornaments, emptied the
chariots, and shouted. At his shout of joy and the force of his arrows, lord,
the warriors in the battle trembled like cattle stung by the cold. From the
rumble of Drona's chariot, the rub of his bowstring and the twang of his bow, a
great sound rose into the sky. His arrows flew out from him by the thousand,
spread in every direction and fell on elephants, horses, chariots and foot
soldiers. The Panchalas and the Pandavas came up against Drona, whose bow was
fierce and fast and whose weapons blazed like fire. He sent them to Yama's house
with their chariots, elephants and horses, and soon he made the earth a mire of
blood. As Drona spread his great weapons and loosed arrows without pause, a net
of arrows could be seen spread in every direction. His banner could be seen
moving everywhere among the foot soldiers, chariots, horses and elephants, like
lightning among the clouds.

"Drona, whose spirit never failed, crushed five of the best of the Kekayas and
the king of Panchala with his arrows, and rode at Yudhishthira's ranks with his
bow and arrows in his hands. Bhimasena and Dhananjaya, Shini's grandson and
Drupada's son, Shaibya's son, the king of Kashi, and Shibi, shouting in their
joy, covered him with floods of arrows. Then the feathered arrows loosed from
Drona's bow, bright with gold, pierced the bodies of young elephants and horses
and went into the ground, their shafts smeared with blood. The ground was
covered with the hosts of warriors and chariots, and with elephants and horses
cut apart by arrows, as the sky is covered with black clouds. Drona, wanting the
good of your sons, crushed the leaders of the armies of Shini's grandson, Bhima
and Arjuna, and Shaibya and Abhimanyu, with the king of Kashi, and other heroes
in the battle.

"When he had done these and other deeds in battle, lord of the Kauravas, great
Drona scorched the worlds like the sun at the end of time, and then went from
here to heaven, king. So the brave warrior of the golden chariot, when he had
killed hundreds of thousands of the Pandavas' warriors in battle, was brought
down by Parshata. When he had killed more than an akshauhini of brave men who
never turned back, the steadfast man went to the highest state. The warrior of
the golden chariot had done a deed that was very hard to do, king, and then he
was killed by the Pandavas and the Panchalas, men of cruel deeds who bring no
good.

"Then, when the teacher was killed in battle, king, a cry went up from the
creatures in the sky and from the armies. The great cry of the creatures, 'Alas!
Shame!', rang through heaven and earth, the air, the quarters of the sky, the
waters and the spaces between. The gods and the ancestors, and his kinsmen who
had gone before, saw Bharadvaja's son, the great chariot fighter, lying killed
there. The Pandavas had won, and they roared like lions. The earth shook with
that great roar."

**Divergences.**

* **The summary.** At 7.7.24–36 Sanjaya jumps ahead and tells the king in brief that Drona, after killing more than an akshauhini, was brought down by Parshata, Dhrishtadyumna (7.7.30–31). Drona led the army for five days (1.2.26). The full story is told from 7.11 on, after Dhritarashtra's questions (7.8–7.10). This is the same frame as Book 6, where Sanjaya first reports Bhishma's fall (6.14) and then tells the battle from the start. The novel keeps the summary where the CE puts it.
* **"Crushed", not killed.** 7.7.24 says Drona "crushed" (*pramṛdya*) five Kekaya princes and the king of Panchala. Drupada is not killed here. Drona kills him and Virata much later, before the fifteenth dawn (7.161.34).
* **Karna's banner.** Its emblem is an elephant's girth, *hastikakṣyā* (7.6.9; 7.80.12).
* **Eleven akshauhinis.** Duryodhana gives Drona "these eleven akshauhinis" (7.5.28), the Kaurava strength at the start of the war (5.152.23; 1.2.24–25), after ten days of losses.
* Rejected lines not narrated: none in this range beyond those already listed for 7.1–7.4.

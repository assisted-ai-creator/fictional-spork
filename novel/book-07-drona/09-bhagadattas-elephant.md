# Bhagadatta's Elephant

"When the Pandavas had turned back in that way," said Dhritarashtra, "and each
had met his man, how did the Parthas fight, and my own swift warriors? What did
Arjuna do against the army of the sworn band? Or what did the sworn band do to
Partha, Sanjaya?"

"When the Pandavas had turned back and met their men," said Sanjaya, "your son
himself rode at Bhima with his ranks of elephants. Challenged by the king
himself, like an elephant by an elephant, like a bull by a bull, Bhima went at
the elephant ranks. Skilled in battle and strong in the arm, Partha broke the
ranks of elephants in no time, sir. Those elephants, like mountains, pouring rut
on every side, were turned back by Bhimasena's iron arrows and made to lose
their fury. As the wind scatters banks of cloud on every side, so the son of the
Wind scattered those ranks. As he loosed his arrows at the elephants, Bhima
shone like the risen sun loosing its rays on all the worlds. The elephants,
stitched through by Bhima's arrows in their hundreds, looked like the many
clouds in the sky pierced by the rays of the sun.

"As the son of the Wind was making havoc of the elephants, Duryodhana came up in
anger and pierced him with sharp arrows. In a moment Bhima, his eyes like blood,
wanting to destroy the king, pierced him with sharp arrows. His whole body stuck
with arrows, Duryodhana in his anger pierced the Pandava back, as if smiling,
with iron arrows bright as the sun's rays. With two broad arrows the Pandava
quickly cut down the jewelled elephant on his banner, set with gems, and his
bow.

"When he saw Duryodhana hard pressed by Bhima, sir, the king of the Angas came
up on an elephant to throw him into confusion. As the elephant came on,
trumpeting like a cloud, Bhimasena struck it hard between the bumps on its
forehead with an iron arrow. It split its body and sank into the ground, and the
elephant fell like a mountain struck by a thunderbolt. As the mleccha fell with
his elephant sinking under him, Wolf-belly, quick to act, cut off his head with
a broad arrow. When that hero fell, the army fled, its horses, elephants and
chariots in panic, trampling the foot soldiers.

"As all those ranks fled on every side, the king of Pragjyotisha rode at Bhima
on his elephant. It was the finest of elephants, the one with which Maghavan had
beaten the daityas and the danavas, and it charged at Bhima all at once. It came
at the Pandava with its ears, its feet and its curled trunk, its eyes rolling in
fury, as if to burn him up. Then a great cry went up from the whole army: 'Oh!
Bhima has been killed by the elephant!' Terrified by that cry, sir, the Pandava
army rushed at once to where Wolf-belly stood.

"Then King Yudhishthira, thinking Wolf-belly killed, surrounded Bhagadatta on
every side with the Panchalas. The best of chariot fighters surrounded him on
every side with their chariots and covered him with sharp arrows, by hundreds
and thousands. But the lord of the mountains fended off the arrows with his goad
and scattered the Pandavas and the Panchalas with his elephant. We saw a marvel
there, lord of the people: what old Bhagadatta did with his elephant in that
battle.

"Then the king of the Dasharnas rode at the king of Pragjyotisha on a swift
elephant in rut that charged from the side. There was a fight between those two
terrible elephants like the one long ago between two winged mountains covered in
trees. The elephant of the lord of Pragjyotisha closed with it, wheeled, and
split the Dasharna king's elephant in the side and brought it down. Then
Bhagadatta, with seven lances bright as the sun's rays, killed his enemy on his
elephant as his seat shifted.

"Then Yudhishthira came up to King Bhagadatta and surrounded him on every side
with a great host of chariots. On his elephant, surrounded on every side by
chariot fighters, he shone like a fire blazing on a mountain in the middle of a
forest. The elephant wheeled round inside the close ring of chariot fighters
with their fierce bows, as they scattered showers of arrows. Then the king of
Pragjyotisha, taking hold of his fine elephant, sent it at once at Yuyudhana's
chariot. The great elephant seized the chariot of Shini's grandson and hurled it
away with great force, but Yuyudhana got away. His charioteer lifted the great
Sindhu horses to their feet, came up to Satyaki and stood there, and he leapt
back into the chariot. Finding a gap, the elephant broke quickly out of the ring
of chariots and flung aside all the kings. The bulls among men, terrified by
that swift elephant, thought that one elephant in battle was a hundred. The
Pandavas were driven by Bhagadatta on his elephant as the danavas were driven by
the king of the gods on Airavata. As the Panchalas fled here and there, a great
and terrible noise arose from the elephants and horses.

"As Bhagadatta drove the Pandavas in the battle, Bhima in his anger came at the
king of Pragjyotisha again. The elephant drenched his horses with water blown
from its trunk as they charged, and frightened them, and they carried Partha
away. Then Akriti's son Ruchiparvan rode at him quickly, like Death in his
chariot, pouring a shower of arrows. The lord of the mountain, fine of joint,
sent Ruchiparvan to Yama's house with a smooth-jointed arrow.

"When that hero fell, Subhadra's son and Draupadi's sons, Chekitana,
Dhrishtaketu and Yuyutsu, attacked the elephant. Roaring terribly, wanting to
kill it, they poured streams of arrows on it, as clouds pour streams of rain.
Then the elephant, urged on by its skilled rider with heel, goad and toe,
charged fast, its trunk stretched out, its ears and eyes fixed. It trod on
Yuyutsu's horses and crushed his charioteer, and your son in his panic leapt
onto Subhadra's son's chariot. On his elephant, the king loosed his arrows at
his enemies and shone like the sun loosing its rays over the worlds. Arjuna's
son pierced him with twelve arrows, Yuyutsu with ten, and Draupadi's sons and
Dhrishtaketu with three each. Covered with the arrows his enemies had driven
into it, the elephant looked like a great cloud stitched through by the rays of
the sun. Tormented by the enemy's arrows, and sent on by its rider's skill and
effort, the elephant flung its enemies away to the left and the right. As a
herdsman in the forest drives his herds with a stick, so Bhagadatta rounded up
that army again and again. The cry of the fleeing Pandavas was loud, like the
cry of crows set on by a hawk.

"That king of elephants, struck by the fine goad, put terror into the enemy,
king, like a winged mountain long ago, like the stirred-up sea to a band of
merchants. Then the terrible noise made by elephants, chariots, horses and kings
fleeing in fear filled the earth, the air, the sky and the quarters of the sky,
king, in that battle. On that finest of elephants the king plunged deep into the
enemy army, as Virochana long ago plunged into the army of the gods, well
guarded as it was. The wind, friend of fire, blew hard, and the dust rose into
the sky and covered the soldiers again and again. And people thought that one
elephant was herds of elephants running everywhere.

"You ask me about Partha's deeds in battle," said Sanjaya. "Hear, great king,
what Partha did in the fighting. Bhagadatta was breaking the army. When Kunti's
son saw the dust rising and heard the trumpeting of the elephant, he said to
Krishna:

"'Madhusudana, it must be the king of Pragjyotisha charging on his elephant, and
that is surely his noise. He is not inferior to Indra in battle. He is the first
or the second master of fighting from an elephant on earth, I think. And his
fine elephant has never met its match in battle. It rises above all the noise of
battle, it has done great deeds and it never tires. It can bear the blows of
weapons and the touch of fire, blameless one. Clearly it will destroy the
Pandava army alone today. There is no one but the two of us who can hold it
back. Go quickly to where the lord of Pragjyotisha is. He is proud of his
friendship with Shakra, of the strength of his elephant and of his age. Today I
will send him as a welcome guest to the killer of Bala.'

"At Savyasachin's word, Krishna drove to where Bhagadatta was tearing the
Pandava army apart. As he went, the great chariot fighters of the sworn band
came up behind him, challenging him, fourteen thousand of them, ten thousand of
them Trigartas, king, and four thousand Vasudeva's own followers. When he saw
the army being torn apart by Bhagadatta, sir, and heard them challenging him,
his heart was split in two. 'Which deed would be better?' he thought. 'Should I
turn back from here, or go to Yudhishthira?' When Arjuna had thought it over,
best of the Kurus, his mind was more firmly set on killing the sworn band. So
the son of Vasava, with the fine monkey on his banner, turned back at once to
kill thousands of chariots in battle alone.

"For that was the plan of both Duryodhana and Karna, a way to kill Arjuna, and
that is why they divided his mind. The Pandava was torn in two, but he made the
plan of those great chariot fighters false with his one chariot. Then the great
chariot fighters of the sworn band loosed hundreds of thousands of
smooth-jointed arrows at Arjuna, king. Kunti's son Partha could not be seen, nor
Krishna Janardana, nor the horses, nor the chariot, king, so covered were they
with arrows. When Janardana grew faint and began to sweat, Partha killed most of
them with the thunderbolt weapon. Hands by the hundred were cut off, with their
arrows, bowstrings, guards and bows, and banners, horses, charioteers and
chariot fighters fell to the ground. Well-armed elephants, like trees, mountain
peaks and clouds, their riders killed, fell to the ground, struck by Partha's
arrows. Horses with their riders, their saddlecloths and reins torn, their
harness cut, lifeless, fell crushed by Partha's arrows. Men's arms fell, cut off
by the diademed one's broad arrows, with their spears, shields and swords like
claws, and their hammers and axes. Heads like the young sun, like lotuses and
like the moon, cut off by Arjuna's arrows, fell to the ground, sir. The army
blazed with the ornamented, life-eating arrows of every kind that angry Phalguna
loosed as he killed his enemies.

"As he churned the army, like an elephant in a lotus pond, the hosts of beings
praised Dhananjaya with cries of 'Well done! Well done!' When Madhava saw what
Partha had done, like Vasava, he was filled with wonder and clapped his hands in
praise. Then, having killed most of the sworn band who had stood their ground,
Partha urged Krishna on: 'Drive to Bhagadatta.'

"Then Krishna sent Partha's swift white horses, covered in gold, towards Drona's
ranks, where Partha wanted to go. As the best of the Kurus went to save his own
men, burned by Drona, Susharma, eager for battle, followed him with his
brothers. Then Jaya, with his white horses, said to unbeaten Krishna, 'Here is
Susharma with his brothers, challenging me, Achyuta. And here our army is being
torn apart in the north, killer of enemies. The sworn band have split my mind in
two today. Should I kill the sworn band, or protect our own men, hard pressed by
the enemy? You know my thought. Which of these would be the better deed?'

"When he said this, the Dasharha turned the chariot round towards where the lord
of the Trigartas was challenging the Pandava. Arjuna pierced Susharma with seven
swift arrows and cut his banner and his bow with two razor-headed ones. Quickly
Partha sent the brother of the lord of the Trigartas to Yama's abode with six
iron arrows, with his horses and his charioteer. Then Susharma hurled at Arjuna
an iron spear like a snake, and aimed a lance at Vasudeva. Arjuna cut the spear
with three arrows and the lance with three, bewildered Susharma with swarms of
arrows, and turned back.

"As he came on like Vasava, pouring fierce floods of arrows, king, no one in
your army held him back. Then Dhananjaya came on, killing the Kaurava great
chariot fighters with his arrows, like fire burning dry brush. They could not
bear the unbearable force of Kunti's wise son, as creatures cannot bear the
touch of fire. Wrapping the ranks in a shower of arrows, the Pandava came on
towards the king of Pragjyotisha, king, with the swoop of Suparna. Once Jishnu
bent that bow in battle to keep the Bharatas from harm and to make their enemies
weep. Now Arjuna drew the same bow, king, for the destruction of the kshatriyas,
because of your son, who played at the evil dice. Your army, great king, shaken
like that by Partha, broke apart like a boat striking a mountain.

"Then ten thousand bowmen turned back, heroes in a fury, their minds set on
victory or defeat in battle. Partha, a chariot fighter with no fear left in his
heart, beyond any law of distress, able to bear every burden in battle, took on
that heavy load. As a sixty-year-old elephant in rut, in its fury, would trample
a bed of reeds, so Partha in his strength trampled your army.

"When that army had been churned up, King Bhagadatta rode at once at Dhananjaya
on that elephant. The tiger among men met him fearlessly with his chariot. The
clash of chariot and elephant was wild. Bhagadatta and Dhananjaya ranged over
the field, on chariot and elephant fitted out by the rules of war. Then
Bhagadatta, like the mighty Indra from a cloud, rained a flood of arrows on
Dhananjaya from his elephant, which looked like a thunder cloud. But mighty
Vasava's son cut that shower of arrows with a shower of his own before it
reached him. Then the king of Pragjyotisha stopped that shower of arrows and
struck strong-armed Partha and Krishna with arrows, Bharata. Having covered them
both with a great net of arrows, he urged the elephant on to kill Achyuta and
Partha.

"When Janardana saw the elephant charging like Death in his fury, he quickly
wheeled the chariot round to the right. Though the great elephant had come
within reach, Dhananjaya, remembering dharma, did not want to send it to death
with its rider, now that it had turned away. But the elephant crushed elephants,
chariots and horses, sir, and sent them to the world of the dead. Then
Dhananjaya grew angry."

**Divergences.**

* **Bhagadatta's elephant.** The CE says it is "the finest of elephants, the one with which Maghavan had beaten the daityas and the danavas" (7.25.20). The name Supratika is not used here; it is given in the CE's list of contents (1.2.161).
* **"Bhima is dead!"** The Pandava army thinks Bhima killed by the elephant (7.25.22–24). He is not; the elephant's spray drives his horses off (7.25.43).
* **Arjuna torn in two.** The sworn band's challenge and Bhagadatta's attack split Arjuna's mind twice (7.26.12–14; 7.27.4–5). The CE says the division was Duryodhana's and Karna's plan to kill Arjuna (7.26.16–17).
* **Arjuna holds back.** When the elephant turns away, Arjuna, "remembering dharma", will not kill it and its rider from behind (7.27.29).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*203–\*209. \*206 has Krishna tell Arjuna to use the thunderbolt weapon; in the CE Arjuna uses it himself (7.26.20).

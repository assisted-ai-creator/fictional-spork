summary: Neither Krishna nor Arjuna sleeps that night, and terrible omens shake the world. Arjuna asks Krishna to comfort Subhadra and Abhimanyu's young wife. Krishna tells his sister that her son has gone where the good go, and that tomorrow she will hear that the boy's killer is dead. Subhadra laments her son, curses the strength of those who let him die, and prays that he may reach the worlds of every kind of good person. Draupadi and Uttara join her, and they fall senseless. Krishna revives them and comforts them. He makes Arjuna's bed of kusha grass ready with weapons round it, and Arjuna offers him the nightly offering meant for Shiva. In the Pandava camp no one sleeps, all wondering how Arjuna can keep his vow. At midnight Krishna wakes and tells his charioteer Daruka that Arjuna is dearer to him than wife, friends or kin, and that tomorrow the worlds will see what he will do for him. He orders Daruka to have his own chariot ready at dawn, with his weapons and his four horses, and to come when he hears Panchajanya.

# Subhadra's Grief

"That night Vasudeva and Dhananjaya could not sleep," said Sanjaya. "They were
stricken with grief and sorrow, and they sighed like two snakes. When the gods
with Vasava knew that Nara and Narayana were angry, they were troubled and
thought, 'What will come of this?' Harsh, dry winds blew, foretelling danger. A
bar with a headless body in it was seen across the sun. Dry thunderbolts fell,
with crashes and lightning. The earth shook with its mountains, woods and
forests. The oceans, homes of the sea monsters, heaved, great king, and the
rivers that run to the sea began to flow backwards. The lips of chariot
fighters, horses, men and elephants twitched, the lower and the upper, to
delight the eaters of flesh and swell the kingdom of Yama. The animals passed
dung and urine, and they wept. When they saw all those terrible, hair-raising
portents, and heard the fierce vow of mighty Savyasachin, all your soldiers were
shaken, bull of the Bharatas.

"Then strong-armed Pakashasana's son said to Krishna, 'Comfort your sister
Subhadra, with her son's wife. Take the grief from the daughter-in-law and her
mother-in-law, blameless one, Madhava. Comfort them with gentle, truthful words,
lord.'

"Then Vasudeva went to Arjuna's house, deeply unhappy, and comforted his sister,
stricken and grieving for her son. 'Do not grieve for the boy, woman of the
Vrishnis, you and your son's wife,' he said. 'This end is made by Time for all
living things, timid one. For a hero born in a noble family, and above all for a
kshatriya, this death is fitting for your son. Do not grieve. By good fortune
the hero, a great chariot fighter, equal to his father in courage, has reached
by the kshatriya's way the goal that heroes long for. He conquered many enemies
and sent them to death, and he has gone to the undying worlds of those who do
good, which yield every wish. Your son has reached the goal that the good seek
by austerity, chastity, learning and wisdom. You are the mother of a hero, the
wife of a hero, the daughter of a hero, the kinswoman of heroes. Do not grieve
for your son, good woman. He has gone to the highest goal.

"'And that mean Saindhava, the killer of a boy, will reap the fruit of this
arrogance, with his friends and kin. When the night is over, lovely woman, the
evil-doer will not escape Partha, even if he goes into Amaravati. Tomorrow you
will hear that his head has been carried off in battle, out of Samantapanchaka.
Do not grieve. Do not weep. Putting the kshatriya's dharma first, the hero has
gone the way of the good, the way we and others who live by the sword may reach
here. Broad-chested, strong-armed, never turning back, a scatterer of the finest
chariot fighters, your son has gone to heaven, lovely woman. Put away this
fever. Mighty, he followed his father and his mother's line. The great chariot
fighter, the hero, killed thousands of enemies and was killed. Comfort your
son's wife, queen. Do not grieve so much, kshatriya woman. Tomorrow you will
hear very dear news, daughter, and be free of grief. What Partha has vowed will
be so, and not otherwise. What your husband means to do can never come to
nothing. If men and snakes, pishachas, night-walkers, birds, gods and demons all
come to the Sindhu king in battle, he will not live past dawn, with them or
without them.'

"When she heard these words of great Keshava, Subhadra, stricken with grief for
her son, lamented in deep sorrow.

"'Oh, my son! Unlucky me! How could you go into battle and meet your death, my
child, you whose courage was equal to your father's? How is your face seen now,
my darling, dark as a blue lotus, with its fine teeth and lovely eyes, covered
with the dust of battle? Surely the creatures look at you, fallen, the hero who
never turned back. They see your fine head, neck, arms and shoulders, your broad
chest and flat belly, all your limbs lovely and full, and your fine eyes, and
you are covered with the wounds of weapons. Surely they look at you as at the
risen moon. Your bed was once spread with coverlets men envied. How do you lie
on the ground today, flung down, you who were used to comfort? The strong-armed
hero once had lovely women sitting by him. How do jackals sit by him today,
fallen in battle? He who was once praised by glad bards, heralds and minstrels
is waited on today by terrible hosts of flesh-eaters, howling.

"'With the Pandavas as your protectors, and the Vrishni heroes, lord, and the
Panchala heroes, who killed you as if you had no protector? I never had enough
of seeing you, my son, blameless one. Unlucky as I am, I will surely go to
Yama's abode today. When will I see your face again, my son, with its wide eyes
and lovely hair, its sweet words and sweet smell, without a wound? Shame on
Bhimasena's strength! Shame on Partha's archery! Shame on the courage of the
Vrishni heroes, and shame on the strength of the Panchalas! Shame on the
Kekayas, and on the Chedis, the Matsyas and the Srinjayas, who did not know that
you had fallen when you went into battle! Today I see the earth empty, as it
were, and without its light, as I do not see Abhimanyu, my eyes blind with
grief. Son of Vasudeva's sister, son of the bearer of the Gandiva, how shall I
see you, the hero, without your chariot, brought down by others?

"'Oh, my hero, seen and lost to me, like wealth in a dream! Oh, how fleeting is
human life, as unsteady as a bubble on the water! This young wife of yours,
flooded with grief for you, how shall I hold her up, like a cow that has lost
her calf? Oh, you have set out before your time, my little son, leaving me when
the fruit was due, me who longed so much to see you. Surely the way of Death is
very hard to know, even for the wise, since you were killed in battle like one
with no protector, with Keshava as your protector.

"'May you reach the goal of those who sacrifice and those who give, of brahmins
who have mastered themselves, who have kept chastity and bathed at holy fords.
May you reach the goal of the grateful and the generous, of those who serve
their teachers and those who give a thousand in gifts. Go by the way of brave
men who fight and do not turn back, who kill their enemies and are killed in
battle. Go by the good way of those who give a thousand cows and those who give
sacrifices, and of those who give a house that is wanted. Go, my little son, by
the way that sages of strict vows go by their chastity, and that women who have
one husband go. May you reach the everlasting way of kings through good conduct,
the way of those purified by the merits of the four stages of life, well kept.
Go, my little son, by the way of those who pity the wretched and always share
what they have, and of those who have turned away from slander. Go by the way of
those who keep vows and follow dharma, who serve their teachers and whose guests
never go away empty. Go by the way of those high-minded men who go to their own
wives in the right season and do not go to the wives of others. May you reach
the way of those who come to all beings with gentleness, free of jealousy, who
do not wound, and who are patient. Go, my little son, by the way of those who
have given up honey and meat, pride, deceit and lies, and who have given up
causing others pain. Go, my little son, by the way that good men go, modest,
knowing all the teachings, content with knowledge, their senses mastered.'

"As Subhadra lamented like this, miserable and worn with grief, the Panchala
princess came to her with Virata's daughter. They wept to their hearts' content
and lamented in great sorrow, and then, like madwomen, king, they fell senseless
to the ground. Krishna, deeply grieved himself, tended her in her grief. He
sprinkled her with water, comforted her, and spoke words for her good. Then the
lotus-eyed one said this to his sister, who was weeping, half senseless,
stricken and trembling.

"'Subhadra, do not grieve for your son. Panchala princess, comfort Uttara.
Abhimanyu, the bull of the kshatriyas, has gone to a famous goal. May all the
other men of our family, lovely-faced one, reach the goal of famous Abhimanyu.
May we and our friends do such a deed as your son, the great chariot fighter,
did alone today.'

"When he had comforted his sister, and Draupadi and Uttara, the strong-armed
tamer of enemies went back to Partha's side. Then, lord, Krishna took leave of
the kings and of his kin and went into the inner rooms, king, and the others
went to their own quarters.

"Then the lotus-eyed lord went into Arjuna's matchless house. He touched water,
and on an auspicious altar-ground he spread a lovely bed of darbha grass the
colour of cat's-eye gems. Then he adorned that bed with garlands in the proper
way, with parched grain and lucky perfumes, and surrounded it with the finest
weapons. When Partha had touched water, well-trained attendants showed him the
regular nightly offering to the Three-eyed God. Then Partha, glad at heart,
adorned Madhava with perfumes and garlands and offered him that nightly
offering. Govinda smiled and said to Phalguna, 'Sleep, Partha, and bless you. I
am going for your good.'

"When he had posted doorkeepers and guards, men with weapons in their hands,
glorious Krishna went into his own camp, followed by Daruka. He lay down on his
bright bed, thinking of all that had to be done.

"No one slept that night in the Pandavas' camp, lord of the people. Everyone lay
awake. 'The great bearer of the Gandiva, overcome with grief for his son, has
vowed in haste to kill the Sindhu king. How can strong-armed Vasava's son, the
killer of enemy heroes, make his vow come true?' they thought. 'The great
Pandava has undertaken a hard thing. Stricken with grief for his son, he has
made a great vow. And his brothers are brave, and his forces many, and
Dhritarashtra's son has told him everything. May Dhananjaya kill the Saindhava
in battle and come back. May Arjuna beat the hosts of his enemies and fulfil his
vow. If he does not kill the Sindhu king, he will enter the fire. Partha
Dhananjaya cannot make this false. How will the son of Dharma be king if Arjuna
dies? For the Pandava has laid his whole victory on him. If we have any merit,
if we have given anything, offered anything, by the fruit of all of it may
Savyasachin beat his enemies.' As they talked like this, hoping for victory,
lord, the night passed with great difficulty, king.

"In the middle of that night Janardana woke. Remembering Partha's vow, he spoke
to Daruka.

"'Arjuna, in his pain, his kinsman killed, has vowed, "Tomorrow I will kill
Jayadratha," Daruka. When Duryodhana hears of it, he will take counsel with his
counsellors so that Partha does not kill Jayadratha in battle. All those
akshauhinis will guard Jayadratha, and Drona with his son, who has gone to the
far shore of the rules of every weapon. Not even the thousand-eyed one, the only
hero, the crusher of daityas and danavas, would dare to kill him in battle when
Drona guards him. So tomorrow I will act so that Kunti's son Arjuna kills
Jayadratha before the sun has set.

"'Not wives, not friends, not kinsmen, not relations, no one is dearer to me
than Kunti's son Arjuna. I could not look at this world without Arjuna even for
a moment, Daruka, and it will not be so. Tomorrow I will kill the enemies'
armies, with their horses, chariots and elephants, with Karna and Suyodhana, for
Arjuna's sake. Tomorrow let the three worlds see my courage in the great battle,
Daruka, as I strike out for Dhananjaya's sake. Tomorrow thousands of kings and
hundreds of princes, with their horses, elephants and chariots, will flee in
battle, Daruka. Tomorrow you will see that army of kings crushed by the discus,
brought down by me in my anger in battle for the Pandava's sake. Tomorrow all
the worlds, with the gods and gandharvas, pishachas, snakes and rakshasas, will
know me as Savyasachin's friend. Whoever hates him hates me. Whoever follows him
follows me. Let it be fixed in your mind that Arjuna is half of my body.

"'Before this night turns to dawn, make my fine chariot ready as the rules
require. Take it, charioteer, with a mind held to your vow, and put in it the
divine mace Kaumodaki, the spear, the discus, my bow and arrows, and all the
gear. Make a place in the chariot for my banner, for brave Vinata's son, who
graces the chariot in battle. Yoke the fine horses, Balahaka, Meghapushpa,
Sainya and Sugriva, adorned with divine nets of gold like the sun and fire, made
by Vishvakarman, and my umbrella. Stand ready in your armour, Daruka. When you
hear the terrible sound of Panchajanya, sounded on the rishabha note, come to me
quickly. In a single day, Daruka, I will take away my cousin's anger and all his
sorrows. By every means I will strive so that Bibhatsu kills Jayadratha in
battle while the sons of Dhritarashtra look on. Whoever Bibhatsu strives to
kill, charioteer, I am sure he will surely win.'

"'Victory is surely his,' said Daruka. 'How could there be defeat for him, tiger
among men, when you have become his charioteer? I will do all this as you
command me. This night will dawn well for Vijaya's victory.'"

**Divergences.**

* **The head carried off.** Krishna tells Subhadra she will hear that Jayadratha's head "has been carried off in battle, out of Samantapanchaka" (7.54.20). How it happens is told when Jayadratha dies.
* **The offering.** Attendants show Arjuna "the regular nightly offering to the Three-eyed God"; Arjuna adorns Krishna with perfumes and garlands and offers the nightly offering to him (7.56.3–4).
* **"Arjuna is half of my body."** Krishna says this to Daruka at midnight (7.56.30), with "Whoever hates him hates me. Whoever follows him follows me."
* **Krishna's chariot.** Krishna has Daruka make his own chariot ready, with his weapons and his four horses, and tells him to come when he hears Panchajanya (7.56.31–36). Whether Krishna needs it is told later.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*393–\*409. \*395 is a verse on what kind of son each class of woman bears; \*397 has Subhadra call her son to her breast; \*403 has Krishna prepare a yoga for Arjuna.

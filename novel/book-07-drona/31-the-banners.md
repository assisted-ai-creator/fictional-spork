summary: Eight great warriors, Bhurishravas, Shala, Karna, Vrishasena, Jayadratha, Kripa, Shalya and Ashvatthama, surround Arjuna with Duryodhana to guard Jayadratha. The conches of Krishna and Arjuna drown out all the rest, and arrows fly on both sides. At Dhritarashtra's request Sanjaya describes the banners: Arjuna's monkey, Ashvatthama's lion's tail, Karna's golden elephant rope, Kripa's bull, Vrishasena's peacock, Shalya's golden furrow, Jayadratha's silver boar, Bhurishravas's sacrificial post, Shala's silver elephant and Duryodhana's jewelled snake. Meanwhile, back at the array, the Panchalas attack Drona in the afternoon and each side pairs off. Yudhishthira hurls a spear at Drona, and Drona burns it up with the Brahma weapon, which Yudhishthira calms with his own. Their maces meet in the air. Drona kills Yudhishthira's horses and cuts his bow and banner, and the king stands on the ground with his arms raised, unarmed. As Drona rushes at him and the Pandavas cry that the king is taken, Yudhishthira climbs onto Sahadeva's chariot and escapes.

# The Banners

"Your men saw the best of the Vrishnis and Andhakas and the best of the Kurus,"
said Sanjaya, "and they hurried first to kill them, and Vijaya did the same to
his enemies. They came in great chariots, loud-sounding, bright with gold,
covered in tiger skins, lighting up every quarter like blazing fires. Their bows
were backed with gold and hard to look at, lord of the earth, and hummed with
sounds beyond compare, like angry snakes. They were Bhurishravas, Shala, Karna,
Vrishasena and Jayadratha, Kripa and the king of the Madras, and Drona's son,
the best of chariot fighters. Those eight great warriors seemed to drink the sky
with their horses, and lit up the ten quarters with their tiger skins and golden
moons. Armoured and in great fury, in chariots that thundered like masses of
cloud, they covered every quarter, and Partha, with sharp arrows.

"Bright horses from Kuluta drew those great warriors, and shone as they went
fast, lighting up the ten quarters. There were fine horses of good breed, very
swift, from many lands, from the mountains, from the river country and from
Sindhu. The best of the Kuru fighters, king, wanting to save your son, rushed
quickly at Dhananjaya's chariot from every side. Those best of men took up their
great conches and blew them, filling heaven, king, and the earth with its seas.

"And Vasudeva and Dhananjaya blew their conches, the best of all beings, with
the best of all conches on earth: Kunti's son blew Devadatta and Keshava
Panchajanya. The sound of Devadatta, blown by Dhananjaya, covered the earth, the
sky and the quarters. So did Panchajanya, blown by Vasudeva. It rose above all
other sounds and filled heaven and earth.

"While that dreadful din went on, which frightened cowards and made brave men
glad, the war drums were beaten, king of kings, with cymbals, kettledrums and
tabors, many of them. The great warriors of many lands, famed as great warriors,
who wanted Duryodhana's good, the greatest bowmen, guarding their own armies,
could not bear that sound and grew angry. The heroes, great warriors, blew their
great conches, to answer what Keshava and Arjuna had done. Stirred by the sound
of the conches, your army, with its chariots, elephants and horses, was troubled
and seemed sick, lord. The sky, ringing with the conches blown by the heroes,
was deeply troubled, as if it were ringing with thunder. That very great sound,
king, filled every quarter and terrified the army, as if it had been gathered at
the end of an age.

"Then Duryodhana and those eight kings, great warriors, surrounded the Pandava
to protect Jayadratha. Drona's son struck Vasudeva with seventy-three arrows,
and Arjuna with three broad arrows, and his banner and horses with five. With
Janardana wounded, Arjuna, in the greatest anger, struck him with six hundred
arrows. The mighty man pierced Karna with twelve and Vrishasena with three, and
cut Shalya's bow, with an arrow on it, at the grip.

"Shalya took another bow and pierced the Pandava. Bhurishravas pierced him with
three gold-feathered arrows whetted on stone, Karna with thirty-two, Vrishasena
with five, Jayadratha with seventy-three, Kripa with ten and the Madra king with
ten, in the battle. Then Drona's son covered Partha with sixty arrows, Vasudeva
with seventy and Partha again with five.

"But the tiger among men, with his white horses and Krishna as his charioteer,
laughed, and struck them all in return, showing the speed of his hands. He
pierced Karna with twelve arrows and Vrishasena with three, and cut Shalya's bow
at the grip in the battle. He pierced Somadatta's son with three arrows and
Shalya with ten, and Drona's son with eight sharp arrows like flames of fire. He
struck Gautama with twenty-five arrows, the Saindhava with a hundred and Drona's
son again with seventy. Bhurishravas, in anger, cut Hari's whip and struck
Arjuna with seventy-three arrows. Then the man with the white horses, in anger,
quickly drove back those enemies with hundreds of sharp arrows, as a great wind
drives back clouds."

Dhritarashtra said, "Tell me about the banners, Sanjaya, of many shapes, shining
with great beauty, those of the Parthas and those of my men."

Sanjaya said, "Hear about the banners of many shapes of those great-souled men.
Learn from me their forms, their colours and their names. On the chariots of
those leading warriors, king of kings, banners of many kinds were seen, like
blazing fires. They were of gold, with golden crests and golden garlands, like
golden peaks of the great golden mountain. Those banners were surrounded on
every side by flags of many colours and shades, and shone all round. The flags,
tossed by the wind, looked like dancing girls on a stage. The flags, bull of the
Bharatas, with the colours of the rainbow, fluttered and made the chariot
fighters' great chariots beautiful.

"In the battle we saw Dhananjaya's banner, marked with a lion-tailed monkey with
a fierce face, and it was dreadful. That best of monkeys, king, on the banner of
the Gandiva bowman, adorned with flags, terrified that army.

"We saw the top of the banner of Drona's son, Bharata, a lion's tail, bright as
the young sun. It was golden and tossed by the wind, bright as Shakra's banner,
raised high as Drona's son's sign, and it gladdened the Kaurava lords.

"On the banner of Adhiratha's son was a golden elephant's girth rope. In battle,
great king, it seemed to fill the sky. Karna's banner in the battle, with its
flag and its golden garland, seemed to dance on his chariot, tossed by the wind.

"The teacher of the Pandavas, the famous brahmin Kripa Gautama, had a well-made
bull. With that bull the great warrior shone, king, as the chariot of the
destroyer of Tripura shines with its bull.

"Vrishasena had a golden peacock set with jewels and gems. It stood as if about
to cry out, and made the front of the army beautiful. With that peacock the
great-souled man's chariot shone, king of kings, as Skanda's shines with its
bright peacock.

"At the top of the banner of Shalya, king of the Madras, we saw a golden furrow,
like a flame of fire, lovely and beyond compare. That furrow shone on his
chariot, sir, like a ploughed furrow in its beauty, where every seed has
sprouted.

"On the Sindhu king's banner shone a silver boar at the top, bright as the sun
before it reddens, adorned with a net of gold. With that silver banner
Jayadratha shone, as Pushan shone long ago in the battle of the gods and asuras.

"Somadatta's son, wise and given to sacrifice, had a sacrificial post for his
banner. It shone like the sun, and the moon was seen there too. That golden post
of Somadatta's son shone, king, like the post set up at the Rajasuya, the best
of sacrifices.

"Shala had a great silver elephant for his banner, great king, adorned with
golden peacocks with bright bodies. That banner made your army beautiful, bull
of the Bharatas, as the great white elephant makes the army of the king of the
gods beautiful.

"The king's banner was a snake made of jewels, covered with gold, jingling with
a hundred little bells, shining on his fine bright chariot. With that great
banner, king, lord of the people, your son, the bull of the Kurus, shone
brightly in the battle.

"These nine great banners stood high in your host and lit up the army, like the
suns at the end of an age. The tenth was Arjuna's, the one great monkey, and
with it Arjuna blazed like the Himalaya with a fire.

"Then the great warriors, scorchers of enemies, quickly took up their bright,
shining, very great bows against Arjuna. And Partha, destroyer of enemies, of
heavenly deeds, drew his bow Gandiva, king, because of your bad counsel. Through
your offence many men have been killed in battle, called from every quarter,
with their horses, chariots and elephants. They clashed together, roaring at
each other, those led by Duryodhana and the bull of the Pandavas. There Kunti's
son, with Krishna as his charioteer, did the greatest wonder, for he took on
many alone, without fear. The strong-armed man shone, stretching his bow
Gandiva, wanting to beat those tigers among men and to kill Jayadratha. There
Arjuna, great king, scorcher of enemies, made your fighters vanish with the
thousands of arrows he loosed. Then all those great warriors, tigers among men,
made Partha vanish in the battle, with floods of arrows from every side. When
Arjuna, bull of the Kurus, was surrounded by those lions among men, a great
noise rose from his army."

Dhritarashtra said, "When Arjuna had reached the Saindhava, Sanjaya, what did
the Panchalas do, held back by Bharadvaja's son, against the Kurus?"

Sanjaya said, "In the afternoon, great king, in that battle that made the hair
stand on end, the Panchalas and the Kurus played a game of dice with Drona as
the stake. The Panchalas, glad at heart, wanting to kill Drona, roared and
rained arrows on him, sir. Then a wild and wonderful battle began between the
Panchalas and the Kurus, dreadful, like the battle of the gods and the asuras.
All the Panchalas, with the Pandavas, came up to Drona's chariot and showed
their great weapons, wanting to break that division. Chariot fighters in their
chariots came right up to Drona's chariot, shaking it, at a middle pace.

"Brihatkshatra, the great warrior of the Kekayas, came at him, sowing sharp
arrows like the thunderbolt of great Indra. Kshemadhurti, of great fame, quickly
went out against him, loosing sharp arrows by the hundred and the thousand.
Dhrishtaketu, bull of the Chedis, driven on by great strength, rushed quickly at
Drona, like great Indra at Shambara. As he came on, like Death the Ender with
his mouth open, the great bowman Viradhanva hurried out to meet him.

"Mighty Drona held back Yudhishthira, great king, as he stood with his division,
eager to win. Your son Vikarna, bold as he was, lord, went at bold Nakula,
skilled in battle, as he came on. Durmukha, scourge of enemies, covered Sahadeva
as he came on with many thousands of swift arrows. Vyaghradatta held back
Satyaki, the tiger among men, making him shake again and again with very sharp,
keen arrows. Somadatta's son held back the sons of Draupadi, tigers among men,
the best of chariot fighters, angry and loosing fine arrows. The great warrior,
the son of Rishyashringa, dreadful to see, blocked the dreadful Bhimasena as he
came on in anger. The battle between the man and the rakshasa was like the
battle of Rama and Ravana long ago, king.

"Then Yudhishthira struck Drona in every vital place with ninety smooth-jointed
arrows, best of the Bharatas. Angered by Kunti's famous son, Drona struck him
between the breasts with twenty-five arrows. And again, while all the bowmen
watched, Drona piled twenty more arrows on him, with his horses, his charioteer
and his banner. The righteous Pandava held back the arrows Drona loosed with a
rain of arrows, showing the speed of his hands. Then Drona, in great anger in
the battle, the bowman cut the great-souled King of Dharma's bow at a stroke.
The great warrior quickly filled him on every side with many thousands of arrows
as he stood there with his bow cut.

"Seeing the king vanish under the arrows of Bharadvaja's son, all beings thought
Yudhishthira had been killed. Some thought he had been turned back, and said,
'The king has been taken by the famous brahmin,' king of kings.

"The King of Dharma, Yudhishthira, in the greatest danger, threw away the bow
Bharadvaja's son had cut in the battle, and took up another, a heavenly bow,
strong and very swift. The hero cut all those arrows Drona had loosed, by the
thousand, in the battle, and it was a wonder. Having cut the arrows, the king,
his eyes red with anger, seized a spear in the battle that could split even
mountains. It had a golden staff and was very dreadful, with eight bells, a
thing of terror. Lifting it, the mighty man roared with joy, loudly, Bharata, as
if to terrify all beings with his roar. Seeing the spear raised by the King of
Dharma in the battle, all beings cried out at once, 'May Drona be safe!'

"Loosed from the king's arm, like a snake that has shed its skin, lighting up
the sky and the quarters, the spear reached Drona like a snake with a blazing
mouth. Seeing it come at him fast, lord of the people, Drona, the best of those
who know weapons, called up the Brahma weapon. The weapon turned that dreadful
spear to ash and went quickly at the famous Pandava's chariot. Then King
Yudhishthira, in his great wisdom, calmed Drona's weapon as it rose with the
Brahma weapon itself, Bharata. He pierced Drona in the battle with five
smooth-jointed arrows and cut his great bow with a sharp razor arrow.

"Drona, crusher of Kshatriyas, threw away the cut bow and hurled a mace at
Dharma's son, sir. Seeing the mace come at him fast, Yudhishthira, scorcher of
enemies, in his anger took a mace too and hurled it. The two maces, loosed at
once, met each other, struck out fire from the clash, and fell to the ground
together.

"Then Drona, in great anger, killed the King of Dharma's horses with four sharp,
keen, fine arrows, sir. With one arrow he cut his bow, which was like Indra's
banner, and with one he cut his banner, and he struck the Pandava with three.
King Yudhishthira jumped down quickly from his chariot, its horses killed, and
stood with his arms raised, without a weapon, bull of the Bharatas.

"Seeing him without a chariot, and above all without a weapon, Drona dazed his
enemies and all the armies, lord. Loosing swarms of sharp arrows, quick of hand
and firm in his vow, he rushed at the king like a fierce lion at a deer. When
the Pandavas saw him rushed at by Drona, killer of enemies, a sudden cry went up
from them, 'Alas! Alas!' 'The king is taken! The king is taken by Bharadvaja's
son!' The cry went up, very loud, sir, from every side of the Pandava army. Then
Kunti's son, King Yudhishthira, quickly climbed onto Sahadeva's chariot and got
away with its swift horses."

**Divergences.**

* **How many.** The CE names eight great warriors at 7.79.4 and says "Duryodhana and those eight kings" surrounded Arjuna (7.79.21). It then counts nine banners in the Kaurava army (7.80.28), one each for the eight and one for Duryodhana, and Arjuna's as the tenth.
* **Shalya's bow.** Arjuna cuts Shalya's bow at the grip twice (7.79.24, 29). The CE tells the exchange twice over.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*554–\*556.

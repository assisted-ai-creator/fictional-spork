# Arjuna and Duryodhana

"Your men seemed to melt to the marrow with fear, king," said Sanjaya, "when
they saw Vasudeva and Dhananjaya pass them. Then all of them, great-souled men
who knew shame, driven by their courage, turned back in fury and stood firm
against Dhananjaya. Those who went against the Pandava in battle, full of anger
and rage, never came back, even now, as rivers do not come back from the sea.
The bad turned away, like unbelievers from the Vedas, and heading for hell, they
fell into sin.

"Passing the mass of chariots, the two bulls among men came out free, like the
sun and the moon freed from Rahu's mouth. Like two fish that have torn a great
net, the two Krishnas were seen to have torn through the net of the army, and
their fever was gone. Free of the crush of weapons, out of Drona's division, so
hard to break, the two great-souled men looked like two suns risen at the end of
time. Free of the crowd of weapons, out of the press of arms, the great-souled
pair, who crowded their enemies, were seen clear. Like two fish escaped from a
sea monster's mouth, whose touch burns like fire, they churned up the army, as
two sea monsters churn the sea.

"While they were in Drona's division, your men and your sons had made up their
minds: 'These two will not get past Drona.' When they saw that the two, of great
splendour, had passed Drona's division, great king, they had no more hope for
the Sindhu king's life. Your sons had had a strong hope, king, lord, that the
two Krishnas would not get free of Drona and Hardikya. The scorchers of enemies
had made that hope vain, and had crossed Drona's division and the Bhoja's, great
king, so hard to cross. Seeing them through, like two blazing fires, they lost
hope and no longer hoped that the Sindhu king would live.

"The two Krishnas, fearless, who made others afraid, spoke to each other about
the killing of Jayadratha: 'Six great warriors among Dhritarashtra's men have
put him in the middle. Now that he is in sight, the Saindhava will not get away
from us. Even if Shakra with the hosts of the gods were his guard in battle, we
would still kill him.' So the strong-armed Krishnas spoke to each other then,
looking for the Sindhu king, and your sons heard it.

"Like two thirsty elephants that have crossed a desert and drunk water and are
refreshed, so were the two tamers of enemies. They looked like men who have
crossed mountains full of tigers, lions and elephants, strong-armed men who had
passed beyond death and old age. When your men saw the two had got free, they
thought, 'Look at the colour of their faces,' and cried out on every side. They
had got free of Drona, who was like a poisonous snake and a blazing fire, and of
the other kings, and shone like two suns. The tamers of enemies were free of
Drona's division, which was like the sea, and they looked as glad as men who
have crossed the ocean. Free of the great flood of weapons guarded by Drona and
Hardikya, they shone in battle like Indra and Agni. The two Krishnas, bleeding
from wounds made by Bharadvaja's sharp arrows and covered in them, shone like
two mountains covered with karnikara flowers.

"Drona was a pool full of crocodiles. His spears were its poisonous snakes, his
iron arrows its fierce sea monsters, the best of Kshatriyas its water. The twang
of bowstrings on arm guards was its thunder, and maces and swords its lightning.
They had got free of it, of the cloud of Drona's weapons, like the sun and moon
coming out of darkness. It was as if they had swum with their arms across the
rivers that run to the sea, the six whose sixth is the Sindhu. It was the end of
the hot season, and the rivers were full and crowded with great crocodiles. So
all beings thought of the two Krishnas, great bowmen famous in the world, amazed
at the strength of Drona's weapons.

"Looking at Jayadratha close by, wanting to kill him, they stood like two tigers
waiting to catch a ruru deer at a watering place. Your fighters, great king,
thought, 'Look at the colour of their faces. Jayadratha is as good as dead.'
Red-eyed, strong-armed, ready, Krishna and the Pandava saw the Sindhu king and
roared again and again with joy. Shauri with the reins in his hand and Partha
with his bow shone with a brightness like the sun and fire. Free of Drona's
division, they felt only joy, seeing the Saindhava near, like two hawks seeing
meat. Seeing the Saindhava as if right in front of them, they rushed at him in
anger, as fast as two hawks swooping on meat.

"When your son saw that Hrishikesha and Dhananjaya had passed, bold as he was,
he went to protect the Sindhu king. King Duryodhana, wearing the armour Drona
had bound on him, went into battle in a single chariot, lord, a man who knew the
training of horses. Passing Krishna and Partha, the great bowmen, your son
turned to face the lotus-eyed one in front of them, lord of men. Then in all the
armies, as your son passed Dhananjaya, the instruments played gladly. There were
lion roars mixed with the sound of conches and drums, when they saw Duryodhana
standing there in front of the two Krishnas. Jayadratha's guards, who were like
fire, were delighted in the battle to see your son, lord.

"When Krishna saw that Duryodhana had passed them with his followers, king, he
said these timely words to Arjuna.

"Vasudeva said, 'Look, Dhananjaya, at Suyodhana, who has got past us. I think
this is his moment of danger. There is no chariot fighter like him. He strikes
from far off, a great bowman, skilled in weapons, fierce in battle, with strong
weapons, a wonderful fighter. Dhritarashtra's son is very strong. He was brought
up in the greatest comfort and honoured by great warriors. He is always able,
Partha, and he has always hated the Pandavas. I think the time has come for you
to fight him, sinless one. Here the dice have come to you, for victory or the
other thing.

"'Here, Partha, let loose the poison of anger you have gathered so long. This
great warrior is the root of all the Pandavas' wrongs. He has come within your
reach. See how you are rewarded. How else would a king who wants the kingdom
come to battle with you? By good fortune he has come within the range of your
arrows. Act so that he gives up his life, Dhananjaya. Blinded by the pride of
power, he has never known suffering, and he does not know your strength in
battle, bull among men. The three worlds, Partha, with gods, asuras and men,
could not beat you in battle. How much less Suyodhana alone? By good fortune he
has come close to your chariot, Partha. Kill him, strong-armed one, as Purandara
killed Vritra.

"'He has always striven to do you harm, sinless one, and he cheated the King of
Dharma at the dice with his tricks. This evil-minded man has done many very
cruel things to you, giver of honour, who never did wrong. He is ignoble, always
base, a man who does as he likes. Set a noble purpose in your mind for battle,
Partha, and kill him without thinking twice. Keep in your heart the kingdom he
took by fraud, the life in the forest and Krishna's suffering, Pandava, and
fight. By good fortune he is moving within range of your arrows, and by good
fortune he is striving in front of you to block your task. By good fortune he
knows he must fight you in battle, and by good fortune all the wishes you have
wished will bear fruit, Partha. So kill Dhritarashtra's son in battle, Partha,
the lowest of his line, as Indra long ago killed Jambha in the battle of the
gods and asuras. When you have killed him, let this army, without its lord, be
broken. This is the closing bath of the feud. Cut the root of the wicked.'

"Partha said to him, 'So be it. This is the thing I have to do. Leave everything
else and go to where Suyodhana is. He has enjoyed our kingdom for so long
without a thorn in his side. May I show my courage in battle and cut off his
head. May I be able to go to the end of that suffering Krishna did not deserve,
Madhava, when she was dragged by the hair.'

"So the two Krishnas spoke, and gladly drove their fine white horses in the
battle, wanting to reach the king. When your son came close to them, bull of the
Bharatas, he showed no fear, though great fear had come, sir. All the Kshatriyas
there honoured him for going out to meet Arjuna and Hrishikesha without a second
thought. Then a great shout went up from all your army, lord of the people, when
they saw the king in the battle. As that dreadful roar of the people went up,
your son made nothing of his enemy and held him back.

"Held back by your son the bowman, Kunti's son grew angrier still, and so did
that scorcher of enemies against him. The kings, dreadful to see, stood all
round and watched Duryodhana and Dhananjaya in their fury. When your son saw
Partha in his fury, and Vasudeva, sir, he seemed to laugh, and eager to fight,
he challenged them. Then the Dasharha and the Pandava Dhananjaya, full of joy,
gave a great shout and blew their fine conches. When they saw how glad the two
were, all the Kauravas gave up hope for your son's life. All the Kurus fell into
the deepest grief, and thought your son had been poured as an offering into the
mouth of the fire. Seeing Krishna and the Pandava so glad, the fighters,
stricken with fear, cried, 'The king is killed! The king is killed!'

"Hearing the noise of the people, Duryodhana said, 'Put your fear away. I will
send the two Krishnas to death.' Having said this to all his soldiers, the king,
looking for victory, turned to Partha. He spoke in his fury: 'Partha, whatever
weapons you have learned, heavenly and human, show them on me quickly, if you
were born of Pandu. Whatever strength and courage you have, and Keshava too, use
them on me quickly. Let us see your manhood. They talk of the deeds you have
done behind our backs, which have won the honour of your masters. Show them
here.'

"Saying this to Arjuna, the king pierced him with three very swift arrows that
went through the vital places, and his four horses with four. He pierced
Vasudeva between the breasts with ten, and cut his whip with a broad arrow and
brought it down to the ground. Partha, quite calm, quickly pierced him with
fourteen bright-feathered arrows whetted on stone, and they slid off his armour.
Seeing them fail, he sent another nine and five sharp arrows, and they slid off
his armour too.

"Seeing the twenty-eight arrows shot and wasted, Krishna, killer of enemy
heroes, said to Arjuna, 'I see what I have never seen before, as if stones were
creeping along. The feathered arrows you send, Partha, do nothing. Is there
still the same life in Gandiva, bull of the Bharatas? Is your grip what it was,
and the strength of your arms? Or has the last hour, fixed by fate, come today
for you and for this enemy? Tell me, I am asking you. I am amazed, Partha, to
see these arrows of yours falling on Duryodhana's chariot in battle and doing
nothing. Your dreadful arrows, like the thunderbolt and lightning, which split
the bodies of enemies, do nothing, Partha. What mockery is this today?'

"Arjuna said, 'Drona has put this into the mind of Dhritarashtra's son, Krishna.
This wearing of armour was laid down at the end of the weapons' teaching. The
three worlds could be hidden inside this armour, Krishna. Drona alone knows it,
and I learned it from that best of men. This armour cannot be broken by arrows
in any way, not even by the thunderbolt, Govinda, in the hands of Maghavan
himself in battle. You know it too, Krishna, so why do you bewilder me? What has
happened in the three worlds, Keshava, and what is happening, and what will
happen, all of it is known to you. No one knows it as you do, slayer of Madhu.

"'This Duryodhana, Krishna, stands in the battle as if he had no fear, wearing
the armour Drona laid on him. But he does not know what must be done with it,
Madhava. He wears this well-fitted armour like a woman. See the strength of my
arms and my bow, Janardana. I will beat the Kauravya, though he is guarded by
armour. The lord of the gods gave this shining armour to Angiras. The lord of
the gods gave the armour, with its binding, to me in turn. Even if the armour is
divine, or made by Brahma himself, it will not guard this evil-minded man today
from being struck by my arrows.'

"Saying this, Arjuna charged his arrows with a spell and drew them back. As he
drew them, sitting in the middle of the bow, Drona's son cut them with the
weapon that destroys all weapons. Seeing his arrows cut down from far off by
that speaker of the Vedas, the man with the white horses told Keshava in
amazement, 'I cannot use this weapon twice, Janardana. The weapon would kill me
myself. But see my strength today.'

"Then Duryodhana pierced the two Krishnas in battle, king, with nine
smooth-jointed arrows like poisonous snakes, and rained arrows on Krishna and
the Pandava again and again. At that great rain of arrows your men were glad,
and they sounded their instruments and gave lion roars.

"Then Partha, in anger, licked the corners of his mouth in the battle. He could
see no part of the man that was not guarded by the armour. With sharp, well-shot
arrows like Death the Ender, he made his horses lifeless, and both the drivers
who guard the wheels behind. The mighty Left-Handed Archer cut his fine bow and
his hand guard, and began to break his chariot to pieces. Then, when Duryodhana
had no chariot, Arjuna pierced him with two sharp arrows in the palms of both
his hands.

"Seeing him in such distress, hurt by Dhananjaya's arrows, the greatest bowmen
rushed up to save him. They surrounded Dhananjaya with many thousands of
chariots, with elephants and horses in their armour and with angry floods of
foot soldiers. Then Arjuna and Govinda could not be seen, nor their chariot,
covered by the great rain of weapons and the floods of men. Then Arjuna, by the
power of his weapons, cut down that host. Chariots and elephants fell there in
hundreds, crippled. The dead and the dying held back the fine chariot, and it
stood stuck fast, hemmed in for a krosha all round.

"Then the Vrishni hero said quickly to Arjuna, 'Twang your bow as hard as you
can, and I will blow my conch.' Then Arjuna twanged Gandiva mightily and killed
the enemy with a great rain of arrows and the slap of the string on his arm
guard. Keshava blew Panchajanya mightily, with a high note, the lashes of his
eyes clotted with dust, his face running with sweat. At the sound of that conch
and the twang of the bow, weak men and strong alike fell to the ground.

"Free of them, the chariot shone like a cloud driven by the wind. Then
Jayadratha's guards and their followers were shaken. Seeing Partha come on at
once, the Saindhava's guards made noises of many kinds, shaking the earth. The
great-souled men made the fierce sounds of arrows, mixed with the blare of
conches, and gave lion roars. Hearing that dreadful noise from your men,
Vasudeva and Dhananjaya blew their conches. That great sound filled this earth,
lord of the people, with its mountains, its seas and islands and the world
below. The sound spread through all ten directions, best of the Bharatas, and
echoed there in the armies of the Kurus and the Pandavas.

"When your chariot fighters there saw Krishna and Dhananjaya, the great warriors
were filled with the greatest fury and hurried on. Then your men, seeing the two
blessed Krishnas in their armour, rushed at them in anger, and it was a wonder."

**Divergences.**

* **Duryodhana's hands.** In the CE Arjuna pierces Duryodhana "in the palms of both his hands" (*hastatalayor ubhayoḥ*, 7.78.29). Just before this Arjuna "could see no part of the man that was not guarded by the armour" (7.78.26). The arrows "between the nails and the flesh" that make him flee in pain are from the rejected \*552.
* **The armour's line.** Arjuna says Indra gave the armour to Angiras and then to Arjuna himself (7.78.19). \*550, which puts Brihaspati and Purandara in between, is rejected. Drona's account in 7.69 runs from Shiva to Indra, Angiras, Brihaspati, Agniveshya and Drona.
* **Arjuna's spell.** The CE does not name the weapon Arjuna calls on at 7.78.21. The "Manava weapon" is from the rejected \*551.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*547–\*549, \*553.

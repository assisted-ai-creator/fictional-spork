# Bhima Goes In

"In the afternoon, great king," said Sanjaya, "the battle between Drona and the
Somakas began again, with a roar like thunder. The hero among men stood in his
chariot with the red horses, and went at the Pandavas in the battle, steady and
at a middle pace. The great bowman, the mighty son of the fine pot, was set on
doing what would please you and help you. With sharp arrows with bright
feathers, king, mighty Bharadvaja's son played in the battle, as if he were
picking out the best of the fighters one by one.

"Brihatkshatra, a great warrior of the Kekayas, came at him, the eldest of five
heroic brothers, harsh in battle. He loosed sharp arrows and covered the teacher
thickly, like a great cloud pouring rain on Gandhamadana. In great anger Drona
sent seventeen arrows at him, great king, with golden feathers, whetted on
stone. As they flew from Drona's bow, dreadful and like venomous snakes,
Brihatkshatra, full of joy, cut each one of them in the battle with ten arrows.
The best of the brahmins saw how quick he was and laughed, and sent eight
smooth-jointed arrows at him. Seeing them fly fast from Drona's bow, he held
them off with as many sharp, strong arrows of his own. Your army was amazed,
great king, to see Brihatkshatra do a deed so hard to do.

"Then Drona, great in austerity, meaning to outdo the Kekaya, brought out the
divine Brahma weapon in the battle, great king. The strong-armed Kekaya did not
waver. He held off that weapon of Drona's in the battle, king, with the Brahma
weapon itself. Having beaten back Bharadvaja's weapon in the battle, he pierced
the brahmin with sixty arrows with golden feathers, whetted on stone.

"Drona, best of men, struck him with an iron arrow. It split his armour and went
into the ground. As a black snake, let loose, goes into an anthill, best of
kings, so the arrow went through the Kekaya in the battle and into the earth.
Pierced deep by Drona, who knew weapons, great king, the Kekaya was filled with
great anger. Rolling his fine eyes, he pierced Drona with seventy arrows with
golden feathers, whetted on stone, and pierced his charioteer in the arms and
chest with a broad arrow. Drona, pierced many times by Brihatkshatra, sir,
loosed sharp arrows at the Kekaya's chariot. When he had left the great warrior
Brihatkshatra dazed, he loosed one sharp arrow at the Kekaya, Bharata. It
pierced him deep between the breasts, great king, and the tiger among men fell
from his chariot with his heart split.

"When Brihatkshatra, the great warrior of the Kekayas, was killed, king,
Shishupala's son grew very angry and said to his driver, 'Charioteer, go to
where Drona stands in his armour, killing all the Kekayas and the army of the
Panchalas.' Hearing his words, the charioteer brought the best of chariot
fighters to Drona with swift Kamboja horses. Dhrishtaketu, the bull of the
Chedis, rising up in his great strength, rushed at Drona like a moth at a fire.
He pierced Drona with sixty arrows, with his horses, chariot and banner, and
again with other sharp arrows, like a man goading a sleeping tiger.

"As the strong king strove in the battle, Drona cut his bow in the middle with a
sharp razor-headed arrow. Shishupala's son, the great warrior, took another bow
and pierced Drona again with very sharp, strong arrows. Mighty Drona killed his
horses and his charioteer, and then pierced him with twenty-five arrows. Without
chariot or bow, the Chedi king, in great anger, hurled a mace at Bharadvaja's
chariot in the battle. It came at him fast, dreadful to see and frightening,
heavy, as hard as stone and adorned with gold. Bharadvaja's son brought it down
with many thousands of arrows. Struck down by Bharadvaja's son, the mace fell to
the ground in its red garlands and cloth, like a star falling from the sky.

"When he saw the mace destroyed, Dhrishtaketu could not bear it. He quickly
loosed a lance, and a spear shining with gold. Drona cut the lance with three
arrows in the great battle, and the mighty man, sure of hand, quickly cut the
spear. Then mighty Bharadvaja's son loosed a sharp arrow in the battle to kill
him, as he wanted to kill Drona. The arrow split the armour and the heart of
that man of boundless energy, and went into the ground like a swan into a lotus
pool. As a hungry blue jay swallows an insect, king, so brave Drona swallowed
Dhrishtaketu in the great battle.

"When the Chedi king was killed, his son, who knew the highest weapons, took his
father's place in the fight, overcome with rage. Drona laughed and sent him too
to the house of Yama with his arrows, as a strong tiger in a great forest kills
a fawn.

"As the Pandava forces were being worn away like this, Bharata, the hero, the
son of Jarasandha, came at Drona himself. He covered Drona with sharp arrows,
great king, and quickly hid him from sight, as a cloud hides the sun. Drona, the
crusher of kshatriyas, saw how quick he was, and quickly loosed arrows by
hundreds and thousands. He covered the best of chariot fighters as he stood in
his chariot, and killed the son of Jarasandha in the battle while all the bowmen
looked on. Whoever came near Drona, Drona took, as Death takes all living things
when their time comes.

"Then Drona, the great bowman, called out his name in the battle and confounded
the Pandavas with many thousands of arrows. Drona's arrows, marked with his
name, feathered with gold and whetted on stone, killed men, elephants and horses
on every side of the field. Struck down by Drona, like the great asuras by
Shakra, the Panchalas trembled like cows hurt by the cold. As their armies were
cut down by Drona, bull of the Bharatas, a dreadful wail rose from the Pandavas.
Confused by Bharadvaja's rain of arrows in the battle, the great warriors of the
Panchalas were like men whose thighs are seized and will not move.

"Then the Chedis, great king, and the Srinjayas and the Somakas ran at
Bharadvaja's son, full of joy and eager to fight. 'Kill Drona! Kill Drona!' they
cried as they went at him. Those tigers among men strove with all their power
against Drona in his great splendour, wanting to send him to the house of Yama.
But Bharadvaja's son sent those striving heroes to Yama with his arrows, and
above all the chiefs of the Chedis.

"As the Chedi chiefs were being worn away, Bharata, the Panchalas trembled,
tormented by Drona's arrows. When they saw Drona's deeds, sir, they called out
to Bhimasena and to Dhrishtadyumna's chariot. 'Surely the brahmin has practised
great austerities, hard to practise. That is how he burns up the bulls among
kshatriyas with his courage in battle. War is the dharma of a kshatriya, and
austerity is the highest dharma of a brahmin. An ascetic who has mastered his
learning could burn things up with a look. Many bulls among kshatriyas have gone
into Drona's weapon, which is like fire to touch, dreadful and hard to cross,
and there they were burnt, Bharata. With all his strength, all his will and all
his courage, Drona in his great splendour is killing our armies and confusing
every living thing.'

"Hearing them, Kshatradharman stood firm, and with a crescent-headed arrow he
cut Drona's bow with its arrow. Drona, the crusher of kshatriyas, grew angrier.
He took another bow, bright and swifter, and fitted to it a sharp arrow, clean,
strong and heavy-striking. The mighty teacher drew it to his ear and loosed it.
It killed Kshatradharman and went into the ground, and he fell from his chariot
to the earth with his heart split. The armies trembled when Dhrishtadyumna's son
was killed.

"Then the great warrior Chekitana attacked Drona. He pierced Drona in the chest
with ten arrows, his charioteer with four and his four horses with four. The
teacher pierced his right arm with sixteen arrows, his banner with sixteen and
his driver with seven. When his charioteer was killed, his horses, covered with
Bharadvaja's arrows, bolted with the chariot in the battle, sir. When they saw
Chekitana's chariot running away with its driver killed, great fear came over
the Panchalas and the Pandavas. Drona shone brightly, sir, as he drove off on
every side the brave Chedis, Panchalas and Srinjayas gathered in the battle.

"Drona was dark, and white-haired to the ears, and more than eighty years old.
Old as he was, he ranged the battle like a boy of sixteen. As he moved about the
field without fear, great king, his enemies thought the killer of his enemies
was Indra with the thunderbolt in his hand.

"Then wise King Drupada spoke, great king. 'This greedy man kills kshatriyas as
a tiger kills small deer. That wicked, evil-minded Duryodhana will come to hard
worlds, for it is by his greed that the bulls among kshatriyas have been killed
in battle. They lie on the ground by hundreds, cut down like bulls, their limbs
drenched in blood, food for dogs and jackals.' When he had said this, great
king, Drupada, lord of an army, put the sons of Pritha at his head and rushed at
Drona.

"The Pandava arrays were being churned up here and there, and the sons of
Pritha, the Panchalas and the Somakas went far off. That wild battle went on,
making the hair stand on end, a fierce destruction of the world, Bharata, like
the end of an age. Drona was mighty in the battle and roared again and again,
the Panchalas were worn away and the Pandavas were being killed.

"King Yudhishthira, the king of dharma, could see no refuge anywhere, lord of
kings. He thought, 'How will this end?' He looked in every direction, hoping to
see the Left-Handed Archer, but he saw neither Partha nor Madhava. He could not
see the tiger among men with the great monkey on his banner, and he could not
hear the twang of the Gandiva, and his senses were troubled. He could not see
Satyaki either, the finest chariot fighter of the Vrishnis. Yudhishthira, the
king of dharma, was wrapped in worry, and not seeing those two bulls among men,
he found no peace.

"Afraid of what the world would say, the famous, strong-armed king of dharma
thought about Shaineya's chariot. 'I sent Shaineya, Satyaki, true as he is, who
takes away his friends' fear, in the battle on Phalguna's track. What was one
worry has become two for me today. I must find out about Satyaki, and about the
Pandava Dhananjaya. I have sent Satyaki after the Pandava. Whom shall I send in
the battle after the Satvata? If I take pains to look for my brother, and do not
look for Yuyudhana, the world will blame me. "Yudhishthira, the king of dharma,
looked for his brother, and left Satyaki of the Vrishnis to his fate, whose
courage is true." Afraid of what the world would say, I will send Vrikodara, the
son of Pritha, on great-souled Madhava's track.

"'As I love Arjuna, the killer of his enemies, so I love the Vrishni hero, the
Satvata, who is wild in battle. I gave Shaineya's son a heavy load to carry. For
his friend's sake, and to honour me, the mighty man went into the Bharata army
as a crocodile goes into the sea. That noise we hear is surely the brave men who
never turn back, fighting the wise hero of the Vrishnis. I have thought it over
many ways, and it is right and it is time. It is best that Bhimasena the bowman,
the Pandava, should go where the two great warriors have gone.

"'There is nothing on earth that Bhima cannot bear. With the strength of his own
arms he can easily stand against all the bowmen of the earth in battle, however
careful they are. It was on the strength of the great man's arms that we all
came back from living in the forest, and we have never been beaten in battle.
When Bhimasena the Pandava goes from here to the Satvata, the Satvata and
Phalguna will have a protector in battle. Surely the Satvata and Phalguna are
not to be grieved over in battle. Vasudeva guards them, and they themselves are
skilled in weapons. But I must do something to end my own grief. So I will send
Bhima after the Satvata. Then I think I will have done what I should for
Satyaki.'

"Having made up his mind, king, Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, said to his
charioteer, 'Take me to Bhima.' Hearing the king of dharma, the charioteer, who
knew horses, brought the chariot, worked with gold, to Bhima's side. When he
reached Bhimasena, the king thought of what the time called for, and despair
came over him, and he said a great deal there.

"'The one who beat the gods, the gandharvas and the daityas with a single
chariot, your younger brother, I see no sign of him.'

"Then Bhimasena said to the king of dharma, seeing him like that, 'I have never
seen or heard of such despair in you. In the past, when we were torn apart by
sorrow, you were our refuge. Get up, get up, king of kings. Tell me, what shall
I do for you? There is nothing I cannot do or would not do, giver of honour.
Command me, best of the Kurus, and do not let your mind turn to grief.'

"With his eyes full of tears, breathing like a black snake, his face withered,
the king said to Bhimasena, 'We hear the sound of the conch Panchajanya, blown
by famous Vasudeva in his anger. Surely your brother Dhananjaya lies dead today.
With him killed, surely Janardana is fighting. The Pandavas live on the strength
of that brave man. We go to him in danger as the gods go to Indra of the
thousand eyes. That hero went into the Bharata army, wanting to reach the
Saindhava. We know that he went, Bhima, but not that he came back. He is dark
and young, Gudakesha, handsome and strong-armed, with his broad chest and great
shoulders, and the stride of a rutting elephant. He has eyes like a chakora
bird's, red at the corners, and he makes his enemies' sorrow grow.

"'This is what I grieve for, bless you, tamer of enemies, for Arjuna's sake,
strong-armed one, and for the Satvata's. It grows like a fire fed with butter
again and again. I see no sign of him, and so I despair. Know that tiger among
men, and the Satvata, the great warrior, who followed the great warrior, your
younger brother. I cannot see the strong-armed man, and so I despair. That is
why I think Krishna must be fighting in battle, skilled in war as he is, the
mighty one on whose strength the Pandavas live.

"'Go there, son of Kunti, where Dhananjaya has gone, and mighty Satyaki, if you
think you should do what I say, knower of dharma. I am your elder brother. You
should not think so much of Arjuna as of Satyaki. He wanted to do what I asked,
Partha, and went on the track of the Left-Handed Archer, a hard and dreadful
road that men with unformed minds could never travel.'

"Bhimasena said, 'The chariot that once carried Brahma, Ishana, Indra and
Varuna, the two Krishnas have gone in it. They have nothing to fear. But I bear
your command on my head, and I am going. Do not grieve. When I have met those
tigers among men, I will send you word.'"

Sanjaya said, "With that, mighty Bhima set out, after giving Yudhishthira into
the care of Dhrishtadyumna and his friends, again and again. And mighty
Bhimasena said this to Dhrishtadyumna.

"'You know, strong-armed one, how Drona, the great warrior, is using every means
to capture the king of dharma. There is no duty for me in going, Parshata, like
the duty to guard the king. That is what is urgent for us. But Partha has told
me to go, and I cannot answer him back. I will go where the Saindhava stands,
about to die. The word of the king of dharma must be kept without question. So
today, in the battle, guard Yudhishthira, the son of Pritha, with all your care.
In battle this is the highest of all the things to be done.'

"Dhrishtadyumna said to Vrikodara, great king, 'Go as you wish, strong-armed
Partha, without a second thought. Drona will never capture the king of dharma in
battle without killing Dhrishtadyumna first.'

"Then the Pandava gave the king into Dhrishtadyumna's care, saluted his elder
brother and teacher, and set out for where Phalguna was. The king of dharma
embraced the son of Kunti, Bharata, kissed his head and spoke blessings over
him. Strong-armed Bhimasena, the best of chariot fighters, wore armour and fine
earrings, armlets and body armour, and carried arrows. His armour was black iron
worked with gold, very costly, and it shone on him like a cloud with lightning
clinging to a mountain. He was well wrapped in cloths of yellow, red, black and
white, and with his neck guard he shone like a cloud with Indra's bow in it.

"As Bhimasena set out against your army to fight, the dreadful sound of
Panchajanya rose again, lord of the people. When he heard that great and
dreadful sound, which frightens the three worlds, the strong-armed son of Dharma
spoke to Bhima again. 'That king of conches, born in the water, blown hard by
the Vrishni hero, is filling the earth and the sky with sound. Surely the
Left-Handed Archer has met with a great disaster, and the bearer of the discus
and the mace is fighting all the Kurus. Surely noble Kunti, and Draupadi and
Subhadra with their kin, are seeing an evil sign today. So, Bhima, go quickly to
where Dhananjaya is. All the directions, Partha, and the quarters between them,
seem to reel before me in my longing to see Dhananjaya, and for the Satvata's
sake.' And again he said to Bhimasena, 'Go, go!'

"Sent on so urgently by his brother, Bhima, the brother who always did what
pleased his brother, beat his drum and blew his conch again and again. He roared
a lion's roar and drew his bowstring again and again, and showing how terrible
he was, he went at his enemies all at once. His swift, tame horses carried him,
the finest, prancing, as fast as the mind and the wind, steered by Vishoka.
Breaking and smashing, drawing his bowstring with his hand, dragging and
scattering, the son of Pritha churned up the front of the army. As the
strong-armed man went, the brave Panchalas and Somakas followed behind him, as
the gods follow Maghavan.

"His brothers surrounded him with their armies, great king: Duhshala,
Chitrasena, Kundabhedin and Vivimshati, Durmukha, Duhsaha, Vikarna and Shala,
Vinda and Anuvinda, Sumukha, Dirghabahu and Sudarshana, Vrindaraka, Suhasta,
Sushena and Dirghalochana, Abhaya, Raudrakarma, Suvarma and Durvimochana. Those
brave men, best of chariot fighters, came at Bhimasena ready for battle, with
their armies of many kinds and their followers. The mighty son of Kunti,
Bhimasena, looked at them and charged at them hard, like a lion at small deer.
There the heroes showed great divine weapons, holding Bhima back with their
arrows as clouds hold back the rising sun.

"He went past them fast and ran at Drona's division, and covered the troop of
elephants in front of it with rains of arrows. In no time at all the son of the
Wind hurled his swift arrows on every side and destroyed that troop of
elephants. As deer in a forest are frightened by a sharabha's roar, all the
elephants ran, screaming dreadful screams.

"Then again he went on fast and ran at Drona's division. The teacher held him
back, as the shore holds back the rising sea. Smiling, he struck him in the
forehead with an iron arrow, and the Pandava shone there like the sun with its
rays pointing up. The teacher thought, 'He will honour me as Phalguna did,' and
he said to Vrikodara:

"'Bhimasena, you cannot enter the enemy army without beating me in battle, in
the middle of your enemies, mighty one. Your younger brother went in with my
leave. But you cannot enter here.'

"Hearing the teacher's words, Bhima, with no fear in him, spoke in anger to
Drona, his eyes red and copper-coloured, breathing hard. 'Arjuna did not go into
the battlefield with your leave, you brahmin in name only. He cannot be held
back. He would get into Shakra's own army. He did you great honour, and so he
honoured you. I am not Arjuna, Drona, soft-hearted. I am Bhimasena, your enemy.
You are our father, our teacher and our kinsman, and we are your sons. That is
what we all believed, and we have stood bowing to you. Today, as you talk to us,
we see the opposite in you. If you think of yourself as our enemy, then so be
it. Here I am, Bhima, and I will do to you what is fit for an enemy.'

"Then Bhima whirled his mace, like Death whirling the staff of Time, and hurled
it at Drona, king, and Drona leapt down from his chariot. The mace smashed
Drona's chariot, with its horses, driver and banner, and Bhima crushed many
fighters with his might, as the wind crushes trees. Your sons surrounded that
best of chariot fighters again. And Drona, the best of strikers, took his stand
in another chariot.

"Then mighty Bhimasena, great king, in anger, covered the chariot force in front
of him with rains of arrows. Struck down in the battle, your sons, great
warriors wanting victory, fought Bhima of the terrible strength. Then
Duhshasana, in anger, wanting to kill the son of Pandu, hurled a sharp chariot
spear all of iron. As the great spear came at him, sent by your son, Bhima cut
it in two, and it seemed a wonder.

"Then in anger mighty Bhima killed three of them with three more sharp arrows:
Kundabhedin, Sushena and Dirghanetra. Then he killed the hero Vrindaraka, who
added to the fame of the Kurus, while your heroic sons were fighting. Again
Bhima killed three more of your sons with three arrows: Abhaya, Raudrakarma and
Durvimochana. Struck down by the stronger man, great king, your sons surrounded
Bhima, the best of strikers, on every side. As if laughing, the son of Kunti
sent Vinda and Anuvinda together, and your son Suvarma, to the house of Yama
with his arrows. Then, bull of the Bharatas, he quickly pierced your heroic son
Sudarshana in the battle, and he fell and died.

"In no time at all the son of Pandu hurled his swift arrows on every side and
destroyed that force of chariots. Then your sons, lord of the people, struck
down in the battle, fled with all their chariots at the roar of his chariot, as
deer flee at a roar, tormented by fear of Bhimasena. The son of Kunti followed
your sons' great army and pierced the Kauravas on every side in the battle,
king. Struck down by Bhimasena, great king, your men left Bhima in the battle
and went, urging on their fine horses.

"When he had beaten them in battle, mighty Bhimasena the Pandava roared a lion's
roar and slapped his arms. Then, slapping his palms together with a great noise,
mighty Bhima passed the chariot fighters and ran at Drona's division."

**Divergences.**

* **Drona's age.** Ganguli has "full five and eighty years of age" here. The CE line says "more than eighty" (*vayasāśītikāt paraḥ*, 7.101.69). The same verse returns at Drona's death with *vayasāśītipañcakaḥ* (7.165.103).
* **The Chedi, Kashi and Kosala troops.** Ganguli adds Kasis and Kosalas to those who charge Drona. The CE names the Chedis, Srinjayas and Somakas (7.101.50).
* **Bhima's departure.** \*769, in which Bhima walks round the brahmins, touches the eight lucky things and drinks Kairataka liquor before setting out, is rejected. (Satyaki's drink of Kailavata liquor before his own departure is in the CE: 7.87.61, ledger L-211.)
* **Drona after the mace.** \*781 and \*782, in which Drona goes back to the gate of the array and gives up fighting Bhima to guard it, are rejected. The CE says only that he took another chariot (7.102.89).
* **Yudhishthira's worries.** \*760–\*765 add more to Yudhishthira's speech (Arjuna came back from Indra's world for his sake; Bhima should roar a lion's roar as a signal). They are rejected.
* **The three sons killed twice.** 7.102.94 has Bhima kill Kundabhedin, Sushena and Dirghanetra "three with three" arrows. 7.36.29, in almost the same words, has Abhimanyu kill Sushena, Dirghalochana and Kundabhedin on the thirteenth day. The novel tells both as the CE does. Kundabhedin is named again at 7.131.84.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*739–\*759, \*766–\*768, \*770–\*780, \*783–\*789.

**Choices.**

* 7.101.3 *kalaśottamasaṃbhavaḥ*: "the son of the fine pot", Drona's birth from a pot (1.121).
* 7.101.5 *bhrātṝṇāṃ vīrapañcānāṃ jyeṣṭhaḥ*: "the eldest of five heroic brothers", the five Kekaya princes.
* 7.101.8 *ekaikaṃ daśabhir bāṇaiḥ*: "each one of them … with ten arrows". Ganguli has five.
* 7.101.13 *acyutaḥ*: "did not waver".
* 7.101.22 *śaiśupāliḥ*: "Shishupala's son", Dhrishtaketu, named at 7.101.25.
* 7.101.31 *aśmasāramayīm*: "as hard as stone".
* 7.101.37 *cāṣaḥ … pataṃgam*: "a blue jay … an insect".
* 7.101.38 *tat khaṇḍaṃ pitryam āviśat*: "took his father's place in the fight". The son is not named.
* 7.101.40 *jarāsaṃdhasutaḥ*: "the son of Jarasandha", not named here.
* 7.101.44 *yo yaḥ sma līyate droṇam*: "whoever came near Drona".
* 7.101.49 *ūrugrāhagṛhītāḥ*: "like men whose thighs are seized and will not move". Ganguli: "whose thighs had been seized by alligators". *ūrugrāha* is also the name of a stiffness of the thighs.
* 7.101.63 *samārohat*: "attacked", literally "mounted against".
* 7.101.71 *lubdho 'yam*: "This greedy man", Drona. *lubdha* can also mean "hunter". The next verse blames Duryodhana's greed (*lobhāt*).
* 7.101.74 *akṣauhiṇīpatiḥ*: "lord of an army", an *akṣauhiṇī*.
* 7.102.10 *tad idaṃ hy ekam evāsīd dvidhā jātam*: "What was one worry has become two".
* 7.102.16 *mitroparodhena*: "for his friend's sake".
* 7.102.26 *bahu tatra samādiśan*: "he said a great deal there".
* 7.102.36 *cakoranetraḥ tāmrākṣaḥ*: "eyes like a chakora bird's, red at the corners".
* 7.102.43 *brahmeśānendravaruṇān avahad yaḥ purā rathaḥ*: "the chariot that once carried Brahma, Ishana, Indra and Varuna".
* 7.102.52 *abhivādya guruṃ jyeṣṭham*: "saluted his elder brother and teacher".
* 7.102.56 *sendrāyudha ivāmbudaḥ*: "like a cloud with Indra's bow", the rainbow.
* 7.102.65 *viśokenābhisaṃyattāḥ*: "steered by Vishoka", Bhima's charioteer (6.60).
* 7.102.69: Vinda and Anuvinda here are sons of Dhritarashtra, not the Avanti princes killed at 7.74.25–29. Sudarshana is also a son of Dhritarashtra, not the king's son killed by Satyaki at 7.94.
* 7.102.76 *śarabhasya*: "a sharabha's roar", a fabled beast stronger than the lion.
* 7.102.81 *yadi te so 'nujaḥ kṛṣṇaḥ*: "Your younger brother", Arjuna, here called Krishna, "the dark one". The novel leaves out the name to avoid confusion with Vasudeva.
* 7.102.83 *brahmabandho*: "you brahmin in name only".
* 7.102.84 *nārjuno 'haṃ ghṛṇī*: "I am not Arjuna … soft-hearted".
* 7.102.87 *kāladaṇḍam ivāntakaḥ*: "like Death whirling the staff of Time".
* 7.102.94 *dīrghanetram*: "Dirghanetra", the same name as Dirghalochana ("long-eyed") at 7.102.70.

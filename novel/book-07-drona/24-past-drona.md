# Past Drona

"Having destroyed Duhshasana's force," said Sanjaya, "the Left-Handed Archer
Dhananjaya, wanting to reach the Sindhu king, rushed at Drona's division. He
came to Drona, standing at the mouth of the array, and with Krishna's leave he
joined his palms and said this:

"'Think well of me, brahmin, and wish me well. By your grace I want to go into
this army that is so hard to break. You are like a father to me, and like the
King of Dharma, and like Krishna. I tell you the truth. As Ashvatthama is yours
to protect, sinless one, so am I, always, best of the twice-born. By your grace,
best of men, I want to kill the Sindhu king in battle. Guard my vow, my lord.'

"Told this, the teacher answered as if smiling, 'Bibhatsu, Jayadratha cannot be
beaten unless you beat me first.' Saying no more than that, Drona laughed and
covered him with sharp flights of arrows, his chariot, horses, banner and
charioteer. Then Arjuna held off Drona's flights of arrows with his own, and
struck Drona with larger and more dreadful arrows. Keeping to a Kshatriya's
dharma, lord of the people, and having first honoured him, he pierced Drona
again in battle with nine arrows.

"Drona cut his arrows with arrows, and pierced both Krishna and the Pandava with
arrows like blazing fire and poison. The Pandava wanted to cut Drona's bow with
his arrows. But while great-souled Phalguna was still thinking of it, brave
Drona, quite calm, quickly cut his bowstring with arrows. He pierced his horses,
his banner and his charioteer, and smiling, he covered brave Arjuna with arrows.

"Meanwhile Partha strung his great bow. To outdo his teacher, the best of all
who knew weapons, he loosed six hundred arrows so fast they seemed like one.
Then he shot seven hundred more, and a thousand that never turned back, and
others by the ten thousand, and they killed Drona's army. Shot well by that
strong, skilled and wonderful fighter, men, horses and elephants were pierced
and fell lifeless. Chariot fighters, hurt by the arrows, their weapons and lives
cut short, fled and fell from their fine chariots, horses and all. The elephants
fell like mountain peaks crushed by thunder, like clouds scattered by the wind,
like houses burnt by fire. Thousands of horses fell, struck by Arjuna's arrows,
like swans on the heights of the Himalaya beaten down by the rain. Floods of
chariots, horses, elephants and foot, like wonderful floods of water, were
killed by the Pandava's arrows, which shone like the rays of the sun at the end
of an age.

"The Pandava was a sun, and his net of arrows was its rays, scorching the Kuru
heroes in the battle. Then Drona, like a cloud, covered it with the force of his
rain of arrows, as a cloud covers the rays of the sun. Then Drona struck
Dhananjaya in the chest with an iron arrow, shot with great force, that fed on
the lives of enemies. Every limb of him shook, like a mountain in an earthquake.
But Bibhatsu held firm and pierced Drona with his feathered arrows.

"Drona struck Vasudeva with five arrows, Arjuna with seventy-three and his
banner with three. Then Drona, bold and wanting to outdo his pupil, king, hid
Arjuna from sight with rains of arrows in the blink of an eye. We saw
Bharadvaja's arrows falling close together, and his bow looked like a wonderful
circle. Many arrows shot by Drona, with heron feathers, came at Vasudeva and
Dhananjaya in the battle, king.

"Seeing such a fight between Drona and the Pandava, Vasudeva, in his great
wisdom, thought of what had to be done. Then Vasudeva said to Dhananjaya,
'Partha, Partha, strong-armed one, we must not lose time. Let us leave Drona and
go. This is the greater task.' And Partha said to Krishna, 'As you wish,
Keshava.'

"Then the strong-armed man went round Drona, keeping him on his right, and moved
on. Bibhatsu turned and went away, loosing arrows. Then Drona said, smiling,
'Where are you going, Pandava? Surely you do not turn back in battle without
beating your enemy?'

"Arjuna said, 'You are my teacher, not my enemy. I am your pupil, like a son to
you. And there is no man in the world who could beat you in battle.'

"Saying this, strong-armed Bibhatsu, eager to kill Jayadratha, rushed at that
army in haste. The two great-souled Panchalas, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas, his
wheel guards, followed him as he went into your army.

"Then Jaya, great king, and Kritavarma the Satvata, the Kamboja and Shrutayus
blocked Dhananjaya. Ten thousand chariots followed them. There were Abhishahas,
Shurasenas, Shibis and Vasatis, Machellakas, Lalitthas, Kekayas and Madrakas,
the Narayana cowherds and the troops of the Kambojas. Karna had beaten them in
battle long before, and heroes held them in honour. With Bharadvaja's son at
their head, they gave up all thought of their lives and faced Arjuna. He burned
with grief for his son, and was angry, like Death the Ender. He was ready to
give his life in that tumult, armoured, a wonderful fighter. He plunged into the
divisions like an elephant, leader of the herd. They blocked that great bowman,
that bold tiger among men. Then a wild battle began that made the hair stand on
end, as the fighters and Arjuna went for each other. As he came on, that bull
among men, eager to kill Jayadratha, they held him back together, as treatment
holds back a disease that has broken out.

"Partha was held back by them, and Drona followed him quickly. Then the mighty
Partha, best of chariot fighters, scattered sharp swarms of arrows, as the sun
scatters its rays, and scorched that army as a host of diseases scorches the
body. A horse was pierced, a banner cut, an elephant fallen with its rider.
Parasols were thrown aside and chariots robbed of their wheels. Troops fled on
every side, hurt by the arrows. So the battle was a tumult, and nothing could be
made out. As they strained against each other in the fight with their arrows,
king, Arjuna made the army shake again and again.

"Wanting to make his vow come true, true to his word, the man with the white
horses rushed at the best of chariot fighters, the one with the red horses.
Drona the teacher struck his pupil, the great bowman, with twenty-five arrows
that pierced the vital places. Bibhatsu, the best of all who bear weapons,
rushed quickly at him, shooting arrows that broke the force of Drona's arrows.
As Arjuna quickly shot his broad arrows, Drona, whose soul was beyond measure,
struck back with smooth-jointed broad arrows of his own, calling up the Brahma
weapon.

"We saw Drona's wonderful mastery in that battle: young Arjuna tried hard, and
could not pierce him. As a great cloud pours out thousands of streams of rain,
the cloud that was Drona rained arrows on the mountain that was Partha. The
fiery Arjuna, sir, met that rain of arrows with the Brahma weapon, cutting down
arrows with arrows. Drona struck the man with white horses with twenty-five
arrows, and Vasudeva with seventy swift arrows in the arms and chest. Wise
Partha laughed and held off his teacher in the battle, as he poured out his
floods of sharp arrows.

"Then the two, the best of chariot fighters, as Drona struck them, turned aside
from him, hard to face as he was, like the fire risen at the end of an age.
Avoiding the sharp arrows that flew from Drona's bow, Kunti's son, with his
diadem and garland, cut down the Bhoja's division. Avoiding Drona, as one avoids
the Mainaka mountain, he went on between Kritavarma and Sudakshina the Kamboja.

"Then the Bhoja, hard to withstand, best of the Kurus, calmly and quickly
pierced that tiger among men with ten heron-feathered arrows. Arjuna pierced him
in the battle, king, with one sharp feathered arrow, and then with three more,
as if to stun the Satvata. The Bhoja laughed, and struck Partha and Vasudeva of
the Madhu line with twenty-five arrows each. Arjuna cut his bow and pierced him
with twenty-one arrows like tongues of fire, like angry poisonous snakes. Then
the great warrior Kritavarma took another bow and quickly pierced him in the
chest with five arrows, Bharata. Again he pierced Partha with five sharp arrows,
and Partha struck him between the breasts with nine.

"Seeing Kunti's son held up at Kritavarma's chariot, the Varshneya thought, 'We
must not lose time.' Then Krishna said to Partha, 'Show Kritavarma no mercy. Put
aside your kinship, crush him and cut him down.' Then Arjuna stunned Kritavarma
with his arrows and went on with his swift horses to the division of the
Kambojas.

"When the man with white horses had gone in, Hardikya was furious. Shaking his
bow with an arrow on it, he met the two Panchalas. As the two Panchala wheel
guards came on, following in Arjuna's steps, Kritavarma blocked them with the
arrows from his chariot. The Bhoja pierced them with arrows all of iron, three
for Yudhamanyu and four for Uttamaujas. They pierced him in turn, with ten
arrows each, and cut down his banner and his bow. Then Hardikya, faint with
anger, took another bow, left both heroes without their bows, and covered them
with rains of arrows. They strung two other bows and struck the Bhoja. In the
meantime Bibhatsu went into the enemy host. But the two bulls among men, blocked
by Kritavarma, could find no way in, though they fought hard against the
divisions of Dhritarashtra's sons. The man with white horses, killer of enemies,
pressed the divisions in the battle in his haste. He did not kill Kritavarma,
though he had him in reach.

"Seeing him come on like this, brave King Shrutayudha rushed at him, very angry,
shaking his great bow. He struck Partha with three arrows and Janardana with
seventy, and hit Partha's banner with a very sharp razor arrow. Arjuna, very
angry, struck him hard with ninety smooth-jointed arrows, like a great elephant
struck with goads. He could not bear the Pandava's bold attack, king, and struck
him with seventy-seven iron arrows. Arjuna cut his bow, cut his quiver, and in
his anger struck him in the chest with seven smooth-jointed arrows. Then the
king, faint with anger, took another bow and struck Vasava's son with nine
arrows in the arms and chest. Then Arjuna, tamer of enemies, smiling, pressed
Shrutayudha hard with many thousands of arrows, Bharata. The mighty great
warrior quickly killed his horses and his charioteer, and pierced him with
seventy iron arrows.

"His horses killed, King Shrutayudha left his chariot. The brave man raised his
mace and rushed at Partha in the battle. That brave king Shrutayudha was the son
of Varuna. His mother was Parnasha, a great river of cool water. For her son's
sake his mother had said to Varuna, 'Let this son of mine be one whom enemies
cannot kill in the world.'

"Varuna was pleased and said, 'I give him a boon for his good, a heavenly
weapon, by which your son will not be killed. But there is no way at all for a
man to be immortal. Everyone who is born must die, best of rivers. But by the
power of this weapon he will always be hard for enemies to overcome in battle.
Let the fever in your mind be gone.'

"Saying this, Varuna gave him a mace charged with spells. Once he had it,
Shrutayudha was hard to overcome in all the world. And the blessed lord of the
waters told him again, 'It must not be thrown at a man who is not fighting. It
will fall on you yourself.'

"With that mace, the killer of heroes, he struck Janardana. Mighty Krishna took
it on his thick shoulder. It did not shake Shauri, as the wind cannot shake the
Vindhya mountains. It flew back at him, like a witch badly sent, and killed the
hero Shrutayudha where he stood, never willing to bear a wrong. Having killed
brave Shrutayudha, it went into the earth.

"When the armies saw that Shrutayudha, tamer of enemies, had been killed by his
own weapon, a great cry of 'Alas!' went up there. It had been thrown by
Shrutayudha at Keshava, who was not fighting, lord of men, and so the mace
killed him. As Varuna had said, so he met his death in the battle. He fell
lifeless to the ground while all the bowmen watched. As he fell, the dear son of
Parnasha looked like a tree of many branches broken by the wind. Then all the
armies and all the leaders of the army fled, seeing Shrutayudha, tamer of
enemies, killed.

"Then the brave Sudakshina, son of the king of the Kambojas, came at Phalguna,
killer of enemies, with his swift horses. Partha sent seven arrows at him,
Bharata. They split the hero and went into the ground. Pierced deep in the
battle by the sharp arrows sent by Gandiva, he struck back at Arjuna with ten
heron-feathered arrows. He pierced Vasudeva with three and Partha again with
five. Partha cut his bow, sir, and cut down his banner. The Pandava pierced him
with two very sharp broad arrows. He pierced Partha with three, and gave a lion
roar.

"Then brave Sudakshina, in his anger, hurled at the Gandiva bowman a dreadful
spear, all of iron, hung with bells. Blazing like a great meteor, it reached the
great warrior, pierced him and fell to the ground in a shower of sparks. Partha,
whose courage was beyond thought, pierced him with fourteen heron-feathered iron
arrows, with his horses, banner, bow and charioteer. With many more arrows he
broke his chariot to pieces. Then the Pandava pierced Sudakshina the Kamboja,
whose plans and courage had come to nothing, in the heart with a broad-bladed
arrow. His vital parts split, his limbs slack, his crown and armlets fallen, the
hero fell face down, like a banner let loose from its frame. He was like a fine
karnikara tree, grown on a mountain peak, with good branches and firm roots,
broken by the wind at the end of winter. He lay killed on the ground, a man used
to Kamboja rugs. Handsome and copper-eyed, Sudakshina, son of the king of the
Kambojas, had been brought down by Partha with a barbed arrow.

"Then all your son's armies fled, seeing Shrutayudha and Sudakshina the Kamboja
killed."

**Divergences.**

* **Drona's answer.** In the CE, Drona says only that Jayadratha cannot be beaten unless Arjuna beats him first (7.66.7). The line in which he agrees with Arjuna but says a wise man must fight for his master (\*477) is rejected.
* **Krishna's worry.** \*478, in which Arjuna, though young, cannot get past the old Drona, and \*479, in which Krishna sees Arjuna troubled, are rejected. In the CE Krishna simply thinks of what must be done (7.66.28).
* **Shrutayudha's death.** In the CE the mace comes back and kills him (7.67.52–55). \*483, in which Arjuna first cuts off his arms with two razor arrows, and \*484, in which he falls headless and armless, are rejected.
* **Two different men.** Shrutayus, who blocks Arjuna at 7.66.36, is not Shrutayudha, killed at 7.67.53, and not the Kalinga Shrutayus killed on the second day (6.50).
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*480–\*482, \*485–\*491. \*486 has Arjuna faint from Sudakshina's spear.

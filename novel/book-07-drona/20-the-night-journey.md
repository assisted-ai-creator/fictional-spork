summary: Arjuna, thinking over his vow, falls into a troubled sleep, and in a dream Krishna comes to him. Arjuna fears he cannot reach Jayadratha behind eleven akshauhinis before the sun sets. # The Night Journey

"Kunti's son Dhananjaya, whose courage was beyond thought," said Sanjaya, "kept
thinking of what had been said and guarding his vow, and he fell into a daze. As
he lay burning with grief, thinking, the one with the Garuda banner, great in
splendour, came to him of the monkey banner in a dream. In every state the
righteous Dhananjaya never failed to rise for Krishna, out of devotion and love.
He rose, gave Govinda a seat, and did not think of a seat for himself. Then
Krishna, great in splendour, knowing Partha's resolve, sat down and said this to
Kunti's son, who stood:

"'Do not let your mind sink into despair, Partha. Time is hard to overcome. Time
drives all creatures to what is fated. Tell me why you despair, best of
speakers. The wise should not grieve. Grief destroys the work to be done. The
man who grieves delights his enemies and wears down his kin, and he wastes away
himself. So you should not grieve.'

"When Vasudeva said this, the unbeaten, wise Bibhatsu answered with words full
of meaning. 'I have made a great vow to kill Jayadratha: "Tomorrow I will kill
the evil man who killed my son," Keshava. To break my vow, they say, the sons of
Dhritarashtra will put the Saindhava behind them, guarded by all the great
chariot fighters, and those eleven akshauhinis, Krishna, so hard to beat. And if
my vow fails, how could a man like me live? With no way out of my pain, hero, my
wish is turning, and the sun goes quickly. That is why I say this.'

"When he heard the cause of Partha's grief, Krishna, whose banner bears the
bird, touched water and sat facing east. The lotus-eyed one, great in splendour,
said this for the good of Pandu's son, bent on the killing of the Saindhava.
'Partha, there is a supreme, eternal weapon named Pashupata, with which the god
Maheshvara killed all the daityas in battle. If you know it today, tomorrow you
will kill Jayadratha. So turn your mind to the god with the bull on his banner,
to learn it. Think of that god in your mind and be still, Dhananjaya. Then by
his grace, as his devotee, you will win that great weapon.'

"When he heard Krishna's words, Dhananjaya touched water, sat on the ground with
his mind fixed on one thing, and went to Bhava in his mind. Then, at the lucky
hour of Brahma, when his mind was fixed, Arjuna saw himself in the sky with
Keshava. As swift as the wind, Partha with Keshava went into the sky, crowded
with lights and haunted by siddhas and charanas. Held by the lord Keshava by his
right arm, he went on, looking at many wonderful things.

"In the north the righteous one saw the White Mountain, and in Kubera's pleasure
grounds a pool adorned with lotuses. He saw the Ganga, best of rivers, full of
water, with trees always in flower and fruit, and with crystal stones. It was
crowded with lions and tigers and herds of every kind of beast, lovely, with
holy hermitages, the haunt of charming birds. He saw the slopes of Mandara,
ringing with the songs of the kinnaras, bright with peaks of gold and silver and
with herbs of every kind, and graced by flowering mandara trees. He came to the
Black Mountain, like a heap of glossy collyrium, to the holy foot of the
Himalaya, to Mount Maniman, to Brahmatunga, and to other rivers and lands. As he
went with Keshava, Partha saw Sushringa and Shatashringa, the forest of
Sharyati, the holy place of the Horse's Head and the place of Atharvan. He saw
Vrishadamsha, the lord of mountains, and great Mandara, crowded with apsarases
and graced with kinnaras. The mountains were lovely with springs and adorned
with golden ores. He saw the earth, its body bright as moonlight, garlanded with
cities, and oceans of wonderful form with many mines of treasure. Looking at the
air, the sky and the earth as he went along Vishnu's path, amazed, with Krishna,
he sped on like a loosed arrow.

"Then Partha saw a mountain that seemed to blaze, with a brightness equal to
that of the planets, the stars and the moon, the sun and fire. When he reached
that mountain, he saw on its summit the great god with the bull on his banner,
always at his austerities. He blazed with his own splendour like a thousand
suns. He held a trident, wore matted hair, and was fair, dressed in bark and
deerskin, his body wonderful with a thousand eyes, of great might. The god was
with Parvati and shining hosts of beings. There was the sound of songs and
music, clapping and dancing, leaping, the slapping of arms and shouting, and
holy scents. Sages who speak of Brahman praised him with divine hymns. He was
the protector of all beings, the bearer of the bow, the unfallen.

"When righteous Vasudeva saw him, he bowed his head to the ground with Partha,
chanting the eternal Brahman. Krishna worshipped him with speech, mind, thought
and deed. He was the beginning of the worlds, the maker of all, the unborn, the
Lord, the imperishable, the highest source of mind. He was the sky, the wind,
the store of lights, the maker of the streams of rain and the highest nature of
the earth. He was the means of gods, danavas, yakshas and men, the highest
Brahman of the yogis, manifest, the treasure of those who know Brahman. He was
the creator and destroyer of all that moves and does not move, whose anger is
Time, the great one, the source of the virtues of Shakra and the sun. The two
went for refuge to Bhava, the unborn, whose self is the cause of all, whom the
wise see, seeking the subtle state of the highest self. And Arjuna bowed to that
god again and again, knowing him as the one beginning of what is and what will
be, the source of all beings.

"Then Sharva, as if smiling, said to the two who had come, 'Welcome to you, best
of men. Rise, and be free of weariness. And what do you wish, heroes? Tell me
quickly. I will accomplish for you the work you have come for. Choose what is
best for yourselves, and I will grant it all.'

"When they heard his words, the wise Vasudeva and Arjuna rose, joined their
hands, and praised Sharva.

"'Homage to Bhava, to Sharva, to Rudra, to the giver of boons, to the lord of
beasts, always, to the fierce one, to the one with braided hair. To Mahadeva, to
the terrible one, to Tryambaka, to Shambhu, to Ishana, to the destroyer of
Bhaga, homage to the killer of Andhaka. To Kumara's father, always, to the
blue-necked one, to the ordainer, to the red one, to the smoky one, to the
hunter, to the unconquered. Always to the one with the blue crest, to the one
with the trident, to the one with the divine eye. To the killer and protector,
to the three-eyed one, to the hunter, to the one whose seed is wealth. To the
one beyond thought, to Ambika's husband, to the one praised by all the gods. To
the one with the bull banner, to the tawny one, to the one with matted hair, to
the brahmacharin. To the one who does austerity in the water, to the friend of
brahmins, to the unconquered, to the self of all, the maker of all, who stands
covering all. Homage, homage to you, who are to be served, the source of beings,
always. To the one with Brahma's face, to Sharva, to Shankara and to Shiva.
Homage to the lord of speech, homage to the lord of creatures. Homage to the
lord of all, homage to the lord of the great. Homage to the one with a thousand
heads, a thousand arms and a thousand wraths, a thousand eyes and feet, homage
to the one whose deeds are countless. Homage to the golden one, to the one with
golden armour, who is always kind to his devotees. Let our wish be granted,
lord.'

"When they had praised Mahadeva like this, Vasudeva with Arjuna won Bhava's
favour, to obtain the weapon. Then Arjuna, glad at heart, bowed to the god with
the bull banner, and with wide eyes he saw the whole treasury of splendour. And
close to the Three-eyed God he saw the nightly offering he had made himself, his
regular one, which he had given to Vasudeva. Then the Pandava worshipped Sharva
and Krishna in his mind, and said to Shankara, 'I want the divine weapon.'

"Knowing that Partha's words were asking for a boon, the lord, the god, smiling,
spoke to Vasudeva and Arjuna. 'Near here, killers of enemies, is a divine lake
of nectar. There I placed long ago that divine bow and arrow of mine, with which
I brought down all the enemies of the gods in battle. Bring from there,
Krishnas, the finest bow with its arrow.'

"'So be it,' said the two heroes to Sharva. With his followers they set out for
that divine lake, surrounded by hundreds of divine wonders. It was the holy
lake, which brings about every purpose, that the one marked with the bull had
shown them. The two seers, Nara and Narayana, went there unshaken. When they
reached that lake, bright as the sun's disc, Arjuna and Achyuta saw a terrible
snake in the water, and a second, the finest, with a thousand heads, pouring out
great flames, like fire. Then Krishna and Partha touched water, joined their
hands, and stood before the two snakes, bowing to the god with the bull banner.
Chanting that Brahman, the Shatarudriya, as men who knew the Veda, and bowing to
Bhava with all their being, they went up to them. Then by the greatness of Rudra
the two great snakes left their forms and became that pair, the bow and the
arrow that kill enemies. The two great men took the shining bow and arrow in
delight, brought them back and gave them to the great god.

"Then from the side of the one marked with the bull a brahmacharin came forward,
tawny-eyed, a field of austerity, strong, blue and red. He took the finest bow
and stood in position, attentive, and drew the finest bow with its arrow in the
proper way. The Pandava, whose courage was beyond thought, watched his
bowstring, his grip and his stance, heard the spell that Bhava spoke, and took
it in. The mighty lord loosed the arrow into the lake itself, and the hero put
the bow back into the lake.

"Then Arjuna, knowing that Bhava was pleased, and remembering, thought in his
mind of the boon given in the forest and his sight of Shankara: 'Let that come
to me.' Knowing his thought, Bhava was pleased and granted the boon, the
terrible Pashupata, and the fulfilment of his vow. The invincible one, his hair
standing on end, thought his work done. The two bowed their heads to Maheshvara
in their joy. At that moment, with Bhava's leave, the two heroes Arjuna and
Keshava came back to their own camp. They were full of the greatest joy, like
Indra and Vishnu when, wanting Jambha's death, they had pleased the god."

**Divergences.**

* **A dream.** Krishna comes to Arjuna "in a dream" (*svapne*, 7.57.2), and the journey begins when Arjuna sits in meditation at "the hour of Brahma" (7.57.19–20). The CE does not say plainly where the dream ends and waking begins.
* **The offering.** Arjuna sees beside Shiva "the nightly offering he had made himself … which he had given to Vasudeva" (7.57.61), the offering of 7.56.3–4.
* **The Pashupata.** Arjuna had already been given the Pashupata by Shiva in the forest (3.41.7–13). Here, "remembering … the boon given in the forest", he asks that it come to him, and Shiva grants it "and the fulfilment of his vow" (7.57.78–79).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*410–\*427. \*414 adds Vedic names to the praise; \*421 adds a long string of Vedic epithets to the hymn.

summary: Inside the array Abhimanyu seizes Satyashravas, kills Shalya's son Rukmaratha, who boasted he would take him alive, and with the gandharva weapon Arjuna brought from Tumburu cuts down a hundred of Rukmaratha's princely friends. Duryodhana, frightened, attacks and is driven off. Duryodhana's son Lakshmana, young and fearless, stands by his father; Abhimanyu tells him to take a last look at the world and cuts off his head, and Duryodhana cries out for Abhimanyu's death. Six great chariot fighters surround the boy. He kills Kratha's son, Vrindaraka and the king of Kosala, Brihadbala, and many more, and keeps Karna and the others at bay. Drona, delighted by him, tells Karna that the boy's armour cannot be pierced, but his bow and reins and horses can be struck, and that he must be made chariotless. Karna cuts his bow, Kritavarma kills his horses, Kripa his drivers, and the six rain arrows on the boy. Chariotless, he leaps into the air with sword and shield; Drona cuts his sword and Karna his shield, and Abhimanyu seizes a chariot wheel and rushes at Drona, like Vishnu with the discus.

# Lakshmana

"Arjuna's son was taking the lives of heroes," said Sanjaya, "like Time, the
ender of all creatures, when their hour has come. Mighty Abhimanyu, the son of
Shakra's son, as bold as Shakra, shone as he stirred up the army. As soon as he
went in, king of kings, like the Ender of the lords of the kshatriyas, he seized
Satyashravas, as a tiger seizes a great deer. When Satyashravas had been seized,
the great chariot fighters grabbed their weapons and charged at Abhimanyu. 'Me
first! Me first!' cried the bulls among the kshatriyas, vying with one another,
wanting to kill Arjuna's son. As the ranks of the kshatriyas charged at him, he
met them and swallowed them, like a whale swallowing small fish in the ocean.
Those who went near him never came back, any more than rivers come back from the
sea. That army shook like a ship lost at sea, as if seized by a great sea
monster and tormented by the force of the wind.

"Then mighty Rukmaratha, the son of the lord of the Madras, fearless himself,
steadied the frightened army and said, 'Enough of fear, heroes! He is nothing
while I stand here. I will take him alive, beyond doubt.' When he had said this,
the brave man rushed at Subhadra's son, carried in a shining, well-fitted
chariot. He pierced Abhimanyu in the chest with three arrows and roared, then
with three sharp ones in the right arm and three in the left. Phalguna's son cut
his bow, and quickly brought to the ground his right and left arms and his head,
with its fine eyes and brows.

"The princes, mad for battle, strikers and friends of Shalya's son, with banners
of worked gold, saw proud Rukmaratha, Shalya's son, who had wanted to take
famous Subhadra's son alive, broken by him. Drawing bows as tall as palm trees,
those great chariot fighters surrounded Arjuna's son on every side with a shower
of arrows. Duryodhana saw brave Subhadra's son, unbeaten and alone in the
battle, covered with swarms of arrows by brave young men, skilled, strong and
very angry. He was delighted, and thought him already gone to Vaivasvata's
house. In the blink of an eye those princes made Arjuna's son invisible with
golden-feathered arrows of every kind, three each. We saw his chariot, sir, with
its driver, horses and banner, stuck all over like a porcupine with its quills.

"Deeply pierced, angry as an elephant goaded, he called up the gandharva weapon
and joined to it the magic of the chariot. It was the weapon Arjuna had won from
the gandharvas, from Tumburu and the others, by his austerities, and with it
Abhimanyu bewildered his enemies. He was seen as one, and a hundred, and a
thousand, king, showing his weapons swiftly in battle like a whirling firebrand.
With the magic of his chariot work and his weapons he bewildered them, tamer of
enemies, and split the bodies of the kings a hundred ways, king. The lives of
the living, sent off by his sharp arrows, reached the other world, king, and
their bodies went to the earth. With sharp broad arrows Phalguna's son cut their
bows, horses and drivers, their banners, their arms with their bracelets, and
their heads. As a mango grove in fruit, five years old, falls when it is broken,
so a hundred princes fell, killed by Subhadra's son.

"When he saw those tender young men, used to comfort, like angry poisonous
snakes, killed by one man, Duryodhana was afraid. When he saw chariot fighters,
elephants, horses and foot soldiers crushed, Duryodhana went quickly at him in a
rage. There was a battle between the two, as if unfinished, for a moment, and
then your son turned away, tormented by hundreds of arrows."

"Charioteer," said Dhritarashtra, "what you tell me of the wild, fierce battle
of one against many, and the victory of that great man, and of Subhadra's son's
courage, is hard to believe, like a marvel. But it is no great wonder in those
whose refuge is dharma. When Duryodhana had turned away and a hundred princes
had been killed, what did my men do about Subhadra's son?"

"Their mouths dry and their eyes darting," said Sanjaya, "sweating, their hair
on end, your men were eager to flee and had no heart to beat the enemy. They
left their brothers, fathers, sons, friends, relations and kin lying dead, and
went off, urging on their horses and elephants. When Drona, Drona's son,
Brihadbala, Kripa, Duryodhana, Karna, Kritavarma and Subala's son saw them
broken, they charged furiously at Subhadra's son, who was never beaten. And they
too, king, were mostly turned away by your grandson.

"But one man, who had grown up in comfort, fearless from youth and pride, a
master of the bow, full of power, went at Arjuna's son: Lakshmana. His father,
anxious for his son, turned back right behind him, and the other great chariot
fighters turned back after Duryodhana. They showered him with arrows, as clouds
shower a mountain with rain, and he alone crushed them on every side, as the
wind does the clouds. Your grandson Lakshmana was so hard to overcome, and
handsome. He stood near his father, a hero with his bow raised, brought up in
great comfort, like the son of the lord of wealth. Krishna's son came up against
him in the battle, like an elephant in rut against an elephant in rut.

"When he met Lakshmana, Subhadra's son, the killer of enemy heroes, was struck
in the arms and chest with very sharp arrows. Angry like a snake struck with a
stick, your grandson, great king, spoke to your grandson. 'Take a good look at
the world. You are going to the other world. While your kin look on, I will send
you to Yama's house.' When he had said this, strong-armed Subhadra's son, the
killer of enemy heroes, drew out a broad arrow like a snake that has shed its
skin. Loosed from his arm, it struck off Lakshmana's handsome head, with its
fine nose and brows, its hair and its earrings. When they saw Lakshmana killed,
the people cried out, 'Alas!'

"When his dear son had fallen, Duryodhana, the bull among the kshatriyas, cried
to the kshatriyas in fury, 'Kill him!' Then Drona, Kripa, Karna, Drona's son,
Brihadbala and Kritavarma Hardikya, six chariot fighters, surrounded him.
Arjuna's son pierced them with sharp arrows, turned them away, and rushed
furiously at the Saindhava's great army. The Kalingas, the Nishadas and Kratha's
mighty son blocked his way in armour with a rank of elephants, and the battle
was fierce and close, lord of the people. Then Arjuna's son destroyed that proud
rank of elephants, as the ever-moving wind blows away hundreds of clouds in the
sky. Then Kratha covered Arjuna's son with swarms of arrows, and the others, the
chariot fighters led by Drona, turned back again and rushed at Subhadra's son,
shaking their great weapons. Arjuna's son held them off with arrows and struck
at Kratha's son, wanting to kill him quickly, with a boundless flood of arrows.
He brought down his arms, with bow, arrows and bracelets, his crowned head, his
umbrella and banner, his driver and his horses. When that hero was killed, who
had family, character, learning and strength, fame and the strength of weapons,
the heroes mostly turned away."

"Young Subhadra's son had gone in like that," said Dhritarashtra, "never beaten,
doing deeds worthy of his family, never running in battle. His well-bred, strong
three-year-old horses carried him as if flying through the air. Which heroes
held him back?"

"Once he had gone in, the delight of the Pandavas turned all your kings away
with sharp arrows," said Sanjaya. "But Drona, Kripa, Karna, Drona's son with
Brihadbala, and Kritavarma Hardikya, six chariot fighters, surrounded him. When
your army saw that too great a load had been laid on the Saindhava, great king,
it rushed at Yudhishthira. Other great chariot fighters, drawing bows as tall as
palm trees, poured showers of arrows on the hero Subhadra's son. Subhadra's son,
the killer of enemy heroes, held off with his arrows in the battle all those
great archers, who were masters of every skill.

"Arjuna's son pierced Drona with fifty arrows, Brihadbala with twenty,
Kritavarma with eighty and Kripa with sixty, and Ashvatthama with ten swift
arrows with golden feathers, drawn to the ear. In the middle of his enemies
Phalguna's son pierced Karna in the ear with a sharp, yellow, barbed arrow. He
brought down Kripa's horses and both his flank drivers, and then pierced him in
the chest with ten arrows. Then, while your brave sons looked on, the mighty one
killed the hero Vrindaraka, who added to the fame of the Kurus. Drona's son
pierced him with twenty-five small arrows as he broke, fearless, the best of his
enemies one after another. While Dhritarashtra's sons looked on, Arjuna's son
quickly pierced Ashvatthama back with sharp arrows, sir. Drona's son pierced him
with sixty fierce, sharp, well-honed arrows, but could not shake him, any more
than Mount Mainaka. Mighty and full of power, he pierced back his assailant,
Drona's son, with seventy- three golden-feathered straight arrows.

"Drona, anxious for his son, loosed a hundred arrows at him, and Ashvatthama
eight, wanting to save his father in battle. Karna loosed twenty-two broad
arrows, Kritavarma fourteen, Brihadbala fifty and Kripa, Sharadvat's son, ten.
He struck them all back with ten arrows each. As he was tormented on every side
with sharp arrows, the lord of the Kosalas struck him in the heart with a barbed
arrow. Abhimanyu brought his horses, banner, bow and driver down to the ground.
Then the king of the Kosalas, without his chariot, holding a sword and shield,
wanted to cut Phalguna's son's head, with its earrings, from his body. He
pierced the lord of the Kosalas, Prince Brihadbala, in the heart with an arrow,
and he fell with his heart split. And he broke ten thousand great men, king, who
held swords and bows and spoke words of ill omen. Having killed Brihadbala like
that, Subhadra's son ranged over the battle, holding back your archers with
showers of arrows.

"Phalguna's son again pierced Karna in the ear with a barbed arrow, and angered
him greatly by piercing him with fifty arrows. Radheya pierced him back with as
many, and covered all over with them, Bharata, he looked very fine. In his anger
he made Karna stream with blood, and brave Karna, bright with arrows and bathed
in blood, looked fine too. Both great men, their bodies bright with arrows and
drenched in blood, looked like flowering kimshuka trees. Then Subhadra's son
killed six of Karna's counsellors, brave and wonderful fighters, with their
horses, drivers, banners and chariots. Then, unshaken, he pierced the other
great archers with ten arrows each, and it was a marvel. He killed the young son
of the Magadha king with six straight arrows and brought down Ashvaketu with his
horses and driver. He struck down, with a razor-headed arrow, the Bhoja of
Martikavata, whose banner bore an elephant, and roared, loosing arrows.

"Duhshasana's son pierced his four horses with four arrows and his driver with
one, and Arjuna's son with ten. Then Krishna's son pierced Duhshasana's son with
seven swift arrows and, his eyes red with rage, said loudly, 'Your father left
the battle and ran like a coward. It is lucky that you know how to fight. But
you will not get away today.' When he had said so much, he loosed at him an iron
arrow polished by the smith. Drona's son cut it with three arrows. Arjuna's son
cut his banner and struck Shalya with three arrows. Shalya struck him with nine
arrows fledged with vultures' feathers. Arjuna's son cut his banner and both his
flank drivers, and pierced him with six iron arrows, and he moved away to
another chariot. He killed five, Shatrunjaya, Chandraketu, Meghavega, Suvarchas
and Suryabhasa, and pierced Subala's son.

"Subala's son pierced him with three arrows and said to Duryodhana, 'Let us all
crush him together, before he kills us one by one.' Then Vaikartana Karna,
Vrisha, said to Drona, 'Tell us quickly how to kill him, before he crushes us
all.'

"Then Drona, the great archer, spoke to them all. 'Do any of you see any gap in
the boy at all? Look at the speed of this lion among men, the Pandava, as he
ranges in every direction in his father's way today. Only the circle of his bow
is seen in the paths of his chariot, as he fits his arrows and looses them so
fast. Subhadra's son, the killer of enemy heroes, almost wounds my very life and
bewilders me with his arrows, and yet he delights me more and more. Subhadra's
son gives me great joy as he ranges over the battle, and the angry great chariot
fighters see no gap in him. As he shoots his great arrows in every direction
with his quick hand, I see no difference between him and the bearer of the
Gandiva in battle.'

"Then Karna, tormented by Arjuna's son's arrows, said to Drona again, 'I stay
here only because I must stay. Abhimanyu is crushing me. The terrible arrows of
the brilliant boy, fierce as fire, are wearing away my heart today.'

"The teacher, as if laughing, said slowly to Karna, 'His armour cannot be
pierced, and he is young and quick in his courage. I taught his father how to
wear armour, and surely this conqueror of enemy cities knows it all. But his bow
and its string can be cut with well-aimed arrows, and his reins, his horses and
both his flank drivers. Do that, great archer, Radheya, if it can be done. Then,
when you have turned him away, strike him afterwards. With his bow in his hand
he cannot be beaten, even by the gods and demons. Make him chariotless and
bowless, if you want to.'

"When he heard the teacher's words, Vaikartana Karna quickly cut with his arrows
the bow of the swift-handed boy as he shot. The Bhoja killed his horses, and
Gautama his flank drivers. The rest showered the boy with arrows once his bow
was cut. Quick when quickness was needed, the six great chariot fighters,
without mercy, showered the boy, alone and without his chariot, with arrows.
Bowless and chariotless, keeping to his own dharma, the splendid boy took up his
sword and shield and leapt into the sky. With the kaushika pass and the others,
with speed and strength, Arjuna's son moved in the sky like the king of birds.
'He is coming down on me with his sword!' the great archers thought, looking up,
and they pierced him in the battle, watching for a gap. Drona cut the sword,
with its hilt of jewels, at the grip, and Radheya shattered his fine shield with
sharp arrows.

"Without sword or shield, his body full of arrows, he came down from the sky to
the ground, raised a chariot wheel and rushed in fury at Drona. His body shone,
brilliant with the dust of the wheel, and he looked very fine, the wheel raised
high in his hand. In battle Abhimanyu, a delight like the night, was imitating
Vasudeva. His face coloured all over by the blood that ran from him, his brows
knitted, roaring loudly, the lord of boundless strength, Abhimanyu, shone out in
the battle in the midst of the kings."

**Divergences.**

* **Satyashravas.** Abhimanyu "seized" (*ādatta*) Satyashravas (7.44.3). The CE does not say what became of him.
* **Lakshmana.** Duryodhana's son Lakshmana is killed by Abhimanyu with a broad arrow, after "Take a good look at the world" (7.45.15–17).
* **Six chariot fighters.** Drona, Kripa, Karna, Ashvatthama, Brihadbala and Kritavarma, "six chariot fighters", surround Abhimanyu (7.45.19; 7.46.4). After Brihadbala is killed (7.46.22), "the six great chariot fighters" who shower the chariotless boy (7.47.33) are not named. The popular "seven great chariot fighters" is not in the CE. See ledger L-204.
* **Drona's advice.** Drona tells Karna that Abhimanyu's armour cannot be pierced, because Drona taught his father how to wear it, and that he must be made chariotless and bowless (7.47.26–30). Karna cuts his bow as he shoots; Kritavarma kills his horses; Kripa his flank drivers (7.47.31–32). The CE does not say that Karna shot from behind.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*305–\*328. \*316 gives numbers for Abhimanyu's slaughter; \*322 is a longer complaint by Karna; \*326 has the six shout, "The boy has lost his mark! Kill him!"

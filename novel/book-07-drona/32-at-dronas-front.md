# At Drona's Front

"As Brihatkshatra the Kekaya, firm in courage, came on, great king," said
Sanjaya, "Kshemadhurti pierced him in the chest with arrows. King Brihatkshatra
quickly struck him in battle with ninety smooth-jointed arrows, wanting to break
Drona's division. Kshemadhurti, in anger, cut the great-souled Kekaya's bow with
a sharp, well-tempered broad arrow. Then, with his bow cut, he quickly pierced
him in the heart with a smooth-jointed arrow, the best of all bowmen. Then
Brihatkshatra took another bow, and as if laughing, he left the great warrior
Kshemadhurti without horses, charioteer or banner. With another sharp,
well-tempered broad arrow he took the king's head from his body, in its blazing
earrings. Cut off at a stroke, the head with its curling hair and its crown fell
to the earth and shone like a star fallen from the sky. Having killed him in
battle, the great warrior Brihatkshatra, full of joy, fell on your army at once
for Partha's sake.

"As Dhrishtaketu came on for Drona's sake, the bold great bowman Viradhanva held
him back, Bharata. The two, swift and bold, with arrows for fangs, met and
struck each other with many thousands of arrows. The two tigers among men fought
each other like two leaders of herds, elephants fierce with rut, in a great
forest. Like two angry tigers that have come to a mountain cave, the two men of
great courage fought, each wanting to kill the other. That battle was wild and
worth watching, lord of the people, a wonder and an amazement to the hosts of
Siddhas and Charanas.

"Then Viradhanva, in anger, as if laughing, cut Dhrishtaketu's bow in two with a
broad arrow, Bharata. The great warrior, king of the Chedis, threw away the cut
bow and seized a great iron spear with a golden staff. Raising that mighty spear
with both arms, Bharata, he hurled it hard and with care at Viradhanva's
chariot. Struck hard by that hero-killing spear, his heart split open, he fell
quickly from his chariot to the ground. When that brave great warrior of the
Trigartas was killed, your army broke on every side before the Pandavas, lord.

"Durmukha loosed sixty arrows at Sahadeva, and gave a great roar, threatening
the Pandava in the battle. The son of Madri, in anger, pierced Durmukha with ten
arrows, as if laughing, brother striking brother as he came on. Seeing mighty
Sahadeva fierce in battle, Durmukha struck him with nine arrows, Bharata. The
mighty Sahadeva cut Durmukha's banner with a broad arrow and killed his four
horses with four sharp arrows. With another sharp, well-tempered broad arrow he
cut the charioteer's head from his body, in its blazing earrings. With a sharp
razor arrow Sahadeva cut the Kauravya's great bow in the battle and pierced him
with five.

"Durmukha left his chariot, its horses killed, and in dismay climbed onto
Niramitra's chariot, king, Bharata. Then Sahadeva, killer of enemy heroes, in
anger, killed Niramitra in the great battle, in the middle of the army, with a
broad arrow. Niramitra, lord of men, son of the Trigarta king, fell from his
chariot, and your army was shaken. Having killed him, strong-armed Sahadeva
shone like Rama, son of Dasharatha, when he had killed mighty Khara. A great cry
of 'Alas!' went up from the Trigartas, lord of men, when they saw the mighty
prince Niramitra killed.

"Nakula, king, beat your son, broad-eyed Vikarna, in a moment in the battle, and
it was a wonder.

"Vyaghradatta, with smooth-jointed arrows, made Satyaki vanish in the middle of
the army, with his horses, charioteer and banner. Brave Shaineya, as a skilled
man does, held off those arrows, and with his arrows brought down Vyaghradatta,
with his horses, charioteer and banner. When that prince, the son of the Magadha
king, was killed, lord, the Magadhas rushed at Yuyudhana from every side, on
their guard. Loosing arrows and lances by the thousand, and javelins, spears,
hammers and clubs, the brave men fought the Satvata, fierce in battle, in the
fight. The mighty Satyaki, fierce in battle, beat them all, bull among men,
without much trouble, laughing. When your army saw the Magadhas who were left
alive fleeing on every side, it broke, lord, hurt by Yuyudhana's arrows. Having
destroyed your army in battle, the best of the Madhavas, of great fame, shone as
he shook his fine bow. Your army, broken by the great-souled Satvata and
frightened by the strong-armed man, did not come back to fight. Then Drona, in
great anger, his eyes rolling, rushed at Satyaki, whose deeds were true,
himself.

"Somadatta's famous son pierced each of the sons of Draupadi, those great
bowmen, with five arrows, and again with seven. Hurt badly and all at once by
that fierce man, lord, they were dazed and could not tell what to do in the
battle. Then Nakula's son Shatanika, scorcher of enemies, pierced the bull among
men, Somadatta's son, with two arrows and roared with joy. The others too, ready
in the battle, quickly pierced angry Somadatta's son with three straight arrows
each. He loosed five arrows at them, great king, and the famous man struck each
of them in the heart with one. Then the five brothers, pierced with arrows by
the great-souled man, surrounded the hero with their chariots and pierced him
hard with arrows.

"Arjuna's son, in anger, sent his horses to the house of Yama with four sharp
arrows. Bhimasena's son cut great-souled Somadatta's son's bow, gave a mighty
roar and pierced him with sharp arrows. Yudhishthira's son cut down his banner
and threw it to the ground, and Nakula's son struck his charioteer from the
chariot box. Then Sahadeva's son, knowing he had been turned back by the
brothers, cut off his head with a razor arrow, king, great-minded as he was.
That head, adorned with gold, fell to the ground and lit up the battlefield,
bright as the young sun. When they saw the head of great-souled Somadatta's son
fall, your men, king, fled in terror in many directions.

"Alambusa, in the battle, fought mighty Bhimasena in anger, as Ravana's son
fought Lakshmana. Seeing the man and the rakshasa locked in battle, all beings
were amazed and thrilled. Then Bhima, laughing, pierced the son of
Rishyashringa, the angry lord of rakshasas, with nine sharp arrows, king.
Pierced in the battle, the rakshasa gave a dreadful cry and rushed at Bhima,
with all his followers. He pierced Bhima with five smooth-jointed arrows, and
the tamer of enemies quickly killed thirty of the chariots that followed Bhima.
Then he killed four hundred more and pierced Bhima with feathered arrows.
Pierced deep by the rakshasa, mighty Bhima sank down in his chariot box,
overcome by a faint.

"Then the son of the Wind came to himself, faint with anger. He drew his
dreadful bow, the best, able to bear any weight, and struck Alambusa with sharp
arrows in every limb. Pierced by many arrows, looking like a heap of black
collyrium, he shone on every side, king, like a kimshuka tree ablaze with
flowers. As the arrows from Bhima's bow struck him in the battle, he remembered
his brother's death at the hands of the great-souled Pandava. He took on a
dreadful shape and said to Bhimasena, 'Stand now in battle, Partha, and see my
courage today. You fool, the mighty Baka, best of rakshasas, was my brother. You
killed him when I was not there.'

"Saying this to Bhima, he vanished and covered him deeply with a great rain of
arrows. With the rakshasa unseen, king, Bhima filled the sky with smooth-jointed
arrows in the battle. As Bhima struck at him, the wretch was in his chariot in
the blink of an eye, then he went into the earth, and then at once into the sky.
He took many forms, high and low, and spoke words high and low on every side.
The elephants of the Pandava army were crushed by him in battle, king, and many
horses and foot soldiers too. Chariot fighters fell from their chariots, struck
by his arrows. He set flowing a river full of hosts of rakshasas. Blood was its
water, chariots its whirlpools, and it was crowded with elephants for
crocodiles. Parasols were its swans, it was thick with mud, and it was crowded
with arms for snakes. It carried away many Chedis, Panchalas and Srinjayas,
king.

"Seeing him move so fearlessly in the battle, the Pandavas were deeply troubled,
and saw his courage. Your army grew glad, and the sound of instruments rose,
fierce and very loud, making the hair stand on end. Hearing that dreadful noise
from your army, the Pandava could not bear it, as an elephant cannot bear the
sound of clapping hands. Then, his eyes red with anger, like a fire burning
everything up, he set on his bow the weapon of Tvashtri, as if he were Tvashtri
himself, sir. From it came thousands of arrows on every side, and those arrows
put your army to great flight. The weapon Bhimasena sent in the battle destroyed
the rakshasa's great illusion and hurt the rakshasa. Struck many times by
Bhimasena, the rakshasa left Bhima in the battle and ran to Drona's division.
When the great-souled man had beaten the lord of rakshasas, king, the Pandavas
filled every quarter with lion roars. Gladly they honoured the son of the Wind,
the mighty, as the hosts of Maruts honoured Shakra when he had beaten Prahrada
in battle.

"As Alambusa moved so fearlessly in the battle, Hidimba's son went quickly at
him and pierced him with sharp arrows. The battle between those two lions among
rakshasas was terrible, as they worked illusions of every kind, like Shakra and
Shambara. Alambusa, in great anger, struck Ghatotkacha. Then Ghatotkacha pierced
Alambusa between the breasts with twenty iron arrows and roared again and again
like a lion. In the same way Alambusa, king, pierced Hidimba's son, fierce in
battle, again and again, and roared with joy, filling the sky on every side.

"So the two mighty lords of rakshasas, in great anger, fought each other with
illusions, and neither was better than the other. Proud, making hundreds of
illusions, bewildering each other, the two, skilled in the battle of illusion,
fought a battle of illusion. Whatever illusion Ghatotkacha showed in the battle,
king, Alambusa destroyed with an illusion of his own. When the Pandavas saw
Alambusa, lord of rakshasas, skilled in the battle of illusion, fighting like
this, they grew angry.

"The best of them, Bhimasena and the others, in great anger, rushed at him from
every side with their chariots, king. They shut him in with a line of chariots,
sir, and poured arrows on him from every side, like men throwing torches at an
elephant. He struck back the force of their weapons with the illusion of his own
weapons, and got free of that mass of chariots like an elephant from a forest
fire. He drew his dreadful bow, which sounded like great Indra's thunderbolt. He
pierced the son of the Wind with twenty-five arrows and Bhima's son with five.
He pierced Yudhishthira with three and Sahadeva with seven, Nakula with
seventy-three and the sons of Draupadi with five each, sir, and gave a dreadful
roar.

"Bhimasena pierced him with nine arrows and Sahadeva with five. Yudhishthira
pierced the rakshasa with a hundred, Nakula with sixty-four and the sons of
Draupadi with three each. Hidimba's son pierced the rakshasa in battle with
fifty arrows, and again with seventy, and the mighty one roared. Pierced deep on
every side by those great warriors, the great bowman pierced them all back with
five arrows each. Hidimba's son, the rakshasa, angry at the angry rakshasa in
battle, pierced him with seven arrows, best of the Bharatas. Pierced deep by the
strong one, the mighty lord of rakshasas quickly loosed gold-feathered arrows
whetted on stone. Those smooth-jointed arrows went into the rakshasa like
fierce, mighty, angry snakes going into a mountain.

"Then the Pandavas, king, and Hidimba's son Ghatotkacha, in alarm, sent sharp
arrows at him from every side. As the Pandavas, flushed with victory, struck him
in the battle, Ghatotkacha lifted him in his arms. He looked like the peak of a
burnt mountain, like a broken heap of collyrium. Ghatotkacha whirled him round
again and again and quickly smashed him on the ground, like a full pot on a
rock. Bhimasena's son, full of strength and speed and courage, in his anger in
the battle, frightened all the armies. Every limb burst open, his bones and his
ornaments crushed to powder, Alambusa of Salakatankata's line was killed by the
hero Ghatotkacha.

"Then the Parthas, glad at heart, now that the night-walker was dead, shouted
lion roars and waved their clothes. And your armies, bull of the Bharatas, when
they saw the mighty lord of rakshasas, dreadful Alambusa, dead like a mountain
fallen to pieces, cried out, 'Alas!' People looked at the rakshasa with
curiosity, lying on the ground like a lump of coal fallen there by chance.
Having killed that rakshasa, the best of the strong, Ghatotkacha gave a mighty
roar, like Vasava when he had killed Bala.

"Honoured by his fathers and his kin for the hard deed done, Ghatotkacha, having
killed his enemy Alambusa, rejoiced, as over a ripe alambusa fruit. Then a very
great noise went up, with conches and the whizzing of arrows of every kind.
Hearing it, the Kauravas roared back, and the sound reached out and touched the
whole world."

**Divergences.**

* **Which son of Somadatta.** The CE calls the man Draupadi's sons kill "Somadatta's son" (*saumadatti*, 7.83.1). It cannot be Bhurishravas, who fights on later in this book. The CE does not name him here.
* **Baka's brother.** Alambusa, son of Rishyashringa, calls Baka "my brother" (7.83.23). Bhima killed Baka at Ekachakra (1.151–152).
* **Durmukha.** Sahadeva fights Durmukha "brother against brother" (7.82.20), as cousins.
* **How Ghatotkacha seized him.** In the CE he simply lifts Alambusa in his arms (7.84.21–22). \*571, in which he leaps from chariot to chariot and plucks him out like Garuda seizing a snake, is rejected, as is the passage @10.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*557–\*570, \*572–\*574, @9. \*574 has Ghatotkacha go to Yudhishthira, who kisses his head.

* 7.82.33 *kumāre … magadhasya sute*: "that prince, the son of the Magadha king".

* 7.83.33 *yathā nāgas talaśabdam*: "as an elephant cannot bear the sound of clapping hands".
* 7.83.34 *tvāṣṭram astram*: "the weapon of Tvashtri".
* 7.83.39 *prahrādam*: Prahrada, the demon king beaten by Indra.

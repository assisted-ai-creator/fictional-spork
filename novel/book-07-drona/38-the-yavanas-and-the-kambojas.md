# The Yavanas and the Kambojas

"When he had killed Sudarshana in battle," said Sanjaya, "Satyaki, the wise and
great-souled bull of the Vrishnis, spoke to his charioteer.

"'We have crossed the great sea of Drona's army, my friend, and it was hard to
cross. It was thick with chariots, horses and elephants, and its waves were
garlands of arrows and spears. Swords were its fish and maces its crocodiles,
and the weapons of the brave made its great roar. It was fierce and it took
lives. It thundered with the din of instruments. For fighters who wanted
victory, its touch brought no joy, and it was hard to overcome. With the troops
of Jalasandha it was as if it were ringed with man-eaters. What is left of the
army after that, I think, is like a poor little stream, with little water, to be
waded across. Drive the horses on without fear.

"'I think the Left-Handed Archer is within my reach now, since I have beaten
Drona, hard to hold, with his followers, in battle, and Hardikya, the best of
fighters. I think I have reached Dhananjaya already. No fear rises in me when I
see armies in their many kinds, any more than in a blazing fire in the hot
season that sees a heap of dry grass. Look at the ground where the crowned one,
the chief of the Pandavas, has gone. It is made uneven by the masses of fallen
foot soldiers, horses, chariots and elephants. I think the man with the white
horses and Krishna for his charioteer is close. That sound we hear is the
Gandiva, whose strength has no measure. From the signs that are appearing to me,
Arjuna will kill the Saindhava before the sun sets.

"'Go on carefully towards the enemy's army, and let the horses breathe as you
go. There, where these men stand in their body armour, with Suyodhana at their
head, the armoured Kambojas are waiting, cruel in their deeds and wild in
battle, and the Yavanas with their arrows and bows, hard strikers. The Shakas,
Kiratas, Daradas, Barbaras and Tamraliptakas are there, and many other mlecchas
with weapons of many kinds in their hands. All of them stand facing me, eager
for battle. Once I have killed them in battle, with their chariots, elephants,
horses and foot soldiers, count this terrible stronghold as crossed.'

"The charioteer said, 'I have no fear, man of the Vrishnis, whose courage is
true. Even if Jamadagni's son stood in front of me in great anger, or Drona,
best of chariot fighters, or Kripa, or the lord of the Madras, still I would
feel no fear, strong-armed one, with you to shelter me. You have beaten a great
many in battle, killer of your enemies. I have never felt any fear before, at
any time. How much less now, hero, when I come to this battle no bigger than a
cow's hoofprint? Long may you live. By which way shall I take you to Dhananjaya?
Man of the Vrishnis, at whom are you angry? Whose death has come? Whose minds
are set on going today to the city of Yama? Who will see you in battle, full of
courage and like Death and Yama at the end of time, and run from the fight? Whom
is King Vaivasvata thinking of today, strong-armed one?'

"Satyaki said, 'I will kill these shaven-headed men as Vasava killed the
danavas. I will keep my promise. Take me to the Kambojas. When I have made a
slaughter of them today, I will go quickly to the Pandava. Today the Kauravas
and Suyodhana will see my courage, Suta, when the army of the shaven heads is
destroyed, and the whole army again and again. Today Suyodhana will hear the
many cries of the Kaurava army as it is torn apart in battle, and he will
grieve. Today, in battle, I will show the way that my teacher showed me, the
chief of the Pandavas, the great-souled one with the white horses. Today King
Duryodhana will see the chief fighters killed by my arrows in their thousands,
and he will be sorry. Today the Kauravas will see my bow, as I loose my fine
arrows with quick hands, like a whirling wheel of fire. When he sees his
soldiers killed, their bodies heaped with my arrows and pouring blood, Suyodhana
will grieve. Today, when I am angry and kill the best of the best, Suyodhana
will think there are two Arjunas in this world. Today, in the great battle, when
he sees thousands of kings killed by me, King Duryodhana will grieve. By killing
thousands of kings today, I will show the kings my love and devotion for the
great-souled Pandavas.'

"When he had been told this," said Sanjaya, "the charioteer urged on the horses
hard, well trained and good at the pull, the colour of the moon. Those fine
horses, as fast as the mind and the wind, seemed to drink up the sky, and they
quickly brought Yuyudhana to the Yavanas.

"Satyaki never turned back in battle. The Yavanas came up to him, many of them,
quick-handed, and covered him with rains of arrows. With his smooth-jointed
arrows Satyaki cut their arrows and weapons at speed, king, and their arrows did
not reach him. Fierce as he was, with very sharp, straight arrows, feathered
with gold and with vultures' feathers, he cut off the heads of the Yavanas, and
their arms too. His arrows split the armour of iron and of bronze on every side,
went through their bodies and into the ground. Struck by the hero Satyaki in the
battle, the mlecchas fell there on the ground by hundreds, lifeless. With arrows
loosed from a bow drawn full and wide, in streams unbroken and packed close, he
split the Yavanas five, six, seven and eight at a time.

"There, lord of the people, Shaineya was wearing down your army. With the
Kambojas in their thousands, and the Shakas, the Shabaras, the Kiratas and the
Barbaras, he made the earth impassable, a mire of flesh and blood. Here and
there the ground was strewn with the heads of those Dasyus, in their helmets,
with their hair shorn, like birds without feathers. With headless trunks, their
limbs all drenched in blood, the whole field of battle looked like the sky
covered with copper-coloured clouds. They were killed with their horses and
their carriages by straight, smooth-jointed arrows whose touch was like the
thunderbolt, and they covered the earth.

"Few were left, great king. Broken, barely alive and out of their senses, the
armoured men were beaten in battle by Yuyudhana. They beat their horses with
heels and whips, and at the greatest speed they could find they fled on every
side in fear. The Kamboja army was hard to beat in battle, Bharata, but he
routed it, and the army of the Yavanas, and the great force of the Shakas. Then
Satyaki, the tiger among men, whose courage was true, was full of joy at having
beaten your men, and he urged on his charioteer: 'Go!' Seeing him go on to guard
Arjuna's back, lord of the people, the charanas, the wandering singers of the
gods, were full of joy and honoured him, and so did your own men.

"Having beaten the Yavanas and the Kambojas," said Sanjaya, "Yuyudhana, best of
chariot fighters, went on towards Arjuna through the middle of your army. The
tiger among men, with arrows for teeth and his armour shining in many colours,
frightened your army as a tiger frightens deer when it scents them. As he went
on his roads in his chariot, he whirled his bow hard, a bow backed with gold,
very swift, crowded with golden moons. His armlets and helmet were gold, he was
covered in golden armour, and he had a fine golden banner, and the hero shone
like the peak of Meru. With his bow drawn to a circle in battle, and rays
shining with his splendour, he shone like the autumn sun risen, a sun among men.
With the shoulders and the stride of a bull, with a bull's eyes, the bull among
men shone in the midst of your men like a bull in the midst of the cows.

"He stood like a rutting elephant in the midst of the herd, and he looked like a
rutting elephant and moved like one. Your men ran at him in battle like tigers
wanting to kill. He had passed Drona's division and the Bhoja division, hard to
cross. He had crossed the sea of Jalasandha and the host of the Kambojas. He had
got free of the crocodile that was Hardikya and crossed the sea of the army.
Then your chariot fighters surrounded Satyaki in great anger. Duryodhana,
Chitrasena, Duhshasana and Vivimshati, Shakuni and Duhsaha, and young
Durmarshana and Kratha, and many other brave men with weapons, hard to face, ran
after Satyaki from behind as he went, unable to bear it.

"A great noise went up from your army, sir, like the noise of the sea at the
change of the moon, when the wind whips it up. Seeing them all running at him,
the bull of the Shinis said to his charioteer, as if laughing, 'Go slowly.

"'This army of Dhritarashtra's son is rising up and coming quickly straight at
me, with elephants, horses, chariots and foot soldiers. It fills all the
quarters with the roar of its chariots, charioteer, and it shakes the earth, the
sky and the seas as well. I will hold back this sea of an army in the great
battle, my friend, as the shore holds back the ocean when it rises at the full
moon. See my courage, Suta, like Indra's in a great battle. I will scatter the
enemy's armies with sharp arrows. See the foot soldiers, horses, chariots and
elephants killed in battle by my arrows, which are like fire, their bodies and
their lives parted, in thousands.'

"While Satyaki, whose strength had no measure, was saying this, the soldiers
came up to him quickly, eager to fight, shouting, 'Kill! Charge! Stand! Look,
look!' As the heroes shouted, Satyaki killed three hundred horses with sharp
arrows, and four hundred elephants. The fight between him and those bowmen was
wild, and it destroyed men, like the battle of the gods and the asuras. The army
of your son, sir, like a mass of clouds, Shini's grandson met with arrows like
venomous snakes. Though he was covered with nets of arrows in the battle, great
king, the mighty man did not lose his head, and he killed many of your men.

"I saw a very great wonder there, lord of kings. Not one of Satyaki's arrows was
wasted, my lord. The great sea of the army, thick with chariots, elephants and
horses and churned by waves of foot soldiers, came up against the shore that was
Shaineya and stood still. That army, its men, elephants and horses all confused,
was struck on every side by his arrows. It turned round and round again, and
wandered here and there like cows hurt by the cold. I saw no foot soldier there,
no chariot, elephant, rider or horse that Yuyudhana's arrows had not pierced.
Phalguna had made no slaughter there like the destruction of the armies that
Satyaki made, king. Shini's grandson was fighting beyond Arjuna, bull of the
Bharatas.

"Then King Duryodhana pierced the Satvata's charioteer with three sharp arrows,
and his four horses with four. He pierced Satyaki with three, and again with
eight. Duhshasana pierced the bull of the Shinis with sixteen, Shakuni with
twenty-five and Chitrasena with five, and Duhsaha pierced Satyaki in the chest
with fifteen. Struck by those arrows, the tiger of the Vrishnis smiled and
pierced every one of them with three each, great king. He wounded his enemies
deeply with very sharp arrows, and Shaineya moved about the battle like a hawk,
light and quick. He cut Saubala's bow and cut off his hand guard, and pierced
Duryodhana between the breasts with three arrows. The bull of the Shinis pierced
Chitrasena with a hundred, Duhsaha with ten and Duhshasana with twenty.

"Then your brother-in-law took another bow, lord of the people, and pierced
Satyaki with eight arrows, and again with five. Duhshasana pierced Satyaki with
ten, Duhsaha with three and Durmukha with twelve, king. Duryodhana pierced
Madhava with seventy-three, Bharata, and then pierced his charioteer with three
sharp arrows. All those brave great warriors were together and doing their
utmost, and Satyaki pierced each of them again with five arrows. Then the best
of chariot fighters quickly struck your son's charioteer with a broad arrow, and
he fell to the ground, dead.

"When the charioteer fell, my lord, your son's chariot was carried out of the
battle by his horses, racing like the wind. Then your sons, king, and your
soldiers, lord of the people, saw the king's chariot and fled by hundreds.
Seeing that army fleeing, Bharata, Satyaki covered it with sharp arrows
feathered with gold and whetted on stone. When he had routed all your armies on
every side, king, Satyaki went on towards the chariot of the man with the white
horses. Your own men honoured him as he took up his arrows, guarded his
charioteer and kept himself safe."

Dhritarashtra said, "My sons have no shame. When they saw Shaineya crushing the
great army and going on to Arjuna, what did they do, Sanjaya? How could they
keep their nerve in battle, when they were about to die, after they had seen
Shaineya's deeds, which were like the Left-Handed Archer's? What will they say
of the warrior's code, beaten in the middle of their army? And how did Satyaki,
whose fame is great, get past them in battle? How did Shaineya go on through the
battle while my sons were alive? Tell me truly, Sanjaya.

"This is a great wonder that I hear from you, my friend: the fight of one man
against many enemies, great warriors all. I think things have turned against my
unlucky sons, since the great-souled Satvata killed them in battle. My army is
not enough for that one man, Sanjaya, for Yuyudhana in his anger. Let all the
Pandavas stand aside. Once he has beaten Drona in battle, skilled and wild in
war as he is, he will kill my sons as a lion kills herds of beasts. Many brave
men, Kritavarma and the others, did their utmost in battle and could not kill
Yuyudhana, that bull among men. Phalguna did not fight such a fight there as the
famous grandson of Shini fought."

Sanjaya said, "This comes of your bad counsel, king, and of what Duryodhana has
done. Listen carefully to what I tell you, Bharata.

"At your son's command they turned back again, binding one another by oath, and
set their minds on the fight. There were three thousand horsemen with Duryodhana
at their head: Shakas, Kambojas, Bahlikas, Yavanas and Paradas, Kunindas,
Tanganas and Ambashthas, Paishachas and Mandaras. They ran at Shaineya like
moths at a fire. Five hundred brave men of the mountains, who fight with stones,
came at Shaineya in chariots made ready, king. Then with a thousand chariots, a
hundred great warriors, a thousand elephants and two thousand horses, the great
warriors ran at Shaineya, loosing rains of arrows of every kind, and foot
soldiers beyond counting. Duhshasana urged them all on, Bharata. 'Kill him!' he
said, great king, and he surrounded Satyaki.

"There we saw a great wonder in what Shaineya did, for he fought alone against
many without losing his head. He destroyed that force of chariots and that army
of elephants, and all those horsemen, and the Dasyus on every side. The earth
there was strewn with smashed wheels and broken fine weapons, with axles broken
in many pieces and poles and shafts and yokes, with crushed yoke poles and
fallen banners, with armour and fly whisks. The earth was covered there, sir,
with garlands, ornaments and clothes and chariot beams, as the sky is with
planets, Bharata.

"Fine elephants had fallen too, like hills. Many fine tuskers lay there dead,
king, born in the line of Anjana and in the line of Vamana, Bharata, in the line
of Supratika and the line of Mahapadma, in the line of Airavana and in other
lines. Satyaki killed fine horses there, king: Vanayu horses and mountain
horses, Kamboja, Aratta and Bahlika horses. Shaineya killed foot soldiers there
too, by hundreds and thousands, from many lands and of many races.

"As they were being driven off, Duhshasana said to the Dasyus, 'Turn back, you
who know nothing of dharma! Fight! What good is running to you?' Your son
Duhshasana saw them all, and urged on the brave mountain men who fight with
stones. 'You are skilled in fighting with stones,' he said. 'Satyaki does not
know it. Kill him. He does not know how to fight with stones, though he is eager
for battle. And none of the Kurus is skilled in fighting with stones either.
Charge, and do not be afraid. Satyaki will not reach you.'

"Then the men who live in the mountains stood facing Yuyudhana with stones like
young elephants raised in their hands, bent on death. Others, urged on by your
son, wanting to kill the Satvata, blocked him on every side with slings. As they
came on, wanting to fight with rocks, Satyaki fitted thirty arrows and sent them
at them. With iron arrows like snakes, the bull of the Shinis broke up the wild
rain of stones that the mountain men had loosed. The stone dust blazed like
swarms of fireflies, and it killed most of those troops, sir, and they cried out
in pain. Then five hundred brave men with great rocks raised fell to the ground,
king, their arms cut off. He killed many thousands of the brave stone fighters
as they stood there doing their utmost, and it seemed a wonder.

"Then again a rain of stones fell on every side, loosed by men with goats'
faces, by men with iron in their hands and spears in their hands, by Daradas,
Khashas and Tanganas, Ambashthas and Kunindas. As each rain was loosed, mighty
Satyaki watched it and pierced it with his iron arrows. At the sound of the
rocks shattering in the air under his sharp arrows, king, the elephants, horses,
chariots and foot soldiers ran. Men and birds, covered with the stone dust,
could not stay where they were, as if they had been stung by bees. The elephants
that were left alive, bloodied, their heads and the domes of their foreheads
split, turned back from Yuyudhana's chariot.

"Then a noise went up from your army, sir, as Madhava pressed it, as dreadful as
the noise of the sea. Hearing that wild noise, Drona said to his charioteer,
'Suta, that great warrior of the Satvatas is angry in the battle. He is tearing
the army apart in many places and moving through the battle like Time. Take the
chariot, Suta, to where that wild noise is. Yuyudhana must have met the men who
fight with stones. That is why all the chariot fighters are being carried off by
their bolting horses. They are falling here and there, wounded, without weapons
or armour, and in the uproar the drivers cannot hold their horses.'

"As wise Bharadvaja's son said this, king, the charioteer answered Drona, best
of all who bear weapons. 'Long may you live. The Kaurava army is running on
every side. See the fighters broken in battle, running this way and that. And
these brave Panchalas, together with the Pandavas, are running at you from every
side, wanting to kill you. Settle here what must be done, now that the time has
come, tamer of your enemies: whether to stand or to go. Satyaki has gone far.'

"While Bharadvaja's son was speaking like this, sir, Shaineya came into view,
killing chariot fighters of many kinds. Your men, struck down in battle by
Yuyudhana, left Yuyudhana's chariot and ran towards Drona's division. And the
chariot fighters with whom Duhshasana had turned back before all ran in fear
towards Drona's chariot."

**Divergences.**

* **Satyaki's list of victims.** \*661, a long list of peoples Satyaki has killed (Nishadas, Kalingas, Magadhas, Kekayas, Shibis, Kosalas, yatudhanas and more) and a river of blood, is rejected. So is \*660, which adds that he beat Kritavarma and Drona.
* **The stone fighters.** \*697 would add that a thousand, and then a hundred thousand, stone fighters fell with their arms cut off before they could reach Satyaki; the CE has five hundred (7.97.37) and then "many thousands" (7.97.38). \*694–\*696 and \*698 are also rejected.
* **The Yavanas' cry.** \*673, in which the mighty Yavanas all shout "kebe", is rejected.
* **The elephants' names.** \*692, a list of elephant kinds (mriga, manda, bhadra and their mixtures), and \*693 (elephants of Kumuda's line) are rejected. The CE names the lines of Anjana, Vamana, Supratika, Mahapadma and Airavana (7.97.24–25).
* **The charanas and gandharvas.** \*676, in which the charanas and gandharvas honour a deed "never done before by others", is rejected. The CE has the charanas and Dhritarashtra's own men honour him (7.95.47).
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*662–\*672, \*674–\*675, \*677–\*691, \*699–\*701.

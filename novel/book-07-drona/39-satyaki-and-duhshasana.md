summary: Drona shames Duhshasana for running from Satyaki alone, reminding him of the dice and of Draupadi's torment and telling him to make peace or fight. Duhshasana says nothing and goes back to Satyaki. Drona kills the Panchala prince Viraketu and his four brothers; Dhrishtadyumna, weeping, leaves Drona senseless and climbs onto his chariot with a sword, but Drona drives him off with short arrows and kills his charioteer. Satyaki breaks the Trigarta chariots and leaves Duhshasana without bow, chariot or driver, but does not kill him, remembering Bhima's vow. Dhritarashtra asks how one man got through. Sanjaya tells of the Pandavas' charge, of Duryodhana alone against them, of Yudhishthira cutting his bow, and of Drona meeting the Panchalas.

# Satyaki and Duhshasana

"Bharadvaja's son saw Duhshasana's chariot drawn up near him," said Sanjaya,
"and he spoke to Duhshasana.

"'Duhshasana, why are all these chariots running? Is the king safe? Is the
Saindhava alive? You are a king's son here, a king's brother and a great
warrior. Why do you run from the battle, when you have the rank of heir? You
made the great feud yourself with the Panchalas and the Pandavas. How can you be
afraid in battle when you meet only Satyaki? When you took up the dice in the
gaming hall long ago, did you not know that they would become these arrows, like
dreadful venomous snakes? The cruel words to the Pandavas above all, and the
torment of Draupadi, had their root in you. Where is your pride now, and your
arrogance, and where is that roaring, hero? Where will you go, now that you have
angered the sons of Pritha, who are like venomous snakes?

"'This army of the Bharatas is to be pitied, and King Suyodhana too, if you, his
hard brother, are set on running away. Surely it was for you, hero, to guard the
army with the strength of your own arms as it was torn apart and hurt by fear.
Yet today you leave the battle in fear and give joy to the enemy. When you, the
army's leader, run away, killer of your enemies, who else will stand in the
battle? When the one they lean on is afraid, they will be afraid. You are
fighting only one man of the Satvatas today, blameless one, and your mind turns
to running from the battle. When you see the one with the Gandiva bow in battle,
Kaurava, and Bhimasena and the twins, what will you do then? Satyaki's arrows,
which you are running from in fear, are not equal to Phalguna's arrows in
battle, which blaze like the sun and fire.

"'If your mind is made up to run, then let the earth be given to the king of
dharma in peace. Make peace with the Pandavas before Phalguna's iron arrows,
like snakes that have shed their skins, go into your body. Make peace with the
Pandavas before the great-souled sons of Pritha kill your hundred brothers in
battle and take the earth from you. Make peace with the Pandavas before King
Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, grows angry, and Krishna, who glories in
battle. Make peace with the Pandavas before strong-armed Bhima plunges into the
great army and crushes your brothers.

"'Bhishma told your brother Suyodhana long ago, "The Pandavas cannot be beaten
in battle. Make peace with the Pandavas, my dear." But your brother Suyodhana
was a fool and did not do it. So set your mind firmly on battle, and fight the
Pandavas with all your care. Go quickly, in your chariot, to where Satyaki
stands. Without you this army will run, Bharata. For your own sake, fight
Satyaki in battle, whose courage is true.'

"Your son said nothing at all to this. He acted as if he had not heard what he
had heard, and went the way Satyaki had gone. With a great army of mlecchas who
never turned back, he met Yuyudhana in battle and fought him with all his care.

"Drona, best of chariot fighters, in great anger, went at the Panchalas and the
Pandavas at a middle pace. He went into the Panchalas' army in the battle and
drove off their fighters by hundreds and thousands. Then, great king, Drona
called out his name in the battle and made a great slaughter of the Pandavas,
the Panchalas and the Matsyas.

"As Bharadvaja's son was beating the armies here and there, a shining son of the
Panchala king, Viraketu, came at him. He pierced Drona with five smooth-jointed
arrows, pierced his banner with one and his charioteer with seven. I saw a
wonder there in that battle, great king: Drona did not move against the fierce
Panchala prince. The Panchalas saw Drona held back in the battle, sir, and
surrounded him on every side, king, wanting victory for the son of Dharma. With
arrows like fire, costly lances and weapons of many kinds, king, they covered
Drona, who was alone. Drona destroyed those flights of arrows on every side,
king, as the wind blowing in the sky scatters great rainclouds.

"Then the killer of enemy heroes fitted an arrow, terrible, like the sun and
fire, and aimed it at Viraketu's chariot. The arrow split the Panchala prince,
king, the joy of his family, and went quickly into the ground, wet with blood,
as if blazing. The Panchala prince, the joy of his family, fell quickly from his
chariot, like a great champaka tree falling from a mountain top, struck by the
wind.

"When that mighty prince and great bowman was killed, the Panchalas quickly
surrounded Drona on every side. Chitraketu, Sudhanvan, Chitravarman and
Chitraratha, Bharata, worn by grief for their brother, ran together at
Bharadvaja's son, eager to fight, loosing rains of arrows like clouds at the end
of the hot season. Struck many times by those princes, those great warriors,
Drona grew angry in battle and left the young men without horses, drivers or
chariots. Then with other very sharp broad arrows the famous man struck off
their heads, as if he were picking flowers. Killed, they fell from their
chariots to the ground, king, shining, like the daityas and danavas long ago in
the battle of the gods and the asuras. When he had killed them in battle, king,
Bharadvaja's mighty son whirled his bow, backed with gold and hard to face.

"Dhrishtadyumna saw the Panchalas killed, great warriors like gods, and he grew
very angry. With tears falling from his eyes, in his anger he went in the battle
at Drona's chariot. Then all at once a cry of 'Alas!' went up, king, when they
saw Drona covered with arrows by the Panchala prince in the battle. Covered many
times over by great-souled Parshata, Drona was not troubled. He fought back,
smiling.

"Then the Panchala prince, beside himself with anger, struck Drona in the chest
with ninety smooth-jointed arrows, great king. Pierced deeply by the strong man,
Bharadvaja's famous son sank down on the floor of his chariot and fainted.
Seeing him like that, mighty Dhrishtadyumna quickly threw down his bow and
seized a sword. The great warrior leapt quickly from his chariot and climbed
onto Bharadvaja's chariot, sir, with his eyes red with anger, wanting to cut the
head from the body.

"Then mighty Drona came to himself and took his bow, king, and in the battle he
fought the great warrior Dhrishtadyumna with span-long arrows, which are always
used for fighting at close quarters. For Drona knew those arrows called
span-long, king, made for fighting close, and they drove Dhrishtadyumna back.
Struck by many of those arrows, the mighty man leapt quickly from the chariot,
his force broken. The hero climbed back into his own chariot and took up his
great bow, and the great warrior Dhrishtadyumna pierced Drona in the battle.

"The hosts of beings honoured that wonderful fight between the two of them,
great king, and so did the kshatriyas and the other soldiers there. 'Surely
Drona has met Dhrishtadyumna in battle and will fall into the power of our
king,' the Panchalas shouted. But Drona, quickly, in the battle, struck the head
from Dhrishtadyumna's charioteer like a ripe fruit from a tree. Then the great
man's horses bolted, king. As they bolted, mighty Drona drove off the Panchalas
and the Srinjayas in the battle here and there. When he had beaten the Pandavas
and the Panchalas, the mighty son of Bharadvaja, tamer of his enemies, took his
place in his own array again and stood firm. And the Pandavas did not dare to
try to beat him in battle, my lord.

"Then Duhshasana ran at Shaineya, king, scattering thousands of arrows like a
raincloud pouring rain. He pierced Satyaki with sixty arrows, and then with
sixteen, but could not shake him as he stood in the battle, any more than the
mountain Mainaka. And the hero covered Duhshasana thickly with arrows, as a
spider covers a mosquito that has come near with its web.

"When the king saw Duhshasana covered with hundreds of arrows like that, he
urged the Trigartas on against Yuyudhana's chariot. They came up to Yuyudhana,
cruel in their deeds: three thousand Trigarta chariots, skilled in war. They
surrounded him with a great line of chariots, set their minds firmly on battle
and swore oaths to one another. As they strove in the battle, loosing rains of
arrows, he struck down five hundred of their chief fighters at the front of
their army. Killed by the arrows of the chief of the Shinis, they fell quickly,
like great trees broken by the force of a great wind. The earth there, lord of
the people, with its chariots cut to pieces and banners, and horses in golden
headdresses, fallen, cut apart by Shaineya's arrows and flooded with blood,
shone, great king, as if with flowering kimshuka trees. Struck down in the
battle by Yuyudhana, your men found no one to save them, like elephants sunk in
the mud. Then they all turned back towards Drona's chariot, like great snakes
making for their holes in fear of the king of birds.

"When he had killed five hundred fighters with arrows like venomous snakes, the
hero went on slowly towards Dhananjaya's chariot. As the best of men went, your
son Duhshasana quickly pierced him with nine smooth-jointed arrows. The great
bowman pierced him back with five sharp, straight arrows with golden shafts and
vultures' feathers. Duhshasana, as if laughing, great king, pierced Satyaki with
three arrows, Bharata, and again with five. Shaineya pierced your son with five
swift arrows, cut his bow in the battle, and went on to Arjuna, smiling.

"Then, in anger, wanting to kill him, Duhshasana hurled a spear all of iron at
the Vrishni hero as he went. But Satyaki cut your son's dreadful spear into a
hundred pieces, king, with sharp heron-feathered arrows. Then your son took
another bow, lord of men, pierced Satyaki with ten arrows and roared a lion's
roar. Satyaki, angry in the battle, left your son dazed and struck him between
the breasts with arrows like tongues of fire, and pierced him again with eight
sharp-pointed arrows all of iron. Duhshasana pierced Satyaki back with twenty,
and the Satvata, great king, pierced him between the breasts with just three
very swift smooth-jointed arrows.

"Then the great warrior, in great anger, killed his horses with sharp arrows,
and his charioteer with smooth-jointed arrows. He cut his bow with a single
broad arrow and his hand guard with five. Knowing the highest weapons, he cut
his banner and his chariot spear with two broad arrows, and with sharp arrows he
cut down both his flank drivers. With his bow cut, his chariot lost, his horses
and driver killed, Duhshasana was carried away by the commander of the Trigarta
army in his own chariot. Shaineya ran after him for a moment, Bharata, but the
strong-armed man did not kill him, for he remembered Bhimasena's words. For
Bhimasena had vowed in the middle of the assembly hall, Bharata, to kill all
your sons in battle. So Satyaki beat Duhshasana in battle, my lord, and went on
quickly, king, the way Dhananjaya had gone."

Dhritarashtra said, "Were there no great warriors in that army of mine, then, to
kill Satyaki as he went, or even to stop him? He did those deeds alone in
battle, whose courage is true, with strength like Shakra's, like great Indra
among the danavas. Or was the way that Satyaki took empty, as that bull among
men, alone, crushed so many armies? How did Shaineya get away, one against many
great-souled men who were fighting? Tell me that, Sanjaya."

Sanjaya said, "King, the muster of your army, with chariots, elephants, horses
and foot soldiers, was as wild as the end of an age. Of all the daily musters of
your army, giver of honour, I think there was never one like it in the world.
The gods and the charanas gathered there said, 'These will be the last musters
on the earth.' There was never an array like the one Drona made for the killing
of Jayadratha, lord of the people. As the masses of troops ran at one another in
battle, their noise was like the noise of the seas struck by a fierce wind. Many
kings were gathered in your army and in the Pandavas', best of men, by hundreds
and by thousands. The angry heroes were firm in their deeds in battle, and there
was a great and wild noise that made the hair stand on end.

"Then Bhimasena cried out, sir, and Dhrishtadyumna, Nakula and Sahadeva, and
Yudhishthira the Pandava, the king of dharma. 'Come on! Strike! Charge with all
your strength! The two heroes, Madhava and the Pandava, have gone into the enemy
army. Act quickly, so that they can go on easily to kill Jayadratha.' So they
urged on the armies. 'If those two are lost, the Kurus will have got what they
want, and we will be beaten. So all of you together, quickly, stir up this sea
of an army, as great winds with all their force stir up the ocean.'

"Urged on by Bhimasena and the Panchala prince, king, they struck the Kauravas
in battle, giving up their own dear lives. Men of the highest energy, they
wanted death in battle by weapons, and for the sake of heaven and for their
friends' cause they did not guard their lives. In the same way your men, king,
seeking great fame, set their minds on the noble way of battle and stood up only
to fight.

"In that wild and fearful battle, Satyaki killed all the armies in his way and
went on to Arjuna. The gleam of armour there, lit up with the sun's rays, struck
back the eyes of the soldiers in the battle on every side. And while the sons of
Pandu were striving like that, great king, Duryodhana plunged fearlessly into
their great army. That clash between them and him was wild, Bharata, and a great
destruction of all the armies."

Dhritarashtra said, "When the armies were in that state, and he himself was in
such trouble, surely Duryodhana did not turn his back in the battle, Suta? The
clash of one against many in a great battle seems uneven to me, and above all
for a king. He was brought up in the greatest comfort, lord of fortune and of
the world. Surely he did not turn away, one man facing many?"

Sanjaya said, "King, Bharata, listen as I tell of your son's battle, a wonder,
one against many. Duryodhana all at once churned up the Pandava army in the
battle on every side, as an elephant churns up a lotus pool. When they saw the
army treated like that by your son, Kaurava, the Panchalas ran at him with
Bhimasena at their head.

"He pierced Bhimasena with ten arrows and each of Madri's sons with three,
Virata and Drupada with six, Shikhandi with a hundred, Dhrishtadyumna with
twenty and the son of Dharma with seven. He pierced the Kekayas with ten and
each of Draupadi's sons with three. In his anger he cut down other fighters by
hundreds in the battle, and chariots with elephants, with fierce arrows, as
Death cuts down living things. He was not seen fitting his arrows or loosing
them. With his bow drawn to a circle, he was seen only killing his enemies, by
his training and the power of his weapons.

"As he was killing his enemies, the eldest Pandava cut his great bow, backed
with gold, in three pieces with two broad arrows, sir. Then he pierced him with
many sharp, well-aimed arrows. But they reached his armour and quickly broke,
and fell into the ground. Then the sons of Pritha, full of joy, surrounded
Yudhishthira, as the gods with the great seers surrounded Shakra in their joy at
the killing of Vritra.

"Then King Duryodhana took up a strong bow and went at the Pandava king,
shouting, 'Stand, stand!' As your son, the great warrior, shouted this, king,
the Panchalas went out to meet him, full of joy and hungry for victory. Drona
met them, wanting to take the Pandava in battle, as a mountain meets rainclouds
full of water, driven by a fierce wind. There, king, a great battle grew and
grew. It was like the playground of Rudra, a destruction of all living beings."

**Divergences.**

* **Drona's rebuke.** \*702, in which Drona quotes Duhshasana's words to Draupadi at the dice ("you are a slave won at dice… your husbands are no better than sesame seeds without kernels"), is rejected. The CE has Drona say only that the cruel words to the Pandavas and "the torment of Draupadi" had their root in him (7.98.6). \*705, "I have heard that Bhima will drink your blood too", is also rejected. \*703 (go back into Gandhari's womb) and \*704 are rejected.
* **Duhshasana's first attack.** \*717, a longer account of Duhshasana's chariots attacking and fleeing before 7.99.1, is rejected.
* **The noise where Arjuna was.** \*737, which closes the section with the uproar round Arjuna and a summary of the three battles (Arjuna's, Satyaki's and Drona's at the gate of the array), is rejected. Ganguli has it.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*706–\*716, \*718–\*736, \*738.

**Choices.**

* 7.98.3 *yauvarājyam avāpya*: "when you have the rank of heir", the place of *yuvarāja*, the heir apparent.
* 7.98.5 *durodare*: "in the gaming hall".
* 7.98.24 *javam āsthāya madhyamam*: "at a middle pace".
* 7.98.26 *nāma viśrāvya*: "called out his name".
* 7.98.27 *pāñcālaputro … vīraketuḥ*: "a son of the Panchala king, Viraketu". 7.98.34–36 call him *pāñcālyaṃ kulanandanam* and *rājaputre*. The CE does not say which Panchala king is his father.
* 7.98.29 *yad droṇo rabhasaṃ yuddhe pāñcālyaṃ nābhyavartata*: "Drona did not move against the fierce Panchala prince", which the next verse calls Drona being "held back" (*saṃniruddham*). Ganguli: "could not approach".
* 7.98.32 *mātariśvā vivān iva*: "as the wind blowing in the sky".
* 7.98.37 *bhrātṛvyasanakarṣitāḥ*: "worn by grief for their brother", Viraketu. The four are his brothers.
* 7.98.50–51 *śarair vaitastikaiḥ … āsannayodhibhiḥ*: "span-long arrows … for fighting at close quarters". A *vitasti* is a span.
* 7.98.55: the Panchalas' shout, *vaśam eṣyati no rājñaḥ*, "will fall into the power of our king".
* 7.99.2 *mainākam iva parvatam*: "the mountain Mainaka".
* 7.99.6 *bhūtvā saṃśaptakā mithaḥ*: "swore oaths to one another", as at 7.97.12.
* 7.99.10 *kiṃśukaiḥ*: "kimshuka trees", the flame-of-the-forest, with red flowers.
* 7.99.12 *patagarājasya*: "the king of birds", Garuda.
* 7.99.17 *vismayan*: "smiling". Ganguli: "smilingly".
* 7.99.18 *sarvapāraśavīṃ śaktim*: "a spear all of iron".
* 7.99.21 *mohayitvā*: "left your son dazed".
* 7.99.24 *pārṣṇisārathī*: "both his flank drivers", as at 7.20.14.

* 7.100.39 *bhūrivardhanaḥ*: "grew and grew". *rudrasyākrīḍasaṃkāśaḥ*: "like the playground of Rudra".

summary: Satyaki answers that he would fight the three worlds for Arjuna, but that Arjuna left the king in his keeping, since Drona has vowed to take him and only Pradyumna or Satyaki can match Drona. If Yudhishthira is captured, they will all go back to the forest. Yudhishthira answers that he is uneasy about Arjuna and names the heroes who will guard him, above all Dhrishtadyumna, born from the fire to kill Drona. Afraid that people will say he held back out of fear, Satyaki agrees. He names the forces he must break: the elephants of the Anjana line with their mleccha riders, the Rukmaratha princes, the Kirata elephants that were once Yudhishthira's own, and the Kambojas. His horses are rested and watered and his chariot fully loaded. He bathes, gives gold to a thousand brahmins, drinks Kailavata liquor, touches the heroes' bronze vessel and bows at Yudhishthira's feet. He sends Bhima back to guard the king and drives into the Kaurava army.

# Yudhishthira Sends Satyaki

"The King of Dharma's words were full of love, pleasing to the heart and sweet
in sound, fitting the moment, fine, and spoken as to one of his own," said
Sanjaya. "When the bull of the Shinis, Satyaki, had heard them, best of the
Bharatas, he answered Yudhishthira.

"'I have heard all you have said, Achyuta. It is just, and fine, and it brings
fame, for Phalguna's sake. At a time like this, seeing a man like me held in
honour, you should speak to me as you would to Partha, king of kings. I would
never guard my life in any way for Dhananjaya's sake. And sent by you, what
would I not do in a great battle? Sent by you, lord of men, I would fight the
three worlds, gods, asuras and men, let alone this very weak army. I will fight
Suyodhana's army today on every side and beat it in battle, king. I tell you the
truth. Safe myself, I will reach Dhananjaya safe, and when Jayadratha is killed,
king, I will come back to you.

"'But I must tell you everything, lord of men, what Vasudeva said and what wise
Phalguna said. Arjuna charged me firmly, again and again, in the middle of the
whole army, while Vasudeva listened: "Today, Madhava, guard the king with care.
Set a noble purpose in your mind for battle, until I kill Jayadratha. I leave
the king with you, strong-armed one, or with the great warrior Pradyumna, and
then I can go against Jayadratha without looking back. You know Drona in battle,
fierce and held to be the best. And you have heard Drona's vow, Madhava.
Bharadvaja's son is set on capturing the King of Dharma, and Drona is able to
take Yudhishthira in battle. So I place the King of Dharma, best of men, in your
care, and today I will go to kill the Saindhava.

"'"I will surely kill Jayadratha and come back, Madhava. But Drona might take
the King of Dharma by force in battle. If the best of men were captured by
Bharadvaja's son, Madhava, the Saindhava would not be killed, and I would be
full of grief. If that happened, best of men, to the truthful Pandava, we would
clearly go back to the forest. This victory of mine would clearly be in vain, if
Drona in his anger took Yudhishthira in battle. So today, strong-armed Madhava,
for my sake, for victory and for fame, guard the king in battle."

"'So you are the trust the Left-Handed Archer has placed in my keeping, lord,
for he always sees the danger from Bharadvaja's son. He always sees that there
is no one to fight Bharadvaja's wise son in battle, strong-armed lord, except
Rukmini's son, or else me. I cannot put this trust and my teacher's word behind
me, lord of the earth, or leave you. The teacher, with his quick hands and his
armour that cannot be broken, would get hold of you in battle and play with you
as a child plays with a bird. If Krishna's son, with the fish on his banner,
were here with his bow in his hand, I would leave you to him, and he would guard
you as Arjuna would.

"'You must see to your own safety. Who will guard you when I have gone, and face
Drona in battle while I go to the Pandava? Do not fear for Arjuna today, king.
The strong-armed man never sinks once he has taken up a load. Think of the
Sauvira fighters, the Saindhavas and Pauravas, the northerners and the
southerners, and the other great warriors. Think of those famous chariot
fighters led by Karna, king. They are not worth a sixteenth part of Arjuna in
his anger. The whole earth might rise up, king, with gods, asuras and men, with
the hosts of rakshasas, the Kinnaras and the great snakes, with all that moves
and all that stands still. It would not be a match for Partha in battle. Knowing
this, great king, let your fear for Dhananjaya go. Where the two brave Krishnas
are, great bowmen whose courage is true, nothing can go wrong with the work.

"'Think of your brother's fortune, his mastery of weapons, his skill, his fury
in battle, his gratitude and his compassion. And think, king, of Drona's
wonderful weapons in battle once I have gone off to Arjuna. The teacher is set
hard on capturing you, king, to keep his vow and make it true, Bharata. See to
your own safety today. Who will guard you when I have gone, so that I can trust
him and go to Phalguna, Partha? I will not go anywhere, great king, without
leaving you in someone's care in this great battle, Kauravya. I tell you the
truth. Think this over many times with your mind, best of the wise. When you
have seen with your mind what is best, then command me, king.'

"Yudhishthira said, 'It is as you say, strong-armed Madhava. But my heart is not
easy about the man with the white horses, sir. I will take the greatest care to
guard myself. You have my leave. Go where Dhananjaya has gone. I have weighed
the two in my mind, guarding myself in battle and your going to Arjuna, and I
choose that you go. Get ready to set out for where Dhananjaya has gone. Mighty
Bhima will guard me. Prishata's grandson with his brothers, the mighty kings and
the sons of Draupadi will guard me, my son, without doubt. The five Kekaya
brothers, the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, Virata and Drupada, the great warrior
Shikhandi, mighty Dhrishtaketu and Kuntibhoja, sir, Nakula and Sahadeva, the
Panchalas and the Srinjayas are all ready. They will guard me, my son, without
doubt.

"'Neither Drona with his army nor Kritavarma will be able to reach me in battle,
or to overpower me. Dhrishtadyumna, scorcher of enemies, will boldly hold back
angry Drona in battle, as the shore holds back the sea. Wherever Prishata's
grandson, killer of enemy heroes, stands in battle, Drona's army, strong as it
is, will never get past. He was born from the fire to destroy Drona, in his
armour, with arrows, sword and bow, and fine ornaments. Go without fear,
Shaineya. Do not be anxious for me. Dhrishtadyumna, angry in battle, will hold
Drona back.'

"The bull of the Shinis heard the King of Dharma's words. He feared Partha's
blame for leaving the king, and above all he wanted to guard himself against
blame from the world, so that people would not say, as he came to Phalguna, 'He
was afraid.' Satyaki, fierce in battle, weighed it many ways. Then he said these
words to the King of Dharma, bull among men:

"'If you think your guard is set, bless you, lord of the people, I will follow
Bibhatsu and do as you say. There is no one in the three worlds dearer to me
than the Pandava, king. I tell you the truth. I will go along his track at your
command, giver of honour. For your sake there is nothing at all I would not do.
As my teacher's word is special to me, best of men, so your word is more special
still. The two brothers, Krishna and the Pandava, are set on your good, and know
that I am set on theirs, bull among kings. I take your command on my head, lord,
and for the Pandava's sake I will break this army that is so hard to break, and
go, best of men.

"'I go into Drona's division like an angry fish into the sea. I will go where
King Jayadratha is, king, where he stands afraid of the Pandava, sheltering in
the army, guarded by the best of fine chariot fighters, Drona's son, Karna,
Kripa and the others. I think it is a road of three yojanas from here, lord of
the people, to where Partha stands, set on killing Jayadratha. Though he has
gone three yojanas, I will follow his track with a firm heart, king, until the
Saindhava is killed. What man would fight unbidden by his elder? And bidden by
you, king, who like me would not fight? I know the place where I will go, lord.
I will stir up that sea of an army, crowded with clubs, spears, maces, lances,
swords, shields, javelins and darts, arrows and fine weapons.

"'This mass of a thousand elephants you see, of the line called Anjanaka, full
of strength, is ridden by many mlecchas, eager for battle and hard strikers. The
elephants are like clouds, king, streaming like rainclouds. Once sent on by
their riders, they will never turn back. There is no beating them, king, except
by killing them. And the chariot fighters you see on every side, king, are the
princes called Rukmaratha, great warriors, skilled in chariots, in weapons and
on elephants, lord of the people. They have mastered the science of the bow and
know boxing, and are experts in the mace fight and skilled in wrestling. They
are trained in striking with the sword and in the clash of sword and shield.
They are brave and learned, and they rival each other, and in battle they always
want to beat men, king. Karna beat them, king, and they follow Duhshasana. Even
Vasudeva praises them as fine chariot fighters. They always want to please Karna
and are under his command. At his word, king, they turned back from the man with
the white horses. They are not wounded and not tired, with strong armour and
bows. They are surely waiting for me, at the order of Dhritarashtra's son. I
will crush them in battle for your sake, Kaurava, and then follow the track of
the Left-Handed Archer.

"'And there are those other seven hundred elephants you see, king, covered in
armour, ridden by Kiratas. The Kirata king gave them when the Left-Handed Archer
captured him, wanting to save his own life, along with servants in their finery.
They were once your servants, king, loyal ones. Today they want to fight you.
See the turning of Time. Their drivers are Kiratas, fierce in battle, who know
the training of elephants, all born of fire. All of them were beaten in battle
by the Left-Handed Archer. Today, under Duryodhana's command, they are ready for
me. I will break these Kiratas, fierce in battle, with my arrows, king, and
follow the Pandava, set on killing the Saindhava.

"'Then there are the great elephants of the Anjana line, harsh and trained, rut
streaming from their temples and mouths, all well adorned with golden armour,
sure of their mark in battle, king, like Airavana. They come from the northern
mountain and are ridden by fierce Dasyus, hard, fine fighters in armour of black
iron. Some are born of cows, some of monkeys, others of many wombs, and some of
human wombs. This smoke-coloured army of bad men is said to be the mlecchas,
doers of evil, who dwell in the strongholds of the Himalaya. Duryodhana has got
this whole ring of elephants, and Kripa and Somadatta's son and Drona, best of
chariot fighters, the Sindhu king and Karna. So he scorns the Pandavas, and
driven by Time, he thinks he has already won.

"'All of them have come within reach of my iron arrows. They will not get free,
son of Kunti, even if they are swift as thought. He always honours them, he who
lives on the strength of others, and they will be destroyed, crushed by the
floods of my arrows. And the chariot fighters you see with golden banners, king,
are Kambojas called Durvarana, if you have heard of them. They are brave and
learned and grounded in the science of the bow, bound tightly together, each
wanting the other's good. The akshauhinis of Dhritarashtra's son stand ready and
furious, Bharata, waiting for me, guarded by Kuru heroes. They are on their
guard, great king, and they have come out against me. I will crush them as a
fire crushes grass.

"'So let the chariot fitters put all the quivers and all the gear on my chariot
in the proper way, king. In this battle one must take weapons of every kind. Let
the chariot be made up five times over, as the teachers taught. I will meet the
Kambojas, furious as poisonous snakes, fighters with weapons of every kind and
loads of them. I will meet the Kiratas, like poison, hard strikers, always
spoiled by the king, who want Duryodhana's good. I will meet the Shakas too,
equal to Shakra in courage, like fire, hard to resist, like blazing flames. I
will meet in battle, king, many other fighters of many kinds, like Time, hard to
come at, fierce in battle. So let my best horses, rested and of good marks, be
rolled and watered and yoked to my chariot again.'

"The king had all the quivers, all the gear and weapons of every kind placed on
his chariot. Then men unyoked the four fine horses on every side and gave them a
tasty drink that stirs the spirit. The four horses were watered and rolled,
bathed and fed and adorned, and the arrows were drawn out of them. With golden
garlands, ready, the colour of gold, trained, swift, glad and calm, they were
yoked in the proper way to the chariot, properly fitted out. It had a great
banner, a lion with a golden mane, and was covered with golden emblems set with
jewels and coral, and adorned with flags like white clouds. It had a parasol
raised on a golden staff and was equipped with many weapons. They yoked the
horses in their golden harness in the proper way. Daruka's younger brother, his
charioteer and dear friend, told him the chariot was yoked, as Matali tells
Vasava.

"Then Satyaki bathed and made himself pure, and had the lucky rites performed.
He had gold coins given to a thousand brahmins who had finished their studies,
and he was wrapped in blessings, Satyaki, best of the glorious. Then, worthy of
the honey drink of welcome, he drank Kailavata liquor, and he shone there with
red eyes, his eyes rolling with drink. He touched the heroes' bronze vessel,
full of great joy, his fire doubled, blazing like fire. The best of chariot
fighters took his bow with its arrow into his lap. Brahmins spoke blessings over
him, and in his armour and ornaments he was welcomed with parched grain, scents
and garlands, and by young girls. He bowed at Yudhishthira's feet with his palms
joined, and the king kissed his head, and he climbed into the great chariot.
Then the glad horses, well fed and swift as the wind, not to be beaten, Sindhu
horses, carried the victorious chariot along, prancing.

"Then Satyaki, his whole body thrilled with joy, said to Bhima, 'You, Bhima,
guard the king. That is the most important thing for you to do. I will break
through this army, ripe for Time, and go in. Now and in the future, the best
thing is to guard the king. You know my courage and I know yours, tamer of
enemies. So go back, Bhima, if you want to please me.'

"Told this, he said to Satyaki, 'Go, and do what has to be done. I will guard
the king, best of men.'

"Told this, Madhava answered Bhimasena, 'Go, go quickly, Partha. Victory is
surely mine today, since you are loving and loyal to me today and do as I ask,
and since the signs, Bhima, speak to me of good fortune. When the evil Saindhava
has been killed by the great-souled Pandava, I will embrace the righteous king,
without a doubt.'

"Having said this much and sent Bhima back, the great-minded man looked at your
army like a tiger at herds of deer. When they saw him about to go in, lord of
men, your army was more confused than ever and shook hard. Then Satyaki went
into your army at once, king, wanting to see Arjuna, at the King of Dharma's
command."

* **Satyaki's drink.** Before setting out, Satyaki "drank Kailavata liquor … his eyes rolling with drink" (*pītvā kailāvataṃ madhu … madavihvalalocanaḥ*, 7.87.61). The horses too are given "a tasty drink that stirs the spirit" (*pānaṃ madasamīriṇam*, 7.87.54).
* **Bhima.** In the CE Bhima does not set out with Satyaki. Satyaki sends him back to guard the king (7.87.66–73). \*587 and \*596, in which Bhima follows Satyaki at first, are rejected.

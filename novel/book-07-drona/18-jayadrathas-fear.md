summary: Jayadratha hears the Pandavas' roar and his spies' report of Arjuna's vow. Terrified, he goes to the kings and asks leave to go home, or for their protection. Duryodhana tells him not to fear: he names the warriors and the eleven akshauhinis who will guard him. Jayadratha goes with Duryodhana to Drona at night and asks who is the better archer, he or Arjuna. Drona says they had the same teaching, but Arjuna is better through his discipline and hardship; still, Drona will guard him and make an array Arjuna cannot cross, and in any case all must die, and a kshatriya who dies fighting reaches the worlds of the ascetics. Meanwhile Krishna tells Arjuna that his vow was rash, made without consulting him. His spies report the Kauravas' fear and their plan: six great chariot fighters will guard Jayadratha at the needle's eye of Drona's array. Arjuna answers that the six are not worth half of him, that he will break through Drona first, and that Jayadratha will die tomorrow though every god and creature guard him. He asks Krishna to have his chariot ready at dawn.

# Jayadratha's Fear

"Jayadratha heard that great noise of the Pandavas, eager for their son," said
Sanjaya, "and spies told him what it meant. He got up, his heart bewildered with
grief and struck down by sorrow, as if sinking in a great, bottomless sea of
grief. Thinking hard, the Saindhava went to the gathering of the kings. In front
of those gods among men, afraid of Abhimanyu's father and ashamed, he spoke,
lamenting.

"'The one born in Pandu's field, they say, by lustful Shakra, the fool means to
send me alone, they say, to Yama's abode. So farewell to you. I will go home, to
save my life. Or, bulls among the kshatriyas, if you are strong enough to
protect me, heroes, give me safety from what Partha seeks. Drona, Duryodhana and
Kripa, Karna, the lord of the Madras and Bahlika, Duhshasana and the rest could
protect even one who was held by Death. How much more from Phalguna alone, bent
on killing me! Could all of you, the lords of the earth together, not protect
me? When I hear the Pandavas' joy, my fear is great. My limbs seem to sink,
kings, like those of a man about to die. Surely the bearer of the Gandiva has
vowed my death. That is why the Pandavas are shouting with joy, even at a time
of grief. Neither gods nor gandharvas, nor demons, snakes or rakshasas, can make
it turn out otherwise, let alone kings. So give me leave, and bless you, bulls
among men. I will go out of sight, and the Pandavas will not see me.'

"As he lamented like this, his mind confused with fear, King Duryodhana, who put
his own business first, said to him, 'Do not be afraid, tiger among men. Who
could reach you in battle, bull among men, standing among the kshatriya heroes?
I will be there, and Vaikartana Karna, Chitrasena, Vivimshati, Bhurishravas,
Shala, Shalya and invincible Vrishasena. Purumitra will be there, and Jaya, the
Bhoja and Sudakshina the Kamboja, strong-armed Satyavrata, Vikarna, Durmukha and
Saha. So will Duhshasana, Subahu and the Kalinga with his weapons raised, Vinda
and Anuvinda of Avanti, and Drona and Drona's son with Subala's son. You
yourself are the best of chariot fighters, brave and of boundless splendour. How
can you see danger from the Pandavas, Saindhava? My eleven akshauhinis will
fight carefully to guard you. Do not be afraid, Saindhava. Let your fear go.'

"When your son had comforted him like this, king, the Saindhava went with
Duryodhana to Drona at night. He clasped Drona's feet, lord of the people, sat
down close to him, bowing, and asked him this. 'In aim, in shooting far, in
speed of hand and in striking hard, tell me, lord, the difference between me and
Phalguna. I want to know truly, teacher, the difference in skill between me and
Arjuna. Tell me as it really is, lord.'

"'Your teaching was the same, my son,' said Drona, 'yours and Arjuna's. Through
his discipline and his being used to hardship, Arjuna is better than you. But
you must not be afraid of Partha in battle in any way. For I will guard you from
danger, my son, beyond doubt. Not even the immortals can prevail against one
guarded by my arms. And I will make an array that Partha will not get across. So
fight. Do not be afraid. Keep to your own dharma. Follow the road of your
fathers and grandfathers, lord of men. You have studied the Vedas as the rules
require, you have made the offerings to the fires well, and you have sacrificed
with many sacrifices. You need have no fear of the fear of death. You have won
great good fortune, which foolish men can hardly win, and you will reach the
finest divine worlds, won by the strength of your arms. The Kurus, the Pandavas,
the Vrishnis and other men, and I with my son, think that none of us is lasting.
One after another, all of us, killed by mighty Time, will go to the other world,
each with his own deeds. The worlds that ascetics reach by their austerities,
brave kshatriyas who hold to the kshatriya's dharma reach as well.'

"Comforted like this by Bharadvaja's son, king, the Saindhava put away his fear
of Partha and set his mind on battle.

"When Partha had made his vow to kill the Sindhu king, strong-armed Vasudeva
said to Dhananjaya, 'You made your vow in words, "Tomorrow I will kill the
Saindhava", knowing your brothers' mind. That was very rash. You have taken up
too great a load without consulting me. How shall we not be laughed at by the
whole world? I sent spies into the camp of Dhritarashtra's son, and they have
come back quickly and told us the news. When you vowed to kill the Sindhu king,
they heard a great lion's roar here, with music. Frightened by that sound, the
sons of Dhritarashtra and the Saindhava thought, "This lion's roar is not for
nothing," and stood ready. There was a great tumult of sound among the Kauravas,
strong-armed one, and a fearful rumble of elephants, horses, foot soldiers and
chariots. They thought, "Surely Dhananjaya, stricken by the news of Abhimanyu's
killing, will come out at night in his anger," and they stood ready. As they
made ready, they heard of your true vow, lotus-eyed one, you who are true, your
vow to kill the Sindhu king.

"'Then Suyodhana's counsellors and King Jayadratha were all downcast and afraid,
like small deer. Then the lord of the Sauviras and the Sindhus got up in misery
with his counsellors and went to his own camp, deeply grieved. At the time for
counsel he took counsel on all that would be best for him. Then he said this to
Suyodhana in the gathering of kings: "Tomorrow Dhananjaya will come against me,
thinking, 'This is my son's killer.' He has vowed my death in the middle of the
army. Neither gods nor gandharvas, nor demons, snakes or rakshasas, can make
Savyasachin's vow turn out otherwise. So protect me in battle, so that
Dhananjaya does not set his foot on your heads and reach his mark. Plan for
this. Or, if you do not protect me in battle, joy of the Kurus, give me leave,
king, and I will go home."

"'When he said this, Suyodhana bowed his head in dismay. Hearing that you had
vowed it, he sank into thought. When the Saindhava king saw him so troubled,
they say, he spoke gently, for his own good, with a hint in it. "I do not see
among you an archer of such power as could strike back at Arjuna's weapon with a
weapon in the great battle. Who could stand in front of Arjuna, with Vasudeva to
help him, as he shakes the Gandiva, even Shatakratu himself? We hear that Partha
fought even great Maheshvara long ago, on foot, on the Himalaya mountain. Urged
on by the king of the gods, he killed thousands of danavas who lived in the
golden city with a single chariot. Joined with wise Vasudeva, Kunti's son could
kill the three worlds with the immortals, I think. So I want leave to go, or to
be protected by great Drona and his son, the hero, if you think fit."

"'So the king himself begged the teacher earnestly, Arjuna. Arrangements have
been made, and the chariots are ready, they say. Karna, Bhurishravas, Drona's
son, invincible Vrishasena, Kripa and the king of the Madras, these six will be
at his front. Drona has planned the array: a cart, and a half-lotus. In the
middle of the lotus's heart, at the needle's eye, the Sindhu king will stand,
guarded by heroes mad for battle. In bow and weapons, in courage, in breath and
in chest, these six chariot fighters are firmly held to be the hardest to
withstand, Partha. Without beating them and their troops, Jayadratha cannot be
reached. Think about the courage of each of the six. Together, tigers among men
that they are, they cannot easily be beaten. And I will think again about the
policy for our own good, with counsellors who know counsel and with friends, so
that the work may succeed.'

"'The six chariot fighters of Dhritarashtra's son, whom you think so strong,'
said Arjuna, 'I see that their strength is not equal to half of mine. You will
see all their weapons broken by my weapon, Madhusudana, as I seek Jayadratha's
death. While Drona looks on and his followers wail, I will bring the Sindhu
king's head down to the ground. The Sadhyas and the Rudras may protect the
Sindhu king, Madhusudana, and the Vasus with the Ashvins, the Maruts with Indra,
the Vishvedevas and the demons. So may the ancestors with the gandharvas, the
Suparnas, the oceans and mountains, the sky, the air and this earth, and the
quarters with their lords. So may the beings of village and forest, all that
stands and moves. Even so, you will see him killed by my arrows tomorrow in
battle. I swear to you by the truth, Krishna, and I touch my weapon as I swear.

"'The great archer who is the guardian of that evil fool, Drona, I will attack
first, Keshava. Suyodhana thinks this game of dice hangs on him. So I will break
the front of his army and go on to the Saindhava. Tomorrow you will see great
archers split apart by me in battle with iron arrows of fierce sharpness, like
mountain peaks by thunderbolts. Blood will pour from the bodies of men,
elephants and horses as they fall and lie fallen, split by sharp arrows. The
arrows sent by the Gandiva, as swift as thought and wind, will leave thousands
of men, elephants and horses without bodies and without life. Men will see in
battle the terrible weapons I received from Yama, Kubera, Varuna, Rudra and
Indra. You will see the weapons of all the Saindhava's protectors struck down by
my weapon of Brahma in battle. Tomorrow, Keshava, you will see the earth strewn
by me in battle with the heads of kings, cut off by the force of my arrows. I
will glut the eaters of flesh, rout the enemy, delight my friends and bring down
the Saindhava.

"'He has done much wrong, a bad kinsman, born in a land of evil. Killed by me,
the Saindhava king will make his own people grieve. They eat every kind of milk
food, and their ways are evil. Driven by my arrows on the battlefield, the
Saindhavas will perish with their king. At dawn, Krishna, I will act so that
Suyodhana will think there is no other archer in the world equal to me in
battle. The divine bow Gandiva, and I the fighter, bull among men, and you the
driver, Hrishikesha: what could I fail to conquer? As the mark is fixed in the
moon and water in the ocean, know that this vow of mine is true, Janardana. Do
not look down on my weapons. Do not look down on my firm bow. Do not look down
on the strength of my arms. Do not look down on Dhananjaya. As it is true that
when I go to battle I am never beaten and always win, by that truth, know that
Jayadratha is killed in battle. Truth is fixed in the brahmin, humility in the
good, fortune in the skilful, and victory in Narayana.'"

"When he had said this to Hrishikesha," said Sanjaya, "Arjuna, Vasava's son,
roaring, charged himself and gave orders to Keshava, his lord. 'See that my
chariot is made ready when the night is over, Krishna, for a great work lies
before us.'"

**Divergences.**

* **"Lustful Shakra."** Jayadratha sneers at Arjuna's birth: "born in Pandu's field … by lustful Shakra" (7.52.4).
* **Drona's comparison.** Asked who is the better archer, Drona says their teaching was the same, but Arjuna is better "through his discipline and his being used to hardship" (7.52.24).
* **Krishna reports.** Krishna tells Arjuna what his spies heard in the Kaurava camp (7.53.4–30), including Jayadratha's plea to Duryodhana and the plan of the six chariot fighters: Karna, Bhurishravas, Ashvatthama, Vrishasena, Kripa and Shalya (7.53.26).
* **Arjuna's contempt for the Sindhus.** Arjuna calls Jayadratha "born in a land of evil" and the Saindhavas eaters of "every kind of milk food", with evil ways (7.53.47–48). These are Arjuna's words in anger, not the narrator's.

# The End of the Twelfth Day

"When Pandu's son had broken those ranks," said Dhritarashtra, "how did you
feel, Sanjaya, as you wavered and ran? When ranks have been broken and see no
place to stand, it is hard to join them again. Tell me about it, Sanjaya."

"Even so, lord of the people," said Sanjaya, "the great heroes, wanting to
please your son and guarding their fame in the world, followed Drona. When the
weapons were raised and Yudhishthira had come close, they did noble deeds in
that terrible battle, as if fear were not real. They fell on the gaps round
Bhimasena, of boundless power, round brave Satyaki and Dhrishtadyumna, lord. 'At
Drona! At Drona!' the fierce Panchalas urged. 'Not Drona!' your sons urged all
the Kurus. Some cried 'At Drona!' and others 'Not Drona!' It was a game of dice
for Drona, played by the Kurus and the Pandavas. Whatever group of Panchala
chariots Drona went for, there Dhrishtadyumna of Panchala placed himself. In
that terrible battle every share was turned upside down. Heroes met heroes, and
cowards went to others.

"The Pandavas could not be shaken by their enemies there. They shook the ranks,
remembering all they had suffered. In the grip of their rage, modest and driven
by courage, they gave up their lives and turned back, striking at Drona in the
great battle. The clash of those men of boundless power, gambling with their
lives in that wild battle, was like the clash of iron on iron, or of stone on
stone. Not even the old men remember a battle like it, great king, seen or heard
of before. The earth seemed to shake in that slaughter of heroes, weighed down
by the great flood of the army as it poured on. The roar of the army, as it
heaved back and forth, seemed to hold up the sky, the roar of angry
Ajatashatru's army and your son's.

"Drona, ranging through the battle, came up against the Pandava ranks by the
thousand and broke them with his sharp arrows. As Drona, whose deeds were
marvellous, churned them up, the commander himself came up and surrounded Drona.
The fight between Drona and the Panchala was a marvel. I do not think there
could be anything to match it.

"Then Nila, like fire, burned up the Kuru army, his arrows the sparks and his
bow the flame, like a fire burning dry brush. As he burned up the ranks, mighty
Drona's son, who was always the first to speak, smiled and said to him very
gently, 'Nila, what is the good of burning so many warriors with the flame of
your arrows? Fight me alone. Strike me with your swift arrows in your anger.'
Drona's son looked like a mass of lotuses, his eyes were like lotus petals, and
his face like an open lotus. Nila pierced him with arrows. Pierced deeply by
him, Drona's son at once cut his enemy's bow, banner and umbrella with three
sharp, broad arrows. Nila leapt from his chariot with a fine sword and shield,
meaning to strike Drona's son's head from his body like a bird of prey. As he
raised his sword, Drona's son, smiling, struck off his head, with its fine nose
and its earrings, with a broad arrow, blameless one. His face was like the full
moon, his eyes like lotus petals. Tall, the colour of the inside of a water
lily, he fell dead to the ground.

"Then the Pandava army was shaken and thrown into turmoil, now that Nila,
blazing with power, had been killed by the teacher's son. All the great chariot
fighters of the Pandavas thought, 'How will Vasava's son save us from our
enemies, sir?' For the mighty one was making havoc to the south of the army, of
what was left of the sworn band and of the army of the Narayanas.

"Wolf-belly would not bear the check to his army. He pierced Bahlika with sixty
arrows and Karna with ten. Drona, wanting to end his life, quickly struck him in
the vital parts with sharp, keen-edged iron arrows. Then Karna struck him with
twelve arrows, Ashvatthama with seven and King Duryodhana with six, and covered
him. Mighty Bhimasena pierced them all back, Drona with fifty arrows and Karna
with ten, Duryodhana with twelve and Drona's son with eight swift arrows, and
raising a wild roar he came on at them in the battle. As he was giving up his
life, made equal with death, Ajatashatru urged his warriors, 'Save Bhima!' They
went to Bhimasena, of boundless power, Yuyudhana and the rest, and the two sons
of Madri, the Pandavas.

"In a fury, those bulls among men came together, eager to break Drona's ranks,
guarded by the finest archers. Bhima and the rest, great chariot fighters, fell
on them, and Drona, the best of chariot fighters, met them without flinching.
Your men put their fear of death behind them and went at the Pandavas, mighty
heroes beyond chariot fighters who shone in battle. Horsemen struck horsemen,
and chariot fighters chariots. There was a clash of spears and swords, and a
fight with axes. There was close fighting with swords, bitter in its outcome,
and a most terrible fight with ranks of elephants. One man fell from an
elephant, another head first from a horse, another from a chariot, pierced by an
arrow, sir. There, in the crush, an elephant trod on the chest of another man
who had fallen without his armour, and crushed his head. Other elephants killed
other fallen men. Going down on their knees, they tore many chariot fighters
open with their tusks. Some elephants, with men's entrails caught on their
tusks, roamed about in their hundreds, trampling men in their hundreds.
Elephants pounded fallen men, horses, chariots and elephants, in their armour of
bronze and iron, like thick reeds.

"Kings, modest men, ripe for Time, lie in great pain on beds covered with
vultures' feathers. Here a father kills his son and drives his chariot over him,
and a son his father, in his folly. All order was gone. An axle was broken, a
banner cut down, an umbrella thrown to the ground. A horse ran off dragging half
a yoke. An arm fell with its sword, and a head was cut off with its earrings. A
mighty elephant flung a chariot down and smashed it to powder on the ground. An
elephant struck by a chariot fighter's iron arrow fell lifeless, and a horse and
rider fell, struck hard by an elephant. So the great battle went on, terrible
and without any order. 'Oh, father! Oh, son! Friend, where are you? Stand! Where
are you running? Strike! Bring it! Kill him!' With laughter, war cries and
roars, such words of every kind were heard. The blood of men, horses and
elephants ran together. It settled the dust of the earth, and the cowards
fainted.

"Men seized one another by the hair, and there was terrible fighting with fists,
and with the nails and teeth of heroes who sought an island where there was
none. There a hero's arm was cut off, raised with its sword, and another's with
its bow, arrow and goad. One man cried out to another, and another turned and
ran. One cut off the head of another who reached him. One ran towards a sound,
and another ran hard away from it. One killed his own side, another the enemy,
with sharp arrows. An elephant like a mountain peak, brought down by an iron
arrow, fell to the ground like a river bank in the hot season. An elephant,
streaming with rut like a mountain, crushed a chariot fighter with his horses
and charioteer, and stood on him with its foot on the ground. When they saw the
brave masters of weapons striking, covered in blood, many cowards, weak of
heart, lost their wits. Everything was in turmoil, and nothing could be made
out. With the army blotted out by dust, all order was gone.

"Then the commander, saying 'Now is the time', quickly urged on the Pandavas,
who were always in haste. Doing his bidding, the famous Pandavas rushed at
Drona's chariot, killing as they went, like swans flying down to a lake. 'Seize
him! Run at him! Don't be afraid! Cut him down!' A wild din went up round the
chariot of the invincible man. Then Drona, Kripa, Karna, Drona's son, King
Jayadratha, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti and Shalya held them back. Roused by
the noble law, hard to hold back and hard to face, the Panchalas and the
Pandavas, though tormented by arrows, did not let Drona go. Then Drona in a
great fury, loosing hundreds of arrows, made a great slaughter of the Chedis,
the Panchalas and the Pandavas. The slap of his bowstring was heard in every
direction, sir, like the crash of thunderbolts, and it terrified many Pandavas.

"In the meantime mighty Jishnu, having killed the sworn band, came to where
Drona was crushing the Pandavas. Having killed the sworn band, Phalguna was seen
to have crossed that great lake whose water was blood and whose great whirlpools
were floods of arrows. We saw his emblem, the famous monkey banner, blazing with
power, of a man whose splendour was like the sun's. With the rays of his weapons
he dried up that ocean of the sworn band, and then that sun of the end of the
age of the Pandavas scorched the Kurus too. Arjuna burned up all the Kurus with
the power of his weapons, like the comet that rises at the end of the age and
burns all creatures. Struck by thousands of floods of his arrows, the fighters
on elephants, horses and chariots fell to the ground, dropping their weapons,
tormented by arrows. Some cried out in pain, and some roared. Some, struck by
Partha's arrows, fell lifeless. Some warriors were leaping up, and some had
fallen or turned their backs. Arjuna did not kill them, remembering the vow of
warriors.

"The Kurus, their chariots, horses and elephants shattered, most of them turned
away, cried out, 'Karna! Karna!' and 'Alas!' When Adhiratha's son heard the cry
of those seeking refuge, he promised them, 'Do not be afraid!', and went to face
Arjuna. The best of the Bharata chariot fighters, who gave joy to all the
Bharatas, the best of those who know weapons, called up the fire weapon.
Dhananjaya blew away with a net of arrows the floods of arrows of Karna, with
his blazing arrows and his blazing bow. Adhiratha's son held off Arjuna's weapon
with a weapon of his own and roared, loosing arrows. Dhrishtadyumna, Bhima and
the great chariot fighter Satyaki came up to Karna and pierced him with three
straight arrows each. Radheya held off Arjuna's weapon with showers of arrows,
and cut the bows of those three with three arrows. With their weapons cut, the
heroes were like snakes without venom. They raised chariot spears and roared
loudly like lions. Flung from their hands with great force, like snakes, the
great spears went blazing at Adhiratha's son. Mighty Karna cut them with three
sharp, straight arrows each, and roared, loosing arrows at Partha.

"Then Arjuna pierced Radheya with seven swift arrows, and killed Karna's younger
brother with three sharp arrows. Then Partha killed Shatrunjaya with six
straight arrows, and at once struck Vipata's head from his chariot with a broad
arrow. While the sons of Dhritarashtra looked on, three brothers born of one
womb were killed by the diademed one alone, before the charioteer's son's eyes.
Then Bhima leapt from his chariot like Vinata's son and with his fine sword
killed fifteen of Karna's supporters. He mounted his chariot again, took up
another bow, and pierced Karna with ten arrows, and his charioteer and horses
with five. Dhrishtadyumna too took his fine sword and his shining shield and
killed Chandravarman and Brihatkshatra, the Paurava. Then the Panchala mounted
his chariot again, took up another bow, and pierced Karna with seventy-three
arrows, roaring in the battle. Shaineya too took up another bow, bright as the
rainbow, pierced the charioteer's son with sixty-four arrows and roared like a
lion. With two broad arrows well loosed he cut Karna's bow, and then struck
Karna with three arrows in the arms and the chest.

"Then Duryodhana, Drona and King Jayadratha pulled Radheya out of the ocean of
Satyaki as he was drowning. Dhrishtadyumna and Bhima, Subhadra's son and Arjuna,
Nakula and Sahadeva guarded Satyaki in the battle. So this terrible battle went
on, to the ruin of all the archers, yours and theirs, as they gave up their
lives. Foot soldiers, chariots, elephants and horses met elephants, horses,
chariots and foot soldiers. Chariot fighters met elephants, foot soldiers and
horses, and chariots and foot soldiers met chariots and elephants. Horses were
seen locked with horses, elephants with elephants, chariot fighters with chariot
fighters, and foot soldiers with foot soldiers. So the battle was a great
tangle. It delighted the eaters of flesh, and it swelled Yama's kingdom with
those great, fearless men.

"Then men, chariots, horses and elephants killed many elephants, chariots,
horses and foot soldiers. Elephants were killed by elephants, chariots with
their weapons raised by chariot fighters, horses by horses and foot soldiers by
hosts of foot soldiers. Elephants were killed by chariots, great horses by the
finest elephants, men by horses and horses by the best chariot fighters. They
lay on the ground with their tongues, teeth and eyes thrust out, their armour
and ornaments crushed. Killed by the enemy with the finest weapons of many
kinds, they lay on the ground, a fearful sight. They were pounded, struck by the
feet of horses and elephants, and cut up by chariot wheels and hooves. In that
dreadful slaughter of men, a delight to beasts of prey, birds and rakshasas, the
mighty men raged, in a fury with one another, killing with all their strength.

"Then, as the two armies, battered and flooded with blood, looked at each other,
and the sun slowly went down behind the mountain of sunset, both went back to
camp, Bharata.

"Before that, when we had been broken by Phalguna, whose power had no measure,
when Drona's purpose had come to nothing and Yudhishthira had been saved, all
your men were beaten in battle, their armour smashed. Covered in dust, badly
shaken, looking in all ten directions, they withdrew with Bharadvaja's son's
consent, downcast, mocked by the enemy who had found their mark in battle. As
all beings praised Phalguna's boundless virtues, and spoke of Keshava's
friendship for Arjuna, they were like men accused, and they fell silent, lost in
thought.

"Then, at dawn, Duryodhana spoke to Drona, in anger, out of affection and pride,
unhappy at the enemy's growth, a man skilled with words, while all beings
listened. 'Surely, best of those who know Brahman, we are among those you mean
to kill. For you did not take Yudhishthira today when he was close by. If you
wanted to, an enemy who once came into your sight in battle would not get away,
even if the Pandavas and the gods guarded him while you tried to seize him. You
gave me a boon in your kindness, and then you changed. The noble never break the
hopes of one devoted to them.'

"Bharadvaja's son was displeased at being spoken to like that, and he said to
the king, 'You should not think otherwise of me, when I strive for what you
want. The worlds, with gods, demons and gandharvas, with yakshas, snakes and
rakshasas, cannot beat the army the diademed one guards. Where Govinda is, the
creator of all, and Arjuna, the enemy of armies, whose strength could prevail
there, except the lord Tryambaka's? But I tell you the truth today, and it will
not be otherwise: today I will bring down one of their great chariot fighters, a
leading hero. And I will make an array today that even the gods cannot break.
But king, let Arjuna be drawn away by some means. For there is nothing in battle
he does not know or cannot do. He has gathered all knowledge from everywhere.'

"When Drona had said this, the sworn band challenged Arjuna again to battle in
the south. There Arjuna fought the enemy in a battle like no other ever heard of
or seen. Then the array Drona made shone, king, like the midday sun, burning and
hard to look at. At the word of his eldest uncle, Bharata, Abhimanyu broke that
wheel array, so hard to break, in many places in the battle. He did a deed that
was hard to do and killed heroes by the thousand, and then, caught by six
heroes, he fell into the power of Duhshasana's son. We were overjoyed, king, and
the Pandavas, worn down by grief, withdrew when Subhadra's son had been killed."

"My heart is breaking, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "to hear that the son of
that lion among men was killed in battle before he was grown. Cruel is this law
of the kshatriyas, laid down by the makers of the law, when brave men who wanted
the kingdom let fall their weapons on a boy. A boy, who had always lived in
comfort, ranging about fearlessly, and many masters of weapons killed him. Tell
me how, son of Gavalgana. Tell me, Sanjaya, how Subhadra's son of boundless
power played in the battle as he tried to break the chariot ranks."

"You ask me about the fall of Subhadra's son, king of kings," said Sanjaya. "I
will tell you all of it. Listen closely, king, to how the boy played as he tried
to break the ranks. Your men were as frightened as forest creatures caught by a
forest fire in woods thick with bushes, grass and trees."

**Divergences.**

* **Nila.** Ashvatthama kills Nila with a broad arrow as he leaps at him with a sword (7.30.24–25).
* **Arjuna spares the fallen.** Arjuna does not kill men who are leaping up, fallen or turned away, "remembering the vow of warriors" (7.31.48).
* **Karna's brothers.** Arjuna kills "Karna's younger brother", Shatrunjaya and Vipata, "three brothers born of one womb", before Karna's eyes (7.31.58–60). The first is not named in the CE.
* **The end of the twelfth day.** Both armies withdraw at sunset (7.31.77). 7.32.1–4 goes back to tell how the Kauravas withdrew, beaten. Duryodhana speaks to Drona "at dawn" (7.32.5), the morning of the thirteenth day.
* **A summary first.** As at the start of the book, Sanjaya first gives the end in brief: Abhimanyu broke the wheel array at Yudhishthira's word, killed thousands, was caught by six heroes and "fell into the power of Duhshasana's son" (7.32.18–19). The full story follows.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*232–\*250. \*247 names those Abhimanyu killed, among them Brihadbala of Kosala and Lakshmana.

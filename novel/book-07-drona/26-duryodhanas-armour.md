summary: With Arjuna through the array and Sudakshina and Shrutayudha dead, Duryodhana drives to Drona and reproaches him. Drona favours the Pandavas, he says, and is like a razor smeared with honey. He would have let Jayadratha go home if Drona had not promised him help. Drona answers without anger. He is old and cannot keep up with Krishna's horses, and he must stay at the mouth of the array to take Yudhishthira, as he vowed. Duryodhana should go after Arjuna himself. When Duryodhana asks how he can face the man who has beaten Drona, Drona binds on him a golden armour that no weapon can pierce, speaking blessings from gods, sages and kings. He tells how Shiva gave this armour and its spell to Indra to kill Vritra, and how the spell came down through Angiras, Brihaspati and Agniveshya to Drona. Duryodhana sets out after Arjuna with a thousand Trigarta chariots, a thousand elephants and ten thousand horse.

# Duryodhana's Armour

"Kunti's son had gone in to kill the Sindhu king, breaking through Drona's
division and the Bhoja's division, which was so hard to cross," said Sanjaya.
"The Kamboja's heir Sudakshina had been killed, king, and bold Shrutayudha had
been killed by the Left-Handed Archer. The divisions fled and were scattered on
every side. Your son saw his army broken and went to Drona. Hurrying in a single
chariot, he came to Drona and said:

"'That tiger among men has gone, crushing this great army. Think now what must
be done next to stop Arjuna, in this dreadful slaughter of men. Do whatever will
keep that tiger among men Jayadratha from being killed. Bless you, you are our
last refuge. That fire, Dhananjaya, fanned by the wind of his anger, is burning
the dry brush of my army, like a fire that has sprung up in dry brush. Now that
Kunti's son has broken through the army and passed, scorcher of enemies,
Jayadratha's guards are in the gravest doubt. The kings were firmly sure, best
of those who know Brahman, that Dhananjaya would never pass Drona alive. And
that Partha has passed, while you looked on, you of great splendour. I think
everything is lost today. This army is nothing to me now.

"'I know, blessed one, that you are devoted to the good of the Pandavas. And so
I am lost, brahmin, as I think of what must be done. As far as I can I give you
the best of livings, brahmin, and as far as I can I please you, and you do not
see it. You never want what is good for us, though we are devoted to you, you
whose courage has no measure. You always please the Pandavas, who delight in
harming us. You live on us, and you delight in harming us. I did not know you
were like a razor smeared with honey.

"'If you had not given me the promise that you would capture the Pandava, I
would not have stopped the Sindhu lord from going home. I was a fool. Hoping for
protection from you, I gave the Sindhu lord heart and in my folly handed him
over to death. A man might get free even from between Yama's teeth. But
Jayadratha, once in Arjuna's power, will not get free in battle. So act, you of
the red horses, that the Saindhava may be protected. Do not be angry at my wild
words in my distress. Save the Saindhava.'

"Drona said, 'I do not hold your words against you. You are like Ashvatthama to
me. But I will tell you the truth. Take it to heart, lord of the people. Krishna
is the best of charioteers and his horses are the best and fastest. Dhananjaya
makes a small opening and goes through it fast. Do you not see the floods of
arrows falling a krosha behind the diadem-wearer's chariot? He shot them as he
went, he goes so fast. I am not able to travel fast today. I am old. And the
Parthas' army is here at our front. I must take Yudhishthira while all the
bowmen watch. I vowed it in the midst of the Kshatriyas, strong-armed one.
Dhananjaya has left him, and he stands in front of me. So I will not leave the
mouth of the array and go after Phalguna.

"'He is one enemy, your equal in birth and in deeds. You have allies. Go and
fight him. Do not be afraid. You are the lord of this world. You are the king,
brave, skilled and able, and you made this feud with the Pandavas. Go yourself,
hero, quickly, where Dhananjaya has gone.'

"Duryodhana said, 'He has passed even you, teacher, the best of all who bear
weapons. How can I hold Dhananjaya back? Purandara with the thunderbolt in his
hand might be beaten in battle, but Arjuna, conqueror of enemy cities, cannot be
beaten in battle. By the heat of his weapons he beat the Bhoja Hardikya and you,
who are like one of the gods. He killed Shrutayus. Sudakshina has been killed,
and King Shrutayudha. Shrutayus and Achyutayus and the mlecchas have been killed
by the hundred. How am I to fight back against the Pandava, whom no one can
withstand, as he burns up so many of his enemies in battle? Tell me that, you
who know weapons. If you think I am fit to fight him, then instruct me today. I
am in your hands. I will do as you bid me. Protect my good name.'

"Drona said, 'You speak the truth, Kauravya. Dhananjaya is hard to withstand.
But I will act so that you can stand up to him. Let all the bowmen in the world
see a wonder today: Kunti's son held up by you while Vasudeva looks on. I will
bind this golden armour on you, king, so that no arrows and no weapons will get
through it in battle. If the gods and the asuras, the yakshas, the snakes and
the rakshasas, the three worlds and their men, all fought you, you would have
nothing to fear. Not Krishna, not Kunti's son, not any other bearer of weapons
in battle will be able to set arrows in your armour. So put on this armour and
go yourself, quickly, against Arjuna in his anger today. He will not stand up to
you.'

"Saying this," said Sanjaya, "Drona quickly touched water. Muttering the spell
as it is laid down, he bound on him the shining, most wonderful armour, for your
son's victory in that great battle. The best of those who know Brahman wanted to
amaze the world with his learning.

"Drona said, 'May Brahma bless you, and the twice-born, and the best of the
serpents, Bharata. May Yayati and Nahusha, Dhundhumara and Bhagiratha and all
the royal seers bless you in every way. Blessings on you, always, from the
creatures with one foot and with many feet, and from those with no feet at all,
in this great battle. May Svaha, Svadha and Shachi bless you always. May Lakshmi
and Arundhati bless you, sinless one. May Asita Devala, Vishvamitra and Angiras,
Vasishtha and Kashyapa bless you, king. May Dhatri and Vidhatri, the lord of the
worlds, the quarters and the lords of the quarters, and six-faced Karttikeya
give you blessing today. May the blessed Vivasvat bless you in every way, and
the four elephants of the quarters, the earth, the air, the sky and the planets.
May Shesha, best of snakes, who always holds up the earth from below, king, give
you blessing.

"'Long ago, Gandhari's son, the demon Vritra showed his courage in battle and
beat the best of the gods. Their bodies were broken by the thousand. Then all
the dwellers in heaven, with Indra, their fire and strength taken from them,
went to Brahma for shelter, afraid of the great asura Vritra.

"'The gods said, "Best of gods, be the refuge of the gods crushed by Vritra.
Best of the immortals, save us from this great fear."

"'Then Brahma spoke these true words to Vishnu, who stood at his side, and to
the best of the gods, Shakra and the others, in their despair. He said, "I must
always protect the gods, with Indra and the twice-born. Tvashtri's fire, by
which Vritra was made, is very hard to bear. Long ago Tvashtri practised
austerity for a million years and made Vritra, gods, with leave from the Great
Lord. By the Great Lord's grace that mighty enemy would kill you. The blessed
Hara is not seen unless one goes to Shankara's dwelling. Once you have seen him,
you will kill your enemy. Go quickly to Mandara, where he lives, the womb of
austerity, the destroyer of Daksha's sacrifice, the bearer of the Pinaka bow,
the lord of all beings, who struck out Bhaga's eyes."

"'The gods went together with Brahma to Mandara. They saw a mass of fire, as
bright as ten million suns. He said, "Welcome, gods. Tell me, what shall I do?
To see me is never fruitless. So may you get what you wish."

"'At this all the dwellers in heaven answered him, "Vritra has taken our fire.
Be the refuge of the dwellers in heaven. Look at our bodies, god, battered by
his blows. We have come to you for shelter. Be our refuge, Great Lord."

"'The Great Lord said, "I know, gods, that this is a mighty, dreadful being
raised by a spell, born of Tvashtri's fire, very hard for those who are not
ready to hold back. But I must surely help all the dwellers in heaven. Take this
shining armour from my body, Shakra, and bind it on with this spell spoken in
the mind, lord of the gods."

"'Saying this, the giver of boons gave him the armour and the spell. Protected
by that armour, he went against Vritra's army. Of all the floods of weapons of
every kind that fell on it in that great battle, not one could break a joint of
that bound armour. Then the lord of the gods himself killed Vritra in the
battle. He gave the binding made of spells, and the armour, to Angiras. Angiras
told it to his son Brihaspati, who knew spells, and Brihaspati told it to wise
Agniveshya. Agniveshya gave it to me. With it I bind your armour on you today
with the spell, best of kings, to guard your body.'

"Having said this, Drona, of great splendour, the bull among teachers, spoke to
your son again, slowly: 'I bind your armour on you with Brahma's thread, king,
as Hiranyagarbha bound Vishnu's in battle long ago. As Brahma bound Shakra's
heavenly armour in the war over Taraka, so I bind yours.'

"When the brahmin had bound on his armour with the spell, in the proper way, he
sent the king to the great battle. Armoured by the great teacher, the
strong-armed king set out towards Arjuna's chariot. He had with him a thousand
chariots of hard-striking Trigartas, a thousand mighty elephants in rut, ten
thousand horses and other great warriors. He went with the sound of instruments
of every kind, like Vairochani, Bali. Then there was a great noise from your
armies, Bharata, as they saw the Kaurava set out like a sea without bottom."

**Divergences.**

* **Who sends the gods to Shiva.** In the CE Brahma, with Vishnu at his side, sends the gods to Shiva on Mandara (7.69.52–56), and it is Shiva who gives Indra his own armour and its spell (7.69.62–63).
* **Duryodhana's list.** At 7.69.29–30 he names "Shrutayus" twice: once alone, and once with Achyutayus. The CE has two men of that name in the last chapter.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*500–\*512. \*502 adds Karttikeya's part in the Taraka war; \*504 adds more blessings.

# Abhimanyu Alone

"When Duryodhana saw his army broken by Subhadra's son of boundless power," said
Sanjaya, "he rode at Subhadra's son himself in a fury. When Drona saw the king
turn to face Subhadra's son in the battle, he said to the warriors, 'Surround
the king. Mighty Abhimanyu is killing the one we must guard, before our eyes.
Run at him! Don't be afraid! Save the Kaurava quickly!'

"Then his grateful, strong friends, who had won their battles, surrounded your
son to save the hero from danger. Drona, Drona's son, Kripa, Karna, Kritavarma
and Subala's son, Brihadbala, the king of the Madras, Bhuri, Bhurishravas,
Shala, the Paurava and Vrishasena, loosing sharp arrows, covered Subhadra's son
with a great shower of arrows. They bewildered him and set Duryodhana free.
Arjuna's son could not bear it, like a morsel snatched from his mouth. With a
great flood of arrows Subhadra's son turned those great chariot fighters away,
with their horses and charioteers, and roared like a lion.

"When they heard his roar, like a lion's hungry for flesh, the chariot fighters
led by Drona could not bear it, in their fury. They penned him in with a line of
chariots, sir, and loosed nets of arrows of every kind at him in masses. Your
grandson cut them in the air with sharp arrows, and pierced them back, and it
was a marvel. Angered by him with his arrows like poisonous snakes, they
surrounded Subhadra's son, who never ran, wanting to kill him. Abhimanyu held
back alone that ocean of your army, as it heaved about, as the shore holds back
the home of the sea monsters. As the heroes fought and killed one another,
neither Abhimanyu nor his enemies turned their backs.

"In that terrible, frightening battle, Duhsaha pierced Abhimanyu with nine
arrows, Duhshasana with twelve, Kripa, Sharadvat's son, with three, and Drona
with seventeen arrows like poisonous snakes. Vivimshati pierced him with twenty,
Kritavarma with seven, Brihadbala with eight and Ashvatthama with seven.
Bhurishravas pierced him with three arrows, the lord of the Madras with six
swift ones, Shakuni with two arrows and King Duryodhana with three. Mighty
Abhimanyu pierced each of them back with three straight arrows, great king, as
if dancing, bow in hand.

"Then Abhimanyu, in a fury, tormented by your sons, showed the great strength
that came from his training and his own nature. With horses that obeyed their
driver's word, tame, as swift as Garuda and the wind, he drove quickly at the
son of the Ashmaka king. The Ashmaka pierced him with ten arrows and said,
'Stand!' With ten arrows, smiling, Abhimanyu brought down to the ground his
charioteer, his horses and his banner, his arms, his bow and his head. When the
hero, the lord of the Ashmakas, had been killed by Subhadra's son, the whole
army wavered, bent on running away.

"Then Karna, Kripa, Drona, Drona's son, the king of Gandhara, Shala, Shalya,
Bhurishravas, Kratha, Somadatta and Vivimshati covered him with showers of
arrows. So did Vrishasena and Sushena, Kundabhedin and Pratardana, Vrindaraka
and Lalittha, Prabahu, Dirghalochana and Duryodhana in his fury. Abhimanyu,
furious at the great archers with their straight arrows, took up an arrow for
Karna that would split an enemy's body. It split his armour, went through his
body and into the ground, king, like a snake into an anthill. Shaken by that
great blow, as if losing his wits, Karna trembled in the battle like a mountain
in an earthquake. Then, with other sharp arrows, the mighty one in his fury
killed three, Sushena, Dirghalochana and Kundabhedin, with three arrows. Karna
struck him with twenty-five iron arrows, Ashvatthama with twenty and Kritavarma
with seven.

"His whole body stuck with arrows, the son of Shakra's son was seen in his fury
ranging through the army like Death with his noose in his hand. He covered
Shalya, who was near him, with a shower of arrows. The strong-armed one shouted,
terrifying your army. Pierced by that master of weapons with straight arrows
that split the vital parts, king, Shalya sank down in his chariot and fainted.
When they saw him wounded like that by famous Subhadra's son, the whole army
fled while Bharadvaja's son looked on. Watching that strong-armed boy covered in
golden-feathered arrows, your men fled like deer set on by a lion.

"The hosts of the ancestors, the gods, the charanas, the siddhas and the yakshas
honoured him for his fame in battle, and so did the hosts of beings on the
earth. He shone out like a fire sprinkled with ghee."

"When Arjuna's son was crushing them like that with his straight arrows," said
Dhritarashtra, "which of my men covered that great archer?"

"Hear, king, how the boy played greatly in the battle," said Sanjaya, "as he
tried to break the chariot ranks guarded by Bharadvaja's son. When Shalya's
younger brother saw the lord of the Madras brought down by Subhadra's son's
swift arrows, he came on in a fury, scattering arrows. He pierced Arjuna's son
with ten arrows, with his horses and his driver, and shouted loudly, 'Stand!
Stand!' With his arrows Arjuna's son struck down his head and neck, his hands
and feet, his bow and his horses, his umbrella and banner and his driver. He
struck down the three bamboos, the pin and the fittings, the wheels, the yoke
and the pole, the quivers and the under-frame, the flag, the two wheel guards
and all his gear. He did it so fast that no one saw him do it. The man fell to
the ground, finished, his ornaments and clothes flung about, like a great sacred
tree shattered by the wind of boundless force, and his followers fled in terror
in every direction.

"When they saw what Arjuna's son had done, all beings cried out on every side,
'Well done! Well done!', Bharata. When Shalya's brother had fallen, many of his
soldiers came at Arjuna's son in a fury, with every kind of weapon in their
hands, calling out to him the names of their families, homes and selves. Some
came in chariots, some on horses and elephants, and some, fierce in their
strength, on foot. There was the great sound of arrows and the noise of hooves
and wheels, grunts, shouts, cries and roars, lions' roars and bellowing, and the
slap of bowstrings on their guards. They roared at the son of Arjuna, 'You won't
get away from us alive!'

"When Subhadra's son saw them saying this, he almost laughed, and pierced with
his arrows whoever struck at him first. Wanting to show his wonderful, quick
weapons, brave Arjuna's son fought gently at first in the battle. Krishna's son
showed whatever weapon he had received from Vasudeva and from Dhananjaya, and
there was no difference between him and the two Krishnas. Shooting far, bearing
a heavy load, fitting and loosing arrows again and again, he was seen to do it
all without a pause. His bow was seen, a quivering circle, in every direction,
like the disc of the blazing sun as it kills the darkness. The sound of his
bowstring and the terrible slap of his palm were heard like the thunder of a
cloud that lets fall great thunderbolts in its season.

"Modest, proud, generous in honouring others and handsome to see, Subhadra's son
fought wanting to honour the heroes and the archers. He began gently, great
king, and then grew terrible, like the blessed sun in autumn when the rains have
passed. In his fury he loosed wonderful great arrows by the hundred, with golden
feathers, sharpened on stone, as the sun looses its rays. The famous one covered
the chariot ranks under Bharadvaja's son's eyes with razor-headed arrows,
calf-tooth arrows and vipathas, with iron arrows and half-iron arrows, with
broad arrows and anjalikas. Then that army, tormented by arrows, turned away."

"My mind is split in two, Sanjaya, between shame and pleasure," said
Dhritarashtra, "that Subhadra's son held back my son's army. Tell me all of it
again at length, son of Gavalgana, how the boy played in the battle, like Skanda
with the demons."

"I will tell you of the terrible struggle," said Sanjaya, "how the wild battle
went between one man and many. Abhimanyu, full of spirit, in his chariot, gave
heart even to all your chariot fighters, full of spirit as they were, the tamers
of enemies. Drona, Karna, Kripa, Shalya, Drona's son, the Bhoja, Brihadbala,
Duryodhana, Somadatta's son and mighty Shakuni were there, with many kings and
princes and armies of every kind. Ranging among them all like a whirling
firebrand, he came at them with his arrows. Killing his enemies, Subhadra's son,
a master of the greatest weapons, powerful and full of splendour, showed himself
in every direction, Bharata. When they saw what Subhadra's son of boundless
power did, your armies trembled again and again.

"Then great, wise Bharadvaja's son, his eyes wide with delight, quickly spoke to
Kripa, as if striking at your son's vital parts, sir, when he saw Abhimanyu, so
skilled in battle. 'Here goes young Subhadra's son at the head of the Parthas.
He gives joy to all his friends, to King Yudhishthira, to Nakula and Sahadeva
and to Bhimasena the Pandava. He gives joy to his kin and his relations, and to
the others, the neutral and the friendly. I do not think there is any other
bowman equal to him in battle. He could kill this army if he wanted to. For some
reason he does not want to.'

"When he heard Drona's words, full of affection, your son grew angry with
Arjuna's son, and looked at Drona almost smiling. Then Duryodhana spoke to
Karna, Bahlika and Kripa, to Duhshasana, the king of the Madras and the other
great chariot fighters. 'The teacher of all the anointed kings, the best of
those who know Brahman, does not want to kill Arjuna's foolish son here. For not
even Death could get away from this man if he drew his bow on him, let alone any
other mortal. I tell you the truth. He is protecting Arjuna's son because Arjuna
was his pupil. Sons and pupils are dear to the righteous, and so are their
children. Protected by Drona, the fool thinks he is brave. He thinks too much of
himself. Crush him, and quickly.'

"When the king said this, they went at the son of the Satvata woman in a fury,
wanting to kill him, while Bharadvaja's son looked on. When Duhshasana, the
tiger of the Kurus, heard Duryodhana's words, he said this to Duryodhana. 'I
will kill him, great king, I tell you. While the sons of Pandu and the Panchalas
look on, today I will swallow Subhadra's son as Rahu swallows the sun.' And he
shouted again to the king of the Kurus, 'The two Krishnas are so proud. When
they hear that I have swallowed Subhadra's son, they will go from the world of
the living to the world of the dead, without a doubt. And when they hear that
those two are dead, the sons of Pandu, born of his field, will give up their
lives in one day, with all their friends, out of weakness. So when this enemy is
killed, all your enemies are killed. Wish me well, king. I am going to kill your
enemy.'

"When he had said this, king, your son Duhshasana rode roaring at Subhadra's son
in a fury, covering him with showers of arrows. As your son came on in a fury,
Abhimanyu, the tamer of enemies, struck him with twenty-six sharp arrows.
Duhshasana, in a fury like an elephant in rut, fought Subhadra's son, and
Abhimanyu fought him in the battle. The two of them, masters of the art of the
chariot, fought, circling to left and right in wonderful rings with their
chariots. Then men made a great din of tabors, drums and kettledrums, of
krikaras, great war drums, trumpets and cymbals, mixed with lions' roars like
the roar of the salt sea."

**Divergences.**

* **Drona's praise.** Drona says of Abhimanyu that no bowman is his equal and that "he could kill this army if he wanted to" (7.38.13). Duryodhana takes it as Drona sparing his pupil's son (7.38.16–19).
* **Shalya's brother.** "Shalya's younger brother" is not named in the CE (7.37.3).
* **Duhshasana's boast.** Duhshasana says that the news of Abhimanyu's death will kill Krishna and Arjuna, and that the Pandavas will then give up their lives (7.38.23–24).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*271–\*281.

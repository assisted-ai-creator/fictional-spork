# Drona and Dhrishtadyumna

"So that battle went on, making the hair stand on end," said Sanjaya, "and the
Pandavas fell on the Kauravas, who had split in three. Strong-armed Bhimasena
held back Jalasandha, and Yudhishthira, with his division, Kritavarma.
Dhrishtadyumna, great king, rushed at Drona in battle, pouring rains of arrows
and shining like the rayed sun. Then battle began, as all the bowmen of the
Kurus and the Somakas hurried at each other in anger.

"In that dreadful slaughter, as the armies fought in pairs without fear, Drona,
the strong, loosed floods of arrows at the strong son of the Panchala king, and
it was a wonder. Drona and the Panchala prince scattered men's heads everywhere,
like lotus beds torn up. The clothes, ornaments and weapons of heroes, their
banners, their armour and their arms, lay strewn about the divisions on every
side. Warriors bright with gold and soaked in blood looked like masses of cloud
locked together, lit with lightning. Great warriors brought down elephants,
horses and men in battle with their feathered arrows, drawing bows as tall as
palm trees. The swords and shields, bows, heads and armour of great-souled
heroes were scattered in the fight. Countless headless trunks were seen standing
up all round in that great crush, great king. Vultures, herons, ravens and
hawks, crows and jackals, and many other eaters of flesh were seen there, sir.
They ate the flesh and drank the blood, king. They tore at the hair and the
marrow in many places. They dragged off bodies and parts of bodies, and the
heads of men, horses and elephants, here and there.

"Skilled in weapons, consecrated with the vows of battle, bearing arrows,
wanting victory in battle, they fought hard then. Your men went through the many
moves of the sword in battle. Others, in anger, struck each other with spears,
darts and lances, with tridents, javelins and axes, with maces and iron bars.
Others had no weapons and fought with their bare arms. They were men who had
come out onto the stage of battle. Chariot fighters fought chariot fighters,
riders fought riders, elephants fought the best of elephants and foot soldiers
fought foot. Some, like drunk men or madmen, like actors on a stage, shouted at
each other and struck each other in the battle.

"As the battle went on like this, without any bounds, lord of the people,
Dhrishtadyumna drove his horses in among Drona's horses. Mingled together, swift
as the wind, those horses looked very fine, the dove-grey and the deep red, and
in the battle, king, they shone like clouds with lightning.

"When Dhrishtadyumna saw Drona close at hand, the hero dropped his bow and took
up sword and shield, Bharata. Wanting to do a hard thing, Prishata's grandson,
killer of enemy heroes, crossed along the pole and got onto Drona's chariot. He
stood in the middle of the yoke, on the yoke straps and on the hindquarters of
the horses, and the armies cheered him. As he moved with his sword, standing on
the red horses, Drona could see no opening, and it was a wonder. Like the swoop
of a hawk in the woods, greedy for meat, was his rush as he tried to kill Drona.

"Then Drona struck away the son of Drupada's shield, with its hundred moons,
with a hundred arrows, and his sword with ten. The strong man killed his horses
with sixty-four arrows, and with two broad arrows his banner and his parasol,
and both the drivers who guard the wheels behind. Then quickly he drew another
arrow to his ear and loosed it at him to end his life, as the wielder of the
thunderbolt hurls the thunderbolt. Satyaki cut that arrow with fourteen arrows
and saved Dhrishtadyumna, caught in the grip of the chief of teachers. As a deer
is caught by a lion, so the Panchala prince was caught by Drona, a lion among
men, and the bull of the Shinis set him free, sir.

"Seeing Satyaki guarding the Panchala prince in that great battle, Drona quickly
struck him with twenty-six arrows. Then Shini's grandson pierced Drona, who was
swallowing up the Srinjayas, with twenty-six sharp arrows between the breasts.
When Drona turned on the Satvata, all the Panchala chariot fighters, greedy for
victory, quickly rescued Dhrishtadyumna."

Dhritarashtra said, "When that arrow was cut, Sanjaya, and Dhrishtadyumna
rescued by Yuyudhana, the Vrishni hero, what did Drona do in battle to Shini's
grandson? He was a great bowman, the best of all who bear weapons, a tiger among
men, and he could not bear it."

Sanjaya said, "He rushed at Yuyudhana like a great snake hissing. Anger was his
poison, his bow was his gaping mouth, his sharp-edged arrows were his teeth and
his whetted iron arrows his fangs. His eyes were red with fury and rage. He came
with his red horses, very swift, delighting in heroes, as if flying into the
sky, as if striding everywhere, loosing gold-feathered arrows.

"Drona was a raincloud that no one could hold back. The fall of his arrows was
its great rain, the rumble of his chariot its thunder. It was flung out by the
drawing of his bow, with iron arrows for its many lightnings, carrying spears
and swords like thunderbolts. It rose on the force of his anger and was driven
by the wind that was his horses. Brave Shaineya, conqueror of enemy cities,
fierce in battle, saw it come down on him. He said to his charioteer with a
laugh, 'Go out gladly to meet this cruel brahmin, who does not keep to his own
work, the refuge of King Dhritarashtra's son, who brings us grief and fear. Go
fast, with the horses at full speed, against the teacher of princes, who always
thinks himself a hero.'

"Then Madhava's fine horses, the colour of silver, went quickly towards Drona,
swift as the wind. There was a dreadful, unbroken darkness, covered with a net
of arrows, and no other hero seemed able to come near it. Between Drona and the
Satvata, both quick with their weapons, lions among men, no gap was seen in the
rains of arrows. As the arrows met, there was a sound like streams of rain
beating down, like the thunderbolts Shakra hurls. Arrows pierced by iron arrows
looked like snakes bitten by poisonous snakes, Bharata. The twang of their
bowstrings on their arm guards was heard, very cruel, without a pause, like
mountain peaks struck by the thunderbolt.

"The two chariots, king, their horses and their two charioteers were covered
with gold-feathered arrows. They were a strange sight. The fall of clean,
straight iron arrows, like snakes that have shed their skins, was very cruel,
lord of the people. Both their parasols fell, and both their banners. Both men
were soaked in blood and both wanted victory. With their limbs running with
blood they were like elephants streaming with rut, and they pierced each other
with arrows that could end life.

"The roars and shouts, the blare of conches and the sound of drums stopped,
great king. No one spoke. The divisions fell silent, and the fighters stopped
fighting. People watched their duel, full of curiosity. Chariot fighters,
elephant drivers, horsemen and foot soldiers stood round the two bulls among
chariot fighters and watched with eyes that did not move. The divisions of
elephants and of horses stood still, and so did the hosts of chariots, drawn up
facing them.

"They were bright with banners set with pearls and coral, adorned with jewels
and gold. There were bright ornaments and golden armour, pennants and flags, the
elephants' housings and blankets, clean, sharp weapons and the horses' plumes.
There were the gold and silver garlands on the elephants' brows, Bharata, and
the bands on their tusks. They looked like nets of clouds at the turning of the
hot season, with cranes and fireflies, with rainbows and lightning.

"Our men and Yudhishthira's men stood and watched that battle between Yuyudhana
and great-souled Drona. The gods watched from the tops of their sky chariots,
led by Brahma and Shakra, and the hosts of Siddhas and Charanas, the Vidyadharas
and the great snakes. They were amazed at the many strange moves of those two
lions among men, going forward and back, striking and parrying weapons.

"Showing the speed of their hands with their weapons, the two mighty men, Drona
and Satyaki, pierced each other with arrows. Then the Dasharha, in the battle,
cut Drona's arrows with strong feathered arrows, and quickly cut his bow, great
in splendour as Drona was. In the blink of an eye Bharadvaja's son strung
another bow, and Satyaki quickly cut that too. Then Drona, hurrying, stood with
a bow in his hand again, and again and again, as soon as he strung one, Satyaki
cut it with his sharp arrows.

"Drona saw Yuyudhana's more than human deed in the battle, king of kings, and
thought in his heart: 'This strength with weapons, that I see in this best of
the Satvatas, is found in Rama, in Kartavirya, in Dhananjaya and in Bhishma,
that tiger among men.' And in his heart the best of the twice-born honoured his
courage, seeing a speed like Vasava's. The best of those who know weapons was
pleased, and so were the gods with Vasava. The gods and the Gandharvas, lord of
the people, and the hosts of Siddhas and Charanas had never seen such speed from
Yuyudhana, quick in action as he was. They had known what Drona could do.

"Then Drona, crusher of Kshatriyas, the best of those who know weapons, took
another bow and fought with weapons, Bharata. Satyaki struck back his weapons
with the magic of his own, and struck him with sharp arrows, and it was a
wonder. Seeing his more than human deed in battle, which no one else could
equal, your men who knew the skill of arms honoured it as the work of true
skill. Whatever weapon Drona loosed, Satyaki loosed the same, and the teacher,
scorcher of enemies, fought him without being shaken.

"Then the master of the science of the bow, in his anger, great king, called up
a heavenly weapon to kill Yuyudhana. Seeing that dreadful, enemy-killing fire
weapon, the great bowman called up the heavenly weapon of Varuna. A great cry
went up when they were seen holding heavenly weapons. Even the creatures that
move in the sky stopped moving through it. The weapons of Varuna and of fire had
been set on their arrows by the two of them, and had not yet met, when the sun
turned past noon.

"Then King Yudhishthira and Bhimasena the Pandava, Nakula and Sahadeva, guarded
Satyaki. With Dhrishtadyumna and the others, Virata with the Kekayas, the
Matsyas and the Shalveya troops came swiftly against Drona. Thousands of
princes, with Duhshasana at their head, came to Drona, who was surrounded by his
enemies. Then battle began, king, between your bowmen and theirs, as the world
was covered with dust and with nets of arrows. Everything was in turmoil, and
nothing could be made out. With the army lost in dust, the fighting went on
without any bounds."

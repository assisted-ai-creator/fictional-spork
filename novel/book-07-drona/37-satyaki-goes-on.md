# Satyaki Goes On

"While Shaineya was driving your armies this way and that," said Sanjaya,
"Bharadvaja's son covered him with great flights of arrows. Then a wild fight
broke out between Drona and the Satvata while all the armies watched, like the
fight of Bali and Indra long ago.

"Drona pierced Shini's grandson in the forehead with three bright arrows made
all of iron, shaped like venomous snakes. With those straight arrows standing in
his forehead, great king, Yuyudhana looked like a mountain with three peaks.
Then Bharadvaja's son, watching for an opening, sent more arrows at him in the
battle, and they sounded like Indra's thunderbolt. As they flew from Drona's
bow, the Dasharha, who knew the highest weapons, cut each one with two
well-feathered arrows of his own.

"Drona saw how quick he was, lord of the people. He laughed, and all at once
pierced the bull of the Shinis with twenty arrows. Then he pierced him again
with fifty, and with a hundred, outdoing Yuyudhana's quickness with his own
speed. As angry snakes rise up out of an anthill, king, so the arrows that cut
through bodies rose from Drona's chariot. In the same way the arrows loosed by
Yuyudhana, hundreds of thousands of them, eaters of blood, covered Drona's
chariot. We could find no difference, sir, between the quickness of the chief of
the brahmins and the quickness of the Satvata. The two bulls among men were
equal.

"Then Satyaki, in great anger, struck Drona hard with nine smooth-jointed
arrows. He struck his banner with sharp arrows, and his charioteer with a
hundred, while Bharadvaja's son looked on. The great warrior Drona saw how quick
Yuyudhana was. He pierced Satyaki with seventy arrows and each of his horses
with three, and with a single arrow he pierced the banner standing on Madhava's
chariot. With another arrow, a broad-headed one with golden feathers, he cut
great-souled Madhava's bow in the battle.

"Satyaki, the great warrior, threw the bow aside in anger. He seized a huge mace
and hurled it at Bharadvaja's son. The mace was iron, bound round with bands,
and it came at Drona fast. He held it off with many arrows of many kinds. Then
Satyaki, whose courage was true, took another bow and pierced the hero,
Bharadvaja's son, with many arrows whetted on stone.

"Having pierced Drona in the battle, he let out a lion's roar. Drona, the best
of all who bear weapons, could not bear it. He took up an iron spear with a
golden shaft and hurled it with force at Madhava's chariot. The spear was like
Death. It did not reach Shaineya, but it split his chariot and went into the
ground with a dreadful sound.

"Then Shini's grandson pierced Drona with an arrow, king. It struck his right
arm and hurt him, bull of the Bharatas. In the battle Drona cut Madhava's great
bow with a crescent-headed arrow, and struck his charioteer with a chariot
spear. Struck by the spear, the charioteer fainted. He sank down on the floor of
the chariot and lay there for a while.

"There, king, Satyaki did a deed beyond what men can do. He went on fighting
Drona, and he took the reins himself. Then Yuyudhana, the great warrior, full of
joy, pierced the brahmin with a hundred arrows in the battle, lord of the
people. Drona sent five arrows at him, Bharata. They split his armour and drank
his blood.

"Pierced by those terrible arrows, Satyaki grew very angry. The hero loosed his
arrows at the man in the golden chariot. With one arrow he brought Drona's
driver down to the ground, and with more arrows he sent the great man's horses
running, now that their driver was dead. The chariot ran wild across the field,
king. The silver chariot went round in thousands of circles, shining like the
sun.

"'After him! Catch them! Run for Drona's horses!' All the princes and the kings
shouted it. Those great warriors left Satyaki at once, king, and all of them ran
to where Drona was. Your army saw them all running, hurt by the Satvata's
arrows, and it broke again and fell into confusion.

"The horses, hurt by the Vrishni's arrows, carried Drona away, racing like the
wind, and he went back to the gate of the array and took his stand there. The
mighty man saw that the Pandavas and the Panchalas had broken the array. He made
no more effort over Shaineya, but set himself to guard the array. He held back
the Pandavas and the Panchalas, and the fire that was Drona stood there as if
burning them up. He blazed with the fire of his anger, like the sun that rises
at the end of time.

"When he had beaten Drona and your men led by Hardikya, best of the Kurus, the
hero of the Shinis laughed and spoke to his charioteer.

"'We are only the means here, Suta,' he said. 'Our enemies have been burnt
already by Keshava and Phalguna. We are killing men who were killed already, by
that bull among men who was born from the lord of the gods.'

"When he had said this, the bull of the Shinis, the finest of bowmen, the killer
of his enemies, strong as he was, scattered arrows on every side in the great
battle and swooped at once, as a hawk swoops on its meat. He went on, plunging
into the army with horses the colour of the moon and of conches, and no troop on
any side could stop him, Bharata. He was the first among men, and he was like
the rays of the sun. His courage could not be borne and his spirit never sank.
His power was like that of Indra with his thousand eyes, and he was like the sun
in the sky when the clouds have gone.

"Then Sudarshana, a fine king, full of anger, stood in his way and held him back
by force. He was a fighter of many skills, and he had a bow and golden armour. A
fierce fight broke out between the two of them, Bharata, and your fighters and
the Somakas praised it, as the hosts of the gods praised the fight of Vritra and
Indra.

"Sudarshana pierced the chief of the Satvatas with hundreds of very sharp arrows
in the battle. But the bull of the Shinis cut them with his own arrows before
they reached him. In the same way, whatever arrows Satyaki shot at Sudarshana,
Satyaki who was like Indra, Sudarshana cut them in two or three with his own
fine arrows, standing in his fine chariot. Sudarshana saw his arrows destroyed
by the force of Satyaki's arrows, and his fierce energy flared. In anger, as if
he wanted to burn Satyaki up, he loosed arrows worked with gold. Again he
pierced him with three arrows like fire, sharp and well-feathered, drawn back to
the ear. They split Satyaki's armour and went into his body. Then the king's son
fitted other blazing arrows and struck his four horses, bright as silver, with
four of them.

"Struck by him like that, Shini's swift grandson, who was Indra's equal in
courage, quickly killed Sudarshana's horses with very sharp arrows and roared.
Then the hero of the Shinis cut off the head of his charioteer with a broad
arrow like the thunderbolt, and with a razor arrow he cut off Sudarshana's head
by force. He cut from the body the shining face with its earrings, bright as the
full moon, as the holder of the thunderbolt, king, once cut off the head of
mighty Bala in battle.

"Having killed that son and grandson of kings in the battle, the swift bull of
the Yadus was filled with great joy, king, and shone like the king of the gods.
Then the hero went on the way Arjuna had gone. He held back your army with
floods of arrows, and his chariot with its fine horses carried him through, and
he meant to make the worlds wonder. The best of the fighters, gathered there,
honoured that deed, the most wonderful of all. Every enemy who came within reach
of his arrows, he burnt with them, as fire burns."

**Divergences.**

* **Satyaki's words.** \*658 would have him say "I, with Bhima and with Parshata," will kill those already killed by the one with the crown. It is rejected; in the CE he says only "we" (7.94.2).
* **Drona's charioteer.** \*657 repeats the killing of Drona's driver and has the red horses (*śoṇāśvāḥ*) run off with the chariot in every direction. It is rejected; the CE tells it once (7.93.28–29).
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*655, \*656, \*659.

**Choices.**

* 7.93.2 *balivāsavayor iva*: "like the fight of Bali and Indra". Bali is the asura king, Virochana's son.
* 7.93.6 *dvābhyāṃ dvābhyām*: "each one with two" arrows.
* 7.93.7 *viṃśatyā*: "twenty". Ganguli has thirty.
* 7.93.11 *dvijamukhyasya*: "the chief of the brahmins", literally of the twice-born.
* 7.93.13 *saptatyā sātyakiṃ viddhvā*: Drona pierces Satyaki himself with seventy arrows. Ganguli has the seventy strike his charioteer.
* 7.93.13 *dhvajam ekena vivyādha*: Drona "pierced" the banner with one arrow; Ganguli has him cut it off. The novel keeps "pierced", since Satyaki's banner is not said to fall.
* 7.93.16 *paṭṭabaddhām*: "bound round with bands".
* 7.93.22 *rathaśaktyā*: "with a chariot spear".
* 7.93.27 *rukmarathaṃ prati*: "at the man in the golden chariot", Drona, as in earlier passages.
* 7.93.29 *rājataḥ*: "the silver chariot". Ganguli reads "bright".
* 7.93.33: the horses carry Drona away, and he takes his stand again at the gate of the array.
* 7.93.35 *kālasūrya ivoditaḥ*: "like the sun that rises at the end of time".
* 7.94.1 *kurupuṃgavāgrya*: "best of the Kurus", Sanjaya's address to Dhritarashtra.
* 7.94.2 *nimittamātraṃ vayam atra*: "We are only the means here". *sureśātmasamudbhavena*: Arjuna, "born from the lord of the gods".
* 7.94.6 *aticitrayodhī*: "a fighter of many skills". *rājavaraḥ*: "a fine king". This Sudarshana is not otherwise identified here; 7.94.12 calls him *avanipālaputra*, "the king's son", and 7.94.16 *pārthivaputrapautra*, read as "son and grandson of kings" (Ganguli: "grandson of a prince").
* 7.94.15 *balasya … atibalasya*: "mighty Bala", the asura Indra killed.

summary: Drona turns on Satyaki and wears him down until he no longer knows what to do. Yudhishthira sends Dhrishtadyumna and the whole army to save him, and Drona receives them like guests, feeding them arrows, and kills twenty-five Panchala great warriors and a hundred Kekayas. Then Yudhishthira hears Krishna's conch sounding over the roar of the Kauravas, and no longer hears Gandiva. Fearing that Arjuna is in trouble, he begs Satyaki with tears to go after him. Dying for a friend is worth as much as giving away the earth. Arjuna once praised Satyaki above every Vrishni, and he himself has seen Satyaki's devotion. # Satyaki and Drona

Dhritarashtra said, "How did Yuyudhana hold Bharadvaja's son back in battle?
Tell me truly, Sanjaya. I am very curious."

Sanjaya said, "Hear, wise king, of the battle that made the hair stand on end,
between Drona and the Pandavas led by Yuyudhana. Seeing his army being killed by
Yuyudhana, sir, Drona himself rushed at Satyaki, whose courage was true. As the
great warrior Bharadvaja's son came at him fast, Satyaki struck him with
twenty-five small arrows. Drona too, bold in battle and steady, quickly pierced
Yuyudhana with five gold-feathered arrows whetted on stone. They split his very
strong armour and, feeding on the flesh of enemies, went into the ground, king,
like hissing snakes. The long-armed man, in great anger, like an elephant hurt
by the goad, pierced Drona with fifty iron arrows like fire. Pierced quickly in
the battle by Yuyudhana, Bharadvaja's son pierced Satyaki with many arrows as he
struggled. Then the mighty great bowman, angrier still, pressed the Satvata with
a hundred smooth-jointed arrows.

"Struck in the battle by Bharadvaja's son, Satyaki could not tell what to do,
lord of the people. Yuyudhana's face fell, king, as he watched Bharadvaja's son
loosing sharp arrows in the battle. Seeing him, your sons and your soldiers,
lord of the people, were glad at heart and roared again and again like lions.

"Yudhishthira heard that dreadful roar and saw Madhava hard pressed. He said to
all his armies, king, Bharata, 'This hero, the best of the Vrishnis, Satyaki,
whose deeds are true, is being swallowed in battle by a hero, like the sun by
Rahu. Rush there, go where Satyaki is fighting.' And he said this to
Dhrishtadyumna of Panchala, lord of men: 'Rush at Drona quickly. Why do you
stand there, Prishata's grandson? Do you not see the dreadful danger that has
come on us from Drona? That great bowman Drona is playing with Yuyudhana in
battle as a boy plays with a bird tied on a string. Let all the chariot
fighters, led by Bhimasena, go there with you, ready, to Yuyudhana's chariot. I
will follow behind you with my soldiers. Save Satyaki today. He has gone between
the teeth of Yama.'

"Saying this, the Pandava king rushed at Drona in battle with his whole army,
for Yuyudhana's sake. Then there was a great uproar, bless you, from the
Pandavas and the Srinjayas on every side, as they all wanted to fight Drona
alone. Those tigers among men came up to the great warrior Bharadvaja's son and
poured sharp arrows on him, feathered with heron and peacock feathers. Drona,
smiling, received the heroes himself, as a host receives guests who have come,
with water and a seat. They were filled with the arrows of Bharadvaja's son the
bowman, king, as guests are filled when they come to a hospitable house.

"None of them could look at Bharadvaja's son, lord, any more than at the
thousand-rayed sun at midday. Drona, best of all who bear weapons, scorched all
those great bowmen with flights of arrows, as the sun does with its rays. Struck
in the battle, king, the Pandavas and the Srinjayas found no one to save them,
like elephants sunk in mud. Drona's great arrows were seen spreading out like
the rays of the sun, burning on every side.

"Drona killed twenty-five Panchalas there, known as great warriors and valued by
Dhrishtadyumna. In all the armies of the Pandavas and of the Panchalas, men saw
brave Drona killing the best of the best. Having killed a hundred of the Kekayas
and scattered them on every side, Drona stood, great king, like Death the Ender
with his mouth open. The strong-armed Drona beat the Panchalas, the Srinjayas,
the Matsyas, the Kekayas and the Pandavas, by the hundred and the thousand. As
they were struck by Drona's arrows, the noise from them was like the noise of
forest creatures in a wood burned by fire. There the gods, with the Gandharvas
and the ancestors, said, king, 'Look, the Panchalas and the Pandavas are
running, with their soldiers.' And as Drona killed the Somakas in the battle
like this, no one went against him, and no one pierced him.

"While that dreadful slaughter of the best heroes went on, the son of Pritha
heard, all at once, the sound of Panchajanya. The king of conches, blown by
Vasudeva, sounded loud. The heroes guarding the Saindhava were fighting, and
Dhritarashtra's men were roaring at Vijaya's chariot. And the sound of Gandiva
was lost on every side. The Pandava king, struck with dismay, thought, 'Surely
all is not well with Partha, from the way the king of conches is sounding and
the Kauravas are roaring with joy again and again.'

"Thinking this, with his heart in turmoil, Kunti's son Ajatashatru spoke to the
Satvata, fainting again and again, his voice choked with tears, looking to what
had to be done next. He said to Shaineya, the bull of the Shinis:

"'The eternal dharma that good men saw long ago, Shaineya, of helping a friend
in battle, has come to its time. When I think of all our fighters, bull of the
Shinis, I know no one who is a better friend than you, Satyaki. The man who is
always loving and always loyal is the one to set to a task in battle. So I
think. As Keshava is always the Pandavas' refuge, so are you, Varshneya, equal
to Krishna in courage. I will lay a burden on you, and you should carry it. You
should not make my wish vain. In this trouble, bull among men, help Arjuna in
battle, who is your brother, your friend and your teacher.

"'You are true to your vow and brave, and you make your friends safe. You are
known in the world for your deeds, hero, as a man whose word is true. Shaineya,
a man who gives up his body fighting for a friend, and a man who gives the earth
to brahmins, are equal. We have heard of many kings who have gone to heaven
after giving this whole earth to brahmins in the proper way. So I too beg you,
righteous one, with my palms joined. The fruit would equal the gift of the
earth, lord, or be greater. Krishna alone always makes his friends safe and
gives up his life in battle, and you are the second, Satyaki. For a bold hero
who seeks fame in battle, only a brave man can be a helper, and no ordinary man.
Madhava, in such a crush as this, there is no one but you to protect Vijaya in
battle.

"'The Pandava praised your deeds by the hundred, and gave me joy by telling of
them again and again. He said, "He is quick with weapons, a wonderful fighter
and quick in courage, wise, a knower of all weapons, brave, and never confused
in battle. He has great shoulders, a great chest, great arms and a great bow,
great strength and great courage, that great-souled great warrior. He is my
pupil and my friend. I am dear to him and he is dear to me. With Yuyudhana to
help me, I will crush the Kauravas. King of kings, Keshava might arm himself for
us, or Rama, or Aniruddha, or the great warrior Pradyumna, or Gada, or Sarana,
or Samba with the Vrishnis, to help us at the very head of battle. Even then,
great king, I would still choose Shaineya, the tiger among men, whose courage is
true, to help me. There is no one else equal to him."

"'So Dhananjaya spoke to me in the Dvaita forest, my son, telling of your true
virtues in an assembly of noble men while you were not there. You should not
make his hope vain, Varshneya, Dhananjaya's, or mine, or Bhima's. When I was
going round the holy places, I came to Dvaraka, and there I saw your devotion to
Arjuna. I have not seen in others such friendship, Shaineya, as you show us in
our trouble. Think of your birth and your devotion, your friendship and your
teaching, your affection, your courage and your good family, Madhava. Think of
your truth, strong-armed one, and your pity. Then, great bowman, you should do a
deed worthy of them.

"'Suyodhana has gone off in haste, armoured by Drona, and the great warriors of
the Kauravas went before him. A very great noise is heard around Vijaya. You
should go there fast, Shaineya, Madhava. Bhimasena and I, ready, with our
soldiers, will hold Drona back if he goes after you. Look, Shaineya, at the
armies running in the battle. Hear the great noise in the battle, and see the
Bharata host torn apart. As the sea is tossed by a great gale at the change of
the moon, so Dhritarashtra's son's army has been scattered by the Left-Handed
Archer, my son. With chariots, men and horses running everywhere, the army
whirls round in the dust it raises.

"'Phalguna, killer of enemy heroes, is surrounded by the Sindhu-Sauviras, brave
men, well honoured, who fight with claw-shaped lances. Jayadratha cannot be
killed unless this army is held back. All these have given up their lives for
the Saindhava. Look at the division of Dhritarashtra's sons, a forest of arrows,
spears and banners, crowded with horses and elephants, very hard to come at.
Hear the roar of the drums and the many conches, the lion roars and the rumble
of chariot wheels. Hear the sound of the elephants and the foot soldiers in
their thousands, and of the horsemen running and shaking the earth. In front is
the Saindhava's division, and behind is Drona's division. By its numbers it
could crush even the king of the gods, tiger among men. Sunk in that endless
army, he might even give up his life. And if he were killed in battle, how could
someone like me live? I have come to the hardest of lives.

"'The dark young Pandava, Gudakesha, handsome, quick with weapons and a
wonderful fighter, went into the Bharata host at sunrise, my son, and now the
day is passing. I do not know, Varshneya, whether he is alive or not. And the
army of the Kurus is great, like the sea. Bibhatsu went alone, my son, into the
Bharata host, which even the gods could not bear in a great battle. My mind will
not work at all in battle today. Drona too, fierce in battle, is pressing my
army. You see for yourself, strong-armed one, how that brahmin moves. You are
wise in tasks that all come at once, Madhava. You should do the great one with
the light one.

"'Of all my tasks, this is always the one I think must be done: to protect
Arjuna in battle. I do not grieve for the Dasharha, the protector and lord of
the world. He could beat the three worlds in battle if they all came together,
my son. I tell you the truth, tiger among men. How much less this very weak army
of Dhritarashtra's son? But Arjuna, Varshneya, pressed by many in battle, might
give up his life. That is why I am in despair. Go along his track, as one like
you should go for one like him at a time like this, urged by one like me.

"'Among the Vrishni heroes only two are known as great beyond other warriors,
strong-armed Pradyumna and you, famous Satvata. In weapons you are equal to
Narayana, in strength to Samkarshana, and in courage, tiger among men, to
Dhananjaya. Good men in the world call you, tiger among men, skilled in every
kind of battle, above Bhishma and Drona. "Nothing in the world is impossible for
Satyaki," they say, Madhava. So do what I tell you, mighty one. The world thinks
highly of you, and so does Partha. You should not make that otherwise,
strong-armed one. Give up your dear life and move in battle like a hero. The
Dasharhas do not guard their lives in battle, Shaineya. Not fighting, not
standing firm, running from battle, that is the way of cowards and bad men. The
Dasharhas do not follow it.

"'Arjuna is your teacher, my son, the righteous bull of the Shinis. And Vasudeva
is the teacher of both you and wise Partha. Knowing these two reasons, I have
spoken to you. Do not scorn my words. I am your teacher's elder. This is
Vasudeva's wish, and mine, and Arjuna's. I have told you the truth. Go where
Dhananjaya is. Take my words to heart, you whose courage is true, and go into
this army of evil-minded Dhritarashtra's son, my son. Go in as you should, meet
the great warriors, and show in battle deeds worthy of yourself, Satvata.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Yudhishthira's order to Dhrishtadyumna.** In the CE he tells him to rush at Drona and save Satyaki (7.85.15–18). \*575, "stop Drona, let the brahmin not kill Satyaki", is rejected.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*576–\*581. \*581 has people everywhere say Satyaki could fight the gods, asuras and Gandharvas.

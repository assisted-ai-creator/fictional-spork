# Jalasandha

"Hear with an undivided mind, king, what you ask me," said Sanjaya. "The army
was being put to flight by great-souled Hardikya. The Pandavas were bowed with
shame and your men were full of joy. Shaineya was an island for the Pandavas as
they looked for a footing in the bottomless deep. He heard the dreadful roar of
your men in the great battle and went quickly at Kritavarma, king.

"Kritavarma Hardikya, in great anger, covered Shaineya with sharp arrows, and
Satyaki grew angry. Shaineya sent a very sharp broad arrow at Kritavarma in the
battle, and four other arrows. The arrows killed his horses, the broad arrow cut
his bow, and he pierced his back guard and his charioteer with sharp arrows.
Having left him without a chariot, Satyaki, whose courage was true, pressed his
army hard with smooth-jointed arrows. The army broke, hurt by Shaineya's arrows,
and then Satyaki, whose courage was true, went on quickly.

"Hear, king, what the mighty man did among your armies. When he had passed that
great sea of Drona's division, great king, and beaten Kritavarma in battle, the
hero, full of joy, said to his driver, 'Go slowly, calmly.' He saw your army
crowded with chariots, horses and elephants and filled with foot soldiers. He
spoke to his charioteer again: 'That great mass of elephants, like a cloud, on
the left of Drona's division, has Rukmaratha at its head. There are many of
them, Suta, and they are hard to hold back in battle. Duryodhana has sent them
against me, and they have given up their lives. They are princes, great bowmen,
all bold fighters. The fine chariot fighters of the Trigartas, with banners
worked in gold, stand facing me, ready to fight. Take me there quickly. Urge the
horses on, charioteer. I will fight the Trigartas while Bharadvaja's son
watches.'

"Then, doing as the Satvata wished, the charioteer went on slowly, in the bright
chariot with its flags, the colour of the sun. The fine horses, obedient to the
charioteer, drew him, prancing, as swift as the wind in battle, bright as
jasmine, the moon and silver. As the chariot came on with its fine horses, the
colour of conches, the brave men surrounded it on every side with the mass of
elephants, scattering sharp arrows of every kind that pierced lightly. The
Satvata fought the mass of elephants with sharp arrows, as a great cloud at the
end of the hot season rains on mountains. Struck by arrows like the touch of the
thunderbolt, sent by the strength of the Shini hero, the elephants left the
battle and fled.

"Their tusks were broken and they streamed blood. Their heads and temples were
split, and their ears, faces and trunks torn. They had lost their drivers and
their flags, their armour and bells were split and their great banners cut down.
With their riders killed and their blankets fallen, they ran to every quarter,
king. They made cries of every kind, with a sound like thunderclouds, torn open
by the Satvata's iron arrows and calf-tooth arrows.

"When the mass of elephants had fled, the great warrior Jalasandha came
carefully, bringing his elephant up to the chariot with the silver horses. The
hero had golden arms and golden armlets, and was clean, with earrings, a crown
and a conch, smeared with red sandal paste. He wore a blazing garland of gold on
his head and a gold ornament and a shining necklace on his chest. Shaking his
bow worked in gold on the elephant's head, he shone, great king, like a cloud
with lightning. As the Magadha's great elephant came at him, Satyaki held it
back as the shore holds back the swollen sea.

"Seeing his elephant held back by Shaineya's fine arrows, the mighty Jalasandha
grew angry in the battle, king. Then the great bowman, in anger, pierced Shini's
grandson in his broad chest with arrows able to bear any weight. With another
sharp, well-tempered broad arrow he cut the Vrishni hero's bow as he shot. As if
laughing, Bharata, the brave Magadha pierced Satyaki with five sharp arrows, his
bow cut. Pierced by many arrows from Jalasandha, the mighty strong-armed man did
not flinch, and it was a wonder. The strong man took little notice of the
arrows, and was not troubled. He took another bow and said, 'Stand, stand!'
Having said this much, Shaineya, as if laughing, pierced Jalasandha very hard in
his broad chest with sixty arrows. With a well-tempered razor arrow he cut
Jalasandha's great bow at the grip and pierced him with three arrows.

"Jalasandha threw away that bow with its arrow and quickly hurled a lance at
Satyaki, sir. It split Madhava's left arm in the great battle and went into the
ground, dreadful, like a great hissing snake. With his left arm split, Satyaki,
whose courage was true, struck Jalasandha with thirty sharp arrows. Then mighty
Jalasandha seized a sword and a great bull-hide shield adorned with a hundred
moons. He whirled the sword and let it fly at the Satvata. It cut Shaineya's bow
and fell to the ground, and on the ground it gleamed like a wheel of fire.

"Then Madhava took another bow, able to split any body, like the trunk of a sal
tree, sounding like great Indra's thunderbolt. In anger he drew it and pierced
Jalasandha with an arrow. Then, as if laughing, the best of the Madhavas cut off
Jalasandha's arms, with their ornaments and armlets, with two razor arrows. The
two arms, like iron bars, fell from the fine elephant like two five-headed
snakes fallen from a mountain. Then with a third razor arrow Satyaki cut off his
head, with its fine teeth, fine jaw, lovely earrings and high nose. The trunk,
its head and arms struck off, was dreadful to see, and drenched Jalasandha's
elephant with blood. Having killed Jalasandha in battle, the Satvata quickly
brought down the elephant driver from the elephant's shoulder, lord of the
people. Its body soaked in blood, Jalasandha's elephant carried the dangling
body still clinging to the fine seat. Hurt by the Satvata's arrows, the great
elephant crushed its own army, gave a dreadful cry of pain and ran.

"A great cry of 'Alas!' went up from your army, sir, when they saw Jalasandha
killed by the bull of the Vrishnis. Your fighters turned and ran on every side,
eager to flee and with no heart for beating the enemy.

"Meanwhile, king, Drona, best of all who bear weapons, came with his swift
horses at the great warrior Yuyudhana. Seeing Shaineya rising up like that, the
bulls of the Kurus, in anger, surrounded Satyaki together with Drona. Then a
dreadful battle began between the Kurus and the Satvata, and Drona, king, like
the battle of the gods and the asuras.

"All of them, ready hard strikers, scattering flights of arrows, fought
Yuyudhana in haste, great king. Drona struck him with seventy-seven sharp
arrows, Durmarshana with twelve and Duhsaha with ten. Vikarna pierced him with
thirty sharp heron-feathered arrows in the left side and between the breasts.
Durmukha pierced Shaineya with ten arrows, Duhshasana with eight and Chitrasena
with two, sir. Duryodhana pressed Madhava in the battle with a great rain of
arrows, king, and so did other brave great warriors. Pierced on every side by
your sons, the great warriors, Shaineya pierced each of them in turn with
straight arrows. He pierced Bharadvaja's son with three arrows and Duhsaha with
nine, Vikarna with twenty-five and Chitrasena with seven, Durmarshana with
twelve and Vivimshati with four, Satyavrata with nine and Vijaya with ten.

"Then the great warrior, shaking his bow with its golden bands, went quickly at
your son, the great warrior. He struck hard with his arrows the king of the
whole world, the best of all who bear weapons, and a battle began between them.
Loosing sharp arrows and setting others to the string, the two great warriors
made each other vanish in the battle. Pierced by the Kuru king, Satyaki shone
brightly, streaming blood like a sandal tree streaming its own sap. Pierced by
the Satvata's floods of arrows, your son, with his golden crest, shone like a
sacrificial post raised high.

"Then Madhava, as if laughing, cut the Kuru king's bow with a razor arrow in the
battle, king, and covered him with many arrows as he stood with his bow cut.
Pierced by the arrows of his quick-handed enemy, the king could not bear this
sign of his enemy's victory in battle. He took another bow, backed with gold and
hard to face, and quickly pierced Satyaki with a hundred arrows. Pierced deep by
your son, the strong bowman, Satyaki, in the grip of rage, pressed your son
hard.

"Seeing the king pressed, your sons, the great warriors, quickly covered the
Satvata with a rain of arrows. Covered by your many sons, the great warriors, he
pierced each of them with five arrows, and again with seven. Quickly he pierced
Duryodhana with eight swift arrows, and laughing, he cut his bow, which
frightened his enemies. With his arrows the famous man brought down his banner,
the snake made of jewels. He killed his four horses with four sharp arrows and
felled his charioteer with a razor arrow. Meanwhile, full of joy, he covered the
great warrior, the Kuru king, with many arrows that split the vital places.
Struck in the battle by Shaineya's fine arrows, your son Duryodhana ran off at
once, king, and leapt onto the chariot of Chitrasena the bowman.

"The whole world cried out 'Alas!' when it saw the king in battle being
swallowed by Satyaki, like the moon in the sky by Rahu. Hearing that great
noise, the great warrior Kritavarma went at once to where the lord Madhava was,
shaking his fine bow, urging on his horses and threatening his charioteer, 'Go
on, go on!' Seeing him come on like Death the Ender with his mouth open,
Yuyudhana, great king, said to his driver, 'Here is Kritavarma, coming fast in
his chariot with his arrows. Go out to meet him in the chariot, the best of all
bowmen.'

"Then with his horses urged to full speed and his chariot fitted out in the
proper way, he met the Bhoja in battle, the model of bowmen. Then the two tigers
among men met, in the greatest anger, like two blazing fires, like two swift
tigers. Kritavarma struck Shaineya with twenty-six sharp arrows, and his driver
with seven. With four fine arrows he pierced the Satvata's four fine horses,
well-trained Sindhu horses. With his golden banner, his golden armlets and his
golden armour, he drew his great bow, backed with gold, and scattered
gold-feathered arrows.

"Then Shini's grandson, in haste, eager to see Dhananjaya, sent eighty arrows at
Kritavarma. Pierced deep by his strong enemy, the scorcher of enemies, hard to
face as he was, shook like a mountain in an earthquake. Satyaki quickly pierced
Kritavarma's four horses with sixty-three sharp arrows and his charioteer with
seven. Then Satyaki set on his bow a gold-feathered arrow, with a great flame,
like an angry snake, and loosed it. That arrow, like the staff of Yama, went
into Kritavarma, split his bright armour worked with gold, and went into the
ground, fierce and soaked in blood. Bleeding in the battle, hurt by the
Satvata's arrows, he reeled, let go his bow and fell in his fine chariot. The
bull among men, lion-toothed, of boundless courage, sank to his knees in his
chariot box, hurt by Satyaki's arrows.

"Having held back Kritavarma, who was like the Thousand-Armed and not to be
stirred, like the sea, Satyaki went on. The bull of the Shinis went through the
middle of the army. It was strewn with swords, spears and bows and crowded with
elephants, horses and chariots, and there bulls among Kshatriyas had set fierce
blood flowing by the hundred. While all the armies watched, he broke through the
host, as the killer of Vritra broke through the army of the asuras. And
Hardikya, mighty as he was, came round, took another great bow and stood there,
holding back the Pandavas in battle."

**Divergences.**

* **Satyaki's plan.** He says he will fight the Trigartas "while Bharadvaja's son watches" (7.91.15). The CE then tells of the elephants and Jalasandha, and the Trigartas are not mentioned again in this passage.
* **Duryodhana and his brothers.** \*642, in which Duryodhana and his brothers attack Satyaki as soon as Jalasandha falls, is rejected. In the CE they come with Drona (7.91.53) and fight at 7.92.2–24.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*627–\*641, \*643–\*654. \*629 has Satyaki say he cannot reach Jayadratha or Partha without beating these troops; \*654 has him fight many more Kshatriyas on his way.

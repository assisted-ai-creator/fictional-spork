summary: Dhritarashtra asks what his men did on the morning after Abhimanyu's death, knowing what Arjuna could do. He mourns the silence that has fallen over the tents of his sons and their allies, and recalls how he begged Duryodhana to make peace through Krishna and was ignored. No one alive, he says, can fight the Pandavas. Sanjaya answers that the fault is the king's own. He could have stopped the dicing, stopped the war, or reined in his son, and his grief now is like building a dam when the water has gone. The Kauravas are doing all that brave men can. Then Sanjaya tells the fourteenth morning. Drona himself stands at the mouth of the cart, and Duryodhana rejoices.

# The Cart and the Needle

Dhritarashtra said, "What did they do the next morning, full of pain and grief?
With Abhimanyu killed, which of my men fought there? They knew what the
Left-Handed Archer could do. Tell me, how could my men do that wrong and still
have no fear? How could they look at that tiger among men as he came on in
battle, burning with grief for his son and angry as Death the Ender? What did my
men do when they saw him in the fight, the man with the king of monkeys on his
banner, shaking his great bow, wasted with grief for his son?

"What happened to Duryodhana in the battle, Sanjaya? I hear great mourning
there, and no rejoicing. The sounds that used to fill the heart and please the
ear are not heard today in the Saindhava's quarters. In my sons' camp I do not
hear today the troops of minstrels and bards, the dancers, all singing praise.
My ears were always ringing with that sound, and today I do not hear it rise
from men who are downcast. Sitting in the house of Somadatta, who holds to the
truth, I used to hear the finest sound, my son. Today, my merit spent, I see my
sons' quarters echoing with cries of pain and robbed of heart. The sound is not
what it was in the tents of Vivimshati, Durmukha, Chitrasena and Vikarna, or of
my other sons.

"Drona's son, the great bowman, was my sons' refuge. Brahmins, Kshatriyas and
Vaishyas and his pupils waited on him. Day and night he took his pleasure in
argument and talk, in offerings, requests and greetings, and in songs of every
kind that he loved. Many of the Kurus, the Pandavas and the Satvatas waited on
him. Today, Suta, the sound in his house is not what it was. The singers and
dancers who were always about the great bowman are not heard. The great noise
heard every evening in the camp of Vinda and Anuvinda is not heard the same
today, nor in the quarters of the Kekayas. The loud sound of clapping and song
from the troops who danced there, always merry, is not heard today, my son. The
priests who served Somadatta's son, a treasure of learning, as they spread out
his sacrifices, are not heard. The twang of bowstrings, the sound of the Vedas,
the noise of spears, swords and chariots never stopped in Drona's house. I do
not hear it. The great sound of songs from many lands, and of instruments
playing, is not heard today.

"When Janardana, Achyuta, came from Upaplavya wanting peace, out of pity for all
beings, I said to foolish Duryodhana, 'Make peace with the Pandavas, my son,
through Vasudeva. I think the time has come. Do not go against me, Duryodhana.
If you refuse Keshava when he asks for peace and speaks for our good, you will
not stay unbeaten.' He refused the Dasharha, the bull among all bowmen, who
spoke to win him over. He would not take it, from folly. The fool left me and
followed Duhshasana and Karna, dragged along by Time.

"I told my son, 'I do not want the dicing. Vidura does not praise it. The
Saindhava does not want the dicing, and nor does Bhishma. Shalya, Bhurishravas,
Purumitra and Jaya, Ashvatthama, Kripa and Drona do not want the dicing,
Sanjaya. If my son would follow what they think, he would live long and well,
with his kin and friends and allies. The Pandavas are gentle and sweet-spoken,
kind of word among their kin, well born, respected and wise. They will find
happiness. A man who looks to dharma finds happiness everywhere, always, and
after death he finds blessing and grace. They have the strength and the means,
and they deserve to enjoy half the earth. For them too the sea-bounded earth
belongs to their fathers and grandfathers. If they are charged to, the Pandavas
will keep to the path of dharma. We have kinsmen whom the Pandavas will listen
to, my son: Shalya, Somadatta and great Bhishma, Drona, Vikarna, Bahlika, Kripa
and the other great elders of the Bharatas. If they speak for you, my son, the
Pandavas will do as they advise for your good. Which of them do you think would
tell you anything else? Krishna would not give up dharma, and they all follow
him. And when I speak to those heroes words bound up with dharma, they will not
act otherwise. The Pandavas are righteous.'

"So I spoke to my son again and again, Suta, wailing, and the fool did not
listen to me. I think it is the turning of Time. Wolf-Belly and Arjuna are on
that side, and the Vrishni hero Satyaki, Uttamaujas of Panchala and Yudhamanyu,
hard to beat, Dhrishtadyumna whom no one can face and Shikhandi who has never
been beaten. The Ashmakas are there, and the Kekayas and Kshatradharma the
Somaka, the Chedi king and Chekitana, and the mighty son of the king of Kashi,
the sons of Draupadi, Virata and the great warrior Drupada, and the twins,
tigers among men, and the slayer of Madhu to counsel them. Who in this world
would fight them, if he wanted to live? Those tamers of enemies would sweep
everyone away with their heavenly weapons. Apart from Duryodhana and Karna, and
Shakuni, Subala's son, and Duhshasana as the fourth, I see no fifth.

"Those who have Vishvaksena in their chariot, reins in hand, and Arjuna armed as
their fighter, cannot be beaten. Duryodhana will not remember my wailing now.
You tell me that Bhishma and Drona, tigers among men, have been killed. My sons
see what Vidura's far-seeing words have come to, and I think they grieve. As a
great fire driven by the wind burns dry brush at the end of winter, so
Dhananjaya will burn my army.

"Tell us all of it, Sanjaya, for you tell it well. When Abhimanyu was killed, my
son, and you had done Partha that wrong in the evening, what was in your minds?
Having done the wielder of Gandiva so great a wrong, my men will never be able
to bear what he does in battle. What did Duryodhana say must be done? What did
Karna say? And Duhshasana and Subala's son, and all my sons gathered together,
when things had come to this? Tell me what happened in battle, my son, through
the misdeeds of that fool, twisted by greed, warped by anger, greedy for a
kingdom, deluded, his mind struck by passion. Whether it was planned well or
badly, tell me, Sanjaya."

Sanjaya said, "I will tell you everything, for I saw it with my own eyes. Be
steady and listen. The great fault is yours. Your wailing, king, is like a dam
built after the water has gone. It is useless. Do not grieve, bull of the
Bharatas. This strange law of the Ender cannot be passed over. Do not grieve,
best of the Bharatas. This was fated long ago.

"If you had kept Kunti's son Yudhishthira and your sons from the dicing, this
disaster would not have come to you. If you had held them back when the time for
war came and they were angry, this disaster would not have come to you. If you
had once told the Kurus, 'Bind Duryodhana, he will not be ruled,' this disaster
would not have come to you. Now the Pandavas, the Panchalas, all the Vrishnis
and the other great men will see how your judgement failed. If you had done a
father's duty, set your son on the good path and kept to dharma yourself, this
disaster would not have come to you.

"You are the wisest man in the world, and you left the eternal dharma and
followed what Duryodhana, Karna and Shakuni thought. I have listened to all your
wailing, king. You were set on gain, and it is like honey mixed with poison.
Once Krishna did not think as highly of the Pandava king, or of Bhishma, or of
Drona, as he thought of you, king. But from the time he knew you had fallen from
a king's dharma, Krishna has not thought so highly of you. You let harsh words
be said to the Parthas and did nothing, wanting the kingdom for your sons, and
now the price of it has reached you. The kingdom of your fathers and
grandfathers was lost then, sinless one, and then you came to have the whole
earth, won by the Parthas. Pandu won the kingdom and the fame of the Kurus, and
the Pandavas, living by dharma, added more still. All they did came to nothing
once it reached you, since you, greedy for the prize, cast them out of their
father's kingdom.

"Now that the war has come, king, you blame your sons and list their faults at
length. That is not fitting now. Kings who fight in battle do not guard their
lives. These bulls among Kshatriyas plunge into the Parthas' army and fight. The
army guarded by Krishna and Arjuna, by Satyaki and Wolf-Belly, who but the
Kauravas would fight it? Their fighter is Gudakesha, their counsellor Janardana,
their guards Satyaki and Wolf-Belly. What mortal bowman could stand to fight
them, apart from the Kauravas and those who follow in their steps? Whatever can
be done by loyal kings, brave men who love a Kshatriya's dharma, the Kauravas
are doing. Now hear truly how the Kurus fought the Pandavas, those tigers among
men, in a battle of the utmost danger.

"When that night had passed, Drona, best of those who bear weapons, began to
draw up all his divisions. Brave men roared, king, angry and impatient, and
strange words were heard from men eager to kill each other. They stretched their
bows and ran their hands along the strings, and sighing, they shouted, 'Where is
Dhananjaya now?' Some tossed up swords, out of their sheaths, with fine hilts,
sharp and ready, bright as the sky. Thousands of brave men, their minds on
battle, were seen going through the moves of sword and bow as they had been
taught. Others raised maces hung with bells, smeared with sandal paste and set
with gold and diamonds, and asked for the Pandava. Others, drunk with the pride
of strength, strong in the arm, crowded the sky with iron bars like raised
banners of Indra. Other brave men, their minds on battle, stood here and there
with weapons of every kind, wearing bright garlands. They called out challenges
in the battle: 'Where is Arjuna? Where is Govinda? Where is proud Wolf-Belly?
Where are their friends?'

"Then Drona blew his conch, urged on his horses himself, and moved fast here and
there, setting them in their places. When all those divisions, who loved battle,
stood in their places, great king, Bharadvaja's son said to Jayadratha, 'You and
Somadatta's son and the great warrior Karna, Ashvatthama and Shalya, Vrishasena
and Kripa, with a hundred thousand horse, sixty thousand chariots, fourteen
thousand elephants in rut and twenty-one thousand armoured foot, stand three
gavyutis away, some twelve miles, and do not come near me. There the gods with
Vasava could not stand against you, still less all the Pandavas. Take heart,
Saindhava.'

"Told this, Jayadratha, king of Sindhu, took heart. He went off with the
Gandharas, surrounded by those great warriors, and with armoured horsemen, ready
and firm, lances in hand. All Jayadratha's horses, king of kings, wore yak-tail
crests and gold ornaments and ran well. There were seven thousand of them, and
two thousand Sindhu horses besides.

"Your son Durmarshana, ready to fight, took his place at the head of the whole
army with fifteen hundred elephants, maddened, dreadful to see, armoured and
fierce in their work, ridden by skilled drivers. Then your sons Duhshasana and
Vikarna took their places in the front ranks, to win the Sindhu king's cause.

"The array Bharadvaja's son made was a wheel and a cart. It was twelve gavyutis
long and five wide at the rear. Drona himself had formed it, with brave kings of
many lands set here and there, and floods of chariots, horses, elephants and
foot. In its rear half was a lotus, an array within the array, very hard to
break. In the middle of the lotus another array was made, hidden, a needle.

"So Drona formed this great array and took his place. At the needle's mouth
stood the great bowman Kritavarma. Next to him stood the Kamboja king and
Jalasandha, sir, and next to them Duryodhana with his ministers. Then hundreds
of thousands of fighters who would never turn back were all set in the cart to
guard the needle. Behind them, king, surrounded by a great force, Jayadratha
took his place at the side of the needle.

"At the mouth of the cart, king of kings, stood Bharadvaja's son, and behind him
was the Bhoja, guarding him himself. In white armour, clothes and turban,
broad-chested and strong-armed, Drona stood stretching his bow, like Death the
Ender in his anger. The Kurus saw Drona's chariot, with its flags and red
horses, and its banner of an altar and a black antelope skin, and they were
glad. The hosts of Siddhas and Charanas were filled with wonder to see the array
Drona had made, like a churning sea. All beings thought the array would swallow
the whole earth, with its mountains, seas and forests and its many peoples.

"Many chariots, men, horses, foot and elephants were in it, with a noise that
struck fear, a wonder to look at, fit to break the hearts of the enemy. The king
looked at the great cart that had been made, and rejoiced."

**Divergences.**

* **Bhishma and Drona.** At 7.61.44 Dhritarashtra says "you tell me the tigers among men, Bhishma and Drona, have been killed" (*hatau … bhīṣmadroṇau*). Drona is still alive at this point. The novel keeps the CE's words. G has only Bhishma.
* **Jayadratha's horses.** 7.63.17 gives "seven thousand, and two thousand Saindhavas". G has seven thousand and three thousand.
* **Jayadratha's place.** The CE puts him at the "side" of the needle (*sūcipāśe*, 7.63.27), behind the hundreds of thousands who guard it. Drona told him to stand three gavyutis from Drona himself (7.63.14).
* **The Bhoja.** Kritavarma stands at the needle's mouth (7.63.24), and "the Bhoja" also guards Drona at the mouth of the cart (7.63.28). The CE does not explain how he is in both places.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*446–\*458. \*454 has Sanjaya say "first you shave your head, then ask about the stars" (asking for a lucky day after the deed). \*453 has the Kaurava army already beaten by Satyaki and Arjuna.

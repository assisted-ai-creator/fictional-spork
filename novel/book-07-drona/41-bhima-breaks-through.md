summary: Bhima routs Dhritarashtra's sons with his mace, walks into Drona's rain of arrows with his eyes shut and throws Drona's chariot aside by its pole, breaks through the Bhojas and Kambojas and sees Arjuna; his roar tells Yudhishthira that Arjuna and Satyaki are alive. Dhritarashtra asks who faced Bhima. Karna does, and Bhima cuts his bowstring and kills his horses and driver, and Karna goes on fighting him from Vrishasena's chariot. Duryodhana reproaches Drona for letting three warriors past. Drona tells him the battle is the dice game Shakuni began, with Jayadratha for the stake, and sends him to guard the Saindhava's guards. # Bhima Breaks Through

"Bhima came out of the chariot force like the sun out of darkness," said
Sanjaya, "and the teacher, wanting to hold him, covered him with rains of
arrows. As if he were drinking down the floods of arrows from Drona's fine bow,
Bhima went at his cousins, confusing the army with his craft. Urged on by your
sons, the finest bowmen surrounded him on every side in the battle at their
greatest speed.

"Surrounded like that, Bharata, Bhima, as if laughing, raised his dreadful mace
at them, roaring like a lion, and the strong man hurled it at them with all his
force and crushed them. Like Indra's thunderbolt hurled by Indra with his mind
set on it, the mace filled the earth, as it were, with its great noise, king.
Blazing with fire, dreadful, it terrified your sons. When they saw it falling at
great speed, wrapped in fire, all your men ran, crying dreadful cries. When they
heard its sound, sir, which could not be borne, men fell there, and chariot
fighters fell from their chariots. The son of Kunti routed his enemies in the
battle, hard to face as they were, and passed through the army with the speed of
Suparna, the king of birds.

"As Bhimasena harried the leaders of the chariot troops like that, great king,
Bharadvaja's son came at him. Drona held Bhima back in the battle with waves of
arrows, and all at once roared, striking fear into the Pandavas. That was a
great and dreadful fight, great king, like the battle of the gods and the
asuras, between Drona and great-souled Bhima.

"When the heroes were being killed in the battle by hundreds and thousands with
the sharp arrows loosed from Drona's bow, the Pandava leapt from his chariot and
ran at his greatest speed. He shut his eyes, king, and went at Drona on foot. As
a bull takes the rain as if it were play, so Bhima, the tiger among men, took
the rain of arrows. Struck down in the battle as he was, sir, the mighty man
seized Drona's chariot by the pole with his hand and flung it away. Thrown aside
by Bhima in the battle, king, Drona quickly took his stand in another chariot
and went to the gate of the array.

"At that moment Bhimasena's charioteer quickly urged on his horses, Kaurava, and
it seemed a wonder. Then mighty Bhimasena took his stand in his own chariot and
ran at full speed at your son's army. He crushed kshatriyas in the battle as a
rising wind crushes trees, and he went on, splitting the army as a river in
flood splits the trees.

"He came to the Bhoja division, guarded by Hardikya, crushed it in many places,
king, and went on. Terrifying the divisions with the slapping of his palms, sir,
he beat all the armies, as a tiger beats bulls. He passed the Bhoja division and
the army of the Kambojas, and many other troops of mlecchas, skilled in war.
Then, seeing Satyaki, that bull among men, fighting there, the son of Kunti went
on at speed, careful in his chariot, wanting to see Dhananjaya.

"Bhimasena the Pandava passed your fighters in the battle, great king, and there
he saw Arjuna, the bull among men, fighting, the mighty man striving to kill the
Saindhava. When he saw Arjuna there, he let out great roars. Partha heard that
great roar of his, and then Partha and Madhava roared great roars and came on,
great king, bellowing like two bulls. When Vasudeva and Arjuna heard the roar of
that fiery man, they roared again and again, wanting to see Vrikodara.

"When Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, heard Bhimasena's roar and the roar of
Phalguna the bowman, great king, he was glad. The king's grief left him when he
heard that great noise, and the lord hoped for Dhananjaya's victory in the
battle. As Bhimasena, wild in battle, went on roaring, strong-armed
Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, smiled. The best of those who keep dharma
thought it over and spoke in his mind what was in his heart.

"'You have sent me word, Bhima, and done what your elder told you. There is no
victory in battle, Pandava, for those whom you hate. Thank heaven the
Left-Handed Archer, Dhananjaya, is alive in the battle. Thank heaven the hero
Satyaki is safe, whose courage is true. Thank heaven I hear Vasudeva and
Dhananjaya roaring.

"'He beat Shakra in battle and satisfied the Fire. That killer of his enemies in
battle, Phalguna, is alive, thank heaven. We all live by the strength of his
arms. That killer of the enemy's armies, Phalguna, is alive, thank heaven. With
a single chariot he beat the Nivatakavachas, whom even the gods could hardly
beat. Thank heaven that Partha is alive. At the city of the Matsyas he beat all
the Kauravas, who had come together to take the cattle. Thank heaven that Partha
is alive. In the great battle he killed fourteen thousand Kalakeyas with the
strength of his arms. Thank heaven that Partha is alive. For Duryodhana's sake
he beat the mighty king of the gandharvas with the power of his weapons. Thank
heaven that Partha is alive. Strong, wearing his crown and garland, with his
white horses and Krishna for his charioteer, always dear to me, thank heaven
Phalguna is alive.

"'Burning with grief for his son, and wanting to do a thing hard to do, he made
his vow, seeking the death of Jayadratha. Will Dhananjaya kill the Saindhava in
battle? Will I meet Arjuna before the sun sets, guarded by Vasudeva, with his
vow fulfilled? Will the Saindhava king, who is devoted to Duryodhana's good, be
felled by Phalguna and give joy to his enemies? When King Duryodhana sees the
Saindhava felled by Phalguna in battle, will he make peace with us? When he sees
his brothers killed in battle by Bhimasena, will that fool Duryodhana make peace
with us? When he sees so many other fighters struck down on the ground, will
that fool Duryodhana be sorry? Will our feud end with Bhishma alone? Will
Suyodhana make peace to save the rest?'

"While he, full of pity, was thinking of many things like this, lord of the
earth, the terrible battle went on."

Dhritarashtra said, "Bhimasena was roaring like that, with a noise like the
thunder of a cloud. What heroes surrounded him? I can see no one in the three
worlds, Sanjaya, who could stand in front of Bhimasena in battle when he is
angry. When he raises his mace in the great battle like Time itself, I can see
no one, my friend, who could stand on the field. He can kill a chariot with a
chariot and an elephant with an elephant. Who could stand against him in battle,
even Shatakratu himself? When Bhimasena was angry and wanted to kill my sons,
who stood in front of him for Duryodhana's good? Bhimasena is a forest fire, and
my sons are grass. When he was about to burn them up at the front of the battle,
which heroes stood facing him? When they saw Bhima driving my sons in the
battle, as Time drives all living things, who surrounded Bhima? Bhima is a
blazing fire, wanting to burn up my sons. What brave men turned to face him?
Tell me that, Sanjaya."

Sanjaya said, "As the great warrior Bhimasena roared like that, mighty Karna
fell on him with a wild noise. Karna could not bear it. He drew his strong bow
very far, wanting battle, and meaning to show his strength. At the meeting of
Karna and Bhima, the limbs of the chariot fighters and the horsemen seemed to
tremble when they heard the slapping of their palms. When they heard Bhimasena's
dreadful roar on the field, the bulls among kshatriyas thought the sky and the
earth were joined. At the dreadful roar of the great-souled Pandava again, the
bows of all the fighters in the battle fell to the ground. All the animals were
terrified, great king. They let fall their dung and urine and lost heart. Many
dreadful signs appeared, king, at that wild meeting of Bhima and Karna.

"Then Karna struck Bhima with twenty arrows, and quickly pierced his charioteer
with five swift ones. Bhimasena laughed and, quick in action, the mighty man
paid Karna back in the battle with sixty-four arrows. Karna, the great bowman,
shot four arrows at him. Before they reached him, king, Bhima cut them into many
pieces with smooth-jointed arrows, showing how quick his hands were. Karna
covered him with many flights of arrows. Covered many times by Karna, the great
warrior, the son of Pandu, cut Karna's bow at the grip and pierced him with many
smooth-jointed arrows.

"The son of the suta took another bow and strung it, and the great warrior of
terrible deeds pierced Bhima in the battle. Bhima was very angry, and in his
fury he drove three smooth-jointed arrows into the chest of the suta's son. With
those arrows standing in the middle of his chest, bull of the Bharatas, Karna
shone like a tall mountain with three peaks. Blood flowed from him where the
fine arrows had pierced him, like streams of red chalk from a mountain oozing
its minerals. Karna was shaken a little, hurt by the fine blows, sir. He set an
arrow to his bow and pierced Bhima, and loosed arrows at him again by hundreds
and thousands.

"Covered all at once by Karna, the bowman with the strong bow, the son of Pandu
smiled and quickly cut his bowstring. With a broad arrow the great warrior sent
his charioteer to the house of Yama, and killed his four horses in the battle.
Karna leapt from his chariot, its horses killed, lord of the people, and the
great warrior climbed into Vrishasena's chariot.

"When mighty Bhimasena had beaten Karna in battle, he roared a very great roar,
like the thunder of a raincloud. When he heard that roar, Yudhishthira was full
of joy, Bharata, for he thought Karna had been beaten by Bhimasena. Then on
every side the Pandava army sounded its conches. Hearing the sound of the enemy
army, your men roared too. Partha twanged the Gandiva and Krishna blew his
conch. But above that sound, great king, the roar of Bhima was heard through all
the armies, Bharata.

"Then those two tamers of their enemies strove against each other with their
weapons, each in his own way, Radheya gently and the Pandava hard."

"When the army was thrown into turmoil like that," said Sanjaya, "and Arjuna had
gone after the Saindhava, and the Satvata and Bhimasena too, your son went to
Drona. He hurried there in a single chariot, thinking of the many things to be
done. Your son's chariot, fast as the mind and the wind, went at the greatest
speed and quickly reached Drona.

"Your son spoke to him in anger, his eyes red. 'Arjuna, Bhimasena and the
unbeaten Satyaki, great warriors, tormentors of their enemies, have beaten all
our great armies and reached the Sindhu king. All of them are fighting there,
unbeaten. If Partha, the great warrior, got past you in battle, how did Satyaki
and Bhima get past you, giver of honour? That you were beaten by the Satvata and
Arjuna, and by Bhimasena too, first of the brahmins, is a wonder in the world,
like the drying up of the sea. People everywhere are saying it: "How was Drona
beaten in battle, when he has mastered the science of the bow?" This must be my
ruin in battle, unlucky as I am, when three chariot fighters have got past a
tiger among men like you.

"'Now things have come to this, tell me what you have to say. What is gone is
gone. Think about what is left, giver of honour. Tell me quickly what must be
done for the Sindhu king now that the time has come, and let it be done well.'

"Drona said, 'There is much to think about, great king. Hear what must be done.
Three great warriors of the Pandavas have got past. There is as much danger
behind us as there is in front of them. I think it weighs more where Krishna and
Dhananjaya are. The Bharata army is seized from the front and from behind. I
think the thing to do is to guard the Saindhava. He is the one we must guard
most, my friend, for he is afraid of Dhananjaya in his anger. The heroes
Yuyudhana and Vrikodara have gone to the Saindhava.

"'The game of dice that came from Shakuni's mind has come round now. There was
no winning in the hall, and no losing there either. Here, today, as we play,
there will be winning and losing, my friend. The dreadful things Shakuni threw
in the assembly of the Kurus, thinking they were dice, were arrows, hard to
face. Here, where so many Kurus stand, my friend, know that the army is the
gaming board and the arrows are the dice, lord of the people. The stake is the
Saindhava, king, and here the game will be decided. The great game with the
enemy hangs on the Saindhava. Here, great king, all of you must give up your own
lives and guard the Saindhava in battle as it should be done. There, as we play,
winning and losing are certain, my friend. Where those great bowmen are
carefully guarding the Saindhava, go there quickly yourself, and guard his
guards. I will stay right here and send others on. I will hold back the
Panchalas, together with the Pandavas and the Srinjayas.'

"At the teacher's command Duryodhana set out quickly with his followers, bracing
himself for fierce deeds. The two wheel guards, the Panchalas Yudhamanyu and
Uttamaujas, had come round the outside of the army and were going to the
Left-Handed Archer. Earlier, great king, when Arjuna went into your army to
fight, Kritavarma had held them back. Now mighty Duryodhana, the Bharata, went
quickly into a great fight with those two brothers as they hurried on. The two
ran at him, both with their bows raised, famous as great warriors and the best
of kshatriyas in battle.

"Yudhamanyu, in anger and in haste, loosed thirty iron arrows into your son's
chest. Duryodhana, lord of kings, killed the Panchala Uttamaujas's four horses,
and both his flank drivers. With his horses and his driver killed in the battle,
Uttamaujas quickly climbed into his brother Yudhamanyu's chariot. When he
reached his brother's chariot, he struck Duryodhana's horses with many arrows,
and they fell dead on the ground. When the horses had fallen, Yudhamanyu quickly
cut Duryodhana's bow and his quiver in the battle with a fine arrow.

"Your son, the great warrior, leapt down from his chariot, its horses and driver
killed, took up his mace and ran at the two Panchalas. When they saw that angry
conqueror of enemy cities coming at them, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas leapt down
from the floor of their chariot. Then with his mace he smashed that fine
chariot, worked with gold, with its horses, driver and banner, in the battle.
Having smashed it, your son, the tormentor of his enemies, his own horses and
driver killed, quickly climbed into the Madra king's chariot. And the two mighty
princes, the chiefs of the Panchalas, climbed into another chariot and went on
towards Dhananjaya."

**Divergences.**

* **Eight chariots.** \*795, in which Bhima runs after Drona, seizes each new chariot by the yoke and throws it, "and so eight chariots of the teacher were thrown by Bhima in play", is rejected. The CE has Bhima smash one chariot with his mace (7.102.87–88) and fling a second by the pole (7.103.15–16). Ledger L-212.
* **Karna and Bhima.** Appendix I No. 11 (after 7.104.33), in which Bhima breaks Karna's chariot, cuts his sword and shield and kills Duhshala, on whose chariot Karna has taken refuge, is rejected. In the CE this first fight ends with Karna on Vrishasena's chariot and the two fighting on (7.104.28, 33).
* **Duryodhana against the wheel guards.** \*818–\*820 give other exchanges (Yudhamanyu's thirty arrows at Duryodhana's charioteer, Uttamaujas killing Duryodhana's charioteer). They are rejected.
* **Bhima's charge.** \*793, in which Bhima runs at Drona with his head down on his shoulder and his hands on his chest, is rejected.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*790–\*792, \*794, \*796–\*817, \*821–\*822.

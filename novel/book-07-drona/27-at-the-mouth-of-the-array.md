# At the Mouth of the Array

"Partha and the Varshneya had gone in, great king," said Sanjaya, "and
Duryodhana, that bull among men, had gone after them. Then the Pandavas and the
Somakas rushed at Drona with a great noise, and battle began. That battle of the
Panchalas and the Kurus, in front of the array, was dreadful and wild, and it
made the hair stand on end. It was a wonder. King, lord of the people, never
have we seen or heard of a battle like the one fought when the sun stood at
midday.

"The Parthas, led by Dhrishtadyumna, hard strikers in their ordered divisions,
all poured rains of arrows on Drona's army. We, with Drona at our head, the best
of all who bear weapons, rained arrows on the Parthas led by Prishata's
grandson. The two great armies, lovely and bright with chariots, shone at the
front like two great clouds swelling at the end of the cold season, with winds
blowing from both sides. They met and struck together with great force, like the
Ganga and the Yamuna in the rains, when their waters rise high.

"It was a great storm cloud of battle, very dreadful. Weapons of every kind were
the wind before it, elephants, horses and chariots made its mass, and maces were
its lightning. Driven by the wind that was Bharadvaja's son, with its thousands
of streams of arrows, it rained on the Pandava army, which blazed like a fire.
As a great, dreadful wind at the end of the hot season stirs up the sea, so the
best of the twice-born stirred up the Pandava divisions. They too rushed at
Drona with all their might, like strong floods of water wanting to break a great
dam. Drona held them back like a mountain holding back floods of water: the
Pandavas, angry in battle, and the Panchalas and the Kekayas. Then other kings,
mighty and brave, turned back from every side and blocked the Panchalas in the
battle.

"Then Prishata's grandson, a tiger among men, with the Pandavas, struck Drona
again and again, wanting to break the enemy's host. Just as Drona rained arrows
on Prishata's grandson, so Dhrishtadyumna rained arrows on him. Dhrishtadyumna
was a raincloud. Swords were the wind before it, spears, lances and darts made
its mass, the bowstring was its lightning and the bow's twang its thunder. He
loosed streams of arrows like hail in every direction, killing floods of fine
chariots and horses, and covered the enemy host. Whatever mass of Pandava
chariots Drona went for with his arrows, Prishata's grandson drew Drona away
from it with his own arrows.

"So, Bharata, as Drona strove in battle, his army met Dhrishtadyumna and split
in three. Some turned towards the Bhoja, others towards Jalasandha, and others,
killed by the Pandavas, went to Drona. Whatever troops Drona, best of chariot
fighters, brought together, the great warrior Dhrishtadyumna scattered. Split in
three, Dhritarashtra's men were being killed by the Pandavas and Srinjayas, like
cattle with no herdsman in a forest, set on by many beasts of prey. People
thought that Time was swallowing the fighters in that wild battle, while
Dhrishtadyumna confused them. As a bad king's country is ravaged by famine,
disease and thieves, so your host, in its trouble, was put to flight by the
Pandavas. The sun's rays struck back from the weapons and the armour, and the
dust raised by the army blinded the eye.

"As the army, split in three, was being killed by the Pandavas, Drona, in his
fury, cut down the Panchalas with his arrows. As he crushed those divisions and
killed them with his arrows, Drona looked like the fire of Time, blazing. The
great warrior split chariot, elephant, horse and foot soldier, lord of the
people, each with a single arrow in the battle. In the Pandava army, Bharata,
there was no one who could bear the sharp arrows flying from Drona's bow. The
army of Prishata's grandson, baked by the sun and scorched by Drona's arrows,
wandered here and there, Bharata. In the same way your army, driven by
Prishata's grandson, blazed on every side, like a dry forest set on fire. As
Drona's arrows and those of Prishata's grandson killed the armies, the soldiers
gave up their lives and fought with all their strength. Among your men and the
enemy, bull of the Bharatas, great king, not one left the battle out of fear.

"Kunti's son Bhimasena was surrounded by brothers of one mother, Vivimshati,
Chitrasena and the great warrior Vikarna. Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti and the
mighty Kshemadhurti, three of them, followed your three sons. The splendid king
of the Bahlikas, a great warrior of good family, with his army and his
ministers, blocked the sons of Draupadi. King Govasana of the Shibis, with no
fewer than a thousand fighters, blocked the bold son of Abhibhu of Kashi.
Shalya, lord of the Madras, a king, surrounded Kunti's son the king,
Ajatashatru, who blazed like fire. Duhshasana set his own division in its place
and went angrily against Satyaki, the brave, the best of chariot fighters. I, in
my armour, with my own division, blocked Chekitana with four hundred great
bowmen. Shakuni with his division blocked Madri's son, with seven hundred
Gandharas armed with bows, spears, arrows and swords. Vinda and Anuvinda of
Avanti, the two great bowmen, went for Virata of Matsya. They had given up their
lives and risen up to fight for their friend. Bahlika, ready for him, blocked
the bold Shikhandi, son of Yajnasena, who had never been beaten and who was
holding the Kauravas back. The Avanti prince, with the Sauviras, blocked
Dhrishtadyumna of Panchala, who looked full of anger, with the cruel
Prabhadrakas. Alayudha rushed quickly at the brave, cruel-fighting rakshasa
Ghatotkacha as he came on in anger. The great warrior Kuntibhoja, with a great
army, blocked Alambusa, lord of rakshasas, who looked full of anger.

"The Saindhava was at the rear of the whole army, Bharata, guarded by great
bowmen in their chariots, Kripa and the others. The Saindhava had two very great
wheel guards: Drona's son on the right, king, and the son of the Suta on the
left. Guarding his back were Somadatta's son and the others with him, Kripa and
Vrishasena, Shala and Shalya, hard to beat. They were all wise in policy, great
bowmen, skilled in battle. Having set the Saindhava's guard in this way, they
fought.

"King, hear me tell of that wonderful battle, how the Kurus and the Pandavas
fought. The Parthas came up to Bharadvaja's son, standing at the mouth of the
array, and fought, wanting to break Drona's division. And Drona's soldiers,
guarding their own array, fought the Parthas in the battle, seeking great fame.

"Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, in great anger, wanting your son's good, struck
Virata with ten arrows. Virata, great king, fought the two bold men as they
stood in battle, and their followers, with boldness of his own. Their fight was
cruel, with blood for water, like a lion's fight with two great elephants in rut
in the forest.

"Mighty Yajnaseni struck Bahlika, fierce in battle, with sharp, dreadful arrows
that split the vital places and the bones. Bahlika, in great anger, struck
Yajnaseni hard with nine smooth-jointed arrows, gold-feathered and whetted on
stone. That battle was dreadful, full of arrows and spears. It frightened the
cowards and made the brave rejoice. The arrows they loosed covered the whole sky
and all the quarters, and nothing could be made out.

"Shaibya Govasana, with his army, fought the great warrior, the son of the Kashi
king, as one elephant fights another. The king of the Bahlikas, in his fury,
fighting the great warriors, the sons of Draupadi, shone like the mind fighting
the five senses. They fought him hard with floods of arrows from every side, as
the objects of the senses always fight the body, best of the embodied.

"Your son Duhshasana struck Satyaki the Varshneya in battle with nine sharp,
smooth-jointed arrows. Pierced deep by that strong great bowman, Satyaki, whose
courage was true, quickly fell into a slight faint. When the Varshneya came to,
he quickly pierced your son, the great warrior, with ten heron-feathered arrows.
The two pierced each other hard, each wounded by the other's arrows, and they
shone in the battle, king, like two flowering kimshuka trees.

"Alambusa, in his anger, hurt by Kuntibhoja's arrows, shone with great beauty,
like a kimshuka rich in flowers. Then the rakshasa pierced Kuntibhoja with many
iron arrows and gave a terrible roar at the front of your army. All creatures
watched the two brave men fighting each other in the battle, like Shakra and
Jambha long ago.

"The two angry sons of Madri struck Shakuni with arrows in the battle, Bharata,
the man fierce in battle who had made the feud. That slaughter of men, great
king, came from him. You gave it birth, and Karna made it grow very great, and
your son fanned the fire of anger, king, that is ready to burn up the whole
earth. The two sons of Pandu turned Shakuni back with their arrows, and in the
battle he did not know what to do, or how to show his courage. Seeing him turned
away, the two great warriors, Madri's sons, rained arrows on him again, like two
clouds on a great mountain. Struck by many smooth-jointed arrows, Subala's son
went off with his swift horses to Drona's division.

"Ghatotkacha went at the brave rakshasa Alayudha, fierce in battle, at a middle
pace. Their fight, great king, was a strange thing to see, like the battle of
Rama and Ravana long ago.

"Then King Yudhishthira pierced the king of the Madras in the battle with fifty
arrows, and again with seven. Then a most wonderful fight began between them,
king, like the great fight long ago between Shambara and the king of the
immortals.

"Vivimshati, Chitrasena and your son Vikarna, surrounded by a great army, fought
Bhimasena."

**Divergences.**

* **Sanjaya in the battle.** At 7.70.41 Sanjaya says, "I, in my armour, with my own division, blocked Chekitana with four hundred great bowmen." He speaks of the Kaurava side as "we" (7.70.6).
* **Shalya in two places.** Shalya surrounds Yudhishthira (7.70.39; 7.71.29) and is also named among Jayadratha's rear guards (7.70.50). The CE does not explain this.
* **Vinda and Anuvinda.** They follow Duryodhana's brothers against Bhima (7.70.36) and also go against Virata (7.70.43; 7.71.4). The novel keeps both.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*513–\*517. \*516 has the two Avanti brothers fight Dhrishtadyumna.

* 7.70.4 *madhyagate sūrye*: "when the sun stood at midday".
* 7.70.7 *miśravātau himātyaye*: "at the end of the cold season, with winds blowing from both sides".
* 7.70.18 *śaradhārāśmavarṣāṇi*: "streams of arrows like hail".
* 7.70.35 *sodaryāḥ*: "brothers of one mother".
* 7.70.36 *trayāṇāṃ tava putrāṇāṃ traya evānuyāyinaḥ*: "three of them followed your three sons".

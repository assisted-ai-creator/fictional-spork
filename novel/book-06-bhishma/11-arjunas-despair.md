# Arjuna's Despair

"On the field of dharma, on the field of the Kurus," said Dhritarashtra, "when
my sons and the sons of Pandu had gathered, eager for battle, what did they do,
Sanjaya?"

"When King Duryodhana saw the Pandava army drawn up," said Sanjaya, "he went to
the teacher and spoke.

"'Teacher, look at this great army of the sons of Pandu, drawn up by Drupada's
son, your clever pupil. Here are heroes, great archers, equal to Bhima and
Arjuna in battle. Here are Yuyudhana, Virata and Drupada, the great chariot
fighter, Dhrishtaketu, Chekitana and the mighty king of Kashi. Here are Purujit
Kuntibhoja and Shaibya, a bull among men, valiant Yudhamanyu and mighty
Uttamaujas, Subhadra's son and the sons of Draupadi, all great chariot fighters.
Now hear of our best men, best of the twice-born, the leaders of my army. I name
them so that you know them: you yourself, Bhishma, Karna, and Kripa, victorious
in battle, Ashvatthama, Vikarna and Somadatta's son. And there are many other
heroes who have given up their lives for me, with weapons of many kinds, all
skilled in war. That force of ours, guarded by Bhishma, is not enough. But this
force of theirs, guarded by Bhima, is enough. All of you, standing in your
places in all the lines of advance, as you have been assigned, must guard
Bhishma above all.'

"To give him joy, the mighty grandfather, the eldest of the Kurus, roared aloud
a lion's roar and blew his conch. Then conches and drums, tabors, kettledrums
and cow-horns were sounded all at once, and the noise was tumultuous. Then,
standing in their great chariot yoked with white horses, Madhava and the Pandava
blew their heavenly conches. Hrishikesha blew Panchajanya, Dhananjaya Devadatta,
and Vrikodara of terrible deeds blew the great conch Paundra. King Yudhishthira,
Kunti's son, blew Anantavijaya, and Nakula and Sahadeva Sughosha and
Manipushpaka. The king of Kashi, the supreme archer, Shikhandi the great chariot
fighter, Dhrishtadyumna, Virata and unconquered Satyaki blew their conches.
Drupada and the sons of Draupadi, lord of the earth, and strong-armed Subhadra's
son all blew their conches, each his own. That tumultuous sound tore the hearts
of Dhritarashtra's sons and made the sky and the earth echo.

"Then, seeing Dhritarashtra's sons drawn up, as the clash of weapons began, the
Pandava with the monkey banner raised his bow and said this to Hrishikesha, lord
of the earth. 'Achyuta, stop my chariot between the two armies, so that I can
see these men standing here eager to fight, and know with whom I must fight in
this effort of war. I want to look at those who are about to fight, who have
gathered here wanting to please evil-minded Dhritarashtra's son in battle.'

"Spoken to like this by Gudakesha, Bharata, Hrishikesha stopped the fine chariot
between the two armies, in front of Bhishma, Drona and all the kings, and said,
'Partha, look at these Kurus gathered here.'

"There Partha saw, standing in both armies, fathers and grandfathers, teachers,
uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons and friends, fathers-in-law and companions.
When Kunti's son saw all those kinsmen standing there, he was overcome with
great pity, and sinking in despair he said this.

"'Krishna, when I see my own people standing here, eager to fight, my limbs give
way and my mouth goes dry. My body trembles and my hair stands on end. Gandiva
slips from my hand, and my skin burns. I cannot stand, and my mind seems to
whirl. And I see evil omens, Keshava. I see no good in killing my own people in
battle. I do not want victory, Krishna, or a kingdom, or pleasures. What use is
a kingdom to us, Govinda, or pleasures, or life? We wanted a kingdom, enjoyments
and pleasures for their sake, and they stand here in battle, having given up
their lives and their wealth: teachers, fathers, sons and grandfathers too,
uncles, fathers-in-law, grandsons, brothers-in-law and other kin. I do not want
to kill them, Madhusudana, even if they kill me, not even for the kingdom of the
three worlds, let alone for the earth.

"'What joy could we have, Janardana, from killing Dhritarashtra's sons? Only
evil would cling to us if we killed these men, though they have their bows drawn
against us. So we should not kill Dhritarashtra's sons and their kin. How could
we be happy, Madhava, if we killed our own people? These men, their minds struck
by greed, may not see the evil of destroying a family and the crime of betraying
friends. But how can we not know enough to turn back from this evil, Janardana,
when we see the evil of destroying a family?

"'When a family is destroyed, the eternal dharmas of the family perish. When
dharma perishes, adharma overcomes the whole family. When adharma overcomes it,
Krishna, the women of the family are corrupted. When the women are corrupted,
Varshneya, the classes are mixed. That mixing brings to hell the family and
those who destroyed it. Their ancestors fall, for the offerings of rice and
water to them cease. By these faults of those who destroy a family, which cause
the mixing of classes, the dharmas of caste and the eternal dharmas of the
family are overthrown. We have heard, Janardana, that men whose family dharmas
are overthrown must surely live in hell.

"'Oh, what a great evil we have set out to do, ready to kill our own people out
of greed for the pleasures of a kingdom! If the sons of Dhritarashtra, weapons
in hand, killed me in battle, unresisting and unarmed, that would be better for
me.'

"When he had said this in the battle, Arjuna sat down on the chariot seat and
let go of his bow and arrows, his mind shaken by grief."

**Divergences.**

* **The Bhagavad Gita.** CE 6.23–40, eighteen adhyayas, corresponding to chapters 1–18 of the Gita as usually printed. This chapter is Gita 1. Ledger L-35.
* Rejected lines (\*85–\*86) add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* **The Gita in this novel.** Every verse is rendered, plainly, as in the rest of the book. Terms with no plain English equivalent (dharma, yoga, Brahman) are kept; others are given in English, with the Sanskrit in the notes where it matters.
* 6.23.1 *dharmakṣetre kurukṣetre*: "on the field of dharma, on the field of the Kurus".
* 6.23.10 *aparyāptam … paryāptam*: "not enough … enough". The verse can also be read the other way ("unlimited … limited"); the novel keeps the plainer sense of the words.
* 6.23.15–16: the conches are kept by name: Panchajanya, Devadatta, Paundra, Anantavijaya, Sughosha and Manipushpaka.
* 6.23.36 *ātatāyinaḥ*: "though they have their bows drawn against us"; the word means an aggressor who may lawfully be killed.
* 6.23.41 *varṇasaṃkara*: "the classes are mixed".

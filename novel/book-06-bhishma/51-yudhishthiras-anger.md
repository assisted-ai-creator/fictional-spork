# Yudhishthira's Anger

"Then, when the sun had reached the middle of the sky," said Sanjaya, "King
Yudhishthira saw Shrutayu and urged on his horses. The king ran at Shrutayu,
tamer of enemies, striking him with nine sharp arrows with bent joints. The
king, a great archer, warded off the arrows sent by the son of Dharma and fixed
seven arrows in the Kaunteya. They split his armour in battle and drank his
blood, as if searching out the life in the great-souled man's body. The Pandava,
badly wounded by that great-souled king, pierced the king in the heart in battle
with a boar-eared arrow. Then with another broad arrow Partha, the best of
chariot fighters, quickly brought the great-souled man's banner down from his
chariot to the ground. Seeing his banner fallen, King Shrutayu pierced the
Pandava with seven sharp arrows, king.

"Then Yudhishthira, son of Dharma, blazed with anger, like fire about to burn up
all beings at the end of an age. Seeing the Pandava angry, the gods, gandharvas
and rakshasas trembled, great king, and the world was thrown into confusion.
This was in the minds of all beings: 'Today this king in his anger will burn the
three worlds.' The seers and the gods made great prayers for good fortune, king,
for the peace of the worlds, now that the Pandava was angry. Possessed by anger,
licking the corners of his mouth, he took on a terrible form, like the sun at
the end of an age. Then all your troops, lord of the people, lost hope of life
there, Bharata.

"But the glorious one held back that anger with firmness and cut Shrutayu's
great bow at the grip. Then, when his bow was cut, the king pierced him between
the breasts with an iron arrow while the whole army watched. The very strong one
quickly killed the great-souled man's horses with arrows in a moment, king, and
his charioteer. Seeing the king's manhood, Shrutayu left his chariot with its
horses killed and fled at speed from the battle. When that great archer had been
beaten in battle by the son of Dharma, king, Duryodhana's whole army turned its
back. Having done this, great king, Yudhishthira, son of Dharma, killed your
army like Time with his mouth open.

"Chekitana the Varshneya covered Gautama, the best of chariot fighters, with
arrows while all the troops watched. Kripa, Sharadvat's son, warded off those
arrows in battle and pierced Chekitana, ready in battle, with feathered arrows,
king. Then with another broad arrow he cut his bow, sir. Quick of hand, he
brought down his charioteer in battle and killed his horses, king, and both his
rear guards. The Satvata leapt quickly from his chariot and took up a club. With
that hero-killing club the best of club fighters killed Gautama's horses and
brought down his charioteer. Standing on the ground, Gautama shot sixteen arrows
at him. They split the Satvata and went into the ground. Then Chekitana in anger
hurled the club again, wanting to kill Gautama, as Purandara hurled at Vritra.
As that great clean club with stone inside came at him, Gautama held it off with
many thousands of arrows. Then Chekitana drew a sword from its sheath, Bharata,
and with the greatest lightness ran at Gautama. Gautama too threw down his bow,
took up a well-sharpened sword and ran at Chekitana with great speed, king. Both
full of strength, holding fine swords, they cut each other with very sharp
swords. Struck by the force of the swords, the two bulls among men fell to the
earth, where all beings live, their limbs overcome by faintness, stunned by the
effort. Then Karakarsha, his friend, ran up at speed. Seeing Chekitana, mad for
battle, in that state, he took him up onto his chariot while the whole army
watched. In the same way the brave Shakuni, your brother-in-law, lord of the
people, quickly took Gautama, the best of chariot fighters, onto his chariot.

"The very strong Dhrishtaketu in anger quickly pierced Somadatta's son in the
chest with ninety arrows, king. With those arrows in his chest, Somadatta's son
shone brightly, great king, like the sun with its rays at midday. Bhurishravas
in battle made the great chariot fighter Dhrishtaketu chariotless with fine
arrows, his charioteer and horses killed. Seeing him without a chariot, his
horses and charioteer killed, he covered him with a great shower of arrows in
battle. The great-minded Dhrishtaketu left that chariot and climbed onto
Shatanika's carriage, sir.

"Chitrasena, Vikarna and Durmarshana, king, chariot fighters in golden armour,
ran at Subhadra's son. Then a terrible battle went on between Abhimanyu and
them, king, like that of the body with its three humours, wind, bile and phlegm.
Having made your sons chariotless in the great battle, king, the tiger among men
did not kill them, for he remembered Bhima's words.

"Then Bhishma, hard even for the gods to reach, surrounded in battle by many
hundreds of kings riding elephants, horses and chariots, went quickly to protect
your sons. The Kaunteya of the white horses saw him going, and with Abhimanyu in
mind, alone, a boy, though a great chariot fighter, he said this to Vasudeva.
'Drive the horses, Hrishikesha, to where these many chariots are. These are many
brave men, skilled in weapons, mad for battle. Drive so that they do not kill
our army, Madhava.' So addressed by the Kaunteya of measureless power, the
Varshneya drove the chariot yoked to white horses into battle. There was a great
groaning from your army, sir, when Arjuna in anger came up against your men in
battle.

"Reaching those kings who guarded Bhishma, the Kaunteya said this to Susharma,
king. 'I know you are the best in battle, and my very old enemy. See today the
very cruel fruit of that course come to you. Today I will show you your
grandfathers who died long ago.' Though he heard the harsh words of Bibhatsu,
killer of enemies, as he spoke, Susharma, the leader of chariot troops, said
nothing at all to him, good or bad. Surrounded by many kings, he went at the
hero Arjuna. With your sons, faultless one, they surrounded Arjuna in battle in
front, behind and on both sides, on every side, and covered him with arrows, as
clouds cover the sun. Then a very great battle broke out, with blood for water,
between yours and the Pandavas in battle, Bharata.

---

"Struck by arrows, the strong Dhananjaya hissed like a snake struck by a foot.
With arrow after arrow he cut the bows of the great chariot fighters in battle
by force. Having cut the bows of those mighty kings in battle in a moment, the
great-souled one pierced them all at once with arrows, meaning to leave none of
them. Struck by Shakra's son, king, they fell in battle smeared with blood,
their limbs split, their heads fallen, lifeless, their armour and bodies cut.
Overcome by Partha's strength, they went to the ground and perished all at once,
in many forms. Seeing those princes killed in battle, the king of the Trigartas
came forward at once.

"Then thirty-two others, the rear guards of those chariots, fell on Partha. They
too surrounded Partha, drew their loud bows and rained on him a shower of great
floods of arrows, as clouds rain floods of water on a mountain. Hard pressed by
the shower of floods of arrows, Dhananjaya grew angry, and with sixty oil-washed
arrows in battle he killed those rear guards too. Having conquered those sixty
chariots in battle, the glorious Dhananjaya was glad at heart. Jishnu, having
killed the kings' forces in battle, then hurried to kill Bhishma.

"The king of the Trigartas, seeing those great chariot fighters, his kinsmen,
killed, put the kings in front in battle and went quickly at Partha to kill him.
Seeing Dhananjaya, the best of those who bear weapons, attacked, those led by
Shikhandi went out with sharp weapons in hand, wanting to protect Arjuna's
chariot. Partha too, seeing those heroes among men coming with the king of the
Trigartas, the bowman destroyed them in battle with sharp arrows loosed from
Gandiva. Wanting to go to Bhishma in battle, he saw Duryodhana and the kings led
by the Sindhu king. Coming up against those who would hold him back, the hero
fought them with strength for a moment. Then, of endless courage and great
power, he left the king and the kings led by Jayadratha. With bow and arrows in
hand, the high-minded one of terrible strength went to Ganga's son in battle.

"The great-souled Yudhishthira of fierce strength, of endless fame, came up
quickly in anger. He left in battle the lord of the Madras, who had been
allotted as his share, and went with Madri's sons and Bhimasena to fight Bhishma
son of Shantanu. Attacked by those foremost great chariot fighters, the son of
Ganga, the great-souled son of Shantanu, a wonderful fighter in battle, did not
falter before all the sons of Pandu together.

"Then King Jayadratha came up in battle, true to his word, of very fierce
strength, high-minded. With his fine bow he cut by force the bows of those great
chariot fighters. Duryodhana, high-minded, with the poison of anger, enraged,
struck Yudhishthira, Bhimasena, the twins and Partha in battle with arrows
bright as fire. Pierced by the arrows of Kripa, Shalya, Shala and Chitrasena in
battle, lord, whose anger had grown very great, they were like the gods attacked
by the hosts of the daityas together.

"Seeing Shikhandi with his weapons cut by Shantanu's son, King Ajatashatru, the
great-souled, grew angry in battle and spoke to Shikhandi in anger. 'You said in
front of your father, and to me, "I will kill Bhishma, him of the great vow,
with floods of arrows bright as the clear sun. I speak the truth." That was your
promise. You are not making it bear fruit, since you do not kill Devavrata in
battle. Do not be false to your promise, hero among men. Protect your dharma,
your family and your fame. Look at Bhishma in battle, of terrible speed,
scorching all my hosts of troops with nets of floods of very sharp arrows, like
Time made by death in a moment. Your bow cut, careless of battle, beaten by King
Shantanu's son, leaving your kin and your brothers, where will you go? This is
not fitting for you. Seeing Bhishma of endless courage, and the army broken and
running like this, you are surely afraid, son of Drupada, for the colour of your
face is not glad. Even though Dhananjaya is known to be engaged in the great
battle, hero among men, how can you, famed on earth, be afraid of Bhishma today,
hero?'

"Hearing the King of Dharma's words, harsh in their syllables and bound up with
reproach, the great-souled one took them as a rebuke and hurried to kill
Bhishma, king. As Shikhandi came with great speed, running at Bhishma, Shalya
held him back with a terrible weapon, very hard to overcome. Seeing that weapon
rising, like the fire at the end of an age in power, the son of Drupada, like
great Indra in power, king, was not bewildered. The great bowman stood right
there, checking that weapon with arrows. Then Shikhandi took up another weapon,
the fierce weapon of Varuna, to strike it back. The gods standing in the sky and
the kings saw that weapon being split by the other weapon.

"But Bhishma, king, the great-souled hero, cut in battle the very bright bow and
the banner of Pandu's son, King Yudhishthira the Ajamidha, and roared. Then,
seeing Yudhishthira overcome by fear, Bhimasena threw down his bow and arrows,
took up his club and fell on foot on Jayadratha in battle. As Bhimasena came on
with his club at great speed, Jayadratha pierced him on every side with five
hundred terrible sharp arrows like Yama's staff. Taking no thought of those
arrows, Wolf-belly, swift and overcome with anger, killed all the Sindhu king's
horses, bred in Aratta, in battle.

"Then your son, of matchless power, like the king of the gods, seeing this,
hurried in his chariot with weapons raised to kill Bhimasena. Bhima too roared
at once and went against him, threatening him with his club. Seeing that club
raised, like Yama's staff, all the Kurus around him left your fierce son,
wanting to escape the fall of the club. They went away in that tumultuous, very
cruel and bewildering clash, Bharata. But Chitrasena kept his head. Seeing the
great club coming, he left his chariot and, on foot in battle, took up a sword
and a clean shield. He leapt down like a lion from a mountain peak and went to
another spot on the ground. The club, reaching the bright chariot, killed the
horses and charioteer in battle and went into the ground, like a blazing great
meteor fallen from the sky to the earth. Seeing that very great wonder, Bharata,
your men were very glad. All of them roared together on every side and with
their armies honoured your son."

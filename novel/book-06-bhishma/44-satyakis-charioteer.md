# Satyaki's Charioteer

"Seeing his brothers and other kings engaged with Bhishma," said Sanjaya,
"Dhananjaya, his weapons raised, ran at Ganga's son. At the sound of Panchajanya
and of the bow Gandiva, and at the sight of Partha's banner, fear came over all
of us. We saw the banner of Gandiva's bowman, great king, rising like a comet
without catching on the trees, many-coloured, bright and divine, marked with a
monkey. The warriors saw Gandiva in the great chariot, its back of gold, like
lightning shining in the sky in the middle of a cloud. We heard the very
terrible sound of his palms as he roared like Shakra and struck your army.

"Like a cloud in a fierce wind, with lightning and thunder, flooding all the
quarters with showers of arrows on every side, Dhananjaya of the terrible
weapons ran at Ganga's son. Confused by his weapons, we could not tell east from
west. The warriors did not know where they were, bull of the Bharatas. Their
mounts were tired, their weapons struck down and their wits gone. They clung to
one another and took shelter with Bhishma alone, with all your sons. Bhishma son
of Shantanu became their refuge in battle. Chariot fighters leapt from their
chariots in terror, horsemen from their horses' backs, and foot soldiers onto
the ground. Hearing the sound of Gandiva, like the crash of a thunderbolt, all
the troops hid in fear, Bharata.

"Then your son urged on many to surround Subala's son. There was Govasana,
surrounded by tall, swift Kamboja chiefs and by forces of many thousands of
cowherds. There was the lord of the Kalingas, surrounded by the Madras,
Sauviras, Gandharas and Trigartas, and by all the Kalinga chiefs, lord of the
people. There were the elephants and crowds of men led by Duhshasana, and King
Jayadratha with all the kings. There were the best of the horsemen, fourteen
thousand of them. They all surrounded Subala's son. Then all of them together,
bull of the Bharatas, with their chariots and mounts divided, went against the
Pandavas in battle. Stirred up by chariot fighters, elephants, horses and foot
soldiers, the terrible fighting raised dust like a great cloud.

"With a great force of fighters with lances, spears and iron arrows, on
elephants, horses and chariots, Bhishma engaged the diademed one. The king of
Avanti engaged the king of Kashi, and the Sindhu king Bhimasena. Ajatashatru,
with his sons and ministers, engaged the glorious bull of the Madras, Shalya.
Vikarna engaged Sahadeva, and Chitrasena Shikhandi. The Matsyas went against
Duryodhana and Shakuni, lord of the people. Drupada, Chekitana and the great
chariot fighter Satyaki engaged the great-souled Drona and his son. Kripa and
Kritavarma ran at Dhrishtaketu. So the armies engaged and fought on every side,
their horses at full speed, their elephants and chariots wheeling.

"Fierce lightning flashed from a cloudless sky, and the quarters were covered
with dust. Great meteors appeared, with thunderclaps, lord of the people. A
great wind blew and a rain of dust fell. The sun vanished in the sky, covered by
the army's dust. A deep stupor came over all living things, overwhelmed by the
dust and stung by nets of weapons.

"A tumultuous clash arose of the nets of arrows loosed by heroes' arms, arrows
that split every kind of armour. Weapons raised by fine arms, clear as stars,
lit up the sky, bull of the Bharatas. Bright shields of bull's hide, covered
with nets of gold, fell in every direction, bull of the Bharatas. Bodies and
heads were seen falling on every side, in every direction, cut by swords the
colour of the sun. Great chariot fighters fell to the ground here and there,
their wheels, axles and seats broken, their great banners brought down and their
horses killed. Some horses, wounded by weapons, ran about there, dragging
chariots behind them when their chariot fighters had been killed. Fine horses
struck by arrows, their bodies split, still bound by their harness, dragged the
yokes here and there, Bharata. Chariots were seen killed by one strong elephant,
king, with their charioteers, horses and fighters.

"In that clash of floods of troops, many elephants in battle smelled the rut of
the scent elephants and took in the wind. The battlefield was covered with dead
elephants struck by iron arrows, fallen with their riders and their lances. In
the clash of floods of troops, chariots were smashed by fine elephants driven
against them and fell in battle with their warriors and banners. Chariot poles
were seen smashed, great king, flung up in battle by elephants with trunks like
great serpents. Elephants seized chariot fighters by the hair, their nets of
chariots shattered, whirled them like the branches of trees and crushed them in
battle. Fine elephants dragged off chariots tangled with chariots in battle and
ran in every direction, following every sound. As they dragged them, the
elephants looked like elephants dragging tangled masses of lotuses in ponds. So
that great battlefield was covered with horsemen and foot soldiers, and with
great chariot fighters and their banners.

---

"Shikhandi, with Virata the Matsya, lord of the people, quickly reached Bhishma,
the great archer, very hard to defeat. Dhananjaya met Drona, Kripa and Vikarna,
great archers of great strength, and many other brave kings in battle. He met
the Sindhu king, a great archer, with his ministers and kin, and the eastern and
southern kings, bull among kings. Bhimasena went against your son Duryodhana,
the impatient great archer, and Duhsaha in battle. Sahadeva went against Shakuni
and the great chariot fighter Uluka, father and son, great archers, hard to
defeat. Yudhishthira, the great chariot fighter, wronged by your son, great
king, went against the force of elephants in battle. Madri's son Nakula, the
brave Pandava who makes the enemy cry out in battle, engaged the noble chariot
fighters of the Trigartas. Satyaki, Chekitana and the great chariot fighter
Subhadra's son, hard to withstand, went against the Shalvas and Kekayas in
battle. Dhrishtaketu and the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, very hard to defeat, went out
against your sons' chariot force. The commander of the army, the very strong
Dhrishtadyumna, whose self is beyond measure, met Drona in battle, king, Drona
of Indra's deeds. So these great archers of yours, brave men, met the Pandavas
in battle and fought.

"When the sun had reached midday and the sky was in turmoil, the Kurus and the
Pandavas killed one another. Chariots with banners, their bodies bright with
gold, moving about the battlefield with their flags and their tiger-skin covers,
shone. As they met in battle, each wanting to defeat the other, there was a
tumultuous noise like lions roaring. There we saw a wonderful and very cruel
clash that the Srinjaya heroes fought with the Kurus. We could not see the sky,
king, nor the quarters, scorcher of enemies, nor the sun, nor the points between
the quarters, for the arrows loosed on every side. The shine of spears with
clean points, of lances as they were thrown, of yellow swords like blue lotuses,
and the shine of bright armour and ornaments lit up the sky, the quarters and
the points between them with their brightness. The battlefield shone here and
there, king. The lions and tigers among chariot fighters, coming together in
battle, shone like planets in the sky, king.

"Then Bhishma, the best of chariot fighters, in anger held off the very strong
Bhimasena while the whole army watched. The gold-feathered, stone-whetted,
oil-washed, very sharp arrows loosed by Bhishma struck Bhima in battle. The very
strong Bhimasena hurled at him a spear of great speed like an angry venomous
snake, Bharata. As that gold-handled spear, hard to withstand, rushed at him,
Bhishma cut it in battle with arrows with bent joints. Then with another yellow,
sharp broad arrow he cut Bhimasena's bow in two, Bharata.

"Then Satyaki quickly reached Bhishma in battle and struck your father with many
arrows, lord of the people. Then Bhishma fitted a very sharp and most cruel
arrow and brought down the Varshneya's charioteer from his chariot. With the
driver of his chariot killed, king, his horses bolted. They ran this way and
that, swift as the mind or the wind. Then a tumultuous noise arose from the
whole army, and a cry of grief from the great-souled Pandavas. 'Run! Catch them!
Stop the horses! Run!' Such was the tumultuous noise around Yuyudhana's chariot.

"At that very time Bhishma son of Shantanu again killed the Pandava army, as the
killer of Vritra killed the asura army. Being killed by Bhishma, the Panchalas
with the Somakas set their minds on noble battle and ran at Bhishma himself. The
Parthas led by Dhrishtadyumna too ran at Shantanu's son in battle, wanting to
defeat your son's army. So too your men, king, led by Bhishma and Drona, ran at
the enemy with speed, and then the battle went on."

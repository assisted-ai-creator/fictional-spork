# Three Kinds of Faith

"'Those who cast aside the rule of the science but sacrifice with faith,
Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'what is their standing? Is it goodness, passion or
darkness?'

"'The faith of the embodied is of three kinds,' said the Blessed One, 'born of
their own nature: of goodness, of passion and of darkness. Hear of it.
Everyone's faith is in keeping with his being, Bharata. A man is made of his
faith. As his faith is, so is he. Men of goodness sacrifice to the gods, men of
passion to yakshas and rakshasas, and others, men of darkness, sacrifice to
ghosts and hosts of spirits. Men who practise terrible austerity not laid down
by the science, full of hypocrisy and self-regard, with the force of desire and
passion, senselessly tormenting the host of elements in the body and me too who
dwell within the body, know them to be of demonic resolve.

"'The food that each likes is also of three kinds, and so are sacrifice,
austerity and giving. Hear how they differ. Foods that increase life, goodness,
strength, health, happiness and delight, that are tasty, rich, lasting and
pleasant to the heart, are dear to men of goodness. Foods that are bitter, sour,
salty, very hot, sharp, dry and burning are liked by men of passion. They bring
pain, grief and sickness. Food that is stale, tasteless, putrid and kept
overnight, left over and unclean, is dear to men of darkness.

"'Sacrifice offered by the rule by those who want no fruit, with the mind set on
this alone, "It must be offered," is of goodness. But sacrifice offered aiming
at the fruit, or for show, best of the Bharatas, know to be of passion.
Sacrifice without rule, with no food given out, without mantras, without fees
and without faith, they call of darkness.

"'Worship of the gods, the twice-born, teachers and the wise, purity,
uprightness, chastity and not harming are called austerity of the body. Speech
that causes no distress, that is true, pleasant and good, and the practice of
study, are called austerity of speech. Clearness of mind, gentleness, silence,
self-control and purity of feeling are called austerity of mind. This threefold
austerity, practised with the highest faith by disciplined men who want no
fruit, they call of goodness. Austerity practised with hypocrisy for the sake of
respect, honour and worship is called here of passion, unsteady and not lasting.
Austerity practised with a foolish notion, with torment of oneself, or to
destroy another, is said to be of darkness.

"'A gift given because it ought to be given, to one who does nothing in return,
at the right place and time and to a worthy person, is held to be of goodness.
But a gift given to get something back, or aiming at the fruit, or given
grudgingly, is held to be of passion. A gift given at the wrong place and time
to unworthy people, without respect and with contempt, is said to be of
darkness.

"'Om Tat Sat: this is held to be the threefold name of Brahman. By it the
brahmins, the Vedas and the sacrifices were ordained long ago. So the acts of
sacrifice, giving and austerity laid down by the rule always begin, for those
who speak of Brahman, with the word Om. With the word Tat, and without aiming at
the fruit, acts of sacrifice and austerity, and acts of giving of many kinds,
are done by those who seek release. The word Sat is used for what is real and
for what is good. So too, Partha, the word Sat is used for a praiseworthy act.
Steadiness in sacrifice, austerity and giving is also called Sat, and action for
that purpose is called Sat too. What is offered, given or practised as
austerity, or done, without faith, is called Asat, "not real", Partha. It is
nothing after death, nor here.'"

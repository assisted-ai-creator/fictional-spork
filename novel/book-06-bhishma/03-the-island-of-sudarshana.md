# The Island of Sudarshana

When he had said this, Vyasa went away, and Dhritarashtra, having heard it, fell
to brooding. He brooded for a moment, sighing again and again. Then he asked
Sanjaya, whose soul was disciplined, bull of the Bharatas:

"Sanjaya, these brave kings who delight in war kill one another with weapons
high and low. Lords of the earth, for the earth's sake they have given up their
lives. They do not stop killing and filling Yama's house. Wanting lordship over
the earth, they cannot bear one another. I think the earth must have many
virtues. Tell me about it, Sanjaya. Many thousands, millions, hundreds of
millions and tens of millions of the world's heroes have gathered in the Kuru
country. I want to hear truly the measure of the lands and cities, Sanjaya, from
which they have come. You have the eye of knowledge, lit by the lamp of divine
understanding, by the grace of that brahmin seer Vyasa of boundless energy."

"I will tell you the virtues of the earth, wise one, as far as I understand
them," said Sanjaya. "Look with the eye of learning. Homage to you, bull of the
Bharatas.

"There are two kinds of beings here, the moving and the unmoving. The moving
have three kinds of birth: from eggs, from sweat and from the womb. Of all the
moving, king, the womb-born are the best, and of the womb-born the best are men
and the beasts. They take many forms, and there are fourteen kinds of them:
seven live in the forest and seven in villages. Lions, tigers, boars, buffaloes,
elephants, bears and monkeys are held to be the seven of the forest, king. The
cow, goat, man, sheep, horse, mule and donkey are called by the good the seven
beasts of the village. These fourteen beasts, king, of village and forest, are
named in the Veda, and the sacrifices rest on them. Of the village beasts man is
best, and of the forest-dwellers the lion. All beings live on one another. The
unmoving are said to be those that spring from the ground, and they are of five
kinds only: trees, shrubs, creepers, climbing plants and grasses, which have
bark for their pith. Of these nineteen, with the five great elements,
twenty-four are named, the gayatri honoured by the world. One who truly knows
this holy gayatri with all its virtues, best of the Bharatas, is not lost to the
worlds. Everything is born on the earth, and everything perishes on the earth.
The earth is the foundation of beings and their final refuge. Whoever has the
earth has the whole world, moving and unmoving. That is why kings, greedy for
it, kill one another."

---

"Tell me in full, Sanjaya, you who know measures," said Dhritarashtra, "the
names of the rivers and mountains, and of the countries and the others who live
on the earth, and the measure of the whole earth, and its forests."

"Great king," said Sanjaya, "the wise say these five great elements, taken
together, are equal and stand in the whole world: earth, water, wind, fire and
space. Each has more qualities than the next, and earth comes first. Sound,
touch, form, taste and smell, the fifth: these are said by the seers who know
the truth to be the qualities of earth. Water has four qualities, king; smell is
not found in it. Fire has three, sound, touch and form. Wind has sound and
touch, and space sound alone. These five qualities, king, are in the five great
elements, in all the worlds, on which the worlds rest. They do not overpass one
another when there is balance. But when they enter into imbalance with one
another, then bodied beings pass beyond into bodies, and not otherwise. They
perish in order and are born in order. All of them are beyond measure; that is
their lordly form. Elements made of the five are seen here and there, and men
state their measures by reasoning. But one should not try to establish by
reasoning the things that cannot be thought. What is beyond nature is the mark
of the unthinkable.

"I will tell you of the island Sudarshana, joy of the Kurus. That island, great
king, is round, shaped like a wheel. It is covered with rivers and water, with
mountains like clouds, with cities of many shapes and lovely countries. It has
trees bearing flowers and fruit and is rich in wealth and grain, and the salt
ocean surrounds it on every side. As a man sees his own face in a mirror, so the
island of Sudarshana is seen in the circle of the moon. In two parts of it is a
fig tree, and in two parts a great hare, and it is filled on every side with all
kinds of herbs. The rest is to be known as water. That is the island told in
brief."

---

"You have told me the island in brief, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra. "Now tell
it in full. Tell me the measure of the space of earth that is seen in the one
marked by the hare. Then tell me of the fig tree."

Spoken to like this by the king, Sanjaya said, "Great king, these six mountains
of jewels stretch from east to west. They plunge on both sides into the eastern
and western oceans: Himavat, Hemakuta, Nishadha, the best of mountains, Nila
made of beryl, Shveta bright as silver, and the mountain called Shringavat, set
with every ore. These mountains, king, are served by the perfected ones and the
charanas. The distance between them is thousands of yojanas. There are holy
countries there, called *varshas*, Bharata, and beings of many kinds live in
them everywhere. This is the *varsha* of Bharata, beyond it is Haimavata, and
beyond Hemakuta they speak of Harivarsha.

"South of Nila and north of Nishadha, great king, stretches the mountain called
Malyavat, from east to west. Beyond Malyavat is the mountain Gandhamadana.
Between them is Meru, the round mountain of gold, bright as the young sun, like
a fire without smoke. They say it goes down sixteen thousand yojanas, and it is
eighty-four thousand yojanas high, lord of the earth. It stands covering the
worlds above, below and across. Beside it lie four lands, lord: Bhadrashva,
Ketumala and Jambudvipa, Bharata, and the Northern Kurus, the refuge of those
who have done good.

"There, they say, the bird Sumukha, son of Suparna, seeing the crows there
turned to gold, thought, 'Meru makes no difference between the best, the
middling and the lowest birds. So I will leave it.' The sun, lord of the lights,
goes round it always, and the moon with the stars, and the wind, keeping it on
their right. That mountain, great king, has heavenly flowers and fruit and is
covered all over with lovely houses of gold. There, king, hosts of gods,
gandharvas, asuras and rakshasas, with hosts of apsarases, always play on the
mountain. There Brahma, Rudra and Shakra, lord of the gods, meet and sacrifice
with many kinds of sacrifices with many gifts. Tumburu, Narada, Vishvavasu, Haha
and Huhu come to the best of the immortals and praise them with hymns, lord. The
great seven seers and Kashyapa Prajapati go there always at every change of the
moon, bless you. On its summit is Ushanas Kavya with the daityas, lord of the
earth. These jewels are his, and these mountains of jewels are his. From it the
blessed Kubera enjoys a fourth part, and of that wealth he gives a sixteenth
part to men.

"On its northern side is a heavenly, lovely forest of karnikara trees, flowering
in every season, rising from a mass of rocks. There Pashupati himself, the
blessed one who brings beings into being, takes his pleasure, surrounded by
heavenly creatures, with Uma beside him. He wears a garland of karnikara flowers
that hangs to his feet, shining with three eyes like three risen suns. Perfected
ones of fierce austerity, of good vows and true speech, see him, for the great
lord cannot be seen by those of bad conduct. From the peak of that mountain,
lord of men, falls the holy and lovely Ganga Bhagirathi, served by the holiest.
She falls in a stream like milk, thirty arm-spans wide, with a terrible
thundering sound, with unceasing force, into the lovely lake of the moon. She
made that holy lake, which is like an ocean. Long ago the great lord held her on
his head for a hundred thousand years, though even mountains could hardly bear
her.

"On the western side of Meru, lord of the earth, is Ketumala, and there too is
the very great Jambu grove, like Nandana. Life there lasts ten thousand years,
Bharata. The men are the colour of gold and the women like apsarases. Men are
born there without sickness, free of grief, always glad at heart, bright as
burnished gold. On the peaks of Gandhamadana Kubera, lord of the guhyakas, takes
his pleasure with the rakshasas, surrounded by hosts of apsarases. On the
farther slopes of Gandhamadana are the Aparagandikas; the longest life there is
eleven thousand years. The men there are dark, king, full of energy and very
strong, and the women all have the colour of blue lotus petals and are lovely to
see.

"Beyond Nila is Shveta, beyond Shveta Hairanyaka, and beyond Shringavat the land
called Airavata. The two lands at the south and north are shaped like bows,
great king, and Ilavrita is in the middle: so there are five lands. Each land
surpasses the one before it in its virtues, in length of life, in health, in
dharma, pleasure and wealth. The beings in those lands are endowed with them,
Bharata. So, great king, the earth is heaped with mountains.

"The very great Hemakuta is the mountain called Kailasa, where King Vaishravana
takes his pleasure with the guhyakas. North of Kailasa, towards Mount Mainaka,
is the very great mountain Hiranyashringa, heavenly and made of jewels. Beside
it is the great, heavenly, lovely lake called Bindusaras, with sands of gold,
where King Bhagiratha, having seen the Ganga Bhagirathi, lived for many years.
There are sacrificial posts of jewels there and altars of gold. There the
thousand-eyed god of great fame sacrificed and reached perfection. There the
eternal lord of beings, having created all the worlds, of fierce splendour, is
worshipped, surrounded by gathered beings, and Nara and Narayana, Brahma, Manu
and Sthanu, the fifth, worship him.

"There the goddess who goes three ways, the Ganga, first stands, having come
down from the world of Brahma. There she becomes seven: Vasvokasara, Nalini,
Pavana, Sarasvati, Jambunadi, Sita, and the Ganga and the Sindhu, the seventh.
This is the lord's own arrangement, beyond thought, of heavenly purpose. There
they hold a sacrificial session at the end of a thousand ages. The Sarasvati is
seen in some places and unseen in others. These are the seven heavenly Gangas,
famous in the three worlds.

"Rakshasas live on Himavat, guhyakas on Hemakuta, serpents and nagas on
Nishadha, and ascetics on Gokarna. The Shveta mountain is said to be the home of
the gods and asuras. Gandharvas live on the Nishadha mountain and brahmin seers
on Nila, king. Shringavat, great king, is the resort of the ancestors. These,
great king, are the seven lands in their parts, and beings dwell in them, moving
and fixed. Their prosperity, divine and human, is seen to be of many kinds. It
cannot be counted, but one who wishes to prosper should believe it.

"You ask me, king, about that heavenly shape of a hare. On either side of the
hare are the two lands, south and north. Its ears are Nagadvipa and
Kashyapadvipa. Its head, king, is the glorious Malaya mountain, copper-coloured.
This is the second part of the island that is seen in the shape of a hare."

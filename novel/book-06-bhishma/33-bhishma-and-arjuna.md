# Bhishma and Arjuna

"When the armies were drawn up like this, mine and the others'," said
Dhritarashtra, "how did the best of fighters set about the fight?"

"The armies were drawn up evenly," said Sanjaya, "in armour, with bright
banners. Your son King Duryodhana saw a force like the ocean, as if without a
farther shore. Standing among them, he said to all your men, 'Fight, in your
armour.' Setting their minds on cruelty, giving up their lives, all of them,
banners raised, went against the Pandavas. Then the battle began, tumultuous and
making the hair stand on end, between yours and the enemy, chariots and
elephants entangled. Gold-feathered, sharp arrows loosed by the chariot fighters
fell, their points unblunted, on elephants and horses.

"When the battle had begun like this, strong-armed Bhishma of terrible courage,
in armour, raised his bow. He fell on Subhadra's son, on Bhimasena, on the great
chariot fighter Shaineya, on the Kekaya, on Virata and on Dhrishtadyumna
Prishata's grandson. On these heroes among men, and on the Chedis and Matsyas on
every side, the old grandfather of the Kurus rained showers of arrows. The great
array shook at that meeting of heroes, and there was a great confusion of all
the armies. With banners and elephants brought down, their finest horses killed,
their ranks of chariots scattered, the Pandavas were in disorder.

"But Arjuna, tiger among men, seeing the great chariot fighter Bhishma, said in
anger to the Varshneya, 'Go where the grandfather is. This Bhishma, Varshneya,
very angry, devoted to Duryodhana's good, will clearly destroy my army. Here are
Drona, Kripa, Shalya and Vikarna, Janardana, and the sons of Dhritarashtra
together, led by Duryodhana. Guarded by that strong archer, they will kill the
Panchalas. So I will go to Bhishma, for the army's sake, Janardana.'

"'Be ready, Dhananjaya,' said Vasudeva. 'Here, I will bring you to the
grandfather's chariot, hero.' When he had said this, Shauri brought that
chariot, famous in the world, to Bhishma's chariot, lord of men. It had many
fluttering flags and horses the colour of cranes, and its raised, very terrible
monkey banner roared. With its great noise like a cloud, bright as the sun, the
Pandava came, striking down the Kaurava army and the Shurasenas. He drove his
arrows fast, the destroyer of his friends' grief. He came on with force like an
elephant in rut, terrifying the heroes in battle and bringing them down with
arrows. Bhishma son of Shantanu, guarded by the men of Sindhu at their head, by
the easterners, Sauviras and Kekayas, went out at once to meet Arjuna. For what
other chariot fighter but the grandfather of the Kurus, or Drona or Vaikartana,
could go against the bearer of Gandiva?

"Then Bhishma, great king, grandfather of the Kauravas, covered Arjuna with
seventy-seven iron arrows. Drona pierced him with twenty-five, Kripa with fifty,
Duryodhana with sixty-four, Shalya with nine, the king of Sindhu with nine,
Shakuni with five, and Vikarna with ten broad-headed arrows, king. Pierced on
every side by those sharp arrows, the strong-armed great archer did not tremble,
like a mountain being split. The diademed one, of boundless spirit, pierced back
Bhishma with twenty-five arrows, Kripa with nine and the tiger among men Drona
with sixty. He pierced Vikarna with three, Artayani with three arrows and the
king with five, bull of the Bharatas. Satyaki, Virata, Dhrishtadyumna Prishata's
grandson, the sons of Draupadi and Abhimanyu surrounded Dhananjaya. Then the
Panchala prince, with the Somakas, rained on the great archer Drona, who was
devoted to the good of Ganga's son.

"Bhishma, the best of chariot fighters, quickly pierced the Pandava with eighty
sharp arrows, and your men cried out. Hearing the shouts of those glad men, the
mighty lion among chariot fighters entered their midst as if glad. Reaching the
middle of those lions among chariot fighters, Dhananjaya played with his bow,
king, making the great chariot fighters his targets.

"Then King Duryodhana, lord of men, saw his own army tormented by Partha in
battle. He said to Bhishma, 'Father, this mighty son of Pandu with Krishna is
cutting at our root while all our troops strive, while you, Ganga's son, and
Drona, the best of chariot fighters, are alive. Because of you even Karna, the
great chariot fighter, who always wants my good, has laid down his weapons and
does not fight Partha in battle. So act, son of Ganga, so that Phalguna is
killed.' Spoken to like this, king, your father Devavrata said, 'Shame on the
dharma of kshatriyas!' and went towards Partha's chariot.

"Seeing the two with white horses engaged, king, the kings roared lions' roars
loudly and blew their conches, Bharata. Drona's son, Duryodhana and your son
Vikarna surrounded Bhishma in the battle and stood ready to fight, sir. So too
all the Pandavas surrounded Dhananjaya and stood ready for the great fight, and
the battle began. Ganga's son struck Partha in battle with nine arrows, and
Arjuna pierced him back with ten that pierced the vitals. Then the Pandava
Arjuna, proud in battle, held off Bhishma's quarters with a thousand well-aimed
arrows. Then Bhishma son of Shantanu held off Partha's net of arrows with a net
of arrows, Kaurava. Both of them very glad, both delighting in battle, they
fought without difference, each wanting to repay the other. Masses of nets of
arrows loosed from Bhishma's bow were seen scattered, broken by Arjuna's arrows.
So too nets of arrows loosed by Arjuna fell to the ground in parts, driven off
by Ganga's son's arrows. Arjuna struck Bhishma with twenty-five sharp arrows,
and Bhishma pierced Partha in battle with thirty. Piercing each other's horses
and banners, the two mighty tamers of enemies played on chariot pole and chariot
wheel.

"Then, great king, Bhishma, the best of fighters, in anger struck Vasudeva with
three arrows between the breasts. Pierced by the arrows loosed from Bhishma's
bow, Madhusudana shone in the battle, king, like a flowering kimshuka. Then
Arjuna, very angry, seeing Madhava pierced, pierced Ganga's son's charioteer
with three arrows. The two heroes, striving to kill each other, could not then
get the better of each other in battle. They showed wonderful circlings, goings
and returnings, in many ways, through the skill and lightness of their
charioteers. Looking for an opening between blows, king, the two great chariot
fighters stood again and again in the way of openings. Both made the sound of
conches mixed with lions' roars, and the twang of their bows. With the sound of
their conches and the rumble of their chariot wheels the earth split, shook and
roared.

"No one saw any difference between them, bull of the Bharatas. Both were mighty
and brave in battle, each like the other. The Kauravas knew Bhishma there only
by his emblem, and so the sons of Pandu knew Partha only by his emblem. Seeing
such courage in those two best of men, all beings were filled with wonder in the
battle, Bharata. No one saw a gap in either of them in the battle, Bharata, as
no one sees a fault anywhere in a man who stands in dharma. Both became
invisible in nets of arrows, and quickly both became visible again in the
battle. There the gods with the gandharvas, the charanas and the seers saw their
courage and said to one another, 'These two great chariot fighters, roused in
battle, can never be conquered, even by the worlds with the gods, asuras and
gandharvas. This battle is a wonder in the worlds, most marvellous. There will
never be battles like this. Bhishma cannot be conquered in battle by wise Partha
while he has his bow and stands on his chariot, sowing arrows in battle. In the
same way Bhishma could not conquer the Pandava in battle, the archer whom even
the gods could hardly approach.' Such words were heard, going up here and there,
full of praise for Ganga's son and Arjuna in the battle, lord of the people.

"Your fighters and the Pandavas, Bharata, killed one another in the battle while
those two showed their courage. With sharp-edged swords, bright axes, many
arrows and weapons of many kinds the heroes of both armies cut one another down
in battle. While that terrible, very dreadful battle went on, king, there was a
great clash between Drona and the Panchala prince."

---

"How did the great archer Drona and the Panchala, Prishata's grandson, meet
ready in battle?" said Dhritarashtra. "Tell me that, Sanjaya. I think fate is
greater than human effort, Sanjaya, since Bhishma son of Shantanu did not get
the better of the Pandava in battle. For Bhishma in his anger could kill the
worlds, moving and unmoving. How did he not get the better of the Pandava in
battle by his strength, Sanjaya?"

"Listen, king, and be steady, to this very dreadful battle," said Sanjaya. "The
Pandava cannot be conquered even by the gods with Vasava. Drona fought
Dhrishtadyumna with sharp arrows and brought down his charioteer from the
chariot floor with a broad-headed arrow. Then in his anger he harried
Dhrishtadyumna's four horses with four of his best arrows, sir. Dhrishtadyumna,
laughing, pierced Drona with ninety sharp arrows, the hero, and cried, 'Stand!
Stand!' Then mighty Bharadvaja's son, of boundless spirit, again covered
impatient Dhrishtadyumna with arrows. He took up a terrible arrow to kill
Prishata's grandson, striking like Shakra's thunderbolt, like a second rod of
death. There was a great outcry from the whole army, Bharata, when they saw that
arrow set to the bow by Bharadvaja's son in the battle. There we saw
Dhrishtadyumna's wonderful manhood, that the hero stood alone in the battle
unmoving like a mountain. He cut that blazing, terrible arrow, his own death, as
it came, and loosed a shower of arrows at Bharadvaja's son. Then all the
Panchalas with the Pandavas cried out when they saw that very hard deed done by
Dhrishtadyumna.

"Then, wanting Drona's death, the valiant one hurled a spear of great speed
adorned with gold and beryl. As that spear adorned with gold came on,
Bharadvaja's son, as if laughing, broke it in three in the battle. Seeing the
spear struck down, mighty Dhrishtadyumna rained showers of arrows on Drona, lord
of men. Holding off that shower of arrows, Drona of great fame cut Drupada's
son's bow in the middle. With his bow cut, the mighty one of great fame hurled a
heavy club made of the essence of mountains at Drona. Loosed with force, that
club went to kill Drona, and there we saw Bharadvaja's son's wonderful courage.
With lightness he made the club adorned with gold miss. Then he sent at
Prishata's grandson very sharp, yellow broad-headed arrows with golden feathers,
whetted on stone. They pierced his armour and drank his blood in the battle.

"Then great-minded Dhrishtadyumna took another bow, showed his courage against
Drona in battle and pierced him with five arrows. Smeared with blood, those two
bulls among men shone like two flowering kimshukas in spring, king. Then Drona,
impatient, king, showing his courage at the head of the army, again cut
Drupada's son's bow. Then he of boundless spirit covered him, his bow cut, with
arrows with bent joints, like a cloud raining on a mountain. He brought down his
charioteer from the chariot floor with a broad-headed arrow and then struck down
his four horses with four sharp arrows in the battle, and roared a lion's roar.
Then with another broad-headed arrow he cut the bow from his hand. With his bow
cut, without chariot, horses or charioteer, he got down with a club in hand,
showing his great manhood. Bharata, Drona quickly brought that club down with
arrows before he could get down from the chariot, and it was a wonder. Then the
strong-armed, mighty one took a large, bright shield with a hundred moons and a
large, heavenly sword. He ran at Drona with speed, wanting to kill him, like a
lion wanting meat running at an elephant in rut in the forest.

"There we saw Bharadvaja's son's wonderful manhood, Bharata, his lightness, his
use of weapons and the strength of his arms. He held off Prishata's grandson
with a shower of arrows, and strong as he was, the prince could not go on in the
battle. There we saw the great chariot fighter Dhrishtadyumna standing, warding
off floods of arrows with his shield, like a man skilled with his hands. Then
strong-armed, mighty Bhima rushed at once to help the great Prishata's grandson
in battle. He pierced Drona with seven sharp arrows, king, and quickly put
Prishata's grandson on another chariot. Then King Duryodhana urged on the
Kalinga with a great army to protect Bharadvaja's son. Then that great army of
the Kalingas, lord of men, went quickly at Bhima at your son's command. Drona
too, the best of chariot fighters, leaving the Panchala prince, fought the two
old men, Virata and Drupada, together, and Dhrishtadyumna went in the battle to
the King of Dharma. Then a tumultuous battle began, making the hair stand on
end, between the Kalingas and great Bhima, a fight that destroyed the world,
terrible in form and dreadful."

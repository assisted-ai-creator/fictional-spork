# Nara and Narayana

"'Then the Blessed One, the god, the highest lord of the worlds,'" said Bhishma,
"'answered Brahma in a deep, warm voice. "Son, by yoga I know all that you want.
It will be so." Having said this, he vanished right there. Then the gods, seers
and gandharvas, filled with the greatest wonder and curiosity, said to the
grandfather, "Who is this, lord, whom the Blessed One bowed to humbly and
praised with the finest words? We wish to hear of him."

"'So addressed, the Blessed One, the grandfather, answered all the gods, brahmin
seers and gandharvas in a sweet voice. "He who is beyond that, and what will be,
and what must be, the highest; he who is the self of beings, the lord, the
brahman and the highest state: with him, gracious, I have been talking, bulls
among the gods. I asked the lord of the world, for the world's sake, 'Take your
place in the world of men, known as Vasudeva. Be born on earth to kill the
asuras.' The daityas, danavas and rakshasas who were killed in battle have been
born among men, terrible in form and very strong. To kill them the Blessed One,
self-controlled, will take a human birth and move on the earth together with
Nara. Nara and Narayana, the two ancient seers, the best of seers, have been
born together in the world of men, of measureless splendour. Together, and ready
in battle, they cannot be conquered even by the immortals. But the deluded do
not know them as the seers Nara and Narayana. I, Brahma, the lord of the whole
world, am his son. Vasudeva, the great lord of all the worlds, is to be
worshipped by you. Best of gods, the bearer of the conch, discus and club, of
great courage, must never be scorned with the thought, 'This is a man.' This is
the highest secret, this is the highest state. This is the highest brahman, this
is the highest glory. This is the imperishable, the unmanifest, this is the
eternal and the great. This is what is called the Person: it is sung of, and it
is not known. This is the highest splendour, this is the highest happiness. This
is the highest truth, as the maker of all has declared it. So Vasudeva, the lord
of measureless courage, must not be scorned by any of the gods with Indra, or by
the worlds, with the thought, 'This is a man.' Whoever, very dull of mind, says,
'This is only a man,' scorning Hrishikesha, they call the lowest of men. Whoever
looks down on Vasudeva, the great-souled yogin who has entered a human body,
people call a man of darkness. Whoever does not know the god, the self of all
that moves and does not move, marked with the shrivatsa, very bright, with the
lotus at his navel, people call a man of darkness. Whoever scorns the
great-souled one who wears the diadem and the Kaustubha jewel, who frees his
friends from fear, sinks into terrible darkness. Knowing the truth of this, best
of gods, Vasudeva, the lord of the lords of the worlds, must be bowed to by all
the worlds."

"'Having said this to all the hosts of gods long ago, the Blessed One, the self
of all the worlds, sent them away and went to his own dwelling. Then the gods
with the gandharvas, the sages and the apsarases, having heard that story told
by Brahma, went to heaven, pleased.

"'I heard this, son, from seers of perfected self who spoke of Vasudeva when
they met long ago. I heard it, you who are learned in what is heard, from Rama
son of Jamadagni, from the wise Markandeya, from Vyasa and from Narada. Knowing
this, and having heard of the unchanging lord Vasudeva, the great soul, the lord
of the lords of the worlds, whose son is Brahma, the father of the whole world,
how should Vasudeva not be worshipped and honoured with sacrifice by men? You
were held back long ago, son, by sages who have mastered the Veda. "Do not go to
battle with the wise Vasudeva," they said, "nor with the Pandavas." In your
folly you do not understand that. I think you are a cruel rakshasa, and you are
covered in darkness, since you hate Govinda and Dhananjaya the Pandava, the gods
Nara and Narayana. No other human being would hate them. So I tell you, king, he
is eternal and unchanging. He is in all the worlds, everlasting, the ruler, the
creator, the upholder, the fixed one. He holds up the three worlds, the teacher
of all that moves and does not move, the lord. He is the fighter, the victory
and the victor, the source of all nature, the lord. King, he is made of
goodness, free from darkness and passion. Where Krishna is, there is dharma.
Where dharma is, there is victory. By the yoga of his greatness and the yoga of
his own self, king, Pandu's sons are upheld, and victory will be theirs. He
always gives the Pandavas the understanding that leads to good, gives them
strength in battle and protects them from dangers. He is the eternal god, made
of every secret, the auspicious one, who is to be known as Vasudeva. That is
what you ask me, Bharata. He is served and worshipped by brahmins, kshatriyas,
vaishyas and shudras, marked by their duties, each always devoted to his own
work. At the end of the Dvapara age and the beginning of the Kali age,
Samkarshana sang of him, following the Satvata rite. Vasudeva creates the whole
world of asuras and mortals, and the cities girdled by the sea. Age after age he
takes up his dwelling among men, again and again.'

---

"Duryodhana said, 'Vasudeva is spoken of in all the worlds as a great being. I
want to know his origin and his standing, grandfather.'

"Bhishma said, 'Vasudeva is a great being, born together with the gods, bull of
the Bharatas. Nothing is seen higher than the lotus-eyed one. Markandeya too
tells great wonders of Govinda. The self of beings, the great soul, the highest
Person, created all beings, and these three, water, wind and fire. Having
created the earth, the god, the lord of all the worlds, the great-souled highest
Person, made his bed in the waters. The god who is all the waters slept there by
yoga. From his mouth he created fire, and from his breath the wind. From his
mind the unfalling one created Sarasvati and the Vedas. He created the worlds in
the beginning, and the gods with the hosts of seers. The unchanging source of
creatures, he also created their end and death. He is dharma and the knower of
dharma, the giver of boons, the granter of all desires. He is the doer and the
deed, the first god, his own lord. Janardana first created what was, what will
be and what is to be, the two twilights, the quarters, the sky and the rule.
Govinda created the seers and austerity. The great-souled, unchanging lord also
created the creator of the world. He created Samkarshana, first born of all
beings. He created the god Shesha, whom they know as Ananta, who holds up the
beings and this earth with its mountains. Brahmins know that very powerful one
by the yoga of meditation.

"'Madhu, a great asura born from the ear, fierce, of fierce deeds, set on a
fierce purpose, the highest Person killed to do honour to Brahma. Because he
killed him, son, gods, danavas and men and seers call Janardana the Killer of
Madhu. The lord is the boar and the lion, and he of the three strides. Hari is
the mother and father of all living beings. There never was and never will be
anything higher than the lotus-eyed one. From his mouth he created the brahmins,
from his arms the kshatriyas, from his thighs the vaishyas, king, and from his
feet the shudras. Disciplined by austerity, the god is the treasure house of all
embodied beings. A man who serves Keshava, who is brahman and yoga, on the new
moon and the full moon, will gain the great reward. Keshava is the highest
splendour, the grandfather of all the worlds. So the sages speak of Hrishikesha,
lord of men. Know him like this, as teacher, father and guru. Those with whom
Krishna is pleased have won the imperishable worlds. A man who goes to Keshava
for refuge in a place of fear, and who always recites this, will be well and
happy. Men who take refuge in Krishna are not confused. Janardana always
protects those who are sunk in great fear. Knowing this as it truly is, Bharata,
Yudhishthira has taken refuge with all his soul in the great-souled Keshava,
lord of the world, the lord and master of yoga, king.'

"Bhishma said, 'Hear this too, great king, my hymn of him who is brahman, which
brahmin seers and gods told long ago on the earth. "Narada said that you are the
lord, the lord of the god of gods even of the Sadhya gods, who knows the being
of the worlds and brings them into being. Markandeya said that you are what was,
what will be and what is to be. The Blessed One Bhrigu said that you are the
sacrifice of sacrifices, the austerity of austerities, the god even of the gods.
In the old story, Vishnu, your form is terrible, and you are called the lord of
beings. Dvaipayana said, 'You are Vasudeva of the Vasus, the one who set up
Shakra, the god of gods among the gods.' In the first creations of creatures
they called Daksha the lord of creatures. Angiras then said that you are the
creator of all beings. Devala Asita said that the unmanifest arose from your
body, that the manifest rests in your mind, and that the gods were born from
your speech. The sky is pervaded by your head, the earth is held up by your
arms, the three worlds are your belly. You are the eternal Person. So men who
are made pure by austerity know you. You are the best of seers who are content
with seeing the self. Killer of Madhu, you are the goal of noble royal seers who
do not turn back in battle and who put dharma above all."

"'This, son, is the account of Keshava as it truly is, told both at length and
in brief. Be well disposed to Keshava.'

"When your son had heard this holy story, great king," said Sanjaya, "he came to
think highly of Keshava and of the Pandavas, the great chariot fighters. Bhishma
son of Shantanu spoke to him again, great king. 'You have heard, king, the
greatness of the great-souled Keshava, and of Nara too, as it truly is, as you
asked me. You have heard for what purpose the two, Nara and Narayana, were born
among men, and how the heroes cannot be killed and are unconquered in battle,
and how the Pandavas cannot be reached in battle by anyone, king. Krishna has a
strong love for the glorious Pandavas. So I tell you, king of kings, let there
be peace with the Pandavas. Rule the earth, self-controlled, with your strong
brothers. If you scorn the gods Nara and Narayana, you will perish.'

"Having said this, lord of the people, your father fell silent. He sent the king
away and went to his bed. The king too bowed to the great-souled one and went to
the camp. He lay down on his bright bed that night, bull of the Bharatas."

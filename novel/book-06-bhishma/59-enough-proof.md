# Enough Proof

"Then King Duryodhana," said Sanjaya, "Shakuni son of Subala, your son
Duhshasana and the charioteer's son, hard to conquer, met together, great king.
They held the counsel they had in mind: how the sons of Pandu and their troops
could be beaten in battle. Then King Duryodhana spoke to all his counsellors,
addressing the charioteer's son and the very strong son of Subala. 'Drona,
Bhishma, Kripa, Shalya and Somadatta's son do not hold back the Parthas in
battle. I do not know the reason. And they, not being killed, are wearing down
my army. So my strength is worn away, Karna, and my weapons are worn away in
battle. I am wronged by the brave Pandavas, whom even the gods cannot kill. In
this doubt, how am I to fight the battle?'

"The charioteer's son said to the lord of men, great king, 'Do not grieve, best
of the Bharatas. I will do what you want. Let Bhishma son of Shantanu leave the
great battle at once. When Ganga's son has turned back from the battle and laid
down his weapons, Bharata, I will kill the Parthas with all the Somakas while
Bhishma watches. I swear it to you by the truth, king. Bhishma always shows pity
to the Pandavas, king, and Bhishma is unable to defeat these great chariot
fighters in battle. Bhishma is proud in battle and always loves battle. How then
will he defeat the Pandavas when they come together in battle, son? So go
quickly from here to Bhishma's camp. Get Bhishma's consent and make him lay down
his weapons, Bharata. When Bhishma has laid down his weapons, see the Pandavas
killed in battle by me alone, king, with their hosts of friends and kin.'

"So addressed by Karna, your son Duryodhana said this to his brother Duhshasana
there. 'Duhshasana, quickly get everything ready, so that all my escort is
prepared on every side.' Having said this, king, the lord of the people said to
Karna, 'Having got the consent of Bhishma, the best of men, I will come back
from there quickly to you, tamer of enemies. Then you, tiger among men, will do
battle.'

"Then your son went out quickly, lord of the people, with all his brothers, like
Shatakratu with the gods. Then his brother Duhshasana quickly helped that tiger
among kings, of a tiger's courage, onto a horse. Wearing armlets, his crown
bound on, with ornaments on his hands, the son of Dhritarashtra shone, great
king, like great Indra. He was anointed with fragrant sandal paste of the
highest value, the colour of bhandi flowers or of gold, and wrapped in a
spotless garment. With the playful gait of a lion, the king shone like the sun
with clear rays in autumn. As the tiger among men went to Bhishma's camp, great
archers, bowmen of the whole world, followed him, and his brothers, great
archers, like the thirty gods following Vasava. Some rode horses, Bharata, some
elephants, and other fine men surrounded him on every side in chariots. Friends
with weapons in their hands appeared together to protect the lord of the earth,
like the immortals around Shakra in heaven. Honoured by the Kurus, the great
chariot fighter of the Kauravas went to the dwelling of the glorious son of
Ganga, king, followed on every side by his brothers together.

"Raising at the right moment his skilful right arm, like an elephant's trunk,
trained, the destroyer of all enemies, he accepted the joined palms of men
raised on every side. He heard the sweet words of people who lived in many
countries. The glorious one was praised by sutas and Magadhas, and he, the lord
of the lords of all the world, honoured them all. They surrounded the
great-souled one on every side with blazing golden lamps fed with scented oil.
Surrounded by those fine golden lamps, the king shone like the moon joined with
blazing great planets. Men in jackets and turbans, with canes and rattles in
their hands, gently moved the people aside on every side.

"Reaching Bhishma's fine dwelling, the king got down from his horse, came to
Bhishma, greeted him and sat on a fine golden seat, splendid on every side,
covered with a choice rug. He joined his palms. His throat was choked with tears
and his eyes were full of them. He said to Bhishma, 'Relying on you in battle,
destroyer of enemies, we would dare to conquer in battle even the gods and
asuras with Indra. How much more the heroic sons of Pandu with their hosts of
friends and kin? So, son of Ganga, you should take pity on me, lord. Kill the
heroic sons of Pandu as great Indra killed the danavas. Before, strong-armed
one, you said, "I will kill the Somakas, the Panchalas with the Pandavas, and
the Karushas," Bharata. Let that word be true. Kill the Parthas who have come
together, and the Somakas, great archers. Be true to your word, Bharata. If out
of pity, king, or out of hatred for me, lord, or through my bad luck, you
protect the Pandavas, then give leave in battle to Karna, who shines in battle.
He will defeat the Parthas in battle with their hosts of friends and kin.'
Having said so much, King Duryodhana, your son, said nothing more to Bhishma of
terrible courage.

---

"Pierced deeply by your son with the darts of his words, the grandfather was
overcome with great grief, but said not the least unpleasant thing. He brooded
for a very long time, full of grief and anger, breathing like a snake struck
with a stick. Then he rolled his eyes in anger as if he would burn up the world
with its gods, asuras and gandharvas. The best of those who know the world said
these words to your son, beginning gently.

"'Why, Duryodhana, do you pierce me so with the darts of your words? I strive as
hard as I can and do what you want, offering up my life in battle for your good
alone. When the brave Pandava satisfied Agni at Khandava, defeating Shakra in
battle, that was enough proof. When the son of Pandu by his might freed you,
strong-armed one, after the gandharvas had carried you off, that was enough
proof. When your brave brothers ran, lord, and Radha's son the charioteer's son
too, that was enough proof. When he alone rose up against all of us together in
the city of Virata, that was enough proof. When he defeated in battle Drona,
roused in battle, and me, and Karna, and you, and Drona's son, and the great
chariot fighter Kripa, and took our clothes, that was enough proof. When Partha
defeated in battle the Nivatakavachas, whom even Vasava could hardly conquer,
that was enough proof. Who could conquer the impetuous Pandava in battle? But
you in your folly do not know what should be said and what should not,
Suyodhana. A man about to die sees all trees as golden. So you too, son of
Gandhari, see everything upside down. You yourself have made great enmity with
the Pandavas and the Srinjayas. Fight them in battle today. We shall see. Be a
man.

"'But I will kill all the Somakas and the Panchalas who have come together,
tiger among men, except Shikhandi. Either I will be killed by them in battle and
go to Yama's home, or I will kill them in battle and give you joy. For Shikhandi
was born a woman before, in the king's house. By a granted boon she became a
man, but she is the woman Shikhandini. Her I will not kill, even if I lose my
life, Bharata. She whom the Creator made first is the woman Shikhandini. Sleep
well, son of Gandhari. Tomorrow I will fight a great battle that people will
talk of as long as the earth stands.'

"So addressed, your son went out, lord of the people. He bowed his head to his
elder and went to his own quarters. Having come there, the king, the bringer of
destruction to his enemies, dismissed the great crowd and quickly went into his
tent. Having gone in, the lord of the earth passed that night."

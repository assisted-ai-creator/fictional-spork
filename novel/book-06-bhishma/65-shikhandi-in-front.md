# Shikhandi in Front

Dhritarashtra said, "How did Shikhandi go against Ganga's son in battle, and the
Pandavas against Bhishma? Tell me that, Sanjaya."

"At clear dawn," said Sanjaya, "as the sun rose, with drums, mridangas and
kettledrums sounding and curd-white conches blown on every side, the Pandavas
went out to battle with Shikhandi in front. They made an array, great king, that
destroyed all enemies, and Shikhandi was at the head of all the troops, lord of
the people. Then Bhimasena and Dhananjaya were his wheel guards. Behind were the
sons of Draupadi and the mighty son of Subhadra. Satyaki and the great chariot
fighter Chekitana were their guards. Then behind came Dhrishtadyumna, protected
by the Panchalas. Then King Yudhishthira, the lord, went with the twins, making
the battle ring with a lion roar, bull of the Bharatas. Virata came behind with
his own army, and then Drupada, great king, ran up behind. The five Kekaya
brothers and the mighty Dhrishtaketu guarded the rear of the Pandava army,
Bharata. Having arrayed their great army so, the Pandavas ran at your army in
battle, giving up their own lives.

"So too the Kurus, king, put the very strong Bhishma at the head of all their
troops and went against the Pandavas. He was guarded by your sons, very strong
and hard to attack. Then came the great archer Drona and his son, a great
chariot fighter. Behind came Bhagadatta, surrounded by a force of elephants, and
Kripa and Kritavarma following Bhagadatta. Then behind came the strong king of
Kamboja, Sudakshina, Jayatsena of Magadha, and Brihadbala son of Subala. The
other great archers, kings led by Susharma, guarded the rear of your army,
Bharata. Day after day, as each day came, Bhishma son of Shantanu made arrays in
battle after the manner of the asuras, the pishachas and the rakshasas.

"Then the battle began between yours and theirs, Bharata, as they killed one
another, king, swelling the kingdom of Yama. The Parthas led by Arjuna, with
Shikhandi in front, went against Bhishma in battle, scattering arrows of many
kinds. There, Bharata, your men, hurt by Bhima's arrows, drenched in floods of
blood, went to the other world. Nakula, Sahadeva and the great chariot fighter
Satyaki came up against your army and pressed it with force. Being killed in
battle, bull of the Bharatas, your men could not hold back the great army of the
Pandavas. Then your army, killed on every side, ran in all directions, king,
driven by the great chariot fighters. Being killed by the Pandavas and the
Srinjayas with sharp arrows, bull of the Bharatas, your men found no protector."

Dhritarashtra said, "Seeing the army hard pressed by the Parthas, what did the
mighty Bhishma do in battle in his anger? Tell me that, Sanjaya. How did the
scorcher of enemies go out against the Pandavas in battle, killing the heroic
Somakas? Tell me that, Sanjaya."

"I will tell you, great king," said Sanjaya, "what the grandfather did when your
son's army was hard pressed by the Pandavas and the Srinjayas. Glad at heart,
elder brother of Pandu, the brave Pandavas came on, killing your son's army.
Bhishma could not bear that destruction of men, elephants and horses, lord of
men, that killing of the army in battle by the enemy. The unassailable great
archer ran at the Pandavas, the Panchalas and the Srinjayas, giving up his own
life. With his arrows, iron arrows, calf-toothed arrows and sharp crescent
arrows, king, he held off the five foremost great chariot fighters of the
Pandavas, who had their weapons in hand and were ready in battle. In his anger
he killed countless elephants and horses in battle. The bull among men brought
down chariot fighters from their chariots, king, horsemen from their horses'
backs, foot soldiers who had come together, and elephant riders from their
elephants, causing the enemy fear. The Pandavas closed together on that one
great chariot fighter, Bhishma, hurrying in battle, as the asuras closed on the
wielder of the thunderbolt. Loosing sharp arrows with a touch like Shakra's
thunderbolt, he was seen in every direction, wearing a terrible form. His great
bow, like Shakra's bow, was always seen drawn into a circle as he fought in
battle. Seeing that deed in battle, lord of the people, your sons were filled
with the greatest wonder and honoured the grandfather. The Parthas, dispirited,
looked at your father as he fought in battle, the hero, as the immortals looked
at Vipracitti. And they could not hold him back, like Death with his mouth open.

"When the tenth day had come, he burned Shikhandi's chariot force with sharp
arrows, as a fire whose path is black burns a forest. Shikhandi pierced him
between the breasts with three arrows, as he was like an angry venomous snake,
like Death let loose by Time. Badly pierced by him, Bhishma looked at Shikhandi.
Unwilling, though in anger, laughing, he said, 'Strike or not, as you like. I
will never fight you. You are the same woman Shikhandini that the Creator made.'

"Hearing his words, Shikhandi, wild with anger, licking the corners of his
mouth, said to Bhishma in battle, 'I know you, strong-armed one, destroyer of
kshatriyas. I have heard of your battle with Jamadagni's son, and I have heard
many times of this divine power of yours. Knowing your power, I will still fight
you today. Doing what is dear to the Pandavas and to myself, best of men, I will
fight you today in battle, best of men. And I will surely kill you. I swear it
by truth before you. Having heard my words, do what you see fit. Strike or not,
as you like. You will not get away from me alive. Take a good look at this
world, Bhishma, conqueror in battle.' Having said this, he pierced Bhishma in
battle with five arrows with bent joints, king, Bhishma already struck by the
arrows of his words.

"Hearing his words, the left-handed archer, scorcher of enemies, thought, 'This
is the time,' and urged Shikhandi on. 'I will follow you, driving off the enemy
with arrows. Run at Bhishma of terrible courage in fury. The very strong one
cannot hurt you in battle. So today, strong-armed hero, run at Bhishma. If you
go away without killing Bhishma in battle, sir, you will be a laughing stock to
the world, and I with you. Make such an effort in battle, hero, that we are not
laughed at in the great battle. Bring down the grandfather. I will protect you
in battle, scorcher of enemies, holding off all the chariot fighters. Bring down
the grandfather. I will hold them all off in battle, as the shore holds off the
sea: Drona and Drona's son, Kripa and Suyodhana, Chitrasena, Vikarna and
Jayadratha of Sindhu. I will hold off Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, Sudakshina
of Kamboja, the brave Bhagadatta and the great chariot fighter of Magadha. I
will hold off Somadatta's brave son, the rakshasa son of Rishyashringa, and the
king of the Trigartas with all the great chariot fighters. I will hold off all
the Kurus together, and all their soldiers standing there, in battle. Bring down
the grandfather.'

---

Dhritarashtra said, "How did Shikhandi, the Panchala, run in anger in battle at
Ganga's son the grandfather, the righteous one of controlled vows? Which great
chariot fighters of the Pandava army, wanting victory, hurrying when hurrying
was needed, protected Shikhandi with his weapon raised? How did Bhishma son of
Shantanu, of great courage, fight the Pandavas and the Srinjayas on that tenth
day? I cannot bear it, that Shikhandi went against Bhishma in battle. Did his
chariot break, or did his bow shatter as he shot?"

"His bow did not shatter, bull of the Bharatas," said Sanjaya, "nor did his
chariot break as Bhishma fought in battle, killing enemies with arrows with bent
joints. Many hundreds of thousands of your great chariot fighters, king, crowds
of chariots and elephants, and well-equipped horses, went into battle with the
grandfather in front. True to his promise, Kauravya, he who conquers in combat,
Bhishma, made continual slaughter of the Parthas in combat. The Panchalas with
the Pandavas all held off the great archer as he fought, killing enemies with
arrows. When the tenth day came he scorched the enemy army, scattered in
hundreds and thousands by his sharp arrows. The Pandavas, elder brother of
Pandu, could not conquer the great archer Bhishma in battle, like Death with the
noose in his hand.

"Then, great king, the left-handed archer, scorcher of enemies, the unconquered
Bibhatsu, terrified all the chariot fighters. Roaring loudly like a lion and
twanging his bowstring again and again, loosing floods of arrows, he moved about
the battle like Time. Terrified by his sound, your men ran in great fear, bull
of the Bharatas, like deer from a lion, king. Duryodhana saw the Pandava winning
and your army hard pressed. Badly hurt, he said to Bhishma, 'This son of Pandu
with white horses and Krishna for his charioteer is burning all my men, son, as
a fire whose path is black burns a forest. See the armies running on every side,
son of Ganga, best of fighters, driven by the Pandava in battle. As a herdsman
drives herds of beasts in a forest, so this army of mine is being driven,
scorcher of enemies. Broken by Dhananjaya's arrows, it runs this way and that.
This Bhima too, hard to attack, is driving off my force. Satyaki and Chekitana,
Madri's two Pandava sons and the bold Abhimanyu are burning my army. The brave
Dhrishtadyumna and the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, very strong both, have put my army
to flight at a stroke. As my army is being killed by all these very strong men,
I see no other refuge, in standing or in fighting, Bharata, except you, tiger
among men, whose courage is like a god's. You are enough. Be quickly the refuge
of the hard pressed.'

"So addressed, great king, your father Devavrata thought for a moment and made
his decision. Steadying your son, Shantanu's son said, 'Duryodhana, understand
this and be steady, lord of the people. Long ago I promised you, very strong
one: "Having killed ten thousand great-souled kshatriyas, I will withdraw from
the battle. This is my daily task." And I have done that, bull of the Bharatas,
as I said. Today too I will do a great deed in the great battle. Either I will
be killed and lie down, or today I will kill the Pandavas. Today, tiger among
men, I will repay the great debt I owe you, king, for my master's bread, killed
at the head of the army.'

"Having said this, the best of the Bharatas, scorching the kshatriyas with
arrows, reached the army of the Pandavas, unassailable. As Ganga's son stood in
the middle of the army like an angry venomous snake, bull of the Bharatas, the
Pandavas surrounded him. On that tenth day, showing his power, king, he killed a
hundred thousand, joy of the Kurus. He took the splendour of the best of the
Panchalas, the very strong princes, as the sun takes water with its rays. Having
killed ten thousand swift elephants with their riders, great king, and ten
thousand horses again, and two full hundred thousand foot soldiers, the best of
men blazed in battle like a smokeless fire. None of the Pandavas could look at
him, burning like the sun on its northern course. Hard pressed by the great
archer, the angry Pandavas and the great chariot fighters of the Srinjayas ran
at Bhishma to kill him. Fighting many, Bhishma son of Shantanu, the strong-armed
one, was covered, like a mountain by dark clouds. Then your sons surrounded
Ganga's son on every side with a great army, and then the battle went on."

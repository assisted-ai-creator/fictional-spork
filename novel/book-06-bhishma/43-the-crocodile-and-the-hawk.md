# The Crocodile and the Hawk

"When the night was over and the sun had risen, great king," said Sanjaya, "both
armies came together for battle. Angry, each wanting to defeat the other, they
all ran at one another together in battle, seeing one another, the Pandavas and
the sons of Dhritarashtra, king, through your bad counsel. Furious, having drawn
up their arrays, they fought, hard hitters. Bhishma guarded the crocodile array
on every side, king. In the same way the Pandavas guarded their own array, king.

"Your father Devavrata, the best of chariot fighters, went out with the chariot
force, surrounded by a great line of chariots. Chariot fighters, foot soldiers,
elephants and horsemen followed one another, each standing in his own place.
Seeing them advancing to battle, the glorious Pandavas drew up the hawk, the
king of arrays, unconquerable in battle. At its beak the very strong Bhimasena
shone. Its eyes were Shikhandi, hard to withstand, and Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's grandson. Its head was the hero Satyaki, of true courage. Partha,
shaking Gandiva, was at its neck. Its left wing was the great-souled, glorious
Drupada with his son and a full akshauhini in the battle. Its right wing was the
Kaikeya, lord of an akshauhini. At its back were the sons of Draupadi and the
mighty son of Subhadra. At the back too stood the glorious King Yudhishthira
himself, the wise one of handsome courage, with his twin brothers.

"Then Bhima went into the crocodile at its mouth in the battle, reached Bhishma
and covered him with arrows. Then Bhishma brought down great weapons in that
great battle, Bharata, confusing the arrayed army of Pandu's sons. With the army
confused, Dhananjaya hurried and pierced Bhishma with a thousand arrows at the
head of the battle. He warded off the weapons Bhishma loosed in battle and stood
ready to fight with his own glad force.

"Then King Duryodhana, the best of the strong, a great chariot fighter,
remembering the terrible killing of his army before and of his brothers in
battle, spoke to Bharadvaja's son. 'Teacher, faultless one, you always wish me
well. Relying on you and on Bhishma the grandfather, we would hope to conquer
even the gods in battle, no doubt. How much more the sons of Pandu, who are
lacking in strength and courage?'

"So addressed by your son, sir, Drona broke into the Pandava army while Satyaki
watched. Then Satyaki held Drona off, Bharata, and a tumultuous battle broke out
that made the hair stand on end. The mighty son of Bharadvaja, angry, as if
laughing, pierced the Shaineya at the collarbone with sharp arrows in battle.
Then Bhimasena, angry, pierced Bharadvaja's son, protecting Satyaki, king, from
Drona, the best of those who bear weapons. Then Drona, Bhishma and Shalya, sir,
in anger covered Bhimasena with arrows in battle. There Abhimanyu in anger, and
the sons of Draupadi, sir, pierced all of them with sharp arrows as they raised
their weapons.

"As the very strong Bhishma and Drona came on in anger, the great archer
Shikhandi went out against them in that great battle. Taking up his bow, which
sounded like a cloud, the hero drew it with strength and quickly rained arrows,
covering the sun. When the grandfather of the Bharatas came up against Shikhandi
in battle, he avoided him, remembering that he had been a woman. Then, great
king, Drona ran at him in battle, guarding Bhishma, urged on by your son.
Shikhandi, coming up against Drona, the best of those who bear weapons, avoided
him in battle, as one avoids the fierce fire at the end of an age. Then your
son, lord of the people, came with a great force and guarded Bhishma, seeking
great glory. So too the Pandavas, king, put Dhananjaya in front and went against
Bhishma alone, their minds firmly set on victory. That battle was terrible, like
that of the gods with the danavas, as they always longed for victory and the
most wonderful glory.

---

"Then Bhishma son of Shantanu fought a tumultuous battle, wanting to save your
sons from fear of Bhimasena. That very fierce battle of the kings went on in the
morning, of the Kurus and the Pandavas, destroying the chief heroes. In that
confused battle, full of great danger, a tumultuous noise arose that touched the
great sky. It grew tumultuous with great elephants trumpeting, horses neighing,
and the sound of drums and conches. Bold and very strong, eager to fight for
victory, they roared at one another like great bulls in cow pens.

"As heads were brought down in battle by sharp arrows, it was like a rain of
stones in the sky, bull of the Bharatas. Heads were seen fallen, bull of the
Bharatas, wearing earrings and turbans, bright with gold. The earth was covered
with limbs torn by arrows, with arms still holding bows, and with others, hands
with their ornaments. In a moment, lord of the earth, the whole earth was strewn
with limbs in armour, with adorned hands, with fine faces like the moon with
red-cornered eyes, and with every limb of elephants, horses and men. With
tumultuous clouds of dust lit by the lightning of weapons, the clash of arms was
like thunder. That tumultuous, bitter clash, with blood for water, went on
between the Kurus and the Pandavas, Bharata.

"In that terrible, tumultuous, very dangerous battle that made the hair stand on
end, kshatriyas mad for battle rained showers of arrows. Elephants, yours and
the enemy's, scorched by the showers of arrows, screamed in the battle, best of
the Bharatas. Horses whose riders were killed ran in the ten directions. Some
warriors, yours and the enemy's, hurt by the blows of arrows, leapt up and fell,
bull of the Bharatas. Heaps of horses, elephants and chariots that had
overturned were seen here and there, lord of the people. Driven by Time,
kshatriyas killed one another there with clubs, swords, spears and arrows with
bent joints. Other heroes, skilled in wrestling, grappled in battle in many ways
with arms like iron bars. Your heroes and the Pandavas struck one another with
fists, with knees and with palms, lord of the people. Chariot fighters who had
lost their chariots, holding fine swords, ran at one another, each wanting to
kill the other.

"Then King Duryodhana, surrounded by many Kalingas, put Bhishma in front and
went against the Pandavas in battle. So too all the Pandavas surrounded
Wolf-belly and ran at Bhishma in anger in the battle, their mounts at full
speed."

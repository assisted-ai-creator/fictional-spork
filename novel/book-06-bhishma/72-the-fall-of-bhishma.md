# The Fall of Bhishma

"So all the Pandavas, with Shikhandi in front," said Sanjaya, "surrounded
Bhishma in battle and pierced him. All the Srinjayas together struck Bhishma in
battle with very terrible shataghnis, with axes and lances, and with hammers,
pestles, spears and slings on every side. They struck him with gold-feathered
arrows, darts, javelins and kampanas, and with iron arrows, calf-toothed arrows
and bhushundis, Bharata. His armour split, pressed hard by many, Ganga's son did
not falter, though his vital points were being pierced. To the enemy Bhishma
became like the fire at the end of an age. Blazing arrows and his bow were his
flames, the spreading of his weapons his wind, the rumble of his chariot rims
his roar, the rising of his great weapons his fire. His bright bow was his great
blaze, the destruction of heroes his great fuel. Breaking out from among the
crowds of chariots, he was seen moving again in the middle of the kings.

"Then, passing the king of the Panchalas and Dhrishtaketu, he reached the middle
of the Pandava army at speed. Then he struck these six, Satyaki and Bhima,
Dhananjaya the Pandava, Drupada and Virata, and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's
grandson, with six arrows like the sun, of terrible sound and great speed,
splitting enemy elephants. The great chariot fighters warded off his sharp
arrows and pressed Bhishma with force, with ten arrows each. The gold-feathered,
stone-whetted arrows Shikhandi loosed at him of the great vow in battle quickly
went into Bhishma. Then the diademed one in fury went against Bhishma himself,
with Shikhandi in front, and cut his bow.

"The great chariot fighters could not bear the cutting of Bhishma's bow. Drona,
Kritavarma, Jayadratha of Sindhu, Bhurishravas, Shala, Shalya and Bhagadatta,
these seven, in the greatest anger, ran at the diademed one. Showing supreme
divine weapons, the great chariot fighters fell on him in great anger and
covered the Pandavas. The sound of them falling on Phalguna was heard like the
sound of the oceans risen at the destruction of an age. 'Kill! Bring him! Seize
him! Fight! Cut him down!' Such was the tumultuous noise round Phalguna's
chariot. The great chariot fighters of the Pandavas heard that tumultuous noise.
They ran up, bull of the Bharatas, wanting to save Phalguna: Satyaki, Bhimasena,
Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, Virata and Drupada, the rakshasa
Ghatotkacha and Abhimanyu in anger. These seven, wild with anger, ran up
quickly, holding bright bows. Their battle was tumultuous and made the hair
stand on end, best of the Bharatas, like that of the gods with the danavas.

"Shikhandi, the best of chariot fighters, protected by the diademed one, pierced
Bhishma, his bow cut, with ten arrows in battle. He struck his charioteer with
ten and cut his banner with one. Ganga's son took another bow of greater speed,
and Phalguna cut that too with three sharp broad arrows. So the Pandava, the
left-handed archer, scorcher of enemies, in anger cut Bhishma's bow again and
again as soon as he took it. His bow cut, in anger, licking the corners of his
mouth, he took up a spear that could split even mountains. In anger he hurled it
at Phalguna's chariot. Seeing it come like a blazing thunderbolt, the joy of the
Pandavas took up five sharp broad arrows. With five arrows, in anger, best of
the Bharatas, he cut into five pieces that spear driven by the strength of
Bhishma's arm. Cut by the angry diademed one, it fell like lightning broken from
a mass of clouds.

"Seeing the spear cut, Bhishma was filled with anger. The hero, conqueror of
enemy cities, thought in battle with his understanding. 'With one bow I could
kill all the Pandavas, if the very strong Vishvaksena were not their protector.
For two reasons I will not fight the Pandavas: because the sons of Pandu must
not be killed, and because Shikhandi was a woman. Long ago, when he married
Kali, my father, pleased, gave me death at my own choosing, and not to be killed
in battle. So I think the time for my death has come.'

"Knowing that this was the resolve of Bhishma of measureless splendour, the
seers and the Vasus, standing in the sky, said to Bhishma, 'What you have
resolved, hero, is very dear to us. Do it, great archer. Turn your mind from
battle.' At the end of their words a kind wind blew, fragrant, in the right
direction, with drops of water. The drums of the gods sounded with a great
noise, and a rain of flowers fell on Bhishma, king. No one heard them speaking,
king, except strong-armed Bhishma and me, through the power of the sage. There
was great alarm among the thirty gods, lord of the people, that Bhishma, dear to
all the worlds, would fall from his chariot. Having heard those words of the
hosts of gods, the great-minded Bhishma son of Shantanu did not go against
Bibhatsu, though he was being split by sharp arrows that split every kind of
armour.

"Shikhandi, great king, in anger struck the grandfather of the Bharatas in the
chest with nine sharp arrows. Struck by him in battle, Bhishma, grandfather of
the Kurus, did not tremble, great king, like a mountain in an earthquake. Then
Bibhatsu laughed and, drawing his bow Gandiva, fixed twenty-five small arrows in
Ganga's son. Again, hurrying, Dhananjaya in anger struck him with a hundred
arrows in all his limbs and all his vital points. Pierced hard like that by
others too in the great battle, those gold-feathered, stone-whetted arrows did
not give him pain. Then the diademed one in fury went against Bhishma himself,
with Shikhandi in front, and cut his bow. Then he pierced him with ten arrows,
cut his banner with one, and shook his charioteer with ten arrows. Ganga's son
took another, stronger bow. Arjuna knocked that away too, in three pieces with
three sharp broad arrows, in the space of a blink, as soon as he took it. So he
cut very many bows of his in battle. Then Bhishma son of Shantanu did not go
against Bibhatsu.

"Then he struck him with twenty-five small arrows. Badly pierced, the great
archer said to Duhshasana, 'This angry Partha, the great chariot fighter of the
Pandavas, shoots at me alone in battle with many thousands of arrows. Not even
the wielder of the thunderbolt could conquer him in battle. And all the heroes
together, gods, danavas and rakshasas, could not conquer me, let alone mortals,
very weak.'

"While the two were talking like this, Phalguna, with Shikhandi in front,
pierced Bhishma in battle with sharp arrows. Then, badly pierced by the sharp
arrows of Gandiva's bowman, he spoke again to Duhshasana, smiling. 'These
sharp-pointed arrows driven into me, with a touch like the thunderbolt and
lightning, loosed in an unbroken line, are not Shikhandi's arrows. Cutting my
vital points, splitting firm armour, striking me like pestles, these are not
Shikhandi's arrows. With a touch like Brahma's staff, with the force of the
thunderbolt, hard to withstand, breaking my life, these are not Shikhandi's
arrows. Like angry snakes, licking, swollen with poison, going into my vital
points, these are not Shikhandi's arrows. As if destroying my life like Yama's
messengers sent against me, with a touch like a club or an iron bar, these are
not Shikhandi's arrows. They cut my limbs as the month of Magha cuts the cattle.
These are Arjuna's arrows. These are not Shikhandi's arrows. All the other kings
together could not cause me pain, except the hero Jishnu with Gandiva and the
monkey banner.'

"Saying this, Shantanu's son, as if wanting to burn the Pandava, hurled a spear
with a blazing point and sparks, Bharata. While all the Kuru heroes watched
there, Bharata, he cut it with arrows and brought it down in three pieces with
three. Then Ganga's son took a shield adorned with gold and a sword, wanting
either death first or victory. But before he could get down from the chariot, he
broke that shield of his into a hundred pieces, though he was in armour, and it
was a wonder. Then he roared aloud like a lion and urged on his own ranks: 'Run
at the son of Ganga! Let there be not the least fear among you!'

"Then they ran at that one man on every side with lances, spears and floods of
arrows, with axes and swords and weapons of many kinds, with calf-toothed arrows
and broad arrows. A terrible lion roar of the Pandavas arose. So too your sons,
king, wanting Bhishma's victory, went against that one man and roared lion
roars. There was a tumultuous battle there between yours and the enemy on the
tenth day, king of kings, at the meeting of Bhishma and Arjuna. For a moment, as
the armies fought and killed one another, there was a whirlpool like that of the
Ganga meeting the ocean. The earth, smeared with blood, became impassable. Level
and uneven could not be told apart at all. Having killed ten thousand warriors
on that tenth day, Bhishma stood in battle while his vital points were being
split.

"Then Partha Dhananjaya, standing at the head of the army, drove the army
through the middle of the Kuru armies. We, afraid of the Kaunteya Dhananjaya
with the white horses, pressed by sharp weapons, ran from the great battle.
Twelve peoples did not leave Bhishma in battle as he fought the diademed one,
though hurt by arrows and pained by wounds. They were the Sauviras, Kitavas,
easterners, westerners, northerners and Malavas, the Abhishahas, Shurasenas,
Shibis and Vasatis, the Shalvashrayas, the Trigartas and the Ambashthas with the
Kekayas. Then many surrounded that one man on every side, drove off all the
Kurus and covered him with showers of arrows. 'Bring him down! Seize him! Pierce
him! Drag him!' Such was the tumultuous noise round Bhishma's chariot, king.
Having struck him with floods of arrows in hundreds and thousands, there was not
a finger's breadth on his limbs that was not pierced.

---

"So, lord, your father was cut to pieces by Phalguna's sharp-pointed arrows in
battle. When a little of the day was left, while your sons watched, he fell from
his chariot with his head to the east. As Bhishma fell from his chariot there
was a very great cry of 'Alas! Alas!' from the gods in heaven and from the kings
on every side. Seeing the great-souled grandfather falling, all our hearts fell
with Bhishma. The strong-armed one fell, making the earth resound, like Indra's
banner thrown down, the banner of all bowmen. Covered with crowds of arrows, he
did not touch the ground.

"As the great archer, the bull among men, lay on a bed of arrows, fallen from
his chariot, a divine state came over him. Parjanya rained, the earth trembled,
and as he fell he saw the sun dimmed. The hero came to his senses, thinking of
the time, Bharata. He heard a divine voice in the sky on every side: 'The
great-souled son of Ganga is the best of all who bear weapons, the tiger among
men. How can he make his death while the sun is on its southern course?' Hearing
that, the son of Ganga said, 'I am still here.' Though he had fallen to the
ground, Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, kept his life, wanting the northern
course of the sun.

"Knowing his mind, Ganga, Himavat's daughter, sent great seers there in the form
of swans. Then the swans, flying, hurrying, living on Lake Manasa, came together
to see Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, where the best of men, the
grandfather, lay on his bed of arrows. Coming to Bhishma, the sages in the form
of swans saw Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, lying on his bed of arrows.
Seeing the great-souled son of Ganga, best of the Bharatas, they walked round
him, keeping him on their right. Then the wise ones saw the sun on its southern
course and said to one another, 'Bhishma, great-souled as he is, will be one who
dies in the southern course.'

"Having said this, the swans set off towards the southern quarter. Seeing them,
the very wise son of Shantanu thought, Bharata, and said to them, 'I will not go
at all while the sun is turned to the south. This is settled in my mind. I will
go to my own place, which was mine of old, when the sun has turned to the north,
swans. I tell you the truth. I will keep my life, longing for the northern
course. For I am master of the giving up of my life, since I am in control of my
life. So I will keep my life, wishing to die in the northern course. The boon my
great-souled father gave me, that my death would be at my own choosing, let that
boon of his be so. So I will keep my life, since the giving up of it is in my
control.' Having said this to the swans then, he lay on his bed of arrows.

"When Bhishma of great power, the peak of the Kurus, had fallen like that, the
Pandavas and the Srinjayas roared lion roars. When that great being, by no means
the least of the Bharatas, was killed, your sons, bull of the Bharatas, did not
know what to do. A tumultuous bewilderment came over the Kurus then. The kings
led by Duryodhana sighed and wept. In their despair they stood for a long time
with their senses gone. They brooded, great king, and did not set their minds on
battle. As if seized by the thighs, they did not run at the Pandavas. When
Bhishma of great power, the son of Shantanu, who could not be killed, was
killed, a very great disaster came on the Kurus, king, unwearied. Our best
heroes killed, cut by sharp arrows, beaten by the left-handed archer, we did not
know what we should do.

"But the Pandavas, having won victory, and the highest state hereafter, all blew
their great conches, heroes with arms like iron bars, and the Somakas with the
Panchalas rejoiced, lord of men. Then, as thousands of trumpets sounded, the
very strong Bhimasena slapped his arms hard and danced. When Ganga's son had
been brought down, the heroes of both armies laid down their weapons and brooded
on every side. Some cried out, others fell, others fainted. Some blamed the
kshatriya's way, and some honoured Bhishma. The seers and the ancestors praised
him of the great vow, and the earlier Bharatas praised him too. Taking up the
yoga of the great Upanishad, the mighty and wise son of Shantanu, murmuring
prayers, remained, waiting for his time."

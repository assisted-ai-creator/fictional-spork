# The Way of Action

"'If you think understanding is better than action, Janardana,' said Arjuna,
'why do you urge me to this terrible deed, Keshava? You seem to confuse my
understanding with mixed words. Tell me the one thing, for certain, by which I
may reach what is best.'

"'In this world, sinless one,' said the Blessed One, 'I taught long ago that
there are two ways to stand: by the yoga of knowledge for the followers of
Samkhya, and by the yoga of action for the yogins. A man does not reach freedom
from action by not starting actions, nor does he reach perfection by renouncing
alone. For no one ever stays even for a moment without acting. Everyone is made
to act, helplessly, by the qualities born of nature. One who holds back his
organs of action but sits remembering the objects of the senses in his mind is
confused in himself and is called a hypocrite. But one who controls his senses
with his mind, Arjuna, and without attachment begins the yoga of action with his
organs of action, is better. Do the work that is laid down for you, for action
is better than inaction. Without action you could not even keep your body going.

"'This world is bound by action, except for action done for sacrifice. Do your
work for that, son of Kunti, free of attachment. Long ago Prajapati created
creatures together with sacrifice and said, "By this you shall bring forth. Let
this be your cow that gives all you desire. By this make the gods flourish, and
let the gods make you flourish. Making one another flourish, you will reach the
highest good. For the gods, made to flourish by sacrifice, will give you the
pleasures you desire." One who enjoys what they give without giving to them is
simply a thief. The good, who eat what is left of the sacrifice, are freed from
all faults. But the wicked who cook for their own sake eat sin. Beings come from
food, food comes from rain, rain comes from sacrifice, and sacrifice is born of
action. Know that action comes from Brahman, and Brahman comes from the
imperishable. So the all-pervading Brahman always stands in sacrifice. One who
does not keep turning the wheel that has been set turning like this lives in
sin, Partha, delighting in the senses, and lives in vain.

"'But a man who delights in the self alone, is satisfied in the self and is
content in the self alone has nothing he must do. He has no purpose here in what
is done or in what is not done, and he depends on no being for any purpose. So
always do the work that must be done, without attachment. For a man who does his
work without attachment reaches the highest. By action alone Janaka and the
others reached perfection. You too should act, looking only to the holding
together of the world. Whatever the best man does, others do too. Whatever
standard he sets, the world follows.

"'There is nothing in the three worlds, Partha, that I must do, nothing I have
not gained that I need to gain, and yet I go on acting. For if I did not go on
acting, never resting, men would follow my path in every way, Partha. These
worlds would fall into ruin if I did not act. I would be the maker of confusion
and would destroy these creatures. As the ignorant act, attached to action,
Bharata, so the wise should act without attachment, wanting to hold the world
together. He should not split the understanding of the ignorant who are attached
to action. The wise man, acting with discipline, should make them enjoy all
actions.

"'All actions are done in every way by the qualities of nature. One whose self
is confused by self-regard thinks, "I am the doer." But one who knows the truth
about the division of the qualities and their actions, strong-armed one, thinks,
"The qualities act on the qualities," and does not cling. Those confused by the
qualities of nature cling to the actions of the qualities. One who knows the
whole should not unsettle the slow, who do not know the whole. Renouncing all
actions in me, with your mind on the self, without hope and without "mine",
fight, free of your fever. Men who always follow this teaching of mine, with
faith and without finding fault, are freed from actions too. But know that those
who find fault with it and do not follow my teaching, confused in all knowledge,
are lost and without sense.

"'Even a man of knowledge acts according to his own nature. Beings follow
nature. What will holding back do? Passion and hatred lie in wait in each sense
for its object. One should not come under their power, for they are his enemies
on the road. One's own dharma, even without merit, is better than another's
dharma well done. Death in one's own dharma is better. Another's dharma brings
danger.'

"'Then what drives a man to do evil, Varshneya,' said Arjuna, 'even against his
will, as if forced?'

"'It is desire, it is anger, born of the quality of passion,' said the Blessed
One, 'all-devouring, very evil. Know that this is the enemy here. As fire is
covered by smoke, a mirror by dust, and an embryo by the womb, so this world is
covered by it. Knowledge is covered by this eternal enemy of the man of
knowledge, son of Kunti, in the form of desire, a fire hard to fill. The senses,
the mind and the understanding are said to be its seat. Through them it confuses
the embodied one, covering his knowledge. So, bull of the Bharatas, first
control your senses, and kill this evil thing that destroys knowledge and
discernment. They say the senses are high. Higher than the senses is the mind,
higher than the mind is the understanding, and what is higher than the
understanding is he. So, knowing what is higher than the understanding,
steadying the self by the self, kill the enemy in the form of desire,
strong-armed one, so hard to reach.'"

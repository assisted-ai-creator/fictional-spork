# Renouncing Action

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'you praise renouncing actions, and then yoga. Tell me
for certain which one of the two is better.'

"'Renouncing and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good,' said the
Blessed One. 'But of the two, the yoga of action is better than renouncing
action. Know him as one who always renounces, who neither hates nor desires.
Free of the pairs of opposites, strong-armed one, he is easily freed from
bondage. Children, not the learned, speak of Samkhya and yoga as different. One
who stands rightly in even one of them finds the fruit of both. The place
reached by the followers of Samkhya is reached by the yogins too. One who sees
Samkhya and yoga as one, sees. But renouncing is hard to reach without yoga,
strong-armed one. The sage disciplined in yoga soon reaches Brahman.

"'Disciplined in yoga, his self purified, his self conquered, his senses
conquered, his self become the self of all beings, he is not stained even when
he acts. The disciplined man who knows the truth should think, "I do nothing at
all." So he thinks while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking,
sleeping and breathing, talking, letting go, taking, opening and closing his
eyes, holding that the senses move among their objects. One who acts, placing
his actions in Brahman and giving up attachment, is not stained by evil, as a
lotus leaf is not wetted by water. Yogins do their work with body, mind and
understanding, even with the senses alone, giving up attachment, to purify the
self. The disciplined man, giving up the fruit of action, reaches lasting peace.
The undisciplined man, acting from desire, attached to the fruit, is bound.

"'Renouncing all actions with the mind, the embodied one sits happily in control
in the city of nine gates, neither acting nor causing to act. The lord does not
create agency for the world, or actions, or the joining of action to its fruit.
It is nature that moves. The all-pervading one does not take on anyone's evil or
good deed. Knowledge is covered by ignorance, and so creatures are confused. But
for those whose ignorance is destroyed by knowledge of the self, their knowledge
lights up that highest, like the sun. With their understanding on that, their
self in that, standing in that, intent on that, their faults shaken off by
knowledge, they go where there is no return.

"'The wise see the same in a brahmin rich in learning and good manners, in a
cow, an elephant, a dog, and an outcaste who cooks dogs. Even here, creation is
conquered by those whose mind stands in sameness. For Brahman is without fault
and the same, so they stand in Brahman. One who knows Brahman and stands in
Brahman, steady in understanding and not confused, should not rejoice when he
meets what he likes, nor be troubled when he meets what he dislikes. One whose
self is not attached to outer contacts finds the happiness that is in the self.
Joined to Brahman by yoga, he enjoys undying happiness. For the pleasures born
of contact are wombs of pain. They have a beginning and an end, son of Kunti,
and the wise man does not delight in them. One who can bear here, before he is
freed from the body, the force born of desire and anger, is disciplined and is a
happy man.

"'One whose happiness is within, whose delight is within, whose light is within,
that yogin becomes Brahman and reaches the peace of Brahman. Seers whose faults
are destroyed, whose doubts are cut, who are self-controlled and delight in the
good of all beings, reach the peace of Brahman. For ascetics free of desire and
anger, their thought controlled, who know the self, the peace of Brahman is
close at hand. He shuts out outer contacts and fixes his gaze between the brows.
He makes the in-breath and the out-breath even as they move within the nose, and
controls senses, mind and understanding. The sage intent on release, from whom
desire, fear and anger are gone, is truly free for ever. Knowing me as the
enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities, the great lord of all the worlds, the
friend of all beings, he finds peace.'"

# Bhishma Has Fallen

Then wise Sanjaya, son of Gavalgana, came back from the battle. He saw
everything with his own eyes and knew what had been, what was and what would be.
He came at once and in grief to Dhritarashtra as he brooded, and told him that
Bhishma, the foremost of the Bharatas, had been killed.

"I am Sanjaya, great king. Homage to you, bull of the Bharatas. Bhishma,
Shantanu's son, the grandfather of the Bharatas, has been killed. The peak of
all fighters, the home of all archers, the grandfather of the Kurus lies today
on a bed of arrows. Bhishma, relying on whose courage your son played the dice,
lies killed in battle, king, by Shikhandi. With a single chariot that great
chariot fighter beat all the kings of the earth gathered in a great battle at
the city of Kashi. The son of the Vasus fought Rama son of Jamadagni in battle
and was not killed by Jamadagni's son. He has been killed today by Shikhandi. He
was like great Indra in courage, like Himavat in steadiness, like the ocean in
depth and the earth in patience. Unassailable, with arrows for fangs, a bow for
a mouth and a sword for a tongue, a lion among men, your father has been brought
down today by the Panchala prince.

"When the great army of the Pandavas saw him rise up in battle, it trembled,
struck with fear, like a herd of cattle seeing a lion. He guarded your army for
ten nights, that destroyer of armies, and then he set like the sun, having done
a very hard deed. Unshakeable like Shakra, raining arrows by the thousand, he
killed a hundred million fighters in ten days in battle. He lies groaning on the
ground like a tree broken by the wind, because of your bad counsel, king, as he
did not deserve, Bharata."

---

"How was Bhishma, the bull of the Kurus, killed by Shikhandi?" said
Dhritarashtra. "How did my father, who was like Vasava, fall from his chariot?
How were my sons, Sanjaya, without Bhishma, the mighty one like a god, who lived
chaste for his father's sake? When that great-spirited one was killed, the great
archer, the mighty, great chariot fighter, the tiger among men, what was their
mind then? The greatest pain comes over me, since you tell me he is killed, the
hero, the bull of the Kurus, the unshakeable bull among men.

"Who followed him as he went, and who went before him? Who stood, who turned
back, who went forward, Sanjaya? Which heroes followed close behind that tiger
among chariot fighters, the unfallen bull of the kshatriyas, as he plunged
suddenly into the ranks of chariots? He drove off the enemy army as the sun
drives off darkness, the slayer of enemies, like the thousand-rayed sun, putting
fear into the enemy, and he did a hard deed in battle at the Kaurava's command.
How did the Pandavas hold back Shantanu's son in battle, as he swallowed the
ranks, skilled, hard to withstand, coming on rightly and near? He cut down the
ranks, swift, with arrows for fangs, terrible, with his bow for a gaping mouth
and his sword for a tongue, hard to approach. How did Kunti's son bring him down
in battle, the unconquered, modest one who surpassed other tigers among men?

"He was a fierce archer with fierce arrows, standing on the finest chariot,
gathering the heads of the enemy with sharp arrows. When the great army of the
Pandavas saw him rise up in battle, hard to withstand like the fire of time, it
always shrank. He dragged my army about for ten nights, that destroyer of
armies, and then he set like the sun, having done a very hard deed. Like Shakra,
pouring out an endless rain of arrows, he killed a hundred million fighters in
ten days in battle. He lies groaning on the ground like a tree broken by the
wind, because of my bad counsel, as he did not deserve, the Bharata.

"How could the Pandavas' army, seeing Shantanu's son there, strike at Bhishma of
terrible courage? How did Pandu's sons fight a battle with Bhishma? And how did
Bhishma not win, while Drona lived, Sanjaya? With Kripa near, and Bharadvaja's
son too, how did Bhishma, the best of fighters, meet his death? And how was the
atiratha Bhishma, whom even the gods could hardly withstand, killed in battle by
Shikhandi the Panchala prince? He always rivalled mighty Jamadagni's son in
battle, unconquered by Jamadagni's son, equal to Shakra in courage. Tell me,
Sanjaya, of that hero killed in battle, Bhishma, worthy of the strength of great
chariot fighters, without whom we know no peace.

"Which of my great archers did not leave the unfallen one, Sanjaya? Which heroes
surrounded him at Duryodhana's command? When all the Pandavas, with Shikhandi at
their head, attacked Bhishma, surely the Kurus did not leave the unfallen one in
fear, Sanjaya? He was a great cloud raised high, thundering with the twang of
his bowstring, his arrows its drops, the twang of his bow its great roar. He
rained on the sons of Kunti, the Panchalas and the Srinjayas, killing enemy
chariot fighters as the thunderbolt-bearer kills the danavas. He was a terrible
ocean of arrows and weapons, hard to approach, its crocodiles the arrows, its
waves the bows. It was endless, without an island, without a boat in battle,
with clubs and swords for its sea monsters and whirlpools, its horses for its
crocodiles, full of elephants. He sank horses, elephants, foot soldiers and
chariots in battle, many of them, swiftly, carrying off enemy heroes, burning
with anger and splendour, the scorcher of enemies. Which heroes held him in, as
the shore holds the home of sea monsters?

"When Bhishma, the slayer of enemies, did his deed in battle for Duryodhana's
good, Sanjaya, who were before him then? Who guarded the right wheel of Bhishma
of boundless splendour? Which heroes of firm vows held back the enemy from
behind? Who stood in front, guarding Bhishma close? Which heroes guarded the
left wheel of the hero as he fought? Standing at the left wheel, who killed the
Srinjayas, Sanjaya? Who guarded the unassailable one at the head of the ranks?
Who went at his sides, going the hard road? Which heroes fought the enemy heroes
in the crush, Sanjaya? Guarded by heroes, and guarding them, how did they not
swiftly conquer in battle the ranks that were hard to conquer? How could the
Pandavas, Sanjaya, even strike at him, who was like the lord of all the worlds,
the supreme Prajapati?

"You tell me, Sanjaya, that Bhishma, that tiger among men, is sunk, the island
the Kurus relied on as they fought the enemy. How was he killed by the enemy, on
whose courage my son, very strong, relied and thought nothing of the Pandavas?
My father, of great vows, fierce in war, was wanted long ago as an ally by the
gods with Indra when they were killing the danavas. When he was born, of great
courage, the joy of the world, Shantanu gave up sorrow, grief and misery at the
beauty of his son. How can you tell me he is killed, wise, the final refuge,
knowing it, devoted to true dharma, pure, knowing the truth of the Vedas and
their limbs? When I hear that Shantanu's son is killed, trained in every weapon,
self-controlled, calm and high-minded, I think the rest of the army is killed.

"I think adharma has become stronger than dharma, since the Pandavas want the
kingdom by killing their old teacher. Long ago Rama son of Jamadagni,
unsurpassed, knowing every weapon, ready for battle for Amba's sake, was beaten
in battle by Bhishma. You tell me Bhishma is killed, whose deeds were equal to
Indra's, the peak of all archers. What grief could be greater than this? He beat
the hosts of kshatriyas again and again in battle, and Rama son of Jamadagni
too, that slayer of enemy heroes. Surely, then, Shikhandi son of Drupada is
greater in energy, courage and strength than the mighty Bhargava, fierce in war,
since he killed the hero, skilled and brave in battle, versed in every science,
knowing the highest weapons, the bull of the Bharatas.

"Which heroes followed that slayer of enemies in the assembly of enemies? Tell
me how Bhishma's battle with the Pandavas went. My son's army, Sanjaya, is like
a woman whose hero is killed. My army is like a herd of cattle without a
herdsman, running wild. When he, whose manhood was the greatest in the whole
world, was overthrown in the great battle, what was your mind then? What
strength have we left to live, Sanjaya, now that our very mighty father, the
most dharmic in the world, has been killed? I think my sons grieve deeply now
that Bhishma is killed, like men who want to cross over and see their boat sunk
in deep water. Surely my heart is made of the essence of rock, very hard, since
it does not break when I hear that Bhishma, that tiger among men, is killed. How
was he killed in battle, the bull of the Bharatas, unassailable, in whom were
weapons, wisdom and policy beyond measure?

"No one is freed from death by weapons, courage, austerity, wisdom, steadiness
or renunciation. Surely time is very mighty, and no one in all the world can
pass beyond it, since you tell me, Sanjaya, that Bhishma son of Shantanu is
killed. Burning with grief for my sons, not thinking of great sorrow, I hoped
once for protection from Bhishma, Shantanu's joy. When Duryodhana saw Shantanu's
son fallen on the ground like the sun, Sanjaya, what did he do then? Thinking it
over with my understanding, Sanjaya, I see nothing left of the kings, of ours or
of the enemy, in the opposing ranks.

"Cruel is this dharma of the kshatriyas that the seers have shown, since the
Pandavas want the kingdom by killing Shantanu's son, or we want the kingdom by
having the grandfather killed. The Parthas keep to the dharma of the kshatriyas,
and my sons do not offend. This is what a noble man must do in hard troubles,
Sanjaya: show courage with all his power, and that was in him. How did Pandu's
sons bring down Shantanu's son, my child, modest and unconquered, as he killed
the ranks? How were the ranks yoked, how did the battle go with those great men,
and how was my father Bhishma killed by the enemy, Sanjaya? What did Duryodhana
and Karna, Shakuni son of Subala and the gambler Duhshasana say when Bhishma was
killed?

"The fools, the gamblers, entered the hall of battle, hard to approach, strewn
with the bodies of men, elephants and horses, terrible, whose dice were arrows,
spears, clubs, swords and lances. Which bulls among men played there in that
fearful game of lives? Who won there, and who lost, their marks struck and
brought down, others besides Bhishma son of Shantanu? Tell me that, Sanjaya. I
have no peace here, now that I have heard Devavrata is killed in battle, my
father of terrible deeds. Grief has come over me. You, Sanjaya, pour clarified
butter on the great pain grown in my heart, caused by my son, as on a fire, and
make it blaze. I think my sons grieve, seeing Bhishma killed, who had lifted a
great, famous burden known to the whole world. I will hear those sorrows caused
by Duryodhana. So tell me everything that happened there, Sanjaya. Tell me what
happened in the battle of the kings, born of the fool's folly, whether
ill-judged or well-judged. And tell me in full all that Bhishma did in the
battle, wanting victory, full of energy, master of weapons. Tell me how the
battle between the Kuru and Pandava armies went, in what order, at what time,
and what happened and how."

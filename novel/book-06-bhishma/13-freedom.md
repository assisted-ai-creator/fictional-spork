summary: The last three chapters of the Bhagavad Gita, told simply, with footnotes. Krishna describes the godlike and the demonic natures, and names desire, anger and greed as the three gates of hell; he explains the three kinds of faith, food, sacrifice, austerity and giving, and the words Om Tat Sat; he explains renunciation and relinquishing, the causes of action, and the three kinds of knowledge, action, doer, understanding, steadiness and happiness; he describes the duties of the four classes, how a person reaches Brahman, and gives his last word: take refuge in me alone, and I will free you from all evil; Arjuna says his confusion is gone and that he will do as Krishna says; Sanjaya tells Dhritarashtra that where Krishna and Arjuna are, there is fortune and victory.
---

# Freedom

"'Some people are born with the godlike nature, Arjuna,' said Krishna. 'They
have fearlessness, a pure heart, steadiness in the yoga of knowledge, giving,
self-control, sacrifice, study of scripture, austerity and honesty. They have
not harming, truth, freedom from anger, giving up, and peace. They do not speak
ill of others. They are kind to all beings, not greedy, gentle, modest and not
fickle. They have energy, patience, firmness and cleanness, bear no malice and
are not too proud.[^b6c13-1] Showing off, arrogance, too much pride, anger,
harshness and ignorance belong to the one born with the demonic nature, Partha.

"'The godlike nature leads to freedom, and the demonic to bondage. Do not worry,
Arjuna. You were born with the godlike nature.

"'There are two kinds of beings in this world, the godlike and the demonic. I
have told you about the godlike at length. Now hear about the demonic, Partha.
Demonic people do not know when to act and when to hold back. There is no
cleanness in them, no good conduct and no truth. They say the world has no
truth, no foundation and no Lord. They say it came about with no order, from
nothing but desire. What else could it be? Holding on to this view, these lost
souls of little understanding do cruel deeds and rise up as enemies of the
world, to destroy it.

"'They give themselves to desire that can never be filled. Full of showing off,
pride and arrogance, they grab hold of false ideas in their confusion, and go
about with unclean vows. They are gripped by endless worry that lasts until
death. For them, enjoying their desires is the highest thing, and they are sure
that this is all there is. Tied by hundreds of cords of hope, given over to
desire and anger, they try to pile up wealth by wrong means, to enjoy their
desires.

"'"I got this today. I will get that thing I want. This wealth is mine, and that
will be mine too. I have killed that enemy, and I will kill others as well. I am
the lord. I am the one who enjoys. I am successful, strong and happy. I am rich
and well born. Who else is like me? I will sacrifice, I will give, I will enjoy
myself." So they talk, deluded by ignorance.[^b6c13-2] Confused by many
thoughts, caught in the net of delusion, hooked on the enjoyment of desires,
they fall into a foul hell.

"'Full of themselves, stubborn, drunk with wealth and pride, they sacrifice in
name only, for show, and not by the rules. Clinging to the sense of "I", to
force, arrogance, desire and anger, these jealous people hate me, who live in
their own bodies and in the bodies of others. Again and again I throw these
cruel haters, the lowest of men, these evil people, into demonic wombs in the
round of births. Born in demonic wombs, deluded birth after birth, they never
reach me, Arjuna, and they sink to the lowest place.

"'There are three gates to this hell, which destroy the self: desire, anger and
greed. So a person should give up these three.[^b6c13-3] A person freed from
these three gates of darkness, Arjuna, does what is good for himself, and then
he goes to the highest goal. The one who throws away the rules of scripture and
does what he likes reaches neither perfection, nor happiness, nor the highest
goal. So let scripture be your guide in deciding what should and should not be
done. Know what the rules of scripture say, and then do your work
here.'[^b6c13-4]

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'what about people who set aside the rules of scripture
but worship with faith? Where do they stand: in goodness, passion or darkness?'

"'The faith of people with bodies is of three kinds,' said Krishna, 'and it
comes from their own nature: good, passionate or dark. Hear about it. Everyone's
faith matches their nature, Arjuna. A person is made of their faith. Whatever
their faith is, that is what they are.[^b6c13-5] Good people worship the gods.
Passionate people worship yakshas and rakshasas. Dark people worship ghosts and
hordes of spirits. Know that people who practise terrible austerities not laid
down in scripture, full of showing off and the sense of "I", driven by desire,
passion and force, are demonic in their purpose. Mindlessly they torture the
elements in their bodies, and me too, who live within the body.

"'The food each person likes is also of three kinds, and so are sacrifice,
austerity and giving. Hear how they differ.[^b6c13-6] Food that increases life,
goodness, strength, health, happiness and cheerfulness, food that is tasty,
rich, lasting and pleasing, is dear to the good. Food that is bitter, sour,
salty, too hot, sharp, dry and burning is liked by the passionate. It brings
pain, grief and sickness. Food that is stale, tasteless, rotten and left
overnight, leftovers and what is unclean, is dear to the dark.

"'A sacrifice offered by the rules, by people who do not want its fruit, with
the mind fixed on the thought "This should be offered", is good. A sacrifice
offered with a reward in mind, or for show, best of the Bharatas, know that it
is passionate. A sacrifice without the rules, without giving out food, without
the sacred words, without a gift to the priests, and without faith, is called
dark.

"'Honouring the gods, the twice-born, teachers and the wise, cleanness, honesty,
celibacy and not harming: this is called the austerity of the body. Speech that
does not upset anyone, and is true, kind and helpful, is called the austerity of
speech, along with the regular study of scripture.[^b6c13-7] Calmness of mind,
gentleness, silence, self-control and a pure heart: this is called the austerity
of the mind. When people practise this threefold austerity with the highest
faith, not wanting its fruit, and with a steady mind, it is called good.
Austerity done to win respect, honour and worship, and for show, is called
passionate here. It is unsteady and does not last. Austerity done from a foolish
idea, by torturing oneself, or to destroy someone else, is called dark.

"'Some gifts are given because they ought to be given, to someone who cannot
give anything back, at the right place and time, to a worthy person. Such a gift
is called good.[^b6c13-8] A gift given to get something back, or with a reward
in mind, or given unwillingly, is called passionate. A gift given at the wrong
place and time, to unworthy people, without respect and with contempt, is called
dark.

"'"Om Tat Sat" is said to be the threefold name of Brahman.[^b6c13-9] By it the
brahmins, the Vedas and the sacrifices were made long ago. So those who speak of
Brahman always say "Om" when they begin sacrifice, giving and austerity as the
rules lay down. People who want release say "Tat", "That", and do their many
acts of sacrifice, austerity and giving without aiming at the fruit. "Sat" is
used to mean what is real and what is good. And "Sat" is used for a praiseworthy
act, Partha. Being steady in sacrifice, austerity and giving is also called
"Sat", and so is any act done for their sake. But whatever is offered, given,
suffered in austerity or done without faith is called "Asat", Partha. It is no
good either after death or here.'

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'I want to know the truth about renouncing, and about
relinquishing, and the difference between them.'

"'The wise know that renouncing means giving up actions done for desire,' said
Krishna. 'The clear-sighted say that relinquishing means giving up the fruit of
all actions.[^b6c13-10] Some thinkers say that all action should be given up,
because it is flawed. Others say that acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity
should not be given up. Hear my final view on relinquishing, best of the
Bharatas. Relinquishing is said to be of three kinds, tiger among men. Acts of
sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be given up. They should be done, for
sacrifice, giving and austerity make the wise pure. But even these acts should
be done without clinging and without wanting their fruit, Partha. That is my
final and highest view.

"'It is not right to give up a duty that is required. Giving it up out of
confusion is called dark. If a person gives up an action because it is painful,
out of fear of bodily trouble, his relinquishing is passionate, and he does not
get the fruit of relinquishing. When a required action is done because it ought
to be done, Arjuna, without clinging and without wanting its fruit, that
relinquishing is called good. The one who relinquishes like this, filled with
goodness, wise, with his doubts cut away, does not hate unpleasant work and does
not cling to pleasant work.

"'No one with a body can give up action completely. But the one who gives up the
fruit of action is called one who relinquishes. For those who do not relinquish,
the fruit of action after death is of three kinds: unwanted, wanted and mixed.
But for those who renounce, there is none at all.

"'Learn from me, strong-armed one, the five causes of every action, as taught in
the Sankhya. They are the body, the doer, the various instruments, the many
different movements, and fate, the fifth.[^b6c13-11] Whatever action a person
starts with body, speech or mind, right or wrong, these five are its causes. So
a person who sees himself alone as the doer, because his understanding is not
trained, does not see, the fool. But think of the one who has no sense of "I did
it", whose understanding is not stained. Even if he kills these people, he does
not kill, and he is not bound.[^b6c13-12]

"'Knowledge, what is known and the knower are the three things that drive
action. The instrument, the action and the doer are the three parts of an
action. Knowledge, action and doer are also of three kinds, according to the
strands, as the teaching of the strands says. Hear about them too.

"'The knowledge by which a person sees one unchanging being in all beings,
undivided in the divided, is good. The knowledge that sees in all beings many
different things, each separate, is passionate. The knowledge that clings to one
small thing as if it were everything, without reason, missing the truth, and
narrow, is called dark.

"'Required action, done without clinging, without liking or disliking, by
someone who does not want its fruit, is called good. Action done by someone who
wants his desires, or full of "I", with a great deal of strain, is called
passionate. Action begun in confusion, without thinking of the results, the
loss, the harm to others or one's own strength, is called dark.

"'A doer who is free from clinging, who does not talk about himself, who is full
of steadiness and energy, and is not changed by success or failure, is called
good. A doer full of passion, who wants the fruit of his work, greedy, violent,
unclean, swinging between joy and grief, is called passionate. A doer who is
undisciplined, coarse, stubborn, dishonest, spiteful, lazy, gloomy and slow to
act is called dark.

"'Now hear, Arjuna, the three kinds of understanding and of steadiness,
according to the strands, told fully and separately. The understanding that
knows when to act and when to hold back, what should and should not be done,
what is to be feared and what is not, what binds and what frees, is good,
Partha. The understanding by which a person knows dharma and adharma, what
should be done and what should not, but wrongly, is passionate. The
understanding that is covered by darkness and thinks adharma is dharma, and sees
everything backwards, is dark, Partha.

"'The steadiness by which a person holds the workings of the mind, the breath
and the senses firm, with a yoga that never wavers, is good. The steadiness by
which a person holds on to dharma, pleasure and wealth, wanting their fruit as
each comes up, is passionate. The steadiness by which a fool will not let go of
sleep, fear, grief, gloom and pride is dark.

"'Now hear from me the three kinds of happiness. There is a happiness a person
comes to enjoy by practice, in which sorrow comes to an end. It is like poison
at first and like nectar in the end. It comes from the calm of one's own
understanding, and it is called good.[^b6c13-13] The happiness that comes from
the senses meeting their objects, which is like nectar at first and like poison
in the end, is passionate. The happiness that deludes the self at the beginning
and in the end, and comes from sleep, laziness and carelessness, is called dark.

"'There is nothing on earth, or among the gods in heaven, that is free from
these three strands born of nature.

"'The work of brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras is divided according to
the strands that come from their own nature.[^b6c13-14] Calm, self-control,
austerity, cleanness, patience and honesty, knowledge, wisdom and faith are the
work of a brahmin, born of his nature. Courage, energy, steadiness, skill, not
running away in battle, generosity and leadership are the work of a kshatriya,
born of his nature. Farming, looking after cattle and trade are the work of a
vaishya, born of his nature. Work that serves others is the work of a shudra,
born of his nature.

"'A person who is devoted to his own work reaches perfection. Hear how a person
devoted to his own work finds perfection. By worshipping, through his own work,
the one from whom all beings come and by whom all this is filled, a person finds
perfection. It is better to do your own duty, even badly, than to do someone
else's duty well. Doing the work that your own nature lays down, a person does
no wrong. No one should give up the work he was born to, Arjuna, even if it has
faults. For every undertaking is covered by some fault, as fire is covered by
smoke.

"'The one whose understanding clings to nothing, who has conquered himself and
is free from longing, reaches by renouncing the highest perfection of freedom
from action. Hear from me briefly, Arjuna, how the one who has reached
perfection reaches Brahman, the highest state of knowledge. He is joined to a
pure understanding. He controls himself with steadiness, gives up sound and the
other things of the senses, and throws off liking and disliking. He lives in a
quiet place, eats little, and controls speech, body and mind. He is always
devoted to the yoga of meditation, and holds to detachment. He lets go of the
sense of "I", of force, arrogance, desire, anger and possessions. Free from
"mine" and at peace, he is fit to become Brahman.

"'Having become Brahman, calm in himself, he does not grieve and does not long.
He is the same to all beings, and he reaches the highest love for me. By love he
comes to know me truly, how great I am and who I am. And when he knows me truly,
he enters into me at once. Even though he is always doing all kinds of work, if
he takes shelter in me, he reaches by my grace the eternal, unchanging place.

"'In your mind, give all your actions to me. Make me your goal. Hold to the yoga
of understanding, and keep your mind always on me. With your mind on me, you
will cross every difficulty by my grace. But if, out of the sense of "I", you
will not listen, you will be destroyed.

"'If, holding on to the sense of "I", you think, "I will not fight", your
decision is useless. Your own nature will make you fight. Bound by your own
work, which comes from your nature, Arjuna, you will do, even against your will,
what you do not want to do in your confusion. The Lord lives in the heart of all
beings, Arjuna, turning all beings round by his power, as if they were mounted
on a machine.[^b6c13-15] Go to him alone for shelter, with your whole being,
Arjuna. By his grace you will reach the highest peace, the eternal home.

"'So I have told you this knowledge, more secret than any secret. Think it over
fully, and then do as you wish.[^b6c13-16]

"'Hear once more my highest word, the most secret of all. You are very dear to
me, and so I will tell you what is good for you. Fix your mind on me. Love me.
Worship me. Bow down to me. You will come to me. I promise you this truly, for
you are dear to me. Give up all dharmas and come to me alone for shelter. I will
set you free from all evil. Do not grieve.[^b6c13-17]

"'Never tell this to anyone who has no discipline, who has no love, who does not
want to listen, or who finds fault with me. But whoever teaches this highest
secret to those who love me shows the highest love for me, and will surely come
to me. No one among people does anything dearer to me than that, and no one on
earth will be dearer to me. And whoever studies this conversation of ours about
dharma, I think, will be worshipping me with the sacrifice of knowledge. Even a
person who only listens to it, with faith and without finding fault, will be set
free and reach the happy worlds of those who do good.

"'Partha, have you heard this with a focused mind? Has your confusion, born of
ignorance, gone, Dhananjaya?'

"'My confusion is gone,' said Arjuna. 'By your grace, Achyuta, I have remembered
who I am. I stand firm. My doubts are gone. I will do what you say.'[^b6c13-18]"

"So I heard this wonderful conversation," said Sanjaya, "between Vasudeva and
the great Partha, and it made my hair stand on end. By Vyasa's grace I heard
this highest secret, this yoga, told by Krishna, the lord of yoga, himself, in
his own words. King, as I remember this wonderful, holy conversation of Keshava
and Arjuna again and again, I rejoice again and again. And as I remember again
and again that wonderful form of Hari, I am filled with great wonder, king, and
I rejoice again and again. Wherever Krishna, the lord of yoga, is, and wherever
Partha the archer is, there are fortune, victory, well-being and good policy. Of
that I am certain."[^b6c13-19]

## Explanations

[^b6c13-1]: **Godlike and demonic natures.** *What it means:* the Gita's
    "demonic" (*āsura*) does not mean horned monsters. It means a way of living:
    proud, greedy, harsh, showing off. The "godlike" (*daiva*) way is fearless,
    honest, kind and humble. Everyone leans one way or the other by their
    choices. Krishna comforts Arjuna: his nature is the godlike one.

[^b6c13-2]: **"Who else is like me?"** *What it means:* Krishna describes how a
    proud person talks: "I got this, I will get that, I killed my enemy, I am
    the lord, who is like me?" *In the story:* before the war, Duryodhana
    boasted to his father in much the same way: "By me alone the doings of gods
    and asuras are set going … The highest understanding, the highest splendour
    and the highest strength are in me" (CE 5.60.14, 27).

[^b6c13-3]: **The three gates of hell.** *What it means:* desire, anger and
    greed. Krishna calls them "gates", because each one is a way in to ruin.
    Shut all three, and the way to the good is open.

[^b6c13-4]: **Scripture as a guide.** *What it means:* Krishna tells Arjuna not
    to decide right and wrong just by what he feels like, but to take the
    guidance of the sacred teachings. *Everyday example:* a young doctor follows
    the proven rules of medicine instead of guessing, until experience makes
    those rules part of them.

[^b6c13-5]: **"A person is made of their faith."** *What it means:* what you
    truly believe in, and trust, shapes who you become. *Everyday example:* a
    person who believes kindness is weakness becomes hard. A person who trusts
    in goodness grows gentle.

[^b6c13-6]: **Three kinds of food, sacrifice, austerity and giving.** *What it
    means:* the three strands show up even in everyday things. *Everyday
    example:* fresh, wholesome food that gives energy and calm is "good". Very
    spicy, salty or fried food that excites and upsets the body is "passionate".
    Stale, rotten or leftover food that makes you dull is "dark".

[^b6c13-7]: **Austerity of speech.** *What it means:* the discipline of speaking
    words that are true, kind, helpful, and do not upset people. *Everyday
    example:* before you speak, ask: is it true? is it kind? will it help?

[^b6c13-8]: **Three kinds of giving.** *What it means:* the best gift is given
    because it is right, to someone who needs it and cannot pay you back, at the
    right time. A gift given to get something back is "passionate". A gift
    thrown at someone with contempt is "dark". *Everyday example:* helping a
    stranger who will never know your name is the first kind.

[^b6c13-9]: **Om Tat Sat.** *What it means:* three sacred words for the highest
    reality. *Om* is the sacred sound said at the start of rites. *Tat* means
    "That", the highest, beyond names. *Sat* means "real" and "good". Saying
    them reminds a person to act for the highest, not for selfish gain.

[^b6c13-10]: **Renouncing and relinquishing (*saṃnyāsa* and *tyāga*).** *What
    they mean:* two words for "giving up". *Renouncing* is giving up actions
    done for selfish desire. *Relinquishing* is giving up the *fruit* of all
    actions. Krishna says you should not give up good work (sacrifice, giving,
    discipline). You should give up clinging to its results.

[^b6c13-11]: **Fate, the fifth cause.** *What it means:* any action needs five
    things: a body, a doer, tools, effort, and *daiva*, fate or the unseen power
    beyond us. So no one can say "I alone did this." *In the story:* when
    Bhishma was afraid of Amba's austerities, Narada and Vyasa told him, "Who
    can turn back fate by human effort?" (CE 5.187.16–17).

[^b6c13-12]: **"He does not kill, even if he kills."** *What it means:* a hard
    saying. Krishna means that a person who acts without the sense of "I",
    purely as duty, is not tied by the action, even when the duty is as terrible
    as fighting a war. It is not permission to kill. It describes a person who
    has no selfish motive at all.

[^b6c13-13]: **Poison first, nectar later.** *What it means:* true happiness
    often feels hard at the start and sweet at the end. Cheap pleasure feels
    sweet at the start and bitter at the end. *Everyday example:* exercise,
    study or saving money feel like "poison" at first, and bring "nectar" later.
    Overeating is nectar at first and poison later.

[^b6c13-14]: **The duties of the four classes.** *What it means:* Krishna says
    each class's work comes from its nature: the brahmin's is calm, learning and
    faith; the kshatriya's is courage, leadership and not fleeing from battle;
    the vaishya's is farming, cattle and trade; the shudra's is service. The
    Gita's point for Arjuna is that his own nature is a kshatriya's, and that
    doing one's own work well is itself a way to worship God (CE 6.40.46).

[^b6c13-15]: **As if mounted on a machine.** *What it means:* the Lord, inside
    every heart, moves all beings as if they were figures on a turning machine
    or wheel. Arjuna's own nature will make him fight in the end, whatever he
    says now.

[^b6c13-16]: **"Do as you wish."** *What it means:* after all this teaching,
    Krishna does not order Arjuna. He tells him to think it over fully and
    choose. The choice stays Arjuna's.

[^b6c13-17]: **"Give up all dharmas and come to me alone."** *What it means:*
    the Gita's most famous verse, and one of the most debated. In simple words:
    stop worrying over every rule and duty, and trust God completely; he will
    free you from all evil. Some commentators read "give up all dharmas" as
    "give up relying on your own good works"; others as "give up the sense that
    you are the doer"; others as "give up all other paths for devotion". The
    text itself does not say which, so the translation keeps it plain.

[^b6c13-18]: **"I have remembered who I am."** *What it means:* Arjuna's word is
    *smṛti*, "memory". His grief had made him forget himself. Now he remembers
    his true self and his duty. His doubt is gone, and he will fight.

[^b6c13-19]: **Where Krishna and Arjuna are.** *What it means:* Sanjaya ends the
    Gita by telling blind Dhritarashtra, in effect, that the side with Krishna
    and Arjuna must win. *In the story:* before the battle Arjuna told
    Yudhishthira the same thing he had heard from Narada: "Where Krishna is,
    there is victory" (CE 6.21.12–14).

**Divergences.**

* This chapter is Gita chapters 16, 17 and 18, the end of the Gita.
* **The Gita's length.** A rejected line (\*112) says Krishna spoke 620 verses, Arjuna 57, Sanjaya 67 and Dhritarashtra one: 745 in all. The CE Gita has 700 (see ledger L-191, L-192).
* **The Gita's praise of itself.** \*113 ("Let the Gita be well sung; what need of other books?") and \*114 are later verses in praise of the Gita. Appendix I No. 3, after 6.40.78, is a long later text of yoga teachings (the "Gita-sara"). None is in the CE.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*110–\*114 and App. I No. 3.

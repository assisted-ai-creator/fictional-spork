# The Blessings of the Elders

"When they saw Dhananjaya with his arrows and Gandiva," said Sanjaya, "the great
chariot fighters gave a great roar again. The Pandavas, the Somakas and those
who followed them, heroes, blew their sea-born conches joyfully. Then drums,
tabors, saws and cow-horns were struck all at once, and there was a great noise.
Then the gods with the gandharvas, the ancestors, lord of men, and the hosts of
perfected ones and charanas came together, wanting to see. The fortunate seers,
putting Shatakratu at their head, came together to see that great slaughter.

"Then Yudhishthira, seeing the two armies, like oceans, ready for battle and
moving again and again, king, took off his armour, the hero, laid down his fine
weapon, got down quickly from his chariot and went on foot, with joined hands.
Looking at the grandfather, Yudhishthira, the King of Dharma, went in silence
eastward, towards the enemy army. Seeing him go, Kunti's son Dhananjaya got down
quickly from his chariot and followed with his brothers. The blessed Vasudeva
followed behind, and the chief kings followed eagerly.

"'What have you decided, king,' said Arjuna, 'that you leave us and go on foot,
facing east, towards the enemy army?'

"'Where will you go, king of kings,' said Bhimasena, 'with your armour and
weapons laid down, leaving your brothers, lord of the earth, while the enemy
armies are in armour?'

"'When you, my eldest brother, go like this, Bharata,' said Nakula, 'fear
torments my heart. Tell me, where are you going?'

"'When this gathering for battle is here, so very fearful, king,' said Sahadeva,
'and we must fight, where will you go, towards the enemy?'

"Though his brothers spoke to him like this, joy of the Kurus, Yudhishthira said
nothing, keeping silent, and went on. Then the wise, great-minded Vasudeva said
to them, as if smiling, 'I know what he means. He will fight the enemy, this
lord of the earth, after he has asked leave of Bhishma, Drona, Gautama, Shalya
and all his elders. For it is heard that in the old days a man who fights
without asking leave of his elders is surely cursed by those greater than he.
But I think that one who fights his elders after asking their leave as the
science lays down is sure of victory in battle.'

"As Krishna was saying this, there was a great outcry in the army of
Dhritarashtra's son, while others were silent. Seeing Yudhishthira from far off,
the soldiers of Dhritarashtra's son said to one another, 'This disgrace to his
family is a nobody. Clearly that King Yudhishthira is coming near to Bhishma in
fear, with his brothers, begging for refuge. How can the Pandava come in fear
when Dhananjaya is his protector, and Vrikodara the Pandava, and Nakula and
Sahadeva? Surely he was not born in a kshatriya family famous on earth, so
frightened is his heart, this man of little spirit in battle.' Then all those
kshatriyas praised the Kauravas and, glad and happy, waved their cloths. Then
all the fighters there blamed Yudhishthira with his brothers, lord of the
people, and Keshava too. Then, having cried shame on Yudhishthira, the Kaurava
army quickly fell silent again, lord of the people. 'What will the king say?
What will Bhishma answer? What will Bhima, proud in battle, and Krishna and
Arjuna say? What does he mean?' There was great doubt about it in both armies,
king, because of Yudhishthira.

"Plunging into the enemy army, crowded with arrows and spears, he went quickly
to Bhishma, surrounded by his brothers. Then the Pandava king pressed the feet
of Bhishma son of Shantanu with his hands, and said to him as he stood ready for
battle, 'I ask your leave, unassailable one. I will fight with you, my father.
Give me leave, my father, and give me your blessings.'

"'If you had not come to me like this in battle, lord of the earth,' said
Bhishma, 'I would have cursed you to defeat, great king, Bharata. I am pleased,
my son. Fight and win victory, Pandava. Whatever else you wish, may you gain it
in battle. Choose a boon, Partha. What do you want from me? Since this is so,
great king, there will be no defeat for you. A man is the slave of wealth, but
wealth is no one's slave. That is the truth, great king. I am bound to the
Kauravas by wealth. So I speak to you like a eunuch, joy of the Kurus. I have
been bought by wealth, Kauravya. What do you want, other than battle?'

"'Give me counsel, very wise one, always wishing me well,' said Yudhishthira,
'and fight for the Kaurava. That is always my boon.'

"'King, what help can I give you here, joy of the Kurus?' said Bhishma. 'I will
fight for the enemy, as is my choice. Tell me what you want to say.'

"'How can I conquer you in battle, unconquered as you are?' said Yudhishthira.
'Advise me on this, for my good, if you see what is best.'

"'I do not see, son of Kunti,' said Bhishma, 'any man who could conquer me in
battle while I fight, not even Shatakratu himself.'

"'Then I ask you, grandfather, homage to you,' said Yudhishthira. 'Tell me the
way to victory over you in battle by your enemies.'

"'My son, I see no enemy who could conquer me in battle,' said Bhishma. 'The
time of my death has not yet come. Come back again.'

"Then Yudhishthira accepted Bhishma's words with his head, joy of the Kurus,
greeted him again, and the strong-armed one went with his brothers to the
teacher's chariot, through the middle of the whole army as they watched. He
greeted Drona, walked round him keeping him on his right, and spoke words for
his own good to the unassailable one. 'I ask your leave, lord. I will fight,
free of fault, and with your leave, twice-born one, I will conquer all my
enemies.'

"'If you had not come to me, resolved to fight,' said Drona, 'I would have
cursed you to utter defeat, great king. So, Yudhishthira, I am pleased, and
honoured by you, sinless one. I give you leave. Fight and win victory. And I
will do what you wish. Tell me what you want. Since this is so, great king, what
do you want, other than battle? A man is the slave of wealth, but wealth is no
one's slave. That is the truth, great king. I am bound to the Kauravas by
wealth. So I speak to you like a eunuch. What do you want, other than battle? I
will fight for the Kaurava. I must wish you victory.'

"'Wish me victory, brahmin,' said Yudhishthira, 'and give me counsel for my
good, and fight for the Kaurava. That is the boon I choose.'

"'Your victory is sure, king,' said Drona, 'since Hari is your counsellor. And I
acknowledge that you will conquer your enemies in battle. Where dharma is, there
is Krishna. Where Krishna is, there is victory. Go and fight, son of Kunti. Ask
me, what shall I tell you?'

"'I ask you, best of the twice-born,' said Yudhishthira. 'Hear what I want to
say. How can I conquer you in battle, unconquered as you are?'

"'There is no victory for you while I fight in battle,' said Drona. 'Strive
quickly with your brothers for my death, king.'

"'Then, strong-armed one,' said Yudhishthira, 'tell me the way to kill you. I
bow to you, teacher, and ask you. Homage to you.'

"'I see no enemy, my son,' said Drona, 'who could kill me as I stand in battle,
fighting in fury, pouring floods of rains of arrows, except when I have resolved
to die, laid down my weapons and am senseless. Then a fighter could kill me in
battle. I tell you the truth. And I would give up my weapons in battle if I
heard something very terrible from a man whose word I trusted. I tell you the
truth.'

"When he had heard this from wise Bharadvaja's son, great king, the king took
leave of the teacher and went to Sharadvata. The king, skilled in speech,
greeted Kripa, walked round him keeping him on his right, and spoke very bold
words. 'I ask your leave, teacher. I will fight, free of fault. With your leave,
sinless one, I will conquer all my enemies.'

"'If you had not come to me, resolved to fight,' said Kripa, 'I would have
cursed you to utter defeat, great king. A man is the slave of wealth, but wealth
is no one's slave. That is the truth, great king. I am bound to the Kauravas by
wealth. I think I must fight for their sake, great king. So I speak to you like
a eunuch. What do you want, other than battle?'

"'Then I ask you, teacher,' said Yudhishthira. 'Hear my words.'

"Saying this, the king, distressed and almost out of his senses, said no more.
Gautama understood what he meant and answered, 'I cannot be killed, lord of the
earth. Fight and win victory. I am pleased that you came to me, lord of men, and
I will rise every day and wish you victory. I tell you the truth.'

"When he had heard these words of Gautama, great king, the king took leave of
Kripa and went to where the king of the Madras was. He greeted Shalya, walked
round him keeping him on his right, and spoke words for his own good to the
unassailable one. 'I ask your leave, my elder. I will fight, free of fault. With
your leave, great king, I will conquer my enemies.'

"'If you had not come to me, resolved to fight,' said Shalya, 'I would have
cursed you to defeat in battle, great king. I am pleased and honoured. Let it be
as you wish. I give you leave. Fight and win victory. Tell me what else you
need, hero. What shall I give you? Since this is so, great king, what do you
want, other than battle? A man is the slave of wealth, but wealth is no one's
slave. That is the truth, great king. I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth. I
will do what you wish, my sister's son, as you want it. So I speak to you like a
eunuch. What do you want, other than battle?'

"'Give me always the best counsel for my good, great king,' said Yudhishthira,
'and fight for the enemy, as is your choice. That is the boon I choose.'

"'Tell me what help I can give you here, best of kings,' said Shalya. 'I will
fight for the enemy, as is my choice. I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth.'

"'The boon is the same one you gave me during the preparations,' said
Yudhishthira. 'You must sap the spirit of the suta's son in battle.'

"'Your wish will be fulfilled as you want it, son of Kunti,' said Shalya. 'Go
and fight without fear. I promise you victory.'

---

"Then Kunti's son took leave of his uncle, the lord of the Madras, and went out
of the great army surrounded by his brothers. Vasudeva went to Radha's son on
the field and said to him, for the Pandavas' sake, the elder brother of Gada: 'I
have heard, Karna, that you will not fight because you hate Bhishma. Choose us,
Radha's son, until Bhishma is killed. When Bhishma is killed, Radha's son, you
can come back to the battle to help Dhritarashtra's son, if you think it fair.'

"'I will do nothing to displease Dhritarashtra's son, Keshava,' said Karna.
'Know that I have given up my life for Duryodhana's good.'

"When he heard this, Krishna turned back, Bharata, and joined the Pandavas with
Yudhishthira at their head. Then the eldest of the Pandavas cried out in the
middle of the army, 'Whoever chooses us, I choose him, for his help.' Then,
looking at them, Yuyutsu said this gladly to the King of Dharma, Kunti's son
Yudhishthira: 'I will fight Dhritarashtra's sons in battle while they look on,
for your sake, great king, if you choose me, sinless one.'

"'Come, come,' said Yudhishthira. 'We will all fight your foolish brothers.
Yuyutsu, Vasudeva and all of us say this. I choose you, strong-armed one. Fight
for me. In you are seen Dhritarashtra's line and the offering of rice to his
ancestors. Take our side, prince, as we take yours, splendid one. Evil-minded,
very impatient Dhritarashtra's son will cease to be.'

"Then Yuyutsu the Kaurava left your sons and went over to the army of Pandu's
sons, having the drums sounded. Then King Yudhishthira, overjoyed, with his
younger brothers, took up again his shining armour, bright with gold. All those
bulls among men mounted their own chariots, and they drew up their array as
before. They sounded drums and hundreds of pushkaras, and those bulls among men
roared many kinds of lions' roars. Seeing the Pandavas, tigers among men,
standing in their chariots, all the kings, Dhrishtadyumna and the rest, were
glad again. Seeing the respect of Pandu's sons, who honoured those worthy of
honour, the kings there praised them highly. The kings spoke of the friendship,
the timely pity and the very great kindness of those great men towards their
kin. 'Good! Good!' Holy words full of praise went out everywhere, for those
famous men, gladdening hearts and minds. Mlecchas and Aryas who saw it or heard
of the conduct of Pandu's sons wept, choked with tears. Then, glad and
high-spirited, they beat great drums and hundreds of pushkaras, and blew conches
white as cow's milk."

**Divergences.**

* **"Bound by wealth."** Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and Shalya each say, "A man is the slave of wealth … I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth" (6.41.36, 51, 66, 77). Ledger L-70.
* **Drona's death foretold.** Drona says he would give up his weapons on hearing "something very terrible" from a man whose word he trusted (6.41.61). Ledger L-190.
* **Yuyutsu.** Dhritarashtra's son Yuyutsu goes over to the Pandavas (6.41.90–95). Ledger L-190.
* Rejected lines (\*115–\*133) add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 6.41.3 *krakacāḥ*: "saws", a kind of musical instrument.
* 6.41.37 *klībavat*: "like a eunuch", the elders' own words for their position.
* 6.41.43 *punarāgamanaṃ kuru*: "Come back again", that is, ask again later.

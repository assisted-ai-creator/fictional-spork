summary: Chapters 11 and 12 of the Bhagavad Gita, told simply, with footnotes. Arjuna asks to see Krishna's divine form; Krishna gives him a divine eye and shows a form with countless faces, bright as a thousand suns, into whose mouths the warriors of both armies are rushing; Krishna says he is Time, the destroyer, and that Arjuna is only the instrument; Arjuna, terrified, asks forgiveness for having treated him as a friend and begs to see his familiar form again; then Arjuna asks which worshippers are best, and Krishna teaches the way of love, step by step, and describes the devotee who is dear to him.

# The Form of All

"'You have told me, out of kindness to me, the highest secret, about the self,'
said Arjuna. 'Your words have taken away my confusion. I have heard from you in
full how beings come and go, lotus-eyed one, and about your greatness that never
ends. It is just as you say about yourself, Lord. But I want to see your divine
form, best of persons.[^b6c11-1] If you think I am able to see it, lord of yoga,
then show me your unchanging self.'

"'Look, Arjuna,' said Krishna, 'at my forms, by the hundred and the thousand,
divine, of many kinds, many colours and many shapes. See the Adityas, the Vasus,
the Rudras, the two Ashvins and the Maruts. See many wonders no one has seen
before. See the whole world here today, all that moves and all that does not,
standing together in my body, and anything else you want to see. But you cannot
see me with these eyes of yours. I give you a divine eye.[^b6c11-2] See my power
as Lord.'"

"When he had said this, king," said Sanjaya, "Hari, the great lord of yoga,
showed Arjuna his highest form as Lord. It had many mouths and eyes, and many
wonderful sights. It had many divine ornaments, and held up many divine weapons.
It wore divine garlands and clothes, and was anointed with divine perfumes. It
was the god made of every wonder, without end, facing every way. If the light of
a thousand suns were to rise in the sky all at once, it might be like the light
of that great one. There, in the body of the god of gods, Arjuna saw the whole
world in one place, divided in many ways. Filled with wonder, his hair standing
on end, Arjuna bowed his head to the god, joined his palms and spoke.

"'God, I see all the gods in your body, and all the crowds of beings. I see
Brahma the Lord, sitting on the lotus, and all the seers and the divine
serpents. I see you everywhere, with endless form, with many arms, bellies,
mouths and eyes. I see no end to you, no middle and no beginning, Lord of all,
form of all. I see you with a crown, a mace and a discus, a mass of light
shining everywhere, hard to look at on every side, blazing like fire and the
sun, beyond measure.

"'You are the Imperishable, the highest thing to be known. You are the final
resting place of this world. You never change. You guard the eternal dharma. I
think you are the eternal Person. I see you without beginning, middle or end,
with endless power and endless arms, with the moon and sun for your eyes, your
mouth a blazing fire, burning up this whole world with your brightness.

"'This space between heaven and earth, and all the directions, are filled by you
alone. When they see this wonderful, terrible form of yours, the three worlds
tremble, great one. These crowds of gods are entering you. Some, afraid, praise
you with joined palms. Crowds of great seers and perfected ones say, "May all be
well!" and praise you with many hymns. The Rudras, the Adityas, the Vasus, the
Sadhyas, the All-gods, the two Ashvins, the Maruts and the ancestors, the crowds
of gandharvas, yakshas, asuras and perfected ones, all gaze at you in amazement.

"'When they see your huge form, with its many mouths and eyes, its many arms,
thighs and feet, its many bellies and its terrible fangs, the worlds tremble,
and so do I. When I see you touching the sky, blazing, many-coloured, with
gaping mouths and huge blazing eyes, my heart shakes, and I can find no courage
and no calm, Vishnu. When I see your mouths, terrible with fangs, like the fire
at the end of time, I lose all sense of direction and find no shelter. Be kind,
lord of gods, home of the world.

"'And all these sons of Dhritarashtra are here, with the crowds of kings.
Bhishma, Drona and that son of a charioteer, Karna, are here, and the chief
warriors on our side too. They are rushing into your terrible mouths, fearful
with fangs. Some are caught between your teeth, their heads crushed to powder.
As the many rushing streams of rivers run towards the ocean, so these heroes of
the world of men enter your blazing mouths. As moths rush into a blazing fire to
their death, so these worlds rush into your mouths to their death. You lick your
lips as you swallow all the worlds on every side with your flaming mouths. Your
fierce light fills the whole world with brightness and scorches it, Vishnu.

"'Tell me who you are, in this fierce form. I bow to you, best of gods. Be kind.
I want to know you, the first one. I do not understand what you are doing.'

"'I am Time,[^b6c11-3] grown old, the destroyer of worlds,' said Krishna. 'I am
at work here to sweep the worlds away. Even without you, all these warriors
standing in the opposing armies will cease to be. So stand up and win glory.
Conquer your enemies and enjoy a rich kingdom. I have already killed them. Be
only the instrument, Arjuna.[^b6c11-4] Drona, Bhishma, Jayadratha, Karna and the
other heroes are already killed by me. You kill them. Do not tremble. Fight. You
will beat your rivals in battle.'"

"When he heard these words of Keshava's," said Sanjaya, "Arjuna, trembling,
joined his palms and bowed. Then, terrified, he bowed again and spoke to Krishna
in a choking voice.

"'It is right, Krishna, that the world rejoices and delights in praising you,
that the rakshasas run in fear in all directions, and that all the crowds of
perfected ones bow to you. And why should they not bow to you, great one? You
are greater even than Brahma. You are the first maker. Endless one, lord of
gods, home of the world, you are the Imperishable, what is and what is not, and
what is beyond both. You are the first god, the ancient Person. You are the
final resting place of this world. You are the knower and what is to be known,
and the highest home. This world is filled by you, whose forms have no end.

"'You are Vayu, Yama, Agni, Varuna and the moon. You are Prajapati and the
great-grandfather. I bow to you a thousand times, and again and again I bow to
you. I bow to you in front, and behind. I bow to you on every side, you who are
everything. Your strength has no end and your power no measure. You fill
everything, and so you are everything.

"'Not knowing this greatness of yours, I thought of you as a friend. Carelessly,
or out of love, I called you "Krishna!", "Yadava!", "Friend!" Perhaps I made fun
of you and was disrespectful to you, at play, in bed, sitting or at meals, alone
or in front of others. If so, Achyuta, I ask you to forgive me, you who cannot
be measured.[^b6c11-5] You are the father of the world, of all that moves and
does not. You are its teacher, the most worthy of worship. No one is equal to
you in the three worlds. How could anyone be greater, you whose power has no
equal? So I bow down, laying my body at your feet, and beg your kindness, Lord,
you who are to be praised. As a father forgives a son, as a friend forgives a
friend, as a lover forgives the one he loves, please forgive me, God.

"'I am thrilled to have seen what no one has seen before, but my mind is shaken
with fear. Show me that other form of yours, God. Be kind, lord of gods, home of
the world. I want to see you as before, with your crown, your mace and the
discus in your hand. Take that four-armed form again, you of a thousand arms,
you whose body is the world.'[^b6c11-6]

"'Out of kindness to you, Arjuna,' said Krishna, 'I have shown you by my own
yoga this highest form, made of light, the whole world, endless and first, which
no one but you has seen before. In the world of men, no one but you can see me
in this form, hero of the Kurus. Not the Vedas, not sacrifice or study, not
gifts, rituals or harsh austerities can make it possible. Do not be afraid, and
do not be confused at seeing this terrible form of mine. Free from fear, and
glad at heart, look again at this form of mine.'"

"When he had said this to Arjuna," said Sanjaya, "Vasudeva showed him his own
form again. Taking back his gentle shape, the great one comforted Arjuna in his
fear.

"'Now that I see this gentle human form of yours, Janardana,' said Arjuna, 'I
have come to my senses and I am myself again.'

"'This form of mine that you have seen is very hard to see,' said Krishna. 'Even
the gods always long to see it. I cannot be seen as you have seen me through the
Vedas, or austerity, or gifts, or sacrifice. But by love that turns to nothing
else, Arjuna, I can be known like this, and seen truly, and entered. The one who
does my work, who has me as his goal, who loves me, who is free from clinging
and bears no hatred towards any being, comes to me, Arjuna.'

"'There are people who are always joined to you and worship you with love,' said
Arjuna, 'and there are others who worship the Imperishable, which cannot be
seen. Which of them knows yoga best?'[^b6c11-7]

"'Those who fix their minds on me,' said Krishna, 'who worship me, always
joined, with the highest faith, I think are the most joined. Others worship the
Imperishable, which cannot be named or seen. It is everywhere, beyond thought,
unchanging, unmoving and fixed. They control all their senses, see all things
the same, and delight in the good of all beings. They reach me too. But the
struggle is greater for those whose minds are fixed on the unseen. For people
with bodies, the unseen goal is hard to reach. But some give all their actions
to me and have me as their goal. They worship me and meditate on me with a yoga
that turns to nothing else, and their minds are fixed on me. For them, Arjuna, I
soon become the one who lifts them out of the ocean of death and rebirth.

"'Fix your mind on me alone. Let your understanding rest in me. Then you will
live in me from now on. There is no doubt of that. If you cannot hold your mind
steady on me, then try to reach me by the yoga of practice, Arjuna. If you
cannot practise, then make my work your goal. Even by doing work for my sake,
you will reach perfection. If you cannot do even this, then take shelter in my
yoga, control yourself, and give up the fruit of all your actions.[^b6c11-8]
Knowledge is better than practice. Meditation is better than knowledge. Giving
up the fruit of action is better than meditation. And from giving up comes peace
at once.

"'Some people hate no being. They are friendly and kind, free from "mine" and
"I", the same in pain and pleasure, and patient. They are always content, yogis,
self-controlled and firm in purpose, with mind and understanding given to me.
The one who loves me like this is dear to me.[^b6c11-9]

"'The one who does not upset the world, and whom the world does not upset, who
is free from excitement, impatience, fear and worry: that one is dear to me. The
one who wants nothing, who is pure and capable, who does not take sides and is
free from anxiety, is dear to me. So is the one who has given up all selfish
undertakings and loves me. The one who neither rejoices nor hates, neither
grieves nor longs, who has given up both good and bad, and is full of love: that
one is dear to me.

"'Some are the same to enemy and friend, the same in honour and dishonour, in
cold and heat, in pleasure and pain. They are free from clinging and take blame
and praise alike. They are quiet, content with whatever comes, without a fixed
home, steady in mind and full of love. Such a person is dear to me. And some
follow this deathless teaching of dharma as I have told it, with faith, making
me their goal and loving me. They are very, very dear to me.'"

## Explanations

[^b6c11-1]: **The divine form (*viśvarūpa*).** *What it means:* "the form of
    all": God seen as the whole universe at once, all gods, all beings, all
    time. *In the story:* in the Kuru hall, when Duryodhana planned to seize
    him, Krishna laughed, and gods and heroes came out of his body in shapes
    like lightning (CE 5.129; see ledger L-30 and Book 5, ch 38). Here in the
    Gita Krishna says no one but Arjuna has seen this form before (CE 6.33.47).
    The two passages are both kept as the text has them.

[^b6c11-2]: **The divine eye.** *What it means:* ordinary eyes see only ordinary
    things. To see God as he is, a different kind of sight is needed, and it has
    to be given. *In the story:* the same gift is given to Sanjaya by Vyasa, so
    that he can see everything on the battlefield and tell it to Dhritarashtra
    (CE 6.2.9–11). That is how we are hearing the Gita at all.

[^b6c11-3]: **"I am Time" (*kāla*).** *What it means:* Time is the force that
    brings everything to an end. Krishna shows himself as that force. *In the
    story:* before the war, Vyasa told Dhritarashtra the same thing in other
    words: "Your sons and the other kings: their time has come" (CE 6.2.4), and
    "This is time, born to you in the form of a son" (CE 6.4.5).

[^b6c11-4]: **"Be only the instrument."** *What it means:* the outcome is
    already decided by a larger power. Arjuna's part is to do his duty, not to
    think of himself as the one who decides who lives and dies. *Everyday
    example:* a surgeon's hand holds the knife, but it is the need of the
    patient, and the knowledge behind the hand, that decides where it cuts.

[^b6c11-5]: **Friend and God.** *What it means:* Arjuna suddenly realises that
    the friend he joked with, ate with and teased is God himself, and he is
    ashamed. *In the story:* their friendship runs through the whole epic. In
    the forest Krishna told Arjuna, "You are mine and I am yours. Whoever hates
    you hates me … You are Nara, and I am Narayana" (CE 3.13.38–40). Later in
    this war he tells Yudhishthira that Arjuna is "my friend, my kinsman and my
    pupil", and that he would cut off his own flesh for him (CE 6.103.33).

[^b6c11-6]: **The four-armed form.** *What it means:* the familiar form of
    Vishnu, with four arms holding a mace, a discus and other emblems, and a
    crown on his head. Arjuna asks for this gentler form, and then Krishna takes
    his ordinary human form again.

[^b6c11-7]: **Two ways of worship.** *What it means:* some people worship God
    with a form, as a person they can love (the way of *bhakti*, devotion).
    Others try to meditate on the formless, unseen reality. Krishna says both
    reach him, but the first is easier for people who live in bodies.

[^b6c11-8]: **A ladder of steps.** *What it means:* Krishna gives a ladder for
    people at every level. Best, fix your mind on me. If you can't, practise. If
    you can't practise, work for me. If you can't even do that, just give up the
    fruits of your work. Everyone can start somewhere.

[^b6c11-9]: **The devotee who is dear to God.** *What it means:* in the last
    verses of this chapter Krishna lists the marks of a true devotee: no hatred,
    friendliness, kindness, calm in pleasure and pain, not upsetting others, not
    being upset by them, taking praise and blame alike. *Everyday example:* a
    person who stays kind and steady whether people praise or insult them.

* This chapter is Gita chapters 11 and 12.
* **Who has seen the form.** Krishna says no one but Arjuna has seen this form before (6.33.47–48). In Book 5 he showed a cosmic form in the Kuru hall (5.129; ledger L-30). Both are kept as told, and the footnote says so.
* **The named warriors.** Krishna names Drona, Bhishma, Jayadratha and Karna as already killed by him (6.33.34). How each dies is told later, in its place.

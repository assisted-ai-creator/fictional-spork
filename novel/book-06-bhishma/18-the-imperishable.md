# The Imperishable

"'What is that Brahman?' said Arjuna. 'What is the self, what is action, best of
men? What is called the ground of beings, and what is said to be the ground of
the gods? Who, and how, is the ground of sacrifice here in this body,
Madhusudana? And how are you to be known at the hour of departure by those who
are self-controlled?'

"'Brahman is the highest imperishable,' said the Blessed One. 'Its own being is
called the self. The sending forth that brings beings into being is called
action. The ground of beings is perishable being. The Person is the ground of
the gods. And I myself am the ground of sacrifice here in the body, best of the
embodied.

"'Whoever, at the hour of his end, leaves the body and goes forth remembering me
alone, comes to my state. There is no doubt of this. Whatever state a man
remembers at the end, when he leaves the body, to that state he goes, son of
Kunti, always shaped by that state. So at all times remember me, and fight. With
mind and understanding fixed on me, you will come to me alone, without doubt.
With thought disciplined by the yoga of practice, not wandering elsewhere,
dwelling on him, Partha, one goes to the highest divine Person.

"'Think of one who remembers the poet, the ancient, the ruler, smaller than the
small, the upholder of all, whose form cannot be thought, the colour of the sun,
beyond darkness. He remembers him at the hour of departure, with an unmoving
mind, joined to devotion and the power of yoga, setting the breath rightly
between the brows. He goes to that highest divine Person. I will tell you
briefly of that place which those who know the Veda call imperishable, which
ascetics free of passion enter, and wanting which men live the life of chastity.
He controls all the gates, shuts the mind in the heart, sets his breath in the
head and is firm in the concentration of yoga. Speaking the one syllable Om,
which is Brahman, and remembering me, one who goes forth leaving the body goes
to the highest end.

"'For the yogin who remembers me always, his thought never elsewhere, always
disciplined, I am easy to reach, Partha. Coming to me, the great souls who have
reached the highest perfection do not come again to birth, the home of sorrow,
which does not last. The worlds up to the world of Brahma return again, Arjuna.
But coming to me, son of Kunti, there is no birth again.

"'Those who know day and night know that a day of Brahma lasts a thousand ages,
and a night ends after a thousand ages. At the coming of the day all manifest
things come forth from the unmanifest. At the coming of the night they dissolve
into that very thing called the unmanifest. This same host of beings, coming
into being again and again, dissolves helplessly at the coming of night, Partha,
and comes forth at the coming of day. But beyond that unmanifest is another,
eternal, unmanifest being, which does not perish when all beings perish. It is
called the unmanifest, the imperishable. They call it the highest end. Those who
reach it do not return. That is my highest home. That highest Person, Partha, in
whom beings stand and by whom all this is pervaded, is reached by devotion to
him alone.

"'Now I will tell you, bull of the Bharatas, the times at which yogins who go
forth return, or do not return. Fire, light, day, the bright fortnight, the six
months of the sun's northern course: going forth then, men who know Brahman go
to Brahman. Smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern
course: going forth then, the yogin reaches the light of the moon and returns.
These two paths of the world, the bright and the dark, are held to be eternal.
By one a man goes and does not return. By the other he returns again. Knowing
these two paths, Partha, no yogin is confused. So at all times be disciplined in
yoga, Arjuna. Whatever fruit of merit is laid down for the Vedas, sacrifices,
austerities and gifts, the yogin who knows this goes beyond it all and reaches
the highest, first place.'"

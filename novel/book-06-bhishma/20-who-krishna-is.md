summary: Dhritarashtra asks why the Pandavas cannot be killed, and Sanjaya tells him that they fight by dharma while his sons do evil. Sanjaya then tells how, on the night of the fourth day, Duryodhana asked Bhishma the same question. Bhishma answers with an old story. On Gandhamadana Brahma saw a shining chariot in the sky and praised the god in it as Vasudeva, the lord of all, asking him to be born among men to kill the asuras and set up dharma. The god agreed and vanished, and Brahma told the gods that Vasudeva and Nara, the ancient seers Nara and Narayana, would be born together and must never be despised as mere men. Bhishma tells Duryodhana who Krishna is, what the sages say of him, and why Arjuna and Krishna cannot be beaten, and begs him again to make peace. Duryodhana, for a while, holds Krishna and the Pandavas in high regard.

# Who Krishna Is

"Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "a very great fear has come on me, and wonder
too, hearing of the deeds of Pandu's sons, deeds even the gods would find hard
to do. And hearing of my sons' defeat on every side, Sanjaya, I have a great
worry: how will this end? Surely Vidura's words will burn my heart. For
everything seems to happen as fate brings it together, Sanjaya. The Pandava
armies fight the heroes led by Bhishma, who know weapons, the best of warriors,
men who strike hard. Why can the great, mighty sons of Pandu not be killed, my
son? Who gave them boons? What knowledge do they have, that they are not
destroyed, like the hosts of stars in the sky?

"Again and again I cannot bear it, that the army has been killed by the
Pandavas. A very cruel punishment falls on me alone, by fate. Tell me all this
truly, Sanjaya: how the sons of Pandu cannot be killed, and how my sons can. I
see no far shore of this sorrow, like a man swimming across the great ocean with
his arms. I think a very cruel disaster has surely come to my sons. Bhima will
kill all my sons, no doubt of it. I see no hero who could save my sons in
battle. Surely my sons will be destroyed in the battle, Sanjaya. So, charioteer,
tell me the cause, and above all the reason, since I ask you truly today. And
what did Duryodhana do, when he saw his own men turned back in battle? What did
Bhishma and Drona, Kripa, Subala's son and Jayadratha decide, or the great
archer, Drona's son, or mighty Vikarna? What was the resolve of those great men,
wise one, when my sons had turned back, Sanjaya?"

"Listen, king, with attention," said Sanjaya, "and when you have heard, take it
to heart. The Pandavas use no spells, no magic of that sort, no tricks to
frighten, king. They fight fairly, and they are strong in battle. The Parthas
always begin everything they do by dharma, Bharata, as it has been taught,
seeking great glory. They do not turn back from battle. They hold to dharma,
they are mighty, and the highest fortune is with them. Where dharma is, there is
victory. That is why the Parthas cannot be killed in battle, lord of the earth,
and why victory is with them.

"Your sons are evil-hearted, always taking pleasure in wrong. They are harsh and
do base deeds. That is why they are losing in battle. Your sons have done a
great many cruel things to the Pandavas here, lord of the people, like low men.
And the Pandavas always passed over all your sons' offences and hid them, elder
brother of Pandu. Yet your sons do not think much of them, lord of the people.
Now the fruit of that evil deed, done without end, has come, very great and
terrible, like the kimpaka fruit, sweet at first and then poison. Eat it, great
king, with your sons and your friends.

"You did not understand, king, when your friends held you back: Vidura, Bhishma
and great Drona. And I too held you back again and again, and you would not take
good and healthy advice, as a dying man will not take healthy medicine. You took
your sons' view, and thought the Pandavas were beaten. Now listen again, best of
the Bharatas, to the true cause you ask me about, the reason for the Pandavas'
victory, tamer of enemies. I will tell it to you as I heard it.

"Duryodhana asked the grandfather about this very thing. He had seen all his
brothers, great chariot fighters, beaten in battle. At nightfall the Kaurava,
his heart bewildered with grief, went humbly to the wise grandfather. Hear what
your son said to him, lord of the people.

"'You, Drona and Shalya,' said Duryodhana, 'Kripa and Drona's son, Kritavarma,
son of Hridika, and Sudakshina of Kamboja, Bhurishravas, Vikarna and mighty
Bhagadatta: all of you are known as great chariot fighters. You are men of good
family, ready to give up your bodies. I think you are enough for all three
worlds. All the Pandavas together cannot stand against your valour. So a doubt
has come to me. Tell me, since I ask: who is it the sons of Kunti rely on, that
they beat us at every step?'

"'Listen, king, to what I shall tell you, Kaurava,' said Bhishma. 'I have told
you many times, and you have not done what I said. Make peace with the Pandavas,
best of the Bharatas. I think that is right for the earth and for you, lord.
Enjoy this earth, king, happy, together with your brothers, making all who wish
you ill burn with envy and bringing joy to your kin. You did not listen to me
before, my son, when I cried out to you. Now this has come, because you despise
the sons of Pandu. Hear, great king, from me, the reason why those men of
unwearied deeds cannot be killed. There is no being in the worlds, there has
been none and there will be none, who could beat the Pandavas in battle while
the bearer of the Sharnga bow guards them. Hear, my son, who knows dharma, the
old story as it was told to me by sages who had perfected themselves.

"'Long ago, they say, all the gods and the seers gathered and sat before the
Grandfather on Mount Gandhamadana. As the Lord of Creatures sat among them, he
saw a splendid chariot of the gods standing in the sky, blazing with light.
Brahma knew by meditation who it was. Composed, he joined his hands, and with a
glad heart he bowed down to the highest, supreme lord. When the seers and the
gods saw Brahma standing up, they all stood with their hands joined, watching
that great wonder. Brahma, best of those who know Brahman, the maker of the
world, who knows the highest dharma, worshipped him as was fitting and spoke
these high words.

"'"You are the wealth of all, the form of all, the lord of all. You are
Vishvaksena, maker of all, the master. You are Vasudeva, the lord of all, and so
I come to you, the god whose self is yoga.

"'"Victory to you, the All, great god! Victory to you, who delight in the good
of the worlds! Victory, lord of yogis, all-pervading! Victory, you who are
yoga's first and last! Lotus-womb, wide-eyed one, victory, lord of the lords of
the worlds! Lord of what was, what will be and what is, victory, gentle one,
born of your own self! Unconquered one, whose virtues cannot be counted,
victory, refuge of all! Narayana, whom no one can cross, victory, bearer of the
Sharnga bow! Holder of every hidden virtue, form of all, untouched by sickness,
lord of all, strong-armed one, victory, you who are given to the good of the
worlds!

"'"Great serpent, first Boar, golden-haired one, all-pervading, victory!
Dwelling of Hari, lord of the peoples, dwelling of all, boundless and
unchanging! Manifest and unmanifest, whose place has no measure, master of the
senses and yet with senses, knower of the countless forms of the self, victory,
deep one, giver of desires! Endless one, of known wisdom, ever bringing beings
into being, whose work is done, whose wisdom is made, knower of dharma, victory,
conqueror unconquered! Hidden self, self of all beings, source of all that has
plainly come to be, truth of all that is, lord of the worlds, victory, you who
bring beings into being!

"'"Source of yourself, blessed one, you who draw in the world at the end of an
age, you who raise it, born of the mind, victory, dear to the brahmins! You who
delight in making and unmaking, lord of desire, supreme lord, source of the
deathless, true being, fire at the end of an age, giver of victory! Lord of the
Lord of Creatures, god, lotus-navel, of great strength, the self of beings, the
great being, whose self is action, victory, giver of action!

"'"The goddess Earth is your feet, the quarters your arms, heaven your head. I
am your form, the gods your body, and the moon and the sun your eyes. Strength,
austerity and truth, dharma born of desire, are yours, lord. Fire is your power,
the wind your breath, and the waters were born from your sweat. The Ashvins are
always your ears, and the goddess Sarasvati is your tongue. The Vedas rest on
you as their ground. This world depends on you.

"'"We do not know your number or your measure, your power or your valour, your
strength or your birth, lord of the yogis of yoga. Devoted to you, god, bound to
you by our vows, we always worship you, Vishnu, the supreme lord, the great
lord. Seers, gods and gandharvas, yakshas, rakshasas and serpents, pishachas and
men, beasts, birds and creeping things: all these I made on the earth by your
grace, lotus-navel, wide-eyed one, Krishna, destroyer of bad dreams. You are the
way of all beings. You are the leader. You are the face of the world. By your
grace, lord of gods, the gods are always happy. By your grace, god, the earth
has always been free of fear.

"'"So, wide-eyed one, be born to increase the line of Yadu. To establish dharma,
to kill the sons of Diti, and to hold up the world, do what I ask of you, lord.
This highest secret, lord, made of your grace, Vasudeva, I have sung to you as
it truly is. Krishna, you made the god Samkarshana of yourself, by yourself, and
from yourself you made Pradyumna, born of you. From Pradyumna you made
Aniruddha, whom they know as the undying Vishnu. And Aniruddha made me, Brahma,
who hold up the worlds. So I am made of Vasudeva. I was made by you alone.
Divide yourself into parts, lord, and go into the human state. There you will
kill the asuras for the happiness of all the worlds. You will establish dharma
and win glory, and then you will truly come to yoga. For the brahmin seers and
the gods in the world, you of boundless valour, sing of you, the supreme self,
by all those names, devoted to you. All the hosts of beings rest in you,
strong-armed giver of boons, taking you for their shelter. The brahmins call you
without beginning, middle or end, of boundless yoga, the bridge of the world."'

"'Then the blessed god, the supreme lord of the worlds, answered Brahma in a
deep, warm voice,' said Bhishma. '"By yoga I know all that you want, my son. It
will be so." And with that he vanished on the spot.

"'Then the gods, seers and gandharvas were full of wonder. All of them, eager to
know, said to the Grandfather, "Who was this, lord, whom your blessed self bowed
to so humbly and praised with the finest words? We want to hear of him."

"'Asked this, the blessed Grandfather answered all the gods, brahmin seers and
gandharvas in a sweet voice. "He is that highest one. He is what will be and
what must be, the highest. He is the self of beings, the lord, the Brahman that
is the highest state. I have spoken with him, bulls among the gods, and he was
pleased with me. For the good of the world I asked the lord of the world, 'Go
into the human world, known as Vasudeva, and be born on the earth to kill the
asuras.' The daityas, danavas and rakshasas who were killed in battle have been
born among men, terrible in form and mighty. To kill them the blessed master,
together with Nara, will take a human womb and walk the earth. Nara and
Narayana, those two ancient, most excellent seers, of boundless splendour, have
been born together in the human world. Set on it and together, they cannot be
beaten in battle even by the immortals. But fools do not know them, the seers
Nara and Narayana.

"'"I, Brahma, lord of the whole world, am his son. You must worship Vasudeva,
the great lord of all the worlds. Best of the gods, he of great valour, bearer
of the conch, discus and mace, must never be despised with the thought, 'This is
a man.' This is the highest secret. This is the highest state. This is the
highest Brahman. This is the highest glory. This is the imperishable, the
unmanifest, the eternal, the great. This is what is called the Person. It is
sung of, and it is not known. This is the highest power, the highest happiness,
the highest truth, as the Maker of All has declared. So all the gods with Indra,
and all the worlds, must never despise Vasudeva, the lord of boundless valour,
with the thought, 'This is a man.'

"'"Whoever, very dull of mind, says, 'He is only a man,' they call the lowest of
men, for despising Hrishikesha. Whoever despises Vasudeva, the great yogi who
has entered a human body, people say is sunk in darkness. The god is the self of
all that moves and does not move, marked with the Shrivatsa, of great splendour,
with the lotus at his navel. Whoever does not know him, people say is sunk in
darkness. Whoever despises the great one who wears the crown and the Kaustubha
jewel, who makes his friends free of fear, sinks into terrible darkness. Knowing
the truth of it like this, best of gods, all the worlds must bow to Vasudeva,
the lord of the lords of the worlds."

"'When he had said this to all the hosts of gods, long ago, the blessed self of
all the worlds sent them away and went to his own home. And the gods, the
gandharvas, the sages and the apsarases, glad at having heard that story sung by
Brahma, went to heaven.

"'I heard this, my son, from seers who had perfected themselves, speaking of
Vasudeva in their gathering, long ago. I heard it, learned one, from Rama
Jamadagnya, from wise Markandeya, and from Vyasa and Narada. I know this, and I
have heard of the undying lord Vasudeva, the great one, lord of the lords of the
worlds. His son is Brahma, the father of the whole world. How should Vasudeva
not be honoured and worshipped by men?

"'You were held back long ago, my son, by sages who had mastered the Vedas: "Do
not go to war with wise Vasudeva, nor with the Pandavas." And in your delusion
you do not understand it. I think you are a cruel rakshasa, and you are wrapped
in darkness, since you hate Govinda and Dhananjaya the Pandava, the gods Nara
and Narayana. No other man would hate them.

"'So I tell you, king: he is eternal and unchanging, made of all the worlds,
everlasting, the ruler, the maker, the bearer, the unshaken. He holds up the
three worlds. He is the master and teacher of all that moves and does not move.
He is the fighter, the victory and the victor, the lord who is the nature of all
things. For he is made of goodness, king, free of darkness and passion. Where
Krishna is, there is dharma. Where dharma is, there is victory. The sons of
Pandu are upheld, king, by the yoga of his greatness and the yoga of his self,
and victory will be theirs. He always gives the Pandavas a mind set on what is
best, and strength in battle, and he always guards them from dangers. He is the
eternal god, made of every secret, kindly. Know that he is Vasudeva, the one you
ask me about, Bharata. Brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras, each with
their marks, serve and worship him, always given to their own work. At the end
of the Dvapara age and the beginning of the Kali age, Samkarshana sang of him in
the Satvata rite. Age after age Vasudeva makes again and again the world of gods
and mortals, the cities girdled by the ocean, and his own home among men.'

"'Vasudeva is spoken of in all the worlds as a great being,' said Duryodhana. 'I
want to know his origin and his standing, grandfather.'

"'Vasudeva is a great being,' said Bhishma, 'born together with the gods.
Nothing higher than the lotus-eyed one is seen, bull of the Bharatas. Markandeya
tells of Govinda as a great wonder. The great one, the self of beings, the
highest Person, made all beings: water, wind and fire, these three. The god, the
lord, the ruler of all the worlds, having made the earth, made his bed on the
waters, the great highest Person. The god made of all the waters slept there by
yoga. From his mouth the Unfallen made fire, from his breath the wind, and from
his mind Sarasvati and the Vedas. He made the worlds in the beginning, and the
gods with the hosts of seers. He is the unchanging source of creatures, and
their end and death. He is dharma, the knower of dharma, the giver of boons, who
grants every wish. He is the doer and the deed, the first god, lord of himself.

"'Janardana first made the past, the present and what is to come, the two
twilights, the quarters, the sky and restraint. Govinda made the seers and
austerity. The great, unchanging lord made the maker of the world. He made
Samkarshana, first-born of all beings. He made the god Shesha, whom they know as
Ananta, who holds up all beings and this earth with its mountains. Brahmins know
him by meditation as the one of great power. The highest Person killed the great
asura named Madhu, born from the ear, fierce, of fierce deeds, of fierce mind,
to honour Brahma. Because he killed him, my son, gods, danavas and men, and the
seers, call Janardana Madhusudana, the slayer of Madhu. He is the Boar and the
Lion, the lord whose way is the three strides.

"'He, Hari, is the mother and father of all living beings. There has been
nothing higher than the lotus-eyed one, and there will be nothing. From his
mouth he made the brahmins, from his arms the kshatriyas, from his thighs the
vaishyas, king, and from his feet the shudras. The god, disciplined by
austerity, is the storehouse of all embodied beings. One who serves Keshava, who
is Brahman and yoga, on the new moon and the full moon, will reach the great
one. Keshava is the highest light, the grandfather of all the worlds. So the
sages speak of Hrishikesha, lord of men. Know him like this, as teacher, father
and guru. Those with whom Krishna is pleased have won the undying worlds. The
man who goes to Keshava for refuge in a place of fear, and always reads this,
will be well and happy. Men who go to Krishna are not bewildered. Janardana
always protects those sunk in great fear. Yudhishthira knows this as it truly
is, Bharata, and with his whole self, king, he has gone for refuge to great
Keshava, lord of the world, master of yogas, the lord.

"'Hear too, great king, my hymn to him who is Brahman, which the brahmin seers
and the gods told long ago on earth. "Narada called you the lord of the god of
gods even of the Sadhya gods, the knower of what brings the worlds into being.
Markandeya called you what was, what is to be and what will be. The blessed
Bhrigu called you the sacrifice of sacrifices, the austerity of austerities, the
god even of the gods, and in the old story the terrible form, Vishnu, lord of
beings. Dvaipayana said, 'You are Vasudeva of the Vasus, the one who set Shakra
in his place, the god of gods among the gods.' In the first making of creatures
they called Daksha the Lord of Creatures, and then Angiras called you the maker
of all beings. Devala Asita said, 'The unmanifest rose from your body, and the
manifest rests in your mind, and the gods were born of your speech.' Heaven is
filled by your head, the earth is held up by your arms, the three worlds are
your belly. You are the eternal Person. So men made pure by austerity know you.
You are the best of the seers who are content with seeing the self. Madhusudana,
you are the way of the noble royal seers who never turn back in battle, for whom
dharma comes first in all things."

"'This is the full account of Keshava, my son, and the short one, told as it
truly is. Be gladly devoted to Keshava.'"

"When he had heard this holy story, great king," said Sanjaya, "your son held
Keshava in high regard, and the Pandavas, those great chariot fighters. Bhishma,
son of Shantanu, spoke to him again, great king. 'You have heard, king, of the
greatness of great Keshava, and of Nara, as it truly is, as you asked me. You
have heard why Nara and Narayana have both been born among men, and how those
two heroes cannot be killed and are never beaten in battle. You have heard how
the Pandavas, king, cannot be reached by anyone in battle. Krishna loves the
famous Pandavas deeply. So I tell you, lord of kings, let there be peace with
the Pandavas. Enjoy the earth together with your mighty brothers, master of
yourself. If you despise the gods Nara and Narayana, you will be destroyed.'

"When he had said this, your father fell silent, lord of the people. He sent the
king away and went to his bed. And the king bowed to the great man and went to
his camp, and lay down that night on his bright bed, bull of the Bharatas."

**Divergences.**

* **"Where dharma is, there is victory."** Sanjaya says it here (6.61.16), and Bhishma adds "Where Krishna is, there is dharma" (6.62.34). The saying comes thirteen times in the CE, from many speakers. See ledger L-195 on Gandhari's use of it.
* **The four forms.** Brahma says that Krishna made Samkarshana, Pradyumna and Aniruddha from himself, and that Aniruddha made Brahma (6.61.65–67). The rejected \*258 has Brahma ask him to take this fourfold form. The CE's line is narrated as it stands.
* **Duryodhana moved, for a while.** After the story, Duryodhana "held Keshava in high regard, and the Pandavas" (6.64.11). The text says nothing more of it here, and the war goes on.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*256–\*270. They include a longer list of sages Bhishma heard (\*263), the lotus from Narayana's navel with Brahma born in it (\*264), and Krishna killing the demon who stole the Vedas (\*265).

**Choices.**

* This is Bhishma's story within Sanjaya's report, so the hymn is nested three deep: Sanjaya quotes Bhishma, who quotes Brahma. The quotation marks follow that nesting.
* The long list of titles in 6.61.42–53 is kept in full, each rendered by its plain sense. Where a title is a name (Vishvaksena, Narayana, Vasudeva), it is kept as a name. *saumyātmajātmaja* (6.61.44) is uncertain; "gentle one, born of your own self" follows the sense Ganguli takes.
* 6.61.20 *kiṃpāka*: a fruit that looks and tastes sweet but is poison, glossed in the line.
* 6.62.8 *vāsudeva iti śrutaḥ*: "known as Vasudeva".
* 6.62.39 *sātvataṃ vidhim āsthāya*: "in the Satvata rite", the Satvatas' form of worship.
* 6.63.13 *madhusūdana*: "the slayer of Madhu", explained as the text explains it.

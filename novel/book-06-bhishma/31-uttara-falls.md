# Uttara Falls

"King, hundreds of thousands fought there, here and there, at that time, without
any bounds," said Sanjaya. "I will tell you of it, Bharata. The son did not know
his father, nor the father the son of his body, nor brother brother, nor uncle
nephew, nor nephew uncle, nor friend friend. The Pandavas fought the Kurus as if
possessed. Some tigers among men attacked the rank of chariots with their
chariots, and yokes broke against yokes, bull of the Bharatas, chariot poles
against chariot poles and shafts against chariot shafts. Some, joined together,
met others joined together, wanting to kill each other. Some chariots, crashing
into chariots, could not move. Huge elephants in rut, crashing into elephants,
tore each other in many places with their tusks in their anger. Elephants with
lances and flags rushed at enemy elephants, great king, and struck by the tusks
of swift great elephants, cried out in great pain. Led on by training, struck
with goads and hooks, elephants in full rut went face to face against elephants
in rut. Some great elephants, engaged with others in rut, gave a cry like cranes
and ran here and there. Fine elephants, well led, their temples and mouths
running with rut, pierced by spears, lances and iron arrows, screamed with their
vitals pierced and fell lifeless. Some ran off in every direction, crying
terrible cries.

"The guards of the elephants' feet, broad-chested strikers, ran here and there
in great fury, great king, wanting to kill one another. They held spears and
bows and bright axes, clubs, pestles, slings and lances, iron bars and bright,
sharp swords. Shining swords, wet with men's blood, were seen in the hands of
heroes running at one another. A tumultuous noise arose from swords swung and
brandished by heroes' arms as they fell on the vitals of their enemies. Here and
there, Bharata, crowds of men called to one another like ghosts, with dreadful
cries. They had been broken by clubs and pestles, cut by fine swords, pierced by
elephants' tusks and crushed by elephants.

"Horsemen with yak-tail plumes rushed at one another on horses as swift as
swans. Great lances adorned with gold, swift, bright and sharp, flew from their
hands like snakes. Some heroes, horsemen on the swiftest horses, leapt up on
great chariots and took the heads of the chariot fighters. A chariot fighter,
finding many horsemen come within range of his arrows, killed them with
broad-headed arrows with bent joints. Elephants like clouds or mountains,
adorned with gold, in rut, threw down horses and trampled them with their feet.
Some elephants, their temples and flanks split, struck by lances, cried out in
great pain. Some fine elephants seized horses with their riders and threw them
in that terrible confusion. Elephants tossed horses and riders on the tips of
their tusks and went round crushing masses of chariots with their banners. Some
great elephants, for their male strength and their rut, killed horses and riders
with their trunks and feet. Some elephants seized chariots with their horses in
their trunks and dragged them in every direction, going towards every noise.

"Swift, bright, sharp arrows like snakes fell, piercing the bodies of men and
horses and their iron armour. Bright spears, hurled by heroes' arms, fell here
and there, like great meteors, terrible, lord of the people. With bright swords
drawn from sheaths covered in panther skin and tiger skin, they struck their
enemies in battle. They rushed in with swords, shields and axes, showing an
enemy leaping at them in fury, torn open on one side. Some were torn by spears,
others cut by axes, others crushed by elephants and others trampled by horses.
Cut by chariot wheels and cut by sharp arrows, men cried out here and there,
king, to their kin. Some called to their sons, others to their fathers, to
brothers and kinsmen, uncles and nephews, and even to their enemies in the
battle. Very many were seen with their entrails spilled and thighs broken,
Bharata, with arms cut off and sides torn open, crying, thirsty, wanting to
live. Some, of little strength, overcome by thirst, fallen on the ground in
battle, lord of the people, begged only for water. Drenched in floods of blood,
suffering, Bharata, they cursed themselves bitterly, and your sons who had
gathered there.

"Other brave kshatriyas, who had feuds with one another, neither let go their
weapons nor cried out, sir, but threatened one another gladly here and there.
Biting their lips with their teeth in anger, they looked at one another with
faces twisted in frowns. Others, mighty and strong of spirit, suffering, pained
by wounds and tormented by arrows, fell silent. Other heroes, without chariots,
begging for another's chariot in battle, fell, crushed by fine elephants, and
shone, great king, like flowering kimshukas. Many terrible cries arose in the
armies in that very dreadful destruction of the finest heroes. Father killed son
and son father in battle, nephew uncle and uncle nephew, friend friend, king,
and kinsman kin. So the Kurus fought the Pandavas there. In that fearful great
battle without bounds, the army of the Parthas shook when it met Bhishma. With
his tall silver banner, a palm tree with five stars, on his great chariot,
strong-armed Bhishma shone then, king, like the moon on Meru.

---

"Most of the forenoon of that dreadful day had gone, in that very terrible
destruction of the finest heroes. Durmukha, Kritavarma, Kripa, Shalya and
Vivimshati, urged on by your son, came to Bhishma and guarded him. Guarded by
these five atirathas, bull of the Bharatas, the great chariot fighter plunged
into the ranks of the Pandavas. Bhishma's palm-tree banner was seen moving about
in many places among the Chedis, Kashis, Karushas and Panchalas, Bharata.
Bhishma cut off heads, and arms with their weapons, with swift broad-headed
arrows with bent joints. As Bhishma danced along the chariot roads, bull of the
Bharatas, some elephants, struck in the vitals, cried out in pain.

"Abhimanyu, in great anger, mounted his chariot yoked with fine tawny horses and
went towards Bhishma's chariot. His banner was a karnikara tree, bright with
gold. He rained arrows on Bhishma and on those fine chariot fighters. The hero
struck the palm-tree banner with a sharp feathered arrow and fought Bhishma and
his followers. He pierced Kritavarma with one arrow and Shalya with five of
iron, and wounded the great-grandfather with nine sharp-pointed ones. With one
arrow, drawn full and loosed and well aimed, he pierced the banner adorned with
gold. With a broad-headed arrow with bent joints that pierced every covering, he
took the head of Durmukha's charioteer from his body. With a sharp-pointed
broad-headed arrow he cut Kripa's bow, adorned with gold, and struck them with
sharp-mouthed arrows, very angry, the great chariot fighter, as if dancing.
Seeing his lightness, even the gods were pleased. Because Krishna's son hit his
mark, all the chariot fighters led by Bhishma thought him a mighty man, like
Dhananjaya himself. His bow, moving along the paths of lightness, bright as a
firebrand, went round the quarters, twanging like Gandiva.

"Bhishma, slayer of enemy heroes, met him and quickly pierced Arjuna's son in
battle with nine swift arrows. Bhishma of strict vows cut down the banner of
that very energetic boy with three broad-headed arrows and struck his charioteer
with three arrows. In the same way Kritavarma, Kripa and Shalya pierced
Krishna's son, sir, and did not shake him, as one does not shake Mount Mainaka.
Surrounded by those great chariot fighters of Dhritarashtra's side, the hero,
Krishna's son, rained showers of arrows on the five chariots. Holding back their
great weapons with showers of arrows, mighty Krishna's son roared, loosing
arrows at Bhishma. The very great strength of his arms was seen there, king, as
he strove in battle, harrying Bhishma with arrows. Bhishma too shot arrows at
him as he showed his courage, and he cut those arrows loosed from Bhishma's bow
in the battle. Then the hero, whose arrows never failed, cut down Bhishma's
banner in the battle with nine arrows, and the people shouted aloud. That silver
palm tree, with its great trunk, adorned with gold, cut down by the arrows of
Subhadra's son, fell to the ground, Bharata. Seeing the banner fallen to the
arrows of Subhadra's son, bull of the Bharatas, Bhima roared gladly, cheering
Subhadra's son.

"Then mighty Bhishma, very terrible, brought forth at that moment many great
heavenly weapons. Then the great-grandfather, of boundless spirit, covered
Subhadra's son with a hundred thousand arrows with bent joints. Then ten great
archers, great chariot fighters of the Pandavas, rushed quickly in their
chariots to protect Subhadra's son: Virata with his son, Dhrishtadyumna
Prishata's grandson, Bhima, the Kekayas and Satyaki, lord of the people. As they
came on swiftly, Bhishma son of Shantanu struck the Panchala prince with three
sharp arrows in battle, and Satyaki too. With one arrow, drawn full and loosed,
a sharp razor-headed one, he cut down Bhimasena's banner. Bhimasena's golden
banner, a lion, best of men, struck by Bhishma, fell from the chariot. Bhimasena
pierced Bhishma son of Shantanu in battle with three arrows, Kripa with one, and
Kritavarma with eight.

"Virata's son Uttara, on an elephant with its trunk raised, rushed at the king,
the lord of the Madras. Shalya, on his chariot, held back in battle the
matchless speed of that lord of elephants as it came on swiftly. That lord of
elephants, in anger, set its foot on the yoke of his great, well-going chariot
and killed his four horses. Standing in his chariot with its horses killed, the
lord of the Madras hurled an iron spear like a snake that put an end to Uttara.
Pierced through his armour by it, Uttara entered deep darkness and fell from the
elephant's neck, dropping his goad and lance. Shalya took up his sword, leapt
down from his fine chariot, and with a bold stroke cut off the great trunk of
the lord of elephants. Its vitals pierced by masses of arrows and its trunk cut
off, the elephant gave a terrible cry of pain, fell and died. Having done such a
deed, the great chariot fighter, the king of the Madras, quickly mounted
Kritavarma's shining chariot.

"When Virata's son Shankha saw his fine brother Uttara killed, and Shalya
standing with Kritavarma, he blazed with anger like fire with butter. Bending
his great bow adorned with gold, the mighty one rushed at Shalya, lord of the
Madras, wanting to kill him. Surrounded on every side by a great line of
chariots, pouring a rain of arrows, he went towards Shalya's chariot. He came on
with the courage of an elephant in rut. Seven chariot fighters of yours
surrounded him on every side, wanting to save the king of the Madras, who had
gone between the fangs of death. Then strong-armed Bhishma, roaring like a
cloud, took up a bow the size of a palm tree and rushed at Shankha in battle.
Seeing that mighty great archer raised up, the Pandava army was terrified, like
a ship struck by a gale.

"There Arjuna quickly went in front of Shankha, thinking, 'He must be protected
from Bhishma today,' and the battle began. There was a great outcry from the
fighters in the battle. 'Splendour has met splendour,' they said, and they were
amazed. Then Shalya, club in hand, got down from the great chariot and killed
Shankha's four horses, bull of the Bharatas. Shankha ran quickly from his
chariot with its horses killed, sword in hand, reached Bibhatsu's chariot, and
found safety there. Then arrows flew swiftly from Bhishma's chariot, covering
the sky and the earth on every side. Bhishma, the best of fighters, brought down
the Panchalas, the Matsyas, the Kekayas and the Prabhadrakas with his arrows.
Leaving the Pandava, the left-handed archer, quickly in the battle, he rushed at
Drupada the Panchala, surrounded by his army, and covered his dear kinsman with
many arrows, king. Drupada's armies, burned by arrows, looked like forests
burned by fire at the end of winter. Bhishma stood in the battle like a fire
without smoke.

"The Pandava's fighters could not look at Bhishma, burning with splendour like
the sun at midday. The Pandavas, tormented by fear, looked round on every side
and found no protector, like cows tormented by cold. With the army killed and
fled, without spirit and crushed, a great cry of 'Alas!' arose among the Pandava
troops, Bharata. Then Bhishma son of Shantanu, his bow always drawn into a
circle, loosed arrows with blazing points like poisonous snakes. Making all the
quarters one road with his arrows, the man of strict vows killed the Pandava
chariot fighters, naming them one by one, Bharata. Then, with the armies broken
and crushed on every side, and the sun gone down, nothing could be made out.
Seeing Bhishma rising up in the great battle, the Parthas withdrew their troops,
bull of the Bharatas."

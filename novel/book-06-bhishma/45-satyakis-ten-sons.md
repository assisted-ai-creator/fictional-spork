# Satyaki's Ten Sons

"Then Virata," said Sanjaya, "the great chariot fighter, struck the great
chariot fighter Bhishma with three arrows and pierced his horses with three.
Bhishma son of Shantanu, the very strong great archer, practised of hand,
pierced him back with ten gold-feathered arrows. Drona's son, the great chariot
fighter with the terrible bow, firm of hand, pierced Gandiva's bowman between
the breasts with six arrows. Phalguna, killer of enemy heroes, cut his bow, and
the destroyer of enemies pierced him hard with sharp feathered arrows. Wild with
anger, unable to bear Partha's cutting his bow in battle, he took up another bow
of great speed. He pierced Phalguna with ninety sharp arrows, king, and pierced
Vasudeva with seventy fine arrows.

"Then Phalguna, his eyes copper-red with anger, and Krishna with him, sighed
long and hot and thought again and again. The destroyer of enemies, Gandiva's
bowman, in anger gripped his bow with his left hand and took up sharp arrows
with bent joints, terrible, that bring an end to life. With them he quickly
pierced Drona's son in battle, the best of the strong. They split his armour and
drank his blood in the battle. Pierced through by Gandiva's bowman, Drona's son
did not falter. Unshaken, he loosed showers of arrows in return and stood in the
battle, king, wanting to protect him of the great vow. The bulls among men
praised that very great deed of his, for he was not ashamed to stand up to the
two Krishnas together in battle. He always fought fearless in the ranks, for he
had received from Drona the whole store of weapons, with the means of calling
them back, which is very hard to win.

"'This is my teacher's son, Drona's dearly loved son,' thought the hero
Bibhatsu, scorcher of enemies, 'and a brahmin besides, whom I must above all
honour.' With this in his mind the best of chariot fighters took pity on
Bharadvaja's grandson. Leaving Drona's son in battle, the mighty Kaunteya,
scorcher of enemies, fought on, hurrying, killing your men.

"Duryodhana struck the great archer Bhimasena with ten stone-whetted arrows with
golden feathers and vulture plumes. Bhimasena in anger took up a firm, bright
bow that takes life away, and ten sharp arrows. Calmly he quickly pierced the
Kuru king in his broad chest with sharp, swift, keen-pointed arrows drawn to the
ear. The jewel on a golden string on his chest, surrounded by arrows, shone like
the sun surrounded by planets. Your splendid son, struck by Bhimasena, could not
bear it, as a snake cannot bear the sound of a clap. Then in anger, great king,
he pierced Bhima with stone-whetted, gold-feathered arrows, terrifying the army.
The two of them fought in battle, badly wounding each other, and your two very
strong sons shone like gods.

"Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, pierced Chitrasena, the tiger among
men, with ten arrows, and Purumitra with seven. Having pierced Satyavrata with
seventy, the hero, like Shakra in battle, seemed to dance in the fight, and he
made us suffer. Chitrasena pierced him back with ten arrows, Satyavrata with
nine and Purumitra with seven. Pierced, streaming blood, Arjuna's son cut
Chitrasena's great bright bow that held off enemies. He split his armour and
struck him in the chest with an arrow. Then your heroes, princes and great
chariot fighters, came together in battle and in fury pierced him with sharp
arrows. He who knew the highest weapons struck them all with sharp arrows.

"Seeing that deed of his, your sons surrounded him as he burned your army in
battle as a fire burns dry brush. Like a blazing fire when the cold season has
gone, Subhadra's son shone out as he cut down your troops. Seeing what he was
doing, lord of the people, your grandson Lakshmana quickly fell on the son of
the Satvata woman in battle. Abhimanyu in anger pierced Lakshmana, of fine
marks, with six arrows, and his charioteer with three. So too Lakshmana, king,
pierced Subhadra's son with sharp arrows, great king, and it was a wonder. The
very strong son of Subhadra killed his four horses and his charioteer and ran at
Lakshmana with sharp arrows. Standing on his chariot with its horses killed,
Lakshmana, killer of enemy heroes, in anger hurled a spear at the chariot of
Subhadra's son. As it came at him, terrible in form, hard to withstand, like a
snake, Abhimanyu cut it with sharp arrows. Then Gautama took Lakshmana up on his
own chariot and carried him off from the battle while the whole army watched.

"Then, in that confused and very dangerous battle, they ran at one another, each
wanting to kill the other. Your great archers and the great chariot fighters of
the Pandavas killed one another, offering up their lives in battle. With hair
loose, armour gone, chariots lost and bows cut, the Srinjayas fought the Kurus
with their bare arms. Then the strong-armed, very strong Bhishma in anger killed
the army of the great-souled Pandavas with divine weapons. The earth was strewn
with elephants whose masters were killed, with men and horses brought down, with
chariot fighters and horsemen.

---

"Then, king, the strong-armed Satyaki, mad for battle, drew his fine bow that
could bear any load in battle. He loosed feathered arrows like venomous snakes,
showing a bright, light and wonderful lightness with weapons. As he drew his bow
and loosed arrows, took up more and fitted others, as he shot arrows in battle
and killed enemies, his form looked very fine, like a cloud pouring rain. Seeing
him rising, King Duryodhana sent ten thousand chariots against him, Bharata.
Satyaki, of true courage, the mighty, the greatest archer, killed all those
great archers with a divine weapon.

"Having done that cruel deed, the hero, bow in hand, came up against
Bhurishravas in battle. For he who increases the Kurus' glory, seeing that army
brought down by Yuyudhana, ran at him in anger. He drew his great bow, the
colour of Indra's rainbow, and loosed arrows like thunderbolts, like venomous
snakes, in thousands, great king, showing the lightness of his hands. Then
Satyaki's followers, king, could not bear those arrows whose touch was death.
They ran off on every side, king, leaving Satyaki, mad for battle.

"Seeing him, Yuyudhana's ten very strong sons, famed as great chariot fighters,
with bright armour, weapons and banners, came up against the great archer
Bhurishravas in battle. In great fury they all said to him of the sacrificial
post banner in that great battle, 'Ho, ho, heir of the Kauravas, very strong
one! Come and fight with us in battle, all of us together or one by one. Either
you defeat us and win glory in battle, or we defeat you and give our father
joy.'

"So addressed by the heroes, the very strong one, the best of men, who prided
himself on his courage, seeing them standing there, said to them, 'Well spoken,
heroes, if that is your mind today. Fight together, and take care. I will kill
you in battle.'

"So addressed, those great archers, heroes quick to act, rained a great shower
of arrows on the tamer of enemies. In the afternoon, great king, a tumultuous
battle broke out on the battlefield between one and many together. They covered
that one best of chariot fighters with showers of arrows, as clouds pour rain on
a great mountain in the rains, king. But the great chariot fighter quickly cut
down the floods of arrows they loosed, bright as Yama's staff and the
thunderbolt, before they could reach him. There we saw the wonderful courage of
Somadatta's son, since he alone fought many in battle as if without fear.

"Having let go that shower of arrows, king, the ten great chariot fighters
surrounded the strong-armed one and set about killing him. Then Somadatta's son
in anger, Bharata, cut their bows with ten arrows in the blink of an eye, the
great chariot fighter. Then, when their bows were cut, he cut off their heads in
battle with sharp broad arrows with bent joints, king. Killed, they fell to the
ground like trees broken by a thunderbolt.

"Seeing his very strong sons, the heroes, killed in battle, the Varshneya,
roaring, went at Bhurishravas, king. The two very strong great chariot fighters
pressed chariot against chariot in battle. Each killed the other's chariot
horses, and without their chariots they leapt about and came together. The two
tigers among men shone, standing ready for battle, holding great swords and fine
shields. Then Bhimasena quickly came up to Satyaki, who held a fine sword, and
took him up on his chariot, king. Your son too, king, quickly took Bhurishravas
up on his chariot in battle while all the bowmen watched.

"While the battle went on like this, the Pandavas in fury fought the great
chariot fighter Bhishma, bull of the Bharatas. As the sun turned red,
Dhananjaya, hurrying, killed twenty-five thousand great chariot fighters. Sent
by Duryodhana to destroy Partha, they were destroyed as soon as they reached
him, like moths reaching a fire. Then the Matsyas and the Kekayas, skilled in
the science of the bow, surrounded the great chariot fighter Partha and his son.

"At that very time, as the sun was setting, a stupor came over all the armies.
Then at twilight, great king, your father Devavrata, his mounts tired, withdrew
the armies. With the Pandavas and the Kurus having met each other, the two
armies, badly shaken, went to their own quarters. Then, Bharata, they went to
their own camps and rested there by the rule, the Pandavas with the Srinjayas,
and the Kurus."

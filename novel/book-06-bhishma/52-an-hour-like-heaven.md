# An Hour Like Heaven

"Vikarna, your son," said Sanjaya, "came up to the high-minded Chitrasena, now
without a chariot, and took him onto his own chariot. While that very
tumultuous, crowded fight went on, Bhishma son of Shantanu quickly ran at
Yudhishthira. Then the Srinjayas, with their chariots, elephants and horses,
trembled. They thought Yudhishthira had come into the mouth of death.
Yudhishthira too, the Kauravya, the lord, went with the twins against the great
archer Bhishma son of Shantanu, the tiger among men. Then the Pandava, loosing
thousands of arrows in battle, covered Bhishma as a cloud covers the sun.
Ganga's son took the nets of arrows he sent so well, in hundreds and thousands,
Bharata. In the same way the nets of arrows Bhishma shot were seen in the sky,
sir, like flocks of birds. In half a blink Bhishma son of Shantanu made the
Kaunteya invisible in battle with nets of arrows, part by part.

"Then King Yudhishthira in anger sent an iron arrow like a venomous snake at the
great-souled Kauravya. Before it reached him, king, the great chariot fighter
Bhishma cut that arrow loosed from his bow in battle with a razor-headed arrow.
Having cut that iron arrow, like Time, in battle, Bhishma killed the
gold-adorned horses of the lord of the Kauravas. Leaving the chariot with its
horses killed, Yudhishthira, son of Dharma, quickly climbed onto the
great-souled Nakula's chariot. Bhishma, conqueror of enemy cities, very angry,
came up against the twins too in battle and covered them with arrows. Seeing the
two of them hard pressed by Bhishma's arrows, great king, he fell into deep
thought, wanting Bhishma's death.

"Then Yudhishthira urged on the kings who were under his command, his hosts of
friends: 'All of you, kill Bhishma son of Shantanu!' Hearing Partha's words, all
those kings surrounded the grandfather with a great line of chariots. Surrounded
on every side, your father Devavrata played with his bow, king, bringing down
great chariot fighters. The Parthas saw the Kaurava moving about in battle like
a lion cub that has gone among deer in the forest. Seeing him threatening the
brave men in battle and terrifying them with his arrows, they trembled, great
king, like herds of deer before a lion. The kshatriyas saw the lion of the
Bharatas move in battle like fire, with the wind for its ally, wanting to burn
dry brush. Bhishma brought down the heads of chariot fighters in battle as a
skilled man brings down ripe fruit from palm trees. As the heads fell on the
ground, great king, there was a tumultuous sound like falling stones.

"In that very cruel, tumultuous battle, there was a great confusion of all the
armies. With the arrays broken, the kshatriyas challenged one another one by one
and stood to fight. Shikhandi came up to the grandfather of the Bharatas, ran at
him at speed and said, 'Stand! Stand!' Bhishma ignored Shikhandi in battle.
Thinking of Shikhandi's womanhood, he went in anger against the Srinjayas.
Seeing the great chariot fighter Bhishma, the Srinjayas were glad and made lion
roars of many kinds mixed with conches. Then a battle broke out with chariots
and elephants tangled, lord, when the sun stood in the west.

"Dhrishtadyumna the Panchala and the great chariot fighter Satyaki pressed the
army hard with showers of spears and lances, and killed your men in battle with
many weapons, king. Being killed in battle, bull among men, your men set their
minds on noble battle and did not leave the fight. The great chariot fighters
killed people in battle as hard as they could. There was a great cry from your
great-souled men, king, as they were killed in battle by the great-souled
grandson of Prishata. Hearing that terrible cry, the two great chariot fighters
of yours, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, came up against Prishata's grandson.
Hurrying, the two great chariot fighters killed his horses and both covered
Prishata's grandson with a shower of arrows. Then the very strong Panchala leapt
quickly from his chariot and climbed quickly onto the chariot of the
great-souled Satyaki.

"Then King Yudhishthira, surrounded by a great army, went against the two
scorchers of enemies of Avanti, angry in battle. In the same way your son, sir,
with all his strength, surrounded Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti and stood by
them. Arjuna too, bull among kshatriyas, in anger fought the kshatriyas in
battle, as the wielder of the thunderbolt fought the asuras. Drona too, in anger
in battle, doing what your son wanted, blew away all the Panchalas as fire blows
away a heap of cotton. Your sons led by Duryodhana, lord of the people,
surrounded Bhishma in battle and fought the Pandavas.

"Then, as the sun turned red, King Duryodhana said to all your men, 'Hurry!'
Bharata. As they fought on like this, doing deeds hard to do, and the sun
climbed the western mountain and no longer shone, a terrible river began to
flow, its waves floods of blood, crowded in a moment with packs of jackals at
the start of night. With the ill-omened jackals howling a fearful howl, the
battlefield became terrible, crowded with hosts of spirits. Rakshasas, pishachas
and other eaters of flesh were seen on every side in hundreds and thousands.

"Then Arjuna, having beaten the kings led by Susharma with their followers in
the middle of the army, went towards his own camp. Yudhishthira too, the
Kauravya, the king, went to his own camp at night with his two brothers,
surrounded by his army. Bhimasena too, king of kings, having beaten the chariot
fighters led by Duryodhana in battle, went towards his own camp. King Duryodhana
too, surrounding Bhishma son of Shantanu in the great battle, went quickly
towards the camp. Drona, Drona's son, Kripa, Shalya and Kritavarma the Satvata
surrounded the whole army and went towards the camp. In the same way Satyaki,
king, and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, surrounded their warriors in
battle and went towards the camp. So, great king, your men and the Pandavas,
scorchers of enemies, turned back together at nightfall.

"Then the Pandavas and the Kurus went to their own camps and settled there,
great king, honouring one another. The heroes set guards for themselves and
placed their companies as was proper. They drew out their arrows and bathed in
many kinds of water. All of them had prayers for good fortune said over them,
and were praised by the bards. The glorious ones amused themselves with the
sound of songs and instruments. For a moment it was all like heaven. The great
chariot fighters spoke no word of war there. The two armies, king, with their
many elephants and horses, the army asleep and its people worn out, were a sight
to see."

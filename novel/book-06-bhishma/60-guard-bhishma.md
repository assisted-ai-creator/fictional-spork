# Guard Bhishma

"When the night had turned to dawn," said Sanjaya, "the king rose early and
ordered the kings, 'Yoke the army! Today Bhishma in his anger will kill the
Somakas in battle.'

"Having heard Duryodhana's many laments in the night, king, Shantanu's son took
them as a rebuke to himself. Falling into deep despair and condemning his
dependence on others, he thought long, wanting to fight Arjuna in battle.
Knowing from his signs what Ganga's son was thinking, great king, Duryodhana
urged on Duhshasana. 'Duhshasana, let the chariots that guard Bhishma be yoked
at once. Urge on all thirty-two divisions. What we have thought of through a
heap of years has come: the killing of the Pandavas and their armies, and the
winning of the kingdom. In this I think the task is to guard Bhishma above all.
If he is guarded, he will bring us happiness and kill the Parthas in battle. For
the pure-hearted one said, "I will not kill Shikhandi. He was born a woman
first, so I must avoid him in battle. The world knows that, wanting to please my
father, I gave up a rich kingdom and women long ago, strong-armed one. I would
never kill a woman, nor one who was a woman before, in battle, best of men. I
tell you this truly. This Shikhandi was a woman before, king, as you may have
heard. As was told at the time of the preparations, so she was born Shikhandini.
Having been a girl, she became a man, and she will fight, Bharata. I will never
loose arrows in front of her. But in battle, son, I will kill all the other
kshatriyas of the Pandavas who seek victory, when they come within range of my
arrows."

"'So Ganga's son, best of the Bharatas, who knows the sciences, told me. So I
think we must guard Bhishma with our whole selves. A wolf might kill a lion left
unguarded in the great forest. Let us not have the tiger killed by a wolf, by
Shikhandi. Let our uncle Shakuni, Shalya, Kripa, Drona and Vivimshati be ready
and guard Ganga's son. If he is guarded, victory is certain.'

"Hearing these words of Duryodhana, the kings surrounded Ganga's son on every
side with a line of chariots. Your sons too surrounded Ganga's son and went
gladly, making earth and sky shake and alarming the Pandavas. With those
well-yoked chariots and elephants the great chariot fighters surrounded Bhishma
in battle and stood in armour. All of them stood guarding that great chariot
fighter, as the thirty gods guarded the bearer of the thunderbolt in the battle
of the gods and asuras. Then King Duryodhana spoke again to his brother.
'Yudhamanyu guards Arjuna's left wheel and Uttamaujas his right. Those two are
Arjuna's guards, and Arjuna is Shikhandi's. Duhshasana, act so that he, guarded
by Partha and avoided by us, does not kill Bhishma.' Hearing his brother's
words, your son Duhshasana put Bhishma in front and went with the army.

"Seeing Bhishma surrounded by the line of chariots, Arjuna, the best of chariot
fighters, said to Dhrishtadyumna, 'Today, tiger among men, faultless one, place
Shikhandi in front of Bhishma, Panchala, and I will be his guard.'

"Then Bhishma son of Shantanu went out with the army and drew up a great array
in battle, the all-sides array. Kripa, Kritavarma, the great chariot fighter
Shaibya, Shakuni, the Sindhu king and Sudakshina of Kamboja, all with Bhishma
and with your sons, Bharata, stood at the head of the array, in front of all the
troops. Drona, Bhurishravas, Shalya and Bhagadatta, sir, stood in armour on the
right wing of the array. Ashvatthama, Somadatta and the two great chariot
fighters of Avanti, with a great army, guarded the left wing. Duryodhana, great
king, surrounded on every side by the Trigartas, stood in the middle of the
array facing the Pandavas, Bharata. Alambusa, the best of chariot fighters, and
the great chariot fighter Shrutayu stood in armour at the rear of the array,
behind all the troops. So your men made their array then, Bharata, and were seen
in armour like blazing fires.

"So too King Yudhishthira, Bhimasena the Pandava, Nakula and Sahadeva, both sons
of Madri, stood in armour at the head of the array, in front of all the troops.
Dhrishtadyumna, Virata and the great chariot fighter Satyaki, destroyers of
enemy armies, stood with a great army. Shikhandi, Vijaya, the rakshasa
Ghatotkacha, strong-armed Chekitana and mighty Kuntibhoja stood in battle, great
king, surrounded by a great army. The great archer Abhimanyu, the great chariot
fighter Drupada and the five Kekaya brothers stood in armour ready for battle.
So the brave Pandavas too drew up a great counter-array, very hard to defeat,
and stood ready for battle, sir.

"Your kings, ready in battle with their armies, put Bhishma in front and went
out against the Parthas, king. So too the Pandavas, king, led by Bhimasena,
wanting victory in battle, went to fight Bhishma. Sounding shouts, kilikila
cries, saws and cow-horns, beating drums, mridangas, tabors and pushkaras, the
Pandavas ran at us roaring fearful roars. With the sounds of drums, mridangas,
conches and kettledrums, with shouts and lion roars and leaping of many kinds,
we roared back at them and went at them quickly. We were roused to anger at
once, and it became a great tumult. Then they ran at one another and fought, and
the earth shook with the great noise.

"Birds flew about crying very terribly. The sun, bright when it rose, lost its
light. Tumultuous winds blew, foretelling very great fear. Terrible jackals
howled there with a terrible sound, great king, announcing that a great
slaughter had come. The quarters blazed, king, and a rain of dust fell, and a
rain of bones mixed with blood. Tears fell from the eyes of the weeping mounts,
and they let go of dung and urine, lord of the people, brooding. Great invisible
roars were heard, bull of the Bharatas, of rakshasas and man-eaters roaring
fearful roars. Jackals, herons and crows were seen flying about, and dogs stood
there barking with many cries. Blazing great meteors struck the sun and fell at
once to the ground, announcing great fear. At that meeting of the Pandavas and
the sons of Dhritarashtra, at that great gathering, the great armies, with the
sound of conches and mridangas, seemed to shake like forests in the wind. As the
armies came on at that inauspicious hour, crowded with kings, elephants and
horses, there was a tumultuous roar, like that of oceans tossed by the wind.

---

"Abhimanyu, the noble chariot fighter, with his fine tawny horses, the splendid
one, ran at Duryodhana's great army, scattering showers of arrows as a cloud
scatters torrents of water. Your bulls among the Kurus could not even hold back
in battle the angry son of Subhadra, killer of enemies, as he plunged with
floods of weapons into the endless ocean of the army. The arrows he loosed in
battle, king, which destroy enemies, took brave kshatriyas to the dwelling of
the king of the dead. Subhadra's son in anger sent arrows in battle, terrible
like Yama's staff, like blazing venomous snakes. Phalguna's son quickly brought
down the chariot fighter from his chariot, the horseman from his horse's back,
and elephant riders with their elephants. Seeing him do that great and wonderful
deed in battle, the kings, glad, honoured and praised Phalguna's son. Subhadra's
son shone as he drove those ranks before him in many directions, as the wind
blows a heap of cotton in all directions. Driven off by him, your troops,
Bharata, found no protector, like elephants sunk in mud. Having driven off all
your troops, king, the best of men, Abhimanyu, stood blazing like a smokeless
fire.

"None of your men could bear that killer of enemies, any more than moths driven
by Time can bear a blazing fire. Striking at all his enemies, the great chariot
fighter of the Pandavas, the great archer, looked like the bearer of the
thunderbolt with his thunderbolt. His gold-backed bow was seen as he moved in
all directions, king, like flashes of lightning in the clouds. Sharp yellow
arrows went out from him in battle, king, like swarms of bees from a forest of
flowering trees. As the great-souled son of Subhadra moved about in his chariot
rumbling like a cloud, people could find no opening in him. Bewildering Kripa,
Drona, Drona's son, Brihadbala and the great archer Sindhu king, he moved about
lightly and well. We saw his bow always drawn into a circle, sir, like the disc
of the sun, as he scorched your army. Seeing him scorching with the flames of
his arrows, the brave kshatriyas thought from his deeds that the world had two
Phalgunas. Hurt by him, great king, that great Bharata army whirled about here
and there like a woman under the power of drink. Having driven off that army and
made the great chariot fighters tremble, he delighted his friends, like Vasava
when he had conquered Maya.

"Driven off by him in battle, your troops gave a terrible cry of distress like
the thunder of Parjanya. Hearing that terrible cry of your army, sir, like the
ocean at the full or new moon when its force is stirred by the wind, King
Duryodhana then spoke to Rishyashringa's son. 'This Krishna's grandson, the
great archer, like a second Phalguna, is driving off the army in anger, as
Vritra drove off the army of the gods. I see no other great remedy for him in
battle but you, best of rakshasas, master of all knowledge. Go quickly and kill
the heroic son of Subhadra in battle. We, led by Bhishma and Drona, will kill
the Parthas.'

"So addressed, the strong and mighty lord of the rakshasas went quickly into
battle at your son's command, roaring a great roar like a cloud in the rains. At
his great sound, king, the great army of the Pandavas surged on every side like
the filling ocean. And many men, king, terrified by his roar, gave up their dear
lives and fell to the ground. Krishna's grandson too, filled with joy, bow in
hand, ran at that rakshasa, as if dancing on the chariot floor. Then the
rakshasa in anger, having reached Arjuna's son in battle, drove off the army not
far from him. Being killed in battle, the great Pandava army went out against
the rakshasa in battle, as the army of the gods against Bali. There was a very
great crush of that army as it was killed in battle by the rakshasa of terrible
form. Then, showing his courage, the rakshasa drove off the great Pandava army
in battle with thousands of arrows. Being killed so by the rakshasa of terrible
form, the Pandava army fled in battle in fear. Having crushed that army as an
elephant crushes a lotus pond, he ran in battle at the very strong sons of
Draupadi.

"Those angry great archers, the sons of Draupadi, hard hitters, all ran at the
rakshasa, as five planets run at the sun. The best of rakshasas was hard pressed
by those mighty ones, as the moon is by five planets at the terrible end of an
age. Then the very strong Prativindhya quickly split the rakshasa with sharp,
unblunted arrows made all of iron. With his armour split by them, the best of
rakshasas shone like a great cloud threaded through with the rays of the sun.
With the arrows stuck in him, their gold trappings shining, Rishyashringa's son
looked, king, like a mountain with blazing peaks. Then the five brothers pierced
the lord of the rakshasas in the great battle with sharp arrows adorned with
gold. Split by those terrible arrows like angry snakes, Alambusa grew very
angry, king, like a lord of serpents. Badly pierced, great king, and hard
pressed by those great chariot fighters, sir, he went for a moment into a long
darkness. Coming to his senses, his anger doubled, he cut their banners and
their bows with arrows. Smiling, as if dancing on the chariot floor, the great
chariot fighter Alambusa struck each of them with three arrows. Hurrying, very
angry, the strong rakshasa killed the horses of those great-souled men, and
their charioteers. Very glad, he pierced them again with well-sharpened arrows
of many shapes, in hundreds and thousands. Having made the great archers
chariotless, the rakshasa, the night-walker, ran at them at speed, wanting to
kill them.

"Seeing them hard pressed in battle by that evil-hearted rakshasa, Arjuna's son
ran at the rakshasa in battle. There was a battle between them like that of
Vritra and Vasava. All your men and the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas
watched it. Meeting in the great battle, blazing with anger at each other, great
king, very strong, their eyes red with anger, they looked at each other in
battle like the fire of Time. Their meeting was terrible and bitter in its
outcome, like that of Shakra and Shambara in the battle of the gods and asuras."

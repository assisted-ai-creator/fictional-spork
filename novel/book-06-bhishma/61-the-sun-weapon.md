# The Sun Weapon

Dhritarashtra said, "How did Alambusa fight in battle against Arjuna's brave
son, the great chariot fighter, as he killed so many, Sanjaya? And how did
Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, fight Rishyashringa's son? Tell me truly
how it went in battle. And what did Dhananjaya do among my troops, Sanjaya, or
Bhima, the best of the strong, or the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, or Nakula, Sahadeva
or the great chariot fighter Satyaki? Tell me all this, for you are skilled,
Sanjaya."

"Come, then," said Sanjaya. "I will tell you, sir, the hair-raising battle of
the lord of the rakshasas and the son of Subhadra, and how Arjuna in battle,
Bhimasena the Pandava, Nakula and Sahadeva showed their courage in battle. And
so too how all your men, led by Bhishma and Drona, did wonderful and varied
deeds as if without fear.

"Alambusa, roaring a very great roar in battle and threatening again and again,
ran at the great chariot fighter Abhimanyu at speed and said, 'Stand! Stand!'
Subhadra's son too, king, roaring again and again like a lion, ran at the great
archer Rishyashringa's son, his father's bitter enemy. Then the man and the
rakshasa, the best of chariot fighters, met quickly in battle in their chariots,
like a god and a danava. The rakshasa was the best of his kind and a master of
illusion. Phalguna's son knew divine weapons. Then Krishna's grandson, great
king, pierced Rishyashringa's son in battle with three sharp arrows and pierced
him again with five. Alambusa too, very angry, pierced Krishna's grandson in the
heart at speed with nine swift arrows, as a great elephant is struck with goads.
Then the night-walker, quick to act, pressed Arjuna's son in battle with a
thousand arrows, Bharata. Then Abhimanyu in anger loosed ninety sharp arrows
with bent joints at the rakshasa's broad chest. They split his body at the vital
points and quickly went in. Split in every limb by them, the best of rakshasas
looked, king, like a mountain strewn with flowering kimshuka trees. Bearing
those gold-feathered arrows, the very strong best of rakshasas shone like a
mountain in flames.

"Then, great king, Rishyashringa's son, very strong, in anger covered Krishna's
grandson, who was like great Indra, with feathered arrows. Those sharp arrows he
loosed, like Yama's staff, split Abhimanyu and went into the ground. In the same
way the gold-adorned arrows Arjuna's son loosed split Alambusa and went into the
ground. Subhadra's son came up against the rakshasa in battle with arrows with
bent joints and turned him back, as Shakra turned back Maya in battle. Turned
back and struck by his enemy in battle, the rakshasa, scorcher of enemies,
brought forth a great illusion of darkness. Then all of them on the ground were
swallowed by the darkness. They could not see Abhimanyu, nor their own men nor
the enemy in battle. Seeing that great darkness, terrible in form, Abhimanyu,
the joy of the Kurus, brought forth the very fierce sun weapon. Then the whole
world became bright, lord of the earth, and he destroyed that illusion of the
evil-hearted rakshasa. The very strong best of men in anger covered the lord of
the rakshasas in battle with arrows with bent joints. Phalguna's son, who knew
all weapons and whose self is beyond measure, warded off the many other
illusions the rakshasa used. His illusions destroyed and himself struck by
arrows, the rakshasa left his chariot right there and ran in great fear.

"When that crooked fighter, the rakshasa, had been quickly beaten, Arjuna's son
crushed your army in battle, as a wild lord of elephants, blind with rut,
crushes a lotus pond with its lotuses. Then Bhishma son of Shantanu, seeing the
army fleeing, surrounded the son of Subhadra with a great line of chariots. The
great chariot fighters, Dhritarashtra's sons, hemmed in that hero. Many against
one, they cut him hard with arrows in battle. That hero was equal in courage to
his father, and like Vasudeva in valour and strength. The best of all who bear
weapons did many deeds in battle against those chariot fighters, deeds worthy of
both his father and his uncle.

---

"Then, king, Dhananjaya, impatient, killing your soldiers, came up against
Bhishma in battle, wanting to rescue his son. So too your father Devavrata came
up against Partha in battle, king, as Svarbhanu comes up against the sun. Then
your sons, lord of the people, with chariots, elephants and horses, surrounded
Bhishma in battle and guarded him on every side. So too the Pandavas surrounded
Dhananjaya and stood in armour, ready for the great battle, bull of the
Bharatas.

"Then Sharadvat's son, king, struck Arjuna, who stood in front of Bhishma, with
twenty-five arrows. Satyaki went out against him and pierced him with sharp
arrows, wanting to do what the Pandava wanted, like a tiger against an elephant.
Gautama too, hurrying, in anger pierced Madhava in the heart with nine
heron-feathered arrows. The Shaineya, badly pierced and very angry, took up a
terrible arrow that would end Gautama. As it came on at speed, bright as
Shakra's thunderbolt, Drona's son, quick to anger, cut it in two. Leaving
Gautama, the best of chariot fighters, the Shaineya ran at Drona's son in
battle, as Rahu runs at the moon in the sky. Drona's son cut his bow in two,
Bharata, and struck him with arrows when his bow was cut. He took up another bow
that killed enemies and could bear the load, and struck Drona's son in the arms
and chest with sixty arrows, great king. Pierced and in pain, he grew faint for
a moment. He sat down on the chariot floor, leaning on his flagstaff.

"Coming to his senses, the mighty son of Drona in anger struck the Varshneya in
battle with an iron arrow. It split the Shaineya and went into the ground, like
a strong young snake going into its hole in spring. Then with another broad
arrow Drona's son cut down Madhava's fine banner in battle and roared a lion
roar. Again he covered him with terrible arrows, Bharata, as a cloud covers the
sun at the end of the hot season, great king. Satyaki, great king, struck down
that net of arrows and quickly fell on Drona's son with nets of arrows of many
kinds. The Shaineya, killer of enemy heroes, scorched Drona's son like the sun
freed from a net of clouds. The very strong Satyaki covered him again with a
thousand arrows as he rose up, and roared. Seeing his son seized like that, like
the moon by Rahu, the mighty son of Bharadvaja ran at the Shaineya. Wanting to
save his son, king, scorched by the Varshneya, he pierced him with a very sharp
arrow in the great battle. Satyaki, having beaten the teacher's son, the great
chariot fighter, in battle, pierced Drona with twenty arrows made all of iron.
At that moment the Kaunteya of the white horses, the great chariot fighter whose
self is beyond measure, ran in anger at Drona in battle. Then Drona and Partha
met in the great battle, great king, like Mercury and Venus in the sky."

Dhritarashtra said, "How did the great archer Drona and Dhananjaya the Pandava,
both heroes, meet in battle? Tell me that, Sanjaya. For the Pandava is always
dear to wise Bharadvaja's son, and the teacher is always dear to Partha in
battle, Sanjaya. How did those two chariot fighters, proud and fierce as lions
in battle, Bharadvaja's son and Dhananjaya, meet in battle?"

"In battle Drona does not count Partha as dear to him," said Sanjaya, "nor does
Partha count his teacher, putting the dharma of the kshatriya first. Kshatriyas
do not avoid one another in battle, king. They fight without limits, even with
fathers and brothers. In battle, Bharata, Partha pierced Drona with three
arrows, and Drona took no thought of those arrows loosed from Partha's bow in
battle. Partha covered him again in battle with a shower of arrows, and he
blazed with fury like a fire risen in a thicket. Then Drona soon held off Arjuna
in battle with arrows with bent joints, king of kings, Bharata.

"Then King Duryodhana urged on Susharma to guard Drona's rear in battle, king.
The king of the Trigartas too, in anger, drew his bow hard and covered Partha in
battle with iron-headed arrows. The arrows loosed by the two of them shone in
the sky, king, like swans in the autumn sky, great king. All those arrows
reached the Kaunteya and went in, lord, as birds go into a sweet tree bent with
its load of fruit. But Arjuna, the best of chariot fighters, roared a roar in
battle and pierced the king of the Trigartas and his son with arrows. Being
killed by Partha like Time at the end of an age, they went against Partha
himself, resolved to die, and loosed a shower of arrows at the Pandava's
chariot. The Pandava took that shower of arrows with a rain of arrows, king of
kings, as a mountain takes the rain. There we saw Bibhatsu's wonderful lightness
of hand, for alone he held off the rain of weapons, hard to withstand, loosed by
many heroes, as the wind holds off masses of clouds. The gods and danavas were
pleased with that deed of Partha's.

"Then Partha in anger loosed the wind weapon at the Trigartas at the head of the
army, great king. A wind arose, stirring the sky, bringing down hosts of trees
and killing soldiers. Seeing that very cruel wind weapon, Drona loosed another
terrible weapon, the mountain weapon, great king. When Drona had loosed that
weapon in the great battle, the wind died down and the quarters grew clear. Then
the hero, the son of Pandu, made the Trigarta's crowds of chariots in battle
lose heart, turn away and lose their courage.

"Then King Duryodhana, Kripa the best of chariot fighters, Ashvatthama, Shalya,
Sudakshina of Kamboja, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, and Bahlika with the
Bahlikas blocked Partha's way on every side with a great line of chariots. So
too Bhagadatta and the very strong Shrutayu blocked Bhima's way with a force of
elephants. Bhurishravas, Shala and Subala's son, lord of the people, quickly
held off Madri's two sons with floods of arrows of many kinds. Bhishma, with all
the soldiers of Dhritarashtra's son, came up against Yudhishthira and surrounded
him on every side.

"Seeing the force of elephants coming, Partha Wolf-belly licked the corners of
his mouth like the king of beasts in the forest. Then the best of chariot
fighters took up his club in the great battle, leapt quickly from his chariot
and terrified your army. Seeing him club in hand, the elephant riders surrounded
Bhimasena in battle on every side, ready. Having reached the middle of the
elephants, the Pandava shone like the sun in the middle of a great net of
clouds. The bull of the Pandavas blew away that force of elephants with his
club, as the wind blows away a great, matchless, spreading net of clouds. Being
killed by the strong Bhimasena, the elephants made a cry of distress in battle,
like thundering clouds. The elephants' tusks tore him in many places there.
Partha shone at the head of the battle like a flowering ashoka tree. Seizing an
elephant by the tusk, he made it tuskless. With that same tusk he struck the
elephant on its frontal globe and brought it down in battle, like Death with his
staff in his hand. Carrying his blood-wet club, his skin smeared with fat and
marrow, wearing blood for armlets, he looked like Rudra. So the great elephants
that were left after the killing fled in all directions, king, crushing their
own army. With those great elephants running on every side, bull of the
Bharatas, Duryodhana's whole army turned its back again."

# The Island of Jambu

When he had said this, Vyasa went away. And when Dhritarashtra had heard it, he
fell to brooding. He brooded for a while, sighing again and again, and then he
questioned Sanjaya, who was steadfast of mind.

"Sanjaya, these kings are heroes who delight in war," he said. "They kill one
another with weapons of every kind. These lords of the earth have given up their
lives for the earth, and they will not stop killing, filling up Yama's house.
They want lordship over the earth, and they cannot bear one another. I think the
earth must have many virtues. Tell me about it, Sanjaya. Many thousands,
millions, hundreds of millions, tens of millions of the world's heroes have come
together in the Kuru country. I want to hear truly the size of the lands and the
cities they have come from, Sanjaya. You have the eye of knowledge and the lamp
of divine understanding, by the grace of that brahmin seer Vyasa, whose power is
beyond measure."

"Wise one, I will tell you as far as I understand the virtues of the earth,"
said Sanjaya. "Look at it with the eye of the teachings. I bow to you, bull
among the Bharatas.

"There are two kinds of beings here, the moving and the fixed. The moving are
born in three ways: from eggs, from sweat and from the womb. Of all the moving,
king, the best are those born from the womb. And of those born from the womb,
the first are men and the animals. They take many forms, and there are fourteen
kinds. Seven live in the forest and seven live in the village. The lion, the
tiger, the boar, the buffalo, the elephant, the bear and the monkey are the
seven of the forest, king. The cow, the goat, man, the sheep, the horse, the
mule and the ass are known to the good as the seven animals of the village.
These fourteen animals of the village and the forest, king, are the ones named
in the Veda, and on them the sacrifices rest. Among those of the village man is
the best, and among those of the forest the lion. All beings live on one
another.

"The fixed are said to be those that grow from the ground, and there are only
five kinds: trees, shrubs, creepers, climbing plants and the grasses, whose pith
is their bark. These make nineteen. With the five great elements they make
twenty-four, and this is called the Gayatri, honoured by the world. Whoever
truly knows this holy Gayatri, which has every virtue, best of the Bharatas, is
not lost to the worlds. For everything is born on the earth and everything
perishes on the earth. The earth is the ground of all beings, and the earth is
their last refuge. Whoever has the earth has the whole world, moving and fixed.
That is why kings covet it and kill one another."

"Tell me the names of the rivers and the mountains, Sanjaya," said
Dhritarashtra, "and of the lands, and of the others who live on the earth. And
tell me in full the size of the whole earth, you who know measures, and its
forests, Sanjaya."

"Great king," said Sanjaya, "the wise say that these five great elements, taken
together, make up the whole world, and that they are equal. Earth, water, wind,
fire and space: each has more qualities than the next, and earth has the most.
Sound, touch, form, taste and smell, the fifth, are the qualities of earth, as
the seers who know the truth have said. Water has four qualities, king, for
there is no smell in it. Fire has three: sound, touch and form. Wind has sound
and touch, and space has sound alone. These five qualities, king, are in the
five great elements, and they are in all the worlds, on which the worlds rest.
While there is balance, they do not overwhelm one another. But when they fall
out of balance with one another, then creatures with bodies arise in bodies, and
not otherwise. They perish in order and they are born in order. All of them are
beyond measure, and that is their lordly form. Everywhere elements made of the
five are seen, and men state their measures by reasoning. But what cannot be
thought should not be settled by reasoning. What is beyond nature is the mark of
the unthinkable.

"I will tell you of Sudarshana island, joy of the Kurus. That island is round,
great king, shaped like a wheel. It is covered with rivers and waters, and with
mountains like clouds, with cities of every kind and lovely lands, with trees
full of flowers and fruit. It is rich in wealth and grain, and the salt ocean
surrounds it on every side. As a man may see his own face in a mirror, so
Sudarshana island is seen in the disc of the moon. Two parts of it are a pipal
tree, and two parts are a great hare, and it is filled out on every side with
all kinds of plants. The rest is to be known as water. That is the island in
brief."

"You have told me the island in brief," said Dhritarashtra. "Now tell it to me
at length, Sanjaya. Tell me the measure of the space of earth that is seen in
the hare-marked moon, and then you can tell me of the pipal."

When the king had said this, said Vaishampayana, Sanjaya answered him.

"Great king, there are six jewel mountains running from east to west, and at
both ends they plunge into the eastern and western oceans: Himavat, Hemakuta,
the great mountain Nishadha, Nila, made of beryl, Shveta, bright as silver, and
the mountain called Shringavat, veined with every mineral. These mountains,
king, are the haunt of siddhas and charanas. The distance between them is
thousands of yojanas. Between them are holy lands, and those are the varshas,
Bharata. Beings of every kind live in them. This one is the varsha of Bharata.
Beyond it is Haimavata, and beyond Hemakuta is the one they call Harivarsha.

"South of Nila and north of Nishadha, great king, the mountain called Malyavat
runs east. Beyond Malyavat is the mountain Gandhamadana. Between them, round,
stands Meru, the mountain of gold. It shines like the young sun, like a fire
without smoke. It goes down sixteen thousand yojanas, they say, and it rises
eighty-four thousand yojanas high, lord of the earth. It stands covering the
worlds above, below and across. Around it lie these four islands, lord, Bharata:
Bhadrashva, Ketumala, Jambudvipa and the Northern Kurus, the refuge of those who
have gained merit.

"There, they say, the bird Sumukha, son of Suparna, saw that the crows were
golden and thought, 'Meru makes no difference between the best birds, the
middling and the lowest. So I shall leave it.'

"The sun, lord of the lights, goes round it always, and so do the moon with the
stars and the wind, keeping it on their right. That mountain, great king, is
full of divine flowers and fruit, and covered with fine houses all of gold.
There, king, hosts of gods, gandharvas, asuras and rakshasas play on the
mountain with troops of apsarases, always. There Brahma, Rudra and Shakra, lord
of the gods, meet and sacrifice with many kinds of sacrifice and rich gifts.
Tumburu, Narada, Vishvavasu, Haha and Huhu come to the best of the immortals and
praise them with hymns, lord. The great Seven Seers and Kashyapa the Prajapati
go there at every change of the moon, good fortune to you. On its summit is
Ushanas Kavya, lord of the earth, with the daityas. These jewels are his, and
these jewel mountains are his. From him the blessed Kubera takes a quarter
share, and of that he gives a sixteenth part of the wealth to men.

"On its north side is a divine and lovely forest of karnikara trees, flowering
in every season, rising out of the rocks. There Pashupati himself, the blessed
maker of beings, takes his pleasure with Uma, surrounded by divine beings. He
wears a garland of karnikara flowers that hangs down to his feet, and he shines
with his three eyes as if three suns had risen. The siddhas of fierce austerity,
of good vows and true speech, see him there. For Maheshvara cannot be seen by
the wicked.

"From the peak of that mountain, lord of men, falls a stream like milk, thirty
arm-spans across, with the terrible noise of thunder. It is the holy and lovely
Ganga Bhagirathi, visited by the holiest, and it falls with endless force into
the lovely Lake of the Moon. That holy lake, like an ocean, was made by her.
Long ago, when not even mountains could hold her, Maheshvara held her on his
head for a hundred thousand years.

"On the west side of Meru, lord of the earth, is Ketumala, and there too is a
very great forest of jambu trees, like Nandana. Life there lasts ten thousand
years, Bharata. The men are the colour of gold and the women are like apsarases.
The people born there are free of sickness and sorrow, always glad at heart, and
bright as refined gold. On the peaks of Gandhamadana, Kubera, lord of the
guhyakas, takes his pleasure with the rakshasas, surrounded by troops of
apsarases. On the far foothills of Gandhamadana, in the Aparagandikas, life
lasts at most eleven thousand years. There, king, the men are dark, full of
energy and strength, and all the women are the colour of lotus petals and lovely
to look at.

"Beyond Nila is Shveta, and beyond Shveta is Hairanyaka. Beyond Shringavat is
the varsha called Airavata. The two varshas at the south and the north are
shaped like bows, great king. Ilavrita is in the middle, and there are five
varshas besides. Each varsha to the north surpasses the one before in its
virtues: in length of life, in health, and in dharma, pleasure and wealth. The
beings in those varshas have all these, Bharata. So, great king, the earth is
heaped with mountains.

"Hemakuta is the very great mountain called Kailasa, where King Vaishravana
takes his pleasure with the guhyakas. North of Kailasa, towards the mountain
Mainaka, is Hiranyashringa, a very great divine mountain made of jewels. Beside
it is the great, divine and lovely lake called Bindusaras, with golden sand,
where King Bhagiratha lived for many years after he had seen Ganga, the
Bhagirathi. There are sacrificial posts made of jewels there, and altars of
gold. There the thousand-eyed god of great fame sacrificed and won success.
There the eternal lord of beings, of fierce splendour, who created all the
worlds, is worshipped, surrounded by the beings gathered round him: Nara and
Narayana, Brahma, Manu, and Sthanu the fifth.

"There the goddess who goes by three paths first comes to rest. Leaving the
world of Brahma, she becomes sevenfold: Vasvokasara, Nalini, holy Sarasvati,
Jambunadi, Sita, Ganga, and Sindhu the seventh. This is the lord's own
arrangement, beyond thought and of divine purpose. There they hold a sacrificial
session at the end of a thousand ages. Sarasvati is seen in some places and not
in others. These are the seven divine Gangas, famous in the three worlds.

"Rakshasas live on Himavat, guhyakas on Hemakuta, snakes and nagas on Nishadha,
and ascetics at Gokarna. Shveta is said to be the home of the gods and the
asuras. The gandharvas live on the mountain Nishadha, and the brahmin seers on
Nila, king. Shringavat, great king, is where the ancestors come and go. These,
great king, are the seven varshas, each in its part. Beings live in them, moving
and fixed. Their wealth, divine and human, is of many kinds. It cannot be
counted, but one who wishes to prosper should believe in it.

"As for the divine shape of the hare that you ask me about, king: on either side
of the hare are the two varshas, the southern and the northern. The ears are
Nagadvipa and Kashyapadvipa. The head, copper-coloured, is the lovely Malaya
mountain, king. That is the second part of the island, which is seen in the
shape of a hare."

"Tell me in full, wise Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "of the north side of Meru
and the east, and of the mountain Malyavat."

"South of Nila and on the north side of Meru, king, are the Northern Kurus, holy
and haunted by siddhas," said Sanjaya. "There the trees bear honey-sweet fruit
and are always in flower and fruit. Their flowers are fragrant and their fruits
are juicy. Some of the trees there, lord of the people, bear fruit that gives
whatever one wishes. Others, lord of men, are called milk-trees. They always
yield a milk of six tastes, like nectar, and they bear clothes, and ornaments in
their fruits. The whole ground is made of jewels, with fine golden sand,
pleasant to the touch everywhere and free of mud, lord of the people. All the
people born there have fallen from the world of the gods, and they are all alike
in looks and virtues, in every circumstance. They are born in pairs, and the
women are like apsarases. They drink the milk of the milk-trees, which is like
nectar. The pair born together grows up together, alike in looks and virtues and
dressed alike, each devoted to the other, like cakravaka birds, lord. Free of
sickness and sorrow and always glad at heart, great king, they live for eleven
thousand years, and they never leave each other. Birds called Bharundas,
sharp-beaked and very strong, carry off the dead and throw them into caves.
That, king, is the Northern Kurus in brief.

"Now I will tell you truly of the east side of Meru. At the head of Bhadrashva,
lord of the people, is the Bhadrasala forest, and the great tree Kalamra. The
Kalamra, great king, is a fine tree, always in flower and fruit, and it stands a
yojana high, visited by siddhas and charanas. The men there are white, full of
energy and very strong. The women are the colour of water lilies, beautiful and
lovely to see. They shine like the moon and are the colour of the moon, with
faces like the full moon and bodies cool as the moon, and they are skilled in
dance and song. Life there lasts ten thousand years, bull among the Bharatas.
They drink the juice of the Kalamra and stay young for ever.

"South of Nila and north of Nishadha stands the great, eternal jambu tree called
Sudarshana. It is holy, it bears fruit that gives whatever one wishes, and
siddhas and charanas visit it. The eternal island of Jambudvipa is named after
it. That king of trees is eleven hundred yojanas high, bull among the Bharatas,
and touches the sky, lord of men. Its fruits, bursting with juice, measure two
thousand five hundred cubits round. As they fall to the ground they make a great
noise, king, and pour out a juice like silver there. The juice of the fruit of
that jambu becomes a river, lord of the people, and goes round Meru, keeping it
on the right, and flows on to the Northern Kurus. The people there drink that
juice gladly, always, lord of the people. When they have drunk the juice of the
fruit, old age does not trouble them. The gold called Jambunada, the ornament of
the gods, is found there, and the people born there are the colour of the young
sun.

"On the peak of Malyavat blazes the fire called Samvartaka, the fire of the end
of time, bull among the Bharatas. On the eastern peak of Malyavat is
Purvantagandika, and Malyavat stretches fifty thousand yojanas. The people born
there look like fine silver. They have all fallen from the world of Brahma, and
all of them speak of Brahman. They practise austerity and keep their seed drawn
upwards. To protect beings, they enter the sun. Sixty-six thousand of them go
ahead of Aruna, surrounding the sun. For sixty-six thousand years they are
scorched by the heat of the sun, and then they enter the disc of the moon."

**Divergences.**

* **The earth in the moon.** Sanjaya says Sudarshana island is seen in the moon's disc "as a man may see his own face in a mirror", two parts a pipal tree and two parts a hare (6.6.15–16; 6.7.52–53). This is the text's own cosmography and is told as Sanjaya tells it.
* **Why the kings fight.** Dhritarashtra's question is why kings will die for the earth (6.5.3–5). Sanjaya's answer ends, "Whoever has the earth has the whole world … That is why kings covet it and kill one another" (6.5.21).

Bhishma charges Arjuna, and Abhimanyu holds off five great chariot fighters alone; Dhrishtadyumna kills the son of Samyamani with his mace; Dhritarashtra laments that fate is stronger than effort; Abhimanyu kills the king of Magadha, and Bhima, on foot with his mace, destroys the Magadha's elephant division; Bhima faints from Duryodhana's arrow, recovers, and kills eight of Dhritarashtra's sons; Bhagadatta's elephant drives back the Pandavas and his arrow makes Bhima faint again; Ghatotkacha appears on an Airavata made by magic, and Bhishma, not wanting to fight the rakshasa with his army tired, calls the halt; that night Duryodhana grieves for his brothers.

# The Fourth Day

"When the night had passed, Bharata," said Sanjaya, "great Bhishma, angry, went
out at the head of the Bharata armies against his enemies, surrounded by his
whole force. Drona, Duryodhana and Bahlika, Durmarshana and Chitrasena, and
mighty Jayadratha with floods of troops, and other kings followed him on every
side. Surrounded by those great chariot fighters, bright with power and courage,
chiefs among kings, he shone, best of kings, like the Wielder of the Thunderbolt
among the gods. At the head of that army great banners, deep red, yellow, black
and white, rode on the shoulders of great elephants and shone as they tossed in
the wind. With King Shantanava, the great chariot fighters, the elephants and
the horses, that army looked like the sky in the rains, full of clouds with
lightning and thunder. Then the very fierce army of the Kurus, guarded by
Shantanava, went quickly out to battle against Arjuna, like the terrible rush of
a river.

"Great Arjuna, whose banner is the king of monkeys, saw from far off that array
like a great cloud. Its core was hidden by savage elephants of many kinds, and
its wings were floods of elephants, horses, foot soldiers and chariots. The bull
among men went out at the head of the army in his chariot with its banner and
its white horses, its protecting frame in place, set on killing all his enemies'
young men. Your Kauravas and your sons lost heart in the battle when they saw
the monkey-bannered chariot, well fitted, with its yoke and seat, and the bull
of the Yadus minding it in the battle. Your men saw that king of arrays, strewn
with four times four thousand savage elephants, guarded by Kiritin with his
weapons raised, the great chariot fighter of the world, drawing his bow. The
Chedi chiefs, with the chiefs of the Panchalas, went to their places and stood
there as in the array the Dharma king, joy of the Kauravas, had made on the day
before.

"Then thousands of drums, beaten with great force, boomed out in the battle, and
the sound of conches and kettledrums with lion roars rose in all the ranks. Then
the heroes stretched their bows, fitted with arrows, with a great noise, and in
a moment it drowned the sound of the drums and tabors and the great noise of the
conches. The sky was filled with the sound of the conches, and there was a net
of earthly dust stirred up and flying. It looked as if a great canopy had been
stretched over it. When they saw that, the heroes charged at once.

"A chariot fighter struck by a chariot fighter fell with his charioteer, his
horses, his chariot and his banner. An elephant struck by an elephant fell, and
a foot soldier struck by a foot soldier. Troops of fine horses, wheeling and
charging, wounded by arrows and struck with lances and swords by troops of fine
horses, were marvellous to see. Shields studded with gold stars, thrown out by
heroes, split by axes, lances and swords, fell to the earth. Some chariot
fighters fell with their charioteers, broken by elephants' tusks and fine
trunks. Bull elephants too fell to the earth, killed by the arrows of bulls
among chariot fighters. Men sank to the ground when they heard the cries of the
young horsemen and foot soldiers, beaten down by the rush of the floods of
elephants and struck by their tusks and bodies.

"It was a time of great terror. The horsemen and young foot soldiers were being
struck, and the elephants, horses and chariots were thrown into confusion. Then
Bhishma saw the one whose banner is the king of monkeys surrounded by great
chariot fighters. The son of Shantanu, whose palm banner rose five palms high,
charged Kiritin with the speed and strength of his fine horses. Kiritin's path
blazed with the thunderbolts of his great weapons and arrows. In the same way
the warriors led by Drona charged Indra's son, who was like Shakra himself:
Kripa and Shalya and Vivimshati, Duryodhana and Somadatta's son, king.

"Then Arjuna's son Abhimanyu, a hero who knew every weapon, in his bright golden
armour, came out from the front of the chariot ranks and charged swiftly at all
of them. Arjuna's son, whose deeds nothing could hold back, struck down the
great weapons of those great chariot fighters. He shone like the blessed fire on
the altar, with its garland of flames fed by great mantras. Then Bhishma, whose
spirit did not fail, quickly made a river with water of blood and foam in that
slaughter of his enemies, got past Subhadra's son and came at Partha, the great
chariot fighter. Then Kiritin, with his garland, laughing, with the great noise
of the Gandiva's twang, marvellous to see, destroyed that net of great weapons
with a net of vipatha arrows. Then great Arjuna, whose banner is the king of
monkeys and whose deeds nothing could hold back, quickly rained floods and nets
of arrows and bright broad arrows on Bhishma, the best of all bowmen. So the
world, the Kurus and the Srinjayas watched that single fight between Bhishma and
Dhananjaya, those two best of good men, with the terrible noise of their bows,
neither giving way.

"Drona's son, Bhurishravas, Shalya and Chitrasena, sir, and the son of Samyamani
fought Subhadra's son. People saw him alone, in close fight with those five
tigers among men of great power, like a lion cub among elephants. No one was
equal to Arjuna's son in aim, in courage or in valour, in weapons or in speed.
When Partha saw his son, that tamer of enemies, showing such valour in the
battle, he roared like a lion. Seeing your grandson harassing that army, lord of
the people, your men surrounded him on every side, lord of kings. Undaunted, the
son of Subhadra, who humbled his enemies, went out against the army of
Dhritarashtra's sons with force and fire. His great bow, bright as the sun, was
seen in the battle as he fought the enemy, always moving in the ways of speed.

"He pierced Drona's son with one arrow and Shalya with five, and brought down
the banner of Samyamani's son with eight. With a sharp feathered arrow like a
snake he struck away the great spear with a golden shaft that Somadatta's son
had hurled. Holding off hundreds of Shalya's very terrible arrows as Shalya shot
them, Arjuna's heir killed his horses in the battle. Bhurishravas and Shalya,
Drona's son, Samyamani and Shala, angry as they were, did not come on against
him, for they feared the strength of his arms.

"Then, lord of kings, the Trigartas and the Madras with the Kekayas, thirty-five
thousand of them, came at your son's command. They were the chief knowers of the
science of the bow, unconquerable by enemies in battle. They surrounded Kiritin
and his son, wanting to kill him. The Panchala commander, conqueror of enemies,
saw the father and son, those bulls among chariot fighters, hemmed in there,
king. He was surrounded by many thousands of elephants and chariots, and by
horses and foot soldiers in hundreds of thousands. The scorcher of enemies
stretched his bow in anger, urged on his army and went against the Madraka army
and the Kekayas. Guarded by that famous man of the strong bow, the army went out
to fight, its chariots, elephants and horses all in use, and it shone.

"With Arjuna going at the front, joy of the Kurus, the Panchala struck Kripa,
Sharadvat's son, with three arrows at the collarbone. Then, glad, he killed the
Madrakas with ten arrows each, and alone he killed Kritavarma's horse with a
broad arrow. The scorcher of enemies also killed Damana, heir of great Paurava,
with a broad-headed iron arrow.

"Then the son of Samyamani pierced the Panchala, mad in battle, with thirty
arrows, and his charioteer with ten. Badly wounded, the great archer, licking
the corners of his mouth, cut his bow with a very sharp broad arrow. Then he
quickly struck him with twenty-five arrows, and killed his horses and both the
drivers who guarded his wheels, king. Standing in his chariot with its horses
killed, bull of the Bharatas, the son of Samyamani saw the son of great
Panchala. He took up a very terrible fine iron sword and went quickly on foot at
Drupada's son, who stood in his chariot. The Pandavas and Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's son, saw him coming like a great flood, like a snake falling from the
sky, whirling his shield and sword, like Death let loose by Time. He seemed to
blaze with the flame of his weapon, with the stride of an elephant in rut. He
ran against the Panchala's son with a sharp sword in his hand and a shield
against arrows. He passed the rush of arrows and came up close to the chariot.
Then the commander, in his anger, quickly split his head with his mace. As he
fell dead, king, the bright sword and the shield dropped from his hand to the
ground. By killing him with the head of his mace, the great son of the Panchala
king, of terrible valour, won great glory.

"When that prince, a great archer and great chariot fighter, was killed, sir, a
great cry of 'Alas!' rose from your army. Then Samyamani, in anger, seeing his
son killed, charged swiftly at the Panchala, mad in battle. All the kings, the
Kurus and the Pandavas, watched those two heroes, best of chariot fighters, meet
in battle. Then Samyamani, killer of enemy heroes, in anger struck Prishata's
son with three arrows, as men strike a great elephant with goads. In the same
way Shalya, who shines in battle, struck brave Prishata's son in the chest in
anger. Then the fight went on."

"I think fate is stronger than human effort, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra,
"since my son's army is being killed by the Pandava army. You always tell me, my
son, that my men are being killed, and you always tell me that the Pandavas are
calm and glad. Today, Sanjaya, you tell me my men are without manhood, fallen,
falling and killed. They fight as hard as they can, striving for victory, and
still the Pandavas win and my men are beaten. I have heard again and again, my
son, sharp sorrows caused by Duryodhana, many of them and hard to bear. I see no
way, Sanjaya, by which the Pandavas might be beaten, or by which my men might
win victory in battle."

"Hear, king, with a steady mind," said Sanjaya, "the destruction of men's
bodies, of elephants, horses and chariots. The great wrong is yours alone.

"Dhrishtadyumna, harassed by Shalya with nine arrows, harassed the lord of the
Madras in anger with iron arrows. There we saw a marvellous feat of Prishata's
son: he quickly held back Shalya, who shines in battle. No one could see any
difference between those two angry men in the battle. For a moment the fight
between them seemed even. Then Shalya cut Dhrishtadyumna's bow in the battle
with a yellow, sharp broad arrow, great king. Then he covered him with a shower
of arrows, Bharata, as rain clouds cover a mountain at the coming of the rains.

"When Dhrishtadyumna was hard pressed, Abhimanyu in anger charged swiftly at the
Madra king's chariot. Reaching the chariot of the lord of the Madras, Arjuna's
son, in great fury, whose spirit had no measure, pierced Artayani with three
arrows. Then your men, king, wanting to take Arjuna's son in the battle, quickly
surrounded the Madra king's chariot and stood there. Duryodhana and Vikarna,
Duhshasana and Vivimshati, Durmarshana and Duhsaha, Chitrasena and Durmukha,
Satyavrata, bless you, and Purumitra, Bharata: these stood in the battle
guarding the chariot of the lord of the Madras.

"Bhimasena in anger, and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, the sons of Draupadi
and Abhimanyu, and the two Pandava sons of Madri came against them, glad. They
loosed weapons of many kinds, lord of the people, each wanting to kill the
other. They met in battle, king, through your bad counsel. In that terrible
battle of ten chariots, the chariot fighters of your side and theirs became
onlookers. Loosing weapons of many kinds and roaring at one another, the great
chariot fighters fought. All of them, set on it, full of fury, eager to kill one
another, loosing great weapons, fell on each other in their anger.

"Duryodhana, in anger, quickly pierced Dhrishtadyumna hard with four sharp
arrows in the great battle. Durmarshana pierced him with twenty, Chitrasena with
five, Durmukha with nine arrows and Duhsaha with seven, Vivimshati with five and
Duhshasana with three. Prishata's son, scorcher of enemies, pierced each of them
back with twenty-five, lord of kings, showing the speed of his hands. Abhimanyu
pierced Satyavrata and Purumitra with ten arrows each in the battle, Bharata.
The sons of Madri, the joy of their mother, covered their mother's brother with
flights of arrows in the battle, and it was a marvel. Then Shalya, great king,
struck back his sister's sons, the best of chariot fighters, who were eager to
return blow for blow, with many arrows. Covered as they were, the two sons of
Madri did not move.

"Then, seeing Duryodhana, mighty Bhimasena, the Pandava, took up his mace,
wanting to put an end to the quarrel. When they saw strong-armed Bhimasena with
his mace raised, like Kailasa with its peaks, your sons ran in fear. Then
Duryodhana in anger urged on the Magadha with a division of ten thousand swift
elephants. Putting the Magadha in front, he went against Bhimasena. Seeing that
division of elephants coming at him, Vrikodara got down from his chariot, mace
in hand, roaring like a lion. Grasping his huge, heavy mace, made of the stuff
of mountains, he ran at the elephant division like Death with his mouth open.
Mighty, strong-armed Bhimasena moved through the battle, killing elephants with
his mace like Vasava with his thunderbolt. At his great roar, which shook their
minds and hearts, the elephants huddled together and writhed as Bhima roared.

"Then the sons of Draupadi and Subhadra's son, the great chariot fighter, Nakula
and Sahadeva, and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, guarded Bhima's back. They
charged the elephants with a shower of arrows, raining on them as clouds rain on
mountains. With razor arrows, horseshoe arrows and broad arrows, yellow and
crescent-headed, the Pandavas brought down the heads of the elephant fighters.
With falling heads, with adorned arms and hands still holding goads, it looked
like a shower of stones. The elephant fighters, their heads cut off, were seen
on the elephants' shoulders like trees with their tops broken on the peaks of
hills. We saw other great elephants killed by Dhrishtadyumna, fallen and
falling, struck by great Prishata's son.

"Then the Magadha king drove an elephant like Airavata at Subhadra's son's
chariot in the battle. Seeing the Magadha's fine elephant coming at him, heroic
Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, killed it with a single arrow. As the
elephant sank, Arjuna's son, conqueror of enemy cities, cut off the king's head
with a silver-feathered broad arrow.

"Bhimasena the Pandava too plunged into that elephant division and moved through
the battle crushing elephants, as Indra crushes mountains. In that battle we saw
elephants killed by Bhimasena with a single blow, like mountains struck by the
thunderbolt. Elephants like mountains were killed with broken tusks, broken
temples, broken legs, broken backs and broken foreheads. Other elephants in the
battle trumpeted and sank, turned and ran, broken and terrified, voiding urine
and dung. In Bhimasena's paths we saw dead elephants like mountains, and others
groaning. Other great elephants, with their foreheads split, vomited blood and
went down to the ground, reeling, like hills on the earth.

"His limbs smeared with fat and blood, soaked in grease and marrow, Bhima moved
through the battle like Death with his staff in his hand. Carrying that mace wet
with the elephants' blood, Vrikodara was terrible and frightening, like the
Bearer of Pinaka. The elephants left alive, crushed by angry Bhimasena, suddenly
ran, trampling your army. Great archers, chariot fighters led by Subhadra's son,
guarded that fighting hero, as the immortals guard the Wielder of the
Thunderbolt. Carrying his mace smeared with blood, drenched in the blood of
elephants, Bhimasena looked like the Ender himself, cruel of spirit. We saw him
wielding his mace in all directions, Bharata, dancing like Shankara dancing.
Great king, we saw his heavy, killing mace, like Yama's staff, with a noise like
Indra's thunderbolt, mixed with hair and marrow and smeared with blood, like the
Pinaka of angry Rudra striking down beasts. As a herdsman drives a herd of
cattle with his stick, so Bhima drove the elephant division with his mace.
Struck by the mace and by arrows on every side, your elephants ran, crushing
their own ranks. Having scattered the elephants as a great wind scatters clouds,
Bhima stood in that tumult like the holder of the trident in the burning ground.

"When that elephant division had been destroyed, your son Duryodhana urged on
all his armies, saying, 'Kill Bhimasena!' Then, at your son's command, all the
armies ran at Bhimasena, making terrible noises. That flood of troops, without
end, hard even for the gods to bear, came on like the sea at the full moon, very
hard to cross. It was full of chariots, elephants and horses, loud with conches
and drums, endless and boundless, a lake still with kings. Bhimasena held back
that unshakable sea of an army in battle as the shore holds back the great
ocean. There, king, we saw a wonder, hard to believe and marvellous, a deed of
Bhimasena's beyond the human. Unshaken, Bhimasena beat back with his mace the
whole earth that had risen against him, with its horses, chariots and elephants.
Having held back those floods of troops with his mace, Bhima, best of chariot
fighters, stood firm in that tumult like Mount Meru.

"In that most terrible, very cruel time, his brothers and sons, Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's son, the sons of Draupadi, Abhimanyu and Shikhandi, the great chariot
fighter, did not leave mighty Bhimasena when fear arose. Then, grasping a heavy,
huge mace of hard iron, he killed your warriors like Death with his staff in his
hand. Crushing troops of chariots and troops of horses, the mighty one moved
through the battle like fire at the end of an age. He killed everyone in the
battle, like Time at the end of an age. The Pandava dragged along nets of
chariots with the rush of his thighs and crushed all the elephants, as an
elephant crushes reed beds. He crushed chariot fighters from their chariots and
elephant fighters from their elephants, horsemen from their horses' backs and
foot soldiers on the ground. With men, elephants and horses killed here and
there, that field of battle became like Death's slaughterhouse. They saw
Bhimasena's cruel, killing mace, like the Pinaka of angry Rudra striking down
beasts, fierce as Yama's staff, with a noise like Indra's thunderbolt. As great
Kaunteya whirled his mace, his form was very terrible, like Time at the end of
an age. Seeing him put that great army to flight again and again, like Death
coming, everyone lost heart. Wherever the Pandava looked with his mace raised,
Bharata, there all the armies broke.

"Bhishma saw Vrikodara, unconquered, of terrible deeds, holding his great mace,
tearing the armies apart with his flood of troops and swallowing the ranks like
Death with his mouth open. He came at him at once, in his great chariot that
thundered like a cloud and shone like the sun. He covered him with a shower of
arrows as Parjanya covers the earth with rain. Seeing Bhishma coming like that,
like Death with his mouth open, strong-armed Bhima went out to meet him in his
fury.

"At that moment Satyaki, true to his word, hero of the Shinis, fell upon the
grandfather, killing enemies with his strong bow and making your son's army
tremble. As he went with his silver horses, blowing out arrows with his strong
bow, none of your troops could stop him then, Bharata. Alambusa, son of
Rishyashringa, best of kings, pierced him with sharp-pointed arrows. The
grandson of Shini, the hero, pierced him back with four and drove on in his
chariot. They saw the best of the Vrishnis come on, wheeling in the middle of
his enemies, turning back the bulls of the Kurus and roaring again and again in
the battle. They could not stop that best of men, burning like the sun at
midday. There was no one there, king, who was not dismayed, except Somadatta's
son. Bhurishravas, Somadatta's son, took up his bow of fierce speed, Bharata,
and when he saw his own chariots being driven off, he went out to meet Satyaki,
wanting to fight him.

"Then Bhurishravas, king, in great anger, pierced Satyaki with nine arrows, as
men strike a great elephant with goads. And Satyaki, whose spirit had no
measure, covered the Kaurava with straight arrows while all the world watched.
Then King Duryodhana, surrounded by his brothers, set on it, surrounded
Somadatta's son on every side in the battle. In the same way all the Pandavas,
of great power, surrounded fiery Satyaki in the battle and stood by him.

"Bhimasena, in anger, raised his mace, Bharata, and with many thousands of
chariots, full of fury and resentment, surrounded all your sons led by
Duryodhana. Your son Nandaka pierced mighty Bhimasena with six sharp arrows,
feathered with heron plumes and whetted on stone. Duryodhana, in anger, struck
mighty Bhimasena in the chest with three sharp arrows in the battle. Then
mighty, strong-armed Bhima climbed into his own fine chariot and said to
Vishoka, 'These brave, mighty great chariot fighters, the sons of Dhritarashtra,
have risen in great anger to kill me in battle. I will kill them today while you
watch, no doubt of it. So hold my horses carefully in the battle, charioteer.'

"Having said this, Partha pierced your son Duryodhana with ten sharp arrows
adorned with gold, and struck back at Nandaka with three arrows in the middle of
his chest. Duryodhana pierced mighty Bhima with sixty, and pierced Vishoka with
three other very sharp arrows. And in the battle, king, the king cut Bhima's
shining bow at the grip with three sharp arrows, almost laughing. Bhima saw his
driver Vishoka hurt by sharp arrows from your son the bowman in the battle. He
could not bear it. In anger he grasped a divine bow to kill your son, great
king, bull of the Bharatas. In a rage Bhima took a razor arrow, fledged with
hair, and with it cut the king's fine bow. Blazing with anger, the king threw
away the broken bow and quickly took another, swifter one. He fitted a terrible
arrow, bright as Time and Death, and with it, in anger, struck Bhimasena in the
middle of the chest. Deeply pierced and in pain, Bhima sank down on the floor of
his chariot. Sitting there, he fainted.

"Seeing Bhima in pain, the great archers, the great chariot fighters of the
Pandavas led by Abhimanyu, could not bear it. Calmly they brought down a
tumultuous shower of fiercely bright weapons on your son's head. Then mighty
Bhimasena came to himself. He pierced Duryodhana with three arrows and then
again with five. The great archer, the Pandava, pierced Shalya with twenty-five
arrows with golden feathers, and Shalya, pierced, left the battle.

"Then fourteen of your sons came against Bhima: Senapati and Sushena, Jalasandha
and Sulochana, Ugra, Bhimaratha, Bhima, Bhimabahu and Alolupa, Durmukha and
Dushpradharsha, Vivitsu, Vikata and Sama. Loosing many arrows, their eyes red
with anger, they charged Bhimasena together and pierced him hard.

"Seeing your sons, mighty, heroic Bhimasena licked the corners of his mouth like
a wolf among cattle. The Pandava cut off Senapati's head with a razor arrow. He
split Jalasandha open and took him to Yama's house. Then he killed Sushena and
sent him to Death. With a broad arrow he brought down Ugra's head to the ground,
helmeted, bright as the moon, adorned with earrings. With seventy arrows Bhima
sent Bhimabahu to the other world, sir, with his horses, his banner and his
charioteer. Almost laughing, Bhimasena took the two brothers Bhima and
Bhimaratha, those fierce ones, to Yama's house, king. Then in the great battle
Bhima took Sulochana to Yama's house with a razor arrow, while all the armies
watched. Seeing Bhimasena's valour, the rest of your sons who were there,
tormented by fear of Bhima, ran in all directions as great Bhima killed them,
king.

"Then the son of Shantanu said to all the great chariot fighters, 'This Bhima,
angry in battle, is bringing down the great chariot fighters, the sons of
Dhritarashtra. He takes them as they come together, by rank, by age and by
courage. He is a fierce bowman. Crush him, kings!' Told this, all the soldiers
of Dhritarashtra's son charged mighty Bhimasena in anger.

"Bhagadatta, lord of the people, fell suddenly on the place where Bhima stood,
on an elephant in rut. As he fell on him in the battle, he hid Bhimasena with
arrows whetted on stone, as a cloud hides the sun. The great chariot fighters
led by Abhimanyu could not bear Bhima being hidden in the battle. Trusting in
the strength of their own arms, they surrounded Bhagadatta on every side with a
shower of arrows, and pierced his elephant everywhere with a shower of arrows.
Struck by that shower of weapons, well tempered and of many kinds, the elephant
of Pragjyotisha, king, ran at twice its pace. With blood gushing from it, it was
a sight to see in the battle, like a great cloud threaded with the rays of the
sun. Urged on by Bhagadatta, the elephant, streaming with rut, charged them all
at double speed, like Death let loose by Time, shaking the earth with its feet.
Seeing that huge shape, all the great chariot fighters thought it unbearable and
lost heart.

"Then the king, in anger, struck Bhimasena in the middle of the chest with a
straight arrow, tiger among men. Badly wounded by the king, the great archer,
the great chariot fighter, fainting in every limb, leaned on his flagstaff.
Seeing them afraid, and Bhimasena fainting, mighty Bhagadatta roared a great
roar.

"Then, king, seeing Bhima in that state, the terrible rakshasa Ghatotkacha in
his anger vanished on the spot. He made a cruel magic that increases the fear of
the timid, and in half the blink of an eye he was seen in a terrible form. He
rode Airavata, made by his own magic, and the other elephants of the quarters
followed him: Anjana, Vamana and shining Mahapadma. These three great elephants
were ridden by rakshasas. Huge, streaming rut from three places, king, full of
fire, courage and strength, they were of great might and valour. Ghatotkacha,
scorcher of enemies, urged his own elephant at Bhagadatta and his elephant,
wanting to kill him. The other elephants, urged on by those mighty rakshasas,
fell on him from the four quarters in great fury, four-tusked, and gored
Bhagadatta's elephant with their tusks. Pressed by those elephants, in pain and
tormented by arrows, it gave a very great scream, like the noise of Indra's
thunderbolt.

"Bhishma heard it scream that very terrible, fearful scream. He said to Drona
and to King Suyodhana, 'The great archer Bhagadatta is fighting in the battle
with the evil-hearted son of Hidimba, and he is in great trouble. The rakshasa
has great magic, and the king is very quick to anger. Those two men of great
valour have met, both like Time and Death. The great noise of the glad Pandavas
can be heard, and the very great scream of the frightened elephant. Let us go
there to protect the king, bless you. If he is not protected in the battle, he
will soon lose his life. Hurry, you of great valour. Why do we wait? A great,
cruel, hair-raising battle is going on. He is loyal, of good family, brave, a
leader of armies. It is right for us to rescue him, you who never fall.'

"Hearing those words of Bhishma, all the kings, led by Bharadvaja's son, went
together at their best speed to where he was, wanting to save Bhagadatta. Seeing
them go, the Panchalas and the Pandavas led by Yudhishthira followed their
enemies from behind. Seeing those armies, the mighty king of the rakshasas
roared a great roar, like the crack of a thunderbolt. Hearing that roar, and
seeing the elephants fighting, Bhishma, son of Shantanu, spoke again to
Bharadvaja's son.

"'I do not like this battle with the evil-hearted son of Hidimba. He is full of
strength and courage now, and he has helpers. He cannot be beaten in battle even
by the Wielder of the Thunderbolt himself. He has found his mark and he strikes
hard, and our mounts are tired. We have been wounded all over all day by the
Panchalas and the Pandavas. So I do not like fighting the Pandavas now that they
have won. Let the withdrawal be proclaimed today. Tomorrow we will fight the
enemy.'

"Hearing the grandfather's words, the Kauravas did so. Tormented by fear of
Ghatotkacha, they withdrew by a stratagem. When the Kauravas had turned back,
the victorious Pandavas gave lion roars, with the sound of conches and flutes.
So that day's battle went, bull of the Bharatas, between the Pandavas and the
Kurus, with Ghatotkacha at the front.

"Then the Kauravas went to their own camp at nightfall, king, ashamed, beaten by
the Pandavas. The sons of Pandu, great chariot fighters, their bodies wounded by
arrows, went to their camp glad at heart, great king. Bhimasena and Ghatotkacha
were at their head, and they honoured each other, full of great joy. They roared
many kinds of roars, mixed with the sound of instruments, and gave lion roars
mixed with the sound of conches. Roaring, shaking the earth and wounding your
son to the quick, sir, the great scorchers of enemies went to their camp at
nightfall.

"But King Duryodhana, wretched at his brothers' death, sat for a while lost in
thought, overcome with tears and grief. Then, when he had done all that the camp
needed as it should be done, he brooded, burning with grief and worn down by the
disaster to his brothers."

**Divergences.**

* **Eight of Duryodhana's brothers.** Bhima kills Senapati, Jalasandha, Sushena, Ugra, Bhimabahu, Bhima, Bhimaratha and Sulochana (6.60.27–32). A ninth, Vichitra, is added only in the rejected \*248. See ledger L-194.
* **Bhima's charioteer.** Here he is Vishoka (6.60.8–14), as he is later in the text (6.73.18; 8.54.15). On the second day the charioteer who brought Bhima a new chariot was named Ashoka (6.50.64, ch 17). Both readings are the CE's and are kept. The rejected \*244 has Bhima address "Ashoka" here.
* **Bhishma's reason for the halt.** He says Ghatotkacha is too strong now, and that the Kaurava mounts are tired (6.60.67–69). The rejected \*254 adds that the sun is setting and one cannot fight at night.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*218–\*255.

**Choices.**

* 6.56.7, 10 *vyāla*: "savage elephants", the military sense of the word.
* 6.56.21 *pañcatālocchritatālaketuḥ*: "whose palm banner rose five palms high".
* 6.56.26 *vipāṭha*: kept as "vipatha arrows", a kind of broad-headed arrow.
* 6.57.1, 8, 21, 24 *sāṃyamaneḥ putraḥ*: "the son of Samyamani", unnamed in the text; his father Samyamani fights on at 6.57.33–35. At 6.57.11 *sāṃyamaniḥ śalaḥ* is taken as two names, "Samyamani and Shala".
* 6.57.18 *so 'rjunaṃ pramukhe yāntam*: "with Arjuna going at the front". *śāradvata*: "Kripa, Sharadvat's son".
* 6.57.23 *pārṣṇisārathī*: "both the drivers who guarded his wheels".
* 6.58.14 *ārtāyani*: Shalya, "son of Ritayana", kept as the text's name for him.
* 6.58.20 *dāśarathe yuddhe*: "that battle of ten chariots", ten on each side.
* 6.59.26 *alambuso rājavarārśyaśṛṅgiḥ*: "Alambusa, son of Rishyashringa, best of kings".
* 6.60.51 *suprabhaḥ*: "shining", describing Mahapadma, since the text counts "these three".
* 6.60.70 *upāyenāpayānam*: "withdrew by a stratagem".

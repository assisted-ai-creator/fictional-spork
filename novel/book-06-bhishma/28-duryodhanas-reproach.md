# Duryodhana's Reproach

"Then, great king," said Sanjaya, "King Duryodhana, Shakuni, son of Subala, your
son Duhshasana and the unbeatable son of the charioteer met. They took counsel
on what they had to decide: how the sons of Pandu and their followers could be
beaten in battle.

"Then King Duryodhana spoke to all those counsellors, addressing the son of the
charioteer and mighty Subala's son. 'Drona, Bhishma, Kripa, Shalya and
Somadatta's son do not hold back the Parthas in the battle. I do not know why.
And the Parthas, not being killed themselves, are wearing down my army. So,
Karna, my forces are worn down and my weapons worn out in the battle. I have
been cheated by the brave Pandavas, whom even the gods cannot kill. I have come
into great doubt. How shall I fight the battle?'

"The son of the charioteer said to the lord of men, great king, 'Do not grieve,
best of the Bharatas. I will do what you want. Let Bhishma, son of Shantanu,
leave the great battle at once. When Ganga's son has turned away from the fight
and laid down his weapons, Bharata, I will kill the Parthas with all the
Somakas, while Bhishma watches in the battle. I swear it to you by the truth,
king. Bhishma always shows pity to the Pandavas, king. And Bhishma is not able
to beat these great chariot fighters in battle. Bhishma is proud in battle and
always loves battle. How will he beat the Pandavas when they stand together in
the fight, my friend? So go quickly from here to Bhishma's tent, pay him your
respects, and make him lay down his weapons in the battle, Bharata. When Bhishma
has laid down his weapons, you will see the Pandavas killed in the battle by me
alone, king, with their friends and kin.'

"When Karna said this, your son Duryodhana spoke to his brother Duhshasana
there. 'Duhshasana, see quickly that everything is ready for my escort on every
side.' Having said this, king, the lord of men said to Karna, 'I will pay my
respects to Bhishma, best of men, and gain his leave for the battle. Then I will
come back to you quickly, tamer of enemies. Then you, tiger among men, will
fight the battle.'

"Then your son went out quickly, lord of the people, with all his brothers, like
Shatakratu with the gods. His brother Duhshasana quickly helped that tiger among
kings, with the valour of a tiger, up on to his horse. Wearing armlets and a
fastened crown, with ornaments on his hands, the son of Dhritarashtra shone like
great Indra, great king. He was anointed with costly, fragrant sandal, the
colour of bhandi flowers and gleaming like gold. Dressed in clean clothes,
moving with the playful gait of a lion, the king shone like the sun in autumn
with its clear rays.

"As that tiger among men went to Bhishma's tent, great archers, bowmen of all
the world, and his brothers, great archers too, followed him as the thirty gods
follow Vasava. Some best of men rode horses, some elephants, and others went in
chariots, Bharata, surrounding him on every side. His friends, with weapons in
their hands, appeared together to guard the lord of the earth, like the
immortals round Shakra in heaven. Honoured by the Kurus, the great chariot
fighter of the Kauravas went to the dwelling of famous Ganga's son, king,
followed on every side by his brothers together.

"Raising at the proper times his right arm, like an elephant's trunk, trained in
arms, the destroyer of all enemies, he took the joined hands that people raised
to him on every side. He heard the sweet voices of people from many lands.
Praised by bards and heralds, the famous man, lord of the lords of all the
world, honoured them all in turn. His people surrounded the great man on every
side with golden lamps burning scented oil. Surrounded by those bright golden
lamps, the king shone like the moon among the great blazing planets. Men there
in coats and turbans, with canes and rattles in their hands, gently moved the
people aside on every side.

"When the king reached Bhishma's fine dwelling, he got down from his horse, went
to Bhishma and greeted him. Then he sat on a fine seat of gold, beautiful on
every side, covered with a costly spread. With joined hands, his throat choked
and his eyes full of tears, he said to Bhishma, 'Relying on you in battle,
destroyer of enemies, we would dare to beat even the gods and asuras with Indra.
How much more the heroic sons of Pandu with their friends and kin! So, son of
Ganga, you should show me pity, lord. Kill the heroic sons of Pandu, as great
Indra kills the danavas.

"'Before, strong-armed one, you said, "I will kill the Somakas, the Panchalas
with the Pandavas, and the Karushas," Bharata. Let those words be true. Kill the
Parthas who have come together, and the great archers of the Somakas. Be true to
your word, Bharata. If it is out of pity, king, or because you hate me, lord, or
because of my bad luck, that you protect the Pandavas, then give leave in battle
to Karna, who shines in battle. He will beat the Parthas in battle with their
friends and kin.'

"Having said this much, King Duryodhana, your son, said nothing more to Bhishma
of terrible valour.

"Pierced through by your son's arrows of words, the grandfather was filled with
great grief, but he said nothing unkind at all. He thought for a very long time,
full of grief and anger, breathing like a snake prodded with a stick. Then,
rolling his eyes in anger as if he would burn up the world with its gods, asuras
and gandharvas, the best of those who know the world spoke to your son in gentle
words.

"'Duryodhana, why do you pierce me like this with arrows of words? I am striving
as hard as I can, doing what you want, and offering up my life in battle for
your good alone. When the heroic Pandava beat Shakra in battle and fed Agni at
Khandava, that was proof enough. The gandharvas carried you off by force,
strong-armed one, and your brave brothers ran, lord, and so did Radha's son, the
son of the charioteer. When the son of Pandu freed you then, that was proof
enough. When he came out alone against all of us together, then, in Virata's
city, that was proof enough. When he beat angry Drona and me in battle, and took
the clothes of Karna, of you, of Drona's son and of Kripa, the great chariot
fighter, that was proof enough. When Partha beat the Nivatakavachas in battle,
whom even Vasava could not beat, that was proof enough.

"'For who can beat the Pandava, so fierce in battle? But in your delusion,
Suyodhana, you do not know what should be said and what should not. A man about
to die sees all the trees as golden. So you too, son of Gandhari, see everything
upside down. You made a great enmity yourself with the Pandavas and the
Srinjayas. Fight them in battle today. We shall see. Be a man.

"'But I will kill all the Somakas and the Panchalas who have come together,
tiger among men, sparing Shikhandi. Either I shall be killed by them in battle
and go to Yama's house, or I shall kill them in battle and give you joy. For
Shikhandi was born a woman first, in the king's house, and became a man by the
gift of a boon. She is Shikhandini, a woman. I will not kill her, Bharata, even
if I lose my life. She whom the Maker made first is Shikhandini, a woman. Sleep
in peace, son of Gandhari. Tomorrow I will fight a great battle, which people
will tell of as long as the earth stands.'

"Told this, your son went out, lord of the people. He bowed his head to his
elder and went to his own quarters. When he got there the king sent away the
crowd and quickly went in, that destroyer of enemies. And when he had gone in,
the lord of the earth passed that night."

**Divergences.**

* **No golden arrows.** A popular story says that on this night Bhishma charged five golden arrows to kill the five Pandavas, and that Arjuna later claimed them from Duryodhana. It is not in the CE or in Ganguli. What Bhishma promises here is to kill the Somakas and Panchalas, "sparing Shikhandi", or be killed by them (6.94.14–15). See ledger L-196.
* **Karna's condition.** Karna offers to fight once Bhishma has "laid down his weapons" (6.93.8–13). In Book 5 he had vowed not to fight "while Ganga's son lives" (5.165.27).
* **"Proof enough."** Bhishma's list of Arjuna's deeds is the text's refrain, *paryāptaṃ tan nidarśanam* (6.94.5–10). The rejected \*378 adds the cattle raid in more detail, and \*380 adds a hymn to Vasudeva as Arjuna's protector.
* **Shikhandi.** Bhishma speaks of Shikhandi as "Shikhandini, a woman", made so first by the Maker (6.94.16–17). The text's feminine forms are kept here, since they are his words.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*374–\*381.

* 6.93.21 *bhāṇḍīpuṣpa*: "bhandi flowers", a flower the colour of red sandal.
* 6.93.27 *dakṣiṇaṃ dakṣiṇaḥ kāle*: "raising at the proper times his right arm", to take the salutes.

# How Bhishma May Be Killed

"As they fought on," said Sanjaya, "the sun set and a terrible twilight came,
and then we could not see the battle. Then King Yudhishthira, Bharata, saw the
twilight, and saw his army being killed by Bhishma, killer of enemies, throwing
down its weapons, turning back, intent on flight. He saw Bhishma, furious in
battle, chasing the great chariot fighters, and the Somakas beaten, the great
chariot fighters without spirit. He thought for a long time and pondered, and
decided on the withdrawal. Then King Yudhishthira withdrew the troops, and your
troops too were withdrawn then. Having withdrawn the troops, the great chariot
fighters rested there, best of the Kurus, wounded all over in battle.

"Thinking about Bhishma's deeds in battle, and badly hurt by Bhishma, the
Pandavas found no peace. Bhishma too, having beaten the Pandavas and the
Srinjayas in battle, honoured by your sons and praised, Bharata, rested with the
Kurus, who were glad on every side. Then came the night that bewilders all
beings. At that terrible start of night the Pandavas with the Vrishnis, and the
Srinjayas, hard to attack, sat down to take counsel. The very strong ones,
skilled in deciding counsel, calmly took counsel about what was best for
themselves at that moment.

"Then King Yudhishthira, having taken counsel for a long time, king, looked at
Vasudeva and said, 'See the great-souled Bhishma of terrible courage, Krishna,
crushing my army as an elephant crushes reed beds. We cannot even look at the
great-souled one, licking at the armies like a growing fire. As the terrible
great serpent Takshaka is swollen with poison, so is Bhishma in battle, Krishna,
mighty, with sharp weapons. Angry Yama could be beaten, bow in hand in battle,
loosing sharp arrows. So could the king of the gods with the thunderbolt in his
hand, or Varuna with his noose, or the lord of wealth with his club. But Bhishma
in great anger cannot be beaten in the great battle.

"'Things being so, Krishna, I am sunk in an ocean of grief, through the weakness
of my own understanding, having met Bhishma in battle. I will go to the forest,
unassailable one. That would be better for me. I do not like war, Krishna, for
Bhishma kills us all the time. As a moth rushing at a blazing fire goes to death
alone, so I have come up against Bhishma. I have been led to ruin, Varshneya, by
my courage for the sake of the kingdom. My brave brothers are hard pressed by
arrows. Because of me, out of brotherly love, they fell from the kingdom, and
Krishna was tormented because of me, Killer of Madhu. I think life is worth
much, for life is hard to come by now. With the rest of my life I will practise
the highest dharma. If I and my brothers deserve your favour, Keshava, tell me
what is good for us, without going against my own dharma, Keshava.'

"Hearing his words, long and full of pity, Krishna answered, comforting
Yudhishthira. 'Son of Dharma, you who are true to your promise, do not despair.
Your brothers are brave, hard to conquer, killers of enemies. Arjuna and
Bhimasena are like the wind and fire in splendour, and Madri's sons are bold,
like lords of the thirty gods. Or appoint me, out of friendship, and I will
fight Bhishma, Pandava. Appointed by you, king, what would I not do in the great
battle? I will challenge Bhishma, the bull among men, and kill him in battle
while the sons of Dhritarashtra watch, if Phalguna does not want to. If you see
victory, king, once Bhishma is killed, Pandava, I will kill the old grandfather
of the Kurus today with a single chariot. See my courage in battle, king, like
great Indra's. I will bring him down from his chariot as he looses great
weapons. Whoever is the enemy of Pandu's sons is my enemy, no doubt. Those who
are for me are for you, and those who are mine are yours alone. Your brother is
my friend, kinsman and pupil. I would cut off my own flesh and give it for
Arjuna's sake, lord of the earth. And this tiger among men would give up his
life for my sake. This is our pact, son: we would save each other. So appoint
me, king of kings, while I am your island.

"'At Upaplavya, before, Partha promised in front of Uluka, "I will kill Ganga's
son." I must guard that word of wise Partha's. But if Partha gives me leave, I
will do it, no doubt. Or else this burden is Phalguna's in battle, and it has
its measure. He will kill Bhishma, conqueror of enemy cities, in battle. For
Partha, once he rises to it, could do even the impossible in battle. Arjuna
could kill in battle the thirty gods in arms together with the daityas and
danavas, let alone Bhishma, lord of men. Bhishma son of Shantanu, very strong as
he is, has gone the wrong way. His courage is gone and he has little life left.
Surely he does not know what he should do.'

"Yudhishthira said, 'It is as you say, strong-armed Madhava. All of these
together are not enough to hold off your charge. I shall surely get everything I
want, since you, very strong tiger among men, are my protector. With you as my
protector, Govinda, best of conquerors, I could conquer even the gods with Indra
in battle, let alone Bhishma in the great battle. But I cannot make you false,
for the sake of my own interest. Give your help without fighting, as you said,
Madhava. Bhishma made a pact with me, Madhava: "I will give counsel for your
sake, but I will not fight at all. I will fight for Duryodhana. This is true,
lord." For he is the giver of the kingdom to me, Madhava, and of counsel. So let
us all, with you, ask Devavrata again about the means of killing himself, Killer
of Madhu. Let us go together quickly to Bhishma, the best of men, if you like,
Varshneya, and ask the Kaurava for counsel. He will tell us good and true words,
Janardana. As he tells us, Krishna, so I will act in battle. He of the firm vow
will be the giver of victory and of counsel to us. We were children without a
father, and he brought us up. And I want to kill him, Madhava, the old
grandfather, the dear father of our father! Shame on the life of a kshatriya!'

"Then, great king, the Varshneya said to the joy of the Kurus, 'I always like
what you say, strong-armed one. Devavrata Bhishma is skilled; he could burn with
a look. Let us go to the son of the river that goes to the sea to ask the means
of killing him. He will tell the truth, especially when you ask him. Let us go
there to ask the grandfather of the Kurus. Let us bow our heads to him and ask
him for counsel. We will fight the enemy with the counsel he gives us.'

"Having taken counsel so, elder brother of Pandu, the heroic Pandavas and mighty
Vasudeva all went together to Bhishma's dwelling, having taken off their weapons
and armour. Going in, they bowed their heads to Bhishma, great king, honouring
him, bull of the Bharatas. Having bowed their heads to Bhishma, the Pandavas
went to him for refuge. Strong-armed Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, said to
them, 'Welcome, Varshneya. Welcome, Dhananjaya. Welcome to the son of Dharma, to
Bhima and to the twins. What can I do for you today to increase your happiness?
I will do it with my whole self, even if it is very hard to do.'

"To Ganga's son, speaking so again and again with affection, Yudhishthira, son
of Dharma, said, sad at heart, 'How can we win, you who know dharma? How can we
gain the kingdom? How can there be no destruction of the people? Tell me that,
lord. Tell us yourself the means of killing you. How can we withstand you in
battle, king? For there is not the smallest gap in you, grandfather of the
Kurus. In battle you are always seen with your bow drawn into a circle. We do
not see you taking an arrow, fitting it or drawing your bow, strong-armed one.
You stand on your chariot like the sun. Killer of men, horses, chariots and
elephants, killer of enemy heroes, what man would dare to kill you, bull of the
Bharatas? Raining great showers of arrows, best of men, you have brought my
great army to destruction. Tell me, grandfather, how I may conquer you in
battle, how the kingdom may be mine, or how my army may have peace.'

"Then Shantanu's son said to the Pandavas, elder brother of Pandu, 'While I
live, Kaunteya, there will be no gain at all for you in battle. I tell you this
truly. But when I have been beaten in battle, you will surely conquer the
Kauravas. Strike me quickly, if you want victory in battle. I give you leave,
Parthas. Strike me as you please. I think this is the right thing, for you know
me. When I am killed, all is killed. So let it be arranged like this.'

"Yudhishthira said, 'So tell us the means by which we may conquer you in battle,
angry in battle like Death with his staff in his hand. The wielder of the
thunderbolt could be conquered, and Varuna and Yama too, but you cannot be
conquered in battle even by the gods and asuras with Indra.'

"Bhishma said, 'It is true, strong-armed Pandava, as you say. I cannot be
conquered in battle, even by the gods and asuras with Indra, while I have my
weapons in hand in battle, ready, holding my fine bow. But when I have laid down
my weapons, king, great chariot fighters could kill me in battle. I do not like
to fight one who has thrown down his weapons, one who has fallen, or one whose
armour and banner are gone. Nor do I like to fight one who is running, one who
is afraid, or one who says, "I am yours." Nor a woman, one with a woman's name,
one who is crippled, one who has only one son, one who has no child, or one who
is ill-favoured. And hear this resolve of mine, Partha, made long ago: I would
never fight on seeing an ill-omened banner.

"'There is in your army, king, the great chariot fighter Shikhandi, Drupada's
son, eager for battle, brave and victorious in combat. You all know exactly how
he was a woman before and later became a man. Let the brave Arjuna put Shikhandi
in front and, in armour, quickly run at me with arrows. With that ill-omened
banner, above all with one who was a woman before, I do not wish to strike at
all, even with an arrow in my hand. Taking that opening, let Dhananjaya the
Pandava quickly have me struck down with arrows on every side, bull of the
Bharatas. I see no one in the worlds who could kill me when I have risen to
fight, except the very fortunate Krishna or Dhananjaya the Pandava. So let
Bibhatsu put someone else in front of me and bring me down. So you will have
victory. Do this, Kaunteya, as I have said. Then you will conquer in battle the
sons of Dhritarashtra who have come together.'

"Given leave, the Parthas went to their own camp, having greeted the
great-souled Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus. When Ganga's son had spoken like
that, consecrated for the other world, Arjuna, burning with grief, said this in
shame. 'How can I fight in battle, Madhava, with the grandfather, my teacher,
the eldest of the family, wise and of perfected understanding? When I was a
child, Vasudeva, playing, I made the great-minded, great-souled one dirty, my
limbs grimy with dust. When I was a child, elder brother of Gada, I climbed onto
his lap and called him "Father", the father of my great-souled father Pandu. "I
am not your father. I am your father's father, Bharata," he said to me when I
was a child. How can I kill him? Let him kill my army if he likes. I will not
fight the great-souled one. Victory or death for me, Krishna, what do you
think?'

"The Blessed Krishna said, 'You promised long ago to kill Bhishma in battle,
Jishnu. How can you not kill him, Partha, if you stand by the dharma of the
kshatriya? Bring him down from his chariot, Partha, like a tree struck by the
thunderbolt. Without killing Ganga's son in battle, you will have no victory.
This was ordained long ago by the gods. Like it or not, it will happen: "He who
comes before Indra will kill Bhishma." That will be so, and not otherwise. For
no one but you could kill Bhishma, unassailable, like Death with his mouth open,
not even the wielder of the thunderbolt himself. Kill Bhishma, strong-armed one.
And hear these words of mine, as the very wise Brihaspati said them to Shakra
long ago: "Shakra, a man may kill even an elder full of good qualities, if he
comes as an assailant to kill, after challenging him." This is the eternal,
settled dharma of kshatriyas, Dhananjaya: to fight, to protect and to sacrifice,
without envy.'

"Arjuna said, 'Shikhandi will surely be Bhishma's death, Krishna. For whenever
Bhishma sees the Panchala, he turns away. So we will put Shikhandi in front of
him and bring down Ganga's son by this means. That is my mind. I will hold off
the other great archers with arrows. Let Shikhandi, the best of fighters, go
against Bhishma alone. You heard the chief of the Kurus: "I will not kill
Shikhandi. She was born a girl, and became a man."'

"Having made their decision so, the Pandavas with Madhava, bulls among men, went
each to his own bed."

**Choices.**

* 6.103.34 *yāvad dvīpo bhavāmy aham*: "while I am your island", a refuge.
* 6.103.44: Bhishma's pact with Yudhishthira, to give counsel but not to fight for him, is reported by Yudhishthira here. It refers to the blessing before the battle, when Yudhishthira asked Bhishma to counsel him while fighting for the Kauravas (6.41.38).
* 6.103.73: the list of those Bhishma will not fight is kept in full, including *ekaputraka*, "one who has only one son".
* 6.103.74 *amaṅgalyadhvaja*: "an ill-omened banner", i.e. Shikhandi.
* 6.103.92 *hantā bhīṣmasya pūrvendraḥ*: "He who comes before Indra will kill Bhishma". The phrase is obscure. It may refer to Arjuna as one born from the earlier Indra.
* 6.103.97–100: Arjuna's plan; Shikhandi is referred to as "she" in Bhishma's quoted words, as in the Sanskrit.

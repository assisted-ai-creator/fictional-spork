# The Garuda Array

"Most of the afternoon of that day had passed, Bharata," said Sanjaya, "in the
great destruction of chariots, elephants, horses, foot soldiers and horsemen.
The Panchala prince was engaged with three great chariot fighters, Drona's son,
Shalya and great Kripa. The mighty heir of the Panchalas killed the horses of
Drona's son, famous in the world, with ten sharp swift arrows. Then Drona's son,
his horses killed, quickly mounted Shalya's chariot and rained arrows on the
heir of the Panchalas. Seeing Dhrishtadyumna engaged with Drona's son, Bharata,
Subhadra's son rushed up quickly, scattering sharp arrows. He pierced Shalya
with twenty-five arrows, bull among men, Kripa with nine, and Ashvatthama with
eight. Then Drona's son quickly pierced Arjuna's son with a feathered arrow,
Shalya with twelve, and Kripa with three sharp ones.

"Your grandson Lakshmana, glad, went against your other grandson where he stood,
and the battle began. Duryodhana's son in his anger pierced Subhadra's son with
nine arrows in battle, king, and it was a wonder. Abhimanyu in his anger, bull
of the Bharatas, quick of hand, pierced his brother with fifty arrows, king.
Lakshmana then cut his bow at the grip with a feathered arrow, great king, and
the people shouted. Subhadra's son, slayer of enemy heroes, threw aside the cut
bow and took another bright, swifter bow. Glad there in the battle, each wanting
to repay the other, those two bulls among men struck each other with sharp
arrows.

"Then King Duryodhana, lord of men, seeing his son, the great chariot fighter,
harried by your grandson, went there. When your son turned back, all the lords
of men surrounded Arjuna's son on every side with a line of chariots. Surrounded
by those heroes, hard to conquer in battle, the hero, equal to Krishna in
courage, was not troubled, king. Seeing Subhadra's son engaged there, Dhananjaya
ran in anger, wanting to save his own son. Then the kings, led by Bhishma and
Drona, with chariots, elephants and horses, came together against the
left-handed archer. At once earthly dust, raised by elephants, horses, chariots
and riders, reached the path of the sun and was seen thick. Those thousands of
elephants and hundreds of kings, coming within range of his arrows, could not go
forward at all. All beings cried out, the quarters became dark, and the
terrible, dreadful wrong policy of the Kurus was seen.

"Neither the sky nor the quarters nor the earth nor the sun could be made out,
best of the Bharatas, for the masses of the diademed one's arrows. With banners
and elephants brought down and horses killed, chariot fighters were badly hurt.
Some leaders of chariot troops were seen with their chariots scattered. Other
chariot fighters without chariots were seen running on every side, here and
there, with their weapons and their arms with armlets. Horsemen left their
horses and elephant riders their elephants, king, and ran on every side in fear
of Arjuna. Kings were seen fallen and falling from chariots, elephants and
horses, struck by Arjuna. Here and there Arjuna took on a terrible form. With
fierce arrows he cut off men's arms raised with clubs and with swords, lord of
the people, with lances, quivers, arrows and bows, with goads and with flags.

"Heaps were seen strewn on the battlefield of great iron bars and hammers, sir,
of lances, slings and swords, of sharp axes and darts, Bharata. There were heaps
of armour and coats of mail thrown on the ground, of banners, shields and fans
on every side. There were umbrellas with golden handles and yak-tail fans,
Bharata, and goads, whips and harness, sir. There was no man in your army,
Bharata, who went against brave Arjuna in battle at all. Whoever went against
Partha in battle, lord of the people, was carried to the other world by sharp
arrows. When all your fighters were running away, Arjuna and Vasudeva blew their
fine conches.

"Seeing that army broken, your father Devavrata said to Bharadvaja's son, the
hero in battle, as if smiling, 'This mighty son of Pandu, with Krishna, is
treating the armies as only Dhananjaya would. He cannot be conquered in battle
today at all, for his form is seen to be like Time, the Ender, or Yama. And this
great army cannot be turned back. See, this host is running, each looking at the
other. And the sun is going down to the best of mountains, as if gathering up
the forms of the whole world. I think it is time to withdraw, bull among men.
Our fighters are tired and afraid and will not fight at all.' Having said this
to Drona, the best of teachers, the great chariot fighter Bhishma withdrew your
troops. Then, as the sun set and the time of twilight came, there was a
withdrawal of your troops and theirs, Bharata.

---

"When the night had dawned, Bhishma son of Shantanu gave orders for the ranks to
set out, Bharata. Bhishma, the grandfather of the Kurus, wanting victory for
your sons, made the great Garuda array. Your father Devavrata himself was at
Garuda's beak. Bharadvaja's son and Kritavarma the Satvata were its eyes. Famous
Ashvatthama and Kripa were the head, with the Trigartas, Matsyas, Kaikeyas and
Vatadhanas. Bhurishravas, Shala, Shalya and Bhagadatta, sir, the Madrakas, the
Sindhu-Sauviras and those of the Five Rivers, with Jayadratha, were placed at
the neck. At the back was King Duryodhana, surrounded by his brothers and
followers. Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti and the Kamboja with the Shakas, and all
the Shurasenas, were the tail, great king. The Magadhas and Kalingas with the
hosts of the Dasherakas stood in armour on the right wing of the array. The
Kananas, Vikunjas, Muktas and Pundravishas, with Brihadbala, took the left wing.

"Seeing that army drawn up, the left-handed archer, scorcher of enemies, with
Dhrishtadyumna, drew up against that very dreadful array in battle with the
half-moon array. Bhimasena shone, taking the right horn, surrounded by kings of
many countries with floods of many weapons. Next to him were Virata and the
great chariot fighter Drupada, and next to them Nila with the Nilayudhas. Next
to Nila was the great chariot fighter Dhrishtaketu, surrounded by the Chedis,
Kashis, Karushas and Pauravas. Dhrishtadyumna, Shikhandi, the Panchalas and the
Prabhadrakas stood in the middle of the great army for battle, Bharata. So too
the King of Dharma, surrounded by a rank of elephants. Then Satyaki, king, and
the five sons of Draupadi, then Abhimanyu, then Iravan, then Bhimasena's son,
king, and the Kekayas, great chariot fighters. Then the best of men took the
left side, the protector of the whole world, whose protector was Janardana.

"So the Pandavas drew up this great array against yours, for the killing of your
sons and those who had joined their side. Then the battle began, with chariots
and elephants entangled, yours and the enemy's killing each other. Floods of
horses and floods of chariots were seen here and there, lord of the people,
rushing together and killing each other. A tumultuous sound arose from the
floods of chariots running and killing, each on its own, mixed with the sound of
drums. In that very tumultuous clash of yours and theirs, Bharata, the noise of
heroes killing one another touched the sky."

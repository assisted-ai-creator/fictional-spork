# The Ring Round Bhishma

"Seeing Bhishma's courage in battle, king," said Sanjaya, "Arjuna said to
Shikhandi, 'Go for the grandfather. Do not be afraid of Bhishma at all today. I
will bring him down from his fine chariot with sharp arrows.' So addressed by
Partha, bull of the Bharatas, Shikhandi ran at Ganga's son. Hearing Partha's
words, Dhrishtadyumna, king, and the great chariot fighter Subhadra's son ran
gladly at Bhishma. The two old kings, Virata and Drupada, and Kuntibhoja in
armour ran at Ganga's son while your son watched. Nakula, Sahadeva and the
mighty King of Dharma, and all the other troops, lord of the people, ran
together at Ganga's son, hearing Partha's words.

"Your men, coming together, went out against those great chariot fighters as far
as their strength and spirit allowed. Hear it as I tell it. Chitrasena, great
king, went against Chekitana as he went in battle, eager to reach Bhishma, like
a tiger cub against a bull. Kritavarma, hurrying, great king, held off
Dhrishtadyumna, ready in battle, as he came near Bhishma. Somadatta's son,
hurrying, great king, held off Bhimasena, very angry, wanting Bhishma's death.
Vikarna, wanting Bhishma to live, held off the hero Nakula, scattering many
arrows. Kripa, Sharadvat's son, in anger held off Sahadeva in battle as he went,
ready, towards Bhishma's chariot. The strong Durmukha ran at the rakshasa of
cruel deeds, Bhimasena's very strong son, who wanted Bhishma's death.
Rishyashringa's son held off Satyaki, angry in battle. Sudakshina of Kamboja,
great king, held off Abhimanyu as he went towards Bhishma's chariot, great king.
Ashvatthama in anger held off the two old destroyers of enemies, Virata and
Drupada, together, Bharata. Bharadvaja's son, ready in battle, held off the
eldest son of Pandu, the son of Dharma, who wanted Bhishma's death. The great
archer Duhshasana held off Arjuna in battle, great king, as he came, impetuous
in battle, with Shikhandi in front, wanting to reach Bhishma and scorching the
ten directions. And your other warriors held off the great chariot fighters of
the Pandavas in battle as they went towards Bhishma.

"Dhrishtadyumna shouted to the troops again and again, 'Run at Bhishma alone,
the very strong one, with fury! Here is Arjuna, the joy of the Kurus, going at
Bhishma in battle. Run! Do not be afraid! Bhishma will not reach you. Even
Vasava would not dare to fight Arjuna in battle, let alone Bhishma, heroes,
whose courage is gone and who has little life left.' Hearing the commander of
the army, the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas ran gladly at the chariot
of Ganga's son. As they came on in battle like strong floods of water, your
bulls among men gladly held them off.

"Duhshasana, great king, the great chariot fighter, giving up fear and wanting
Bhishma to live, ran at Dhananjaya. So too the brave Pandavas ran in battle at
your sons, the great chariot fighters, towards the chariot of Ganga's son. There
we saw a wonder, of many colours, lord of the people: having reached
Duhshasana's chariot, Partha did not get past it. As the shore holds back the
stormy ocean, so your son held back the angry Pandava. Both were the best of
chariot fighters, Bharata, both hard to conquer, both like the moon and the sun
in beauty and brightness, Bharata. Roused with fury, wanting to kill each other,
they met in the great battle like Maya and Shakra long ago.

"Duhshasana, great king, struck the Pandava in battle with three arrows and
Vasudeva with twenty. Then Arjuna, angry at seeing the Varshneya hurt, struck
Duhshasana in battle with a hundred iron arrows. They split his armour and drank
his blood in battle. Then Duhshasana in anger pierced Partha in the forehead
with five arrows with bent joints, best of the Bharatas. With those arrows in
his forehead the best of the Pandavas shone, great king, like Meru with its
peaks raised high. Badly pierced by your son the bowman, the great archer Partha
shone in battle like a flowering kimshuka. Then the Pandava in anger pressed
Duhshasana hard, as fierce Rahu in great anger presses the moon at the full or
new moon. Pressed by the strong one, lord of the people, your son pierced Partha
in battle with stone-whetted, heron-feathered arrows. Partha, mighty, hurrying,
cut his bow and then struck your son with nine arrows. He took another bow and,
standing in front of Bhishma, fixed twenty-five arrows in Arjuna's arms and
chest.

"The Pandava, destroyer of enemies, in anger, great king, sent many terrible
arrows at him like Yama's staff. Your son cut those arrows before they reached
him, and it was a wonder, though Partha was striving. And your son pierced
Partha with sharp arrows. Then Partha in anger fitted to his bow stone-whetted
arrows with golden feathers and sent them in battle. They sank into the
great-souled one's body, great king, as swans sink into a pond, Bharata. Hard
pressed by the great-souled Pandava, your son quickly left Partha in battle and
took shelter at Bhishma's chariot. Bhishma became an island for him as he sank
in the bottomless deep. Coming to his senses, your son, lord of the people, the
brave and mighty one, held him off again. The very mighty one split Partha with
very sharp arrows, as Vritra split Purandara, and did not falter before Arjuna.

---

"The great archer Rishyashringa's son held off Satyaki in battle as he came in
armour against Bhishma. Madhava, very angry, struck the rakshasa with nine
arrows in battle, king, as if laughing, Bharata. So too the rakshasa, king, in
anger hurt Madhava, the bull of the Shinis, with sharp arrows, king of kings.
The Shaineya, killer of enemy heroes, very angry, sent a crowd of arrows at the
rakshasa in battle. Then the rakshasa pierced strong-armed Satyaki, of true
courage, with sharp arrows and roared a lion roar. Madhava, badly pierced by the
rakshasa in battle, holding to his steadiness, the splendid one laughed and
roared.

"Then Bhagadatta in anger struck Madhava in battle with sharp arrows, as a great
elephant is struck with goads. Leaving the rakshasa in battle, the Shaineya,
best of chariot fighters, shot arrows with bent joints at the Pragjyotisha. The
king of Pragjyotisha cut Madhava's great bow with a sharp-edged broad arrow, as
one practised of hand. The killer of enemy heroes took another swift bow and in
anger pierced Bhagadatta in battle with sharp arrows. Badly pierced, licking the
corners of his mouth again and again, the great archer sent at Satyaki a
terrible spear like Yama's staff, of iron, firm, adorned with gold and beryl. As
it came at him at once, driven by the strength of his arm, Satyaki cut it in
three with arrows in battle, king, and it fell to the ground like a great meteor
that has lost its light. Seeing the spear destroyed, lord of the people, your
son held Madhava off with a great line of chariots. Seeing the great chariot
fighter of the Varshneyas surrounded so, Duryodhana, very glad, said to all his
brothers, 'Act so, Kauravyas, that Satyaki does not come out alive in battle
from this great crowd of chariots. When he is killed, I think the great army of
the Pandavas is killed.' 'So be it,' said the great chariot fighters, taking his
words, and they fought the Shaineya in front of Bhishma then.

"The strong king of Kamboja held off Abhimanyu in battle as he came, risen
against Bhishma. Arjuna's son pierced the king with arrows with bent joints and
then pierced him again, king, with sixty-four. Sudakshina, wanting Bhishma to
live, pierced Krishna's grandson in battle with five arrows and his charioteer
with nine. That battle between the two, in their courage, was very great, while
Shikhandi, scorcher of enemies, ran at Ganga's son.

"The two old great chariot fighters, Virata and Drupada, holding off the great
army and roused, ran at Bhishma in battle. Then Ashvatthama, best of chariot
fighters, came up in anger, and then a battle broke out between yours and
theirs, Bharata. Virata struck the striving great archer, Drona's son, who
shines in battle, with ten broad arrows, scorcher of enemies. So too Drupada,
reaching the teacher's son standing in front of Bhishma, pierced him with three
sharp arrows. Then Ashvatthama pierced the two old men, Virata and Drupada,
risen against Bhishma, with ten arrows. There we saw a great deed of the two old
men, for they held off the terrible arrows of Drona's son in battle.

"Kripa, Sharadvat's son, went against Sahadeva as he went, like an elephant in
rut going against an elephant in rut in the forest. Kripa quickly struck Madri's
son, the great chariot fighter, in battle, king, with seventy gold-adorned
arrows. Madri's son cut his bow in two with arrows and then pierced him, his bow
cut, with nine arrows. He took another bow that could bear the load in battle
and, very glad, in anger, wanting Bhishma to live, struck Madri's son in the
chest with ten sharp arrows. So too the Pandava, king, wanting Bhishma's death,
in anger struck the impatient son of Sharadvat in the chest. Their battle was
terrible in form and frightening.

"Vikarna, scorcher of enemies, guarding Bhishma's life, pierced Nakula, angry in
battle, with sixty arrows. Nakula too, badly pierced by your son the bowman,
split Vikarna with seventy-seven arrows. There those two tigers among men,
scorchers of enemies, heroes, struck each other for Bhishma's sake like two
bulls in a cow pen.

"The mighty Durmukha went in battle for Bhishma's sake against Ghatotkacha,
ready in battle and killing your army. Then, king, Hidimba's son in anger struck
Durmukha, scorcher of enemies, in the chest with ninety sharp arrows. The hero
Durmukha, roaring and glad, pierced Bhimasena's son at the head of the battle
with sixty fine-faced arrows.

"Hridika's son, guarding Bhishma's life, held off Dhrishtadyumna as he went in
battle wanting Bhishma's death. The Varshneya pierced the brave grandson of
Prishata with five iron arrows and quickly struck him again between the breasts
with fifty. So too Prishata's grandson, king, pierced Hridika's son with nine
sharp, keen arrows fitted with heron feathers. Their battle in the great battle
for Bhishma's sake was full of each trying to outdo the other, like that of
Vritra and great Indra.

"Bhurishravas went quickly against the very strong Bhimasena as he came towards
Bhishma, and said, 'Stand! Stand!' Then Somadatta's son struck Bhima between the
breasts in battle with a very sharp gold-feathered iron arrow. With that in his
chest the mighty Bhimasena shone, best of kings, like the Krauncha mountain long
ago with Skanda's spear. The two in anger threw at each other again and again in
battle arrows bright as the sun, polished by the smith. Bhima, wanting Bhishma's
death, fought the great chariot fighter, Somadatta's son, and Somadatta's son,
eager for Bhishma's victory, fought the Pandava, each ready to pay back blow for
blow.

"Bharadvaja's son held off Yudhishthira, great king, as he went towards Bhishma
surrounded by a great army. Hearing the rumble of Drona's chariot, like the
thunder of Parjanya, the Prabhadrakas trembled, king, sir. That great army of
the son of Pandu, ready, held off by Drona in battle, king, did not move a step.
Chitrasena, your son, held off Chekitana, angry, lord of men, as he went towards
Bhishma, angry in form. The great chariot fighter Chitrasena, bold for Bhishma's
sake, fought Chekitana with the highest strength, Bharata. So too Chekitana
fought Chitrasena. That battle between the two in their courage was very great.

"Arjuna, though held back many times by your son, turned your son away and
crushed your army. Duhshasana too held Partha back with the highest strength,
having made up his mind, Bharata: 'How can the enemy kill Bhishma?' That army of
your son, being killed in battle, was churned up here and there by the best
chariot fighters, Bharata."

Bhishma forms the Garuda array and the Pandavas the half-moon; Arjuna cuts down the chariot ranks, the Gandharas wreck Satyaki's chariot, and Bhima's arrow knocks Duryodhana senseless, so that the Kaurava army breaks; Duryodhana reproaches Bhishma for sparing the Pandavas, and Bhishma promises to hold them all back; in the afternoon Bhishma seems to be everywhere at once and the Pandava army flees; Arjuna fights him too gently, and Krishna, unable to bear it, leaps down with his discus and runs at Bhishma, who welcomes death at his hands; Arjuna drags Krishna back at the tenth step and swears to fight in earnest, then routs the Kauravas with the Indra weapon until nightfall.

# The Third Day

"When the night had turned to morning," said Sanjaya, "Bhishma, son of Shantanu,
ordered the divisions to march, Bharata. Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus,
wanting victory for your sons, made the great Garuda array. Your father
Devavrata himself was in the Garuda's beak. The eyes were Bharadvaja's son and
Kritavarma the Satvata. The famous Ashvatthama and Kripa were the head, with the
Trigartas, the Matsyas, the Kekayas and the Vatadhanas. Bhurishravas, Shala,
Shalya and Bhagadatta, sir, the Madrakas, the Sindhu-Sauviras and the men of the
Five Rivers were set in the neck with Jayadratha. King Duryodhana, surrounded by
his brothers and their followers, was in the back. Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti,
the Kamboja with the Shakas, and all the Shurasenas were the tail, great king.
The Magadhas and the Kalingas, with the hosts of the Dasherakas, stood in their
armour on the right wing of the array. The Kananas, Vikunjas, Muktas and
Pundravishas, with Brihadbala, held the left wing.

"When Savyasachin, scorcher of enemies, saw that army drawn up, he and
Dhrishtadyumna answered that very terrible array with the half-moon array.
Bhimasena shone on the right horn, surrounded by kings of many lands with floods
of weapons of every kind. Next to him were Virata and Drupada, the great chariot
fighter, and next to them Nila with the Nilayudhas. Next to Nila was the great
chariot fighter Dhrishtaketu, surrounded by the Chedis, the Kashis, the Karushas
and the Pauravas. Dhrishtadyumna, Shikhandi, the Panchalas and the Prabhadrakas
stood in the middle of the great army, ready for battle, Bharata. The righteous
king was there too, surrounded by his division of elephants. Then came Satyaki,
king, and the five sons of Draupadi, then Abhimanyu, then Iravan, then Bhima's
son, king, and the Kekayas, great chariot fighters. And on the left side stood
the best of men, the guardian of the whole world, whose own guardian is
Janardana. So the Pandavas drew up this great array against yours, to kill your
sons and those who had joined their side.

"Then the battle began, chariots and elephants tangled together, yours and
theirs, killing one another. Floods of horses and floods of chariots were seen
here and there, meeting and killing one another. As the floods of chariots ran
and killed one by one, there was a tumultuous noise mixed with the sound of
drums. It reached the sky, Bharata, as the heroes, yours and theirs, killed one
another in that tremendous clash.

"When the armies had been drawn up, yours and theirs, Dhananjaya struck at your
chariot ranks, Bharata. The outstanding chariot fighter brought down the leaders
of chariot troops with his arrows in the battle. As Partha killed them, like
Time at the end of an age, the sons of Dhritarashtra fought the Pandavas
steadily in the battle, seeking bright glory and making death their only way
back. With one mind they broke the Pandava army again and again, king, and they
were broken in the battle too. With the Pandavas and the Kauravas running,
breaking and turning, nothing could be made out. Earthly dust rose and hid the
sun, and no one could tell the quarters from one another. The battle went on
here and there by guesswork, by signs and by the shouting of names and families,
lord of the people. The Kaurava array was never broken, guarded by wise
Bharadvaja's son, true to his word. In the same way the great array of the
Pandavas, guarded by Savyasachin and well guarded by Bhima, was not broken.

"Men came out from the front of the armies and fought there, the chariots and
elephants of both armies tangled together, king. Horsemen brought down horsemen
in the great battle with bright-tipped spears and lances. Chariot fighter
reached chariot fighter and brought him down with arrows adorned with gold in
that most frightful battle. Elephant riders, locked together, brought down
elephant riders in crowds, yours and theirs, with iron arrows, darts and lances.
Crowds of foot soldiers, glad and each wronged by the other, brought down foot
soldiers with javelins and axes. Foot soldier brought down chariot fighter in
the battle, and chariot fighter foot soldier, with sharp weapons, in both
armies. Elephant riders brought down horsemen, and horsemen brought down men on
elephants. It was a marvel. Foot soldiers were seen brought down here and there
by the best elephant riders, and elephant fighters by them. Crowds of foot
soldiers were seen brought down by horsemen, and crowds of horsemen by foot
soldiers, by the hundred and the thousand.

"With cast-off banners, bows and lances, spears, maces, iron bars and darts,
javelins and bright armour, goads and hooks, bright swords and arrows with
golden feathers, saddle-cloths, coverlets and costly blankets, the ground shone,
best of the Bharatas, as if painted with strings of garlands. With the fallen
bodies of men and horses and with elephants in the great battle, the earth could
not be walked on, with its mud of flesh and blood. The earthly dust settled,
soaked with the blood of battle, and all the quarters became clear, lord of men.
Headless bodies rose up on every side without number, signs of the world's
destruction, Bharata. In that fearful, very cruel battle, chariot fighters were
seen running on every side.

"Then Drona and Bhishma, Jayadratha of Sindhu, Purumitra, Vikarna and Shakuni,
son of Subala, these men whom no one could face in battle, with the valour of
lions, broke the Pandava ranks again and again. In the same way Bhimasena and
the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, Satyaki, Chekitana and the sons of Draupadi, Bharata,
drove your men and your sons with all the kings in the battle, as the thirty
gods drive the danavas. So those bulls among kshatriyas, killing one another in
the battle, soaked in blood and fearful to look at, shone like danavas. Having
beaten their enemies, those great warriors of both armies were seen like planets
in the sky.

"Then your son Duryodhana, with a thousand chariots, went against the Pandavas
and the rakshasa Ghatotkacha in the battle. In the same way all the Pandavas
with a great army went against Drona and Bhishma, those two brave destroyers of
enemies. Kiritin, in fury, went against the best of the kings, and Arjuna's son
and Satyaki went against the army of Subala's son. Then again a hair-raising
battle began, yours and theirs, each striving to win.

"When the kings saw Phalguna in the battle, they surrounded him in fury on every
side with many thousands of chariots. They made a pen of chariots round him,
Bharata, and held him off on every side with many thousands of arrows. In fury
they hurled bright, sharp spears, maces with iron bars, lances, axes, hammers
and clubs at Phalguna's chariot. Partha stopped that shower of weapons, coming
on like a swarm of locusts, on every side with his arrows adorned with gold.
When they saw that superhuman speed of Bibhatsu's, the gods, danavas,
gandharvas, pishachas, serpents and rakshasas praised Phalguna, lord of kings,
crying, 'Well done! Well done!'

"The brave Gandharas with the Saubalas, with a great army, held back Satyaki and
Abhimanyu in the battle. There the furious Saubalas cut the Varshneya's fine
chariot into pieces as small as sesame seeds with weapons of every kind. In that
great terror Satyaki left his chariot, and the scorcher of enemies quickly
climbed into Abhimanyu's chariot. Riding together in one chariot, they quickly
scattered Subala's son's army with sharp, straight arrows.

"Drona and Bhishma, striving in the battle, destroyed the righteous king's army
with sharp arrows feathered with heron plumes. Then the son of Dharma, the king,
and the two Pandava sons of Madri charged Drona's division while all the armies
watched. There was a very great battle there, tumultuous and hair-raising, as
fearful as the war of the gods and the asuras long ago.

"As Bhimasena and Ghatotkacha were doing great deeds, Duryodhana came up and
held them both off. There we saw a wonderful feat of Hidimba's son, Bharata. He
fought in the battle, surpassing his father. Bhimasena, in fury, almost
laughing, pierced the angry Duryodhana in the heart with an arrow. Then King
Duryodhana, stunned by that fine blow, sat down on the floor of his chariot and
fainted. Seeing him senseless, his charioteer quickly carried him out of the
battle, king, and then the army broke. Bhima followed the Kaurava army from
behind as it ran in every direction, killing it with sharp arrows. Prishata's
son, best of chariot fighters, and the Pandava son of Dharma killed your son's
army with sharp arrows that destroy enemy ranks, while Drona and Ganga's son
looked on. Bhishma and Drona, great chariot fighters, could not stop your son's
army from running. Though Bhishma and Drona tried to hold it back, lord of the
people, it kept running while they watched.

"As thousands of chariots ran this way and that, the son of Subhadra and the
bull of Shini, riding in one chariot, cut down Subala's son's army on every
side. The bull of the Kurus and Shaineya shone like the moon and the sun
together in the sky on the night of the new moon. Then Arjuna, in fury, rained
showers of arrows on your army, lord of the people, like a cloud pouring
streams. Struck by Partha's arrows in the battle, the Kaurava army ran, shaking
with despair and fear. Seeing them run, Bhishma and Drona, great chariot
fighters, eager for Duryodhana's good, tried in fury to stop them.

"Then King Duryodhana recovered, lord of the people, and turned back the army
running on every side. Wherever each great kshatriya chariot fighter saw your
son, Bharata, there he turned back. Seeing them turned back, other men too stood
their ground, some from rivalry and some from shame, king. As they turned back,
lord of the people, their rush was like the sea rising towards the rising moon.

"When King Suyodhana saw them turned back, he went quickly to Bhishma, son of
Shantanu, and spoke to him. 'Grandfather, hear what I say to you, Bharata. I do
not think it fitting, Kaurava, that this army should run while you are alive,
and Drona, best of those who know weapons, with his son and his friends, and
Kripa too, the great archer. The Pandavas are no match for you in any way, king,
nor for Drona in battle, nor for Drona's son, nor for Kripa. Surely the sons of
Pandu have your favour, grandfather, since you put up with this army being
killed, hero. You should have told me at the beginning, king, "I will not fight
the Pandavas in battle, nor Prishata's son, nor Satyaki." Then, when I had heard
your words and the teacher's and Kripa's, I would have thought with Karna about
what to do. If you two will not abandon me here in battle, let those two bulls
among men fight with valour fit for them.'

"When he heard these words, Bhishma, laughing again and again, rolled his eyes
in anger and said to your son, 'King, I have told you many times what was true
and good for you: the Pandavas cannot be beaten in battle even by the gods with
Vasava. But I will do what an old man can do today, best of kings, as far as I
can. Watch now, with your kin. Today I shall hold back all the sons of Pandu,
with their armies and their kin, alone, while all the world looks on.'

"When Bhishma had said this, lord of men, your sons blew their conches with joy
and beat their drums hard. And when the Pandavas heard that great noise, king,
they blew their conches and beat their drums and tabors."

"When Bhishma had made that promise in that very cruel battle," said
Dhritarashtra, "angered by my son, who was so grieved, what did Bhishma do there
to the Pandavas, Sanjaya? Or what did the Panchalas do to the grandfather? Tell
me, Sanjaya."

"When most of the morning of that day had gone, Bharata," said Sanjaya, "and the
great Pandavas had won and were glad, your father Devavrata, who knew every
point of dharma, charged the Pandava army with swift horses, guarded by a great
army and by all your sons. Then a tumultuous, hair-raising battle began between
us and the Pandavas, Bharata, through your bad policy. The noise of twanging
bows and slapped arm-guards was huge, like mountains splitting. 'Stand!' 'Here I
am!' 'Know him!' 'Turn back!' 'Stand firm!' 'Here I stand! Strike!' These cries
were heard everywhere. There was a noise, on golden armour, crowns and banners,
like stones falling on rocks.

"Heads and adorned arms fell by the hundred and the thousand, and writhed when
they reached the ground. Some of the best of men, with their heads cut off,
still stood holding their bows raised and their weapons gripped. A swift river
of blood began to flow, terrible with the rocks of elephants' bodies, with a mud
of flesh and blood. It sprang from the bodies of fine horses, men and elephants,
and it ran towards the ocean of the other world, a joy to vultures and jackals.
No one has seen or heard of a battle like this one, king, between your sons and
the Pandavas, Bharata. There was no path for chariots there, with the warriors
struck down in battle and the dark fallen elephants lying like mountain peaks.
Strewn with bright armour, banners and umbrellas, sir, that battlefield shone
like the sky in autumn. Some, pierced by arrows and dragging their entrails in
pain, charged the enemy fearlessly in their armour. Others, fallen in the
battle, cried out, 'Father! Brother! Friend! Kinsman! Comrade! Uncle! Do not
leave me!' Others shouted, 'Run! Come! Don't go! What are you afraid of? Where
will you go? I am standing firm. Don't be afraid!'

"There Bhishma, son of Shantanu, his bow always drawn to a circle, shot
blazing-tipped arrows like venomous snakes. Filling all the quarters with his
arrows, the man of strict vows killed the Pandava chariot fighters, naming them
one by one, Bharata. Dancing on the floor of his chariot and showing the speed
of his hands, he was seen here and there, king, like a whirling torch. Because
of his speed, the Pandavas and the Srinjayas saw that one hero in the battle as
many hundreds of thousands. They thought Bhishma had made himself many by magic.
People saw him in the east and then in the west, and when they had seen him in
the north, they saw him again in the south, lord. So Ganga's heroic son was seen
in the battle.

"None of the Pandava side could look at him. They saw only the many arrows
loosed from Bhishma's bow. The heroes there cried out in many ways as they saw
your father doing such deeds in the battle, destroying the army, moving in a
form beyond the human. Kings fell by the thousand, like moths driven by fate,
into the fire of Bhishma's fury, to their destruction. Not one of Bhishma's
arrows missed in the battle, so thick were the bodies of men, elephants and
horses for that swift archer. With a single well-shot feathered arrow he pierced
an elephant's armour, as a thunderbolt pierces a great mountain. With one very
sharp iron arrow your father killed two or three elephant riders at once, even
in their armour and packed together. Whoever came against Bhishma, tiger among
men, in the battle, I saw him for a moment and then saw him struck down on the
ground.

"So the great army of the righteous king, killed by Bhishma of matchless valour,
fell apart in a thousand pieces. Scorched by the shower of arrows, the great
army scattered while Vasudeva and great Partha looked on. Though they tried, the
heroes could not stop the great chariot fighters from running, tormented by
Bhishma's arrows. Killed by that man as mighty as great Indra, the great army
broke, great king, and no two men ran together. With its men, elephants and
horses struck and its banners and chariot shafts brought down, the Pandava army
was crying out and senseless. There father killed son, and son father, and
friend killed dear friend, forced by the power of fate. Other soldiers of the
Pandava's were seen running with their armour thrown off and their hair loose,
Bharata. The Pandava's army looked like a herd of cattle bolting, with its
leaders of chariot troops bolting too, and it cried out in pain.

"Seeing that army being broken, the son of Devaki stopped the fine chariot and
said to Partha Bibhatsu, 'Partha, the time you have wanted has come. Strike him,
tiger among men, unless you are losing your wits. What you said long ago, hero,
in the gathering of the kings, "I will kill all the soldiers of Dhritarashtra's
son, led by Bhishma and Drona, with all their followers, who fight me in
battle", make that come true, son of Kunti, destroyer of enemies. Bibhatsu, look
at your own army being broken on every side, and all the kings in Yudhishthira's
army running. When they see Bhishma in battle, like Death with his mouth gaping,
they run in terror, like small beasts from a lion.'

"When he was told this, Dhananjaya answered Vasudeva, 'Drive the horses to where
Bhishma is, plunging into this ocean of an army.' Then Madhava drove the
silver-white horses towards Bhishma's chariot, king, which was as hard to look
at as the sun. Then the great army of Yudhishthira turned back again, seeing
strong-armed Partha going out to meet Bhishma in the battle.

"Then Bhishma, best of the Kurus, roaring like a lion again and again, quickly
covered Dhananjaya's chariot with showers of arrows. In a moment his chariot,
with its horses and its charioteer, was covered by the great shower of arrows
and could not be seen. But Vasudeva, unshaken, strong in spirit, holding firm,
drove on the horses, pierced as they were by Bhishma's arrows. Then Partha took
up his divine bow, which roars like a cloud, and cut Bhishma's bow with three
arrows and brought it down. With his bow cut, the Kauravya, your father, strung
another great bow in the blink of an eye. He drew with both arms the bow that
roars like a cloud, and Arjuna, in anger, cut that bow too. The son of Shantanu
praised his speed. 'Well done, Partha, strong-armed one! Well done, son of
Pandu! This great deed is fit for you alone, Dhananjaya. I am very pleased, my
son. Fight with me.'

"When he had praised Partha like this, the hero took up another great bow and
shot arrows at Partha's chariot in the battle. Vasudeva showed his great
strength in driving the horses. He made the arrows miss, and moved in swift
circles. Even so, Bhishma pierced Vasudeva and Dhananjaya hard in every limb
with sharp arrows, sir. Wounded by Bhishma's arrows, those two tigers among men
shone like two roaring bulls scored with the marks of horns. Again, in great
fury, Bhishma closed off every direction to the two Krishnas with straight
arrows in the battle. In his anger he made Varshneya tremble with sharp arrows,
smiling again and again, and laughing aloud.

"Then Krishna saw Bhishma's valour in the battle, and saw that Partha was
fighting gently. He saw Bhishma pouring out showers of arrows without pause,
standing between the two armies and burning like the sun, killing the best of
the Pandava's soldiers one after another, and making an end of the world, as it
were, in Yudhishthira's army. The blessed Keshava, killer of enemy heroes, whose
spirit had no measure, could not bear it. He thought, 'Yudhishthira's army is
finished. In a single day Bhishma could destroy the gods and the danavas in
battle, let alone the sons of Pandu with their armies and followers. The great
army of the Pandava is running, and here are the Kauravas, seeing the Somakas
broken, charging gladly in the battle, and gladdening the grandfather. So I
shall arm myself and kill Bhishma today, for the Pandavas' sake. I shall take
this burden off the great Pandavas. Arjuna, struck by sharp arrows in the
battle, does not know what to do, out of respect for Bhishma.'

"While he was thinking this, the grandfather again, in fury, shot arrows at
Partha's chariot. There were so many arrows that every direction was hidden.
Neither sky nor quarters nor earth could be seen, nor the sun in his garland of
rays. Tumultuous winds blew, full of smoke, and all the quarters were thrown
into turmoil. Drona, Vikarna and Jayadratha, Bhurishravas, Kritavarma and Kripa,
Shrutayus, the king of the Ambashthas, Vinda and Anuvinda and Sudakshina, the
easterners and all the hosts of the Sauviras, the Vasatis and the Kshudrakas and
Malavas, all went quickly against Kiritin at the command of King Shantanava.

"The grandson of Shini saw Kiritin being surrounded by nets of floods of horses,
foot soldiers and chariots, many hundreds of thousands of them, and by leaders
of elephant troops. When he saw Arjuna and Vasudeva, the best of those who bear
weapons, attacked on every side by foot soldiers, elephants, horses and
chariots, the hero of the Shinis went quickly to them. That great archer, the
hero of the Shinis, fell suddenly on those ranks and helped Arjuna, as Vishnu
helped the killer of Vritra. Seeing Yudhishthira's army running, with its
elephants, horses, chariots and banners shattered and all its warriors terrified
by Bhishma, the hero of the Shinis called out to them. 'Kshatriyas, where are
you going? This is not the dharma of the good, as the ancients have told it. Do
not break your own promises, heroes. Keep your own dharma as warriors.'

"Indra's younger brother marked those chief kings running on every side. He saw
Partha's gentle fighting, and Bhishma rising up in the battle. He saw the Kurus
charging from every side. The great one, the lord of all the Dasharhas, could
not bear it. Praising famous Shaineya, he said, 'Let those who are going go,
hero of the Shinis. And let those who are standing go too, Satvata. See Bhishma
thrown down from his chariot by me today, and Drona in battle with his
followers. No Kaurava chariot fighter will escape me in battle today when I am
angry, Satvata. So I will take up my fierce discus and take the life of the man
of the great vow. When I have killed Bhishma and his followers in the battle,
and Drona, that hero among chariot fighters, Shaineya, I will bring joy to
Dhananjaya and to the king, to Bhima and to the twin sons of the Ashvins. I will
kill all the sons of Dhritarashtra and the chief kings on their side, and gladly
give the kingdom to King Ajatashatru today.'

"Then Vasudeva's son raised in his arm the discus with its fine nave, bright as
the sun, with the power of a thunderbolt and an edge like a razor. He leapt down
from the chariot, letting go of the horses. Shaking the earth with his feet, the
great Krishna ran swiftly at Bhishma, like a lion wanting to kill a lordly
elephant blind with rut, full of the pride of battle. Indra's younger brother,
the destroyer, ran at Bhishma in fury, in the middle of the army. The end of his
yellow cloth hung down, and he shone like a cloud wrapped in lightning. His
Sudarshana shone, that lotus of a discus with his fine arm for its stalk, like
the first lotus, the colour of the young sun, that grew from Narayana's navel.
Opened by the sunrise of Krishna's anger, with its well-formed petals the sharp
edges of razors, it shone, grown from the great lake of his body, with
Narayana's arm for its stalk.

"When they saw Indra's younger brother in his fury, discus in hand, roaring
loudly, all beings cried out, thinking, 'This is the end of the Kurus.'
Vasudeva, the teacher of the world, with the discus in his hand, leaping up,
shone like the fire at the end of time burning up all beings, as if he meant to
destroy the world of the living.

"When the son of Shantanu saw the god, the best of men, coming at him with his
discus raised, he stood in his chariot, bow and arrow in hand, unshaken, and
said to him, 'Come, come, lord of gods, home of the world! I bow to you, you who
hold the bow Sharnga and the discus. Throw me down from this fine chariot by
force in battle, lord of the world, refuge of all beings. If I am killed by you
here today, Krishna, it will be good for me in the other world and in this one.
I am honoured by the three worlds, hero, lord of the Andhakas and Vrishnis,
since you have come against me.'

"Then Partha leapt quickly down from his chariot, ran after the hero of the
Yadus and seized Hari by his two long, thick, fine arms, with his own long,
thick arms. Held back like that, the first god, the yogi, in great anger, they
say, swept Jishnu along with him at speed, Vishnu did, as a great wind sweeps
along a single tree. But Kiritin, with his garland, braced his feet and forcibly
held him back, king, somehow, at the tenth step, as he ran at speed towards
Bhishma.

"When Krishna had stopped, Arjuna, glad, with his bright golden garland, bowed
down to him and said, 'Keshava, hold back your anger. You are the Pandavas'
refuge. I will not give up the deed I promised. I swear it by my sons and my
brothers, Keshava. I will make an end of the Kurus, with you to drive me,
younger brother of Indra.'

"When he heard that oath and that pledge, Janardana was glad at heart. Standing
ready to do what pleased the best of the Kauravas, he climbed back into the
chariot with his discus. The destroyer of enemies took up the reins again, and
Shauri took his conch and made the quarters ring with the roar of Panchajanya.
When they saw him with his necklace, armlets and earrings swinging, his curling
eyelashes covered with dust, his teeth shining clean, holding his conch, the
Kuru heroes cried out. Then the sound of tabors, drums and kettledrums, of
chariot wheels and war drums, with lion roars, rose fierce in all the Kuru
ranks.

"The twang of the Gandiva, like thunder, went up from Partha to the sky and the
quarters. Bright, clear arrows loosed from the Pandava's bow flew in every
direction. Then the lord of the Kauravas, with his army, with Bhishma and
Bhurishravas, arrows raised in his hand, came against him, like a comet wanting
to burn dry grass. Bhurishravas shot seven broad arrows with golden feathers at
Arjuna. Duryodhana hurled a fierce, swift lance, Shalya a mace and the son of
Shantanu a spear. With seven fine arrows the hero stopped the seven Bhurishravas
had shot, and with a sharp razor-headed arrow he broke the lance thrown by
Duryodhana's arm. With two arrows he cut down the bright spear shining like
lightning that the son of Shantanu had hurled, and the mace thrown by the arm of
the lord of the Madras.

"Then, drawing with both arms the wonderful, measureless bow Gandiva, he brought
out in the sky, in due form, the most terrible and wonderful weapon of great
Indra. With that supreme weapon the great archer with the garland held back all
the divisions with floods and nets of arrows bright as fire. The arrows from
Partha's bow cut through chariots, banner tops, bows and arms, and went into the
bodies of the enemy's kings, lordly elephants and horses. Filling the quarters
and the spaces between with sharp, keen-edged arrows, the one with the garland
shook their hearts with the twang of the Gandiva. In that most terrible fight
the sound of conches and drums and the fierce cries of battle were drowned by
the twang of the Gandiva. Knowing that sound of the Gandiva, the heroes led by
King Virata, and brave Drupada, king of Panchala, came there, undaunted.

"Wherever the roar of the Gandiva came from, all your armies bowed down, and no
one went against it. In that very terrible clash of kings, heroes were killed
with their chariots and charioteers. Elephants with great banners and fine
golden girths, scorched by the falling iron arrows, fell suddenly, lifeless,
their armour and bodies pierced by Kiritin, struck hard by feathered arrows of
fierce speed, by Partha's sharp broad arrows. At the heads of the armies great
banners were brought down by Dhananjaya, their fittings cut and their poles
struck. Crowds of foot soldiers, chariots, horses and elephants, struck by
arrows, quickly lost their lives and fell to the earth, their limbs stiffened,
their armour and bodies pierced in the great battle by that fine weapon of
Indra, king.

"Then with the floods of sharp arrows of Kiritin a very terrible river was set
flowing on that battlefield. Its water was the blood from men's bodies cut by
weapons. Its foam was the fat of men. With a very broad stream and great speed
it flowed, fearful and roaring. Its banks were the bodies of dead elephants and
horses. Its mud was the flesh, filled with men's entrails and marrow. Crowds of
rakshasas and ghosts visited it. Its grass was the hair on the skulls. It
carried thousands of heaped bodies, and it was full of waves of many kinds of
broken armour. Its gravel was the cut bones of men, horses and elephants. It was
frightening, with the depth of destruction and the underworld. They saw it on
every side, its banks thronged with heron-garlanded vultures and kites, with
packs of flesh-eaters and hyenas, cruel, like the great Vaitarani, set flowing
by Arjuna's floods of arrows, carrying fat, marrow and blood, most fearful.

"The Chedis, the Panchalas, the Karushas, the Matsyas and all the Parthas roared
together, terrifying the army of the commanders as a lion terrifies herds of
deer. And the bowman of the Gandiva and Janardana roared too, full of joy. Then
the Kurus, with Bhishma, with Drona, Duryodhana and Bahlika, their limbs torn by
weapons, seeing the sun drawing in its net of rays, and seeing that most
terrible Indra weapon spread out, unbearable, like the end of an age, and seeing
the evening come on, red-streaked by the sun, withdrew. Dhananjaya too, having
won fame and glory in the world and beaten his enemies, went to his camp at
night with the kings and his brothers, his work done.

"Then, at nightfall, a tumultuous and most terrible noise rose among the Kurus.
'In the battle Arjuna has killed ten thousand chariot fighters and seven hundred
elephants. All the easterners, all the hosts of the Sauviras and the Kshudrakas
and Malavas have been struck down. Dhananjaya has done a great deed, such as no
one else could do. Shrutayus, the king of the Ambashthas, Durmarshana and
Chitrasena, Drona, Kripa, the Saindhava and Bahlika, Bhurishravas, Shalya and
Shala, king, and Bhishma too, were beaten by Kiritin, the great chariot fighter
of the world, with the strength of his own arms.' Saying this, all your forces,
Bharata, went to their camps. Lit by thousands of blazing torches and bright
lamps, the Kuru army, all its warriors terrified by Kiritin, made its camp."

**Divergences.**

* **Krishna runs at Bhishma (the first time).** When Arjuna fights "gently" out of respect for Bhishma (6.55.63, 70), Krishna says he will "take up my fierce discus" (*gṛhya rathāṅgam ugram*, 6.55.83) and kill Bhishma himself. He leaps down and runs at him with the discus, named Sudarshana (6.55.86–89). Bhishma welcomes death at his hands (6.55.94–95). Arjuna holds him back "at the tenth step" and swears to fight in earnest (6.55.96–100). See ledger L-36. On the ninth day Krishna runs at Bhishma again, whip in hand (6.102).
* **What is rejected.** The discus "climbing into his hand as soon as he thought of it" (\*201), Krishna's reply to Bhishma that he is "the root of this destruction" (\*204), and Bhishma's hymn after Krishna returns (\*205) are rejected and not narrated.
* **Bhishma's promise.** After Duryodhana's reproach, Bhishma says the Pandavas "cannot be beaten in battle even by the gods with Vasava", but that he will "hold back all the sons of Pandu … alone" today (6.54.40–42).
* **Duryodhana wounded.** Bhima's arrow knocks Duryodhana senseless, and his charioteer carries him off (6.54.15–17).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*187–\*217.

**Choices.**

* 6.52.17: Arjuna is described without being named, as "the guardian of the whole world, whose own guardian is Janardana", as the text does.
* 6.53.6 *anumānena saṃjñābhir nāmagotraiś ca*: "by guesswork, by signs and by the shouting of names and families".
* 6.54.23 *amāvāsyāṃ gatau … somasūryau*: "like the moon and the sun together in the sky on the night of the new moon".
* 6.55.23 *māyākṛtātmānam*: "had made himself many by magic".
* 6.55.83 *rathāṅgam*: "discus". The word can mean "chariot wheel", and some retellings have Krishna pick up a chariot wheel. In this passage the weapon is named Sudarshana (6.55.89), and Bhishma hails him as "you who hold the bow Sharnga and the discus" (*śārṅgarathāṅgapāṇe*, 6.55.94).
* 6.55.89–90: the discus as a lotus growing from Narayana's navel is the text's image, kept whole.
* 6.55.97 *kila*: "they say".
* 6.55.125 *mahāvaitaraṇī*: "the great Vaitarani", the river of the underworld.

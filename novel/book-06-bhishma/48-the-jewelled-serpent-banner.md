# The Jewelled Serpent Banner

"Then King Duryodhana, recovered from the stupor," said Sanjaya, "again held off
the unfalling Bhima with showers of arrows. Your sons, great chariot fighters,
came together again, met Bhima in battle and fought him, ready. Bhimasena too
reached his own chariot again in the battle, climbed onto it, the strong-armed
one, and went to where your son was. He took up his bright bow of great speed,
firm, that takes life away, and pierced your sons with arrows in battle. Then
King Duryodhana struck the very strong Bhimasena hard in the vitals with a very
sharp iron arrow. Badly wounded by your son the bowman, the great archer, his
eyes red with anger, swung up his bow at speed. He struck Duryodhana with three
arrows in the arms and the chest. Struck like that, the king did not move, like
the king of mountains.

"All Duryodhana's younger brothers saw the two of them angry in battle, striking
each other. They were brave men who had given up all thought of life. They
remembered the plan made earlier to seize Bhima of terrible deeds. Making up
their minds, they set about seizing him. As they came at him in battle, the very
strong Bhimasena went out against them, great king, as an elephant goes against
rival elephants. Very angry, the splendid one of great fame struck your son
Chitrasena with an iron arrow, great king. So too he struck your other sons,
Bharata, with arrows of many kinds in battle, gold-feathered and very swift.

"Then came the twelve great chariot fighters led by Abhimanyu, whom the King of
Dharma had sent to follow Bhimasena's tracks. They set their own ranks in order
on every side and went out against your very strong sons, great king. Your very
strong sons saw those brave men on their chariots, splendid as the sun and fire.
They were all great archers, shining, wrapped in glory, blazing in the great
battle, bright in golden armour. Your sons left Bhima in battle. The Kaunteya
could not bear that they should get away alive. He followed them all again and
harried your sons.

"Then the great chariot fighters in your army, led by Duryodhana, saw Abhimanyu
joined in battle with Bhimasena and Prishata's grandson. Bows in hand, they
drove hard with swift horses to where those chariots were. Then in the
afternoon, king, a great battle broke out between your strong men and the enemy,
Bharata.

"Abhimanyu killed Vikarna's very swift horses and then covered him with
twenty-five small arrows. Leaving his chariot with its horses killed, the great
chariot fighter Vikarna climbed onto Chitrasena's shining chariot, king.
Arjuna's son covered the two brothers, standing on one chariot, the increasers
of the Kurus, with a net of arrows, Bharata. Then Durjaya and Vikarna pierced
Krishna's grandson with five iron arrows, and Krishna's grandson did not
tremble, unmoving like Meru. Duhshasana fought the five Kekayas in battle, sir,
king of kings, and it was a wonder. The sons of Draupadi, angry in battle, held
off Duryodhana. Each of them struck your son with three arrows, lord of the
people. Your son, hard to withstand, struck the sons of Draupadi in battle one
by one with sharp arrows, king. Pierced by them too, and drenched in blood, he
shone like a mountain with streams mixed with mineral ores. Bhishma too, king,
mighty in battle, drove the Pandava army as a herdsman drives a herd of beasts.

"Then the sound of Gandiva was heard, lord of the people, to the right of the
army, as Partha killed his enemies. Headless trunks rose up there on every side
in the battle, in the Kuru armies and the Pandavas', Bharata. The tigers among
men crossed the ocean of the army in boats of chariots. Its water was blood, its
whirlpools chariots, its islands elephants, its waves horses. The best of men
were seen fallen there in hundreds and thousands, with hands cut off, armour
gone, bodies broken. With killed elephants in rut, drenched in floods of blood,
the earth looked as if strewn with mountains, best of the Bharatas. There we saw
a wonder, yours and theirs, Bharata: there was not one man there who did not
want to fight. So the heroes, yours and the Pandavas', fought, seeking great
glory and longing for victory in battle.

---

"Then, as the sun was turning red, King Duryodhana, eager for battle, ran at
Bhima wanting to kill him. Seeing that hero among men, his firm enemy, coming,
Bhimasena in great anger said, 'This is the time I have longed for through a
heap of years. Today I will kill you, unless you leave the battle. Today, when
you are killed, I will drive away Kunti's suffering and all our living in the
forest, and Draupadi's suffering. Since you, a gambler, despise the Pandavas,
son of Gandhari, see the disaster that has come of that wrong. Following Karna's
opinion and Subala's son's, you did whatever you liked long ago, giving no
thought to the Pandavas. In your folly you despise the Dasharha when he pleads,
and you gave Uluka his message as if you were glad. Today I will kill you with
your followers and your kin. I will settle that wrong you did long ago.'

"Having said this, he drew his terrible bow and swung it round again and again.
He took up terrible arrows, bright as great thunderbolts. In his anger he
quickly loosed twenty-six at Suyodhana with force, like flames of a blazing
fire, like thunderbolts, flying straight. Then with two he pierced his bow and
with two his charioteer. With four he took his swift horses to Yama's home. With
two well-drawn arrows the crusher of enemies cut the king's parasol in battle
from his fine chariot. With three he cut his blazing, splendid banner, and
having cut it he roared aloud while your son watched. That bright banner,
adorned with many jewels, fell at once from the chariot to the ground, like
lightning from a cloud. All the kings saw the banner of the lord of the Kurus
cut down, blazing, bright as the sun, a fine serpent made of jewels. Then the
great chariot fighter Bhima, as if smiling, struck him in battle with ten
arrows, as a great elephant is struck with goads.

"Then the king of the Sindhus, Jayadratha, the best of chariot fighters, took up
the guard of Duryodhana's rear, as fits a good man. Kripa, the best of chariot
fighters, took the Kauravya of measureless power, the impatient Duryodhana, up
onto his chariot, king. Deeply wounded by Bhimasena in battle and in pain, King
Duryodhana sat down on the chariot floor. Then Jayadratha, wanting to kill
Bhima, surrounded him and blocked his way on every side with many thousands of
chariots.

"Then, king, Dhrishtaketu, the mighty Abhimanyu, the Kekayas and the sons of
Draupadi fought your sons. Chitrasena, Suchitra, Chitrashva, Chitradarshana,
Charuchitra, Sucharu, and Nanda and Upanandaka: these eight great archers,
delicate and glorious, surrounded Abhimanyu's chariot on every side, king. Then
the great-minded Abhimanyu quickly struck them, piercing each with five arrows
with bent joints, like thunderbolts and death, loosed from his bright weapon.
Unable to bear it, they all rained sharp arrows on Subhadra's son, the best of
chariot fighters, as clouds rain on Mount Meru. Abhimanyu was hard pressed in
battle. But he was mad for battle and skilled in weapons, and he made your men
tremble, great king, as the wielder of the thunderbolt made the great asuras
tremble in the battle of the gods and asuras.

"Then, Bharata, the best of chariot fighters sent fourteen terrible broad arrows
like venomous snakes at Vikarna. He cut his banner, charioteer and horses, as if
dancing in the battle. Then the very strong son of Subhadra sent other arrows at
Vikarna, yellow, unblunted, stone-whetted. Dressed in heron and peacock
feathers, those arrows reached Vikarna, split his body and went into the ground
like blazing snakes. Those arrows with golden feathers and tips were seen on the
ground, wet with Vikarna's blood, as if vomiting blood. Seeing Vikarna pierced
through, his own brothers ran in battle at the chariots led by Subhadra's son.
Reaching them quickly, the furious men, mad for battle, pierced one another in
battle, standing on their chariots, bright as the sun.

"Durmukha pierced Shrutakarma with seven swift arrows, cut his banner with one
and his charioteer with seven. He came up and killed his horses, swift as the
wind, covered with nets of gold, with six, and brought down his charioteer.
Standing on his chariot with its horses killed, the great chariot fighter
Shrutakarma in anger hurled a spear like a blazing great meteor. Shining and
very sharp, it split the glorious Durmukha's broad armour, tore through and went
into the ground. Seeing him without a chariot there, the very strong Sutasoma
took him up onto his own chariot while all the troops watched.

"The hero Shrutakirti went against your glorious son Jayatsena in battle, king,
wanting to kill him. As the great-souled Shrutakirti drew his bow, your son
Jayatsena, as if laughing, Bharata, cut it in battle with a very sharp
razor-headed arrow. Seeing his brother with his bow cut, the splendid Shatanika
came to him, roaring again and again like a lion. Shatanika drew his bow hard in
battle and quickly pierced Jayatsena with ten arrows. Then with another very
sharp arrow that split every kind of armour Shatanika pierced Jayatsena hard in
the heart. While that went on, Dushkarna, near his brother, wild with anger, cut
the bow of Nakula's son in battle. Then the very strong Shatanika took up
another fine bow that could bear any load and fitted sharp arrows. Calling out
to Dushkarna in front of his brother, 'Stand! Stand!', he loosed sharp arrows
like blazing snakes. Then with one he quickly cut his bow and with two his
charioteer, sir, and pierced him with seven. Free from blemish himself, he
quickly killed all his speckled horses, swift as the mind, with twelve sharp
arrows. Then in anger he pierced Dushkarna hard in the heart in battle with
another broad arrow, well loosed, that brought him down.

"Seeing Dushkarna killed, king, five great chariot fighters surrounded Shatanika
on every side, wanting to kill him. As the glorious Shatanika was being covered
with floods of arrows, the five Kekaya brothers ran up in fury. Seeing them
come, your sons, great chariot fighters, went out against them, great king, like
elephants against great elephants. Durmukha and Durjaya, the young Durmarshana,
Shatrunjaya and Shatrusaha, all angry and glorious, went out together against
the Kekaya brothers, great king. Their chariots were like cities, yoked to
horses swift as the mind, adorned with flags bright with many colours. The
heroes held fine bows and had bright armour and banners. They went into the
enemy army like lions from one forest into another. Their very tumultuous battle
went on, very fierce, with chariots and elephants tangled, as they killed one
another, king, each having wronged the other, swelling the kingdom of Yama.

"When the sun had set a moment before, they fought a very cruel battle. Chariot
fighters and horsemen were scattered in thousands. Then Bhishma son of Shantanu
in anger destroyed the army of those great-souled men with arrows with bent
joints, and with his arrows took the Panchala troops to Yama's home. Having
broken the Pandava army so, the great archer withdrew the troops and went to his
own camp, king. The King of Dharma too, seeing Dhrishtadyumna and Wolf-belly,
kissed them both on the head and, glad, went to his camp."

# The First Clash

"When the armies were drawn up like this, mine and the others'," said
Dhritarashtra, "who struck first there, the Kurus or the Pandavas?"

"Your son Duryodhana, king, with his brothers, set out with his army, putting
Bhishma at the front," said Sanjaya. "So too all the Pandavas, led by Bhimasena,
set out with glad hearts, wanting to fight Bhishma. There were shouts and cries,
saws and cow-horns, drums, tabors and kettledrums, and the noise of horses and
elephants in both armies, king. Then they charged at us, and we roared back, and
there was a great tumult. In that great gathering of the Pandavas and
Dhritarashtra's sons, the great armies shook with the sound of conches and
drums, like forests shaken by the wind. The noise of the armies, crowded with
kings, elephants, horses and chariots, as they came on at that unlucky hour, was
tumultuous, like oceans stirred up by the wind.

"When that tumultuous noise arose, making the hair stand on end, strong-armed
Bhimasena bellowed like a bull. Bhimasena's roar rose above the sound of conches
and drums, the trumpeting of elephants and the lions' roars of the troops. His
cry rose above all the sounds of the horses neighing in the armies by the
thousand. When they heard his roar, like the roar of a thundering cloud, as loud
as Shakra's thunderbolt, your troops were terrified. All the animals let go dung
and urine at the hero's cry, as other beasts do at a lion's. Showing his
terrible self, roaring like a great cloud, frightening your sons, he came at
your army.

"As that great archer came, his brothers surrounded him, covering him with
masses of arrows as clouds cover the sun: your son Duryodhana, Durmukha,
Duhsaha, Shala, the atiratha Duhshasana and Durmarshana, king, Vivimshati,
Chitrasena, the great chariot fighter Vikarna, Purumitra, Jaya, Bhoja and mighty
Somadatta's son. They shook their great bows like clouds shaking lightning, and
took up iron arrows like poisonous snakes that have shed their skins. Then the
sons of Draupadi, the great chariot fighter Subhadra's son, Nakula, Sahadeva and
Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, went against Dhritarashtra's sons, striking
them with sharp arrows, like thunderbolts of great force striking the peaks of
mountains.

"In that first clash, with the terrible sound of bowstrings and arm guards, no
one turned his back, of yours or of the enemy. I saw the lightness of Drona's
pupils, bull of the Bharatas, hitting their marks, king, as they shot arrows
thick and fast. The roar of the twanging bows did not cease. Blazing arrows went
out like lights from the sky. All the other kings, Bharata, looked on like
spectators at that terrible, splendid meeting of kin. Then the great chariot
fighters, roused, having wronged one another, strove in rivalry, king. The
armies of the Kurus and Pandavas, crowded with elephants, horses and chariots,
shone in the battle like a picture painted on cloth.

"Then all the kings, bows in hand, charged with their armies at your son's
command. And at Yudhishthira's command the kings in their thousands charged,
roaring, at your son's army. The clash of the troops of both armies was fierce.
The sun was hidden, covered by the dust of the army. Among those who fought, who
broke, and who turned back again, no difference could be seen between our own
and the enemy. But in that tumultuous, very terrible battle, your father shone
out above all the armies.

---

"In the forenoon of that terrible day, lord of the people, the very dreadful
battle began that cut the bodies of kings. The Kurus and the Pandavas, wanting
victory in battle, roared like lions, making heaven and earth echo. There were
shouts and cries with the sound of palms and conches, and the lions' roars of
heroes roaring back at one another. Bowstrings struck against arm guards
sounded, bull of the Bharatas, and the tramp of foot soldiers, the great noise
of horses, the falling of goads and hooks, the clash of weapons and the sound of
the bells of elephants charging one another. When that tumultuous noise arose,
making the hair stand on end, the rumble of chariots was like the thunder of
rain clouds. Setting their minds on cruelty, giving up their lives, all of them,
with banners raised, charged the Pandavas.

"Shantanu's son himself, king, rushed at Dhananjaya in battle, taking up his
terrible bow, like the rod of Time. Arjuna too, full of splendour, took up
Gandiva, famous in the world, and rushed at Ganga's son at the head of the
battle. Those two tigers of the Kurus each wanted to kill the other. Mighty
Ganga's son pierced Partha in battle and did not shake him, and in the same way
the Pandava did not shake Bhishma, king.

"The great archer Satyaki went at Kritavarma, and there was a tumultuous battle
between them that made the hair stand on end. Satyaki struck Kritavarma, and
Kritavarma Satyaki, with terrible arrows, carving each other. Those two mighty
men, their bodies covered with arrows, shone like two flowering kimshuka trees
in spring, bright with blossom. The great archer Abhimanyu fought Brihadbala.
The king of Kosala cut down Subhadra's son's banner in the battle and brought
down his charioteer, lord of the people. When his chariot and charioteer had
been brought down, Subhadra's son in anger pierced Brihadbala with nine arrows,
great king. Then the crusher of enemies with two more yellow broad-headed arrows
cut down his banner with one and his rear guard with the other. In their anger,
king, they carved each other with sharp arrows.

"Bhimasena fought your son Duryodhana, proud, arrogant in battle, the great
chariot fighter with whom he had his feud. Those two tigers among men, the
mighty chiefs of the Kurus, rained showers of arrows on each other on the
battlefield. Seeing those two great men, skilled fighters of many skills, all
beings were filled with wonder, Bharata. Duhshasana went at the great chariot
fighter Nakula and pierced him with many sharp arrows that cut the vitals.
Madri's son, as if laughing, cut down his banner and his bow with its arrow with
sharp arrows, Bharata, and then struck him with twenty-five small arrows. Your
son, hard to withstand, cut Nakula's yoke and pole with arrows in the great
battle and brought down his banner. Durmukha went at mighty Sahadeva and pierced
him with a rain of arrows as he strove in the great battle. Then the hero
Sahadeva brought down Durmukha's charioteer in the great battle with a very
sharp arrow. The two of them, fierce in war, meeting each other in battle,
terrified each other with terrible arrows, each wanting to repay the other's
blows.

"King Yudhishthira himself went at the king of the Madras, and the lord of the
Madras cut his bow in two, sir. Kunti's son Yudhishthira threw aside the cut
bow, took another, swifter and stronger, and in his anger covered the lord of
the Madras with arrows with bent joints, crying, 'Stand! Stand!' Then
Dhrishtadyumna rushed at Drona, Bharata. Drona in his great anger cut in three
the strong bow, deadly to life, of Dhrishtadyumna as he strove in battle, and
sent a very terrible arrow, like a second rod of Time, which sank into his body
in the battle. Then Drupada's son took another bow and fourteen arrows and
pierced Drona back in battle. The two of them, very angry, fought each other
fiercely.

"Shankha went at Somadatta's son, fierce in battle, fierce himself, great king,
and cried, 'Stand! Stand!' The hero pierced his right arm in battle, and
Somadatta's son struck Shankha in the region of the collarbone. There was a
terrible battle between those two proud men, lord of the people, swift in battle
like Vritra and Vasava. The great chariot fighter Dhrishtaketu, of boundless
spirit, furious in form, rushed at Bahlika, who was furious in battle, lord of
the people. Then Bahlika, king, struck impatient Dhrishtaketu with many arrows
and roared a lion's roar. The king of the Chedis in his anger quickly pierced
Bahlika with nine arrows in battle, like an elephant in rut piercing another.
The two of them there in battle, very angry and roaring again and again, met
like Mars and Mercury.

"Ghatotkacha, of cruel deeds, went at the rakshasa Alambusa, of cruel deeds, as
Shakra went at Bala in battle. Ghatotkacha in his anger tore that mighty
rakshasa with ninety sharp arrows, Bharata. Alambusa in the battle held off
mighty Bhimasena's son in many ways with arrows with bent joints. The two of
them, wounded by arrows, shone in battle like mighty Bala and Shakra in the war
of the gods and asuras. Mighty Shikhandi went at Drona's son in battle, king.
Then Ashvatthama in his anger pierced Shikhandi as he stood, deeply, with a very
sharp iron arrow, and shook him. Shikhandi too, king, struck Drona's son with a
well-tempered, sharp, whetted arrow, and they struck each other in the fight
with arrows of many kinds.

"Virata, lord of an army, went quickly at Bhagadatta, a hero in battle, king,
and then the battle began. Virata, struck by Bhagadatta with a rain of arrows,
rained on him in anger like a cloud raining on a mountain. Then Bhagadatta
quickly covered Virata, lord of the earth, in battle, as a cloud covers the
rising sun. Kripa son of Sharadvat went at Brihatkshatra the Kaikeya and covered
him with a rain of arrows, Bharata. The Kekaya in anger filled Gautama with a
shower of arrows. Each killed the other's horses and cut the other's bow, and
without chariots the two impatient men met for a sword fight. That battle
between them was terrible and very dreadful. Then King Drupada, scorcher of
enemies, went gladly at Jayadratha of Sindhu, who was glad too. Then the king of
Sindhu struck Drupada in battle with three arrows, and Drupada pierced him back.
There was a terrible, very dreadful battle between them, pleasing to watch, like
that of Venus and Mars.

"Your son Vikarna went with swift horses at mighty Sutasoma, and then the battle
began. Vikarna pierced Sutasoma with arrows and did not shake him, nor did
Sutasoma shake Vikarna, and it was a wonder. The great chariot fighter
Chekitana, valiant for the Pandavas' sake, rushed in great anger at Susharma,
tiger among men. Susharma, great king, held back the great chariot fighter
Chekitana in battle with a great rain of arrows. Chekitana too, in his fury,
covered Susharma with arrows in the great battle, as a great cloud covers a
mountain. Valiant Shakuni rushed at valiant Prativindhya, king of kings, like an
elephant in rut at another. Yudhishthira's son in his anger tore Subala's son in
battle with sharp arrows, as Maghavat tore a danava. Very wise Shakuni tore
Prativindhya with arrows with bent joints as he struck back in battle.
Shrutakarma went at Sudakshina, the valiant great chariot fighter of the
Kambojas, in battle, king of kings. Sudakshina pierced Sahadeva's son, the great
chariot fighter, in battle and did not shake him, like Mount Mainaka. Then
Shrutakarma in his anger struck the great chariot fighter of the Kambojas with
many arrows, as if tearing him apart on every side.

"Then Iravan, in anger, ready, went against impatient Shrutayus, who was readier
still. Arjuna's son, the great chariot fighter, killed his horses in battle and
gave a very great roar that filled the army. Shrutayus in his anger killed
Phalguni's horses in battle with the end of his club, and the fight went on.
Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti engaged the great chariot fighter Kuntibhoja, the
hero, with his army and his son in battle. There we saw the wonderful courage of
the Avantis, who fought steadily with a great army. Anuvinda struck Kuntibhoja
with his club, and Kuntibhoja quickly covered him with masses of arrows.
Kuntibhoja's son pierced Vinda with arrows, and he pierced him back, and it was
a wonder. The five Kekaya brothers fought the five Gandharas in battle, sir,
with their armies. Your son Virabahu fought Virata's son Uttara, the finest
chariot fighter, and pierced him with sharp arrows, and Uttara too pierced that
steady man with sharp arrows. The king of the Chedis rushed at Uluka in battle,
king, and Uluka at him with sharp feathered arrows. There was a terrible battle
between them, lord of the people. Unconquered, in great anger, they tore each
other.

"So there were thousands of single combats of chariots, elephants, horses and
foot soldiers, yours and theirs, in a mass. For a moment that battle was
pleasant to see. Then, king, as if gone mad, nothing could be made out. Elephant
went at elephant in battle, chariot fighter at chariot fighter, horse at horse
and foot soldier at foot soldier. Then the battle became very hard to bear and
confused, as the heroes met one another in the fight. The divine seers, the
perfected ones and the charanas gathered there and watched that terrible battle,
like the battle of the gods and asuras. Then thousands of tuskers and chariots,
sir, floods of horses and floods of men met in confusion. Everywhere chariots,
elephants and foot soldiers, and horsemen, tiger among men, were seen fighting
again and again."

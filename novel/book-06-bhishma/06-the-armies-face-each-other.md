# The Armies Face Each Other

"Just as the blessed Vyasa, Krishna Dvaipayana, had said," said Sanjaya, "all
the kings came together. That day the moon passed into the region of Magha.
Seven great planets came together in the sky, blazing. At its rising the sun
seemed split in two, and it rose in the sky with a blazing crest. In the burning
quarter jackals and crows howled, eaters of flesh and blood, hungry for bodies.

"Day after day the old grandfather of the Kurus and the son of Bharadvaja rose
at dawn, composed themselves and said, 'Victory to the sons of Pandu!' Then
those two subduers of enemies fought for you, as they had agreed.

"Your father Devavrata, who knew every point of dharma, called the kings
together and said this. 'Kshatriyas, this great door to heaven stands open for
you. Go through it to the world of Shakra and of Brahma. This is your eternal
road, which your fathers and their fathers have taken. Honour yourselves, and
keep your minds steady in battle. Nabhaga and Yayati, Mandhatri, Nahusha and
Nriga reached perfection and the highest place by deeds like these. For a
kshatriya it is adharma to die of sickness at home. When he meets his death in
battle, that is his eternal dharma.'

"When Bhishma had spoken to them like this, bull among the Bharatas, the kings
went out to their divisions, adorning them with their fine chariots. But Karna
Vaikartana, with his ministers and kin, had been made by Bhishma to lay down his
weapons in the battle, bull among the Bharatas. Without Karna, your sons and
your kings went out with lion roars, making the ten quarters ring. Those
divisions shone with white umbrellas and flags, with banners, elephants and
horses, with chariots and foot soldiers. The earth was in turmoil with the sound
of drums and tabors, the beat of kettledrums and the rumble of chariot wheels.
The great chariot fighters shone with their golden armlets and bracelets and
their bows, like moving mountains. Bhishma, commander of the Kuru army, stood
under his great palm banner with its five stars, like the spotless sun.

"Your great archers, the kings, bull among the Bharatas, took their places as
Shantanu's son directed, king. Shaibya of the Govasanas went with all the kings
on a lordly elephant fit for a king, with a flag on it. Lotus-coloured, he stood
at the head of all the divisions. Ashvatthama went out ready, with a lion's tail
on his banner. Shrutayus, Chitrasena, Purumitra and Vivimshati, Shalya,
Bhurishravas and Vikarna, the great chariot fighter: these seven great archers,
led by Drona's son, went ahead of Bhishma in their chariots of fine colours.
Their high banners, made of Jambunada gold, were seen shining, adorning their
fine chariots. The banner of Drona, chief of teachers, was a golden altar
adorned with a water pot, with a bow beside it.

"Duryodhana, drawing along a division of many hundreds of thousands, had a great
jewelled elephant on his banner. The Paurava and the Kalinga, Sudakshina of
Kamboja, Kshemadhanva and Sumitra stood in their chariots in front of him. With
a costly chariot and a bull on his banner, the king of Magadha went as if
drawing the van of the army along. That army of the easterners, guarded by the
lord of Anga and by great Kripa, looked like a mass of autumn clouds. Jayadratha
of great fame stood at the head of a division and shone with his fine silver
banner of the boar. A hundred thousand chariots were under his command, eight
thousand elephants and sixty thousand horsemen. That van, guarded by the lord of
Sindhu, king, a great force of countless chariots, elephants and horses, shone.

"With sixty thousand chariots and ten thousand elephants, the lord of all the
Kalingas went out, with Ketuman. His huge elephants shone like mountains,
adorned with engines, lances, quivers and flags. The Kalinga king shone with his
fine banner of a tree, a white umbrella, a gold neck ornament and a fan of yak
tails. Ketuman too, mounted on an elephant with a fine and wonderful goad, shone
in the battle, king, like the sun in a cloud. Blazing with energy, King
Bhagadatta went mounted on a great elephant, like the bearer of the thunderbolt.
Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, on elephants' necks, equal to Bhagadatta, followed
Ketuman.

"That array had divisions of chariots, its head was made of the best of
elephants, and its wings were of horses. It was fierce, it seemed to fly, and it
faced every way as it struck. It was arranged by Drona, king, and by the king,
Shantanu's son, and by the teacher's son, by Bahlika and by Kripa.

"Then, after a moment, a tumultuous sound was heard that shook the heart, great
king, from the warriors eager to fight. With the roar of conches and drums, the
trumpeting of elephants and the rumble of chariot wheels, the earth seemed to
split. With the neighing of horses and the roaring of the warriors there, sky
and quarters were filled with sound in a moment. The armies of your sons and of
the Pandavas, whom no one can face, trembled as they met.

"There the elephants and the chariots adorned with Jambunada gold shone like
clouds with lightning. Your banners of many shapes, lord of men, with their
golden bands, shone like blazing fires. Ours and theirs alike were seen,
Bharata, bright as Indra's banners in Indra's halls. The heroes in their golden
armour, shining like the blazing sun, looked like blazing planets. Bright with
their raised weapons, with leather guards on their arms and flags on their
chariots, the great archers with eyes like bulls stood at the head of the army.

"Your sons guarded Bhishma's back, lord of men: Duhshasana, Durvishaha, Durmukha
and Duhsaha, Vivimshati, Chitrasena and Vikarna, the great chariot fighter,
Satyavrata, Purumitra, Jaya, Bhurishravas and Shala. Twenty thousand chariots
followed them. There were the Abhishahas, the Shurasenas, the Shibis and the
Vasatis, the Shalvas, the Matsyas and the Ambashthas. There were the Trigartas
and the Kekayas, the Sauviras, the Kitavas, and the easterners, the westerners,
the northerners and the Malavas. These twelve peoples, all brave and ready to
give up their bodies, guarded the grandfather with a great line of chariots. The
king of Magadha followed that division of chariots with a division of ten
thousand swift elephants. In the middle of the army were the wheel guards of the
chariots and the foot guards of the elephants, six million of them. The foot
soldiers went in front, with bows, shields and swords in their hands, many
hundreds of thousands, fighting with claw knives and darts. Your son's eleven
akshauhinis, Bharata, looked like the Ganga where it meets the Yamuna, great
king."

"When he saw eleven akshauhinis drawn up," said Dhritarashtra, "how did
Yudhishthira the Pandava array himself against them with his small army? He
knows the human array, the divine, the gandharva and the asura. How did the son
of Kunti draw up the Pandavas against Bhishma?"

"When he saw Dhritarashtra's divisions drawn up," said Sanjaya, "the righteous
king, the Pandava whose soul is dharma, spoke to Dhananjaya.

"'My son, they know from the words of the great seer Brihaspati that a few
should fight in close order, and many may be spread out at will. When a few
fight many, the army should be shaped like a needle's point. And our army is
much smaller than the enemy's. Keep in mind this word of the great seer, and
draw up the array, Pandava.'

"When he had heard the righteous king, Phalguna answered him. 'I shall draw up
for you, king, an array that is very hard to conquer. It is called the
Immovable, the Vajra, and it was made by the god with the thunderbolt in his
hand. Bhima, best of fighters, who is like a gale in battle and unbearable to
the enemy, will fight at our head. That best of men, skilled in the ways of war,
will go ahead of us as our leader, crushing the spirit of the enemy army. When
they see him, all the kings led by Duryodhana will turn back in panic, like
small beasts that see a lion. We shall all take shelter behind Bhima, best of
fighters, a wall with no fear on any side, as the immortals shelter behind the
god with the thunderbolt in his hand. There is no man in the world who can bear
to look at Vrikodara, that bull among men of very fierce deeds, when he is
angry. Carrying his strong mace of adamant and moving with great speed,
Bhimasena could dry up even the ocean. The Kekayas are here, and Dhrishtaketu
and mighty Chekitana. And there, lord of men, with their ministers, stand the
heirs of Dhritarashtra, watching him.'

"So Bibhatsu spoke. And as Partha spoke, sir, all the armies honoured him in the
field with glad words. When strong-armed Dhananjaya had said this, he did it.
Phalguna quickly drew up those forces and advanced.

"When they saw the Kurus advancing, the great army of the Pandavas looked like
the Ganga in flood, still, yet flowing. Bhimasena was their leader, with
Dhrishtadyumna, son of Prishata, Nakula and Sahadeva, and mighty Dhrishtaketu.
Then, behind them, the king, surrounded by an akshauhini, having got it ready,
guarded the rear with his brothers and sons. The two sons of Madri, of great
splendour, guarded Bhima's wheels. Draupadi's sons, with Subhadra's son, guarded
his back with speed. Dhrishtadyumna of Panchala, a great chariot fighter,
guarded them, with the Prabhadrakas, brave in battle and the best of chariot
fighters. Behind them, guarded by Arjuna, went Shikhandi, bull among the
Bharatas, ready for Bhishma's destruction. Yuyudhana, a great chariot fighter,
guarded Arjuna's back, and the two Panchalas, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas, guarded
his wheels.

"King Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, was in the middle division, with great
elephants in rut, like moving mountains. The great-minded Yajnasena of Panchala,
full of valour, followed Virata with an akshauhini, for the Pandavas' sake. On
their chariots stood great banners, king, bright as the sun and moon, adorned
with the finest gold and bearing many devices. Then Dhrishtadyumna, the great
chariot fighter, moved up behind and guarded Yudhishthira with his brothers and
sons. Over all the many banners on your chariots and the enemy's, one banner
stood out alone: Arjuna's, the great monkey.

"The foot soldiers went in front, with swords, spears and lances in their hands,
many hundreds of thousands, guarding Bhimasena. Ten thousand elephants, their
temples and mouths streaming with rut, brave, gleaming with nets of gold like
mountains, dripping with rut like clouds pouring rain, smelling of lotuses,
followed the king like moving mountains. Bhimasena, dragging his terrible mace
like an iron bar, drew the great army along, great-minded, and no one could come
near him. He was hard to look at, like the burning sun in its garland of rays.
The warriors on every side could not look at him when he came close.

"This array, called the Vajra, was hard to break and faced every way. It was
terrible, with bows for its lightning and banners, and it was guarded by the
bowman of the Gandiva. The Pandavas stood drawn up in it against your army.
Guarded by the Pandavas, it could not be beaten in the world of men.

"As the armies stood at dawn, waiting for the sun to rise, a wind blew with
drops of rain, and thunder came from a cloudless sky. Fierce winds blew low from
every side, dragging gravel along. The dust they raised covered the world in
darkness. A great meteor fell towards the east, bull among the Bharatas. It
struck the rising sun and shattered with a great noise. As the armies made
ready, bull among the Bharatas, the sun rose without light. The earth shook with
a roar and split with a noise, best of the Bharatas. There were many claps of
thunder in all quarters, king. A thick dust rose, and nothing could be seen. The
great banners, bright as the sun, with their flags, hung with nets of little
bells and golden garlands, were shaken suddenly by the wind, and everything
jingled, as in a forest of palms.

"So the Pandavas, tigers among men, who delighted in battle, stood drawn up
against your son's army. The marrow of the warriors seemed to melt, bull among
the Bharatas, when they saw Bhimasena standing in front with his mace in his
hand."

"At sunrise, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "who were first to be glad and eager
to fight: mine, led by Bhishma, or the Pandavas, led by Bhima? Whom did the moon
and the sun and the wind stand behind? At whose army did the beasts of prey
howl? Whose young men had bright faces? Tell me all of it truly, as it was."

"Both armies came on alike, lord of men," said Sanjaya, "and both arrays were
glad. Both were bright, like lines of forest, and both were full of elephants,
chariots and horses. Both armies were huge and terrible, and both were hard to
bear, Bharata. Both were let loose to win heaven, and both were guarded by good
and noble men. The Kurus, the sons of Dhritarashtra, stood facing west, and the
Parthas stood facing east, ready to fight. The Kauravas' army was like the army
of the lord of the daityas, and the Pandavas' like the army of the lord of the
gods.

"A clean wind blew behind the Pandavas, and beasts of prey howled at the sons of
Dhritarashtra. Your son's elephants could not bear the strong smell of rut from
the great elephants.

"Duryodhana sat on a lotus-coloured elephant with a golden girth, of good breed
and strength and in rut, in the middle of the Kurus, praised by bards and
Magadhas. A white umbrella as bright as the moon was over him, and a golden
garland shone on his head. Shakuni, king of Gandhara, guarded him on every side
with the mountain men and the Gandharas. Old Bhishma was at the head of the
whole army, with a white umbrella, a white bow and his conch, a white turban, a
pale banner and white horses, bright as a white mountain. With him were all the
sons of Dhritarashtra, a part of the Bahlikas and Shala, the Ambashtha
kshatriyas and those of Sindhu, the Sauviras and the brave men of the Five
Rivers.

"Great-armed Drona, with his golden chariot and red horses, of undaunted spirit,
the famous teacher of all the kings, stood guarding the army from behind, like
Indra. Jayadratha, son of Vriddhakshatra, was in the middle of the whole army,
with Bhurishravas, Purumitra and Jaya. The Shalvas, the Matsyas and all the
Kekaya brothers were ready to fight with their divisions of elephants. Great
Sharadvata, Gautama, the great archer and fighter of many skills, at the
northern end, guarded the army from the north with the Shakas, Kiratas, Yavanas
and Pahlavas. Kritavarma, of great strength, guarded by great chariot fighters,
the Andhakas, Vrishnis and Bhojas, and by the Saurashtrakas and the Nairritas,
weapons in hand, guarded your army from the south.

"Ten thousand chariots of the Samshaptakas, set apart to be Arjuna's death or
his victory, went wherever Arjuna went, king, masters of weapons, with the brave
Trigartas.

"You had more than a hundred thousand elephants, Bharata. To each elephant there
were a hundred chariots, to each chariot a hundred horses, to each horse ten
bowmen, and to each bowman ten men with shields. So Bhishma drew up your
divisions, Bharata. Day after day, as each day came, Bhishma, son of Shantanu,
the leader, formed arrays, human and divine, gandharva and asura. Crowded with
great chariot fighters like the sea at the moon's change, Bhishma's array of the
sons of Dhritarashtra faced west in battle.

"Your army, lord of men, had no end to it and was terrible, and the Pandavas'
was not. But I think that army is the great one and the one no one can overcome
whose leaders are Keshava and Arjuna."

"When King Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, saw the great army of the sons of
Dhritarashtra ready for battle," said Sanjaya, "he lost heart. When the Pandava
saw the unbreakable array Bhishma had made, it seemed to him that it could not
be broken, and in dismay he said to Arjuna, 'Dhananjaya, strong arms, how can we
fight the sons of Dhritarashtra in battle, when the grandfather fights for them?
This array was made by Bhishma, killer of enemies, of great splendour, by the
method laid down in the teachings. It cannot be shaken or broken. We and our
army are in doubt, destroyer of enemies. How shall we come out of this great
array?'

"Then Arjuna, killer of enemies, seeing Partha Yudhishthira dismayed at your
army, king, said to him, 'Hear, lord of the people, how fewer men can beat many
brave men who are greater in wisdom and full of virtues. I shall tell you the
reason, king, since you bear no one ill will. The seer Narada knows it, and so
do Bhishma and Drona, Pandava. They say that long ago, in the war of the gods
and the asuras, the Grandfather told the gods led by great Indra this very
thing: "Those who seek victory do not win so much by strength and valour as by
truth and kindness, by dharma and by effort. Give up adharma, greed and
delusion, take up the effort, and fight without pride. Where dharma is, there is
victory."

"'So know this, king: victory in the battle is certain for us. As Narada told
me, where Krishna is, there is victory. Victory is a quality of Krishna's. It
follows behind Madhava. Victory is one of his qualities, and humility is
another. Govinda, whose energy has no end, is untroubled among hosts of enemies.
He is the most eternal Person. Where Krishna is, there is victory. Long ago he
became Hari Vaikuntha, whose arrows are never blunted, and thundering at the
gods and the asuras he said, "Who is to win?" Those who said, "We shall win with
Krishna," won there. By his grace the gods led by Shakra won the three worlds. I
see no reason for you to be troubled here, Bharata, when the lord of the thirty
gods, who enjoys all things, wishes you victory.'

"Then King Yudhishthira urged on his army, bull among the Bharatas, drawing up
his divisions against Bhishma's. The Pandavas, upholders of the Kurus, drew up
their divisions as they had been told, seeking the highest heaven by fighting
well. In the middle was Shikhandi's division, guarded by Savyasachin, and
Dhrishtadyumna's, guarded by Bhima himself. The right-hand division, king, was
guarded by Yuyudhana, the glorious chief of the Satvatas, a bowman like Shakra.

"Yudhishthira stood in the midst of the elephants in a chariot like great
Indra's, fully equipped, bright with gold and jewels, with golden trappings and
harness. His raised umbrella with ribs of ivory, very white, shone brightly, and
great seers went round the king, keeping him to their right, with hymns of
praise. Priests, old great seers learned in the scriptures, spoke of the enemy's
death and performed blessing rites on every side with prayers, mantras and
herbs. Then that great man, the best of the Kurus, gave clothes and cows, fruit
and flowers and gold coins to the brahmins, and went on like Shakra among the
immortals.

"Arjuna's chariot shone like a thousand suns, with a hundred little bells,
bright with the finest Jambunada gold, garlanded with flames like fire, with
white horses and fine wheels. He stood in it, with the monkey banner, and
Keshava held the reins. He had the Gandiva and arrows in his hand, a bowman
whose equal on earth does not exist and never will. Ready to overturn your son's
army, he wore a very fierce look. The strong-armed man who, without weapons,
with his bare arms could turn men, horses and elephants to ash in battle,
Bhimasena Vrikodara, guarded that hero's chariot with the twins. He was like a
maddened bull or a lion at play, like great Indra on earth. When your warriors
saw Vrikodara at the head of the army, proud as a lord of elephants, their
spirits shook with fear, and they trembled like camels stuck in mud.

"Janardana spoke to Gudakesha, the invincible prince, the best of the Bharatas,
as he stood in the middle of the army. 'He who stands there as their guard,
burning, standing in his strength, who looks at our army like a lion, is
Bhishma, the banner of the Kuru line, who brought thirty horse sacrifices. These
divisions hide that great man as clouds hide the sun. Kill them, hero among men,
and then look for battle with the bull of the Bharatas.'"

"Whose warriors fought gladly in front there, Sanjaya?" said Dhritarashtra. "Who
were high in spirit, and who were downcast and dismayed? In that battle that
shook the heart, who struck first, mine or the Pandavas'? Tell me, Sanjaya. In
whose army rose the scent of perfumes and garlands, and the glad voices of
warriors roaring?"

"The warriors of both armies were full of joy," said Sanjaya. "On both sides
rose the scent of garlands, incense, drinks and perfumes. As the divisions,
closely massed and drawn up, came on in their fury, bull among the Bharatas,
there was a great clash. There was a tumult of music mixed with conches and
drums, of elephants trumpeting and of armies roaring for joy."

**Divergences.**

* **"Victory to the sons of Pandu."** Day after day Bhishma and Drona rise at dawn, say "Victory to the sons of Pandu!", and then fight for Duryodhana "as they had agreed" (6.17.5–6).
* **Karna.** Here Karna "had been made by Bhishma to lay down his weapons" (6.17.13, *nyāsitaḥ … śastraṃ bhīṣmeṇa*). In Book 5 it was Karna who swore not to fight while Bhishma lived (5.153.25; 5.165.27). Both are kept as told. The easterners' army is "guarded by the lord of Anga and by great Kripa" (6.17.28). The text does not say who this lord of Anga is. Ganguli's note naming Karna's son is not in the CE and is not used.
* **Where Krishna is, there is victory.** Arjuna quotes Narada (6.21.12–14). A rejected line (\*77) expands it. See also 6.2.14, "Where dharma is, there is victory", and 6.21.11.
* **No hymn to Durga.** Krishna's advice to Arjuna to sing a hymn to Durga, the hymn and the goddess's blessing are Appendix I No. 1, after 6.22.16. They are not in the CE and are not narrated. See ledger L-34.
* **The monkey on the banner.** In the CE Arjuna's banner is "the great monkey" (6.19.28; 6.22.10). The monkey that roars from it is in a rejected passage (\*74). See ledger L-106.
* **Omens.** Sanjaya's omens on the day (6.17.2–4; 6.19.36–42) are given as he tells them.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*67–\*84 and App. I No. 1.

**Choices.**

* 6.17.20 *govāsanaḥ śaibyaḥ*: "Shaibya of the Govasanas". *Padmavarṇaḥ*, "lotus-coloured", is read with Shaibya, as the CE's verse division has it.
* 6.17.38: the Kaurava array's parts (chariots, elephants for the head, horses for the wings) are given as the text gives them. The array is not named.
* 6.18.16 *śatānām ayutāni ṣaṭ*: "six million" (six times ten thousand hundreds).
* 6.18.17 *nakharaprāsayodhinaḥ*: "fighting with claw knives and darts".
* 6.18.18 *gaṅgeva yamunāntare*: "like the Ganga where it meets the Yamuna"; the sense is uncertain.
* 6.19.14–15: "And there, lord of men, with their ministers, stand the heirs of Dhritarashtra, watching him." The lines are hard; this follows Ganguli's reading.
* 6.19.19 *rājā*: "the king", not named. It may be Virata, whom Drupada follows at 6.19.25.
* 6.19.44 *sraṃsanta iva majjānaḥ*: "The marrow of the warriors seemed to melt."
* 6.20.2 *jaghanyau*: "stand behind", that is, favour.
* 6.20.12 *vārddhakṣatriḥ*: "Jayadratha, son of Vriddhakshatra".
* 6.20.14 *bṛhadbalaḥ*: read as "of great strength", describing Kritavarma. It may instead name Brihadbala of Kosala (6.16.34).
* 6.20.15 *saṃśaptakānām*: "the Samshaptakas", those sworn to fight to the death, here named for the first time.
* 6.21.13 *anyathā vijayaś cāsya saṃnatiś cāparo guṇaḥ*: "Victory is one of his qualities, and humility is another"; the sense is uncertain.
* 6.22.9 *sahasrasūryaḥ*: "shone like a thousand suns".
* 6.22.11: the first half ("Ready to overturn your son's army, he wore a very fierce look") is read of Arjuna; the second half ("The strong-armed man who, without weapons …") of Bhima, who is named in 6.22.12.

# Bharata

"Tell me truly, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "the names of the varshas and the
mountains, and of those who live on the mountains."

"South of Shveta and north of Nila is the varsha called Ramanaka," said Sanjaya.
"The people born there are all of pure birth and lovely to look at, and they are
given to love. They live eleven thousand five hundred years, great king, always
glad at heart.

"South of Shringavat and north of Shveta is the varsha called Hairanvata, where
the river Hairanvati flows. The people there, great king, follow the yakshas.
They are rich, lovely to look at and very strong, and always glad at heart,
king. They live twelve thousand five hundred years, lord of the people.

"Shringavat has three peaks, lord of men. One is made of jewels, one is of
wonderful gold, and one is made of every kind of gem and adorned with mansions.
There the self-shining goddess Shandili always lives. North of that peak, at the
ocean's edge, lord of the people, beyond Shringavat, is the varsha called
Airavata. The sun does not burn there, and the people do not grow old. The moon
and the stars become the only light, as it were. The people born there shine
like lotuses and are the colour of lotuses, with eyes like lotus petals and the
scent of lotus petals. They do not blink, they are fragrant, they eat nothing,
and they have mastered their senses. All of them have fallen from the world of
the gods, and they are free of passion, king. They live thirteen thousand years,
lord of the people, best of the Bharatas.

"North of the ocean of milk the lord Hari Vaikuntha lives, in a cart of gold.
That car has eight wheels. Beings draw it, and it is swift as thought, the
colour of fire, very fast, and adorned with Jambunada gold. He is the lord of
all beings and all-pervading, bull among the Bharatas. He is the drawing in and
the spreading out, the one who acts and the one who makes act. He is earth,
water, space, wind and fire, lord of the earth. He is the sacrifice of all
beings, and fire is his mouth."

When Sanjaya had said this, said Vaishampayana, great-minded King Dhritarashtra
brooded over his sons, lord of the people. When he had thought, great king, he
spoke again.

"No doubt of it, son of a suta, time destroys the world and creates it all
again. Nothing here lasts for ever. The gods call him Nara and Narayana,
all-knowing, who sustains all beings, and Vaikuntha. The Vedas call the lord
Vishnu.

"Now tell me truly of this varsha of Bharata," said Dhritarashtra, "where this
army has swollen so huge, which my son Duryodhana covets beyond all measure. The
sons of Pandu covet it, and my mind clings to it. Tell me, for you are skilled,
Sanjaya."

"The Pandavas are not the ones who covet it," said Sanjaya. "Hear what I say,
king. It is Duryodhana who covets it, and Shakuni, son of Subala, and the other
kshatriyas, lords of many lands, who covet Bharata and cannot bear one another.
Here I shall describe Bharata to you, Bharata. It was dear to the god Indra, and
to Manu Vaivasvata, to Prithu Vainya, king, and to the great Ikshvaku, to
Yayati, Ambarisha, Mandhatri and Nahusha, to Muchukunda and to Shibi Aushinara,
to Rishabha, to Aila and to King Nriga. Bharata was dear to all of them, great
king, lord of kings, and to other mighty kshatriyas, Bharata. I shall tell you
of that varsha as I have heard of it, subduer of enemies. Hear me as I tell you
what you ask, king.

"Mahendra, Malaya, Sahya, Shuktimat, Rikshavat, Vindhya and Pariyatra: these are
the seven great ranges. Near them are thousands of mountains, king, well known,
solid, broad, with bright ridges. Beyond them are others, less known and small,
and small folk live by them. Noble peoples and mlecchas, Kauravya, and men mixed
of both, lord, drink from many rivers. They drink from the Ganga, the Sindhu,
the Sarasvati and the Godavari. There are the Narmada, the great river Bahuda,
the Shatadru and the Chandrabhaga. There are the great river Yamuna, the
Drishadvati, the Vipasha and the Vipapa. There are the Sthulavaluka, the river
Vetravati, the Krishnavena and the Iravati. There are the Vitasta, the Payoshni,
the Devika and the Vedasmriti. There are the Vetasini, the Tridiva, the
Ishkumalini and the Karishini. There are the Chitravaha, the river Chitrasena,
the Gomati and the Dhutapapa. There are the great river Vandana, the Kaushiki,
the Tridiva and the Kritya. There are the Vichitra, the Lohatarini, the
Rathastha and the Shatakumbha. There are the Sarayu, the Charmanvati, the
Vetravati and the Hastisoma. There are the Dish, the Shatavari, the Payoshni and
the Para. There are the Bhaimarathi, the Kaveri, the Chuluka and the Vapi. There
are the Shatabala, the Nichira, the Mahita and the Suprayoga. There are the
Pavitra, the Kundala, the Sindhu and the Vajini. There are the Puramalini, the
Purvabhirama, the Vira and the Bhima. There are the Oghavati, the Palashini, the
Papahara and the Mahendra. There are the Pippalavati, the Parishena, the Asikni
and the Sarala. There are the Bharamardini, the Puruhi, the Pravara and the
Mena. There are the Mogha, the Ghritavati, the Dhumatya and the Atikrishna.
There are the Suchi, the Chhavi, the Sadanira and the Adhrishya. There are the
great river Kushadhara, the Shashikanta, the Shiva and the Viravati. There are
the Vastu, the Suvastu, the Gauri and the Kampana. There are the Hiranvati, the
Hiranvati, the Chitravati and the river Chitrasena. There are the Rathachitra,
the Jyotiratha, the Vishvamitra and the Kapinjala. There are the Upendra, the
Bahula, the Kuchara and the Ambuvahini. There are the Vainandi, the Pinjala, the
Venna and the great river Tungavena. There are the Vidisha, the Krishnavenna,
the Tamra and the Kapila. There are the Shalu, the Suvama, the Vedashva and the
great stream Harisrava. There are the Shighra, the Pichchhila, the river
Bharadvaji and the river Kaushiki. There are the Shona, the Bahuda, the Chandana
and the Durga. There are the Antahshila, the Brahmamedhya, the Brihadvati and
the Charaksha. There are the Mahirohi, the Jambunadi, the Sunasa and the Tamasa.
There are the Dasi, the Trasa, the Varanasi and the Loloddhritakara. There are
the great river Purnasha, the great river Manavi, the great river Vrishabha and
the Sadaniramaya. There are the Vritya, the Mandaga, the Mandavahini and the
Brahmani. There are the Mahagauri, the Durga, the Chitropala and the
Chitrabarha. There are the Manju, the Makaravahini, the Mandakini and the
Vaitarani. There are the great river Koka, the Shuktimati, the Aranya and the
Pushpaveni. There are the Utpalavati, the Lohitya, the Karatoya and the
Vrishabhangini. There are the Kumari, the Rishikulya and the Brahmakulya. And
they drink from the holy Sarasvatis and all the Gangas, sir. All of them are
mothers of the world, and all of them are mighty. There are unknown rivers too,
by the hundred and the thousand. These are the rivers, king, named as I remember
them.

"Now hear me name the peoples. Here are the Kurus and the Panchalas, the
Shalvas, the Madreyas and the Jangalas. There are the Shurasenas, the Kalingas,
the Bodhas and the Maukas. There are the Matsyas, the Sukutis, the Saubalyas and
the Kuntalas. There are the Kashis and the Koshalas, the Chedis, the Vatsas and
the Karushas. There are the Bhojas, the Sindhus and the Pulindakas, the
Uttamaujas and the Dasharnas, and the Mekalas with the Utkalas. There are the
Panchalas, the Kaushijas, the Ekaprishthas and the Yugandharas. There are the
Saudhas, the Madras and the Bhujingas, the Kashis and the Aparakashis. There are
the Jatharas, the Kukkushas and the Sudasharnas, Bharata. There are the Kuntis,
the Avantis and the Aparakuntis. There are the Govindas, the Mandakas, the
Shandas and the Vidarbhas. There are the Anupavasikas, the Ashmakas, the
Pamsurashtras and the Goparashtras. There are the Panitakas, the Adirashtras and
the Sukuttas, Balirashtra and Kevala. There are the Vanarasyas, the Pravahas,
the Vakras, the Vakrabhayas and the Shakas.

"There are the Videhakas, the Magadhas, the Suhmas and the Vijayas. There are
the Angas, the Vangas, the Kalingas and the Yakrillomans. There are the Mallas,
the Sudeshnas, the Prahutas, the Mahishas and the Karshikas. There are the
Vahikas, the Vatadhanas, the Abhiras and the Kalatoyakas. There are the
Aparandhras, the Shudras, the Pahlavas and the Charmakhandikas. There are the
Atavi Shabaras and the Marubhaumas, sir. There are the Upavrishchas and the
Anupavrishchas, the Surashtras and the Kekayas. There are the Kuttas, the
Aparantas, the Dvaidheyas, the Kakshas and the Samudranishkutas. There are the
many Andhras, king, and the Antargiryas. There are the Bahirgiryas, the
Angamaladas, the Magadhas and the Manavarjakas. There are the Mahyuttaras, the
Pravrisheyas and the Bhargavas, lord of the people. There are the Pundras, the
Bhargas, the Kiratas, the Sudoshnas and the Pramudas. There are the Shakas, the
Nishadas, the Nishadhas, the Anartas and the Nairritas. There are the Dugulas,
the Pratimatsyas, the Kushalas and the Kunatas. There are the Tiragrahas, the
Taratoyas, the Rajikas and the tribes of the Rasyakas. There are the Tilakas,
the Parasikas, the Madhumantas and the Prakutsakas. There are the Kashmiras, the
Sindhu-Sauviras, the Gandharas and the Darshakas. There are the Abhisaras, the
Kulutas, the Shaivalas and the Bahlikas.

"There are the Darvikas, the Sakachas and the Darvas. There are the Vatajas, the
Amarathas and the Uragas. There are the Bahuvadyas, Kauravya, the Sudamans and
the Sumallikas. There are the Vadhras, the Karishakas, the Kulindas and the
Upatyakas. There are the Vanayus, the Dashaparshvas, the Romanas and the
Kushabindus. There are the Kachchhas, the Gopalakachchhas, the Langalas and the
Paravallakas. There are the Kiratas, the Barbaras, the Siddhas, the Videhas and
the Tamralingakas. There are the Oshtras and the Pundras with the Sairandhras,
and the mountain peoples, sir.

"And there are other peoples, in the south, bull among the Bharatas. There are
the Dravidas, the Keralas, the Prachyas, the Bhushikas and the Vanavasins. There
are the Unnatyakas, the Mahishakas, the Vikalpas and the Mushakas. There are the
Karnikas, the Kuntikas, the Saudbhidas and the Nalakalakas. There are the
Kaukuttakas, the Cholas, the Konkanas and the Malavanakas. There are the
Samangas, the Kopanas, the Kukuras, the Angadas and the Marishas. There are the
Dhvajins, the Utsavasanketas, the Trigartas and the Sarvasenis. There are the
Tryangas, the Kekarakas, the Proshthas and the Parasancharakas. There are the
Vindhyapulakas, and the Pulindas with the Kalkalas. There are the Malakas, the
Mallakas and the Aparavartakas. There are the Kulindas, the Kulakas, the
Karanthas and the Kurakas. There are the Mushakas, the Stanabalas, the Satis and
the Pattipanjakas. There are the Adidayas, the Siralas, the Stubakas and the
Stanapas. There are the Hrishividarbhas, the Kantikas, the Tanganas and the
Paratanganas.

"And there are the northern peoples and other mlecchas, best of the Bharatas.
There are the Yavanas with the Kambojas, fierce mleccha peoples. There are the
Sakshaddruhs, the Kuntalas, and the Hunas with the Paratakas. There are the
Maradhas, the Chinas and the Dashamalikas. There are the settlements of
kshatriyas, and the families of vaishyas and shudras. There are the Shudras, the
Abhiras, the Daradas, and the Kashmiras with the Pashus. There are the
Khashikas, the Tukharas, the Pallavas and the Girigahvaras. There are the
Atreyas with the Bharadvajas, and the Stanayoshikas. There are the Aupakas, the
Kalingas and the Kirata tribes. There are the Tamaras, the Hamsamargas and the
Karabhanjakas. I have only named these lands, lord, by way of pointing to them.

"According to its virtues and its strength, the earth, well tended, yields the
great fruit of the three aims of life. She will yield like a cow that gives all
one wishes. Brave kings who know dharma and wealth covet her. Greedy for her
riches and full of force, they give up their lives in battle. The earth is the
refuge of beings with divine bodies and with human bodies. Kings who want to
enjoy the earth, best of the Bharatas, snatch at one another like dogs at meat.
And no one here is ever satisfied in his desires. That is why the Kurus and the
Pandavas strive to possess the earth, lord of the earth, by conciliation, by
gifts, by dividing the enemy and by force. For beings, bull among men, the
earth, if she is seen rightly and without a flaw, is father, mother and son, sky
and heaven."

"Son of a suta," said Dhritarashtra, "tell me at length the measure of life in
this varsha of Bharata and in Haimavata, and its fruit, good and bad, past,
future and present, Sanjaya, and of Harivarsha too."

"There are four ages in Bharata, bull among the Bharatas," said Sanjaya. "They
are Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Pushya, you who make the Kurus prosper. First
comes the age called Krita, then the Treta age, lord. Then, as the Dvapara
shrinks, the Pushya begins. In the Krita age the span of life is reckoned at
four thousand years, best of the Kurus, best of kings. In the Treta it is three
thousand, lord of men, and in the Dvapara two thousand. Now it stands at a
hundred. In this Pushya there is no fixed measure, bull among the Bharatas. Here
they die in the womb, and they die after they are born.

"In the Krita age, king, sages were born, mighty and great of spirit, blessed in
their children and rich in austerity. Those born in the Krita age, king, were
full of energy, great of soul, righteous and truthful, rich and lovely to look
at. In the Treta, long-lived heroes are born, the best of bowmen in battle,
brave kshatriyas who rule the whole earth. In the Dvapara, great king, all the
classes are born full of energy and great valour, each seeking the other's
death. In the Pushya, Bharata, men are born with little energy, quick to anger,
greedy and false. In the Pushya, Bharata, envy, pride and anger, deceit and
spite, passion and greed arise in mortals. The shrinking goes on now, king, in
this Dvapara, lord of men. Haimavata is richer in virtues, and Harivarsha beyond
it."

**Divergences.**

* **Who covets the land.** Dhritarashtra says the Pandavas covet it; Sanjaya answers, "The Pandavas are not the ones who covet it" (6.10.3).
* **The fourth age is Pushya.** The CE names the four ages Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Pushya (6.11.3–7, 12–13), not Kali. Sanjaya says "the shrinking goes on now … in this Dvapara" (6.11.14). Both are kept as the text has them.
* **The kings who loved Bharata.** Kushika, Gadhi, Somaka and Dilipa are added in a rejected line (\*43) and are not named.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*40–\*53.

**Choices.**

* The lists of rivers and peoples are given in full, in the text's order, with its repetitions (the Vetravati, Payoshni, Kaushiki, Tridiva, Chitrasena, Hiranvati, Sindhu, Bahuda and Durga each appear twice; the Kalingas, Magadhas, Pundras, Kiratas and others likewise).

# Ghatotkacha and Duryodhana

Dhritarashtra said, "Seeing Iravan killed, what did the Parthas, the great
chariot fighters, do in battle? Tell me that, Sanjaya."

"Seeing Iravan killed in battle," said Sanjaya, "the rakshasa Ghatotkacha,
Bhimasena's son, roared a very great roar. At the sound of his roaring the
earth, clothed in the sea, with its mountains and forests, shook hard, king, and
so did the sky and all the quarters and the points between them. Hearing that
very great roar, Bharata, your army's thighs went stiff, and they trembled and
sweated. All your men, king of kings, lost heart and writhed like snakes, like
elephants afraid of a lion.

"Roaring a very great roar like a thunderclap, the rakshasa raised his blazing
trident and took on a frightening form. Bulls among rakshasas surrounded him
with terrible weapons of many kinds. He came in great anger, like Time, the
ender, Yama. King Duryodhana saw him come, angry and terrible to see, and saw
his own army mostly turned away in fear of him. He took up a great bow and ran
at Ghatotkacha, roaring again and again like a lion. Behind him came the lord of
the Vangas himself, with ten thousand elephants like mountains, streaming with
rut. Seeing your son coming, great king, surrounded by the force of elephants,
the night-walker grew angry. Then a tumultuous battle broke out that made the
hair stand on end, between the rakshasas and Duryodhana's force, king of kings.

"The rakshasas saw the force of elephants rising like a mass of clouds. They ran
at it in anger, weapons in hand. Roaring roars of many kinds like clouds with
lightning, they killed the elephant fighters with arrows, spears, swords and
iron arrows. They killed the great elephants with slings and tridents, with
hammers and axes, with mountain peaks and trees. We saw the elephants being
killed by the night-walkers, great king, their frontal globes split, bloodless,
their bodies broken. As they were destroyed, and the elephant fighters broken,
Duryodhana, great king, ran at the rakshasas. Overcome by impatience, giving up
his own life, the very strong one loosed sharp arrows at the rakshasas. Your son
Duryodhana, the great archer, in anger killed the leading rakshasas there, best
of the Bharatas. With four arrows the great chariot fighter killed four:
Vegavat, Maharaudra, Vidyujjihva and Pramathin. Then again he whose self is
beyond measure loosed a shower of arrows, hard to withstand, at the army of the
night-walkers, best of the Bharatas.

"Seeing that great deed of your son's, sir, the very strong son of Bhimasena
blazed with anger. Drawing his great bow, which sounded like Indra's
thunderbolt, he ran at speed at Duryodhana, the tamer of enemies. Seeing him
come like Death let loose by Time, great king, your son Duryodhana did not
falter. Then the cruel one, angry, his eyes red, said to him, 'With great
cruelty you sent the Pandavas into exile for a long time, king, after they were
beaten in the crooked dice game. Draupadi Krishna was brought into the hall in
one garment, in her period, and you tormented her in many ways, evil-minded one.
The evil-hearted Sindhu king, wanting to please you, harassed her in her
hermitage, despising my fathers. For these insults and others, lowest of your
family, I will take revenge today, unless you leave the battle.'

"Having said this, Hidimba's son drew his great bow, bit his lip with his teeth
and licked the corners of his mouth. He covered Duryodhana with a great shower
of arrows, as a cloud covers a mountain with torrents of water in the rains.

---

"Then the king of kings bore that shower of arrows in battle, hard even for the
danavas to bear, as a great elephant bears the rain. Then, possessed by anger,
hissing like a snake, your son, bull of the Bharatas, in the greatest danger,
loosed twenty-five sharp, keen iron arrows. They fell at once on that bull among
rakshasas, king, like angry venomous snakes on Mount Gandhamadana. Pierced by
them, streaming blood like an elephant in rut, the eater of flesh set his mind
on destroying the king. He took up a great spear that could split even
mountains, blazing, bright as a great meteor, like the thunderbolt Maghavat
raises. The strong-armed one raised it, wanting to kill your son.

"Seeing it raised, the lord of the Vangas hurried and drove his elephant, like a
mountain, at the rakshasa. With that fine elephant, strong and swift, he took
the road to where Duryodhana's chariot was, and with his elephant he shielded
your son's chariot. Seeing his way blocked by the wise king of the Vangas,
Ghatotkacha, great king, his eyes red with anger, hurled that raised great spear
at the elephant. Struck by it, hurled from his arm, king, the elephant gushed
blood, fell and died. As the elephant fell, the strong lord of the Vangas leapt
off quickly and reached the ground.

"Duryodhana too, seeing the fine elephant brought down and his army broken, was
in great pain. Putting the dharma of the kshatriya first, and his own pride, the
king stood like an unmoving mountain, though the moment for retreat had come. In
the greatest anger he fitted a sharp arrow, bright as the fire of Time, and
loosed it at that terrible night-walker. Seeing that arrow coming, bright as
Indra's thunderbolt, the huge-bodied Ghatotkacha dodged it with lightness. His
eyes red with anger, the fierce one roared again, terrifying all beings, like a
cloud at the end of an age.

"Bhishma son of Shantanu heard that terrible roar of the fearful rakshasa. He
went up to the teacher and said, 'From the sound of this terrible roar the
rakshasa is making, Hidimba's son is surely fighting King Duryodhana. No being
could conquer him in battle. Go there, bless you, and protect the king. The
fortunate one has been attacked by the evil-hearted rakshasa. This is the
highest duty for all of us, scorchers of enemies.'

"Hearing the grandfather's words, the great chariot fighters hurried at top
speed to where the Kaurava was. They wanted to save your son Duryodhana, who had
been attacked. There went Drona, Somadatta, Bahlika and Jayadratha, Kripa,
Bhurishravas, Shalya, Chitrasena and Vivimshati, Ashvatthama, Vikarna, the
Avanti king and Brihadbala, and many thousands of chariots that followed them.
That unassailable force, guarded by the best of the world, came at him with
drawn weapons. Seeing it, the strong-armed best of rakshasas did not tremble,
like Mount Mainaka. He took up his great bow, surrounded by his kinsmen with
tridents, hammers and weapons of many kinds in their hands. Then a tumultuous
battle broke out that made the hair stand on end, between the chief of the
rakshasas and Duryodhana's force. The sound of twanging bows was heard on every
side, great king, tumultuous, like bamboos burning. The sound of weapons falling
on the armour of men was like mountains splitting, king. The lances loosed from
heroes' arms, lord of the people, looked in the sky like snakes gliding.

"Then the strong-armed lord of the rakshasas, in the greatest anger, drew his
very great bow and roared a fearful roar. In anger he cut the teacher's bow with
a crescent arrow. With a broad arrow he cut down Somadatta's banner and roared.
He pierced Bahlika between the breasts with three arrows. He pierced Kripa with
one and Chitrasena with three. With an arrow drawn to the full and well aimed,
he struck Vikarna at the collarbone, and he sank onto the chariot floor,
drenched in blood. Then he whose self is beyond measure, in anger, sent fifteen
iron arrows at Bhurishravas, bull of the Bharatas. They split his armour and
quickly went into the ground. He struck the drivers of Vivimshati and Drona's
son, and they fell on the chariot floor, letting go of the horses' reins. With a
crescent arrow, great king, he cut down the Sindhu king's gold-adorned boar
banner, and with a second he cut his bow. With four iron arrows, his eyes red
with anger, he killed the four horses of the great-souled Avanti king. With a
yellow, sharp arrow drawn to the full he split Prince Brihadbala, great king,
and he sat down on the chariot floor, badly wounded and in pain. Then the lord
of the rakshasas, standing on his chariot and filled with great anger, shot
sharp, keen arrows like venomous snakes. They split Shalya, skilled in battle,
great king."

summary: The fifth day. Bhishma forms the makara array and the Pandavas the hawk. Bhima goes at Bhishma, and Duryodhana urges Drona on; Shikhandi charges Bhishma, who turns away from him, remembering that he was born a woman. The armies close in a confused, dusty slaughter. At noon Bhishma cuts Bhima's bow and kills Satyaki's charioteer. Arjuna spares Ashvatthama out of regard for his teacher, and Kripa carries Duryodhana's son Lakshmana away from Abhimanyu. In the afternoon Satyaki destroys ten thousand chariots, but Bhurishravas kills Satyaki's ten sons, and the two of them are left facing each other with swords until Bhima and Duryodhana pull them apart. At sunset Arjuna kills twenty-five thousand chariot fighters, and Bhishma calls the halt.

# The Fifth Day

"When the night had passed and the sun had risen, great king," said Sanjaya,
"the two armies met for battle. They charged in anger, all of them together,
each side wanting to beat the other as they saw each other. So the Pandavas and
the sons of Dhritarashtra, king, through your bad counsel, drew up their arrays
in fury and fought, striking hard. Bhishma guarded the makara array on every
side, king. In the same way the Pandavas guarded their own array.

"Your father Devavrata, best of chariot fighters, went out with the chariot
division, surrounded by a great line of chariots. Chariot fighters, foot
soldiers, elephants and horsemen followed one another, each in its place. Seeing
them ready for battle, the famous Pandavas drew up the hawk, the king of arrays,
which could not be beaten in battle. Mighty Bhimasena shone at its beak.
Unassailable Shikhandi and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, were its eyes. The
hero Satyaki, whose valour was true, was its head. Partha, shaking the Gandiva,
was in its neck. Great, splendid Drupada and his son, with a whole army, made
its left wing. The Kekaya, lord of an army, was its right wing. In the rear were
the sons of Draupadi and brave Subhadra's son. At the back was splendid King
Yudhishthira himself, wise and of fine valour, with his two brothers, the twins.

"Then in the battle Bhima went into the makara by its mouth. He reached Bhishma
and covered him with arrows. Then Bhishma brought down great weapons, Bharata,
bewildering the drawn-up army of the sons of Pandu in the great battle. As the
army was bewildered, Dhananjaya quickly pierced Bhishma with a thousand arrows
at the head of the battle. Holding off the weapons Bhishma had loosed, he stood
ready for battle with his own glad division.

"Then King Duryodhana, the best of the mighty, spoke to Bharadvaja's son. The
great chariot fighter had seen the terrible slaughter of his army earlier and
remembered the killing of his brothers in battle. 'Teacher, you have always
wished me well, blameless one. Relying on you and on the grandfather Bhishma, we
hope to beat even the gods in battle, no doubt of it. How much more the sons of
Pandu, who are weaker in courage and valour.'

"Told this by your son, sir, Drona broke into the Pandava army while Satyaki
watched. Satyaki held Drona back, Bharata, and then a tumultuous, hair-raising
battle began. Bharadvaja's mighty son in anger pierced Shaineya at the
collarbone with sharp arrows, almost laughing. Then Bhimasena in anger pierced
Bharadvaja's son, king, to save Satyaki from Drona, the best of those who bear
weapons. Then Drona and Bhishma and Shalya, sir, covered Bhimasena with arrows
in anger. There Abhimanyu in anger, and the sons of Draupadi, sir, pierced all
those men with their weapons raised with sharp arrows.

"When mighty Bhishma and Drona came on in anger, the great archer Shikhandi went
out to meet them in the great battle. The hero, grasping hard his bow that
roared like a cloud, quickly rained arrows on them, hiding the sun. When the
grandfather of the Bharatas met Shikhandi in battle, he turned away from him,
remembering that he had been a woman. Then Drona, great king, charged him in the
battle, urged on by your son, to protect Bhishma. And Shikhandi, meeting Drona,
the best of those who bear weapons, turned away from him in the battle as from
the blazing fire at the end of an age.

"Then your son, lord of the people, came up with a great force and guarded
Bhishma, seeking great glory. In the same way the Pandavas, king, putting
Dhananjaya in front, went against Bhishma himself, set firmly on victory. That
battle was as terrible as the gods' battle with the danavas, as they longed
always for victory and for glory most marvellous.

"Then Bhishma, son of Shantanu, fought a tumultuous battle, wanting to bring
your sons through their fear of Bhimasena. That morning a very cruel battle of
kings went on between the Kurus and the Pandavas, killing their chief heroes. In
that confused battle, full of great terror, a tumultuous noise rose that touched
the great sky. It was a tumult of trumpeting elephants, neighing horses, and the
noise of drums and conches. The mighty men, eager to fight, showing their valour
for victory, roared at one another like great bulls in cowpens.

"As heads were brought down in the battle with sharp arrows, it was like a
shower of stones from the sky, bull of the Bharatas. Fallen heads were seen,
bull of the Bharatas, with earrings and turbans, shining with gold. The earth
was covered with limbs torn by arrows, with arms still holding bows and arms
still wearing ornaments. In a moment, lord of the earth, the whole earth was
strewn with armoured bodies and adorned hands. It was strewn with fine faces
like the moon, with red-cornered eyes, and with every limb of elephants, horses
and men. With tumultuous clouds of dust lit by the lightning of weapons, the
clash of arms was like thunder. That tumultuous, bitter clash, its water blood,
went on between the Kurus and the Pandavas, Bharata.

"In that very terrible, tumultuous, hair-raising battle, full of great fear, the
kshatriyas, mad for battle, rained showers of arrows. Elephants, yours and
theirs, scorched by the showers of arrows, screamed in the battle, best of the
Bharatas. Horses whose riders had been killed ran in all ten directions. Other
warriors, yours and theirs, hurt by the blows of arrows, leapt up and fell, bull
of the Bharatas. Heaps of horses, elephants and chariots were seen here and
there, lord of the people, as they overran each other. With maces, swords,
lances and straight arrows the kshatriyas killed each other there, driven by
Time. Other heroes, skilled in wrestling, closed with each other in many ways
with their arms, as with iron bars. With fists, knees and slaps, lord of the
people, your heroes and the Pandavas struck each other. Chariot fighters who had
lost their chariots, holding fine swords, ran at one another, each wanting to
kill the other.

"Then King Duryodhana, surrounded by many Kalingas, went against the Pandavas in
the battle, putting Bhishma in front. In the same way all the Pandavas,
surrounding Vrikodara, charged Bhishma in anger in the battle, with swift
horses.

"When Dhananjaya saw his brothers and the other kings locked in battle with
Bhishma, he charged Ganga's son with his weapons raised. When we heard the roar
of Panchajanya and the twang of the Gandiva, and saw Partha's banner, fear came
on all of us. We saw the banner of the Gandiva bowman, great king. It did not
catch on the trees. It rose like a comet, many-coloured and bright, divine,
marked with the monkey. The warriors saw the Gandiva with its gold back in the
great chariot, like lightning shining in the sky in the middle of the clouds. We
heard again and again the very terrible slap of his palms as he killed your
army, like Shakra roaring.

"Like a cloud in a fierce wind, with lightning and thunder, flooding every
direction all round with showers of arrows, Dhananjaya of the terrible weapons
charged Ganga's son. Bewildered by his weapons, we could not tell east from
west. Not knowing where to go, with their mounts tired, their weapons struck
down and their minds struck down, your warriors clung to one another, bull of
the Bharatas. With all your sons, they huddled close to Bhishma himself.
Bhishma, son of Shantanu, was their refuge in the battle as they suffered.
Chariot fighters leapt from their chariots in terror, horsemen from their
horses' backs, and foot soldiers on the ground. Hearing the twang of the
Gandiva, like the crack of a thunderbolt, all the armies shrank back in fear,
Bharata.

"Then Govasana came on, surrounded by great, swift Kamboja chiefs and by forces
of many thousands of cowherds. The lord of Kalinga came, surrounded by Madras,
Sauviras, Gandharas and Trigartas, and by all the Kalinga chiefs, lord of the
people. Elephants and floods of men led by Duhshasana came, and King Jayadratha
with all the kings. Fourteen thousand of the best horsemen, urged on by your
son, surrounded Subala's son. Then all your men together, with their chariots
and mounts in their divisions, went at the Pandavas in the battle, bull of the
Bharatas.

"Stirred up by chariot fighters, elephants, horses and foot soldiers, the battle
became terrible, and dust rose like a great cloud. Bhishma, with a great force
of fighters on elephants, horses and chariots, with javelins, lances and iron
arrows, closed with Kiritin. The Avanti king fought the king of Kashi, and the
Saindhava fought Bhimasena. Ajatashatru, with his son and his ministers, fought
the famous bull of the Madras, Shalya. Vikarna fought Sahadeva, and Chitrasena
Shikhandi. The Matsyas went at Duryodhana and Shakuni, lord of the people.
Drupada, Chekitana and Satyaki, the great chariot fighter, fought great Drona
and his son. Kripa and Kritavarma charged Dhrishtaketu. So the armies closed and
fought on every side, with their horses at full speed and their elephants and
chariots wheeling.

"There was fierce lightning in a cloudless sky, and the quarters were covered
with dust. Great meteors appeared with a crash, lord of the people. A great wind
blew, and a shower of dust fell. The sun vanished in the sky, hidden by the dust
of the army. A deep bewilderment came on all creatures, overwhelmed by the dust
and tormented by the nets of weapons. There was a tumultuous clash of nets of
arrows loosed by heroes' arms, arrows that could split any armour. Weapons
raised by fine arms, bright as stars, lit up the sky, bull of the Bharatas.
Bright shields of bull's hide, covered with gold netting, fell in every
direction, bull of the Bharatas. Bodies and heads cut down on every side by
swords bright as the sun were seen in every direction.

"Great chariot fighters went to the ground here and there, their wheels, axles
and seats broken, their great banners brought down, their horses killed. Some
horses, wounded by weapons, ran about, dragging chariots whose fighters had been
killed. Fine horses, struck by arrows, their bodies torn, still bound by their
traces, dragged their yokes here and there, Bharata. Chariots were seen, king,
with their charioteers, horses and fighters, killed by a single mighty elephant.
Many elephants in the battle smelt the rut streaming from a scent elephant and
took in the wind, as the floods of troops met. The field was covered with
elephants struck by iron arrows, falling lifeless with their riders and their
javelins.

"As the floods of troops met, chariots fell broken in the battle with their
fighters and banners, struck by the fine elephants that were driven at them. The
shafts of chariots were seen broken, great king, where the elephants had seized
them with trunks like great snakes. Elephants seized chariot fighters by the
hair, with their nets of chariots torn apart, swung them like the branches of
trees and crushed them in the battle. Fine elephants dragged chariots locked
with other chariots in every direction, answering every call. The elephants
dragging them looked like elephants in lakes dragging tangled nets of lotuses.
So that great field was covered with horsemen, foot soldiers and great chariot
fighters with their banners.

"Shikhandi, with Virata the Matsya, lord of the people, quickly came against
Bhishma, the great archer, who was so hard to beat. Dhananjaya attacked Drona,
Kripa and Vikarna, mighty great archers, and many other brave kings in the
battle. He attacked the great archer the Saindhava with his ministers and kin,
and the kings of the east and the south, bull among kings. Bhimasena went
against your son Duryodhana, the unforgiving great archer, and against Duhsaha
in the battle. Sahadeva went against Shakuni and the great chariot fighter
Uluka, the father and son, great archers, so hard to beat. Yudhishthira, the
great chariot fighter, whom your son had wronged, went against the elephant
division in the battle, great king. The heroic son of Madri, Nakula the Pandava,
who makes his enemies weep in battle, fought the finest chariot fighters of the
Trigartas. Satyaki, Chekitana and Subhadra's son, the great chariot fighter, so
hard to face, went against the Shalvas and Kekayas in the battle. Dhrishtaketu
and the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, very hard to beat, went out against your sons'
chariot division. The commander, mighty Dhrishtadyumna, whose spirit had no
measure, met Drona in battle, king, he whose deeds are like Indra's. So these
great archers, yours and the Pandavas', brave men, met in battle and fought.

"When the sun had reached midday and the sky was in turmoil, the Kurus and the
Pandavas killed each other. Chariots with banners, bright with gold, covered
with tiger skins, shone as they moved about the field with their flags. As they
met in battle, each wanting to win, there was a tumultuous noise like lions
roaring. There we saw a marvellous, very cruel clash, which the brave Srinjayas
fought with the Kurus. We could not see the sky, king, scorcher of enemies, nor
the quarters, nor the sun, nor the points between, for the arrows loosed on
every side. The gleam of bright-tipped spears and hurled javelins, and of yellow
swords, was like blue lotuses. The gleam of bright armour and ornaments lit up
the sky, the quarters and the points between with its power, king, and the field
shone here and there. Tigers among chariot fighters, meeting in battle, shone
like planets in the sky.

"Bhishma, best of chariot fighters, in anger held back mighty Bhimasena while
the whole army watched. Then golden- feathered arrows loosed by Bhishma, whetted
on stone, washed in oil, well sharpened, struck Bhima in the battle. Mighty
Bhimasena hurled at him a spear of great speed, like an angry snake, Bharata. As
it suddenly came at him, with its golden shaft, hard to withstand, Bhishma cut
it down in the battle with straight arrows. Then with another broad arrow,
yellow and sharp, he cut Bhimasena's bow in two, Bharata.

"Then Satyaki quickly reached Bhishma in the battle and struck your father with
many arrows, lord of men. Then Bhishma fitted a sharp, very cruel arrow, and
brought down the Varshneya's charioteer from his chariot. When the driver was
killed, king, his horses bolted. They ran this way and that, as swift as the
mind or the wind. Then a tumultuous noise rose from the whole army, and a cry of
'Alas!' went up from the great Pandavas. 'Run! Catch the horses! Hold them!
Chase them!' There was a tumultuous shouting round Yuyudhana's chariot.

"At that very time Bhishma, son of Shantanu, killed the Pandava army again, as
the killer of Vritra killed the army of the asuras. Being killed by Bhishma, the
Panchalas and the Somakas set their minds nobly on battle and charged Bhishma
himself. The Parthas led by Dhrishtadyumna also charged Shantanava in the
battle, wanting to beat your son's army. In the same way your men, king, led by
Bhishma and Drona, charged the enemy swiftly, and the battle went on.

"Then Virata struck Bhishma, the great chariot fighter, with three arrows, and
the great chariot fighter pierced his horses with three more. The great archer,
mighty Bhishma, son of Shantanu, of practised hand, pierced him back with ten
arrows with golden feathers. Drona's son, the great chariot fighter of the
terrible bow, firm of hand, pierced the Gandiva bowman in the middle of the
chest with six arrows. Phalguna, killer of enemy heroes, cut his bow, and the
destroyer of enemies pierced him hard with sharp feathered arrows. Beside
himself with anger, and unable to bear Partha cutting his bow in battle, he took
another swift bow. He pierced Phalguna with ninety sharp arrows, king, and
Vasudeva with seventy of his best arrows.

"Then Phalguna, with Krishna, his eyes red with anger, breathed long and hot and
thought again and again. The Gandiva bowman, destroyer of enemies, pressed his
bow with his left hand and in anger took up sharp, straight, terrible arrows
that end lives. With them he quickly pierced Drona's son, the best of the
mighty, in the battle. They split his armour and drank his blood. But Drona's
son, pierced by the Gandiva bowman, did not flinch. He went on loosing showers
of arrows as before, unshaken, and stood in the battle, king, wanting to protect
the man of the great vow.

"The bulls among men praised that very great deed of his, that he did not shrink
in battle from the two Krishnas together. For he always fights without fear at
the head of the armies, having received from Drona the whole body of weapons,
with the power to call them back, so hard to get. 'He is my teacher's son,
Drona's very dear son,' thought Bibhatsu, 'and above all a brahmin, whom I
should honour.' Setting his mind on that, the heroic scorcher of enemies, the
best of chariot fighters, took pity on Bharadvaja's son. Then the valiant son of
Kunti, scorcher of enemies, left Drona's son in the battle and fought on
quickly, killing your men.

"Duryodhana struck the great archer Bhimasena with ten arrows feathered with
vulture plumes, whetted on stone, with golden feathers. Bhimasena in anger took
up a strong, bright bow that takes away life, and ten sharp arrows. Calmly he
quickly pierced the Kuru king in his broad chest with sharp arrows of fierce
speed, drawn to the ear. The jewel on a gold chain on his chest, surrounded by
arrows, shone like the sun surrounded by the planets. But your son, full of
fire, struck by Bhimasena, could not bear it, as a snake cannot bear the sound
of a slap. Then in anger, great king, he pierced Bhima with arrows with golden
feathers, whetted on stone, terrifying the army. As they fought hard in the
battle, wounding each other, your two mighty sons shone like gods.

"Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, pierced Chitrasena, a tiger among men,
with ten arrows, and Purumitra with seven. Piercing Satyavrata with seventy,
like Shakra in battle, the hero seemed to dance on the field and he made us
suffer. Chitrasena pierced him back with ten arrows, Satyavrata with nine and
Purumitra with seven. Pierced and dripping blood, Arjuna's son cut Chitrasena's
bright bow, a great holder-off of enemies. He split his armour and struck him in
the chest with an arrow. Then your heroes, the princes, great chariot fighters,
came together in anger and pierced him with sharp arrows. And he, who knew the
highest weapons, struck them all with sharp arrows. Seeing that deed of his,
your sons surrounded him as he burned up your army in the battle, like a fire
blazing in dry brush. Subhadra's son shone very brightly, cutting down your
armies, like a kindled fire when the cold season has passed.

"Seeing what he did, lord of the people, your grandson Lakshmana quickly fell on
Subhadra's son in the battle. In anger Abhimanyu pierced Lakshmana, who bore
fine marks, with six arrows, and his charioteer with three. In the same way,
king, Lakshmana pierced Subhadra's son with sharp arrows, great king, and it was
a marvel. Then the mighty son of Subhadra killed his four horses and his
charioteer and charged Lakshmana with sharp arrows. Standing in his chariot with
its horses killed, Lakshmana, killer of enemy heroes, in anger hurled a spear at
Subhadra's son's chariot. As it suddenly came at him, terrible, hard to
withstand, like a snake, Abhimanyu cut it down with sharp arrows. Then Gautama
took Lakshmana up into his own chariot and carried him out of the fight while
the whole army watched.

"Then, as that confused battle full of great terror went on, they charged, each
eager to kill the other. Your great archers and the Pandava great chariot
fighters killed each other in the battle, offering up their lives. With their
hair loose, without armour, without chariots, their bows cut, the Srinjayas
fought the Kurus with their arms. Then mighty, strong-armed Bhishma in anger
killed the army of the great Pandavas with divine weapons. The earth was strewn
with elephants whose riders were killed, with fallen men and horses, with
chariot fighters and horsemen.

"Then, king, strong-armed Satyaki, mad in battle, drew his fine bow that could
bear any load. He loosed feathered arrows like venomous snakes, showing bright,
quick and wonderful skill with weapons. As he drew his bow, loosed arrows, took
up more and fitted others, and shot arrows in the battle and killed his enemies,
his form was very like a cloud pouring rain. Seeing him rising up, King
Duryodhana sent ten thousand chariots against him, Bharata. Satyaki, whose
valour was true, the mighty, supreme archer, killed all those great archers with
a divine weapon.

"Having done that cruel deed, the hero, bow in hand, came up against
Bhurishravas in the battle. For when that increaser of the Kurus' fame saw that
army brought down by Yuyudhana, he charged in anger. Stretching his great bow,
the colour of the rainbow, he loosed arrows like thunderbolts, like venomous
snakes, by the thousand, great king, showing the speed of his hands. Satyaki's
followers could not bear those arrows whose touch was death, king. They ran on
every side, leaving Satyaki, mad in battle, on the field.

"Seeing him, the ten mighty sons of Yuyudhana, famous great chariot fighters
with bright armour, weapons and banners, came up to the great archer
Bhurishravas, whose banner is a sacrificial post, in the great battle. All of
them, in great fury, said, 'Heir of the Kauravas, mighty one, come and fight
with us in battle, all of us together or one by one. Either beat us and win
glory in battle, or we will beat you and bring joy to our father.'

"Told this by the heroes, the mighty best of men, proud of his valour, seeing
them there, said to them, 'Well said, heroes, if that is your mind today. Fight
together and with care. I will kill you in battle.' Told this, those
quick-acting great archers, those heroes, rained a great shower of arrows on the
tamer of enemies. In the afternoon, great king, there was a tumultuous battle on
the field between one man and many together. They covered that one best of
chariot fighters with showers of arrows, as clouds pour on a great mountain in
the rains, king. But the great chariot fighter quickly cut down the floods of
arrows they loosed, bright as Yama's staff and the thunderbolt, before they
reached him.

"There we saw the marvellous valour of Somadatta's son, that he fought many
alone in battle, like a man without fear. The ten great chariot fighters, king,
having loosed that shower of arrows, surrounded the strong-armed man and set
about killing him. Then Somadatta's son in anger cut their bows with ten arrows
in the blink of an eye, Bharata. Then, when their bows were cut, he cut off
their heads in the battle, king, with straight, sharp broad arrows. Killed, they
fell to the ground like trees broken by the thunderbolt.

"Seeing his mighty sons killed in battle, the Varshneya, roaring, king, went
against Bhurishravas. Pressing chariot against chariot in the battle, those two
mighty men killed each other's chariot horses. Without their chariots, the two
great chariot fighters leapt about and met. Holding great swords and bearing
fine shields, those tigers among men shone, standing ready to fight. Then
Bhimasena came quickly to Satyaki, who held a fine sword, and lifted him into
his chariot, king. And your son too, king, quickly lifted Bhurishravas into his
chariot in the battle, while all the bowmen watched.

"While that went on, bull of the Bharatas, the Pandavas fought Bhishma, the
great chariot fighter, in fury. As the sun turned red, Dhananjaya quickly killed
twenty-five thousand great chariot fighters. They had been sent by Duryodhana to
kill Partha. They went to their destruction as soon as they reached him, like
moths to a fire. Then the Matsyas and the Kekayas, skilled in the science of the
bow, gathered round Partha, the great chariot fighter, and his son.

"At that very time, as the sun was setting, a bewilderment came on all the
armies. Then your father Devavrata, with his mounts tired, withdrew the armies
at twilight, great king. When the Pandavas and the Kurus had met, the two
armies, very shaken, went to their own quarters. Then the Pandavas with the
Srinjayas, and the Kurus, went to their own camps, Bharata, and rested there as
was fitting."

**Divergences.**

* **Bhishma and Shikhandi.** Bhishma turns away from Shikhandi, "remembering that he had been a woman" (*strītvaṃ tasyānusaṃsmaran*, 6.65.28), as he said he would (5.193.62–64; 6.16.15). Shikhandi in turn turns away from Drona (6.65.30).
* **Arjuna spares Ashvatthama.** Out of regard for his teacher's son, and because he is a brahmin, Arjuna "took pity" on him and fought elsewhere (6.69.13–15).
* **Satyaki's ten sons.** Bhurishravas cuts their bows and heads in the blink of an eye (6.70.11–24). This is the beginning of the feud that ends in Book 7.
* **Yudhishthira's son.** At 6.67.19 Yudhishthira fights Shalya "with his son and his ministers" (*sahaputraḥ sahāmātyaḥ*). The son is not named.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*271–\*292. They include Yudhishthira forming the hawk on Dhaumya's advice (\*272), Drona calling Duryodhana a child (\*274), and Satyaki's skill learned from his friend Arjuna (\*290).

**Choices.**

* 6.65.4 *makaravyūha*: "the makara array", after the sea creature, left untranslated as the text names it.
* 6.67.3 *asajjamānaṃ vṛkṣeṣu*: the banner "did not catch on the trees", as the text says of it.
* 6.67.9 *ārtāyanam*: "refuge … as they suffered".
* 6.67.12 *govāsana*: taken as a king's name, Govasana, as elsewhere in this retelling.
* 6.67.34 *gandhahastin*: "a scent elephant", the elephant whose rut-smell frightens others.
* 6.69.12 *sasaṃhāram*: "with the power to call them back".
* 6.69.24 *ārtiṃ naḥ samajījanat*: "he made us suffer", Sanjaya speaking as one of the Kaurava side.
* 6.69.30 *sātvatīputram*: "Subhadra's son", since Subhadra is the Satvata princess.
* 6.69.36 *gautama*: Kripa.
* 6.70.12 *yūpaketu*: "whose banner is a sacrificial post".

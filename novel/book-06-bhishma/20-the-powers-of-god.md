# The Powers of God

"'Hear again, strong-armed one,' said the Blessed One, 'my highest word, which I
will speak to you, since you take delight in it, wanting your good. Neither the
hosts of the gods nor the great seers know my origin, for I am the beginning of
the gods and the great seers in every way. One who knows me as unborn and
without beginning, the great lord of the world, is not confused among mortals
and is freed from all sins.

"'Understanding, knowledge, freedom from confusion, patience, truth,
self-control, calm, pleasure and pain, being and not being, fear and
fearlessness, not harming, sameness, contentment, austerity, giving, fame and
disgrace: these states of beings, each of its own kind, come from me alone. The
seven great seers of old and the four Manus came from my being, born of my mind,
and from them came these creatures in the world. One who truly knows this power
and yoga of mine is joined to unshakeable yoga. There is no doubt of this. I am
the origin of all. Everything moves forth from me. Thinking this, the wise
worship me, full of feeling. With their thought on me, their life gone to me,
teaching one another and always speaking of me, they are content and take
delight. To those always disciplined, who worship me with love, I give the yoga
of understanding by which they come to me. Out of compassion for them, standing
in their own being, I destroy the darkness born of ignorance with the shining
lamp of knowledge.'

"'You are the highest Brahman, the highest home, the highest purifier,' said
Arjuna, 'the eternal divine Person, the first god, unborn, all-pervading. So all
the seers call you, and the divine seer Narada, and Asita Devala and Vyasa, and
you yourself tell me so. I hold all this true that you say to me, Keshava. For
neither the gods nor the danavas know your manifestation, blessed one. You
yourself know yourself by yourself, highest Person, maker of beings, lord of
beings, god of gods, lord of the world. You should tell me without leaving
anything out your own divine powers, by which you pervade these worlds and
stand. How may I know you, yogin, thinking of you always? In what states,
blessed one, am I to think of you? Tell me again in full your yoga and your
power, Janardana, for I am never satisfied as I hear the amrita of your words.'

"'Come then, I will tell you my divine powers,' said the Blessed One, 'the chief
of them, best of the Kurus, for there is no end to my extent. I am the self,
Gudakesha, standing in the heart of all beings. I am the beginning, the middle
and the end of beings. Among the Adityas I am Vishnu, among lights the shining
sun. I am Marichi among the Maruts, and among the stars I am the moon. Among the
Vedas I am the Sama Veda, among the gods I am Vasava. Among the senses I am the
mind, and in beings I am consciousness. Among the Rudras I am Shamkara, among
yakshas and rakshasas the lord of wealth. Among the Vasus I am Fire, and among
peaks I am Meru. Know me, Partha, as the chief of household priests, Brihaspati.
Among generals I am Skanda, among waters I am the ocean. Among great seers I am
Bhrigu, among words I am the one syllable. Among sacrifices I am the sacrifice
of quiet prayer, among things that do not move the Himalaya.

"'Among all trees I am the ashvattha, and among divine seers Narada. Among
gandharvas I am Chitraratha, among the perfected the sage Kapila. Know me among
horses as Ucchaihshravas, born of the amrita, among lords of elephants as
Airavata, and among men as the king. Among weapons I am the thunderbolt, among
cows the cow that grants all desires. I am Kandarpa who begets, and among
serpents I am Vasuki. Among nagas I am Ananta, among the creatures of the waters
Varuna. Among the ancestors I am Aryaman, and among those who restrain, Yama.
Among the daityas I am Prahlada, among those who count I am time. Among beasts I
am the lord of beasts, and among birds Vinata's son. Among purifiers I am the
wind, among those who bear weapons Rama. Among sea monsters I am the makara, and
among rivers the Jahnavi.

"'Of creations I am the beginning, the end and the middle, Arjuna. Among
sciences I am the science of the self, and among those who speak I am the
debate. Among letters I am the letter A, and among compounds the pair. I am
undying time, I am the disposer, facing every way. I am death who carries off
all, and the origin of things to come. Among feminine things I am fame, fortune,
speech, memory, wisdom, steadiness and patience. Among the Samans I am the
Brihatsaman, among metres the gayatri. Among months I am Margashirsha, among
seasons the one that brings flowers. Of those who cheat I am the dice. I am the
splendour of the splendid. I am victory, I am resolve, I am the goodness of the
good. Among the Vrishnis I am Vasudeva, among the Pandavas Dhananjaya. Among
sages too I am Vyasa, among poets the poet Ushanas. Among those who tame I am
the rod, among those who seek victory I am policy. Among secret things I am
silence, and in the knowing I am knowledge.

"'Whatever is the seed of all beings, that I am, Arjuna. There is nothing,
moving or unmoving, that could be without me. There is no end to my divine
powers, scorcher of enemies. This account of my powers I have given only as
examples. Whatever being has power, glory or might, know that it springs from a
part of my splendour. But what use is all this knowledge to you, Arjuna? I stand
supporting this whole world with a single part of myself.'"

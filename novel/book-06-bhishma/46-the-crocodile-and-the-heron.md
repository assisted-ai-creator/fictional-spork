# The Crocodile and the Heron

"Having rested, king," said Sanjaya, "the Kurus and Pandavas together went out
to battle again when the night was over. There was a great noise there, yours
and theirs, Bharata, of the chief chariots being yoked and the elephants being
made ready, of foot soldiers putting on armour, and of horses, Bharata. A
tumultuous sound of conches and drums arose on every side.

"Then King Yudhishthira said to Dhrishtadyumna, 'Draw up the crocodile array,
strong-armed one, which scorches enemies.' So addressed by Partha, the great
chariot fighter Dhrishtadyumna, the best of chariot fighters, gave orders to the
chariot fighters, great king. Its head was Drupada and Dhananjaya the Pandava.
Its eyes were Sahadeva and the great chariot fighter Nakula. Its snout, great
king, was the very strong Bhimasena. Subhadra's son, the sons of Draupadi, the
rakshasa Ghatotkacha, Satyaki and the King of Dharma took their places at the
neck of the array. At its back, great king, was Virata, lord of an army, with
Dhrishtadyumna, surrounded by a great force. The five Kekaya brothers held its
left flank. Dhrishtaketu, the tiger among men, and the mighty Karakarsha took
their stand on its right wing to guard the array. At its feet, great king, stood
the glorious great chariot fighter Kuntibhoja, and Shatanika, surrounded by a
great force. The strong great archer Shikhandi, surrounded by the Somakas, and
Iravan stood at the tail of the crocodile.

"Having drawn up this great array, Bharata, the Pandavas in their armour at
sunrise, great king, went quickly against the Kauravas again for battle. They
went with elephants, horses, chariots and foot soldiers, with tall, bright
banners and with clean, sharp weapons.

"Seeing that army arrayed, your father Devavrata drew up his host against it in
the great heron array, king. At its beak shone the great archer Bharadvaja's
son. Ashvatthama and Kripa were its eyes, lord of men. Kritavarma, the best of
men and best of all bowmen, was at its head, with the Kambojas, Arattas and
Bahlikas. At its neck, sir, were the Shurasena king and your son Duryodhana,
great king, surrounded by many kings. The Pragjyotisha king, with the Madras,
Sauviras and Kekayas, surrounded by a great force, was at its breast, best of
men. Susharma, lord of Prasthala, with his own force, stood in armour on the
left wing. The Tusharas, Yavanas and Shakas, with the Chuchupas, stood on the
right wing of the array, Bharata. Shrutayu, Shatayu and Somadatta's son, sir,
stood at the rear of the array, guarding one another.

"Then the Pandavas and the Kauravas met for battle at sunrise, great king, and a
great battle began. Chariot fighters went against elephants and elephants
against chariot fighters. Horsemen went against horsemen, and chariot fighters
against horsemen. The chariot fighter went against the charioteer, king, and
against the elephants in the great battle. Elephant riders went against chariot
riders, and chariot fighters against horsemen. Chariot fighters went against
foot soldiers, and horsemen against foot soldiers, king. They ran at one another
in battle, impatient.

"Guarded by Bhimasena, Arjuna and the twins, and by other great chariot
fighters, the Pandava army shone like the night with its stars. So too your
army, with Bhishma, Kripa, Drona, Shalya, Duryodhana and the rest, shone like
the sky filled with planets.

"Seeing Drona, the mighty Bhimasena Kaunteya went with swift horses against
Bharadvaja's son's army. The mighty Drona in anger pierced Bhima in battle with
nine iron arrows, king, aiming at his vitals. Struck hard by Bharadvaja's son in
battle, Bhima sent his charioteer to Yama's home. The mighty son of Bharadvaja
took the horses himself and blew away the Pandava army, as fire blows away a
heap of cotton. Being killed by Drona and Bhishma, best of men, the Srinjayas
with the Kekayas turned to flight. So too your army, wounded by Bhima and
Arjuna, reeled about here and there like a fine woman drunk. Then both arrays
were broken in that destruction of the best heroes, and there was a terrible
confusion of yours and theirs, Bharata. There we saw a wonder, of yours with the
enemy, Bharata, for they all fought as if on one road. Warding off one another's
weapons, lord of the people, the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas and the
Kauravas fought on."

---

Dhritarashtra said, "Our army has so many good qualities, Sanjaya, of so many
kinds. It is arrayed by the rules of the science and it never fails. It is very
well fed, always devoted to us, obedient, free from vices, and has shown its
courage before. Its men are not too old and not boys, not thin and not fat. They
are mostly light in movement and tall, firm of body and healthy. They have put
on armour and taken up weapons, and they carry many weapons. They are skilled in
fighting with swords, in wrestling and in fighting with clubs. In battle they
are skilled with spears, lances and javelins, with iron bars, slings and darts,
with pestles of every kind, with kampanas, bows and kanapas of every kind, with
bright missiles and in boxing. They have seen the sciences for themselves and
worked hard at their exercises. They are thoroughly trained in all the sciences
of handling weapons. They are skilled in mounting, dismounting, advancing,
leaping across, striking well, going forward and going back. They have been
tested many times on elephants, horses and chariots. Having been tested
properly, they have been given their pay.

"They were not taken on for their company, nor for flattery, nor for the sake of
kinship, nor through the strength of friendship, nor as men of low family. Its
people are prosperous and noble, their kin are content and honoured. Most of
them have had favours done them. It is glorious and high-minded. It is guarded,
son, by many leading men who have won victories, men of leading deeds, famed in
the world, like the guardians of the world. It is protected by many kshatriyas
honoured in the world, who came to us by their own wish with their forces and
followers. It is like the great ocean filled on every side by rivers. It is
crowded with chariots and elephants, like wings, though without wings. Its water
is the many warriors, terrible, its waves and ripples are the mounts. It is full
of missiles, swords, clubs, spears, arrows and lances. It is thick with banners
and ornaments and set with strips of jewels. It is stirred with the speed of the
wind by the mounts moving about. It is great, like the ocean, roaring as if
without a farther shore. It is protected by Drona and Bhishma, by Kritavarma, by
Kripa and Duhshasana, by those led by Jayadratha, by Bhagadatta and Vikarna, by
Drona's son, Subala's son and Bahlika. It is protected by great-souled heroes of
the world, men of substance. That it is being killed in battle was decreed of
old.

"Men have never seen such an undertaking, Sanjaya, nor have the very fortunate
ancient seers on earth. When such a flood of troops, fitted with a wealth of
weapons and arms, is being killed in battle, what is it but fate? All this seems
upside down to me, Sanjaya, when so terrible an army could not get the better of
the Pandavas in battle. Or else the gods have gathered there for the sake of the
Pandavas and fight my army, since it is being killed, Sanjaya. Vidura told him
here what was good and wholesome, Sanjaya, and my foolish son Duryodhana would
not take it. I think that great soul who knows everything foresaw this, son,
just as it has come about. He had seen all this before. Or else it was bound to
be so in every way, Sanjaya. As the Creator made it long ago, so it is, and not
otherwise."

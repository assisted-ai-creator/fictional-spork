# The Thunderbolt Array

"Then after a moment, great king," said Sanjaya, "a tumultuous noise was heard
that shook the heart, of the fighters eager for battle. With the sound of
conches and drums, the trumpeting of elephants and the rumble of chariot wheels,
the earth seemed to be splitting. With the neighing of horses and the roaring of
fighters, the sky and the quarters were filled with sound in an instant. Your
sons' armies, hard to withstand, and the Pandavas', shook as they met.

"Elephants and chariots there, adorned with gold, were seen shining like clouds
with lightning. Your banners, lord of men, of many shapes, with golden armlets,
gleamed like blazing fires. Our own and the enemy's were seen, Bharata, bright
like the banners of great Indra in great Indra's houses. The heroes in golden
armour, bright as the blazing sun, were seen like blazing planets. Bright with
raised weapons, with arm guards bound on and flags, the bull-eyed great archers
shone at the head of the armies.

"Your sons guarded Bhishma's rear, lord of men. There were Duhshasana,
Durvishaha, Durmukha and Duhsaha, Vivimshati, Chitrasena and the great chariot
fighter Vikarna. There were Satyavrata, Purumitra, Jaya, Bhurishravas and Shala,
with twenty thousand chariots following them. There were the Abhishahas,
Shurasenas, Shibis, Vasatis, Shalvas, Matsyas and Ambashthas. There were the
Trigartas, Kekayas, Sauviras and Kitavas, and the men of the east, west and
north, the Malavas. These twelve peoples, all brave and ready to give up their
bodies, guarded the grandfather with a great line of chariots. The king of
Magadha followed that rank of chariots with a force of ten thousand swift
elephants. The guards of the chariot wheels and the guards of the elephants'
feet in the middle of the army were sixty hundred thousand. Foot soldiers went
in front in many hundreds of thousands, with bows, shields and swords in their
hands, fighting with claws and darts. The eleven armies of your son, Bharata,
were seen, great king, like the Ganga beside the Yamuna."

---

"When Yudhishthira saw the eleven armies drawn up," said Dhritarashtra, "how did
the Pandava draw up his small army against them? How did Kunti's son, the
Pandava, draw up against Bhishma, who knows the human array, and those of the
gods, the gandharvas and the asuras?"

"When the Pandava, the King of Dharma whose soul was dharma, saw the armies of
Dhritarashtra's sons drawn up," said Sanjaya, "he spoke to Dhananjaya. 'My son,
they tell this from the words of the great seer Brihaspati. One should fight
with a few packed close, and spread out many as one likes. A small force against
many should be a needle-pointed rank. Our army is smaller still than the
enemy's. Bearing in mind this saying of the great seer, draw up the array,
Pandava.'

"When he heard the King of Dharma, Phalguna answered. 'I will draw up for you,
king, the array that is hardest of all to break. It is called the unmoving,
named the thunderbolt, and the wielder of the thunderbolt made it. Bhima, the
best of fighters, like the roused wind, unbearable to the enemy in battle, will
fight in front of us. That best of men, skilled in the ways of war, will go at
our head, crushing the energy of the enemy armies. When they see him, all the
kings, with Duryodhana at their head, will turn back in confusion, like small
beasts seeing a lion. We will all shelter behind Bhima, the best of fighters, as
behind a wall free of all fear. So the immortals shelter behind the wielder of
the thunderbolt. There is no man in the world who could bear to look at
Vrikodara, bull among men, of very fierce deeds, when he is angry. Bhimasena,
carrying his strong club made of the essence of the thunderbolt, moving with
great speed, could dry up even the ocean. The Kekayas, Dhrishtaketu and mighty
Chekitana stand here with their counsellors, lord of men, watching you, the
heirs of Dhritarashtra.' So Bibhatsu spoke, and as Partha spoke like this, sir,
all the armies honoured him in the battle with agreeing words.

"When he had said this, strong-armed Dhananjaya did so. He quickly drew up those
forces, and Phalguna set out. Seeing the Kurus advancing, the great army of the
Pandavas looked like the Ganga in flood, still and flowing. Bhimasena was their
leader, with Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, Nakula, Sahadeva and mighty
Dhrishtaketu. Then the king, having set them forward, surrounded by an army,
guarded them from behind with his brothers and sons. The guards of Bhima's
wheels were Madri's two splendid sons, and the sons of Draupadi with Subhadra's
son were his swift rear guard. Dhrishtadyumna the Panchala, the great chariot
fighter, was their protector, with the brave Prabhadrakas, chief chariot
fighters, heroes of the army. Behind them went Shikhandi, guarded by Arjuna,
ready to destroy Bhishma, bull of the Bharatas. Arjuna's rear guard was the
great chariot fighter Yuyudhana, and his wheel guards were the two Panchala
princes, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas.

"King Yudhishthira, Kunti's son, was in the middle rank, with great elephants in
rut moving like moving mountains. Great-minded Yajnasena the Panchala, mighty,
followed Virata at the rear with an army, for the Pandavas' sake. On their
chariots were great banners with many emblems, king, bright as the sun and moon,
adorned with the finest gold. Then the great chariot fighter Dhrishtadyumna
moved up behind, and with his brothers and sons he guarded Yudhishthira. Above
all the many banners on the chariots of your men and of the enemy, Arjuna's
banner alone stood highest, a great monkey.

"Foot soldiers went in front, with swords, spears and lances in their hands,
many hundreds of thousands, guarding Bhimasena. Ten thousand elephants followed
the king behind like moving mountains. Their temples and mouths ran with rut.
They were brave, shining with golden nets like mountains, dripping like clouds,
wet with rut and smelling of lotuses. Great-minded Bhimasena, hard to withstand,
dragging his terrible club like an iron bar, drew the great army along. Like the
sun, hard to look at, burning, garlanded with rays, the fighters could not look
at him close on any side. This array, called the thunderbolt, hard to break,
facing every way, terrible, with bows for lightning and banners, was guarded by
the bearer of Gandiva. The Pandavas stood drawn up in it against your army.
Guarded by the Pandavas, it cannot be conquered in the world of men.

"As the armies stood at twilight, waiting for sunrise, a wind blew with drops of
rain, with thunder from a cloudless sky. Fierce winds blew in every direction,
low, dragging gravel, and dust was raised that covered the world in darkness. A
great meteor fell eastward, bull of the Bharatas, struck the rising sun and
broke up with a great noise. Then, as the armies were being made ready, bull of
the Bharatas, the sun rose without light, with a roar. The earth shook and split
with a noise, best of the Bharatas. There were many whirlwinds in every quarter,
king. Thick dust arose, and nothing could be seen. The banners were shaken all
at once by the wind. Hung with nets of little bells, with golden garlands,
great, with flags, as bright as the sun, they made everything jingle as in a
forest of palm trees.

"So those tigers among men, the Pandavas who delighted in battle, stood drawn up
against your son's army. The marrow of the fighters seemed to melt, bull of the
Bharatas, when they saw Bhimasena standing in front with his club in his hand."

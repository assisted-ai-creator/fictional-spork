summary: Chapters 6 and 7 of the Bhagavad Gita, told simply, with footnotes. Krishna says the true renouncer is the one who does his work without craving its fruit, that a person must lift himself up by himself, and teaches how to sit and quiet the mind; Arjuna says the mind is as hard to hold as the wind, and Krishna answers that it is held by practice and letting go, and that no one who strives for good is ever lost; then Krishna tells of his lower and higher nature, how all things hang on him like pearls on a thread, how his power of illusion hides him, and the four kinds of people who turn to him.
---

# The Steady Mind

"'The person who does the work he has to do,' said Krishna, 'without depending
on its fruit, is both a renouncer and a yogi. It is not the one who stops
lighting the sacred fire, or stops all work.[^b6c9-1] Know that what they call
renouncing is yoga, Arjuna. No one becomes a yogi without giving up his own
plans and wants.

"'For the sage who wants to climb up to yoga, work is said to be the means. For
the one who has already climbed up to yoga, calm is said to be the means. When a
person does not cling to the things of the senses or to actions, and has given
up all his plans, he is said to have climbed up to yoga.

"'A person should lift himself up by himself. He should not let himself sink.
For the self alone is its own friend, and the self alone is its own
enemy.[^b6c9-2] For the one who has conquered himself by himself, the self is a
friend. But for the one who has not, his own self acts like an enemy.

"'When a person has conquered himself and is at peace, the highest self stays
steady in him, in cold and heat, in pleasure and pain, in honour and dishonour.
The yogi is called "joined" when he is satisfied with knowledge and wisdom,
unshakeable, with his senses conquered, when a lump of earth, a stone and gold
are all the same to him. He is better still if he looks the same way at
well-wishers, friends and enemies, at strangers and people in between, at people
he dislikes and at his own family, at good people and at bad.

"'A yogi should always keep his mind steady. He should stay in a quiet place,
alone, with his mind and body under control, wanting nothing and owning nothing.
In a clean place he should set up a firm seat for himself, not too high and not
too low, with kusha grass, a deerskin and a cloth on top of each other.[^b6c9-3]
Sitting there, he should make his mind one-pointed,[^b6c9-4] control his
thoughts and senses, and practise yoga to make himself pure.

"'He should hold his body, head and neck straight and still, and sit firm. He
should look at the tip of his nose, and not look around in every direction. At
peace, free from fear, keeping his vow of celibacy,[^b6c9-5] he should control
his mind, think of me, and sit joined in yoga, with me as his goal. A yogi who
keeps himself steady like this, with his mind under control, reaches the peace
that rests in me, whose highest point is nirvana.

"'Arjuna, yoga is not for someone who eats too much, or for someone who eats
nothing at all. It is not for someone who sleeps too much, or for someone who
stays awake too much. Yoga takes away sorrow for the person who is moderate in
eating and in play, moderate in his efforts at work, moderate in sleeping and
waking.[^b6c9-6]

"'When the controlled mind rests in the self alone, and the person has no
longing for any pleasure, he is called "joined". "Like a lamp in a place without
wind, which does not flicker": that is the picture used for the yogi who has
controlled his mind and practises the yoga of the self.

"'There is a state where the mind, held back by the practice of yoga, comes to
rest. In it, a person sees the self by the self and is content in the self. He
knows a happiness that has no end, which the understanding can grasp but the
senses cannot. Standing in it, he never moves away from the truth. Once he has
it, he thinks there is nothing better to have. Standing in it, he is not shaken
even by great sorrow. Know that this is called yoga: being cut off from any
contact with sorrow. This yoga should be practised firmly, without losing heart.

"'He should give up completely all the wants that come from his own plans. He
should hold back the whole crowd of the senses on every side with the mind.
Little by little, he should become still, with an understanding held firm by
patience. He should settle the mind in the self, and think of nothing at all.
Whenever the restless, unsteady mind wanders off, he should pull it back from
there and bring it under the control of the self.

"'The highest happiness comes to the yogi whose mind is at peace, whose passion
is calmed, who has become Brahman and is without fault. The yogi who keeps
himself steady like this, with his faults gone, easily enjoys the endless
happiness of touching Brahman. With his self joined in yoga, and seeing the same
everywhere, he sees the self in all beings, and all beings in the self.

"'The one who sees me everywhere, and sees everything in me, is never lost to
me, and I am never lost to him. The yogi who holds to oneness and loves me as
the one who lives in all beings lives in me, whatever he is doing. The yogi who
sees pleasure and pain in everyone as he would in himself, Arjuna, is thought to
be the highest.'

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'you have taught this yoga of sameness, but I cannot
see how it can stay steady, because the mind is so restless. The mind is
restless, Krishna. It is wild, strong and stubborn. I think it is as hard to
hold as the wind.'

"'No doubt the mind is restless and hard to hold, strong-armed one,' said
Krishna. 'But it can be held by practice and by letting go.[^b6c9-7] I think
yoga is hard to reach for someone who has no self-control. But someone who is in
control of himself, and keeps trying in the right way, can reach it.'

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'what happens to someone who has faith but does not try
hard enough, whose mind wanders away from yoga, and who does not reach the goal
of yoga? Does he lose both worlds and vanish like a torn cloud, with nothing to
stand on, lost on the road to Brahman? Please cut away this doubt of mine
completely, Krishna. No one but you can cut it away.'

"'Arjuna,' said Krishna, 'there is no ruin for him, in this world or the next.
No one who does good, my friend, comes to a bad end. The one who has fallen from
yoga goes to the worlds of those who have done good. He lives there for
countless years, and then he is born again in the house of good and well-off
people. Or he may be born into a family of wise yogis, though a birth like that
is very hard to come by in this world. There he gets back the understanding he
had in his earlier body, and from there he tries again for perfection, joy of
the Kurus.[^b6c9-8] His earlier practice carries him on, even without his
wanting it. Even someone who only wants to know about yoga goes beyond the Veda
of words. The yogi who keeps trying hard is made pure of his faults. He becomes
perfect over many births, and then he reaches the highest goal.

"'The yogi is greater than the people who practise harsh austerities. He is
thought to be greater than people of learning, and greater than people who do
rituals. So be a yogi, Arjuna. And of all yogis, the one who has faith and loves
me, with his inner self gone to me, is the one I think is most truly joined.'

"'Now hear, Arjuna,' said Krishna, 'how you will know me fully, without any
doubt, if you keep your mind on me, practise yoga and take shelter in me. I will
teach you all of this knowledge, with understanding. Once you know it, there
will be nothing more left to know here. Among thousands of people, hardly one
tries for perfection. And among those who try and succeed, hardly one knows me
as I truly am.

"'Earth, water, fire, air, space, mind, understanding and the sense of "I": this
is my nature, divided eight ways. This is my lower nature. But know that I have
another, higher nature, strong-armed one. It is life itself, and it holds this
world up.[^b6c9-9] Understand that all beings come from these two. I am the
source of the whole world, and I am its end. There is nothing at all higher than
me, Arjuna. All this is strung on me, like pearls on a thread.

"'I am the taste in water, Arjuna. I am the light of the moon and the sun. I am
the sacred syllable Om in all the Vedas,[^b6c9-10] the sound in space, and the
manhood in men. I am the pure smell of the earth and the brightness in fire. I
am the life in all beings, and the austerity in those who practise it. Know that
I am the eternal seed of all beings, Arjuna. I am the understanding of the wise
and the brightness of the bright. I am the strength of the strong, free from
wanting and passion. I am the desire in beings that does not go against dharma.

"'Know that all states of being, whether of goodness, passion or dullness, come
from me alone. But I am not in them. They are in me. This whole world is
confused by these three states made of the strands, and does not know me, who am
beyond them and never change. This divine power of illusion of mine, made of the
strands, is hard to get past.[^b6c9-11] Only those who come to me for shelter
cross over it. Wrongdoers, fools and the lowest of men do not come to me for
shelter. Their knowledge has been stolen by illusion, and they have taken on the
nature of the asuras.

"'Four kinds of good people love me, Arjuna: the one in trouble, the one who
wants to know, the one who wants wealth, and the one who knows.[^b6c9-12] Of
these, the one who knows, who is always joined and loves me only, is the best.
For I am very dear to the one who knows, and he is dear to me. All of these are
noble, but I think the one who knows is my very self. With his self joined, he
stands firm on me alone as the highest goal. At the end of many births, the one
who knows comes to me, knowing, "Vasudeva is everything."[^b6c9-13] Such a great
soul is very hard to find.

"'People whose knowledge has been carried away by this want or that turn to
other gods. They follow this rule or that, bound by their own nature. Whatever
form a devotee wants to worship with faith, I make his faith in that form
steady. Joined to that faith, he tries to win that god's favour, and he gets
what he wants from it. But it is really I who give those things. The reward that
comes to these people of small understanding comes to an end. Those who worship
the gods go to the gods. Those who love me come to me.

"'People without understanding think that I, who cannot be seen, have become
something that can be seen. They do not know my higher being, which never
changes and has nothing above it. Hidden by my power of yoga, I am not visible
to everyone. This confused world does not know me, the unborn, the unchanging. I
know the beings of the past, Arjuna, and those of the present, and those still
to come. But no one knows me.

"'All beings fall into confusion at birth, Arjuna, through the confusion of the
pairs of opposites, which come from liking and disliking. But people of good
deeds, whose wrongdoing has come to an end, are freed from the confusion of the
pairs. They love me, firm in their vows. Those who take shelter in me and strive
to be free from old age and death know Brahman fully, and the self, and all
action. Those who know me as the one behind all beings, all gods and all
sacrifice, with their minds joined to me, know me even at the hour of death.'"

## Explanations

[^b6c9-1]: **The sacred fire.** *What it means:* a householder kept a sacred
    fire and made offerings into it every day. People who renounced the world
    stopped tending the fire and stopped all their work. Krishna says that
    stopping work is not the point. The true renouncer is the one who gives up
    *craving the result*.

[^b6c9-2]: **The self as its own friend or enemy.** *What it means:* nobody else
    can make you calm or good. Your own mind can lift you up or drag you down.
    *Everyday example:* two people get the same bad news. One sinks into
    despair, the other says, "What can I do now?" The news was the same. The
    difference was in themselves.

[^b6c9-3]: **Kusha grass and the deerskin.** *What it means:* kusha is a sacred
    grass used in rituals. Sages sat on a mat of it, with a deerskin and then a
    cloth on top, so that the seat was clean and comfortable, not too high and
    not too low.

[^b6c9-4]: **A one-pointed mind.** *What it means:* the mind fixed on a single
    thing, not scattered. *In the story:* when Drona tested his pupils, he set a
    model bird on a tree and asked each one what he saw. Yudhishthira said he
    saw the tree, his teacher, his brothers and the bird, and Drona sent him
    away. Arjuna said, "I see only the bird … I see its head, not its body."
    Drona told him to shoot, and he brought it down (CE 1.123.46–67).

[^b6c9-5]: **The vow of celibacy (*brahmacarya*).** *What it means:* keeping
    away from sex, so that the body's energy and the mind's attention are not
    scattered. Students and those in serious practice kept it. *In the story:*
    Bhishma's lifelong vow of celibacy, taken so that his father could marry
    Satyavati, is the most famous vow in the epic (CE 1.94.77–94).

[^b6c9-6]: **The middle way in daily life.** *What it means:* Krishna does not
    ask for starving or sleeplessness. He asks for balance. *Everyday example:*
    an athlete does not train by never eating or never resting. Too much and too
    little both spoil the body. The mind is the same.

[^b6c9-7]: **Practice and letting go (*abhyāsa* and *vairāgya*).** *What it
    means:* two keys to a steady mind. *Practice* means doing it again and
    again. *Letting go* means loosening your grip on wants. *In the story:* one
    night, while Arjuna was eating, the wind blew out the lamp. His hand still
    found his mouth in the dark. He understood that this was the power of
    practice, and began to practise archery at night. When Drona heard the sound
    of his bowstring in the dark, he embraced him (CE 1.123.2–6).

[^b6c9-8]: **The one who falls from yoga.** *What it means:* Arjuna fears that
    someone who tries and fails loses everything. Krishna says no effort for
    good is ever wasted. It carries over into the next life. *Everyday example:*
    someone who learned a language as a child and forgot it will pick it up
    again much faster as an adult.

[^b6c9-9]: **Two natures: lower and higher.** *What it means:* Krishna's "lower
    nature" is the physical and mental world: the five elements, the mind, the
    understanding and the sense of "I". His "higher nature" is life itself, the
    spirit in every living thing. Everything that exists is made of these two,
    and both come from him.

[^b6c9-10]: **Om.** *What it means:* the sacred syllable spoken at the start of
    Vedic prayers, thought to hold the sound of the whole Veda. Krishna says he
    is that syllable.

[^b6c9-11]: **Illusion (*māyā*).** *What it means:* the power by which the world
    hides the Lord behind it. Because of it, people see only the things of the
    world and miss the one who is in them. *Everyday example:* when you watch a
    film, you forget the projector and see only the story on the screen.

[^b6c9-12]: **Four kinds of people who turn to God.** *What it means:* the one
    in trouble, the one who wants to understand, the one who wants something,
    and the one who knows. Krishna calls them all "noble", but loves most the
    one who knows. *In the story:* in the gambling hall, when her husbands sat
    silent, Draupadi turned to Krishna in her distress. Before the war she
    reminds him, "I longed for you in my mind, Govinda: save me!" (CE 5.80.26;
    see ledger L-19).

[^b6c9-13]: **"Vasudeva is everything."** *What it means:* Vasudeva is a name of
    Krishna, "son of Vasudeva". Krishna says that at the end of a long search,
    the wise person sees that he is in everything.

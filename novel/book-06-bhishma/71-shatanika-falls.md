# Shatanika Falls

"With the ranks arrayed like that," said Sanjaya, "and most of them following,
all became intent on the world of Brahma, Bharata. In that confusion rank did
not engage with rank, nor chariots with chariot fighters, nor foot soldiers with
foot soldiers. Horses did not fight horses, nor elephants elephant fighters. A
great and fierce confusion of the two armies came about. With men, elephants and
chariots scattered like that on every side, there was no distinction in that
very fierce destruction.

"Then Shalya, Kripa, Chitrasena, Duhshasana and Vikarna, brave men, mounted
their chariots quickly, Bharata, and made the Pandava host tremble in battle.
Being killed in battle by those great-souled men, the Pandava army found no
protector, like a ship sinking in water. As the cold season cuts the vitals of
cattle, so Bhishma cut the vitals of the sons of Pandu. And of your army,
great-souled Partha brought down very many elephants like mountains and clouds.
Leaders of troops of men were seen crushed by Partha, struck by arrows and iron
arrows in thousands. Great elephants fell here and there, crying out in
distress. Bhishma and Dhananjaya the Pandava were showing their courage in
battle, king, in that very terrible destruction of the best heroes. The
battlefield shone, covered with the bodies of great-souled men killed with their
ornaments bound on, and with heads with earrings.

"Seeing the grandfather showing his courage in battle, king, the Kauravyas did
not turn back, putting the world of Brahma first. Wanting death in battle,
making heaven their goal, they went against the Pandavas in that destruction of
the best heroes. The brave Pandavas too, great king, remembered the many
sufferings of many kinds that you and your son had caused them before, lord of
men. They gave up fear in battle. Putting the world of Brahma first, they fought
your men and your sons as if glad.

"The commander of the army, the great chariot fighter, said to the army in
battle, 'Run at Ganga's son, Somakas, with the Srinjayas!' Hearing the
commander's words, the Somakas with the Srinjayas ran at Ganga's son on every
side with a rain of weapons. Being struck, king, your father, Shantanu's son,
overcome by impatience, fought the Srinjayas. Long ago the wise Rama gave that
famous one, son, his training in weapons. It destroys enemy ranks. Relying on
that training, the old grandfather of the Kurus destroyed the enemy's force. Day
after day Bhishma, killer of enemy heroes, killed ten thousand of the Parthas.
When that tenth day came, bull of the Bharatas, Bhishma alone killed countless
elephants and horses among the Matsyas and Panchalas, and seven great chariot
fighters. The great-grandfather killed five thousand chariot fighters in the
great battle and fourteen thousand men, a thousand elephants and ten thousand
horses again, lord of the people, by the strength of his training. Then he shook
the army of all the kings. He brought down Shatanika, the dear brother of
Virata. Having killed Shatanika in battle, great king, mighty Bhishma brought
down thousands of kings with broad arrows. All the kings of the Parthas who went
with Dhananjaya, having reached Bhishma, went to Yama's home.

"So Bhishma, having passed through the army of the Parthas on every side with
nets of arrows in the ten directions, stood at the head of the army. Having done
a very great deed on that tenth day, he stood between the armies with his bow in
hand. None of the kings could even look at him, king, like the burning sun in
the sky at midday in the hot season. As Shakra scorched the army of the daityas
in battle, so Bhishma scorched the Pandavas, Bharata.

"Seeing him showing his courage like that, the Killer of Madhu, Devaki's son,
said to Dhananjaya with affection, 'Here is Bhishma son of Shantanu, standing
between the armies. Without killing him by force, you will have no victory.
Ready, stop him where this army is breaking. No one else can bear Bhishma's
arrows, lord.'

"Then at that moment, king, urged on, the one with the monkey banner hid
Bhishma, with his banner, chariot and horses, with arrows. And the bull among
the chief Kurus blew away those crowds of arrows sent by the Pandava in many
ways with crowds of arrows. Many were hard pressed by Bhishma's arrows and sunk
in an ocean of grief: the king of the Panchalas, mighty Dhrishtaketu, Bhimasena
the Pandava and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, the twins and Chekitana. So
were the five Kekayas, great king, Satyaki, Subhadra's son and Ghatotkacha, the
sons of Draupadi, Shikhandi and mighty Kuntibhoja, Susharma and Virata. Phalguna
lifted out these very strong Pandava fighters and many others.

"Then Shikhandi, taking up his finest weapon, ran at speed at Bhishma, protected
by the diademed one. Then the unconquered Bibhatsu, who knew the divisions of
battle, killed all his followers and ran at Bhishma himself. Satyaki, Chekitana,
Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, Virata, Drupada and Madri's two Pandava
sons ran at Bhishma in battle, protected by the firm bowman. Abhimanyu and
Draupadi's five sons ran at Bhishma in battle with great weapons raised. All of
them, firm bowmen who never fled in battle, struck Bhishma in many ways with
arrows, having made the arrows their road.

"The grandfather shook off those crowds of arrows loosed by the best of kings.
High in spirit, he plunged into the Pandava army, warding off the arrows as if
playing. Smiling again and again, he did not aim at the Panchala, remembering
that he had been a woman. He did not aim his arrows at Shikhandi. The great
chariot fighter killed seven chariot fighters in Drupada's army. Then in a
moment a kilakila cry arose from the Matsyas, Panchalas and Chedis as they ran
at that one man. That one man was Bhishma son of Bhagirathi, scorching the enemy
in battle. They covered him with crowds of fine horses and chariots and with
elephants and foot soldiers, as clouds cover the sun. Then, in their battle with
him, like that of the gods and asuras, the diademed one came up against Bhishma,
with Shikhandi in front."

summary: Chapters 3 to 5 of the Bhagavad Gita, told simply, with footnotes. Arjuna asks why he must fight if understanding is better than action; Krishna says no one can stop acting, that work done as an offering does not bind, that the wise work to hold the world together, and that desire and anger are the real enemy; he tells how this yoga was handed down, how he is born age after age to protect the good, what true action and inaction are, and the many kinds of sacrifice, above all the sacrifice of knowledge; Arjuna asks whether giving up action or doing it is better, and Krishna says both lead to freedom but doing it is better, and describes the one who is free and at peace.
---

# Action

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'if you think understanding is better than action, then
why are you pushing me into this terrible action? Your words are mixed, and they
confuse me. Tell me one thing, clearly, that will lead me to what is best.'

"'Blameless one,' said Krishna, 'as I said before, there are two paths in this
world. For people of knowledge, there is the yoga of knowledge. For people of
action, there is the yoga of action.

"'A person does not become free from action by not starting any work. And just
giving things up does not bring perfection.[^b6c8-1] No one can go without
acting, not even for a moment. Everyone is made to act, whether they want to or
not, by the strands of their own nature. Someone who holds his body still, but
keeps thinking in his mind about the things of the senses, is fooling himself.
He is called a fake. But the person who controls the senses with the mind,
Arjuna, and does the work of action through the body without clinging, is far
better.

"'Do the work you have to do. Doing is better than not doing. Without work, you
could not even keep your body alive.

"'This world is chained by action, except for action done as a sacrifice. So do
your work as a sacrifice, Arjuna, free from clinging.[^b6c8-2] Long ago, when
the Lord of Creatures made people, he made sacrifice along with them. He said,
"By this you will grow. Let this be the cow that gives you all you wish for.
Feed the gods with it, and let the gods feed you. By feeding each other, you
will reach the highest good. The gods, fed by sacrifice, will give you the good
things you want." A person who enjoys what the gods give, without giving them
anything back, is just a thief. Good people who eat what is left over from the
sacrifice are freed from all their wrongs. But bad people who cook only for
themselves are eating sin.

"'Living beings come from food. Food comes from rain. Rain comes from sacrifice,
and sacrifice comes from action. Know that action comes from Brahman, and
Brahman comes from the Imperishable. So Brahman, which is everywhere, is always
present in sacrifice. This is the wheel that has been set turning. A person who
does not help to keep it turning, and lives only for the pleasure of his senses,
lives in vain, Arjuna.

"'But a person who delights only in the self, who is satisfied with the self and
content in the self, has nothing left that he needs to do. He gains nothing by
acting, and nothing by not acting. He does not depend on any creature for
anything. So always do the work that has to be done, without clinging. By
working without clinging, a person reaches the highest.

"'Janaka and others reached perfection through action alone.[^b6c8-3] You should
act too, if only to hold the world together.[^b6c8-4] Whatever a great person
does, other people do too. Whatever standard he sets, the world follows.

"'Look at me, Arjuna. There is nothing in the three worlds that I have to do.
There is nothing I have not got, or need to get. And yet I keep on working. If I
ever stopped working, without tiring, people everywhere would follow my path. If
I did not work, these worlds would fall to ruin. I would be the cause of
confusion, and I would destroy these people.

"'Foolish people work because they are attached to their work. The wise should
work just as hard, but without attachment, wanting to hold the world together. A
wise person should not unsettle the minds of simple people who are attached to
their work. He should do all his work with a steady mind, and so make them enjoy
their work too.

"'In truth, all actions are done by the strands of nature. But a person confused
by the sense of "I" thinks, "I am the one who does it."[^b6c8-5] The one who
knows the truth about the strands and their work, strong-armed one, sees that it
is only strands acting on strands, and does not get attached. People confused by
the strands of nature cling to the work of the strands. The one who knows the
whole should not disturb those slow people who know only a part.

"'Give all your actions to me, with your mind on the self. Be free from wanting
and from "mine", and fight, with the fever gone from you. People who follow this
teaching of mine always, with faith and without finding fault, are also freed
from their actions. But people who find fault with it and do not follow it are
lost. Know that they are confused about all knowledge, and mindless.

"'Even a wise person acts according to his own nature. All beings follow their
nature. What good will forcing it do? Liking and disliking are fixed in each
sense for its objects. Do not fall under their power. They are the robbers on
your road.

"'It is better to do your own duty, even badly, than to do someone else's duty
well.[^b6c8-6] It is better to die doing your own duty. Someone else's duty is
full of danger.'

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'then what drives a person to do wrong, even when he
does not want to, as if he were being forced?'

"'It is wanting,' said Krishna. 'It is anger. It is born of the strand of
passion. It eats everything, and it is very evil. Know that this is the enemy
here.[^b6c8-7] As fire is covered by smoke, as a mirror is covered by dust, as a
baby in the womb is covered by the skin around it, so this world is covered by
it. Knowledge is covered by this constant enemy of the wise, in the form of
wanting, Arjuna, which is a fire that can never be filled. The senses, the mind
and the understanding are said to be its home. Through them it covers up
knowledge and confuses the one who lives in the body.

"'So first control your senses, Arjuna. Then kill this evil thing, which
destroys knowledge and wisdom. They say the senses are high. Higher than the
senses is the mind. Higher than the mind is understanding. And higher than
understanding is he, the self. Know the one who is higher than understanding,
steady yourself by the self, and kill this enemy in the form of wanting. It is
hard to reach, strong-armed one.'

"'I taught this lasting yoga to Vivasvat, the sun,' said Krishna. 'Vivasvat
taught it to Manu, and Manu taught it to Ikshvaku.[^b6c8-8] So it was handed
down from one to the next, and the royal seers knew it. But over a long time,
Arjuna, this yoga was lost here. Now I have taught you this same ancient yoga,
because you love me and are my friend. It is the highest secret.'

"'Your birth was later,' said Arjuna, 'and Vivasvat's birth was earlier. How am
I to understand that you taught it in the beginning?'

"'I have had many births, Arjuna,' said Krishna, 'and so have you. I know them
all. You do not. I am unborn. My self does not change. I am the Lord of all
beings. Yet I rule over my own nature and come into being by my own power.

"'Whenever dharma grows weak, Arjuna, and adharma rises up, I create myself. To
protect the good, to destroy those who do evil, and to set dharma firmly in
place again, I am born age after age.[^b6c8-9]

"'Whoever truly knows my divine birth and my work does not go to another birth
when he leaves his body. He comes to me, Arjuna. Many people, free from longing,
fear and anger, full of me, taking shelter in me, made pure by the fire of
knowledge, have come to be as I am. However people come to me, that is how I
receive them. People everywhere follow my path, Arjuna. People who want their
actions to succeed worship the gods here, because in the world of people,
success that comes from action comes quickly.

"'I made the four classes, divided by strands and by work.[^b6c8-10] Though I
made them, know that I am not the doer, and I do not change. Actions do not
stain me. I do not long for the fruit of actions. Whoever knows me like this is
not bound by actions. People long ago who wanted freedom knew this and still did
their work. So you too should do your work, as people did long before.

"'What is action? What is inaction? Even wise poets are confused about this. So
I will tell you what action is. When you know it, you will be free from harm.
You have to understand action, you have to understand wrong action, and you have
to understand inaction. The way of action is hard to see into.

"'The person who sees inaction in action, and action in inaction, is wise among
people.[^b6c8-11] He is steady, and he does all his work. When all of a person's
undertakings are free from wanting and from plans for himself, and his actions
are burned up in the fire of knowledge, the wise call him learned. He gives up
clinging to the fruit of action. He is always content and depends on nothing.
Even though he is busy with action, he does nothing at all.

"'Wanting nothing, with his mind and body under control, giving up all his
possessions, doing only the work of the body, he does no wrong. He is happy with
whatever comes by chance. He is beyond the pairs of opposites and free from
envy. He is the same in success and failure. Even when he acts, he is not bound.
He is free from clinging and set free, with his mind fixed in knowledge. When he
acts for the sake of sacrifice, the whole of his action melts away.

"'The offering is Brahman. The thing offered is Brahman. It is offered by
Brahman into the fire, which is Brahman. A person who sees Brahman in every
action will reach Brahman.[^b6c8-12]

"'Some yogis offer sacrifice to the gods. Others offer sacrifice by sacrifice
itself into the fire of Brahman. Some offer their hearing and the other senses
into the fires of self-control. Others offer sound and the other objects of the
senses into the fires of the senses. Others offer all the work of the senses and
all the work of the breath into the fire of the yoga of self-control, lit by
knowledge. Some offer wealth as their sacrifice. Some offer austerity. Some
offer yoga. Some, strict in their vows, offer the study of scripture and
knowledge. Some offer the breath going out into the breath coming in, and the
breath coming in into the breath going out. They hold back the flow of both and
are devoted to the control of breath.[^b6c8-13] Others eat little and offer
their breaths into their breaths.

"'All these people know what sacrifice is, and sacrifice washes away their
faults. They eat the nectar that is left from the sacrifice and go to the
eternal Brahman. Even this world is not for the person who makes no sacrifice,
let alone the other world, best of the Kurus. So many kinds of sacrifice are
spread out before Brahman. Know that all of them come from action. When you know
this, you will be set free.

"'The sacrifice of knowledge is better than a sacrifice of things, Arjuna. All
action, every bit of it, ends in knowledge. Learn it by bowing down, by asking
questions and by serving.[^b6c8-14] The wise, who see the truth, will teach you
knowledge. When you know it, Arjuna, you will never be confused like this again.
By it you will see all beings in your self, and then in me.

"'Even if you are the worst wrongdoer of all, you will cross over all wrong in
the boat of knowledge. As a blazing fire burns firewood to ash, Arjuna, so the
fire of knowledge burns all actions to ash. Nothing in this world makes you as
pure as knowledge. In time, the person who is perfect in yoga finds it for
himself, in the self.

"'The person who has faith, who is devoted to it and controls his senses, gains
knowledge. And when he has gained knowledge, he soon finds the highest peace.
But the ignorant person, without faith, full of doubt, is lost. For the person
full of doubt, there is neither this world nor the next, nor any happiness.
Actions do not bind the person who has given up action through yoga, who has cut
away doubt with knowledge, and who is master of himself, Arjuna. So cut away
this doubt in your heart, which comes from ignorance, with the sword of
knowledge. Hold to yoga. Stand up, Arjuna.'

"'Krishna,' said Arjuna, 'you praise giving up action, and then again you praise
the yoga of action. Tell me for certain which one of these two is better.'

"'Giving up action and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good,' said
Krishna.[^b6c8-15] 'But of the two, the yoga of action is better than giving up
action. Know that the person who neither hates nor wants is always one who has
given up. Free from the pairs of opposites, he is easily set free from bondage,
strong-armed one.

"'Children, not the wise, say that Sankhya and yoga are different. Whoever holds
well to either one gets the fruit of both. The place that is reached by Sankhya
is reached by yoga too. The one who sees that Sankhya and yoga are one truly
sees. But giving up action is hard to reach without yoga, strong-armed one. The
sage who is joined in yoga soon reaches Brahman.

"'A person joined in yoga, pure in himself, who has conquered himself and his
senses, whose self has become the self of all beings, is not stained, even when
he acts. The one who knows the truth, joined in yoga, thinks, "I am not doing
anything at all." He sees, hears, touches, smells, eats, walks, sleeps and
breathes. He talks, lets go, takes hold, opens and shuts his eyes. Through it
all he holds on to this: "It is only the senses moving among the things of the
senses."

"'Whoever places all his actions in Brahman and acts without clinging is not
stained by wrong, as a lotus leaf is not wetted by water.[^b6c8-16] Yogis do
their work with body, mind and understanding, and even with the senses alone,
without clinging, to make themselves pure. The person joined in yoga gives up
the fruit of action and reaches a lasting peace. The one not joined in yoga is
driven by wanting. He clings to the fruit and is bound.

"'The one who lives in the body, in control of himself, gives up all actions
with the mind and sits happily in the city of nine gates,[^b6c8-17] neither
acting nor making others act. The Lord does not create anyone's sense of being
the doer, or anyone's actions, or the joining of action to its fruit. It is
nature that works. The all-pervading one does not take on anyone's wrong, or
anyone's good deed. Knowledge is covered by ignorance, and that is why creatures
are confused. But for those whose ignorance has been destroyed by knowledge of
the self, knowledge lights up that highest reality, like the sun. With their
minds on that, their selves in that, fixed on that, with that as their goal,
they go where there is no return. Their faults have been shaken off by
knowledge.

"'The wise see the same thing in a learned and humble brahmin, a cow, an
elephant, a dog, and even an outcaste who eats dogs.[^b6c8-18] Those whose minds
are fixed in sameness have conquered birth here and now. For Brahman is without
fault, and always the same. So they are fixed in Brahman.

"'A person should not jump for joy when he gets what he likes, or be upset when
he gets what he does not like. With steady understanding and free from
confusion, the one who knows Brahman is fixed in Brahman. When his self is not
attached to outside touches, he finds the happiness that is in the self. Joined
to Brahman by yoga, he enjoys a happiness that never ends. The pleasures that
come from touching things are only sources of pain. They have a beginning and an
end, Arjuna. The wise do not delight in them. The person who can bear the rush
of wanting and anger here, before he leaves his body, is joined in yoga. He is a
happy person.

"'The one whose happiness is within, whose joy is within, whose light is within,
is a yogi. He becomes Brahman and reaches nirvana in Brahman. The seers whose
faults are gone reach nirvana in Brahman. Their doubts are cut away. They are in
control of themselves, and they delight in the good of all beings. For those who
are free from wanting and anger, who have controlled their thoughts and know the
self, nirvana in Brahman is near on every side.

"'The sage shuts out the outside world. He fixes his gaze between the eyebrows.
He makes the breath going out and the breath coming in move evenly through the
nose.[^b6c8-19] He controls his senses, mind and understanding, and has release
as his highest goal. Longing, fear and anger have gone from him. That person is
free for ever. He comes to know me. I am the one who enjoys every sacrifice and
austerity. I am the great Lord of all the worlds, and the friend of all beings.
Knowing me, he reaches peace.'"

## Explanations

[^b6c8-1]: **Freedom from action.** *What it means:* the aim is to be free, so
    that your actions no longer tie you down. Krishna says you cannot get there
    just by stopping work. *Everyday example:* a student who stops going to
    class to "be free" is not free. He has just stopped learning.

[^b6c8-2]: **Sacrifice (*yajña*).** *What it means:* in the old rites, people
    offered food, butter and grain into a sacred fire as gifts to the gods. The
    gods were thought to send rain and good harvests in return. Krishna widens
    the idea: any work done as an offering, not for yourself, is a sacrifice.
    *Everyday example:* a mother cooking for her family is not working for a
    wage. Her work is a kind of offering.

[^b6c8-3]: **Janaka.** *What it means:* a king of Videha, famous for being both
    a busy ruler and a wise man. *In the story:* the forest books tell how the
    boy-sage Ashtavakra went to King Janaka's great sacrifice and won a debate
    there (CE 3.132–134).

[^b6c8-4]: **Holding the world together (*lokasaṃgraha*).** *What it means:*
    doing your part so that the order of the world keeps going, and setting a
    good example. *Everyday example:* when a teacher is always on time, the
    students learn to be on time. When the teacher is late, they learn that too.
    Krishna says the same of himself: he has nothing to gain, and yet he works
    so that people do not lose their way (CE 6.25.22–24).

[^b6c8-5]: **The strands do the work.** *What it means:* Krishna says our bodies
    and minds are part of nature, and nature's three strands (goodness, passion,
    dullness) are what really move them. The mistake is thinking, "I, the self,
    am the doer." *Everyday example:* when you are tired you get cranky, and
    when you are rested you are patient. Much of what we call "my" mood comes
    from the body and its state.

[^b6c8-6]: **Your own duty (*svadharma*).** *What it means:* the duty that
    belongs to you, because of who you are and what you have taken on. Krishna
    says it is better to do your own duty imperfectly than to copy someone
    else's. *Everyday example:* a goalkeeper who leaves the goal to score goals
    may look brave, but the team loses. *In the story:* before the war, Kunti
    sent her sons the tale of Vidura, a mother who shamed her beaten son into
    getting up and fighting like a kshatriya. "It is better to blaze for a
    moment," she tells him, "than to smoulder for a long time" (CE 5.131.13).

[^b6c8-7]: **Wanting and anger, the real enemy.** *What it means:* Krishna names
    the enemy inside: *kāma*, wanting or desire, which turns into anger when it
    is blocked. *In the story:* Nahusha, a human king made king of the gods,
    wanted Indra's wife Shachi. "Let Shachi come to my house at once," he
    demanded (CE 5.11.15). His wanting grew into pride. He made the great seers
    carry him, and the seer Agastya cursed him and threw him down from heaven
    (CE 5.17).

[^b6c8-8]: **Vivasvat, Manu and Ikshvaku.** *What it means:* Vivasvat is the sun
    god. Manu, his son, is the father of the human race. Ikshvaku, Manu's son,
    was the first king of the solar line, the family into which Rama of the
    Ramayana was born. Krishna is saying that this teaching is as old as the
    first kings.

[^b6c8-9]: **"I am born age after age."** *What it means:* the Lord takes birth
    in the world again and again, whenever goodness is in danger, to protect the
    good and put things right. Later tradition calls such a birth an *avatāra*,
    "a coming down". The Gita does not use that word here. *In the story:* the
    same words are spoken in the forest books by the divine child on the banyan
    tree to the sage Markandeya: "Whenever dharma grows weak … I create myself"
    (CE 3.187.26). See ledger L-140.

[^b6c8-10]: **The four classes, by strands and work.** *What it means:* Krishna
    says the four classes (priests and teachers, rulers and warriors, farmers
    and traders, workers) were made according to people's natures (their
    "strands") and their work. He explains this in detail in the last chapter of
    the Gita (chapter 13 of this Book).

[^b6c8-11]: **Inaction in action.** *What it means:* a riddle. A wise person can
    be very busy and yet inwardly still, because he is not clinging to results.
    And someone sitting idle may be full of restless wanting. *Everyday
    example:* a calm surgeon working hard in an emergency is "still" inside. A
    person lying on a sofa and fretting is not still at all.

[^b6c8-12]: **Everything is Brahman.** *What it means:* in this famous verse,
    every part of the rite (the offering, the fire, the one who offers) is seen
    as Brahman, the one highest reality. For a person who sees this way, every
    action becomes holy.

[^b6c8-13]: **The breaths (*prāṇa* and *apāna*).** *What it means:* in yoga,
    *prāṇa* is the breath going out and *apāna* the breath coming in (some texts
    reverse them). "Offering one into the other" means breathing exercises that
    slow and even out the breath, to calm the mind.

[^b6c8-14]: **Learning from a teacher.** *What it means:* knowledge comes by
    humility, honest questions and service to a teacher. *Everyday example:* an
    apprentice learns a craft by watching, asking and helping the master. *In
    the story:* Arjuna himself is a pupil here. Just before this teaching begins
    he says to Krishna, "I am your pupil. Teach me, for I have come to you for
    refuge" (CE 6.24.7).

[^b6c8-15]: **Giving up action or doing it.** *What it means:* *saṃnyāsa* means
    renouncing, leaving the world and its work behind. Krishna says it is good,
    but doing your work in the spirit of yoga is better, and in the end the two
    are one. What matters is giving up clinging, not giving up work.

[^b6c8-16]: **The lotus leaf.** *What it means:* water runs off a lotus leaf
    without wetting it. So a person who works without clinging lives in the
    world without being stained by it.

[^b6c8-17]: **The city of nine gates.** *What it means:* the body. Its nine
    "gates" are its openings: two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, the mouth, and
    the two openings for waste. The self lives in this "city" like a calm ruler
    who does not need to rush about.

[^b6c8-18]: **Seeing all as the same.** *What it means:* the wise see the same
    self in a learned priest and in an animal or an outcaste. "Dog-eater"
    (*śvapāka*) was a word for people outside the four classes, thought of as
    the lowest. *In the story:* at the very end of the epic, Indra comes to take
    Yudhishthira to heaven and tells him to leave behind the dog that has
    followed him. Yudhishthira refuses. "This dog is devoted to me," he says. "I
    will not abandon one who is devoted to me, even for heaven" (CE 17.3.7–9).

[^b6c8-19]: **Gazing between the eyebrows.** *What it means:* a way of sitting
    in meditation, with eyes half-closed, attention resting between the
    eyebrows, and the breath slow and even. Krishna teaches it more fully in the
    next chapter.

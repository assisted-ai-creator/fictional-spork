# Age after Age

"'I taught this undying yoga to Vivasvat,' said the Blessed One. 'Vivasvat told
it to Manu, and Manu spoke it to Ikshvaku. So handed down from one to another,
the royal seers knew it. Over a long time here that yoga was lost, scorcher of
enemies. That same ancient yoga I have told you today, for you are devoted to me
and my friend. This is the highest secret.'

"'Your birth was later,' said Arjuna, 'and Vivasvat's birth was earlier. How am
I to understand that you taught it in the beginning?'

"'Many births of mine have passed, Arjuna, and of yours,' said the Blessed One.
'I know them all. You do not know them, scorcher of enemies. Though I am unborn
and my self does not decay, though I am the lord of beings, I come into being by
my own power, standing on my own nature. Whenever dharma declines, Bharata, and
adharma rises up, then I send myself forth. To protect the good and to destroy
the evil-doers, to set dharma firmly in its place, I come into being age after
age. One who knows truly my divine birth and work, when he leaves the body, is
not born again. He comes to me, Arjuna. Many, free of passion, fear and anger,
absorbed in me, taking refuge in me, made pure by the austerity of knowledge,
have come to my state. As men come to me, so I take their part. Men follow my
path in every way, Partha. Wanting success in their actions, they sacrifice here
to the gods, for success born of action comes quickly in the world of men.

"'The four classes were made by me, by the division of qualities and actions.
Though I am their maker, know me as the undying one who does not act. Actions do
not stain me, and I have no longing for the fruit of action. One who knows me
like this is not bound by actions. Knowing this, the men of old who sought
release did their work. So do your work, as the men of old did it long ago.

"'What is action, and what is inaction? Even poets are confused about this. I
will tell you what action is, and when you know it you will be freed from evil.
One must understand action, understand wrong action, and understand inaction.
The way of action is deep. One who sees inaction in action and action in
inaction is wise among men. He is disciplined and does all his work. One whose
undertakings are all free of desire and purpose, whose actions are burned in the
fire of knowledge, the wise call learned. Giving up attachment to the fruit of
action, always content, depending on nothing, even when he is busy with action
he does nothing at all. Without hope, his thought and self controlled, having
given up all possessions, doing only the work of the body, he incurs no sin.
Content with what comes by chance, beyond the pairs of opposites, free of envy,
the same in success and failure, even when he acts he is not bound. For one
whose attachment has gone, who is free, whose thought stands in knowledge, who
acts for sacrifice, all his action melts away.

"'The offering is Brahman, the oblation is Brahman, poured by Brahman into the
fire that is Brahman. Brahman is what he will reach, who is absorbed in the
action that is Brahman. Some yogins worship by sacrifice to the gods alone.
Others offer sacrifice by sacrifice itself into the fire of Brahman. Others
offer hearing and the other senses into the fires of restraint. Others offer
sound and the other objects into the fires of the senses. Others offer all the
actions of the senses and the actions of the breath into the fire of the yoga of
self-restraint, lit by knowledge. Some, ascetics of strict vows, sacrifice with
wealth, with austerity, with yoga, with study and with knowledge. Others offer
the in-breath into the out-breath and the out-breath into the in-breath,
stopping the course of both, intent on control of the breath. Others,
restricting their food, offer the breaths into the breaths. All these know
sacrifice, and their faults are destroyed by sacrifice. Eating the amrita that
is left of the sacrifice, they go to eternal Brahman. This world is not for one
who does not sacrifice, best of the Kurus. How then the other? So many kinds of
sacrifice are spread out in the mouth of Brahman. Know that all of them are born
of action. Knowing this, you will be free.

"'The sacrifice of knowledge is better than the sacrifice of wealth, scorcher of
enemies. All action without exception, Partha, comes to its end in knowledge.
Learn it by bowing down, by asking, by serving. Those who have knowledge, who
see the truth, will teach you knowledge. When you know it you will not fall into
confusion again like this, Pandava. By it you will see all beings without
exception in the self, and so in me. Even if you were the worst of all
evil-doers, you would cross over all wrong by the boat of knowledge alone. As a
lit fire turns its fuel to ash, Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge turns all
actions to ash. Nothing here purifies like knowledge. One who is perfected in
yoga finds it himself in the self, in time. A man of faith, intent on it, his
senses controlled, gains knowledge. Having gained knowledge he soon reaches the
highest peace. The ignorant man without faith, whose self is doubt, perishes.
Neither this world nor the next nor happiness is for the man whose self is
doubt. Actions do not bind one who has renounced action by yoga, whose doubt is
cut by knowledge, who is master of himself, Dhananjaya. So cut this doubt of
yours, born of ignorance and lodged in your heart, with the sword of knowledge
of the self. Take to yoga. Stand up, Bharata.'"

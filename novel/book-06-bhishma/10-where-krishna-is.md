# Where Krishna Is

"At sunrise, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "which side was first eager for
battle and glad: mine, led by Bhishma in the fight, or the Pandavas, led by
Bhima? For whom were the moon and the sun, with the winds, behind? Against whose
army did the beasts of prey howl? Whose young men's faces were bright? Tell me
all of this truly, as it was."

"The two armies came on as if equal, lord of men," said Sanjaya. "Both arrays
looked glad. Both were bright, shining like lines of forest, both full of
elephants, chariots and horses. Both armies were vast and terrible, Bharata,
both hard to bear, both let loose to win heaven, both guarded by good and noble
men. The Kurus, Dhritarashtra's sons, faced west; the Parthas stood facing east,
ready to fight. The Kauravas' army was like the army of the lord of the daityas,
and the Pandavas' like the army of the lord of the gods. A bright wind blew
behind the Pandavas, and the beasts of prey howled at Dhritarashtra's sons. Your
son's elephants could not bear the sharp smell of rut from the lords of
elephants.

"Duryodhana was mounted on a lotus-coloured elephant with a golden girth, of
good breed and strength, in rut, in the middle of the Kurus, praised by bards
and heralds. His white umbrella shone like the moon, and a golden garland shone
on his head. Shakuni, king of Gandhara, guarded him on every side with the
mountain men and the Gandharas. Old Bhishma was ahead of the whole army, with a
white umbrella, a white bow and a conch, a white turban, a pale banner and white
horses, like a white mountain. With his army were all Dhritarashtra's sons, a
part of the Bahlikas, Shala, the Ambashtha kshatriyas, those of Sindhu, the
Sauviras and the brave men of the Five Rivers. With red horses and a golden
chariot, great Drona, strong-armed and undaunted, the teacher, famous among all
kings, stood behind, guarding the army like Indra.

"Varddhakshatri was in the middle of the whole army, with Bhurishravas,
Purumitra and Jaya, and the Shalvas, the Matsyas and all the Kekaya brothers,
ready to fight with ranks of elephants. Great Sharadvata, the great archer
Gautama, a fighter of many skills, bearing the northern load, guarded the army
from the north with Shakas, Kiratas, Yavanas and Pahlavas. Brihadbala, guarded
by Kritavarma, with great chariot fighters of the Andhakas, Vrishnis and Bhojas,
with the men of Surashtra and armed Nairritas, guarded your force from the
south. Ten thousand chariots of the samshaptakas, let loose for Arjuna's death
or victory, went wherever Arjuna went, king, masters of weapons, and so did the
brave Trigartas.

"You had a full hundred thousand elephants, Bharata. With each elephant were a
hundred chariots, with each chariot a hundred horses, with each horse ten
archers, and with each archer ten shield-bearers. So your ranks were drawn up by
Bhishma, Bharata. Day after day as it came, Bhishma son of Shantanu, the leader,
drew up the human array, the divine, the gandharva and the asura. Vast with
floods of great chariot fighters, like the ocean at the change of the moon,
Bhishma's array for Dhritarashtra's sons faced west in the battle. Your army,
lord of men, was endless in form and terrible, but not the Pandavas'. Yet I
think theirs is the great army, hard to attack, whose leaders are Keshava and
Arjuna.

---

"Seeing the great army of Dhritarashtra's sons ready, King Yudhishthira, Kunti's
son, lost heart. He saw the array Bhishma had made that could not be broken.
Dismayed, as if it were truly unbreakable, the Pandava said to Arjuna,
'Dhananjaya, strong-armed one, how can we fight Dhritarashtra's sons in battle,
when the grandfather fights for them? This array cannot be shaken or broken,
made by Bhishma the slayer of enemies, of great splendour, by the rules seen in
the science. We and our army have come into doubt, crusher of enemies. How shall
we get out of this great array?'

"Then Arjuna, slayer of enemies, seeing your army, king, spoke to Partha
Yudhishthira, who had lost heart. 'Hear, lord of the people, how fewer men can
conquer many brave men who are superior in wisdom and endowed with virtues. I
will tell you the reason, king, since you do not find fault. The seer Narada
knows it, Pandava, and so do Bhishma and Drona. Long ago, they say, in the war
of the gods and asuras, the grandfather relied on this very thing. He told Indra
and the other gods: "Those who want victory do not win so much by strength and
courage as by truth and kindness, by dharma and by effort. Give up adharma,
greed and folly, take to effort, and fight without self-regard. Where dharma is,
there is victory." So know, king, that victory in battle is sure for us. As
Narada told me, where Krishna is, there is victory. Victory is a quality in
Krishna and follows Madhava. And victory is one more of his qualities, and
humility another. Govinda, of endless splendour, unshaken among hosts of
enemies, is the most eternal Person. Where Krishna is, there is victory. Long
ago he became Hari, Vaikuntha, whose arrows are never blunted, and thundering at
the gods and asuras he said, "Who will win?" Those who said, "We will win by
following Krishna," won. By his grace Shakra and the other gods won the three
worlds. I see no cause for distress for you here, Bharata, when the eater of the
universe, the lord of the thirty gods, wishes you victory.'

---

"Then King Yudhishthira urged on his army, bull of the Bharatas, drawing up his
ranks against Bhishma's. The Pandavas, the upholders of the Kurus, drew up their
ranks as they had been assigned, wanting the highest heaven by fighting well. In
the middle was Shikhandi's rank, guarded by the left-handed archer, and
Dhrishtadyumna's, guarded by Bhima himself. The right rank, king, was guarded by
Yuyudhana, the glorious chief of the Satvatas, an archer like Shakra.

"Yudhishthira stood in the middle of a herd of elephants, mounted on a chariot
like great Indra's car, fully fitted, bright with gold and jewels, with golden
harness and reins. His tall umbrella with ivory ribs, very white, shone
brightly, and great seers went round the lord of men, keeping him on their
right, with hymns of praise. Priests spoke of the killing of enemies, old great
seers learned in scripture, and with prayers, mantras and herbs they performed
rites of blessing on every side. Then the great best of the Kurus went on,
giving clothes, cows, fruits, flowers and gold coins to the brahmins, like
Shakra to the immortals.

"Arjuna's chariot, with a thousand suns and a hundred little bells, bright with
the costliest gold, shone like fire garlanded with flames, with white horses and
fine wheels. On it stood the monkey-bannered one, driven by Keshava, with
Gandiva and arrows in his hand, an archer whose equal on earth does not exist
and never will. Ready to overturn your son's army, he took on a very terrible
form. Vrikodara Bhimasena, who without weapons could turn men, horses and
elephants to ashes in battle with his strong arms, was with the twins, the
guardian of the hero's chariot. Your fighters saw him at the head of the army,
hard to approach, playing like a bull in rut or a lion, like great Indra in the
world, Vrikodara with the pride of a lord of elephants. Their spirits shaken by
fear, they trembled like camels sunk in mud.

"Janardana spoke to Gudakesha, the best of the Bharatas, the prince hard to
approach, standing in the middle of the army. 'This one who guards, burning,
standing in his strength, who looks at our army like a lion, is Bhishma, the
banner of the Kuru line, who performed thirty horse sacrifices. These ranks hide
that great one as clouds hide the sun. Kill these, hero among men, and then seek
battle with the bull of the Bharatas.'"

"Which fighters there fought joyfully at the front, Sanjaya?" said
Dhritarashtra. "Who were high-spirited, and who were dejected and dismayed? Who
struck first there in that heart-shaking battle, mine or the Pandavas? Tell me
that, Sanjaya. In whose gathering of armies was there the smell of perfumes and
garlands, and words of good omen from the fighters as they roared?"

"The fighters of both armies there were filled with joy," said Sanjaya.
"Garlands, incense, drink and perfumes arose on both sides. As the ranks, packed
and drawn up, crept forward and rose up, there was a very great crush, bull of
the Bharatas. The sound of instruments was tumultuous, mixed with conches and
drums, and with the trumpeting of elephants and the shouts of the joyful
armies."

**Divergences.**

* **The hymn to Durga.** Before the Gita the vulgate has Arjuna sing a hymn to Durga on Krishna's advice. It is not in the CE (Appendix I No. 1, after 6.22.16). Not narrated. Ledger L-34.
* **Who loses heart first.** In the CE it is Yudhishthira who loses heart at the sight of Bhishma's array, and Arjuna who reassures him (6.21). Arjuna's own despair comes in the next chapter.
* Rejected lines (\*76–\*84) add detail. Not narrated.

* 6.20.15 *saṃśaptaka*: "the samshaptakas", warriors sworn to kill Arjuna or die.
* 6.21.11–14: "Where dharma is, there is victory" and "where Krishna is, there is victory" are kept.

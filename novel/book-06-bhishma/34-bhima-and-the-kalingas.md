# Bhima and the Kalingas

"How did the Kalinga, lord of an army, ordered like this," said Dhritarashtra,
"fight in battle with his army against mighty Bhimasena of wonderful deeds, the
hero moving with his club like the Ender with his rod?"

"Spoken to like this by your son, king of kings," said Sanjaya, "the mighty one,
guarded by a great army, went towards Bhima's chariot. That great army of the
Kalingas came on at once, crowded with chariots, elephants and horses, with
great weapons raised. Bhimasena went against the army of the Kalingas, Bharata,
and against Ketuman the Nishada coming with the Chedis. Then Shrutayus in anger,
with King Ketuman, came at Bhima in battle as the Chedis stood in their ranks.
The lord of the Kalingas with many thousands of chariots, and Ketuman with ten
thousand elephants and the Nishadas, surrounded Bhimasena on every side in
battle, king. The Chedis, Matsyas and Karushas, led by Bhimasena, went at once
against the Nishadas and their kings.

"Then a terrible, fearful battle began. Wanting to kill one another, they did
not know their own fighters. Bhima's battle with the enemy was at once terrible,
great king, like Indra's with the great army of the daityas. As that army fought
in the battle, Bharata, there was a very great noise, like the roaring of the
sea. Cutting one another down, lord of the people, the fighters made the whole
earth like a burning ground, like hare's blood. Wanting to kill, they did not
know their own men from the enemy. Brave men, hard to conquer in battle, even
struck their own. There was a very great crush of few against many, of the
Chedis against the Kalingas and the Nishadas, lord of the people.

"Having shown their manhood as far as they could, the mighty Chedis left
Bhimasena and turned back. When the Chedis had turned back, the Pandava was
faced by all the Kalingas. Relying on the strength of his own arms, he did not
turn back. Mighty Bhimasena did not move from his chariot floor. He covered the
army of the Kalingas with sharp arrows.

"The great archer Kalinga and his son, the great chariot fighter known as
Shakradeva, struck the Pandava with arrows. Then strong-armed Bhima, shaking his
bright bow, fought the Kalingas, relying on the strength of his arms.
Shakradeva, loosing many arrows in battle, killed Bhimasena's horses with his
arrows and rained showers of arrows like a cloud at the end of the hot season.
Standing on his chariot with its horses killed, mighty Bhimasena hurled at
Shakradeva a club all of iron. Struck by it, king, the son of the Kalinga fell
from his chariot to the ground with his banner and his charioteer.

"Seeing his own son killed, the lord of the Kalingas blocked Bhima's quarters
with many thousands of chariots. Then strong-armed Bhima let go his heavy great
club and drew his sword, wanting to do a terrible deed. He took a matchless
shield of bull's hide, king, bull among men, studded with stars and half-moons
of gold. Then the Kalinga in his anger wiped his bowstring, took up a single
terrible arrow like a snake's poison, and sent it at Bhimasena, the lord of men,
wanting to kill him. As that sharp arrow came on with force, Bhimasena cut it in
two with his broad sword, king, and roared gladly, terrifying the army. Then the
Kalinga in his anger quickly sent at Bhimasena in battle fourteen lances whetted
on stone. Before they reached him, while they were still in the air, the
strong-armed Pandava cut them with his fine sword, king, undismayed.

"When he had cut down the fourteen lances in battle, Bhima, bull among men, saw
Bhanuman and ran at him. Bhanuman covered Bhima with a shower of arrows and
roared a mighty roar that filled the sky. Bhima did not bear that lion's roar in
the great battle. Then he roared a great roar in a loud voice. At that sound the
army of the Kalingas was terrified. They did not think Bhima human in the
battle, bull of the Bharatas.

"Then, great king, Bhima, having roared his great roar, leapt with speed, sword
in hand, by the two tusks of a fine elephant, and climbed onto the middle of the
lord of elephants, sir. Then with his broad sword he cut Bhanuman in the middle.
Having killed the prince, who was fighting at close quarters, the tamer of
enemies brought his sword down on the elephant's neck, which could bear heavy
loads. Its neck cut, roaring, the leader of the herd fell, like a mountain with
its ridges broken by the force of the sea. Then the Bharata leapt down from the
elephant, Bharata, sword in hand, undaunted, and stood on the ground in armour.

"He went many ways, fearless, bringing down elephants. He was seen on every side
like a whirling wheel of fire. Among troops of horses, elephants and ranks of
chariots, and among crowds of foot soldiers, the lord went killing, smeared with
blood. Bhima moved like a hawk in battle, fierce among the enemy forces. With
great speed he cut off the bodies and heads of the elephant fighters in battle
with his sharp-edged sword. On foot and alone, in anger, increasing the fear of
his enemies, he bewildered them, like Time, the Ender, or Yama. Bewildered, they
rushed at him in the battle, roaring, as he moved with his sword with the
greatest speed in the great battle. Cutting the chariot poles and yokes of the
chariot fighters in battle, the mighty crusher of enemies killed the chariot
fighters too.

"Bhimasena was seen going many ways. The Pandava showed whirling, circling,
swinging, leaping, rushing forward, going, falling and rising. Some, cut by the
great Pandava's sword point, roared with their vitals pierced and fell lifeless.
Elephants with their tusks and trunks cut off and others with their temples
split, without their riders, killed their own ranks, Bharata, and fell to the
earth roaring great roars. We saw lances and bows cut, and the heads of the
elephant drivers, bright trappings and girths shining with gold. We saw neck
chains, spears, flags and darts, quivers, bright engines and bows. We saw bright
fire pots, goads with their hooks, bells of many kinds, king, and sword hilts
set with gold, falling and fallen, with their riders. With elephants killed,
their limbs, fore-parts and trunks cut off, the ground there was strewn as if
with fallen mountains.

"When he had crushed the great elephants like this, the bull among men crushed
the horses and brought down the finest horsemen, Bharata. That battle of his
with them was terrible, Bharata. Bridles and harness, whips shining with gold,
trappings, lances, costly spears, armour and shields and bright coverings were
seen thrown about here and there in the great battle. With bright headpieces and
with bright weapons he made the earth strewn as if with many-coloured flowers.
Leaping up, the mighty one seized some chariot fighters and brought them down
with his sword, with their banners. The famous one rose up again and again in
every direction, running and going many wonderful ways, and people were amazed
in the battle. He killed some with his foot, dragged others and crushed them,
cut others with his sword and frightened others with his roar. He brought others
down to the ground with the force of his thighs, and others, seeing him, died of
fear. So that great army of the swift Kalingas surrounded Bhishma in the battle
and rushed at Bhimasena.

"Then, at the head of the Kalinga troops, bull of the Bharatas, Bhimasena saw
Shrutayus and went at him. Seeing him come, the Kalinga, of boundless spirit,
pierced Bhimasena between the breasts with nine arrows. Struck by the Kalinga's
arrows like an elephant struck by a goad, Bhimasena blazed with anger like fire
with fuel. Then Ashoka, the charioteer, bringing a chariot adorned with gold,
brought Bhima a chariot. The slayer of enemies, Kunti's son, mounted the chariot
quickly and ran at the Kalinga, crying, 'Stand! Stand!' Then mighty Shrutayus in
anger sent sharp arrows at Bhima, showing his lightness of hand. Struck hard by
nine sharp arrows loosed from the Kalinga's fine bow, king, famous Bhima grew
very angry, like a snake struck with a stick. In anger, drawing his bow
powerfully, Bhima Partha, the best of the strong, killed the Kalinga with seven
iron arrows. With two razor-headed arrows he sent the Kalinga's mighty wheel
guards, Satyadeva and Satya, to the house of Yama. Then, of boundless spirit, he
sent Ketuman to the house of Yama in battle with three sharp iron arrows.

"Then the Kalinga kshatriyas, in anger, held back impatient Bhimasena with many
thousands of troops. Then the Kalingas covered Bhimasena, king, with spears,
clubs, swords, lances, darts and axes. Holding back that terrible shower of
arrows that arose, the mighty one took his club, leapt down with speed, and sent
seven hundred heroes to the house of Yama. Again the crusher of enemies sent two
thousand Kalingas to the world of death, and it was a wonder. So again and again
the hero broke the ranks of the Kalingas in battle, looking at Bhishma of great
vows. Elephants whose riders had been killed by the great Pandava scattered
among the ranks like clouds struck by the wind, crushing their own ranks,
roaring, tormented by arrows.

"Then mighty, strong-armed Bhima blew his conch and shook the hearts of all the
Kalinga troops. Confusion came over the Kalingas, scorcher of enemies, and the
armies and all the animals trembled. As Bhima went many ways in battle like a
lord of elephants, running here and there and leaping up again and again,
confusion arose on every side. The army trembled in fear of Bhimasena, stirred
up as a great lake is stirred by a crocodile. The heroes had been terrified by
Bhima of wonderful deeds, and all the Kalinga fighters were turning back and
running in crowds. Then the commander of the Pandus' army, Prishata's grandson,
said to his own ranks, 'Fight!' Hearing the general's words, the troops led by
Shikhandi went against Bhima alone, with ranks of chariots ready to strike. And
the Pandava King of Dharma supported them all from behind with a great rank of
elephants the colour of clouds. Having urged on all his own ranks like this,
Prishata's grandson took his place guarding Bhimasena's rear, as good men do.
For there is no one in the world dearer to the Panchala prince than his own life
except Bhima and Satyaki.

"Prishata's grandson, slayer of enemy heroes, saw strong-armed Bhimasena, slayer
of enemies, moving among the Kalingas. The scorcher of enemies roared many
times, king, and was glad. He blew his conch in the battle and roared a lion's
roar. Seeing the banner of the kovidara tree on the chariot with dove-coloured
horses adorned with gold, Bhimasena took heart. Seeing Bhimasena set upon by the
Kalingas, Dhrishtadyumna, of boundless spirit, came in the battle to rescue him.
Satyaki saw from far off the two heroes, Dhrishtadyumna and Vrikodara, fighting
the Kalingas in battle, high-spirited. Going there with speed, Shaineya, best of
victors, bull among men, took his place guarding the rear of Partha and
Prishata's grandson. Making slaughter there, with his bow taken up, he took on a
fierce self and killed the enemy in battle. Bhima made a river flow there, born
of the Kalingas, with a mire of flesh and blood, running with blood. Between the
army of the Kalingas and the Pandavas, mighty Bhimasena crossed it, though it
was very hard to cross. Seeing Bhimasena like that, your men cried out, king,
'This is Time in the form of Bhima, fighting the Kalingas.'

"Then Bhishma son of Shantanu, hearing that cry in the battle, came quickly at
Bhima with his ranks drawn up on every side. Satyaki, Bhimasena and
Dhrishtadyumna Prishata's grandson rushed at Bhishma's chariot adorned with
gold. All of them surrounded Ganga's son, fierce in battle, and quickly struck
Bhishma with three terrible arrows each. Your father Devavrata pierced back all
those great archers as they strove, with three straight arrows each. Then,
holding back those great chariot fighters with a thousand arrows, he killed
Bhima's horses with their golden armour with arrows. Standing on his chariot
with its horses killed, mighty Bhimasena hurled a spear with force at the
chariot of Ganga's son. Before it reached him, your father Devavrata cut that
spear in three in the battle, and it shattered on the ground. Then Bhimasena
took a heavy, strong club all of iron and leapt quickly from his chariot, bull
among men. Satyaki too, to please Bhima, quickly brought down the charioteer of
the eldest of the Kurus with arrows. When his charioteer was killed, Bhishma,
the best of chariot fighters, was carried off the battlefield by his horses,
running like the wind.

"When the man of great vows had been carried off, king, Bhimasena blazed like a
fire grown great, burning dry grass. Having killed all the Kalingas, he stood in
the middle of the army, and none of your men dared go against him, bull of the
Bharatas. Dhrishtadyumna, the best of chariot fighters, took him up on his own
chariot and carried the famous one away while all the troops looked on. Honoured
by the Panchalas and the Matsyas, bull of the Bharatas, he embraced
Dhrishtadyumna and then went to Satyaki. Then Satyaki, true in courage, the
tiger of the Yadus, spoke to Bhimasena to cheer him, while Dhrishtadyumna looked
on. 'By good fortune the king of the Kalingas, Prince Ketuman, Shakradeva the
Kalinga and the Kalingas have been killed in battle. With the might and strength
of your own arms you alone have crushed the great array of the Kalingas, crowded
with elephants, horses and chariots.' Having said this, the long-armed grandson
of Shini, tamer of enemies, ran from his chariot to the other chariot and
embraced the Pandava. Then the great chariot fighter mounted his own chariot
again and in anger killed your men, adding to Bhima's strength."

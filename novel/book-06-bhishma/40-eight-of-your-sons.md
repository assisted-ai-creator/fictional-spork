# Eight of Your Sons

"When that force of elephants was destroyed," said Sanjaya, "your son Duryodhana
urged on all his troops: 'Kill Bhimasena!' Then at your son's command all the
ranks ran at Bhimasena, who was roaring fearful roars. That flood of troops was
endless, hard even for the gods to bear. It came on like the ocean at the full
or new moon, very hard to cross, crowded with chariots, elephants and horses,
loud with conches and drums. It was endless and without a farther shore, its
still pools full of kings. Bhimasena held off that unshakeable ocean of an army
in battle, as the shore holds back the boundless great sea.

"There we saw a wonderful, more than human deed of Bhimasena's in battle, king,
astonishing and yet to be believed. Without being flustered, Bhimasena beat back
with his club the whole roused earth with its horses, chariots and elephants.
Having held off those floods of troops with his club, Bhima, the best of chariot
fighters, stood in that tumult unmoving, like Mount Meru. In that very
tumultuous, terrible and most cruel time, his brothers and sons, Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's grandson, the sons of Draupadi, Abhimanyu and the great chariot
fighter Shikhandi did not leave the very strong Bhimasena when fear arose.

"Then he took up a great heavy club of Shaikya iron and killed your warriors
like Death with his staff in his hand. Crushing crowds of chariots and crowds of
horses, the mighty one went about the battle like fire at the end of an age. He
killed everyone in that battle like Time at the end of an age. The Pandava
dragged nets of chariots along with the force of his thighs and crushed all the
elephants, as an elephant crushes reed beds. He crushed chariot fighters from
their chariots, elephant fighters from their elephants, horsemen from their
horses' backs and foot soldiers on the ground. With men, elephants and horses
killed here and there, that battlefield became like Death's slaughterhouse. They
saw Bhimasena's terrible killing club, like the Pinaka of Rudra when he strikes
down beasts in anger, fierce as Yama's staff, sounding like Indra's thunderbolt.
As the great-souled Kaunteya whirled his club, his form looked most terrible,
like Time at the destruction of an age.

"Seeing him put that great army to flight again and again, coming on like death,
everyone lost heart. Wherever the Pandava looked with his club raised, all the
troops there broke apart, Bharata. He split the troops, unconquered by any flood
of troops. He swallowed the ranks like Death with his mouth open. Seeing
Wolf-belly, of terrible deeds, with his great club in his hand, Bhishma came at
him at once. He came in a great chariot that rumbled like a cloud and shone like
the sun, covering him with a shower of arrows like Parjanya pouring rain. Seeing
Bhishma coming like Death with his mouth open, the strong-armed Bhima,
impatient, went out against him.

"At that moment Satyaki, true to his word, the hero of the Shinis, fell on the
grandfather. He killed his enemies with his firm bow and made your son's army
shake. As he went with his silver-white horses, blowing arrows from his firm
bow, none of your bands could hold him back then, Bharata. Alambusa, the best of
kings, Rishyashringa's son, pierced him with sharp-pointed arrows. The hero,
Shini's grandson, pierced him back with four and fell on him with his chariot.
They heard that the best of the Vrishnis had come and was moving about in the
middle of the enemy, putting the Kuru bulls to flight and roaring again and
again in battle. They could not hold back that best of men, blazing like the sun
at midday. No one there was free from despair, king, except Somadatta's son. For
Bhurishravas, Somadatta's son, Bharata, took up his bow of fierce speed. Seeing
his own chariot fighters being driven off, he went out against Satyaki, wanting
to fight.

---

"Then Bhurishravas, king, very angry, pierced Satyaki with nine arrows, as a
great elephant is struck with goads. Satyaki, whose self is beyond measure,
covered the Kaurava with arrows with bent joints while the whole world watched.
Then King Duryodhana, surrounded by his brothers, ready in battle, surrounded
Somadatta's son on every side. So too all the Pandavas, of great power,
surrounded the impetuous Satyaki on every side in battle and stood there.
Bhimasena in anger raised his club, Bharata, and surrounded all your sons led by
Duryodhana.

"With many thousands of chariots, full of anger and impatience, your son Nandaka
pierced the very strong Bhimasena with six sharp, stone-whetted arrows fitted
with heron feathers. Duryodhana in anger struck the very strong Bhimasena in the
chest with three sharp arrows. Then the strong-armed, very strong Bhima climbed
onto his own chariot, the finest of chariots, and said to Vishoka, 'These are
the brave great chariot fighters, the very strong sons of Dhritarashtra. In
great anger they have risen to kill me in battle. Today I will kill them while
you watch, there is no doubt. So, charioteer, take care and keep my horses in
check in the battle.'

"Having said this, Partha pierced your son Duryodhana with ten sharp arrows
adorned with gold. He pierced Nandaka back with three arrows between the
breasts. Duryodhana pierced the very strong Bhima with sixty arrows and pierced
Vishoka with three other very sharp ones. Then the king, as if laughing, cut
Bhima's shining bow in battle at the grip with three sharp arrows. Bhima saw his
driver Vishoka hurt in battle by the sharp arrows of your son the bowman. He
could not bear it. In anger he took hold of a divine bow, great king, bull of
the Bharatas, to kill your son. In his fury he took up a razor-headed arrow
fitted with feathers, and with it Bhima cut the king's fine bow. Blazing as if
with anger, the king threw away the cut bow and quickly took up another bow of
greater speed. He fitted a terrible arrow, bright as Time and Death, and in
anger struck Bhimasena with it between the breasts. Deeply pierced and in pain,
he sank onto the chariot floor. Sitting there on the chariot floor, he fainted.

"Seeing Bhima in pain, the great archers, the great chariot fighters of the
Pandavas led by Abhimanyu, could not bear it. Without faltering they brought
down a tumultuous rain of sharp, fiery weapons on your son's head. Then the very
strong Bhimasena came to his senses. He pierced Duryodhana with three arrows and
pierced him again with five. The Pandava, the great archer, pierced Shalya with
twenty-five arrows with golden feathers, and Shalya, pierced, went away from the
battle.

"Then fourteen of your sons went against Bhima: Senapati, Sushena, Jalasandha,
Sulochana, Ugra, Bhimaratha, Bhima, Bhimabahu, Alolupa, Durmukha,
Dushpradharsha, Vivitsu, Vikata and Sama. Loosing many arrows, their eyes red
with anger, they ran at Bhimasena together and pierced him hard. But the very
strong Bhimasena saw your sons and licked the corners of his mouth, the hero,
like a wolf among cattle. The Pandava cut off Senapati's head with a
razor-headed arrow. He split Jalasandha open and took him to Yama's home. Then
he killed Sushena and sent him to death. He brought Ugra's head, like the moon,
with its helmet and adorned with earrings, down to the ground with a broad
arrow. With seventy arrows Bhima sent Bhimabahu in battle to the other world,
sir, with his horses, banner and charioteer. As if laughing, king, Bhimasena
took the two impetuous brothers Bhima and Bhimaratha to Yama's home. Then in the
great battle Bhima took Sulochana to Yama's home with a razor-headed arrow,
while all the troops watched. Your other sons who were left there, seeing
Bhimasena's courage, were tormented by fear of Bhima. Being killed by the
great-souled one, they fled in all directions, king.

"Then Shantanu's son said to all the great chariot fighters, 'This Bhima, angry
in battle, with his fierce bow, is bringing down the great chariot fighters, the
sons of Dhritarashtra, as they meet him, in order of rank, of age and of
bravery. Crush him, kings!' So addressed, all the soldiers of Dhritarashtra's
son ran in anger at the very strong Bhimasena.

"Bhagadatta, on an elephant in rut, lord of the people, fell at once on the
place where Bhima stood. As he fell on him in battle, he made Bhimasena
invisible with stone-whetted arrows, as a cloud hides the sun. The great chariot
fighters led by Abhimanyu could not bear Bhima's being covered in battle.
Relying on the strength of their own arms, they surrounded Bhagadatta on every
side with a shower of arrows and pierced his elephant on every side with a rain
of arrows. Struck by the rain of weapons, very sharp arrows with many kinds of
marks, the elephant of Pragjyotisha ran at twice its pace, king. With blood
spurting from it, it was a sight to see in battle, like a great cloud threaded
through with the rays of the sun. Urged on by Bhagadatta, streaming with rut,
the elephant ran at them all like Death let loose by Time. It went at twice its
speed, making the earth shake under its feet. Seeing its great form, all the
great chariot fighters thought it unbearable and lost heart.

"Then the king in anger struck Bhimasena between the breasts, tiger among men,
with an arrow with bent joints. Badly wounded by that king, the great archer,
the great chariot fighter, his limbs overcome by a faint, leaned on his
flagstaff. Seeing them frightened and Bhimasena fainting, the mighty Bhagadatta
roared a strong roar.

"Then, king, seeing Bhima in that state, the terrible rakshasa Ghatotkacha in
anger vanished right there. He made a cruel illusion that increased the fear of
the timid, and in half a blink he was seen again in a terrible form. He rode
Airavata, made by himself out of illusion. Other elephants of the quarters
followed it, Anjana, Vamana and the bright Mahapadma. These three great
elephants were ridden by rakshasas. They were huge, king, rut streaming from
them in three places, full of power, strength and courage. Then Ghatotkacha,
scorcher of enemies, urged his own elephant on, wanting to kill Bhagadatta with
his elephant. The other elephants, urged on by those very strong rakshasas, fell
on him from the four quarters, enraged, with four tusks each. They wounded
Bhagadatta's elephant with their tusks. Pressed by those elephants, in pain and
hurt by arrows, it gave a great roar like the sound of Indra's thunderbolt.

"Hearing that very terrible, fearful roar, Bhishma said to Drona and to King
Suyodhana, 'The great archer Bhagadatta is fighting in battle with the
evil-hearted son of Hidimba, and is in trouble. The rakshasa is a great master
of illusion, and the king is very quick to anger. The two have met, both very
strong, like Time and Death. I can hear the great roar of the glad Pandavas, and
the very great noise of an elephant screaming in fear. Let us go there, bless
you, to protect the king. Left unprotected in battle, he will soon give up his
life. Hurry, you men of great strength. Why are we slow to go? A great and
fierce battle is going on that makes the hair stand on end. He is devoted to us,
of good family and brave, a commander of an army. It is right, you who do not
fall, for us to protect him.'

"Hearing Bhishma's words, all the kings together, led by Bharadvaja's son,
wanting to save Bhagadatta, went at top speed to where he was. Seeing them go,
the Panchalas with the Pandavas, led by Yudhishthira, followed behind the enemy.
Seeing those ranks, the mighty lord of the rakshasas roared a very great roar
like the crash of a thunderbolt. Hearing that roar and seeing the elephants
fighting, Bhishma son of Shantanu spoke again to Bharadvaja's son. 'I do not
like this battle with the evil-hearted son of Hidimba. He is filled with
strength and courage now, and he has allies. Not even the wielder of the
thunderbolt himself could defeat him in battle. He has found his target and
strikes hard, while our mounts are tired. We are wounded all over from a whole
day with the Panchalas and the Pandavas. So I do not like a battle with the
Pandavas now that they are triumphant. Let the withdrawal be announced today. We
will fight the enemy tomorrow.'

"Hearing the grandfather's words, the Kauravas did so. Tormented by fear of
Ghatotkacha, they withdrew by a stratagem. When the Kauravas had turned back,
the triumphant Pandavas roared lion roars with the sounds of conches and flutes.
So the battle went that day, bull of the Bharatas, between the Pandavas and the
Kurus, with Ghatotkacha in front. Then, king, the Kauravas went to their own
camp at nightfall, ashamed, beaten by the Pandavas. The sons of Pandu, the great
chariot fighters, their bodies wounded by arrows, went to the camp happy with
the battle, great king. They had Bhimasena and Ghatotkacha in front and honoured
one another, filled with the greatest joy. They roared roars of many kinds,
mixed with the sound of trumpets. They made lion roars mixed with the sound of
conches. The great-souled scorchers of enemies roared and made the earth shake,
striking at your son's vitals, sir, and went to the camp at nightfall.

"But King Duryodhana, wretched at the killing of his brothers, brooded for a
while, overcome with tears and grief. Then, having made all the arrangements for
the camp by the rule, he sank into thought, burning with grief, worn down by the
disaster to his brothers."

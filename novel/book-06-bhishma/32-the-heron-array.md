# The Heron Array

"The troops had withdrawn on the first day, bull of the Bharatas," said Sanjaya.
"Bhishma was furious in battle and Duryodhana glad. The King of Dharma went
quickly to Janardana with all his brothers and all the lords of men. Filled with
the greatest grief, thinking of defeat, he spoke to the Varshneya, king, having
seen Bhishma's courage.

"'Krishna, look at the great archer Bhishma of terrible courage, burning my army
with arrows as fire burns dry grass in summer. How shall we be able even to look
at this great man, licking up my army like fire fed with butter? When they see
this tiger among men, mighty, with his bow, my army runs, struck by his arrows.
Yama in anger could be conquered in battle, or the wielder of the thunderbolt,
or Varuna with his noose, or Kubera with his club. But Bhishma, of great
splendour and great strength, cannot be conquered. Since things are so, I am
sinking in the deep water of Bhishma without a boat. Because of the weakness of
my own understanding, Keshava, having met Bhishma, I will go to the forest,
Govinda. It is better for me to live there than to give these lords of the earth
to Bhishma, to death. Bhishma, master of great weapons, will destroy my army,
Krishna. As moths rush into a blazing fire only to be destroyed, so do my
soldiers.

"'I have been brought to ruin, Varshneya, by my courage, for the sake of a
kingdom. My brave brothers too are worn out and tormented by arrows. For my
sake, out of brotherly love, they have fallen from their kingdom and from
happiness. I value life highly, for life is hard to come by today. With the rest
of my life I will practise hard austerity. I will not have these friends killed
in battle, Keshava. Mighty Bhishma kills my many thousands of chariots, of the
best fighters, ceaselessly with heavenly weapons. What should I do so that it is
done right for me? Tell me quickly, Madhava. I see the left-handed archer in the
battle as if he were neutral. Only strong-armed Bhima fights with all his power,
remembering the dharma of a kshatriya, with the strength of his arms alone. With
his club that kills heroes the great man does very hard deeds, as his spirit
moves him, among elephants, horses, chariots and foot soldiers. But he cannot
destroy the enemy army, sir, by honest fighting alone, hero, even in a hundred
years. Only this friend of yours knows weapons, and he too looks on while we are
burned by Bhishma and great Drona. The heavenly weapons of Bhishma and of great
Drona, used again and again, will burn up all the kshatriyas. Krishna, Bhishma
in his fury, with all the kings, will surely destroy us, such is his courage. So
look, master of yoga, for a great archer, a great chariot fighter, who could
quench Bhishma in battle as a rain cloud quenches a forest fire. By your grace,
Govinda, the Pandavas will kill their enemies, regain their own kingdom and
rejoice with their kin.'

"When he had said this, the great-minded Partha sat brooding for a long time,
his mind turned inward, his thought struck by grief. Seeing the Pandava
tormented by grief, his mind struck by sorrow, Govinda spoke there, cheering all
the Pandavas.

"'Do not grieve, best of the Bharatas. You should not grieve, you whose brothers
are heroes and archers of the whole world. I too do what is dear to you, king,
and so does the great chariot fighter Satyaki, the old men Virata and Drupada,
and Dhrishtadyumna Prishata's grandson. So too all the kings with their armies,
best of kings, wait for your favour and are devoted to you, lord of the people.
This Prishata's grandson, mighty Dhrishtadyumna, always wanting your good and
devoted to what pleases you, has taken command of the army. And Shikhandi,
strong-armed one, is Bhishma's death, they say.'

"When he heard this, the king spoke to the great chariot fighter Dhrishtadyumna
in that assembly, while Vasudeva listened. 'Dhrishtadyumna, hear what I will say
to you, sir. And let the words I speak not be passed over. You are my general,
approved by Vasudeva. As Karttikeya was always the general of the gods long ago,
so you too are the general of the Pandus, bull among men. So, tiger among men,
show your courage and kill the Kauravas. I will follow you, and Bhima and
Krishna, sir, and Madri's two sons together, and the sons of Draupadi in armour,
and the other chief lords of the earth, bull among men.'

"Then Dhrishtadyumna, cheering them all, said, 'I was ordained long ago by
Shambhu to be Drona's end, Partha. In battle today I will fight Bhishma, Drona,
Kripa, Shalya, Jayadratha and all the proud ones, lord of the earth.' Then the
great archers among the Pandavas, fierce in war, shouted aloud when Prishata's
grandson, the slayer of enemies, the lord of kings, stood ready.

"Then Partha spoke to Prishata's grandson, the commander of the army. 'There is
an array called Kraunchaaruna, the heron, which destroys all enemies. Brihaspati
told it to Indra long ago in the war of the gods and asuras. Draw it up
properly, the destroyer of enemy ranks. Let the kings see it with the Kurus,
never seen before.' Spoken to like this by the god among men, as Vishnu by the
wielder of the thunderbolt, he set Dhananjaya at the front of all the troops at
dawn.

"His banner went along the path of the sun, wonderful and lovely, made by
Vishvakarman at the command of Puruhuta. Adorned with flags the colour of the
rainbow, like a thing that moves in the sky, like a city of the gandharvas, it
seemed to dance as the chariot moved, sir. With that jewelled banner, and the
banner with the bearer of Gandiva, Partha was finely equipped, like the
Self-born with the sun.

"King Drupada, surrounded by a great army, was the head. Kuntibhoja and the
Chaidya were the eyes, lord of the people. The Dasharnakas, the Prayagas with
the hosts of the Dasherakas, the Anupagas and the Kiratas were the neck, bull of
the Bharatas. Yudhishthira with the Patachcharas, the Hundas, the Pauravakas and
the Nishadas was the back, king. Bhimasena and Dhrishtadyumna Prishata's
grandson were the wings, with the sons of Draupadi, Abhimanyu and the great
chariot fighter Satyaki. The Pishachas, Daradas, Pundras with the Kundivishas,
the Madakas, Ladakas, Tanganas and Paratanganas, the Bahlikas, Tittiras, Cholas
and Pandyas, Bharata: these peoples, king, took the right wing. The Agniveshyas,
Jagattundas and Paladashas, Bharata, the Shabaras, Tumbupas and Vatsas with the
Nakulas, Nakula and Sahadeva, took the left side.

"There were ten thousand chariots in the wings, a hundred thousand in the head,
a hundred million and twenty thousand in the back, and a hundred thousand and
seventy thousand in the neck. At the tips of the wings, the extremes of the
wings and the ends of the wings went elephants, surrounded, king, like moving
mountains. Virata with the Kekayas guarded the rear, and the king of Kashi and
Shaibya with thirty thousand chariots. Having drawn up this great array like
this, Bharata, the Pandavas stood in armour, ready for battle, waiting for the
sunrise. Their white umbrellas, bright as the sun, clear and large, shone on the
elephants and chariots.

---

"Your son saw the great array, the heron, unbreakable and very terrible, drawn
up by Partha of boundless splendour. He went to the teacher, to Kripa, Shalya,
Somadatta's son, Vikarna and Ashvatthama, sir. He went to all his brothers led
by Duhshasana, Bharata, and to many other heroes who had come to fight. Your son
spoke this at the right time, cheering them. 'All of you know weapons, with
weapons of many kinds. Each of you great chariot fighters alone is able to kill
Pandu's sons in battle with their armies, let alone together. That force of
ours, guarded by Bhishma, is not enough. But this force of theirs, best of
kings, is enough. Let the Samsthanas, the Shurasenas, the Venikas, the Kukuras,
the Arevakas, the Trigartas, the Madrakas and the Yavanas guard Bhishma alone.
Let them guard him with Shatrumjaya and Duhshasana, with the hero Vikarna, with
Nanda and Upanandaka, with Chitrasena and with the Panibhadrakas, with their
armies in front.'

"Then Drona, Bhishma and your son, sir, drew up a great array to stop the
Pandus. Bhishma, surrounded on every side by a great army, went drawing along a
great host like the king of the gods. The great archer, mighty Bharadvaja's son,
followed him, lord of the people, with the Kuntalas, the Dasharnas and the
Magadhas. With the Vidarbhas, the Mekalas and the Karnapravaranas and all their
army he followed Bhishma, who shone in battle. The Gandharas, the
Sindhu-Sauviras, the Shibis and the Vasatis, and Shakuni with his own army,
guarded Bharadvaja's son. Then King Duryodhana with all his brothers guarded
Subala's son's army gladly. With him were the Ashvatakas and Vikarnas, the
Sharmilas and Koshalas, the Daradas, the Chuchupas and the Kshudraka-Malavas.
Bhurishravas, Shala, Shalya and Bhagadatta, sir, and Vinda and Anuvinda of
Avanti guarded the left side. Somadatta's son, Susharma, Sudakshina of Kamboja,
Shatayus and Shrutayus stood on the right side. Ashvatthama, Kripa and
Kritavarma the Satvata stood with a great army at the rear of the army. Its rear
guards were lords of men from many countries: Ketuman, Vasudana and the mighty
son of the king of Kashi.

"Then all your men, glad for the battle, Bharata, blew their conches joyfully
and roared lions' roars. Hearing them glad, the mighty grandfather, the eldest
of the Kurus, roared a lion's roar aloud and blew his conch. Then the conches,
drums and tabors of many kinds of the enemy, and kettledrums, were struck, and
the noise was tumultuous. Then, standing in their great chariot yoked with white
horses, the two blew their fine conches adorned with gold and jewels.
Hrishikesha blew Panchajanya, Dhananjaya Devadatta, and Vrikodara of terrible
deeds the great conch Paundra. King Yudhishthira, Kunti's son, blew
Anantavijaya, and Nakula and Sahadeva Sughosha and Manipushpaka. The king of
Kashi, Shaibya, the great chariot fighter Shikhandi, Dhrishtadyumna, Virata,
famous Satyaki, the great archer the Panchala and the five sons of Draupadi all
blew their great conches and roared lions' roars. That very great sound raised
by those heroes was tumultuous and made the sky and the earth echo. So the Kurus
and the Pandavas, overjoyed, came together again to fight, great king, scorching
one another."

# I Am Time

"'The highest secret, called the self, that you have told me in kindness to me,'
said Arjuna, 'has taken away my confusion. I have heard from you in full of the
coming into being and passing away of beings, lotus-eyed one, and of your
undying greatness. It is as you say of yourself, highest lord. Now I want to see
your lordly form, highest Person. If you think I can see it, lord, master of
yoga, then show me your undying self.'

"'See my forms, Partha,' said the Blessed One, 'by the hundred and the thousand,
of many kinds, divine, of many colours and shapes. See the Adityas, the Vasus,
the Rudras, the two Ashvins and the Maruts. See many wonders never seen before,
Bharata. See here today the whole world in one place, moving and unmoving, in my
body, Gudakesha, and whatever else you want to see. But you cannot see me with
this eye of yours. I give you a divine eye. See my lordly yoga.'

"When he had said this, king," said Sanjaya, "Hari, the great lord of yoga,
showed Partha his highest lordly form. It had many mouths and eyes, many
wonderful sights, many divine ornaments and many divine weapons raised. He wore
divine garlands and clothes and was anointed with divine perfumes, made of every
wonder, the god, endless, facing every way. If the light of a thousand suns were
to rise at once in the sky, it might be like the light of that great one. There
the Pandava saw the whole world in one place, divided in many ways, in the body
of the god of gods. Then Dhananjaya, filled with wonder, his hair standing on
end, bowed his head to the god, joined his hands, and spoke.

"'I see the gods in your body, god, and all the hosts of different beings,
Brahma the lord seated on the lotus, and all the seers and the divine serpents.
I see you with many arms, bellies, mouths and eyes, endless in form on every
side. I see no end, no middle and no beginning of you, lord of all, whose form
is all. I see you with crown, club and discus, a mass of light shining
everywhere, hard to look at on every side, with the brightness of blazing fire
and sun, beyond measure. You are the imperishable, the highest to be known. You
are the highest resting place of this world. You are the undying guardian of
eternal dharma. I think you are the eternal Person.

"'I see you without beginning, middle or end, of endless strength, with endless
arms, the moon and sun your eyes, your mouth a blazing fire, burning this world
with your splendour. This space between heaven and earth, and all the quarters,
are filled by you alone. Seeing this wonderful, terrible form of yours, great
one, the three worlds tremble. These hosts of gods enter you. Some, afraid,
praise you with joined hands. Hosts of great seers and perfected ones say, "May
it be well!" and praise you with rich hymns. The Rudras, Adityas, Vasus and
Sadhyas, the Vishvedevas, the two Ashvins, the Maruts and the ancestors, hosts
of gandharvas, yakshas, asuras and perfected ones all gaze at you in wonder.

"'Seeing your great form, with many mouths and eyes, strong-armed one, with many
arms, thighs and feet, many bellies, terrible with many fangs, the worlds
tremble, and so do I. Seeing you touching the sky, blazing, of many colours,
with mouths gaping and huge blazing eyes, my inner self trembles, and I find no
steadiness or calm, Vishnu. Seeing your mouths, terrible with fangs, like the
fire of time, I do not know the quarters and find no shelter. Be gracious, lord
of gods, home of the world.

"'All these sons of Dhritarashtra, with the hosts of kings, Bhishma, Drona, and
that son of a suta, with our chief fighters too, rush into your mouths, terrible
with fangs, fearful. Some are seen caught between your teeth, their heads
crushed. As the many torrents of rivers run towards the ocean, so these heroes
of the world of men enter your blazing mouths. As moths rush into a blazing fire
to their destruction, so the worlds rush into your mouths to their destruction.
You lick your lips, swallowing all the worlds on every side with your blazing
mouths. Filling the whole world with splendour, your fierce rays burn, Vishnu.
Tell me who you are, of such fierce form. Homage to you, best of gods. Be
gracious. I want to know you, the first, for I do not understand what you are
doing.'

"'I am time,' said the Blessed One, 'grown old, the destroyer of the worlds, set
to gather in the worlds here. Even without you, all these fighters standing in
the opposing ranks will cease to be. So stand up and win glory. Conquer your
enemies and enjoy a rich kingdom. They have been killed by me already. Be only
the instrument, left-handed archer. Kill Drona, Bhishma, Jayadratha, Karna and
the other heroic fighters, who have been killed by me. Do not tremble. Fight.
You will conquer your rivals in battle.'

"When he heard these words of Keshava, the diademed one joined his hands,
trembling, bowed, and spoke again to Krishna, stammering, terrified, bowing low.

"'It is right, Hrishikesha, that the world rejoices and delights in your praise.
The rakshasas run in fear to the quarters, and all the hosts of the perfected
bow down. And why should they not bow to you, great one, greater even than
Brahma, the first maker? Endless one, lord of gods, home of the world, you are
the imperishable, being and non-being, and what is beyond. You are the first
god, the ancient Person. You are the highest resting place of this world. You
are the knower and what is to be known, and the highest home. The world is
pervaded by you, whose form is endless. You are Vayu, Yama, Agni, Varuna, the
moon, Prajapati and the great-grandfather. Homage, homage to you a thousand
times, and again, homage, homage to you. Homage to you in front and behind,
homage to you on every side, you who are all. Of endless strength and boundless
courage, you fill all, and so you are all.

"'Whatever I said rashly, thinking you my friend, "Krishna! Yadava! Friend!",
not knowing this greatness of yours, through carelessness or affection, and
whatever disrespect I showed you in jest, at play, in bed, sitting or eating,
alone or in front of others, Achyuta, I ask your forgiveness for it, you who are
beyond measure. You are the father of the world, moving and unmoving, its
worshipful and weightiest teacher. There is no one equal to you. How could there
be one greater, in all three worlds, you of matchless power? So I bow, lay my
body down, and ask your grace, lord worthy of praise. As a father bears with a
son, a friend with a friend, a lover with a beloved, so should you bear with me,
god. I am glad to have seen what was never seen before, and my mind trembles
with fear. Show me that form of yours, god. Be gracious, lord of gods, home of
the world. I want to see you as before, with crown, club and discus in hand.
Take that four-armed form, thousand-armed one, whose body is all.'

"'In my grace, Arjuna,' said the Blessed One, 'I have shown you by my own yoga
this highest form, made of splendour, the all, endless, first, which no one but
you has seen before. Not by the Vedas, sacrifices or study, not by gifts, rites
or fierce austerities, can I be seen in this form in the world of men by anyone
but you, hero of the Kurus. Do not tremble, do not be confused, now that you
have seen this terrible form of mine. Free of fear and glad at heart, see again
this form of mine.'"

"When he had said this to Arjuna," said Sanjaya, "Vasudeva showed his own form
again. The great one, taking again his gentle body, comforted him in his fear."

"'Seeing this gentle human form of yours, Janardana,' said Arjuna, 'I have come
to myself now, and returned to my nature.'

"'You have seen this form of mine, which is very hard to see,' said the Blessed
One. 'Even the gods always long to see this form. Not by the Vedas, not by
austerity, not by gifts and not by sacrifice can I be seen as you have seen me.
But by devotion to me alone, Arjuna, I can be known and seen like this in truth,
and entered, scorcher of enemies. One who does my work, who is intent on me,
devoted to me, free of attachment, without enmity towards any being, comes to
me, Pandava.'"

summary: Chapters 13 to 15 of the Bhagavad Gita, told simply, with footnotes. Krishna teaches that the body is the field and the self is the one who knows it, lists what makes up the field and what true knowledge is, and describes Brahman, which is everywhere and beyond everything; he explains nature and the Person, and how the three strands of nature, goodness, passion and darkness, bind the self, what each one produces, and how a person rises above them; he describes the world as an upside-down tree with its roots above, to be cut down with the axe of detachment, and names himself the highest Person, beyond the perishable and the imperishable.
---

# The Field and the Strands

"'This body, Arjuna, is called the field,' said Krishna. 'The one who knows it
is called the knower of the field by those who understand these
things.[^b6c12-1] And know that I am the knower of the field in all fields,
Arjuna. I think that real knowledge is knowing the field and the knower of the
field.

"'Hear from me briefly what that field is, what it is like, how it changes and
where it comes from, and who the knower is and what his power is. Seers have
sung of this in many ways, in many kinds of verse, and in the carefully reasoned
words of the sayings about Brahman.

"'The field is made of these. There are the great elements, the sense of "I",
understanding and the unseen. There are the ten senses and the one mind, and the
five fields of the senses. There are wanting, hating, pleasure, pain, the body
as a whole, awareness and steadiness. This, in brief, is the field, with its
changes.[^b6c12-2]

"'Now hear what knowledge is. It is not being proud, not being a show-off, not
harming, patience and honesty. It is serving your teacher, being clean, being
steady and controlling yourself. It is not caring for the things of the senses,
having no sense of "I", and seeing clearly the pain and evil of birth, death,
old age and sickness. It is not clinging, not being wrapped up in children,
wife, home and the rest, and staying always even-minded in good times and bad.
It is steady love for me, with a yoga that turns to nothing else. It is liking
lonely places and not enjoying crowds. It is always holding on to knowledge of
the self, and seeing what the knowledge of truth is for. This is called
knowledge.[^b6c12-3] Everything else is ignorance.

"'I will tell you what is to be known. When a person knows it, he tastes
deathlessness. It is the highest Brahman, which has no beginning. It is not
called being, and it is not called not-being.[^b6c12-4] It has hands and feet
everywhere, eyes, heads and faces everywhere, ears everywhere. It stands
covering everything in the world. It shines through the working of all the
senses, yet it has no senses. It clings to nothing, yet it holds everything up.
It has no strands of nature, yet it enjoys the strands. It is outside beings and
inside them. It does not move, and yet it moves. It is too fine to be known. It
is far away, and it is near. It is undivided, yet it seems to stand divided
among beings. Know that it supports beings. It swallows them up, and it brings
them forth. It is the light of lights, and is said to be beyond darkness. It is
knowledge, what is to be known, and what is reached by knowledge. It is seated
in the heart of everyone.

"'So I have told you briefly about the field, about knowledge and about what is
to be known. When a person who loves me understands this, he comes to my being.

"'Know that nature and the Person both have no beginning.[^b6c12-5] Know that
all changes, and the strands, come from nature. Nature is said to be the cause
of action, of the means of action and of the one who acts. The Person is said to
be the cause of the experience of pleasure and pain. For the Person, living in
nature, experiences the strands that come from nature. His clinging to the
strands is the reason for his births in good and bad wombs. The highest Person
in this body is also called the witness, the one who consents, the supporter,
the one who experiences, the great Lord, and the highest self. The one who knows
the Person, and nature with its strands, in this way is not born again, however
he lives.

"'Some see the self in the self, by the self, through meditation. Others see it
through Sankhya, and others through the yoga of action. Others again, who do not
know this, hear it from others and worship. They too cross over death, because
they hold to what they have heard.

"'Know, Arjuna, that whatever being is born, moving or still, comes from the
joining of the field and the knower of the field. The one who sees the highest
Lord standing the same in all beings, never perishing when they perish, truly
sees. For when he sees the Lord standing the same everywhere, he does not hurt
the self by the self, and so he goes to the highest goal.

"'The one who sees that all actions are done only by nature, and that the self
is not the doer, truly sees. When he sees that the different beings all rest in
the One, and spread out from it alone, then he reaches Brahman.

"'Because it has no beginning and no strands, this highest self never changes,
Arjuna. Though it lives in the body, it does not act, and it is not stained.
Space is everywhere, and because it is so fine it is never stained. In the same
way the self, which is present everywhere in the body, is never stained. The one
sun lights up this whole world. In the same way, Arjuna, the owner of the field
lights up the whole field.[^b6c12-6] Those who, with the eye of knowledge, see
the difference between the field and the knower of the field, and see how beings
are set free from nature, go to the highest.'

"'I will tell you again the highest knowledge,' said Krishna, 'the best of all
knowledge. By knowing it, all the sages have gone from here to the highest
perfection. Those who take shelter in this knowledge become like me. They are
not born when the world is created, and they are not troubled when it is
destroyed.

"'Great Brahman is my womb, Arjuna. In it I place the seed, and from it all
beings are born. Whatever forms are born in any womb, great Brahman is their
womb, and I am the father who gives the seed.

"'Goodness, passion and darkness: these strands, which come from nature, bind
the unchanging self in the body, strong-armed one.[^b6c12-7] Of these, goodness
is clean, and so it gives light and is free from sickness. It binds by
attachment to happiness and attachment to knowledge, blameless one. Know that
passion is made of longing and comes from thirst and attachment. It binds the
self in the body by attachment to action, Arjuna. But know that darkness is born
of ignorance and confuses everyone who has a body. It binds by carelessness,
laziness and sleep, Arjuna.

"'Goodness ties you to happiness, passion to action, Arjuna, and darkness covers
up knowledge and ties you to carelessness. Sometimes goodness rises up, pushing
down passion and darkness. Sometimes passion rises, pushing down goodness and
darkness. And sometimes darkness rises, pushing down goodness and passion.

"'When light and knowledge shine through all the gates of the body, know that
goodness has grown. When passion grows, greed, restlessness, starting new
things, unease and longing appear, Arjuna.[^b6c12-8] When darkness grows, gloom,
inaction, carelessness and confusion appear, joy of the Kurus.

"'If a person dies when goodness is strong, he goes to the pure worlds of those
who know the highest. If he dies in passion, he is born among people attached to
action. If he dies in darkness, he is born into foolish wombs. They say the
fruit of good action is pure and good. The fruit of passion is pain, and the
fruit of darkness is ignorance. Knowledge comes from goodness, greed from
passion, and carelessness, confusion and ignorance from darkness. Those who
stand in goodness go upwards. The passionate stay in the middle. The dark,
living in the lowest strand, go downwards.

"'When the one who sees sees no doer other than the strands, and knows what is
beyond the strands, he comes to my being. When the self in the body goes beyond
these three strands, from which the body comes, it is freed from birth, death,
old age and sorrow, and tastes deathlessness.'

"'Lord,' said Arjuna, 'by what signs is a person known who has gone beyond these
three strands? How does he live? And how does he go beyond the three strands?'

"'Arjuna,' said Krishna, 'he does not hate light, activity or confusion when
they come, and he does not long for them when they go. He sits as if he were not
involved, and the strands do not shake him. Thinking, "It is only the strands at
work," he stays steady and does not waver. He is the same in pain and pleasure.
He is at home in himself. A lump of earth, a stone and gold are all the same to
him. He treats the pleasant and the unpleasant alike, and he is steady. He takes
blame and praise of himself alike, honour and dishonour alike, and he is the
same to friend and foe. He has given up all selfish undertakings. He is said to
have gone beyond the strands. And the one who serves me with the yoga of love
that never wavers goes beyond these strands and is fit to become Brahman. For I
am the ground of Brahman, of deathlessness that never changes, of eternal dharma
and of perfect happiness.'

"'They speak of an eternal ashvattha tree,' said Krishna, 'with its roots above
and its branches below. Its leaves are the Vedic hymns. The one who knows it
knows the Veda.[^b6c12-9] Its branches spread below and above, fed by the
strands, with the things of the senses for their buds. And below, its roots
stretch out, binding people to action in the world of men. Its form cannot be
seen here as it is, nor its end, nor its beginning, nor where it stands. This
ashvattha has deep, strong roots. Cut it down with the strong axe of not
clinging.[^b6c12-10] Then search for that place from which those who go there
never return, saying, "I take refuge in that first Person from whom the ancient
stream of the world flowed out." Free from pride and confusion, having conquered
the fault of clinging, always fixed on the self, with their wants turned away,
freed from the pairs called pleasure and pain, the undeluded go to that
unchanging place. The sun does not light it, nor the moon, nor fire. Those who
go there do not come back. That is my highest home.

"'A part of me, eternal, becomes the living self in the world of the living. It
draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, which rest in nature.
When the lord takes a body, and when he leaves it, he takes these with him. He
goes as the wind carries scents from their place.[^b6c12-11] Through the ear,
the eye, touch, taste and smell, and through the mind, he enjoys the things of
the senses. When he leaves the body, or stays in it, or enjoys, joined to the
strands, the deluded do not see him. Those with the eye of knowledge see him.
Yogis who strive see him standing in themselves. But the thoughtless, who have
not made themselves ready, do not see him, even though they strive.

"'The light in the sun that lights up the whole world, the light in the moon and
the light in fire: know that light is mine. I enter the earth and hold up all
beings with my strength. I become Soma, full of sap, and feed all plants. I
become the fire of life in the bodies of living beings. Joined to the breaths
going out and coming in, I digest the four kinds of food.[^b6c12-12] I am seated
in the heart of everyone. Memory, knowledge and the loss of them come from me. I
am what is to be known through all the Vedas. I am the maker of the end of the
Veda, and the knower of the Veda.

"'There are two Persons in the world, the perishable and the imperishable. The
perishable is all beings. The one that stands unchanging is called the
imperishable. But there is another, the highest Person, called the highest self,
the unchanging Lord who enters the three worlds and holds them up. I am beyond
the perishable, and higher even than the imperishable. So I am known in the
world and in the Veda as the highest Person.[^b6c12-13] The one who is not
deluded, and knows me as the highest Person, knows everything, and loves me with
his whole being, Arjuna. So, blameless one, I have told you this most secret
teaching. By understanding it, Arjuna, a person becomes wise, and has done all
he needs to do.'"

## Explanations

[^b6c12-1]: **The field and the knower of the field.** *What it means:* the body
    (with the mind) is like a field where things grow and happen. The self is
    the one who *knows* the field, the way a farmer knows his field but is not
    the field. *Everyday example:* you can watch your own anger rise. The anger
    is in the "field"; the one watching it is the "knower".

[^b6c12-2]: **What the field is made of.** *What it means:* this is the list of
    an old school of thought called Sankhya: the five great elements (earth,
    water, fire, air, space), the sense of "I", understanding, the unseen source
    of nature, the ten senses (five for knowing: sight, hearing, touch, taste,
    smell; five for acting: speech, hands, feet, and two for the body's work),
    the mind, and the five kinds of things the senses meet. Krishna adds
    feelings like wanting and hating. All of these belong to the field, not to
    the self.

[^b6c12-3]: **What knowledge really is.** *What it means:* for Krishna, real
    knowledge is not just information. It is a way of being: humble, harmless,
    patient, honest, clean, steady, not clinging. *Everyday example:* someone
    who knows a lot about health but still smokes has information. Someone who
    lives healthily has knowledge in Krishna's sense.

[^b6c12-4]: **"Not being, not not-being."** *What it means:* Brahman is so
    different from ordinary things that neither word fits it. It is not a thing
    that exists the way a table exists, and yet it is not nothing. The verses
    that follow describe it with opposites: far and near, moving and still,
    inside and outside.

[^b6c12-5]: **Nature and the Person (*prakṛti* and *puruṣa*).** *What they
    mean:* *nature* is everything that changes: body, mind, the world. *The
    Person* is the conscious self that experiences it. Nature does the acting.
    The Person watches and feels. Trouble comes when the Person clings to nature
    and mistakes it for himself.

[^b6c12-6]: **The sun lighting the world.** *What it means:* one sun lights up
    everything without being changed by what it lights. The self is like that:
    it makes the whole body and mind "light up" with awareness, but it is not
    stained by what happens in them.

[^b6c12-7]: **The three strands in detail.** *What they mean:* *goodness*
    (*sattva*) is clear, calm and bright. It brings knowledge and happiness, but
    even it can tie you, by attachment to feeling good. *Passion* (*rajas*) is
    restless wanting and doing. It ties you to action and its rewards.
    *Darkness* (*tamas*) is dullness, laziness and confusion. It ties you
    through carelessness and sleep. *Everyday example:* a morning when you wake
    up clear and kind (goodness); a day of rushing and grabbing (passion); a day
    of lying in bed and not caring (darkness). Everyone has all three, in
    different amounts at different times.

[^b6c12-8]: **Passion and greed.** *What it means:* when passion grows, it shows
    as greed, restlessness and always starting new things. *In the story:* when
    Dhritarashtra asked about the land of Bharata "which my son Duryodhana
    covets beyond all measure", Sanjaya answered, "The Pandavas are not the ones
    who covet it … It is Duryodhana who covets it, and Shakuni" (CE 6.10.1–3).

[^b6c12-9]: **The upside-down tree.** *What it means:* the world is pictured as
    a great ashvattha (sacred fig) tree growing upside down. Its root is above,
    in God. Its branches spread down into our world, fed by the three strands,
    with sense pleasures as buds. Its leaves are the Vedic hymns. *In the
    story:* in the last chapter Krishna said, "Among all trees I am the
    ashvattha" (CE 6.32.26).

[^b6c12-10]: **The axe of not clinging.** *What it means:* the tree of worldly
    life has deep roots: our wants and habits. It cannot be pulled up gently. It
    has to be cut, with the "axe" of detachment. *Everyday example:* someone who
    wants to give up a bad habit often has to cut it off firmly, not just hope
    it fades.

[^b6c12-11]: **Like the wind carrying scents.** *What it means:* when the self
    leaves one body for another, it carries the senses and mind with it, as the
    wind carries the smell of flowers from one place to another. This is how
    Krishna describes rebirth.

[^b6c12-12]: **The fire of digestion.** *What it means:* *vaiśvānara*, "the fire
    in all men", is the inner fire of the body that digests food. The "four
    kinds of food" were traditionally food that is chewed, swallowed, licked and
    sucked. Krishna says he is present even in this everyday work of the body.

[^b6c12-13]: **The highest Person (*puruṣottama*).** *What it means:* Krishna
    names three levels: the *perishable* (all changing beings), the
    *imperishable* (the unchanging self), and above both, the *highest Person*,
    God himself, who holds up the three worlds. *Puruṣottama*, "best of
    persons", is the title Arjuna has been using for Krishna.

**Divergences.**

* This chapter is Gita chapters 13, 14 and 15.
* **A verse not in the CE.** Many printed Gitas open chapter 13 with Arjuna asking about "nature and the Person, the field and the knower of the field, knowledge and what is to be known". That verse is a rejected star passage (\*108), and the matching line of Krishna's reply is \*109. In the CE the chapter begins with Krishna's "This body is called the field" (6.35.1). This is why some editions count 701 verses, and the CE 700. See ledger L-191.

summary: The afternoon of the ninth day. Bhishma crushes the Pandava army like cattle trampling cut grain, and a river of blood carries off the timid. The soldiers curse Duryodhana, who only tells his elders to fight harder. Arjuna routs Susharma's men, and Duryodhana brings the whole army against him. Bhishma shatters Satyaki's spear, and a hundred thousand of Shakuni's horsemen are cut down by Yudhishthira and the twins. In the evening Bhishma burns through the Pandava ranks like fire, and fourteen thousand chariot fighters of the Chedis, Kashis and Karushas go under. Krishna reminds Arjuna of his vow, and Arjuna, unwilling, goes against Bhishma, but fights gently. Krishna leaps down, whip in hand, and runs at Bhishma, who welcomes death at his hands. Arjuna catches him by the legs at the tenth step and begs him not to break his word that he would not fight. Krishna remounts in silence. At sunset Bhishma is still unbeaten, and the tired armies withdraw.

# Krishna Runs at Bhishma

"At midday, great king," said Sanjaya, "a cruel battle began between Bhishma and
the Somakas, destroying the world. Ganga's son, best of chariot fighters, blew
away the Pandava army with sharp arrows by the hundred and the thousand. Your
father Devavrata crushed that army as herds of cattle trample a heap of cut
grain.

"Dhrishtadyumna, Shikhandi, Virata and Drupada came up to Bhishma in the battle
and struck the great chariot fighter with arrows. Bhishma pierced
Dhrishtadyumna, then Virata with three arrows, and sent an iron arrow at
Drupada, Bharata. Pierced by Bhishma, destroyer of enemies, the great archers
grew angry in the battle, king, like snakes trodden on. Shikhandi pierced the
grandfather of the Bharatas, but the unfallen man, thinking of him in his mind
as a woman, did not strike back. Dhrishtadyumna, blazing with anger in the
battle like fire, planted three arrows in the grandfather's arms and chest.
Drupada pierced Bhishma with twenty-five arrows, Virata with ten and Shikhandi
with twenty-five. Badly wounded by those great men in the battle, great king,
Bhishma looked like a red ashoka tree spotted with flowers in spring. Ganga's
son pierced each of them back with three straight arrows, and cut Drupada's bow
with a broad arrow, sir. Drupada took another bow and pierced Bhishma with five
arrows at the head of the battle, and his charioteer with three very sharp ones.

"Then, great king, Bhima, the five sons of Draupadi, the five Kekaya brothers
and Satyaki the Satvata charged Ganga's son in the battle, wanting
Yudhishthira's good and wanting to protect the Panchalas led by Dhrishtadyumna.
In the same way all your men, lord of men, went out with their armies against
the Pandava army, set on guarding Bhishma. There was a very great battle there,
a confused one, yours and theirs, of men, horses, chariots and elephants,
filling Yama's kingdom.

"Chariot fighter met chariot fighter and sent him to Yama's house. So too others
met men, elephant riders and horsemen and sent them to the other world with
straight arrows and with terrible weapons of many kinds, here and there, lord of
the people. Chariots without their fighters, their charioteers killed and their
horses running, went in every direction in the battle. They crushed many men and
horses, king, and were seen moving like the wind, like the cities of the
gandharvas in the sky. There were chariot fighters without their chariots, in
armour, full of fire, with earrings and turbans, adorned with gold ornaments and
armlets. They were like the sons of gods in beauty and like Shakra in courage in
battle, beyond Vaishravana in wealth and Brihaspati in policy. Those heroes,
lords of the whole world, were seen running here and there, lord of the people,
like common men.

"Elephants without their fine riders, best of men, crushed their own ranks and
ran towards every sound. With their armour, yak-tails, umbrellas and flags, with
their girths, goads, bells and javelins broken, they were seen running in the
ten directions, like mountains or clouds, with a noise like rising
thunderclouds. In the same way we saw elephant riders without their elephants,
lord of the people, running in the confusion, yours and theirs. I saw horses
from many lands, adorned with gold, running like the wind by the hundred and the
thousand. We saw horsemen, their horses killed, sword in hand on every side,
running and being driven off in the battle. An elephant meeting a running
elephant in the great battle went on at speed, trampling foot soldiers and
horses. So too an elephant crushed chariots in the battle, king, and a chariot,
meeting a foot soldier and a horse, crushed horses and men in the battle, king.
So they crushed each other in many ways, king.

"As that cruel battle went on, full of great fear, a terrible river began to
flow, its waves blood and entrails. Its banks were heaped with bones, its weed
and grass were hair. Its lakes were chariots, its whirlpools arrows, its fish
horses, and it was hard to cross. It was strewn with heads for stones and
crowded with elephants for crocodiles. It was rich in the foam of armour and
turbans, with bows for islands and swords for tortoises. It was thick with flags
and banners for trees, carrying away its banks of mortal men, thronged with
hosts of flesh-eaters, filling Yama's kingdom. Many brave kshatriyas crossed
that river, king, on boats of horses, elephants and chariots, throwing off fear
in the great battle. It carried off the timid, overcome with faintness, as the
Vaitarani carries the dead to the city of the king of the dead.

"Seeing that great slaughter, the kshatriyas there cried out, 'Through
Duryodhana's wrongdoing the Kauravas are going to their destruction. How could
King Dhritarashtra's son, evil-hearted, deluded by greed, hate the virtuous sons
of Pandu?' Many kinds of such words were heard there, Bharata, praising the
Pandavas and very cruel to your sons. Hearing those words spoken by all the
warriors, your son Duryodhana, who has wronged the whole world, said to Bhishma,
Drona, Kripa and Shalya, Bharata, 'Fight without pride! Why are you so slow?'

"Then the battle of the Kurus and the Pandavas went on, king, that very terrible
slaughter brought about by the game of dice. You would not hold back your son
before, son of Vichitravirya, though great men tried to hold you back. See its
fruit, of this kind. For neither the sons of Pandu, king, with their armies and
their followers, nor the Kauravas, lord of the people, guard their lives in
battle. That is why this terrible destruction of people goes on, tiger among
men, by fate or by your bad policy, king.

"Arjuna, tiger among men, sent the kings led by Susharma to the house of the
king of the dead with sharp arrows. Susharma too pierced Partha with arrows in
the battle, and Vasudeva with seventy, and Partha again with nine. Holding them
off with a flood of arrows, Shakra's son, the great chariot fighter, sent
Susharma's warriors to Yama's house in the battle. Being killed by Partha like
Time at the end of an age, the great chariot fighters ran from the battle, king,
when fear came on them. Some left their horses, some their chariots, sir, and
others left their elephants and ran in the ten directions. Others, goading their
horses, elephants and chariots, ran from the battle in the greatest haste, lord
of the people. Foot soldiers too threw away their weapons in the great battle
and ran heedless this way and that, Bharata. Though Susharma of Trigarta and
other chief kings tried again and again to hold them back, they did not stand in
the battle.

"Seeing that army fleeing, your son Duryodhana put Bhishma in front, with all
the armies behind him. He charged Dhananjaya with the whole force, great king,
for the sake of the Trigarta lord's life, lord of the people. He alone stood in
the battle with all his brothers, scattering arrows of many kinds. The rest of
the men ran. In the same way the Pandavas, king, in their armour, with their
whole force, went for Phalguna's sake to where Bhishma stood. Though they knew
the terrible valour of the Gandiva bowman in battle, they went at Bhishma on
every side, their courage roused by shouts. Then the palm-bannered hero covered
the Pandava army with straight arrows in the battle. All the Kurus and the
Pandavas, mixed into one, fought, great king, when the sun was at midday.

"Satyaki pierced Kritavarma with five iron arrows and stood in the battle, the
hero, scattering arrows by the thousand. In the same way King Drupada pierced
Drona with sharp arrows, then again with seventy, and his charioteer with seven.
Bhimasena pierced King Bahlika, his great-grandfather, and roared a great roar
like a tiger in the forest. Arjuna's son, pierced by Chitrasena with many swift
arrows, pierced Chitrasena hard in the heart with three. Those two great men,
meeting in the battle, shone, king, like the very terrible Budha and
Shanaishchara in the sky. Killing his four horses and his charioteer with nine
arrows, Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, roared a mighty roar. The great
chariot fighter leapt quickly down from his chariot with its horses killed and
climbed quickly into Durmukha's chariot, lord of the people.

"Valiant Drona, in a hurry, pierced Drupada with straight arrows, and pierced
his charioteer too. Hard pressed at the head of the army, King Drupada withdrew
with his swift horses, remembering their old enmity. In a moment Bhimasena left
King Bahlika without horses, charioteer or chariot while the whole army watched.
In great alarm, in great danger, great king, Bahlika, best of men, leapt down
from his chariot and quickly climbed into the chariot of the great chariot
fighter Lakshmana.

"Satyaki, the great chariot fighter, held back Kritavarma and came at the
grandfather with arrows of many kinds, king. He pierced the Bharata with sixty
sharp arrows fledged with hair and seemed to dance on the floor of his chariot,
shaking his great bow. Then the grandfather hurled at him a great iron spear,
worked with gold, of great speed, beautiful as a naga maiden. As it came
suddenly at him, like death itself, well sharpened, the famous Varshneya struck
it aside with his speed. Without reaching the Varshneya, that most cruel spear
fell to the earth like a great meteor that has lost its light. Then, king, the
Varshneya took his own terrible spear and hurled it with force at the
grandfather's chariot. Driven by the force of the Varshneya's arm in the great
battle, it rushed at speed, as the night of Time rushes at a man. As it suddenly
came at him, Bharata, he cut it in two with two very sharp razor arrows, and it
scattered on the ground. Having cut the spear, Ganga's son, destroyer of
enemies, in anger, laughing, struck Satyaki in the chest with nine arrows. Then,
elder brother of Pandu, the Pandavas with chariots, elephants and horses
surrounded Bhishma in the battle to save Madhava. Then a tumultuous,
hair-raising battle began between the Pandavas and the Kurus in the fight, both
wanting victory.

"Duryodhana saw Bhishma angry in the battle, surrounded by the Pandavas like the
sun in the sky by clouds at the end of the hot season. Great king, he said to
Duhshasana, 'This brave great archer Bhishma, killer of enemies, has been
covered by the brave Pandavas on every side, bull of the Bharatas. You must
guard that great man, hero. If our grandfather Bhishma is guarded in battle, he
will kill the Panchalas with the Pandavas, set on it as they are. I think the
thing to do is to guard Bhishma. For this great archer Bhishma, our grandfather,
is our guardian. So surround the grandfather with the whole army and guard him
in the battle as he does his hard deed.'

"Told this in the battle, your son Duhshasana stood surrounding Bhishma, with a
great army round him. Then Subala's son came with a hundred thousand horses,
proud, swift and strong, with flags. Their riders held bright lances, spears and
javelins, trained men, the best of men, skilled in battle. They surrounded
Nakula, Sahadeva and the Dharma king, the Pandava, best of men, and held them
back on every side. Then King Duryodhana sent ten thousand brave horsemen to
hold back the Pandavas. As they came in with great speed, like Garudas, in the
battle, the earth, struck by their hooves, shook and rang, king. A very great
sound of horses' hooves was heard then, like a great forest of bamboo burning on
a mountain. The great dust they raised as they leapt reached the path of the sun
and hid it. The Pandava army was thrown into turmoil by those swift horses, like
a great lake by swans landing at great speed. And with the noise of their
neighing nothing could be made out.

"Then King Yudhishthira and the two Pandava sons of Madri struck back swiftly at
the charge of the horsemen in the battle, great king. So the shore of the great
ocean holds back the rush of its waters, risen and swollen by the rains at the
full moon. Then those chariot fighters, king, cut the heads of the horsemen from
their bodies with straight arrows. Killed by those strong bowmen, they fell,
great king, like great elephants killed by elephants in a mountain cave. The
horsemen too, going in all ten directions, cut off heads with very sharp lances
and straight arrows. Horsemen coming too close cut off heads with spears, bull
of the Bharatas, like fruit from a great tree. Horses with their riders were
killed here and there, fallen and falling, by the hundred and the thousand.
Being killed, the horses ran in terror, like deer that meet lions and run for
their lives. Having beaten the enemy in the great battle, great king, the
Pandavas blew their conches and beat their drums in the battle.

"Then Duryodhana, seeing the army standing dejected, said this to the Madra
king, best of the Bharatas: 'Uncle, strong-armed one, this mighty eldest son of
Pandu has beaten my men. He is putting the army to flight while we watch. Hold
him back, strong-armed one, as the shore holds back the ocean. For you are famed
as having strength and valour very hard to bear.' Hearing your son's words,
mighty Shalya went with a line of chariots to where King Yudhishthira was. The
Pandava held back Shalya's very great army as it came suddenly on in the battle
with the force of a great flood. Then the Dharma king, the great chariot
fighter, quickly struck the Madra king in the middle of the chest with ten
arrows in the battle, and Nakula and Sahadeva with three straight arrows each.
The Madra king struck each of them with three, pierced Yudhishthira again with
sixty sharp arrows, and struck the two angry sons of Madri with two each. Then
strong-armed Bhima, conqueror of enemies, seeing the king in the Madra king's
power in the battle, as if in the mouth of death, charged to Yudhishthira's side
in the battle. Then a very terrible, most cruel battle began, as the sun stood
in the western quarter, shining.

"Then your father in anger struck the Parthas and their armies on every side in
the battle with the finest sharp arrows. He pierced Bhima with twelve, Satyaki
with nine arrows, Nakula with three and Sahadeva with seven. He planted twelve
in Yudhishthira's arms and chest, and pierced Dhrishtadyumna, and the mighty man
roared. Nakula pierced the grandfather back with six, Madhava with three arrows,
Dhrishtadyumna with seventy, Bhimasena with five and Yudhishthira with twelve.
Drona pierced Satyaki and then Bhimasena, each with five sharp arrows like
Yama's staff. And they pierced Drona, the bull among brahmins, back with three
straight arrows each, as men strike a great elephant with goads.

"The Sauviras, the Kitavas, the easterners, the westerners, the northerners and
the Malavas, the Abhishahas, the Shurasenas, the Shibis and the Vasatis were
being killed with sharp arrows. Still they did not leave Bhishma in the battle.
In the same way others, being killed by the great Pandavas, came on against the
Pandavas with weapons of many kinds in their hands. And the Pandavas too, king,
surrounded the grandfather.

"Surrounded on every side by floods of chariots, the unbeaten man blazed up like
a fire let loose in a thicket, burning his enemies. The fire of Bhishma, with
chariots for its hearth and bows for its flames, swords, spears and maces for
its fuel, and arrows for its sparks, burned up the bulls among kshatriyas. With
arrows feathered with gold, with vulture feathers, well sharpened, with barbed
arrows, reeds and iron arrows, he covered that army. He brought down banners and
chariot fighters with sharp arrows, and made the lines of chariots look like
groves of palm trees with their tops cut off. The strong-armed man, best of all
who bear weapons, left chariots, elephants and horses without men in the battle,
king. Hearing the slap of his bowstring on his arm-guard, like the crack of a
thunderbolt, all creatures trembled, Bharata. Your father's arrows fell without
missing, bull of the Bharatas. The arrows loosed from Bhishma's bow did not stop
at armour. We saw chariots, their heroes killed, king, yoked to swift horses,
being carried about the field, great king.

"There were fourteen thousand great chariot fighters of the Chedis, Kashis and
Karushas, famous, of good families, ready to give up their bodies, who never
turned back, with banners worked with gold. They met Bhishma in the battle, like
Death with his mouth open, and sank into the other world with their horses,
chariots and elephants. We saw chariots by the hundred and the thousand, king,
some with their axles and fittings broken, some with their wheels broken. The
earth was strewn with broken chariots and their frames, with fallen chariot
fighters, with arrows and fine armour cut, and with battle-axes, sir, lord of
the people. It was strewn with maces, pestles, swords and arrows, with chariot
poles, quivers and broken wheels, sir. There were arms, bows, swords and heads
with earrings, arm-guards and finger-guards, banners brought down and bows cut
in many places. Elephants whose riders had been killed, king, and horses whose
riders had been killed ran about quickly there by the hundred and the thousand.
Though they tried, the heroes could not stop the great chariot fighters from
running, tormented by Bhishma's arrows.

"Being killed by that man as mighty as great Indra, the great army broke, great
king, and no two men ran together. With its chariots, elephants and horses
struck and its banners and chariot shafts brought down, the army of the sons of
Pandu was crying out and senseless. There father killed son, and son father, and
friend killed dear friend in the uproar, forced by the power of fate. Other
soldiers of the son of Pandu were seen running with their armour thrown off and
their hair loose, Bharata. The army of the son of Pandu looked like a herd of
cattle bolting, with its chariots and elephants bolting, and it cried out in
pain.

"Seeing the army being broken, the joy of the Yadavas stopped the fine chariot
and said to Partha Bibhatsu, 'Partha, the time you have wanted has come. Strike
him, tiger among men, unless you are losing your wits. Long ago, hero, in the
gathering of the kings in Virata's city, Partha, in Sanjaya's presence, you
said, "I will kill all the soldiers of Dhritarashtra's son, led by Bhishma and
Drona, with all their followers, who fight me in battle." Make that come true,
son of Kunti, tamer of enemies. Remember the dharma of kshatriyas and fight,
bull of the Bharatas.'

"Told this by Vasudeva, Bibhatsu looked aside and down, as if unwilling. He
said, 'Having killed those who should not be killed, shall I have a kingdom
worse than hell, or sorrows in the forest? Which would be the good deed for me?
Drive the horses to where Bhishma is. I will do what you say. I will bring down
the old grandfather of the Kurus, whom no one can face.'

"Then Madhava drove the silver-white horses to where Bhishma was, king, who was
as hard to look at as the sun. Then the great army of Yudhishthira turned back
again, seeing strong-armed Partha going out to meet Bhishma in the battle. Then
Bhishma, best of the Kurus, roaring like a lion again and again, quickly covered
Dhananjaya's chariot with showers of arrows. In a moment his chariot, with its
horses and its charioteer, could not be made out at all under the great shower
of arrows. But Vasudeva the Satvata, unshaken, holding firm, drove on the
horses, pierced as they were by Bhishma's arrows. Then Partha took up his divine
bow, which roars like a cloud, cut Bhishma's bow with sharp arrows and brought
it down. With his bow cut, the Kauravya, your father, strung another great bow
in the blink of an eye. He drew with both arms the bow that roars like a cloud,
and Arjuna in anger cut that bow too. The son of Shantanu praised his speed:
'Well done, Partha, strong-armed one! Well done, son of Kunti!' Having said this
to him, Bhishma took another beautiful bow and shot arrows at Partha's chariot
in the battle. Vasudeva showed his great strength in driving the horses, making
the arrows miss and showing his circles. Bhishma and Partha, those tigers among
men, wounded by arrows, shone like two angry bulls scored with the marks of
horns.

"But Vasudeva saw Partha's gentle fighting, and Bhishma pouring out showers of
arrows without pause in the battle. He saw him standing between the two armies
and burning like the sun. He saw him killing the best of the Pandava's soldiers
one after another, and making an end of the world, as it were, in Yudhishthira's
army. The strong-armed Madhava, killer of enemy heroes, could not bear it.
Letting go of Partha's silver-white horses, sir, the great yogi, in anger, they
say, leapt down from the great chariot, the mighty one, and ran at Bhishma with
his arms for his weapons. Whip in hand, full of fire, roaring like a lion again
and again, the lord of the world seemed to split the world with his feet. His
eyes copper-red with anger, wanting to kill, Krishna of boundless splendour
seemed to swallow the minds of your men in the great battle.

"Seeing Madhava in the uproar rising against Bhishma in the battle, the soldiers
there cried out, 'Bhishma is killed! Bhishma is killed!', and all the men ran in
fear of Vasudeva. Dressed in yellow silk, dark as a sapphire, Janardana shone as
he ran at Bhishma, like a cloud garlanded with lightning. Like a lion at an
elephant, like a bull leading the herd at a bull, the bull of the Yadavas
charged, full of fire, roaring.

"Bhishma saw the lotus-eyed one coming at him in the battle. Not at all alarmed,
he drew his great bow and said to Govinda, with an unshaken mind, 'Come, come,
lotus-eyed one, god of gods! I bow to you. Best of the Satvatas, throw me down
today in the great battle. If I am killed by you in battle, god, blameless one,
it will be the highest good for me, Krishna, in the other world and in this one.
Today in battle, Govinda, I am honoured by the three worlds.'

"Then Partha ran right behind Keshava, and the strong-armed man took hold of him
and held him with both arms. Held back by Partha, the lotus-eyed Krishna, the
highest Person, went on at speed, carrying him along. But Partha, killer of
enemy heroes, braced his feet with force and somehow held Hrishikesha back at
the tenth step. Then Arjuna, killer of enemy heroes, in distress, said to him as
he breathed like a snake, his eyes wild with anger, 'Turn back, strong-armed
one. You should not make a lie of it. You said before, Keshava, "I will not
fight." Madhava, the world will call you a liar. This whole burden is mine. I
will kill the man of the strict vow. I swear to you, Madhava, by our friendship,
by truth and by my good deeds, that I will make an end of our enemies, destroyer
of enemies. See the unassailable man of the great vow brought down this very
day, like the full moon falling at the end of time, as it must.'

"Hearing great Phalguna's words, Madhava said nothing, and climbed back into the
chariot in anger.

"As those two tigers among men stood in the chariot, Bhishma, son of Shantanu,
rained a shower of arrows on them again, as a cloud rains on two mountains. Your
father Devavrata took the lives of the warriors as the sun takes the warmth of
things with its rays at the end of the cold season. As the Pandava broke the
Kurus' armies in the battle, so your father broke the Pandava armies in the
battle. With their armies killed and put to flight, without heart and without
wits, they could not look at Bhishma, matchless in battle, like the sun at
midday scorching with his own heat. Being killed by Bhishma like Time at the end
of an age, great king, the Pandavas looked about them, tormented by fear. They
found no rescuer, like cows stuck in mud, like ants crushed by a strong man in
battle. They could not look at Bhishma, the great chariot fighter, Bharata, so
hard to face, with his floods of arrows, scorching the kings, his arrows for
flames, like the burning sun.

"As he was crushing the Pandava army, the sun of the thousand rays set. Then the
armies, worn out with weariness, set their minds on withdrawing."

**Divergences.**

* **Krishna runs at Bhishma a second time.** This time he goes "with his arms for his weapons … whip in hand" (*bhujapraharaṇaḥ … pratodapāṇiḥ*, 6.102.53–54), not with the discus as on the third day (6.55.86–89). Arjuna again stops him "at the tenth step" (6.102.64), and reminds him that he said he would not fight (6.102.66–67). Krishna "said nothing" and climbed back, still angry (6.102.70). See ledger L-36.
* **The doublet.** The Bhishma–Arjuna duel at 6.102.38–52 repeats the third day's (6.55.47–64) almost word for word. It is told again here because the text tells it again.
* **Arjuna's vow.** Here Krishna places Arjuna's vow "in Virata's city, in Sanjaya's presence" (6.102.32); on the third day he said only "in the gathering of the kings" (6.55.42).
* **Arjuna's reluctance.** He answers "looking aside and with his face down, as if unwilling", asking which is the good deed: a kingdom won by killing those who should not be killed, or sorrow in the forest (6.102.35–36).
* **Bhishma and Shikhandi.** Shikhandi pierces him, but he "did not strike back, thinking of him in his mind as a woman" (6.99.7).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*393–\*422. \*414–\*415 have Bhishma call himself Krishna's servant and speak of the whip.

**Choices.**

* 6.99.20 *gandharvanagaropamāḥ*: "like the cities of the gandharvas in the sky", mirages.
* 6.100.18 *bāhlikaṃ prapitāmaham*: Bahlika is the Pandavas' "great-grandfather" by generation, Shantanu's brother.
* 6.100.20 *budhaśanaiścarau*: "Budha and Shanaishchara", Mercury and Saturn.
* 6.102.12 *muṇḍatālavanāni*: "groves of palm trees with their tops cut off".
* 6.102.53 *kruddho nāma*: "in anger, they say".
* 6.102.69 *yadṛcchayā*: "as it must", the moon falling by the course of things.

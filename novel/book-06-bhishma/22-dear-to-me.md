# Dear to Me

"'Some devotees, always disciplined like this, worship you,' said Arjuna, 'and
others the imperishable, the unmanifest. Which of them know yoga best?'

"'Those who fix their minds on me and worship me, always disciplined, with the
highest faith,' said the Blessed One, 'I think the most disciplined. Others
worship the imperishable, which cannot be pointed out, the unmanifest,
everywhere, unthinkable, standing firm like an anvil, unmoving and fixed. They
control the whole troop of the senses, their understanding is the same
everywhere, and they delight in the good of all beings. They too reach me. But
the trouble is greater for those whose thought clings to the unmanifest. For the
unmanifest way is hard for the embodied to reach.

"'But some renounce all actions in me, intent on me, and worship me, meditating
on me with yoga given to nothing else. Their thought is fixed on me, and I soon
lift them out of the ocean of death and wandering, Partha. Set your mind on me
alone. Let your understanding enter me. From then on you will live in me alone.
There is no doubt of this. But if you cannot fix your thought steadily on me,
then seek to reach me by the yoga of practice, Dhananjaya. If you are not able
even to practise, be intent on work for me. Even doing actions for my sake, you
will reach perfection. If you cannot do even this, take refuge in my yoga. Then,
self-controlled, give up the fruit of all actions. For knowledge is better than
practice, meditation is better than knowledge, giving up the fruit of action is
better than meditation, and peace follows at once on giving up.

"'One who hates no being, who is friendly and compassionate, without "mine" and
without self-regard, the same in pain and pleasure and patient, is dear to me.
Always content, a yogin, self-controlled, firm in resolve, his mind and
understanding offered to me, my devotee is dear to me. One from whom the world
does not shrink, and who does not shrink from the world, who is free of joy,
impatience, fear and agitation, is dear to me. One who wants nothing, who is
pure, skilful, indifferent, free of distress, who has given up every
undertaking, my devotee, is dear to me. One who neither rejoices nor hates,
neither grieves nor longs, who has given up good and evil, full of devotion, is
dear to me. He is the same to enemy and friend, and in honour and dishonour. He
is the same in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, free of attachment. He is the
same in blame and praise, silent, content with whatever comes, without a home.
Steady in mind, full of devotion, that man is dear to me. But those who worship
this amrita of dharma as it has been told, with faith, intent on me, devotees,
are very dear to me.'"

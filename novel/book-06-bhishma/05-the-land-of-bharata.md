# The Land of Bharata

"This land of Bharata," said Dhritarashtra, "where this army has swelled up,
which my son Duryodhana covets beyond measure, which the sons of Pandu covet,
and on which my mind is fixed: tell me the truth of it, Sanjaya, for you are
skilled."

"The Pandavas do not covet it, king," said Sanjaya. "Hear my words. Duryodhana
covets it, and Shakuni son of Subala, and other kshatriyas, lords of many
countries, who covet the land of Bharata and cannot bear one another. Here I
will describe to you, Bharata, the land of Bharata. It was dear to the god Indra
and to Manu son of Vivasvat, to Prithu son of Vena, king, and to great Ikshvaku.
It was dear to Yayati, Ambarisha, Mandhatri and Nahusha, to Muchukunda and Shibi
son of Ushinara, to Rishabha, to Aila and to King Nriga, and to other mighty
kshatriyas, great king. The land of Bharata is dear to all of them, king of
kings, Bharata. I will tell you of that land as I have heard it, tamer of
enemies. Listen to me, king, as I tell you what you ask.

"Mahendra, Malaya, Sahya, Shuktimat, Rikshavat, Vindhya and Pariyatra: these are
the seven chief mountain ranges. Near them, king, are thousands of mountains,
well known, solid, broad, with bright slopes. Beyond them are others, not known,
small, and those who live on them are small. Aryas and mlecchas, Kauravya, and
men mixed of both, lord, drink from many rivers.

"They drink from many rivers. There are the Ganga, the Sindhu and the Sarasvati,
the Godavari, the Narmada and the great river Bahuda. There are the Shatadru,
the Chandrabhaga and the great river Yamuna, the Drishadvati, the Vipasha, the
Vipapa and the Sthulavaluka. There are the river Vetravati, the Krishnavena
flowing down, the Iravati, the Vitasta, the Payoshni and the Devika. There are
the Vedasmriti, the Vetasini, the Tridiva, the Ishkumalini, the Karishini, the
Chitravaha and the Chitrasena flowing down. There are the Gomati, the Dhutapapa
and the great river Vandana, the Kaushiki, the Tridiva, the Kritya, the Vichitra
and the Lohatarini. There are the Rathastha, the Shatakumbha and the Sarayu,
lord of men, the Charmanvati, the Vetravati, the Hastisoma and the Disha. There
are the Shatavari, the Payoshni and the Para, the Bhaimarathi, the Kaveri, the
Chuluka, the Vapi and the Shatabala. There are the Nichira, the Mahita and the
Suprayoga, lord of men, the Pavitra, the Kundala, the Sindhu, the Vajini and the
Puramalini. There are the Purvabhirama, the Vira, the Bhima and the Oghavati,
the Palashini, the Papahara, the Mahendra and the Pippalavati. There are the
Parishena, the Asikni, the Sarala and the Bharamardini, the Puruhi, the Pravara,
the Mena, the Mogha and the Ghritavati. There are the Dhumatya, the Atikrishna,
the Suchi and the Chhavi, Kaurava, the Sadanira, the Adhrishya and the great
river Kushadhara. There are the Shashikanta, the Shiva and the Viravati, the
Vastu, the Suvastu, the Gauri, the Kampana and the Hiranvati. There are the
Hiranvati, the Chitravati and the Chitrasena flowing down, the Rathachitra, the
Jyotiratha, the Vishvamitra and the Kapinjala. There are the Upendra, the
Bahula, the Kuchara and the Ambuvahini, the Vainandi, the Pinjala, the Venna and
the great river Tungavena. There are the Vidisha, the Krishnavenna, the Tamra
and the Kapila, the Shalu, the Suvama, the Vedashva and the great river
Harisrava. There are the Shighra, the Picchila and the Bharadvaji flowing down,
the Kaushiki flowing down, the Shona, the Bahuda and the Chandana. There are the
Durga, the Antahshila, the Brahmamedhya and the Brihadvati, the Charaksha, the
Mahirohi and the Jambunadi. There are the Sunasa, the Tamasa, the Dasi, the
Trasa, another, the Varanasi, the Loloddhritakara and the great river Purnasha.
There are the Manavi and the Vrishabha, great rivers, lord of the people, the
Sadaniramaya, the Vritya, the Mandaga and the Mandavahini. There are the
Brahmani, the Mahagauri and the Durga, Bharata, the Chitropala, the Chitrabarha,
the Manju and the Makaravahini. There are the Mandakini, the Vaitarani and the
great river Koka, the Shuktimati, the Aranya, the Pushpaveni and the Utpalavati.
There are the Lohitya, the Karatoya and the Vrishabhangini, the Kumari, the
Rishikulya and the Brahmakulya, Bharata.

"All the very holy Sarasvatis and all the Gangas, sir, are all mothers of the
universe, and all are very strong. And there are rivers not well known by the
hundred and the thousand. These are the rivers, king, named as I remember them.

---

"Now hear the countries as I tell them. Here are the Kurus and Panchalas, the
Shalvas, the Madreyas and the Jangalas. Here are the Shurasenas, Kalingas,
Bodhas and Maukas, the Matsyas, Sukutyas, Saubalyas and Kuntalas, the Kashis and
Koshalas. Here are the Chedis, Vatsas and Karushas, the Bhojas, Sindhus and
Pulindakas, the Uttamaujas, the Dasharnas and the Mekalas with the Utkalas. Here
are the Panchalas, Kaushijas, Ekaprishthas and Yugamdharas, the Saudhas, Madras
and Bhujingas, the Kashis and the Aparakashis. Here are the Jatharas, Kukkushas
and Sudasharnas, Bharata, the Kuntis and Avantis and the Aparakuntis. Here are
the Govindas, Mandakas and Shandas, the Vidarbhas and the Anupavasikas, the
Ashmakas, Pamsurashtras, Goparashtras and Panitakas. Here are the Adirashtras
and Sukuttas, the Balirashtra and the Kevalas, the Vanarasyas, Pravahas, Vakras,
Vakrabhayas and Shakas. Here are the Videhakas, Magadhas, Suhmas and Vijayas,
the Angas, Vangas and Kalingas and the Yakrillomans. Here are the Mallas,
Sudeshnas and Prahutas, the Mahishas and Karshikas, the Vahikas, Vatadhanas,
Abhiras and Kalatoyakas. Here are the Aparandhras, Shudras, Pahlavas and
Charmakhandikas, the forest Shabaras and the Marubhaumas, sir. Here are the
Upavrishchas, Anupavrishchas, Surashtras and Kekayas, the Kuttaparantas,
Dvaidheyas, Kakshas and Samudranishkutas. Here are the many Andhras, king, and
the Antargiryas, the Bahirgiryas, the Angamaladas, the Magadhas and the
Manavarjakas. Here are the Mahyuttaras, Pravrisheyas and Bhargavas, lord of the
people, the Pundras, Bhargas, Kiratas, Sudoshnas and Pramudas. Here are the
Shakas, Nishadas and Nishadhas, the Anartas and Nairritas, the Dugulas,
Pratimatsyas, Kushalas and Kunatas. Here are the Tiragrahas, Taratoyas, Rajikas
and the Rasyaka tribes, the Tilakas, Parasikas, Madhumantas and Prakutsakas.
Here are the Kashmiras, Sindhusauviras, Gandharas and Darshakas, the Abhisaras,
Kulutas, Shaivalas and Bahlikas. Here are the Darvikas, Sakachas and Darvas, the
Vatajamarathoragas, the Bahuvadyas, Kauravya, the Sudamans and Sumallikas. Here
are the Vadhras, Karishakas and Kulindopatyakas, the Vanayus, Dashaparshvas,
Romanas and Kushabindus. Here are the Kacchas, Gopalakacchas, Langalas and
Paravallakas, the Kiratas, Barbaras, Siddhas, Videhas and Tamralingakas, the
Oshtras, Pundras and Sairandhras, and the Parvatiyas, sir.

"And there are other countries in the south, bull of the Bharatas. There are the
Dravidas, Keralas, Prachyas, Bhushikas and Vanavasins, the Unnatyakas,
Mahishakas, Vikalpas and Mushakas. There are the Karnikas, Kuntikas, Saudbhidas
and Nalakalakas, the Kaukuttakas, Cholas, Konkanas and Malavanakas. There are
the Samangas, Kopanas, Kukuras, Angadas and Marishas, the Dhvajinis,
Utsavasanketas, Trigartas and Sarvasenis. There are the Tryangas, Kekarakas,
Proshthas and Parasamcharakas, the Vindhyapulakas and the Pulindas with the
Kalkalas. There are the Malakas, Mallakas and Aparavartakas, the Kulindas,
Kulakas, Karanthas and Kurakas. There are the Mushakas, Stanabalas, Satis and
Pattipanjakas, the Adidayas, Siralas, Stubakas and Stanapas, the
Hrishividarbhas, Kantikas, Tanganas and Paratanganas.

"And there are the northern peoples, other mlecchas, best of the Bharatas. There
are the Yavanas with the Kambojas, cruel mleccha tribes, the Sakshaddruhs and
the Kuntalas, the Hunas with the Paratakas. There are the Maradhas, the Chinas
and the Dashamalikas, settlements of kshatriyas and families of vaishyas and
shudras. There are the Shudras and Abhiras, the Daradas and the Kashmiras with
the Pashus, the Khashikas, the Tukharas, the Pallavas and the Girigahvaras.
There are the Atreyas with the Bharadvajas, the Stanayoshikas, the Aupakas and
the Kalingas, and the tribes of the Kiratas, the Tamaras, the Hamsamargas and
the Karabhanjakas. I have named the countries only by pointing to them, lord.

"According to its virtues and strength, the earth, rightly tended, yields great
fruit of the three aims of life, and can be milked like a cow that grants every
wish. Kings, brave and skilled in dharma and purpose, covet it. Eager for its
riches, swift men give up their lives in battle for it. The earth is the final
refuge of bodies divine and human, as they wish. The kings tear at one another
like dogs over meat, best of the Bharatas, wanting to enjoy the earth. No one
here is ever satisfied in his desires. So the Kurus and Pandavas strive to take
hold of the earth, lord of the earth, by conciliation, gifts, division and the
rod. Father, mother, son, the air and the sky: the earth becomes all these to
creatures, bull among men, if it is rightly seen and without fault."

**Divergences.** Rejected lines (\*43–\*51) add more names. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* **The lists.** The rivers and peoples are named in full, in the text's order, as the citation rule requires. Names are given in the novel's spelling without explanation; many are known only from this list. Some names repeat (Tridiva, Vetravati, Payoshni, Hiranvati, Kaushiki, Bahuda, Durga), as in the text.
* 6.10.11 *hrasvopajīvinaḥ*: "those who live on them are small"; the sense may be "who live poorly".
* 6.10.30 *trasām anyāṃ varāṇasīm*: "the Trasa, another, the Varanasi"; *anyām*, "another", may be part of a name.
* 6.10.37–68: compounds of peoples' names are divided as seems most likely. Where a name could be read two ways (for example *kukurāṅgadamāriṣāḥ*, *vātajāmarathoragāḥ*), the novel keeps one reading.
* 6.10.39 *uttamaujāḥ*: here a people, not the Panchala fighter.
* 6.10.74: "Father, mother, son, the air and the sky": the earth becomes all these to creatures.

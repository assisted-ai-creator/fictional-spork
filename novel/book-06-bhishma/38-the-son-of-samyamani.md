# The Son of Samyamani

"When the night was over, Bharata," said Sanjaya, "the great-souled Bhishma,
angry, went at the head of the Bharata armies against the enemy, surrounded by
his whole force. Drona, Duryodhana and Bahlika, Durmarshana and Chitrasena, the
very strong Jayadratha with floods of troops, and other kings followed him on
every side. Surrounded by those great chariot fighters, splendid and strong, the
chief of kings, best of kings, shone like the wielder of the thunderbolt among
the gods. At the head of that army great flags shone on the shoulders of great
elephants. They were bright red, yellow, black and white, waving and tangled
together. With King Bhishma son of Shantanu, with great chariot fighters,
elephants and horses, that army looked like the sky in the rains, full of clouds
with lightning and thunder. Then the very fierce army of the Kurus, guarded by
Shantanu's son, rushed against Arjuna to battle, like the terrible rush of a
river.

"From far off the great-souled one with the king of monkeys on his banner saw
that array. It was like a great cloud, with elephants, horses, foot soldiers and
floods of chariots for its wings, and its hidden strength made up of many kinds
of fierce beasts. The hero, the bull among men, set out at the head of the army
in his chariot with its banner, its fender and its white horses. The great soul
was resolved to kill all the young men of the enemy. The bull of the Yadus held
the monkey-bannered chariot ready, with its fine equipment, its upper rail and
its pole. Seeing it in battle, the Kauravas were dismayed, and your sons with
them. Your men saw that king of arrays guarded by the diademed one, the great
chariot fighter of the world, his weapon raised and drawn back. It was scattered
with four thousand fierce beasts on every side. As the King of Dharma, the joy
of the Kauravas, had drawn up the array the day before, so the Chedi leaders
with the Panchala leaders came to their places and stood.

"Then thousands of drums, struck with great force, sounded in the battle. The
sounds of conches and kettledrums rose in all the armies, with lion roars. Then
the heroes drew their bows with arrows on them. In a moment the great twanging
drowned the noise of the drums and tabors and the great sound of the conches.
The sky was filled with the sound of conches, with nets of earthly dust rising
quickly, its light spread over as by a great canopy. Seeing it, the heroes fell
on one another at once.

"Chariot fighter, struck by chariot fighter, fell with his charioteer, horses,
chariot and banner. Elephant fell, struck by elephant, and foot soldier struck
by foot soldier. Crowds of fine horses wheeled and were wounded by arrows.
Crowds of fine horses struck them down with spears and swords, a wonderful
sight. Shields hurled by heroes, adorned with clusters of golden stars, split by
axes, spears and swords, fell on the ground. Some chariot fighters fell with
their charioteers, torn by elephants' tusks and trunks. Bull elephants too fell
on the ground, struck by the arrows of a bull among chariot fighters. Young
horsemen and foot soldiers were beaten down by the rush of crowds of elephants
and struck by tusks and bodies. Men sank to the ground when they heard their
cries of distress.

"In that great danger to the young horsemen and foot soldiers, with elephants,
horses and chariots in confusion, Bhishma saw the one with the monkey king on
his banner being surrounded by great chariot fighters. Shantanu's son, with his
palm banner raised as high as five palm trees, came at the speed of his fine
horses. He ran at the diademed one, whose path blazed with great weapons and
arrows like thunderbolts. So too those led by Drona ran at Indra's son, who was
like Shakra: Kripa, Shalya and Vivimshati, Duryodhana and Somadatta's son, king.

"Then Abhimanyu, Arjuna's son, who knew all weapons, came from the head of the
chariot ranks in bright golden armour. The hero ran quickly at all of them.
Krishna's grandson, whose deeds nothing could hold back, broke the great weapons
of those great chariot fighters. He shone like the Blessed One, Fire, in the
place of sacrifice, his garland of flames fed by great mantras. Then Bhishma,
high in spirit, quickly made a river of blood with foaming water in the
slaughter of his enemies. He passed Subhadra's son and went on to the great
chariot fighter Partha. Then the diademed one, wearing his garland, laughed. He
destroyed the net of great weapons with a net of arrows, a wonderful sight, to
the great roar of Gandiva's twang. Then the great-souled one with the monkey
king on his banner, whose deeds nothing could hold back, quickly showered
Bhishma, the best of all bowmen, with nets of arrows and clean broad arrows. So
people, Kurus and Srinjayas, saw that single combat of Bhishma and Dhananjaya,
the two best of good men, without faltering, with the terrible roar of their
bows.

---

"Drona's son, Bhurishravas, Shalya and Chitrasena, sir, and the son of Samyamani
fought Subhadra's son. People saw him alone, engaged with those five tigers
among men of great splendour, like a lion cub with elephants. In aim, in
bravery, in courage, in weapons and in lightness, no one was equal to Krishna's
grandson. Seeing his son, the tamer of enemies, showing such courage in battle,
Partha, ready in battle, roared a lion's roar. Seeing your grandson harrying the
army, lord of the people, your men surrounded him on every side, king of kings.
But the bright son of Subhadra, whose enemies are the ones to be pitied, went
without pity against the army of Dhritarashtra's sons with his splendour and his
strength. As he fought his enemies in battle, his great bow was seen, bright as
the sun, ever on the path of lightness.

"With one arrow he pierced Drona's son, and Shalya with five, and with eight he
brought down the banner of Samyamani's son. With a sharp feathered arrow like a
snake he carried off the great gold-handled spear that Somadatta's son had
hurled. Arjuna's heir held off Shalya's hundreds of terrible arrows as he shot
them, and killed his horses in battle. Bhurishravas and Shalya, Drona's son,
Samyamani and Shala, though roused, did not come at him, for Krishna's grandson
relied on the strength of his arms.

"Then, king of kings, your son urged on the Trigartas and the Madras, with the
Kekayas, thirty-five thousand of them. They surrounded the diademed one, who was
set on killing, with his son. They were the leading men who knew the science of
the bow, men no enemy could conquer in battle. The Panchala commander of the
army, the conqueror of enemies, saw those two bulls among chariot fighters,
father and son, surrounded there, king. Many thousands of elephants and floods
of chariots surrounded him, with horses and foot soldiers in hundreds of
thousands. The scorcher of enemies, angry, drew his bow and urged on his army.
He went against that Madra force and the Kekayas. Guarded by that famous man
with the firm bow, the army looked fine as it went to fight, with chariots,
elephants and horses at the ready.

"As Arjuna went ahead, joy of the Kurus, the Panchala prince struck Sharadvat's
son with three arrows at the collarbone. Then he killed the Madrakas with ten
arrows each. Glad, alone, he killed Kritavarma's horse with a broad arrow. With
a broad-headed iron arrow the scorcher of enemies killed Damana too, heir of the
great-souled Paurava. Then the son of Samyamani pierced the Panchala prince, mad
for battle, with thirty arrows, and his charioteer with ten. The great archer,
badly wounded, licked the corners of his mouth and cut his bow with a very sharp
broad arrow. Then he quickly struck him with twenty-five arrows and killed his
horses, king, and both his rear guards.

"Standing on the chariot with its horses killed, bull of the Bharatas, the son
of Samyamani saw the son of the great-souled Panchala king. He took up a very
terrible fine sword of iron and, on foot, ran quickly at Drupada's son in his
chariot. The Pandavas there and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, saw him
coming like a great flood, like a snake falling from the sky, whirling his
shield and sword like Death let loose by Time. He seemed to blaze with the light
of his weapon, with the stride of an elephant in rut. He ran straight at the
Panchala's son with a sharp sword in his hand and a shield against arrows. He
got past the rush of arrows and came close to the chariot. Then the commander of
the army, angry, quickly split his head with a club. As he was killed, king, his
shining sword and shield fell from his hand and struck the ground with force.
Having killed him with the head of his club, the great-souled son of the
Panchala king, of terrible courage, won the highest fame.

"When that prince was killed, a great archer and great chariot fighter, a great
cry of grief rose from your army, sir. Then Samyamani, angry at seeing his son
killed, ran at speed at the Panchala prince, mad for battle. All the kings, the
Kurus and the Pandavas, saw those two heroes, the best of chariot fighters, meet
there in battle. Then Samyamani, killer of enemy heroes, struck Prishata's
grandson in anger with three arrows, as a great elephant is struck with goads.
So too Shalya, who shines in battle, angry, struck the brave grandson of
Prishata in the chest. Then the battle went on."

**Choices.**

* 6.56.7 *vyāla*: "fierce beasts". The word covers beasts of prey and fierce elephants.
* 6.56.11: "the day before" (*pūrve 'hani*). On day 3 the Pandavas used the half-moon array (6.52). The text does not name an array for day 4.
* 6.57.9 *saumadatti*: Somadatta's son. Bhurishravas is in the list at 6.57.1.
* 6.57.18: the target is *śāradvata*, "Sharadvat's son", which means Kripa.
* 6.57.6 *dīnaśatrur adīnavat*: "whose enemies are the ones to be pitied ... without pity" is an attempt at the wordplay.

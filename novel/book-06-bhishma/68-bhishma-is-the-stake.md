# Bhishma Is the Stake

"Ten warriors of yours fought Bhimasena," said Sanjaya: "Bhagadatta, Kripa,
Shalya, Kritavarma the Satvata, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, Jayadratha of
Sindhu, Chitrasena, Vikarna and young Durmarshana. They had a great army from
many countries, and they sought great glory in battle for Bhishma's sake, king.
Shalya struck Bhimasena with nine arrows, Kritavarma with three and Kripa with
nine. Chitrasena, Vikarna and Bhagadatta, sir, struck Bhimasena with ten broad
arrows each. The Sindhu king struck him at the collarbone with three, Vinda and
Anuvinda of Avanti with five each, and Durmarshana the Pandava with twenty sharp
arrows.

"Great king, the very strong Bhimasena pierced all of them one by one with many
arrows, those shining heroes of the whole world, the great chariot fighters of
Dhritarashtra's side. He pierced Shalya with fifty and Kritavarma with eight. He
cut Kripa's bow in the middle with its arrow, Bharata, and pierced him again
with five when it was cut. He struck Vinda and Anuvinda with three each,
Durmarshana with twenty and Chitrasena with five. Having pierced Vikarna with
ten arrows and Jayadratha with five, Bhima roared, glad, and pierced the Sindhu
king again with three.

"Then Gautama, the best of chariot fighters, took another bow. In fury he
pierced Bhima with ten sharp arrows. He was pierced by many arrows, like a great
elephant by goads. The mighty, strong-armed Bhimasena grew angry and struck
Gautama with many arrows in battle. Bright as Time the ender, he sent the Sindhu
king's horses and charioteer to the world of death with three arrows. The great
chariot fighter leapt quickly from his chariot with its horses killed and shot
sharp arrows at Bhimasena in battle. Bhima cut the great-souled Sindhu king's
bow in the middle with two broad arrows, best of the Bharatas. His bow cut, his
chariot gone, his horses and charioteer killed, he climbed in haste onto
Chitrasena's chariot, king. The Pandava did a most wonderful deed there in
battle. Having pierced the great chariot fighters with arrows and held them off,
the great chariot fighter left the Sindhu king without a chariot while the whole
world watched.

"Shalya could not bear Bhimasena's courage. Fitting sharp arrows polished by the
smith, he pierced Bhima with seventy and said, 'Stand! Stand!' Kripa, Kritavarma
and Bhagadatta, sir, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, Chitrasena in battle,
Durmarshana, Vikarna and the mighty Sindhu king, those tamers of enemies,
quickly pierced Bhima for Shalya's sake. He pierced each of them back with five
arrows and pierced Shalya with seventy and again with ten. Shalya pierced him
with nine and again with five, and with a broad arrow he pierced his charioteer
deep in a vital spot. Seeing Vishoka split open, mighty Bhimasena fixed three
arrows in the Madra king's arms and chest. He struck the other great archers in
battle with three straight arrows each, and roared like a lion. Those great
archers, ready, struck the Pandava, mad for battle, hard in his vital points
with three unblunted arrows each. Badly pierced, the great archer Bhimasena did
not falter, like a mountain under clouds pouring torrents of rain.

"Having pierced Shalya hard with nine arrows, the very glorious one pierced the
Pragjyotisha firmly in battle with a hundred, king. Then, like one practised of
hand, he cut the great-souled Satvata's bow and arrow with a very sharp
razor-headed arrow. Kritavarma took another bow and struck Wolf-belly between
the brows with an iron arrow, scorcher of enemies. Bhima pierced Shalya in
battle with nine iron arrows, Bhagadatta with three and Kritavarma with eight.
He pierced the chariot fighters led by Gautama with two each, and they pierced
him in battle with sharp arrows, king. Those great chariot fighters pressed him
hard on every side. But he thought them no better than straw and moved about
without pain. Those best of chariot fighters, calm, sent sharp arrows at Bhima
in hundreds and thousands.

"The great chariot fighter Bhagadatta, the hero, hurled at him in battle a spear
of great speed with a golden shaft, very costly. The strong-armed Sindhu king
threw a lance and an axe, Kripa a shataghni, king, and Shalya an arrow in
battle. The other great archers sent five arrows each with force, aiming at
Bhimasena. The son of the Wind cut the lance in two with a razor-headed arrow
and cut the axe with three arrows like a sesame stalk. The very strong one split
the shataghni with nine heron-feathered arrows, cut the arrow loosed by the
Madra king, and at once cut the spear hurled by Bhagadatta in battle. Bhimasena,
who gloried in battle, cut each of the other terrible arrows in three with
arrows with bent joints, and struck all those great archers with three each.

"Then, while that great battle went on, Dhananjaya came there by chariot in
battle, seeing the great chariot fighter Bhima killing enemies in battle and
fighting them with arrows. Seeing the two great-souled Pandavas together there,
your men, bull among men, lost hope of victory. Arjuna was fighting the great
chariot fighter Bhishma in battle, with Shikhandi in front, wanting Bhishma's
death. Then he came up against your ten warriors who stood fighting Bhima in
battle, king. Bibhatsu pierced them, wanting to please Bhima. Then King
Duryodhana urged on Susharma to kill both Arjuna and Bhimasena. 'Susharma, go
quickly, surrounded by floods of troops, and kill these two sons of Pandu,
Dhananjaya and Wolf-belly.' Hearing his command, the Trigarta, lord of
Prasthala, ran at the two bowmen, Bhima and Arjuna, in battle and surrounded
them on every side with many thousands of chariots. Then the battle began
between Arjuna and the enemy.

---

"Arjuna covered the great chariot fighter Shalya, as he strove in battle, with
arrows with bent joints. He struck Susharma and Kripa with three each. With
three arrows each, fitted with heron and peacock feathers, he struck the
Pragjyotisha in battle, Jayadratha of Sindhu, Chitrasena, Vikarna, Kritavarma,
Durmarshana, king of kings, and the two great chariot fighters of Avanti. So the
atiratha pressed your army in battle. Jayadratha, standing on Chitrasena's
chariot, split Partha with arrows, Bharata, and pierced Bhima with force. Shalya
and Kripa, the best of chariot fighters, pierced the strong-armed Jishnu in
battle in many ways with arrows that split the vital points. Your sons led by
Chitrasena, lord of the people, quickly struck Arjuna and Bhimasena in battle
with five sharp arrows each, sir.

"There those two best of chariot fighters, the Kaunteyas, bulls of the Bharatas,
pressed the great army of the Trigartas in battle. Susharma too pierced Partha
in battle with many iron arrows and roared a strong roar, making the sky
resound. The other brave chariot fighters pierced Bhimasena and Dhananjaya with
sharp, straight, gold-feathered arrows. The two Kaunteyas were the best of
chariot fighters, noble and of wonderful form. In the middle of those chariot
fighters they shone as if playing, like two lions, fierce in strength, wanting
meat among cattle. Cutting the bows and many arrows of the heroes in battle, the
two heroes brought down the heads of men in hundreds. Many chariots were broken,
horses killed in hundreds, and elephants with their riders fell to the earth in
the great battle. Chariot fighters and horsemen were seen killed here and there,
writhing in many ways on every side, king. The earth was strewn with killed
floods of elephants and foot soldiers, with horses killed, and with chariots
broken in many ways. Parasols were cut in many ways and banners brought down.
Goads and saddle-cloths were thrown aside, Bharata, with bracelets, armlets and
necklaces and trampled deerskins. Turbans and yak-tail fans were thrown aside.
Arms smeared with sandal paste and the thighs of kings lay thrown here and
there, and so the earth was strewn. There we saw Partha's wonderful courage in
battle, for he warded off those heroes with arrows and killed your army.

"Your son, seeing the meeting of Bhima and Arjuna, went in that great danger
close to the chariot of Ganga's son. Kripa, Kritavarma, Jayadratha of Sindhu,
and Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti came into the fight then. Then the great archer
Bhima and the great chariot fighter Phalguna drove the terrible army of the
Kauravas hard in battle. Then tens of thousands, tens of millions of
peacock-feathered arrows fell quickly on Dhananjaya's chariot in battle. Warding
off those great chariot fighters with a net of arrows, Partha sent them on every
side to death in battle. Shalya, the great chariot fighter, as if playing, in
anger struck Jishnu in the chest in battle with broad arrows with bent joints.
Partha cut his bow and his hand guard with five arrows, and then pierced him
hard in the vitals with sharp arrows. The lord of the Madras took another bow
that could bear the load in battle. In fury he struck Jishnu with three arrows,
great king, and Vasudeva with five, and fixed nine in Bhimasena's arms and
chest.

"Then, great king, Duryodhana sent Drona and the great chariot fighter of
Magadha. They came to the place where Partha and Bhimasena the Pandava, the two
great chariot fighters, were killing the great army of the Kauravya. Young
Jayatsena pierced Bhima of the terrible weapons in battle with eight sharp
arrows, bull of the Bharatas. Bhima pierced him with ten and again with seven,
and with a broad arrow carried his charioteer off the chariot seat. With his
horses bolting and running on every side, the Magadha king was carried away
while the whole army watched. Drona, finding an opening, pierced Bhimasena with
sixty-five very sharp iron arrows. Bhima, who gloried in battle, pierced his
teacher, like a father to him in battle, with nine broad arrows and then sixty,
Bharata. Arjuna, having pierced Susharma with many iron arrows, blew his army
away as the wind blows away great clouds.

"Then Bhishma and the king, and Brihadbala son of Subala, ran in anger at
Bhimasena and Dhananjaya. So too the brave Pandavas and Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's grandson, ran in battle at Bhishma, like Death with his mouth open.
Shikhandi, reaching the grandfather of the Bharatas, ran at him gladly, giving
up his fear of him of the controlled vow. The Parthas led by Yudhishthira, with
Shikhandi in front, united with the Srinjayas, fought Bhishma in battle. In the
same way all your men, with him of the controlled vow in front, fought in battle
the Parthas led by Shikhandi.

"Then a battle broke out, fearful for the Kauravas, there with the sons of
Pandu, over Bhishma's victory. For your men, lord of the people, Bhishma was the
stake in battle. There the dice game had come round again, to victory or the
other. Dhrishtadyumna, great king, urged on all the troops: 'Run at the son of
Ganga! Do not be afraid, best of men!' Hearing the words of the commander of the
army, the army of the Pandavas went quickly at Bhishma alone, giving up their
lives in the great battle. Bhishma too, the best of chariot fighters, received
that army as it came on, great king, as the great ocean receives the shore."

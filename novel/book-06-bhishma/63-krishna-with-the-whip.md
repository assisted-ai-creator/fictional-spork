# Krishna with the Whip

"Duryodhana saw Bhishma angry in battle," said Sanjaya, "surrounded by the
Pandavas like the sun in the sky by clouds at the end of the hot season. Then,
great king, he said to Duhshasana, 'This brave great archer Bhishma, killer of
enemies, is covered on every side by the brave Pandavas, bull of the Bharatas.
You must protect the great-souled one, hero. If Bhishma our grandfather is
protected in battle, he will kill the Panchalas with the Pandavas as they
strive. I think the task is to protect Bhishma above all. For this great archer
Bhishma, our grandfather, is our protector. So surround the grandfather with the
whole army and protect him as he does deeds hard to do in battle.' So addressed
in battle, your son Duhshasana surrounded Bhishma and stood there, surrounded by
a great army.

"Then Subala's son came with a hundred thousand horses, proud, very swift,
strong and bannered. They were ridden by the best of men, trained and skilled in
battle, with clean spears in their hands, carrying lances and javelins. They
surrounded the best of men, Nakula, Sahadeva and Yudhishthira the Pandava, King
of Dharma, on every side and held them off. Then King Duryodhana sent ten
thousand brave horsemen to hold off the Pandavas. As they went in with great
speed, like Garudas in battle, the earth, struck by their hooves, shook and
resounded, king. A very great sound of the horses' hooves was heard then, like
that of a great bamboo forest burning on a mountain. The great dust they raised
as they leapt reached the path of the sun and covered it. The Pandava army was
thrown into turmoil by those swift horses, like a great lake by swans landing at
great speed. With the noise of neighing nothing could be made out.

"Then King Yudhishthira and Madri's two Pandava sons struck back with force at
the charge of the horsemen in battle, great king. They were like the shore of
the great ocean, swollen by the rains, striking back the rush of water at the
full moon. Then the chariot fighters, king, cut the heads of the horsemen from
their bodies with arrows with bent joints. Killed by those firm bowmen, they
fell, great king, like great elephants killed by elephants in a mountain cave.
The horsemen too, moving in the ten directions, cut off heads with very sharp
spears and arrows with bent joints. Horsemen who came too close, bull of the
Bharatas, cut off heads with lances, like fruit from a great tree. Horses with
their riders were killed here and there, king, fallen and falling, in hundreds
and thousands. Being killed, the horses fled in fear, like deer that have met
lions, running for their lives. Having beaten the enemy in the great battle,
great king, the Pandavas blew their conches and beat their drums in battle.

"Then Duryodhana, seeing his army standing dispirited, said this to the king of
the Madras, best of the Bharatas. 'This eldest son of Pandu, uncle, has beaten
my men and is driving off the army while we watch, strong-armed one. Hold him
off, strong-armed one, as the shore holds off the sea. For you are famed as one
whose strength and courage cannot be withstood.' Hearing those words of your
son, the mighty Shalya went with a line of chariots to where King Yudhishthira
was. That great force of Shalya's came on at once with the speed of a great
flood, and the Pandava held it off in battle. The great chariot fighter, the
King of Dharma, quickly struck the king of the Madras between the breasts with
ten arrows in battle, and Nakula and Sahadeva with three straight arrows each.
The king of the Madras struck them all with three each, pierced Yudhishthira
again with sixty sharp arrows, and in fury struck Madri's sons with two each.
Then strong-armed Bhima, conqueror of enemies, seeing the king in the power of
the king of the Madras in battle, as if gone into the mouth of death, ran to
Yudhishthira in battle. Then a very terrible and most cruel battle began, as the
sun stood in the west, shining.

---

"Then your father in anger struck the Parthas and their armies on every side
with sharp, fine arrows. Having pierced Bhima with twelve arrows, Satyaki with
nine, Nakula with three and Sahadeva with seven, he fixed twelve in
Yudhishthira's arms and chest, pierced Dhrishtadyumna, and roared, the very
strong one. Nakula pierced the grandfather back with twelve, Madhava with three,
Dhrishtadyumna with seventy, Bhimasena with five and Yudhishthira with twelve.
Drona pierced Satyaki and pierced Bhimasena, each with five sharp arrows like
Yama's staff. They both pierced him back with three straight arrows each, the
bull among brahmins, as a great elephant is pierced with goads. The Sauviras,
the Kitavas, the easterners, the westerners, northerners and Malavas, the
Abhishahas, the Shurasenas, the Shibis and the Vasatis did not leave Bhishma in
battle, though struck by sharp arrows. So too others, being killed by the
great-souled Pandavas, went against the Pandavas with weapons of many kinds in
their hands. And so too, king, the Pandavas surrounded the grandfather.

"Surrounded on every side by floods of chariots, the unconquered one blazed,
burning the enemy, like a fire let loose in a thicket. Chariots were his fire
house, bows his flames, swords, spears and clubs his fuel, arrows his sparks:
the fire Bhishma burned the bulls among kshatriyas. He covered that army with
gold-feathered arrows with vulture feathers, very sharp, barbed, tubular and
iron. He brought down banners and chariot fighters with sharp arrows, and he
made the crowds of chariots like groves of palm trees with their tops lopped
off. The strong-armed best of all who bear weapons left chariots, elephants and
horses without men in battle, king. Hearing the twang of his bowstring and palm,
like the crash of a thunderbolt, all beings trembled, Bharata. Your father's
arrows fell, bull of the Bharatas, and did not fail. The arrows loosed from
Bhishma's bow did not stick in armour. We saw chariots, king, their heroes
killed, yoked to swift horses, being carried over the battlefield, great king.

"Fourteen thousand of the Chedis, Kashis and Karushas were famed as great
chariot fighters, sons of good families who would give up their bodies. None of
them turned back, and all had gold-worked banners. They met Bhishma in battle
like Death with his mouth open and sank into the other world with their horses,
chariots and elephants. We saw chariots, king, in hundreds and thousands, some
with axles and fittings broken, and some with their wheels broken on every side.
The earth was strewn with broken chariots with their fenders and with fallen
chariot fighters. Arrows lay there, fine armour cut, and axes, sir, clubs,
pestles, swords and arrows. There were chariot bottoms, quivers and broken
wheels, sir, arms, bows and swords, and heads with earrings. There were palm
guards and finger guards, fallen banners, and bows cut in many ways. Elephants
whose riders were killed, king, and horses whose riders were killed, ran there
quickly in hundreds and thousands. Though the heroes tried, they could not stop
the great chariot fighters fleeing, hard pressed by Bhishma's arrows. Killed by
one whose courage was like great Indra's, the great army broke, great king, so
that not even two ran together. With chariots, elephants and horses pierced,
with banners and chariot poles fallen, the army of Pandu's sons cried out,
senseless. There father killed son and son father, and friend, forced by the
power of fate, killed dear friend. Other soldiers of Pandu's son were seen
running with their armour thrown off and their hair loose, Bharata. The army of
Pandu's son looked like a herd of cattle in a panic, its chariots and elephants
bewildered, crying out in distress.

"Seeing the army breaking, the joy of the Yadavas held back the fine chariot and
said to Partha Bibhatsu, 'Partha, the time you wished for has come. Strike him,
tiger among men, unless you are lost in confusion. Hero, long ago you spoke at
the meeting of the kings in Virata's city, Partha, near Sanjaya. You said, "I
will kill all Dhritarashtra's son's soldiers, led by Bhishma and Drona, with
their followers, all who fight me in battle." Make that word true, Kaunteya,
tamer of enemies. Remember the dharma of the kshatriya and fight, bull of the
Bharatas.'

"So addressed by Vasudeva, Bibhatsu, looking sideways and down, said this as if
unwilling. 'Which would be better for me: to kill those who should not be killed
and win a kingdom that ends in hell, or to suffer in the forest? Drive the
horses to where Bhishma is. I will do what you say. I will bring down the
unassailable old grandfather of the Kurus.'

"Then Madhava drove the silver-white horses to where Bhishma was, king, as hard
to look at as the sun. Then that great army of Yudhishthira turned back, seeing
strong-armed Partha going out against Bhishma in battle. Then Bhishma, best of
the Kurus, roaring again and again like a lion, quickly covered Dhananjaya's
chariot with showers of arrows. In a moment his chariot, with its horses and
charioteer, could not be made out at all under the great shower of arrows. But
Vasudeva the Satvata was not flustered. Steady, he drove on the horses, though
Bhishma's arrows had torn them. Then Partha took up his divine bow, which sounds
like a cloud, and cut Bhishma's bow with sharp arrows and brought it down. His
bow cut, the Kauravya, your father, strung another great bow in the space of a
blink. He drew that bow, which sounded like a cloud, with both arms. Then Arjuna
in his anger cut that bow too.

"Shantanu's son praised that lightness of his. 'Well done, Partha, strong-armed
one! Well done, son of Kunti!' Having spoken to him, Bhishma took another
beautiful bow and loosed arrows at Partha's chariot in battle. Vasudeva showed
the highest skill in driving horses, making Bhishma's arrows useless and showing
his circles. Wounded by arrows, Bhishma and Partha, tigers among men, shone like
two angry bulls marked by the scratches of horns.

"But Vasudeva saw how softly Partha fought. He saw Bhishma loosing showers of
arrows without pause in battle, blazing like the sun in the middle of the two
armies. He saw him killing the best of the best soldiers of Pandu's son,
bringing a kind of end of an age on Yudhishthira's force. The strong-armed
Madhava, killer of enemy heroes, could not bear it. Letting go of Partha's
silver-white horses, sir, the great yogin, angry, leapt down from the great
chariot. The strong one ran at Bhishma with his arms for weapons. Whip in hand,
the splendid one roared again and again like a lion, as if splitting the earth
with his feet, the lord of the world. His eyes copper-red with anger, of
measureless splendour, wanting to kill, Krishna seemed to swallow the minds of
your men in the great battle. Seeing Madhava going out against Bhishma in battle
in the tumult, the soldiers there all cried out, 'Bhishma is killed! Bhishma is
killed!' and ran from fear of Vasudeva. Clothed in yellow silk, dark as a jewel,
Janardana shone as he ran at Bhishma, like a cloud garlanded with lightning. As
a lion runs at an elephant, or a bull who leads the herd at a bull, the splendid
bull of the Yadavas ran at him, roaring.

"Bhishma saw the lotus-eyed one coming at him in battle and was not flustered.
He drew his great bow and said to Govinda with an unflustered mind, 'Come, come,
lotus-eyed one, god of gods, I bow to you! Today, best of the Satvatas, throw me
down in the great battle. For if I am killed by you in battle, god, faultless
one, there is the highest good for me, Krishna, in the other world and in this
one. I am honoured by the three worlds today in battle, Govinda.'

"Then strong-armed Partha ran close after Keshava and held him back, grasping
him with both arms. Held back by Partha, the lotus-eyed Krishna, the highest
Person, went on at speed, carrying him with him. But Partha, killer of enemy
heroes, planted his feet with force and somehow held Hrishikesha back at the
tenth step. Then Arjuna, killer of enemy heroes, in distress, said to him as he
breathed like a snake, his eyes troubled with anger, 'Turn back, strong-armed
one. You should not make your word false. You said before, "I will not fight,"
Keshava. The world will call you a liar, Madhava. This whole burden is mine. I
will kill him of the firm vow. I swear by our friendship, Madhava, by truth and
by my good deeds, that I will make an end of the enemy, destroyer of enemies.
See him of the great vow, the unassailable one, brought down this very day, like
the full moon by chance at the end of time.'

"Hearing the words of the great-souled Phalguna, Madhava said nothing, and in
anger climbed onto the chariot again. Bhishma son of Shantanu again rained a
shower of arrows on those two tigers among men standing on the chariot, as a
cloud rains on two mountains. Your father Devavrata took the lives of the
warriors, as the sun takes away all heat with its rays when the cold season has
passed. As the Pandava broke the Kuru armies in battle, so your father broke the
Pandava armies in battle. With their troops killed and fled, without spirit and
out of their minds, they could not look at Bhishma, matchless in battle, blazing
with his own splendour like the sun at midday. Being killed by Bhishma like Time
at the end of an age, great king, the Pandavas looked about them, tormented by
fear. They found no protector, like cattle sunk in mud, like weak ants crushed
by the strong in battle. They could not look at Bhishma, the great chariot
fighter, Bharata, unassailable, with his floods of arrows, scorching the kings,
his arrows his flames, like the burning sun.

"As he crushed the Pandava army, the thousand-rayed sun set. Then the minds of
the armies, worn out with toil, turned to withdrawal."

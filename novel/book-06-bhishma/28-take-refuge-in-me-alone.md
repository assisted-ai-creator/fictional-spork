# Take Refuge in Me Alone

"'Strong-armed one,' said Arjuna, 'I want to know the truth of renouncing,
Hrishikesha, and of giving up, each by itself, slayer of Keshin.'

"'Poets know renouncing as the laying aside of actions done from desire,' said
the Blessed One. 'The wise call giving up the fruit of all actions giving up.
Some thinkers say that action should be given up as faulty. Others say that the
acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be given up. Hear my
judgement on giving up, best of the Bharatas. Giving up, tiger among men, is
said to be of three kinds. The acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should
not be given up. They must be done. Sacrifice, giving and austerity purify the
wise. But even these acts should be done giving up attachment and their fruits.
That is my settled and highest view, Partha.

"'The renouncing of action that is laid down is not right. Giving it up out of
confusion is called of darkness. One who gives up an act because it is painful,
out of fear of bodily hardship, makes a giving up of passion and does not gain
the fruit of giving up. When an act laid down is done only because it must be
done, Arjuna, giving up attachment and its fruit, that giving up is held to be
of goodness. The wise man who gives up, filled with goodness, his doubts cut,
does not hate unpleasant work or cling to pleasant work. For one who bears a
body cannot give up actions entirely. But one who gives up the fruit of action
is called a man who gives up. The fruit of action is of three kinds, unwanted,
wanted and mixed. It comes after death to those who have not given up, but never
to those who have renounced.

"'Learn from me, strong-armed one, these five causes for the accomplishing of
all actions, taught in the Samkhya teaching: the seat, the doer, the various
instruments, the various separate efforts, and fate, the fifth. Whatever act a
man undertakes with body, speech or mind, right or wrong, these five are its
causes. Since this is so, the fool who, through untrained understanding, sees
himself alone as the doer does not see. One who has no sense of "I did it",
whose understanding is not stained, even if he kills these people does not kill
and is not bound.

"'Knowledge, what is known and the knower are the threefold urge to action. The
instrument, the act and the doer are the threefold gathering of action.
Knowledge, action and the doer are said in the counting of the qualities to be
of three kinds each, according to the qualities. Hear of them as they are. The
knowledge by which one sees one undying being in all beings, undivided in the
divided, know to be of goodness. The knowledge that sees in all beings different
states of different kinds, as separate, know to be of passion. And the knowledge
that clings to one single effect as if it were the whole, without reason,
without grasp of the truth, and narrow, is said to be of darkness.

"'Action laid down, done without attachment, without passion or hatred, by one
who does not want its fruit, is said to be of goodness. But action done with
great strain by one who wants his desires, or with self-regard, is said to be of
passion. Action begun out of confusion, without regard to consequences, loss,
harm or one's own power, is said to be of darkness. The doer free of attachment,
not speaking of "I", full of steadiness and zeal, unchanged in success and
failure, is said to be of goodness. The doer full of passion, wanting the fruit
of action, greedy, harmful, impure, given to joy and grief, is called of
passion. The doer who is undisciplined, vulgar, stubborn, deceitful, dishonest,
lazy, despondent and slow is said to be of darkness.

"'Hear now, Dhananjaya, the threefold division of understanding and of
steadiness, according to the qualities, told fully and one by one. The
understanding that knows action and inaction, what should and should not be
done, fear and fearlessness, bondage and release, is of goodness, Partha. The
understanding by which one knows wrongly dharma and adharma, what should and
should not be done, is of passion, Partha. The understanding that, covered by
darkness, thinks adharma is dharma and sees all things upside down is of
darkness, Partha. The steadiness by which one holds the workings of mind, breath
and senses, with yoga that never strays, is of goodness, Partha. The steadiness
by which one holds to dharma, pleasure and wealth, Arjuna, wanting the fruit,
with attachment, is of passion, Partha. The steadiness by which the fool does
not let go of sleep, fear, grief, despondency and pride is of darkness, Partha.

"'And now hear from me, bull of the Bharatas, of the three kinds of happiness.
The happiness in which one delights by practice and comes to the end of pain,
which is like poison at first and like amrita in the end, born of the clearness
of one's own understanding, is called of goodness. The happiness that comes from
the meeting of the senses with their objects, like amrita at first and like
poison in the end, is held to be of passion. The happiness that confuses the
self at first and after, arising from sleep, sloth and carelessness, is said to
be of darkness. There is no being on earth, or in heaven among the gods, that is
free of these three qualities born of nature.

"'The works of brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras, scorcher of enemies,
are divided by the qualities born of their nature. Calm, self-control,
austerity, purity, patience, uprightness, knowledge, discernment and faith are
the work of a brahmin, born of his nature. Bravery, splendour, steadiness,
skill, not fleeing in battle, giving and lordship are the work of a kshatriya,
born of his nature. Farming, keeping cattle and trade are the work of a vaishya,
born of his nature. Work that is service is the work of a shudra, born of his
nature too. Each man, devoted to his own work, reaches perfection. Hear how one
devoted to his own work finds perfection. By worshipping with his own work him
from whom beings come forth, by whom all this is pervaded, a man finds
perfection. One's own dharma, even without merit, is better than another's
dharma well done. Doing the work laid down by one's nature, one incurs no sin.
One should not give up the work one is born to, son of Kunti, even if it has
faults. For all undertakings are covered by fault, as fire by smoke. One whose
understanding is unattached everywhere, who has conquered himself, free of
longing, reaches by renouncing the highest perfection of freedom from action.

"'Learn from me in brief, son of Kunti, how one who has reached perfection
reaches Brahman, the highest state of knowledge. He is joined to a purified
understanding and controls the self by steadiness. He gives up sound and the
other objects of the senses and casts off passion and hatred. He lives in
solitude, eats little, controls speech, body and mind, and is always intent on
the yoga of meditation, taking refuge in detachment. Releasing self-regard,
force, arrogance, desire, anger and possession, without "mine", at peace, he is
fit to become Brahman. Become Brahman, his self clear, he neither grieves nor
longs. The same towards all beings, he gains the highest devotion to me. By
devotion he knows me truly, how great I am and who I am. Then, knowing me truly,
he enters me at once. Always doing all actions, taking refuge in me, by my grace
he reaches the eternal, undying place.

"'Renouncing all actions in me in your thought, intent on me, taking refuge in
the yoga of understanding, keep your thought always on me. With your thought on
me, you will cross all difficulties by my grace. But if out of self-regard you
will not listen, you will perish. If, taking refuge in self-regard, you think,
"I will not fight," your resolve is false. Your nature will compel you. Bound by
your own work, born of your nature, son of Kunti, you will do even against your
will what in your confusion you do not want to do. The lord stands in the heart
of all beings, Arjuna, making all beings turn by his power, as if mounted on a
machine. Go to him for refuge with your whole being, Bharata. By his grace you
will reach the highest peace, the eternal place. So I have told you knowledge
more secret than the secret. Think it over fully, and do as you wish.

"'Hear again my highest word, the most secret of all. You are dearly loved by
me, and so I will tell you what is good for you. Let your mind be on me, be
devoted to me, sacrifice to me, bow to me. You will come to me. I promise you
truly, for you are dear to me. Give up all dharmas and take refuge in me alone.
I will free you from all sins. Do not grieve.

"'This is never to be told to one without austerity, nor to one without
devotion, nor to one who does not want to hear, nor to one who finds fault with
me. One who tells this highest secret to my devotees, showing the highest
devotion to me, will come to me without doubt. No one among men does what is
dearer to me than he, and no one on earth will be dearer to me than he. And one
who studies this dialogue of ours, in keeping with dharma, will have worshipped
me with the sacrifice of knowledge. That is my view. And a man who only hears
it, with faith and without finding fault, he too will be freed and reach the
happy worlds of those who do good. Have you heard this, Partha, with a mind
fixed on one point? Has your confusion born of ignorance been destroyed,
Dhananjaya?'

"'My confusion is destroyed,' said Arjuna, 'and by your grace I have regained my
memory, Achyuta. I stand firm, my doubts gone. I will do as you say.'"

"So I heard this wonderful dialogue of Vasudeva and the great Partha," said
Sanjaya, "which makes the hair stand on end. By Vyasa's grace I heard this
highest secret, this yoga, from Krishna, the lord of yoga, himself speaking it
before my eyes. King, as I remember again and again this wonderful, holy
dialogue of Keshava and Arjuna, I rejoice again and again. And as I remember
again and again that most wonderful form of Hari, great is my wonder, king, and
I rejoice again and again. Where Krishna, lord of yoga, is, and where Partha the
archer is, there are fortune, victory, prosperity and sure policy. That is my
view."

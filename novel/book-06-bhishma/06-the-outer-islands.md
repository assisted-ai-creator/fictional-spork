# The Outer Islands

"Tell me in full, suta, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "the measure of life in
this land of Bharata and in Haimavata, and its good and bad fruits, past, future
and present, and of Harivarsha too."

"In the land of Bharata there are four ages, bull of the Bharatas," said
Sanjaya: "the Krita, the Treta, the Dvapara and the Pushya, you who make the
Kurus grow. First comes the age called Krita, then the Treta age, lord. After
the Dvapara has run its short course, the Pushya begins. In the Krita age the
span of life is counted at four thousand years, best of the Kurus, best of
kings. In the Treta it is three thousand, lord of men, and in the Dvapara two
thousand. Now it stands at a hundred. There is no fixed measure in this Pushya,
bull of the Bharatas. Here they die in the womb, and they die after they are
born. In the Krita, king, very strong, great-spirited sages were born, rich in
austerity, with all the virtues of offspring. Men of great energy were born in
the Krita age, king, great-souled, devoted to dharma, speaking the truth,
wealthy and lovely to see. In the Treta, kshatriyas are born, long-lived, of
great courage, the best of archers in battle, brave, emperors. In the Dvapara,
great king, all the classes are born, of great energy and great courage, wanting
to kill one another. In the Pushya men are born with little energy, angry,
greedy and false, Bharata. Envy, pride, anger, deceit and malice, Bharata,
passion and greed arise in mortals in the Pushya. Now the shortening is going
on, king, in this Dvapara, lord of men. Haimavata surpasses it in virtues, and
Harivarsha is beyond that.

---

"You have told me of the Jambu continent as it is, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra.
"Tell me truly its breadth and extent, and the measure of the ocean, you whose
sight is without fault. Tell me of Shakadvipa and Kushadvipa, Sanjaya, and of
Shalmala and Kraunchadvipa truly. Tell me all, son of Gavalgana, and of Rahu,
the moon and the sun."

"King, there are very many islands by which this world is spread," said Sanjaya.
"But I will tell you of seven, and of the moon, the sun and the planets. The
Jambu mountain is eighteen thousand six hundred yojanas broad, lord of the
people. The breadth of the salt ocean is said to be twice that. It is round,
crowded with many peoples, bright with jewels and coral, adorned with mountains
bright with many ores, and full of perfected ones and charanas.

"I will tell you of Shakadvipa as it is, lord of the earth. Listen to me as I
tell it properly, joy of the Kurus. It is twice the measure of Jambudvipa in
breadth, lord of men, great king, and so is the ocean that surrounds it in its
part, best of the Bharatas, the ocean of milk. There the countries are holy, and
the people do not die there. How could there be famine? They have patience and
energy. That is Shakadvipa in brief, as it is, bull of the Bharatas, great king.
What else do you want to hear?"

"You have told me of Shakadvipa in brief, as it is, Sanjaya, fortunate one,"
said Dhritarashtra. "Now tell it in full and truly."

"There are seven mountains there too, king, adorned with jewels," said Sanjaya,
"and rivers that are mines of jewels. Hear their names. Everything there is very
rich in virtues and holy, lord of the people. The highest is called Meru, with
gods, seers and gandharvas. Stretching to the east, great king, is the mountain
called Malaya, from which the clouds go forth and arise everywhere. Beyond it,
Kauravya, is the great mountain Jaladhara, where Vasava always takes the finest
water and from which the rain comes in the rainy season, lord of men. There is
the high mountain Raivataka, on which the constellation Revati is always fixed
in the sky, an arrangement made by the grandfather. To the north, king of kings,
is the great mountain called Shyama, from which the people have come to be dark,
lord of the people."

"Sanjaya, I have a great doubt about what you have just said," said
Dhritarashtra. "How have the people here come to be dark, son of a suta?"

"In all the islands, very wise one, joy of the Kurus," said Sanjaya, "there are
two colours, fair and black, and the dark colour lies between them, king. I will
tell you why it came about, Bharata. The blessed Krishna lives there, and by his
radiance it became dark.

"Beyond that, lord of the Kauravas, is the mountain Durgashaila, of great rise,
and Kesarin, with its filaments, from which the wind blows. Their breadth in
yojanas is twice as great, part by part. The wise have named the lands on them,
Kauravya. Mahameru is Mahakasha, Jalada is Kumudottara. Beyond Jaladhara, king,
it is known as Sukumara. Raivata's is Kaumara, Shyama's is Manichaka, Kesara's
is Modaki, and beyond that is Mahapuman. Surrounding it, Kauravya, in length and
shortness, it is known like Jambudvipa, and in its middle is a great tree. The
tree called Shaka, great king, is in the middle of that island. There the
countries are holy, and there Shamkara is worshipped. The perfected ones, the
charanas and the gods go there.

"The people there are devoted to dharma, king, and the four classes, Bharata,
keep strictly to their own work. No thief is seen there. They live long, great
king, free of old age and death. The people there grow like rivers in the rains.
The rivers there have holy water, and the Ganga goes there in many ways: the
Sukumari, the Kumari, the Sita, the Kaverka, the Mahanadi, Kauravya, the river
Manijala and the Ikshuvardhanika, best of the Bharatas. From there flow rivers
of holy water by the hundred thousand, upholder of the Kuru family, from which
Vasava rains. Their names and their measure cannot be counted, for they are
holy, the best of rivers.

"There are four holy countries there, honoured by the world: the Magas, the
Mashakas, the Manasas and the Mandagas. The Magas are mostly brahmins, devoted
to their own work, king. Among the Mashakas are kshatriyas, devoted to dharma,
granting every wish. Among the Manasas, great king, are vaishyas, who live by
their work, with every desire fulfilled, brave and settled in dharma and
purpose. And in Mandaga are shudras, men always devoted to dharma. There is no
king there, king of kings, no rod and no one who punishes. They protect dharma
by their own dharma, one another. So much can be told of that island, and so
much should be heard of mighty Shakadvipa.

---

"In the northern islands, Kauravya, a story is heard. Hear me tell it, great
king, as I heard it. There is an ocean of clarified butter there, another of
thin curds, the ocean of wine, and another, the ocean of heat. All the islands
are twice the size of one another, lord of men, and all are surrounded by
mountains, great king. In the middle island is the great mountain Gaura, of red
arsenic. The western mountain is Krishna, like Narayana, king. There Keshava
himself guards the heavenly jewels. Waiting on Prajapati, he grants happiness to
the people.

"In Kushadvipa a clump of kusha grass in the middle of the country is
worshipped, and the Shalmali tree in the Shalmalika island, king. In
Kraunchadvipa the great mountain Krauncha, a mine of heaps of jewels, is always
worshipped by the four classes, great king. The very great mountain Gomanda,
king, with every ore, is where the glorious lotus-eyed one always lives, the
lord Narayana Hari, always praised by those who seek release. In Kushadvipa,
king of kings, is a mountain covered in coral. Sudhaman is its name, hard to
withstand, a second mountain of gold. The third, Kauravya, is called Dyutimat,
the mountain Kumuda. The fourth is called Pushpavat, and the fifth Kusheshaya.
The sixth is called Harigiri. These are the six best mountains. The space
between them is twice as great, part by part. The first land is Audbhida, the
second Venumandala, the third Rathakara, and the fourth is called Palana. The
fifth land is Dhritimat, the sixth land Prabhakara, and the seventh land Kapila.
These are the seven clusters of lands. In them, lord of the world, gods,
gandharvas and people play and take their pleasure, and no one dies in them.
There are no robbers in them, nor mleccha tribes, king. All the people are
mostly fair and delicate, lord of the earth.

"I will tell you of the remaining lands, lord of men, as I have heard them,
great king. Hear it with a calm mind. In Kraunchadvipa, great king, is the great
mountain called Krauncha. Beyond Krauncha is Vamanaka, beyond Vamana
Andhakaraka, and beyond Andhakara, king, Mainaka, the best of mountains. Beyond
Mainaka, king, is Govinda, the best of mountains, and beyond Govinda, king, the
mountain called Nibida. The breadth between them is twice as great, you who make
the line grow. I will tell you the countries there. Hear me as I tell them.
Krauncha's country is Kushala, Vamana's is Manonuga. Beyond Manonuga is the
country Ushna, upholder of the Kuru family. Beyond Ushna is Pravaraka, and
beyond Pravara Andhakaraka. Beyond the country Andhakaraka is said to be
Munidesha, and beyond Munidesha is said to be Dundubhisvana, crowded with
perfected ones and charanas, its people mostly fair, lord of the people. These
countries, great king, are served by gods and gandharvas.

"In Pushkara is the mountain called Pushkara, rich in gems and jewels. There the
god Prajapati himself always lives. All the gods with the great seers always
wait on him, worshipping him with words that please his mind, lord of the
people. From Jambudvipa jewels of many kinds go out to the people in all those
islands, joy of the Kurus. In brahmins' celibacy, truth and self-control, and in
health and length of life, each is twice the one before. There is one country in
these islands, king, Bharata, and in the countries named, one dharma is seen.
The Lord, Prajapati himself, raising the rod, always stands guarding these
islands, great king. He is king, he is Shiva, king, he is father and
grandfather. He protects the people, best of men, foolish and wise. Food comes
to the people there of itself, Kauravya, already cooked, and they always eat it
there, great king.

"Beyond that is seen the order of the world called Sama. It is square, great
king, with thirty-three circles. There stand, Kauravya, the four elephants of
the quarters, honoured by the world, best of the Bharatas: Vamana, Airavata and
the others, and Supratika, king, with its temples and mouth running with rut. I
cannot count its measure here, for it is always beyond counting, across, above
and below. There the winds blow from all the quarters, unchecked, great king,
and those elephants hold them back. With their trunks, like lotuses, huge and
very bright, they slowly and quickly let the winds go again, always. The winds
let go by the elephants of the quarters as they breathe come here, great king,
and so the people live."

---

"You have told the distant expanse in very great detail, Sanjaya," said
Dhritarashtra, "and shown the arrangement of the islands. Tell me what follows,
Sanjaya."

"I have told you of the islands, great king," said Sanjaya. "Hear the planets
truly, and Svarbhanu, best of the Kauravas, as great as he is in power. The
planet Svarbhanu is said to be round, great king. His breadth is twelve thousand
yojanas, sinless one, his girth thirty-six thousand, and the learned who know
the old lore say his extent is six thousand. The moon, king, is said to be
eleven thousand yojanas broad, best of the Kurus, thirty-three thousand round,
and fifty-nine hundred in extent, that great one of cool rays. The sun is eight
thousand and two thousand more broad, joy of the Kurus, and thirty thousand
round, king, and fifty-eight in extent, sinless one: so the very noble bird, the
bright one, is heard to be. This measure of the sun is given here, Bharata.
Rahu, by his greatness, covers both the moon and the sun in their time, great
king. This has been told in brief.

"So, great king, all that you asked has been told truly with the eye of
learning. Be at peace. I have told this world with its end, as I have seen it.
So take comfort, Kauravya, about your son Duryodhana. Hearing this pleasing Book
of the Earth, best of the Bharatas, a prince becomes glorious, wins his aims and
is honoured by the good. His life, strength, courage and energy grow. One who
keeps his vows and hears this at the changes of the moon, lord of the earth,
pleases his fathers and grandfathers. You have heard all of this land of Bharata
where we live, in which merit first goes forward."

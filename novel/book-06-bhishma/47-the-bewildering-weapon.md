# The Bewildering Weapon

"This disaster has come on you, king," said Sanjaya, "through your own fault.
Duryodhana does not see the wrongs that you have seen, king, done through a
confusion of dharma, bull of the Bharatas. Through your fault the dice game
happened long ago, lord of the people. Through your fault the war with the
Pandavas has begun. Now you yourself eat the fruit of the wrong you did. For a
deed done by oneself is reaped by oneself, here or after death. What you have
got, king, is just what was due. So steady yourself, king, now that this great
disaster has come, and hear the battle as it went, sir, as I tell it.

"Bhimasena, having split the great army with sharp arrows, reached all
Duryodhana's younger brothers. Duhshasana, Durvishaha, Duhsaha, Durmada, Jaya,
Jayatsena, Vikarna, Chitrasena, Sudarshana, Charuchitra, Suvarma, Dushkarna and
Karna: seeing these and many other great chariot fighters, Dhritarashtra's sons,
very angry and close at hand, the very strong Bhima went into the great army
that Bhishma guarded in battle. Then they called to one another: 'Here is
Wolf-belly! Let us kings take him alive.'

"Surrounded by those brothers, firm in their resolve, Partha was like the sun
surrounded by cruel great planets at the destruction of creatures. Having
reached the middle of the array, the Pandava felt no fear, any more than great
Indra did when he reached the danavas in the battle of the gods and asuras. Then
hundreds of thousands of chariot fighters, lord, all followed that one man,
covering him with terrible arrows. Taking no thought for Dhritarashtra's sons,
the hero killed their best warriors in battle, on elephants, on horses and
chariots, and their horsemen. Knowing the purpose of those who wanted to seize
him, king, the great-minded Bhimasena made up his mind to kill them all. Then
the Pandava left his chariot, took up his club and struck that great ocean of
the floods of Dhritarashtra's sons' troops.

"When Bhimasena had gone in, Dhrishtadyumna too, Prishata's grandson, left Drona
and went quickly to where Subala's son was. Splitting your great army, the bull
among men came on Bhimasena's empty chariot in battle. Seeing Vishoka,
Bhimasena's charioteer, in the battle, Dhrishtadyumna, great king, was dismayed
and lost his wits. Choked with tears, his voice failing, he asked in his
distress, 'Where is Bhima, who is dearer to me than life?'

"Vishoka, joining his palms, said to Dhrishtadyumna, 'The strong and mighty
Pandava set me here and went into this great ocean of Dhritarashtra's sons'
troops. He said these words to me with affection, tiger among men: "Wait for me,
charioteer, holding the horses in check for a moment, while I quickly kill these
men who have risen to kill me." Then we saw the very strong one running with his
club in his hand, and a clash arose among all the armies. In that tumultuous,
frightening battle, king, your friend broke into the great array and went in.'

"Hearing Vishoka's words, the very strong Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson,
answered the charioteer in the middle of the battle. 'I have no use for life
today, charioteer, if I leave Bhimasena in battle and give up my love for the
Pandavas. If I go without Bhima, what will the kshatriyas say of me, with Bhima
gone into the one road and me standing back in battle? The gods led by Agni
bring ill fortune on the man who abandons his allies and goes home safe. Bhima
is my friend and my kinsman, very strong. He is devoted to us, and I am devoted
to him, the destroyer of enemies. So I will go where Wolf-belly has gone. Watch
me kill the enemy, as Vasava kills the danavas.'

"Having said this, the hero went through the middle of the Bharata army along
Bhimasena's tracks, marked by elephants crushed by his club. Then he saw Bhima
burning the enemy army, breaking the kings in battle by force, as the wind
breaks trees. Being killed in battle, chariot fighters and horsemen, foot
soldiers and elephants made a great cry of distress. A cry of grief rose from
your army, sir, as it was killed by Bhimasena, skilled and fighting in many
ways. Then all those skilled in weapons surrounded Wolf-belly and closed on him
fearlessly on every side with a rain of weapons.

"The strong grandson of Prishata saw Bhimasena, the best of those who bear
weapons, attacked on every side by heroes of the world with a terrible, closely
gathered army. He came up to him, on foot, his limbs torn by arrows, vomiting
the poison of his anger, club in hand like Time at the time of the end.
Prishata's grandson comforted Bhimasena. The great-souled one quickly pulled out
his arrows and took him up on his own chariot. He embraced Bhimasena hard and
comforted him in the middle of the enemy.

"Then your son went to his brothers in that great clash. 'This evil-hearted son
of Drupada has joined Bhimasena,' he said. 'All of you go together to kill him.
Do not let the enemy get at our army.' Hearing that, and unable to bear it,
urged on by the command of their eldest, Dhritarashtra's sons rushed out with
weapons raised to kill him, like fierce comets at the end of an age. The heroes
took up their bright bows, making the earth shake with the twang of bowstrings
and the noise of chariot rims. They rained arrows on Drupada's son, as clouds
rain floods of water on a mountain. He killed them too with very sharp arrows,
and the wonderful fighter did not falter in battle.

"The young son of Drupada, the great chariot fighter, saw your sons rising
against him, heroes standing all around him in battle. He wanted to kill them.
In great anger at your sons, king, like great Indra against the daityas in
battle, he used the fierce bewildering weapon. Then the heroes among men were
bewildered in battle, their minds and spirits struck by the bewildering weapon.
All the Kurus ran on every side with their horses, elephants and chariots,
seeing your sons bewildered, unconscious, as if their time had come.

"At that very time Drona, the best of those who bear weapons, came up against
Drupada and pierced him with three cruel arrows. Badly wounded by Drona in
battle, king, King Drupada went away, remembering their old enmity. Having
beaten Drupada, the mighty Drona blew his conch, and at the sound of his conch
all the Somakas trembled. Then the splendid Drona, the best of those who bear
weapons, heard that your sons had been bewildered in battle by the bewildering
weapon. Then Drona, eager for the princes, quickly left that fight. There the
mighty great archer, Bharadvaja's son, saw Dhrishtadyumna and Bhima moving about
the great battle. The great chariot fighter saw your sons overcome by the
stupor. Then he took up the weapon of understanding and undid the bewildering
weapon. When their life came back, your sons, great chariot fighters, went again
to fight Bhima and Prishata's grandson in battle.

"Then Yudhishthira called his soldiers together and said, 'Let them follow the
tracks of Bhima and Prishata's grandson in battle as best they can. Let twelve
heroes, chariot fighters in armour, led by Subhadra's son, find out what has
happened, for my mind is not at ease.' So permitted, the brave men of bold
fighting, all of them proud of their manhood, said, 'Very well,' and all went
out when the sun had reached midday. The Kekayas, the sons of Draupadi and the
mighty Dhrishtaketu, with Abhimanyu in front, were surrounded by a great army.
Making the needle-mouthed array in battle, the tamers of enemies split the
chariot force of Dhritarashtra's sons. Possessed by fear of Bhimasena and
bewildered by Dhrishtadyumna, your army, lord of the people, could not hold back
those great archers led by Abhimanyu as they advanced. It was like a woman
standing in the road, faint and drunk. The great archers with banners worked in
gold, wanting to rescue Dhrishtadyumna and Wolf-belly, ran at them. And those
two, seeing the great archers led by Abhimanyu, were filled with joy and killed
your army.

"Then the hero, the Panchala prince, Prishata's grandson, seeing his own teacher
coming at speed, gave up hope of killing your sons. Then he put Wolf-belly on
the Kekaya's chariot and ran in great anger at Drona, master of arrows and
weapons. As he quickly fell on him, the mighty son of Bharadvaja, destroyer of
enemies, in anger cut his bow with a broad arrow. He sent hundreds of other
arrows at Prishata's grandson, for Duryodhana's good, remembering his master's
bread. Then Prishata's grandson, killer of enemy heroes, took up another bow and
pierced Drona with seventy stone-whetted, gold-feathered arrows. Drona, the
destroyer of enemies, cut his bow again. With four fine arrows the mighty one
quickly sent his four horses to the terrible home of Vivasvat's son. He sent his
charioteer to death with a broad arrow. The strong-armed great chariot fighter
quickly leapt down from the chariot with its horses killed and climbed onto
Abhimanyu's great chariot.

"Then the army, with its chariots, elephants and horses, shook while Bhimasena
and Prishata's grandson watched. Seeing that army broken by Drona of measureless
splendour, none of those great chariot fighters together could stop it. Being
killed by Drona with sharp arrows, that army whirled about here and there like
the ocean stirred up. Seeing the army like that, your force rejoiced. Seeing the
teacher in his anger burning the enemy army, the warriors on every side cried,
'Well done! Well done!' Bharata."

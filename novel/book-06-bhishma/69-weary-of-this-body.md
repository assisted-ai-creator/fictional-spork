# Weary of This Body

Dhritarashtra said, "How did Bhishma son of Shantanu fight on the tenth day,
Sanjaya, with the very mighty Pandavas and the Srinjayas? And how did the Kurus
hold off the Pandavas in battle? Tell me of the great battle of Bhishma, who
shone in battle."

"I will tell you, as you listen, Bharata," said Sanjaya, "how the Kurus fought
with the Pandavas, and how that battle went. Day after day, the crowds of your
chariots that met the diademed one were sent to the other world by his supreme
weapons. True to his promise, the Kauravya Bhishma, who conquers in combat, made
continual slaughter of the Parthas in combat. Seeing Bhishma, the great chariot
fighter, fighting with the Kurus, and Arjuna with the Panchalas, people were in
doubt. On that tenth day, at the meeting of Bhishma and Arjuna, a very fierce
and continual slaughter went on. There, king, Bhishma son of Shantanu, scorcher
of enemies, who knew the supreme weapons, killed warriors in tens of thousands
and more. Brave men whose names and families were unknown, all of them who did
not turn back, were killed there by Bhishma.

"Having scorched the Pandava army for ten days, the righteous Bhishma, scorcher
of enemies, grew weary of life. He wanted his own death quickly, facing them in
battle. He thought, 'I will not kill the best of men in battle when they face
me.' Then your strong-armed father Devavrata, great king, said to the Pandava
who stood near him, 'Yudhishthira, very wise one, learned in all the sciences,
hear my words, son, which are righteous and lead to heaven. I am very weary of
this body, son, Bharata. My time has passed in killing very many living beings
in battle. So put Partha in front, with the Panchalas and the Srinjayas, and
strive for my death, if you want to do what I want.'

"Knowing his mind, the Pandava who sees the truth went out against Bhishma in
battle, ready, with the Srinjayas. Having heard those words of Bhishma,
Dhrishtadyumna, king, and Yudhishthira the Pandava urged on the army. 'Run at
him! Fight! Conquer Bhishma in battle, protected by Jishnu, true to his word,
who conquers his enemies. And this great archer, Prishata's grandson, the lord
of the army, and Bhimasena will surely guard you in battle. You need have no
fear at all of Bhishma in battle, Srinjayas. We will surely conquer Bhishma,
with Shikhandi in front.'

"Having made that pact on the tenth day, the Pandavas, intent on the world of
Brahma, wild with anger, came together. With Shikhandi and Dhananjaya the
Pandava in front, they made the greatest effort to bring Bhishma down. Then your
son appointed the very strong lords of many countries, with Drona and his son
and their armies. They and the strong Duhshasana with all his brothers guarded
Bhishma in the middle of the battle. Then your brave men, with him of the
controlled vow in front, fought in battle the Parthas led by Shikhandi.

"With the Chedis and the Panchalas, the one with the monkey banner went against
Bhishma son of Shantanu, with Shikhandi in front. Shini's grandson fought
Drona's son, Dhrishtaketu the Paurava, and Yudhamanyu fought Duryodhana and his
ministers. Virata, scorcher of enemies, with his army, came up against
Jayadratha with his army, the heir of Vriddhakshatra. Yudhishthira, protected by
Bhimasena, went against the great archer, the Madra king with his army, and
against the force of elephants. The Panchala with the Somakas went, ready,
against Drona, the best of all who bear weapons, unassailable and not to be held
back. Prince Brihadbala with the lion banner, the tamer of enemies, went out
against Subhadra's son, whose banner bore a karnikara tree. Your sons with the
kings fell on Shikhandi and Dhananjaya the Pandava in battle, wanting to kill
them.

"In that very great and terrible show of courage by the two armies, as the ranks
ran at one another, the earth shook. The ranks of yours and the enemy's,
Bharata, closed with one another on seeing Shantanu's son in battle. Then, as
they strove and ran at one another, a great noise arose in all the quarters,
Bharata. With the sound of conches and drums, the trumpeting of elephants and
the lion roars of the troops, it became fearful. The light of all the kings,
like the moon and the sun, grew dim on the heroes' armlets and diadems. Clouds
of dust arose, wrapped in the lightning of weapons, and the twang of bows became
terrible. The sounds of arrows and conches, the great noise of drums and the
rumble of chariots of both armies came together. Crowded with masses of spears,
lances and javelins and with floods of arrows, the sky above the two armies
became as if without light. Chariot fighters and horses fell on one another in
the great battle, elephants killed elephants, and foot soldiers foot soldiers.
That was a very great battle of the Kurus with the Pandavas for Bhishma's sake,
tiger among men, like that of two hawks over a piece of meat. Their meeting in
battle was terrible, Bharata, as they sought victory on the battlefield, each
wanting to kill the other."

# The Kaurava Array

"This question is fitting for you, great king, as you deserve," said Sanjaya.
"But you should not fasten this fault on Duryodhana. A man who meets misfortune
through his own bad conduct should not suspect another of that sin. Great king,
a man who does among men everything that is blamed deserves death from the whole
world, for doing what is blamed. The Pandavas and their counsellors bore insult
from men whose wisdom was trickery, waiting on you, and endured it a long time
in the forest.

"What I saw with my own eyes, and saw by the power of yoga, of horses, elephants
and heroes of boundless energy, hear it, lord of the earth, and do not set your
mind on grief. Surely this was fated long ago to be so, lord of men. I bow to
your father, the wise son of Parashara. By his grace I have gained unsurpassed
divine knowledge, sight beyond the senses, king, hearing from afar, knowledge of
others' minds and of past and future, knowledge of how all that rises comes to
be, and movement always in the sky, untouched by weapons in battle, by the boon
of that great one. Hear from me in full this varied, most wonderful, great war
of the Bharatas, as it happened, making the hair stand on end.

"When the armies were ready and drawn up by the rules, great king, Duryodhana
said to Duhshasana, 'Duhshasana, let chariots be yoked quickly to guard Bhishma,
and urge on all the ranks at once. This meeting of the Pandavas and the Kurus
with their armies, which I have thought of for years on end, has come to me. I
think nothing in the battle is more important than guarding Bhishma. Guarded, he
would kill the Parthas, the Somakas and the Srinjayas. That man of pure soul
said, "I would not kill Shikhandi. It is heard that he was a woman before, so I
must avoid him in battle." So I think Bhishma must be guarded above all. Let all
my men stand ready to kill Shikhandi. Let the men of the east, the west, the
south and the north, skilled in every weapon, guard the grandfather. For if he
is unguarded, a wolf could kill a mighty lion. Let us not have the lion killed
by Shikhandi as by a jackal. Yudhamanyu guards Phalguna's left wheel and
Uttamaujas his right, and Phalguna guards Shikhandi. Act so, Duhshasana, that
Shikhandi, guarded by Partha and avoided by Bhishma, does not kill Ganga's son.'

---

"Then, when the night had dawned, a great noise arose of kings shouting, 'Yoke!
Yoke!' With the sound of conches and drums, with lions' roars, Bharata, with the
neighing of horses and the rumble of chariot wheels, with elephants trumpeting
and fighters roaring, with whoops, the slapping of arms and shouts, there was an
uproar on every side. At sunrise, great king, the great army of the Kuru and
Pandava hosts, yours and the Pandavas', rose up, all of it yoked, king of kings.
There elephants and chariots adorned with gold were seen shining like clouds
with lightning. Ranks of chariots were seen like many cities. Your father shone
there greatly, like the full moon.

"The fighters stood in their ranks with bows, spears, swords, clubs, darts and
lances, bright weapons. Elephants, chariots, foot soldiers and horses, lord of
the people, stood in hundreds and thousands, shaped like nets. Banners of many
shapes were seen raised high, bright in thousands, of our own side and the
enemy's. The kings' banners, golden, their bodies bright with jewels, blazing
like fires, shone in thousands with their flames, like the bright banners of
great Indra in great Indra's houses. The heroes in armour, eager for battle,
looked at them. Bright with raised weapons, with arm guards bound on and
quivers, the bull-eyed lords of men shone at the head of the armies.

"There were Shakuni son of Subala, Shalya, Jayadratha of Sindhu, Vinda and
Anuvinda of Avanti, and Sudakshina of Kamboja. There were Shrutayudha of
Kalinga, King Jayatsena, Brihadbala of Kosala and Kritavarma the Satvata. These
ten tigers among men, brave, with arms like iron bars, were the lords of armies,
sacrificers who gave rich fees. These and many others who followed Duryodhana's
will, kings and princes, wise in policy and very strong, were seen in armour
standing in their ranks. All of them had black antelope skins bound on and wore
banners and garlands of munja grass. Let loose for Duryodhana's sake,
consecrated for the world of Brahma, they stood holding ten rich armies. The
eleventh, Dhritarashtra's own, the great army of the Kauravas, was ahead of all
the troops, with Shantanu's son as its leader. With a white turban, white horses
and white armour, unfallen, we saw Bhishma, great king, like the risen moon.
With his banner of a golden palm tree, standing in his silver chariot, the Kurus
and Pandavas saw Bhishma like the sharp-rayed sun in a white cloud.

"When they saw Bhishma at the head of the army, the Pandavas trembled, and the
great archers of the Srinjayas, led by Dhrishtadyumna. As small beasts tremble
when they see a great lion yawn, so all of them, led by Dhrishtadyumna, shrank
again and again. These are your eleven glorious armies, Bharata, and the
Pandavas' seven, guarded by great men. They were seen like two oceans met at the
end of the age, full of maddened sea monsters and whirlpools, crowded with great
crocodiles. Never before, king, have we seen or heard of such a gathering of
armies come together.

---

"As the blessed Vyasa, Krishna Dvaipayana, had said, so all the kings came
together. That day the moon entered the region of Magha, and the seven great
planets came together in the sky, blazing. The sun at its rising looked as if
split in two, and the sun rose in the sky with a blazing crest. Jackals and
crows howled in the burning quarter, wanting bodies, eaters of flesh and blood.

"Day after day the old grandfather of the Kurus and Bharadvaja's son rose at
dawn, composed, and the two tamers of enemies said, 'Victory to the sons of
Pandu.' Then they fought for your sake, as the agreement had been made. Your
father Devavrata, who knew the fine points of every dharma, gathered the kings
and said this. 'Kshatriyas, this great door to heaven stands open for you. Go by
it to the world of Shakra and of Brahma. This is the eternal road, taken by
those before you and those before them. Honour yourselves and fight with
untroubled minds. Nabhaga, Yayati, Mandhatri, Nahusha and Nriga won perfection
and reached the highest place by deeds like these. For a kshatriya to die of
sickness at home is against dharma. To meet death in battle is his eternal
dharma.'

"Spoken to like this by Bhishma, bull of the Bharatas, the kings went out to
their ranks, making them shine with their fine chariots. But Karna Vaikartana,
with his counsellors and kin, had been made to lay down his weapons in the
battle by Bhishma, bull of the Bharatas. Without Karna, your sons and your kings
went out with lions' roars, making the ten quarters echo. Those ranks shone with
white umbrellas and flags, with banners, elephants and horses, with chariots and
foot soldiers. The earth was shaken by the sound of drums and tabors, the noise
of kettledrums and the rumble of chariot wheels. With golden armlets and
bracelets and with their bows, the great chariot fighters were seen shining like
moving mountains.

"With his great palm-tree banner with five stars, Bhishma, the commander of the
Kuru army, stood like the clear sun. Your great archers, kings, bull of the
Bharatas, took their places as Shantanu's son ordered, king. Shaibya, who lives
among cows, went with all the kings, on a royal elephant fit for a king, with a
flag, and stood at the head of all the ranks, the colour of a lotus. Ashvatthama
went, ready, with a lion's tail on his banner. Shrutayu, Chitrasena, Purumitra,
Vivimshati, Shalya, Bhurishravas and the great chariot fighter Vikarna: these
seven great archers, led by Drona's son, went before Bhishma in chariots of fine
colour. Their tall golden banners were seen shining, making their fine chariots
shine. The banner of Drona, chief of teachers, was a golden altar adorned with a
water pot, with a bow.

"Duryodhana, drawing a rank of many hundreds of thousands, had a great jewelled
elephant on his banner. The Paurava, the Kalinga, Sudakshina of Kamboja,
Kshemadhanva and Sumitra stood as chariot fighters in front of him. The king of
Magadha went in a costly chariot with a bull on his banner, as if drawing the
front of the army along. That force of the easterners, guarded by the lord of
the Angas and by great Kripa, was like a mass of autumn clouds. Jayadratha of
great fame, standing at the head of his rank, shone with his finest banner, a
silver boar. A hundred thousand chariots obeyed him, eight thousand elephants
and sixty thousand horsemen. That front of the army, guarded by the lord of
Sindhu, king, with endless chariots, elephants and horses, shone, a great force.

"The lord of all the Kalingas went with sixty thousand chariots and ten thousand
elephants, together with Ketuman. His great elephants, like mountains, shone,
adorned with engines, lances, quivers and flags. The lord of the Kalingas shone
with his finest banner, a tree, with a white umbrella, a golden necklace, and a
yak-tail fan. Ketuman too, mounted on an elephant with a bright, fine goad,
stood in the battle, king, like the sun in a cloud. King Bhagadatta went,
blazing with splendour, mounted on the finest elephant, like the wielder of the
thunderbolt. Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, on elephants' necks, equal to
Bhagadatta, followed Ketuman.

"That array, with ranks of chariots, with elephants as its finest head and limbs
and horses for its wings, flying fierce, faced every way and struck. It was laid
out by Drona and by King Bhishma, Shantanu's son, king, and by the teacher's
son, Bahlika and Kripa."

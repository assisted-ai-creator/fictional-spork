# Signs of Victory

"'Donkeys are born of cows,'" said Vyasa. "'Sons take their pleasure with their
mothers. Trees in the forest show flowers and fruit out of season. Pregnant
princesses give birth to frightful things, to flesh-eating birds, jackals and
other beasts. Evil animals are born with three horns, four eyes, five feet, two
sexual organs, two heads and two tails, with fangs, with mouths gaping, uttering
evil cries. Three-footed peacocks are born, and four-fanged, horned birds of
Tarkshya's kind. In the same way the women of those who speak of Brahman are
seen in your city giving birth to Garuda's kind and to peacocks. A mare gives
birth to a calf, lord of the earth, and a bitch to a jackal, and birds to
krakaras, mynahs and parrots that speak of evil. Some women give birth to four
or five girls, and as soon as they are born they dance, sing and laugh. The
small children of common people, still at the breast, in a thief's house, dance
and sing, foretelling great danger. Others, driven by time, draw images armed
with weapons. Children with sticks in their hands run at one another, and
wanting to fight, they lay siege to cities they have built.

"'Lotuses and water lilies grow on trees. Fierce winds blow in every direction,
and the dust does not settle. The earth trembles again and again, and Rahu has
swallowed the sun. The white planet has passed Chitra and stands there. It
foresees ruin, above all for the Kurus. A very terrible comet stands, having
attacked Pushya. The great planet will do terrible harm to both armies. Mars is
retrograde in Magha, and Brihaspati is in Shravana. Bhaga's constellation is
attacked and afflicted by the son of the sun. Shukra, having climbed into the
first Proshthapada, lord of the people, goes round into the second and looks
back. A dark planet, blazing, with smoke and fire, stands having attacked
Indra's bright constellation, Jyeshtha. Dhruva blazes terribly and turns to the
left. A harsh planet stands between Chitra and Svati. The red-bodied one, bright
as fire, moving back and forth in Shravana, stands covering the heap of Brahman.

"'The earth is covered with every crop and garlanded with fruit. Barley has five
heads and rice a hundred. The cows, the chief of the whole world, on whom this
world depends, give blood when their calves suckle. Swords flashed blazing from
their sheaths. The weapons clearly see that war is at hand. The light of the
weapons and the water is the colour of fire. There will be great destruction of
armour and banners. Beasts and birds with blazing mouths cry out in every
quarter, showing calamity, foretelling great danger. A bird with one wing, one
eye and one foot, flying in the sky at night, cries out fiercely in anger,
vomiting blood again and again. Two planets with copper-red crests are fixed as
if blazing, covering the light of the noble seven seers. Brihaspati and Saturn,
the two planets that stay a year, blaze near the two Vishakhas. A fierce planet
blazing in Krittika, the first constellation, stands like a comet, taking away
forms with its light. In all three of the first constellations Mercury falls
again and again, creating very great danger.

"'I have known a new moon on the fourteenth day, the fifteenth, and once before
on the sixteenth. But this one, on the thirteenth, I have never known. Moon and
sun have both been eclipsed in one month, on the thirteenth, out of season.
These two planets will destroy creatures. All the quarters are covered with
dust, with rains of dust everywhere. Fierce clouds of portent rain blood at
night. On the fourteenth of the dark fortnight there was a heavy rain of flesh,
very terrible, at midnight, and the rakshasas could not have their fill. Rivers
and streams flow backwards, with water of blood. Wells foam and roar like bulls.
Meteors fall with whirlwinds, mixed with dry thunderbolts. When this night
dawned today, the sun at its rising was struck by four great blazing meteors on
every side. Great seers who stood worshipping the sun said there, "The earth
will drink the blood of thousands of kings." From Kailasa, Mandara and Mount
Himavat peaks fall in thousands with a great noise. In the earthquake the great
elements stir up the four oceans one by one, again and again, over their shores.
Fierce winds blow, uprooting trees and dragging gravel, and sacred trees fall in
villages and cities.

"'Fire offered by the twice-born burns yellow, red and blue, with flames to the
left, smelling of corpses, full of smoke, with a harsh sound. Touch, smell and
taste are turned upside down, lord of the earth. The kings' banners smoke and
shake again and again. Drums and kettledrums pour out showers of embers. On the
tops of palaces and at the city gates fierce vultures fly about, keeping to the
left. Birds cry very loudly, "Ripe! Unripe!", and settle on the tops of banners,
for the destruction of the kings. Horses and elephants in thousands, brooding,
scattering their dung, trembling, weep miserably. When you have heard this,
decide here what the time requires, so that the world may not go to ruin,
Bharata.'

"When he heard his father's words, Dhritarashtra said, 'I think this was fated
long ago. It will happen, no doubt of it. If kshatriyas are killed in battle by
the dharma of kshatriyas, they will reach the world of heroes and find only
happiness. Fame here, and great happiness in the other world for a long time,
those tigers among men will win when they give up their lives in the great
battle.'

---

Spoken to like this by his son Dhritarashtra, best of kings, the sage, the chief
of poets, fell into deep meditation. Then the great ascetic who speaks of time
said again, "No doubt, king of kings, time destroys the world and creates the
worlds again. Nothing here is lasting. Show the road of dharma to your kin, the
Kurus, your relations and friends, for you are able to hold them back. They call
the killing of kin mean. Do not do what displeases me. Time has been born to you
in the form of a son, lord of the people. Killing is not praised in the Veda. It
is never good. He who would kill his family's dharma kills his own body. Through
time you are going off the road, though you could keep to it. For the
destruction of this family and of the kings, a disaster in the form of a kingdom
has come. Give it up. It brings no happiness. Your wisdom has been stolen by
another. Show dharma to your sons. What use is a kingdom to you, unassailable
one, by which you have earned sin? Guard fame, dharma and glory, and you will
reach heaven. Let the Pandavas have the kingdom, and let the Kauravas find
peace."

While the best of brahmins was speaking, Dhritarashtra son of Ambika, skilled in
speech, interrupted him and went on in his turn. "I know as you know. Being and
not being are known to me as they are. But the world is bewildered about its own
interest, my father, and know that I am one of the world. I beg your grace, you
of matchless power. You are our refuge and our steady guide. My sons are not in
my power, great seer. You should not lay sin on me here. For you are dharma,
purity, glory, fame, steadiness and memory. You are the honoured grandfather of
the Kurus and the Pandavas."

"Son of Vichitravirya, king," said Vyasa, "tell me as you wish what is in your
mind. I will cut away your doubt."

"I want to hear truly, lord," said Dhritarashtra, "all the signs that belong to
those who will win in battle."

"The fire shines clear," said Vyasa, "its rays rising, its flame turning to the
right, without smoke, and the offerings give off a holy smell. They say this is
the form of coming victory. Where conches and drums sound with deep, great
voices, and the sun and moon have pure rays, they say this is the form of coming
victory. The crows' cries are welcome behind those who are setting out and
going. Those behind urge them on, king, and those in front hold them back. Where
birds of good omen, royal swans, parrots, cranes and woodpeckers, go round to
the right in battle, the brahmins say victory is sure. Those whose army is
bright with ornaments, armour and banners, with men's faces glad and golden,
hard to look at, conquer their enemies. Where the fighters' words and spirits
are glad, Bharata, and their garlands do not wither, they cross over their
enemies in battle. A welcome wind for one who has entered, and from the right
for one about to enter, accomplishes the aim from behind and blocks it from in
front. Those who have pleasant sounds, sights, tastes, touches and smells, and
whose fighters are always glad, will surely win. The winds blow behind them, and
so do the clouds and birds, and the clouds and rainbows follow them. These are
the marks of those who are winning, lord of the people. For those who are about
to die they are the opposite, lord of men.

"Whether the army is small or large, this is certain: the gladness of the
fighters is called the one mark of victory. One man who breaks can break even a
very great army, and the bravest fighters break after him when he has broken. A
great army once broken is very hard to stop, like a great rush of water or
frightened herds of deer. A great army cannot be put together again in the
clash. 'It has broken,' and so the bravest fighters break. Seeing the frightened
and the broken, fear grows more. A great army suddenly broken and scattered in
every direction by the enemy cannot be rallied, king, even by heroes.

"A wise king who has gathered a great army of four branches should always
strive, ever ready, to use policy first. They say victory by policy is best, by
division middling, and victory by battle the lowest, lord of the people. A clash
has great faults, and so it is called the worst. Even fifty brave men who know
one another, glad, free of care and resolved, can crush a great army, or even
five, six or seven who do not turn back. Garuda, son of Vinata, does not praise
a great crowd, Bharata, when he sees a great gathering of birds. Victory does
not come from the size of an army, Bharata. Victory is uncertain, and fate
decides it. Even those who win in battle suffer losses."

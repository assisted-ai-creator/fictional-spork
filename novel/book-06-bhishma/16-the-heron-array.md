# The Heron Array

"When the armies withdrew on the first day, bull among the Bharatas," said
Sanjaya, "and Bhishma was full of fury in the battle and Duryodhana was glad,
the righteous king went quickly to Janardana with all his brothers and all the
kings. Filled with the deepest grief, thinking of defeat, king, after seeing
Bhishma's valour, he said to Varshneya,

"'Krishna, look at Bhishma, that great archer of terrible valour, burning up my
army with his arrows as fire burns dry grass in summer. How shall we even look
at that great man, licking up my army like a fire fed with butter? When they see
that tiger among men, that mighty bowman, my army runs, struck by his arrows.
Angry Yama could be beaten in battle, or the god with the thunderbolt, or Varuna
with his noose, or Kubera with his mace. But Bhishma, of great splendour and
great strength, cannot be beaten. I am sunk, and I have no raft, in the
fathomless waters of Bhishma. It was my own weak judgement, Keshava, that
brought me against Bhishma. I will go to the forest, Govinda. It is better for
me to live there than to give these lords of the earth to Bhishma, who is death.
Bhishma, who knows great weapons, will destroy my army, Krishna. My soldiers are
going to their end like moths rushing into a blazing fire.

"'I have been brought to ruin, Varshneya, by striving for a kingdom. My brave
brothers are worn down and tormented by arrows. For my sake, out of love for
their brother, they have lost their kingdom and their happiness. Now I value
life highly, for life is hard to keep today. With what life is left to me I will
practise hard austerity. I will not have these friends of mine killed in battle,
Keshava. Mighty Bhishma is killing thousands of my chariot fighters, the best of
my warriors, without pause, with his divine weapons. Tell me quickly, Madhava,
what should I do to do right?

"'I see Savyasachin standing in the battle as if he were neutral. Only
strong-armed Bhima fights with all his strength, remembering a kshatriya's
dharma, with the might of his arms alone. With his mace, which kills heroes, the
great-minded man does very hard deeds among elephants, horses, chariots and foot
soldiers, as much as he can. But fighting straight, sir, he could not destroy
the enemy's army even in a hundred years. Your friend is the only one here who
knows the great weapons, and even he stands aside while we are burned up by
Bhishma and great Drona. The divine weapons of Bhishma and great Drona, used
again and again, will burn up all the kshatriyas. Krishna, with his valour,
Bhishma in his fury, together with all the kings, will surely destroy us. So
look for a great archer, lord of yoga, a great chariot fighter, who can put out
Bhishma in battle as a raincloud puts out a forest fire. By your grace, Govinda,
the Pandavas will kill their enemies, win back their own kingdom and be happy
with their kin.'

"When he had said this, great-minded Partha sat brooding for a long time, turned
inward, his mind stricken with grief. Seeing that the Pandava was tormented by
grief and had lost heart in his sorrow, Govinda spoke to cheer all the Pandavas.

"'Do not grieve, best of the Bharatas. You should not grieve, when your brothers
are heroes and bowmen famous in all the world. I am here to do you good, king,
and so is Satyaki, the great chariot fighter, and old Virata and Drupada, and
Dhrishtadyumna, son of Prishata. All the kings, with their armies, best of
kings, are waiting for your word, and they are devoted to you, lord of the
people. Here is Prishata's mighty son Dhrishtadyumna, who always wishes you well
and delights in your good, and who has become your commander. And Shikhandi,
strong-armed one, is Bhishma's death, so they say.'

"When he heard this, the king spoke to Dhrishtadyumna, the great chariot
fighter, in that assembly, while Vasudeva listened. 'Dhrishtadyumna, hear what I
say to you, sir, and do not go beyond my words. You are my commander, approved
by Vasudeva. As Karttikeya was always the commander of the gods long ago, so you
are the commander of the Pandavas, bull among men. Tiger among men, show your
valour and kill the Kauravas. I will follow you, sir, and Bhima and Krishna, the
two sons of Madri together, the sons of Draupadi in their armour, and the other
chief lords of the earth, bull among men.'

"Then Dhrishtadyumna cheered them all. 'Partha,' he said, 'long ago Shambhu made
me to be Drona's death. Today I will fight them all in battle, lord of the
earth, Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Shalya and Jayadratha, all of them, proud as they
are.'

"When Prishata's son, the lord of kings, destroyer of enemies, stood ready like
this, the Pandavas, great archers mad for battle, gave a great shout. Then
Partha said to Prishata's son, the commander, 'There is an array called the
Heron, which destroys every enemy. Brihaspati told it to Indra long ago, in the
war of the gods and the asuras. Draw it up properly against them. It destroys
the enemy's army. Let the kings see it with the Kurus. No one has seen it
before.'

"Told this by that god among men, as Vishnu was told by the bearer of the
thunderbolt, he put Dhananjaya at the head of all the armies at dawn. His banner
was wonderful and delightful, moving along the path of the sun, made by
Vishvakarman at Puruhuta's command. It was adorned with flags the colour of
Indra's bow, and moving through the sky like a city of the gandharvas, it seemed
to dance on the chariot's course, sir. Partha was made complete by that jewelled
banner, and the banner by the bearer of the Gandiva, as the Self-born is
completed by the sun.

"King Drupada, with a great army, was the head. Kuntibhoja and the king of the
Chedis were the two eyes, lord of men. The Dasharnakas and the Prayagas, with
the hosts of the Dasherakas, the Anupagas and the Kiratas were in the neck, bull
among the Bharatas. Yudhishthira, with the Patachcharas and the Hundas, king,
the Pauravakas and the Nishadas, was the back. Bhimasena and Dhrishtadyumna, son
of Prishata, Draupadi's sons and Abhimanyu, and Satyaki the great chariot
fighter were the two wings. The Pishachas and the Daradas, the Pundras with the
Kundivishas, the Madakas and the Ladakas, the Tanganas and the Paratanganas, the
Bahlikas and the Tittiras, the Cholas and the Pandyas, Bharata: these peoples,
king, held the right wing. The Agniveshyas, the Jagattundas and the Paladashas,
Bharata, the Shabaras, the Tumbupas and the Vatsas with the Nakulas, and Nakula
and Sahadeva held the left side.

"There were ten thousand chariots in the wings, a hundred thousand in the head,
a hundred million and twenty thousand in the back, and a hundred thousand and
seventy thousand in the neck. At the wing tips, the wing joints and the ends of
the wings, elephants went in close formation, king, like moving mountains.
Virata guarded the rear with the Kekayas, and the king of Kashi and Shaibya,
with thirty thousand chariots.

"When the Pandavas had drawn up this great array, Bharata, they stood in their
armour, ready for battle, waiting for the sun to rise. Their white umbrellas,
bright as the sun, clean and huge, shone on the elephants and the chariots.

"Your son saw the great Heron array, terrible and unbreakable, drawn up by
Partha of boundless splendour. He went to the teacher, sir, and to Kripa,
Shalya, Somadatta's son, Vikarna and Ashvatthama, to all his brothers,
Duhshasana and the rest, Bharata, and to the many other heroes who had come to
fight. To cheer them, your son said at the right moment, 'All of you carry many
kinds of weapons, and all of you know weapons and divine weapons. Each one of
you great chariot fighters could kill the sons of Pandu and their armies in
battle, let alone all of you together. That force of ours, guarded by Bhishma,
cannot be measured. This force of theirs, best of kings, can. Let the
Samsthanas, the Shurasenas, the Venikas and the Kukuras, the Arevakas, the
Trigartas, the Madrakas and the Yavanas guard Bhishma. Let them go with
Shatrunjaya and Duhshasana, with heroic Vikarna, with Nanda and Upananda, with
Chitrasena and with the Panibhadrakas, with their armies in front, and guard
Bhishma above all.'

"Then Drona and Bhishma and your son, sir, drew up a great array to hold off the
Pandavas. Bhishma, surrounded on every side by a great army, went forward
drawing a huge force along like the king of the gods. After him came the mighty
great archer Bharadvaja's son, lord of the people, with the Kuntalas, the
Dasharnas and the Magadhas, with the Vidarbhas, the Mekalas and the
Karnapravaranas, with the whole army following Bhishma, who shone in battle. The
Gandharas, the Sindhu-Sauviras, the Shibis and the Vasatis, and Shakuni with his
own army, guarded Bharadvaja's son. Then King Duryodhana, with all his brothers,
with the Ashvatakas, the Vikarnas, the Sharmilas and Kosalas, the Daradas, the
Chuchupas and the Kshudrakas and Malavas, gladly guarded Subala's son's army.
Bhurishravas, Shala, Shalya and Bhagadatta, sir, and Vinda and Anuvinda of
Avanti guarded the left side. Somadatta's son, Susharma, Sudakshina of Kamboja,
Shatayus and Shrutayus held the right side. Ashvatthama, Kripa and Kritavarma
the Satvata stood with a great army at the rear. Behind them, as guards, were
lords of many lands, Ketuman, Vasudana, and the mighty son of the king of Kashi.

"Then all your men, glad for battle, Bharata, blew their conches with joy and
roared their lion roars. When he heard them, the old grandfather of the Kurus
gave a loud lion roar and blew his conch, full of power. Then conches and drums,
tabors of many kinds and kettledrums were beaten by the enemy, and the noise was
tremendous. Then, standing in their great chariot yoked to white horses, Krishna
and Arjuna blew their fine conches, adorned with gold and jewels. Hrishikesha
blew Panchajanya, Dhananjaya blew Devadatta, and Vrikodara of terrible deeds
blew the great conch Paundra. King Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, blew
Anantavijaya, and Nakula and Sahadeva blew Sughosha and Manipushpaka. The king
of Kashi and Shaibya, Shikhandi the great chariot fighter, Dhrishtadyumna,
Virata and Satyaki of great fame, the great archer of Panchala and the five sons
of Draupadi all blew their great conches and roared their lion roars. That
great, tremendous noise raised by those heroes made the sky and the earth
resound. So, great king, the Kurus and the Pandavas, full of joy, met again for
battle, burning to hurt each other."

"When the armies had been drawn up like this," said Dhritarashtra, "mine and
theirs, how did those best of fighters strike at one another?"

"When the armies had been drawn up alike, in their armour, with their bright
banners," said Sanjaya, "your son King Duryodhana, standing in the middle of
them and seeing that army like a boundless ocean, said to all your men, 'Fight,
in your armour!' Setting their minds on cruelty and giving up all thought of
life, they all charged the Pandavas with their banners raised. Then a
tumultuous, hair-raising battle began, yours and theirs, with chariots and
elephants tangled together. Arrows with golden feathers and sharp, unblunted
points, shot by the chariot fighters, fell on elephants and horses.

"As the battle went on like this, strong-armed Bhishma of terrible valour, in
his armour, raised his bow and charged. The old grandfather of the Kurus rained
showers of arrows on Subhadra's son, on Bhimasena and on the great chariot
fighter Shaineya. He rained them on the Kekaya, on Virata and on Dhrishtadyumna,
son of Prishata, on those heroes among men, and on the Chedis and the Matsyas
round them. The great array shook as that hero met them, and all the armies were
thrown into confusion. Their banners and elephants were brought down, their best
horses killed, their chariot ranks put to flight. The Pandavas were in that
state.

"When Arjuna, tiger among men, saw Bhishma, the great chariot fighter, he said
angrily to Varshneya, 'Go to where the grandfather is. Clearly Bhishma, in his
fury, will destroy my army, Varshneya, devoted as he is to Duryodhana's good.
Here are Drona, Kripa, Shalya and Vikarna, Janardana, and the sons of
Dhritarashtra together, led by Duryodhana. They will kill the Panchalas while
that firm bowman guards them. So I will go to Bhishma, for my army's sake,
Janardana.'

"'Be ready, Dhananjaya,' said Vasudeva. 'I will bring you, hero, to the
grandfather's chariot.' When he had said this, Shauri drove that famous chariot
towards Bhishma's chariot, lord of men. With its many fluttering flags and
horses the colour of cranes, with its terrible roaring monkey banner raised
high, and with the great sound of a thundercloud, bright as the sun, the chariot
came on. The Pandava, destroyer of his friends' grief, came quickly, shooting
his arrows, killing the Kaurava ranks and the Shurasenas.

"He came on at speed like an elephant in rut, frightening heroes in the battle
and bringing them down with his arrows. Then Bhishma, son of Shantanu, guarded
by the Saindhava and the others, by the easterners, the Sauviras and the
Kekayas, suddenly went out to meet Arjuna. For who else but the grandfather of
the Kurus, or Drona and Vaikartana, could face the bowman of the Gandiva as a
chariot fighter?

"Then Bhishma, grandfather of the Kauravas, great king, covered Arjuna with
seventy-seven iron arrows. Drona pierced the Pandava with twenty-five, Kripa
with fifty, Duryodhana with sixty-four, Shalya with nine, the Saindhava with
nine, Shakuni with five, and Vikarna with ten arrows, king. Pierced on every
side with sharp arrows, the strong-armed great archer did not flinch, any more
than a mountain that is struck. Kiritin, whose spirit had no measure, pierced
Bhishma with twenty-five arrows in return, Kripa with nine, Drona with sixty,
Vikarna with three, Artayani with three and the king with five, bull among the
Bharatas.

"Satyaki, Virata, Dhrishtadyumna, son of Prishata, Draupadi's sons and Abhimanyu
surrounded Dhananjaya. Then the Panchala, with the Somakas, rained arrows on the
great archer Drona, who was eager to please Ganga's son. Bhishma, best of
chariot fighters, quickly pierced the Pandava with eighty sharp arrows, and your
men shouted. Hearing the shouts of those glad men, the mighty lion among chariot
fighters gladly plunged into their midst. When Dhananjaya had reached the middle
of those lions among chariot fighters, he played with his bow, king, making the
great chariot fighters his targets.

"Then King Duryodhana, lord of men, saw his army tormented by Partha in battle.
He said to Bhishma, 'My friend, this mighty son of Pandu, with Krishna, is
cutting away our roots while all our armies strive, while you, son of Ganga, and
Drona, best of chariot fighters, are still alive. Because of you, Karna too, the
great chariot fighter, who always wishes me well, has laid down his weapons and
will not fight Partha in battle. So act, son of Ganga, so that Phalguna is
killed.'

"When he was told this, king, your father Devavrata said, 'Shame on the
kshatriya's dharma!' and went towards Partha's chariot.

"When they saw the two white-horsed chariot fighters locked together, king, the
kings gave loud lion roars and blew their conches, Bharata. Drona's son,
Duryodhana and your son Vikarna surrounded Bhishma in the battle, sir, ready to
fight. In the same way all the Pandavas surrounded Dhananjaya, ready for the
great battle, and the fight began.

"Ganga's son struck Partha in the battle with nine arrows. Arjuna pierced him
back with ten that struck the vitals. Then, with a thousand well-aimed arrows,
Arjuna the Pandava, who gloried in battle, closed off every direction to
Bhishma. And Bhishma, son of Shantanu, held off Partha's net of arrows with a
net of arrows, Kaurava. Both were delighted, and both loved battle. They fought
with no difference between them, each trying to pay the other back. Showers of
arrows loosed from Bhishma's bow were seen scattered, broken by Arjuna's arrows.
In the same way the nets of arrows loosed by Arjuna fell to the ground, driven
off by the arrows of Ganga's son. Arjuna struck Bhishma with twenty-five sharp
arrows, and Bhishma pierced Partha with thirty. Those two mighty destroyers of
enemies pierced each other's horses and banners, chariot poles and chariot
wheels, and played.

"Then Bhishma, best of fighters, in anger, great king, struck Vasudeva between
the breasts with three arrows. Pierced by the arrows from Bhishma's bow,
Madhusudana shone in the battle, king, like a flowering kimshuka tree. Then
Arjuna, very angry to see Madhava wounded, pierced the charioteer of Ganga's son
with three arrows in the battle. Those two heroes strove to kill each other, but
neither could get the better of the other in the battle. Through the skill and
speed of their charioteers they showed many wonderful circles, advances and
retreats. Watching for an opening between strokes, king, the two great chariot
fighters kept circling round each other, again and again. Both of them blew
their conches, with lion roars, and made their bows thunder. At the roar of
their conches and the rumble of their wheels, the earth suddenly split, shook
and roared.

"No one could see any difference between them, bull among the Bharatas. Both
were mighty in battle, brave and alike. The Kauravas knew Bhishma there only by
his emblem, and the sons of Pandu knew Partha only by his emblem. Seeing such
valour in those two best of men, king, all beings were amazed at the battle,
Bharata. No one could see a flaw in either of them in the battle, Bharata, just
as no one can see a fault in a man who stands firm in dharma. Both of them
vanished in their nets of arrows, and then quickly appeared again.

"There the gods with the gandharvas, the charanas and the seers, seeing their
valour, said to one another, 'These two great chariot fighters in their fury
cannot be beaten in battle by the worlds, with the gods, asuras and gandharvas.
This battle is a wonder in the worlds, a great marvel. There will never be
battles like this again. Wise Partha cannot beat Bhishma in battle while Bhishma
has his bow and stands in his chariot, shooting his arrows. And in the same way
Bhishma could not hope to beat the Pandava bowman in battle, whom even the gods
can hardly face.' Such words were heard, lord of the people, rising here and
there, in praise of Ganga's son and Arjuna in the battle.

"While those two showed their valour, your warriors and the Pandavas killed one
another in the battle, Bharata. With sharp-edged swords and bright axes, with
many arrows and weapons of every kind, the heroes of both armies cut one another
down. As that terrible, cruel battle went on, king, there was a great meeting of
Drona and the Panchala."

"How did the great archer Drona and Prishata's son of Panchala meet in battle,
both striving?" said Dhritarashtra. "Tell me, Sanjaya. I think fate is stronger
than human effort, Sanjaya, since Bhishma, son of Shantanu, could not get the
better of the Pandava in battle. For Bhishma in his anger could kill the worlds,
moving and still, in battle. How did he fail to get the better of the Pandava by
his strength, Sanjaya?"

"Hear this very terrible battle with a steady mind, king," said Sanjaya. "The
Pandava cannot be beaten even by the gods with Vasava.

"Drona fought Dhrishtadyumna with sharp arrows, and with a broad arrow he
brought down his charioteer from the chariot box. Then, in anger, he tormented
Dhrishtadyumna's four horses with four fine arrows, sir. The hero
Dhrishtadyumna, almost laughing, pierced Drona with ninety sharp arrows and
said, 'Stand! Stand!' Then mighty Bharadvaja's son, whose spirit had no measure,
covered the furious Dhrishtadyumna with arrows again. He took up a terrible
arrow to kill Prishata's son, like Shakra's thunderbolt to touch, like a second
rod of Death. When they saw that arrow fitted to the bow by Bharadvaja's son in
battle, there was a great cry from the whole army, Bharata.

"There we saw a wonderful feat of Dhrishtadyumna's. The hero stood alone in the
battle, unmoving as a mountain. He cut down that blazing, terrible arrow, his
own death, as it came, and loosed a shower of arrows at Bharadvaja's son. Then
all the Panchalas and the Pandavas shouted, seeing the very hard deed
Dhrishtadyumna had done. Then, wanting Drona's death, that valiant man hurled a
very swift spear adorned with gold and beryl. As the gold-adorned spear came
suddenly at him, Bharadvaja's son, almost laughing, broke it in three in the
battle. Seeing the spear destroyed, mighty Dhrishtadyumna rained showers of
arrows on Drona, lord of men. The famous Drona held off that shower of arrows
and cut the bow of Drupada's son in the middle.

"With his bow cut, the famous, mighty man hurled a heavy mace, made of mountain
iron, at Drona. The mace flew swiftly, meant to kill Drona. There we saw a
wonderful feat of Bharadvaja's son. With his speed he made the gold-adorned mace
miss, and when he had made it miss, he shot at Prishata's son very sharp broad
yellow arrows with golden feathers, whetted on stone. They pierced his armour
and drank his blood in the battle. Then great-minded Dhrishtadyumna took another
bow, attacked Drona in the battle and pierced him with five arrows. Smeared with
blood, those two bulls among men shone like two kimshuka trees in flower in
spring, king.

"Then, in fury, king, Drona attacked at the head of the army and cut the bow of
Drupada's son again. And when his bow was cut, Drona, whose spirit had no
measure, covered him with straight arrows, as a cloud covers a mountain with
rain. With a broad arrow he brought down his charioteer from the chariot box,
and with four sharp arrows he brought down his four horses in the battle, and
gave a lion roar. Then with another broad arrow he cut the bow from his hand.
With his bow cut, without a chariot, his horses and charioteer killed,
Dhrishtadyumna got down, mace in hand, showing his great manhood. Drona quickly
brought the mace down with his arrows, Bharata, before he had got down from the
chariot. It was a marvel.

"Then the strong-armed, mighty man took a huge shield marked with a hundred
moons, shining, and a huge divine sword, and charged at speed to kill Drona,
like a lion hungry for meat charging a maddened elephant in the forest. There we
saw a wonderful display of Bharadvaja's son's manhood, Bharata, his speed, his
use of weapons and the strength of his arms. He held Prishata's son off with a
shower of arrows, and mighty as Dhrishtadyumna was, he could not advance in the
battle. We saw the great chariot fighter Dhrishtadyumna standing there, warding
off the floods of arrows with his shield, like a practised fighter.

"Then strong-armed, mighty Bhima rushed in suddenly to help great Prishata's son
in the battle. He pierced Drona with seven sharp arrows, king, and quickly took
Prishata's son up into another chariot. Then King Duryodhana sent the king of
Kalinga, with a great army, to guard Bharadvaja's son. That great army of the
Kalingas, lord of men, went quickly at Bhima at your son's command. Drona, best
of chariot fighters, left the Panchala and fought old Virata and Drupada
together, and Dhrishtadyumna too went to the righteous king in the battle. Then
a tumultuous, hair-raising battle began between the Kalingas and great Bhima,
terrible to see and fearful, destroying the world."

**Divergences.**

* **Yudhishthira's despair.** He speaks of going to the forest and says Arjuna stands "as if he were neutral" (6.46.9–20). This is CE.
* **Dhrishtadyumna commander.** Krishna says Dhrishtadyumna "has become your commander" and that Shikhandi is Bhishma's death, "so they say" (6.46.30, *kila*). Yudhishthira names him commander before them all (6.46.33), as he did before the war (5.154.12). A rejected passage (\*155) adds a consecration by Dhaumya.
* **"Made to be Drona's death."** Dhrishtadyumna says, "Shambhu made me to be Drona's death" (6.46.36). His birth from the fire to kill Drona is told at 1.155.
* **Bhishma's words.** When Duryodhana complains, Bhishma says only, "Shame on the kshatriya's dharma!" (6.48.37).
* **Krishna wounded.** Bhishma strikes Krishna between the breasts with three arrows (6.48.49–50).
* **The monkey on the banner.** The roaring monkey banner is CE (6.48.18); its tail-lashing and tawny-eyed glaring are a rejected line (\*159).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*153–\*164 and App. I No. 5 (a repetition of 6.20 after 6.46.55).

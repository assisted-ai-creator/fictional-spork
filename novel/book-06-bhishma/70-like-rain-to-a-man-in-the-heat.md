# Like Rain to a Man in the Heat

"Abhimanyu, great king," said Sanjaya, "fought your son, mighty, joined with a
great army, for Bhishma's sake. Duryodhana in anger struck Krishna's grandson in
battle with nine arrows with bent joints, and then again with three. Krishna's
grandson in anger sent a spear at Duryodhana's chariot in battle, terrible like
death's sister. As it came at him at speed, terrible in form, lord of the
people, your son, the great chariot fighter, cut it in two with a razor-headed
arrow. Seeing that spear fallen, Krishna's grandson, very quick to anger, fixed
three arrows in Duryodhana's arms and chest. Again he struck the impatient
Duryodhana between the breasts with ten terrible arrows, best of the Bharatas.
That battle was terrible and wonderful in form, Bharata, delighting the
onlookers and honoured by all the kings. For Bhishma's death and Partha's
victory the two heroes fought in battle, Subhadra's son and the bull of the
Kurus.

"Drona's son, the bull among brahmins, scorcher of enemies, in anger struck
Satyaki, impetuous in battle, in the chest with an iron arrow. The Shaineya,
whose self is beyond measure, struck the teacher's son in all his vital points
with nine heron-feathered arrows, Bharata. Ashvatthama in battle fixed nine
arrows in Satyaki and quickly thirty more in his arms and chest. Badly pierced
by Drona's son, the very glorious great archer, the Satvata, struck Drona's son
with three arrows.

"The Paurava met Dhrishtaketu in battle with arrows and tore the great archer,
the great chariot fighter, in many ways. So too the great chariot fighter
Dhrishtaketu, very strong, pierced the Paurava in battle with thirty sharp
arrows. The great chariot fighter Paurava cut Dhrishtaketu's bow, roared a
strong roar and pierced him with ten arrows. He took another bow and struck the
Paurava, great king, with seventy-three sharp arrows. Those two great archers,
great beings, great chariot fighters, rained on each other with a great shower
of arrows. Each cut the other's bow and killed his horses, Bharata, and the two
great chariot fighters, without their chariots, came together to fight with
swords. They took bright shields of bull's hide adorned with a hundred moons,
and very bright clean swords dotted with a hundred stars. They ran at each
other, king, like two lions in a great forest eager for a female in heat. They
moved in wonderful circles, advancing and retreating, showing them off and
seeking each other. The Paurava in anger struck Dhrishtaketu on the temple with
his great sword and said, 'Stand! Stand!' The king of the Chedis too struck the
Paurava, the bull among men, at the collarbone in battle with his sharp-pointed
great sword. Having met in the great battle, great king, struck by each other's
force, the two tamers of enemies fell. Then your son Jayatsena took the Paurava
up on his own chariot, king, and carried him away from the battlefield. The
mighty Sahadeva, Madri's son, scorcher of enemies, carried Dhrishtaketu away
from the battle, king.

"Chitrasena pierced Susharma with nine swift arrows, then again with sixty and
again with nine. Susharma in anger in battle pierced your son with ten sharp
arrows each time, lord of the people. Chitrasena in anger struck him in battle
with thirty arrows with bent joints, king, and he pierced him back, increasing
Bhishma's glory and honour in battle, king.

"Subhadra's son fought Prince Brihadbala. The lord of Kosala pierced Arjuna's
son with five iron arrows and again with twenty arrows with bent joints.
Subhadra's son pierced Brihadbala with nine iron arrows and did not make him
tremble in battle, and pierced him again and again. Phalguna's son cut the
Kosala's bow again and struck him with thirty heron-feathered arrows. Prince
Brihadbala took another bow and in anger pierced Phalguna's son in battle with
many arrows. Their battle for Bhishma's sake, scorcher of enemies, between those
two wonderful fighters, roused in battle, great king, was like that of Maya and
Vasava in the battle of the gods and asuras.

"Bhimasena shone greatly fighting the force of elephants, like Shakra with the
thunderbolt in his hand splitting the best of mountains. Being killed by Bhima,
elephants like mountains fell together to the earth, making it resound. Those
elephants, as big as mountains, like heaps of broken collyrium, shone on the
earth like scattered mountains. The great archer Yudhishthira met the Madra
king, guarded by a great army, and pressed him in battle. The lord of the Madras
too, mighty and roused for Bhishma's sake, pressed the son of Dharma, the great
chariot fighter, in battle. The Sindhu king pierced Virata with nine sharp
arrows with bent joints and pressed him again with thirty. Virata, great king,
struck the Sindhu king between the breasts at the head of the army with thirty
sharp arrows. With bright bows and swords, bright armour, weapons and banners,
the Matsya and the Sindhu king shone in battle in wonderful forms.

"Drona met the Panchala's son in the great battle and made a great show with
arrows with bent joints. Then Drona, great king, cut the great bow of Prishata's
grandson and pierced Prishata's grandson with fifty arrows. Prishata's grandson,
killer of enemy heroes, took another bow and sent arrows in battle while Drona
watched. The great chariot fighter Drona warded off those arrows with crowds of
arrows and sent five arrows at the son of Drupada. In anger, great king,
Prishata's grandson, killer of enemy heroes, hurled at Drona in battle a club
like Yama's staff. As it came at him at once, adorned with bands of gold, Drona
held it off in battle with fifty arrows. Cut in many pieces by the arrows loosed
from Drona's bow, king, it was ground to powder and fell scattered to the
ground. Seeing the club destroyed, Prishata's grandson, destroyer of enemies,
hurled at Drona a fine spear made all of iron. Drona cut it with nine arrows in
battle, Bharata, and pressed the great archer, Prishata's grandson, in battle.
So this great battle of Drona and Prishata's grandson went on, great king,
terrible and frightening, for Bhishma's sake.

---

"Arjuna reached Ganga's son and, pressing him with sharp arrows, ran at him,
ready, as at an elephant in rut in the forest. The mighty Bhagadatta went out
against Partha on an elephant blind with rut, split in three places. As it came
at him at once, like great Indra's elephant, Bibhatsu met it with the greatest
effort. Then the mighty King Bhagadatta, on his elephant, held off Arjuna in
battle with a shower of arrows. Arjuna pierced the elephant as it came, white as
silver, with clean, sharp iron arrows in the great battle. And the Kaunteya
urged Shikhandi on: 'Go! Go! Go against Bhishma, great king, and kill him!' Then
the Pragjyotisha left the Pandava, elder brother of Pandu, and went quickly to
Drupada's chariot, king. Then Arjuna, great king, ran quickly at Bhishma, with
Shikhandi in front, and the battle went on.

"Then your brave men all ran shouting at the Pandava, impetuous in battle, and
it was a wonder. Arjuna blew away your sons' ranks of many kinds, lord of men,
as the wind in time blows away the clouds in the sky. Shikhandi, calm, reached
the grandfather of the Bharatas and quickly covered him with many arrows.
Bhishma killed the Somakas in battle who followed in Partha's tracks, and the
great chariot fighter held off the Pandava army. Chariots were his fire house,
bows his flames, swords, spears and clubs his fuel, crowds of arrows his great
blaze: he burned the kshatriyas in battle. As a great fire moves through dry
brush with the wind, so Bhishma blazed, bringing forth divine weapons. With
sharp gold-feathered arrows with bent joints, the very glorious Bhishma made the
quarters and the points between them ring. Bringing down chariot fighters, king,
and elephants with their riders, he made the crowds of chariots like groves of
palm trees with their tops lopped off. The best of all who bear weapons left
chariots, elephants and horses without men in battle then, king. Hearing the
twang of his bowstring and palm, like the crash of a thunderbolt, the soldiers
trembled on every side, king. Your father's arrows fell, lord of men, and did
not fail. The arrows loosed from Bhishma's bow did not stick in bodies. We saw
chariots without men, well yoked to swift horses, rushing like the wind, being
carried away, lord of the people.

"Fourteen thousand of the Chedis, Kashis and Karushas were famed as great
chariot fighters, sons of good families who would give up their bodies. They
were brave, none turned back, and their banners were worked with gold. They met
Bhishma in battle like Death with his mouth open and went to the other world
with their horses, chariots and elephants. There was no great chariot fighter of
the Somakas there, great king, who, having met Bhishma in battle, set his mind
on life. Seeing Bhishma's courage, people thought all those warriors had been
led to the city of the king of the dead. No great chariot fighter went out
against him in battle, except the heroic son of Pandu with the white horses and
Krishna for his charioteer, and Shikhandi the Panchala of measureless power.

"Shikhandi, bull of the Bharatas, reaching Bhishma in battle, struck him with
ten arrows at a time in the great battle. Ganga's son looked at Shikhandi out of
the corner of his eye, his eye blazing with anger, as if he would burn him,
Bharata. Remembering that he had been a woman, king, Bhishma did not strike him
in battle, while the whole world watched. And Shikhandi did not understand it.
But Arjuna, great king, said to Shikhandi, 'Hurry! Quickly kill the grandfather!
What is the use of talk, hero? Kill Bhishma, the great chariot fighter. I see no
one else in Yudhishthira's army who could fight the grandfather Bhishma in
battle except you, tiger among men. I tell you this truly.' So addressed by
Partha, bull of the Bharatas, Shikhandi quickly ran at the grandfather with
arrows of many kinds. Taking no thought of those arrows, your father Devavrata
held off Arjuna, angry in battle, with arrows. In the same way the great chariot
fighter sent the whole army of the Pandavas to the other world in battle with
sharp arrows, sir. So too the Pandavas, king, surrounded by a great army,
covered Bhishma as clouds cover the sun. Surrounded on every side, the Bharata,
bull of the Bharatas, burned the heroes in battle like a blazing fire in a
forest.

"There we saw the wonderful manhood of your son, for he fought Partha and
guarded him of the controlled vow. All the worlds were pleased with that deed in
battle of your son the bowman, the great-souled Duhshasana. For he fought the
Parthas and their followers alone, and the Pandavas could not hold back the
fierce one in battle. Chariot fighters were made chariotless by Duhshasana in
battle, great king, and horsemen and very strong elephants, split by sharp
arrows, fell to the ground. Others, elephants hurt by arrows, ran in all
directions. As a fire, finding fuel, would blaze, fierce, with bright flames, so
your son blazed, burning up the Pandavas. None of the great chariot fighters of
the Pandavas dared to conquer or even go out against that great Bharata, except
great Indra's son with the white horses and Krishna for his charioteer. For
Vijaya Arjuna, having beaten him in battle, king, ran at Bhishma himself while
the whole army watched. Your son too, beaten, relying on Bhishma's arms,
recovered again and again and fought, fierce in battle. Fighting Arjuna in
battle, king, he shone.

"Shikhandi, king, pierced the grandfather in battle with arrows with a touch
like the thunderbolt, like snake venom. But they caused your father no pain,
lord of men. Smiling, Ganga's son took those arrows then. As a man hurt by the
heat welcomes torrents of rain, so Ganga's son took Shikhandi's torrents of
arrows. The kshatriyas saw that terrible Bhishma in battle, great king, burning
the armies of the great-souled Pandavas.

"Then your son said to all the troops, sir, 'Run at Phalguna in battle with
chariots from every side! Bhishma, who knows dharma, will protect you all in
battle. Give up your great fear and fight the Pandavas. Here stands Bhishma with
his blazing palm banner, protecting us: the shelter and the armour of all
Dhritarashtra's sons in battle. Not even the thirty gods together could overcome
Bhishma, let alone the great-souled Parthas, mortal as they are and weak. So
run, warriors, and meet Phalguna in battle. Today I will fight Phalguna in
battle, ready, together with all of you, lords of the earth, ready on every
side.'

"Hearing those words of your son the bowman, king, the strong great chariot
fighters went ready against Arjuna. The Videhas and Kalingas with the bands of
the Dasherakas, the Nishadas and Sauviras fell on him in the great battle. The
Bahlikas and Daradas, the easterners and northerners, the Malavas, the
Abhishahas, the Shurasenas, the Shibis and the Vasatis came too. The
Shalvashrayas, the Trigartas and the Ambashthas with the Kekayas fell on Partha
in battle like moths on a fire. Dhananjaya, great king, called to mind divine
weapons and fitted them against all those great chariot fighters with their
ranks. With those weapons of great speed, the very strong Bibhatsu quickly
burned them with the heat of his arrows, as a fire burns moths. As the firm
bowman loosed thousands of arrows, Gandiva was seen in the sky as if blazing.
Hurt by the arrows, great king, their chariots and banners scattered, the kings
together did not come back at the one with the monkey banner. Chariot fighters
fell with their banners, horsemen with their horses, elephants with their
riders, struck by the diademed one's arrows. Then the earth was covered on every
side by the armies of the kings, killed by the arrows loosed from Arjuna's arms
and fleeing in many directions.

"Then strong-armed Partha, having put the army to flight, sent arrows at
Duhshasana in battle. Those iron-headed arrows split your son Duhshasana and all
went into the ground like snakes into an anthill. Then he killed his horses and
brought down his charioteer. He made Vivimshati chariotless with twenty arrows,
lord, and struck him hard with five arrows with bent joints. Having pierced
Kripa, Shalya and Vikarna with many iron arrows, the Kaunteya with white horses
made them chariotless too. So, made chariotless, those five, Kripa and Shalya,
sir, Duhshasana and Vikarna, and Vivimshati too, ran in battle, beaten by the
left-handed archer.

"Having beaten the great chariot fighters so in the morning, king, Partha blazed
in battle like a smokeless fire. Like the sun with its rays, he brought down
other kings too with a shower of arrows, great king. He turned the great chariot
fighters away with showers of arrows. Then he set flowing in battle a great
river with blood for water, Bharata, through the middle of the armies of the
Kurus and the Pandavas. Elephants and crowds of chariots were killed in many
ways by chariot fighters, chariots were killed by elephants, and elephants by
horses and foot soldiers. Bodies and heads, cut in between, of fighters on
elephants, horses and chariots, fell in all directions. The battlefield shone,
covered with fallen and falling princes, great chariot fighters, wearing
earrings and armlets. Foot soldiers too were seen cut by chariot rims and
crushed by elephants, and horses with their horsemen. Crowds of elephants,
horses and chariots ran about on every side, and chariots lay scattered on the
ground with broken wheels, yokes and banners. Drenched with the blood of the
floods of elephants, horses and chariots, the covered battlefield shone like a
red cloud in autumn. Dogs, crows and vultures, wolves with jackals, and
misshapen beasts and birds howled, having found food. Winds of many kinds blew
in all directions, with rakshasas and spirits seen roaring. Golden garlands and
costly flags were seen smoking at once, stirred by the wind. Thousands of white
parasols and great chariots with their banners were seen scattered in hundreds
and thousands. Elephants with flags, hurt by arrows, went in all directions.
Kshatriyas, lord of men, holding clubs, spears and bows, were seen fallen on the
ground on every side.

"Then Bhishma, great king, bringing forth a divine weapon, ran at the Kaunteya
while all the bowmen watched. Shikhandi in armour ran at him in battle as he
came on, ready, and then Bhishma drew back that weapon, which was like fire. At
that very time the Kaunteya with white horses, having bewildered the
grandfather, killed your army."

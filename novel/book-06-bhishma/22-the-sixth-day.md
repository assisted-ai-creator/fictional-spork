summary: The sixth day. The Pandavas draw up the makara array and Bhishma the heron. Bhima kills Drona's charioteer. Dhritarashtra laments that so fine an army is being killed, and Sanjaya tells him the fault is his own. Bhima leaves his chariot and goes on foot with his mace into the ranks of Duryodhana's brothers, who mean to take him alive. Dhrishtadyumna follows him, finds him and takes him into his own chariot. He then stuns the Kauravas with the bewildering weapon, until Drona undoes it. Yudhishthira sends Abhimanyu and eleven others after them, and Drona drives Dhrishtadyumna from his chariot. In the afternoon Abhimanyu wounds Vikarna, Draupadi's sons fight Duryodhana's brothers, and at sunset Bhima breaks Duryodhana's bow, cuts down his jewelled serpent banner and wounds him. Bhishma calls the halt, and Yudhishthira kisses the heads of Dhrishtadyumna and Bhima.

# The Sixth Day

"When the Kurus and the Pandavas had rested, king," said Sanjaya, "they went out
together to battle again as the night passed. There was a great noise among your
men and theirs, Bharata, as the finest chariots were yoked and the elephants
made ready. Foot soldiers and horses put on their armour, Bharata, and the sound
of conches and drums rose tumultuously on every side.

"Then King Yudhishthira said to Dhrishtadyumna, 'Strong-armed one, draw up the
makara array, which scorches enemies.' Told this by Partha, Dhrishtadyumna, the
great chariot fighter, best of chariot fighters, gave his orders to the chariot
fighters, great king. Drupada and Dhananjaya the Pandava were its head. Sahadeva
and Nakula, the great chariot fighter, were its eyes. Mighty Bhimasena was its
snout, great king. Subhadra's son, the sons of Draupadi, the rakshasa
Ghatotkacha, Satyaki and the Dharma king stood in the neck of the array. Virata,
lord of an army, was its back, great king, with Dhrishtadyumna, surrounded by a
great army. The five Kekaya brothers took the left side. Dhrishtaketu, a tiger
among men, and brave Karakarsha took the right wing, to guard the array. At its
feet, great king, stood splendid Kuntibhoja, a great chariot fighter, and
Shatanika, surrounded by a great army. Shikhandi, the mighty great archer,
surrounded by the Somakas, and Iravan stood in the makara's tail.

"When the Pandavas had drawn up this great array, Bharata, they put on their
armour again for battle at sunrise, great king. They went quickly at the
Kauravas with elephants, horses, chariots and foot soldiers, with tall bright
banners and clean, sharp weapons.

"Seeing that army drawn up, your father Devavrata answered it with the great
heron array, king. The great archer, Bharadvaja's son, shone at its beak.
Ashvatthama and Kripa were its eyes, lord of men. Kritavarma, best of men and
best of all bowmen, was at its head, with the Kambojas, the Arattas and the
Bahlikas. In the neck, sir, were the Shurasena and your son Duryodhana, great
king, surrounded by many kings. The king of Pragjyotisha was at its breast, best
of men, with the Madras, Sauviras and Kekayas, surrounded by a great army.
Susharma, lord of Prasthala, with his own army, stood in his armour on the left
wing. The Tusharas, the Yavanas and the Shakas, with the Chuchupas, stood on the
right wing of the array, Bharata. Shrutayus, Shatayus and Somadatta's son, sir,
stood at the rear of the array, guarding each other.

"Then the Pandavas and the Kauravas met for battle at sunrise, great king, and a
great battle began. Chariot fighters went at elephants, and elephants at chariot
fighters. Horsemen went at horsemen, and chariot fighters at horsemen. In the
great battle, king, a chariot fighter went at a charioteer and at elephants.
Elephant riders went at men in chariots, and chariot fighters at horsemen.
Chariot fighters with foot soldiers, and horsemen with foot soldiers, charged
each other in the battle, king, full of fury.

"Guarded by Bhimasena, Arjuna, the twins and other great chariot fighters, the
Pandava army shone like the night with its stars. In the same way your army,
with Bhishma, Kripa, Drona, Shalya, Duryodhana and the others, shone like the
sky filled with planets.

"Bhimasena, Kunti's valiant son, seeing Drona, charged Bharadvaja's son's
division with swift horses. In the battle, king, the brave Drona in anger
pierced Bhima with nine iron arrows, aiming at his vital points. Struck hard by
Bharadvaja's son in the battle, Bhima sent his charioteer to the house of Yama.
Taking the reins himself, mighty Bharadvaja's son blew away the Pandava army as
fire blows away a heap of cotton. Killed by Drona and Bhishma, best of men, the
Srinjayas and the Kekayas turned to flight. In the same way your army, wounded
by Bhima and Arjuna, was bewildered here and there, like a fine woman drunk.

"Then, in that destruction of the best heroes, both arrays broke, and there was
a terrible confusion between yours and theirs, Bharata. We saw a wonder there,
Bharata: your men and the enemy all fought as if they were one host. The
Pandavas and the Kauravas, great chariot fighters, fought on, holding off each
other's weapons, lord of the people."

"Our army has so many virtues, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "and so many kinds
of troops. It is drawn up as the science teaches, and it does not fail. It is
very well kept, always devoted to us, humble, free from vice, its valour proved
before. It is not too old and not young, not thin and not fat. Its men are
quick, well built and tall, with hard, sound bodies. They have taken up armour
and weapons, and many kinds of weapons. They are skilled in sword fighting, in
wrestling and in the mace fight. They are skilled with lances, spears and
javelins in battle, with iron bars, short javelins and darts, with clubs of
every kind, with shaking spears, bows, darts and bright slings, and in fighting
with fists. They are not strangers to learning, and they have worked hard at
their exercises. They are masters of all the arts of handling weapons.

"They are skilled in mounting and dismounting, in going forward and leaping
between, in striking well, in advancing and in retreating. They have been tested
many times on elephants, horses and chariots. They were tested as was right and
paid their wages. They were not taken for their talk, or for favour, or for
kinship, or for friendship, and none of them is of low birth. Their families are
rich and noble, their kin content and honoured. They have mostly done us
service, and they are famous and high-minded.

"It is guarded, my son, by leading men who have won victories, who have done
great deeds many times, like the guardians of the world, famous in the world. It
is guarded by many kshatriyas, respected in the world, who came over to us of
their own will, with their forces and their followers. It is like the great
ocean filled by rivers on every side. It is thronged with chariots and elephants
that are wingless but look like winged things. Its water is warriors of many
kinds, terrible, its waves and ripples its mounts. It is crowded with slings,
swords, maces, spears, arrows and lances. It is thick with banners and
ornaments, studded with bands of jewels. It is stirred by the speed of the wind
with its mounts moving about. Roaring as if without a shore, it is great, like
the sea. It is well guarded by Drona and Bhishma, guarded by Kritavarma, by
Kripa and Duhshasana, by those led by Jayadratha, by Bhagadatta and Vikarna, by
Drona's son, Subala's son and Bahlika. It is guarded by great, strong heroes of
the world.

"That such an army is killed in battle was fated long ago. Men have never seen
such a mustering, Sanjaya, nor have the blessed ancient seers on earth. When
such a flood of troops, well provided with weapons, is killed in battle, what
else is it but fate? All this seems upside down to me, Sanjaya, when such a
terrible force could not get the better of the Pandavas in battle. Or else the
gods have come there for the Pandavas' sake and fight my army, Sanjaya, since it
is being killed. Vidura told me what was good and healthy, Sanjaya, and my
foolish son Duryodhana would not take it. I think that great, all-knowing man
saw all this long ago, just as it has come. Or else it had to be so, Sanjaya, in
every way. As the Maker made it long ago, so it is, and not otherwise."

"You have come to this disaster by your own fault, king," said Sanjaya.
"Duryodhana does not see the things that you saw, bull of the Bharatas, in this
confusion of dharma. It was by your fault, lord of the people, that the dicing
happened long ago. By your fault the war with the Pandavas began. You did the
wrong yourself. Now eat its fruit. A deed done by oneself is eaten by oneself,
king, in this world or the next. You have got what was due to you. So, king, now
that this great disaster has come, be steady and hear the battle as it happened,
sir, as I tell it.

"Bhimasena broke through the great army with sharp arrows, and the hero came up
to all Duryodhana's younger brothers: Duhshasana, Durvishaha, Duhsaha, Durmada,
Jaya, Jayatsena, Vikarna, Chitrasena, Sudarshana, Charuchitra, Suvarma,
Dushkarna and Karna. Seeing these and many other great chariot fighters, sons of
Dhritarashtra, close by and in great anger, mighty Bhima entered the great army
that Bhishma guarded in the battle. Then they called to one another, 'Here is
Vrikodara. Kings, let us take him alive.' Surrounded by those brothers, set on
it, Partha was like the sun at the destruction of creatures, surrounded by cruel
great planets. Reaching the middle of the array, the Pandava felt no fear, as
great Indra in the war of the gods and the asuras felt none when he reached the
danavas.

"Then hundreds of thousands of chariot fighters on every side, lord, surrounded
that one man, covering him with terrible arrows. The hero killed their best
warriors in the battle, the fighters on elephants, horses and chariots, not
caring about the sons of Dhritarashtra. Knowing what they meant, that they
wanted to seize him, great-hearted Bhimasena set his mind on killing them all,
king. Then the Pandava left his chariot, took up his mace and killed that great
ocean of a flood of Dhritarashtra's troops.

"When Bhimasena had gone in, Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, left Drona and went
swiftly to where Subala's son was. The bull among men broke through your great
army and came to Bhimasena's empty chariot in the battle. Seeing Vishoka,
Bhimasena's charioteer, in the battle, Dhrishtadyumna, great king, was dismayed
and lost his wits. Choked with tears, in a voice with no sound in it, he asked
in grief, 'Where is Bhima, dearer to me than my life?'

"Vishoka joined his hands and said to Dhrishtadyumna, 'The mighty, valiant
Pandava left me here and went into this great ocean of the sons of
Dhritarashtra's forces. He said to me with affection, tiger among men, "Wait for
me a moment, charioteer, holding the horses, while I quickly kill these men who
have set out to kill me." Then, seeing the mighty man running with his mace in
his hand, all the armies clashed. In that tumultuous, fearful battle, king, your
friend broke through the great array and went in.'

"Mighty Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, heard Vishoka's words. He answered the
charioteer in the middle of the battle, 'I have no use for life today,
charioteer, if I leave Bhimasena in the battle and cast off my love for the
Pandavas. If I go back without Bhima, what will the kshatriyas say of me, when
Bhima went on the one way and I stood by in the battle? The gods with Agni at
their head bring ill fortune on a man who abandons his companions and goes home
safe. Mighty Bhima is my friend and my kinsman. He is devoted to us, and I am
devoted to him, that killer of enemies. So I shall go where Vrikodara has gone.
Watch me kill the enemy, as Vasava kills the danavas.'

"Having said this, the hero went through the middle of the Bharata army by
Bhimasena's paths, marked by elephants crushed by the mace. Then he saw Bhima
burning up the enemy's army, breaking kings in the battle by force as the wind
breaks trees. As they were killed in the battle, the chariot fighters, horsemen,
foot soldiers and elephants cried out loudly in pain. A cry of 'Alas!' rose from
your army, sir, as it was killed by Bhimasena, that skilled and wonderful
fighter. Then all of them, masters of weapons, surrounded Vrikodara and fought
him fearlessly with a shower of weapons on every side.

"Mighty Prishata's son saw Bhimasena, the best of those who bear weapons,
attacked on every side by the world's heroes with a terrible, well-joined army.
He saw him on foot, his limbs torn by arrows, spitting the poison of his anger,
mace in hand, like Time at the end of time. Prishata's son came up to him and
comforted him. The great man quickly drew out the arrows and took him up into
his own chariot. He embraced Bhimasena tightly and comforted him in the middle
of the enemy.

"Then, as that great fight went on, your son came up to his brothers and said,
'This evil-hearted son of Drupada has joined Bhimasena. Go together, all of you,
and kill him. Do not let the enemy go for our army.' Hearing that, unable to
bear it and driven by their eldest brother's command, the sons of Dhritarashtra
rushed out with raised weapons to kill him, like fierce comets at the end of an
age. The heroes took their bright bows and made the enemy tremble with the noise
of their bowstrings and wheels. They rained arrows on Drupada's son as clouds
rain streams on a mountain. The wonderful fighter struck them down with very
sharp arrows and did not flinch in the battle.

"Then the young son of Drupada, the great chariot fighter, saw your heroic sons
standing round him in the battle, rising up against him. Wanting to kill them
fiercely, he used the bewildering weapon, in great anger at your sons, king, as
great Indra was at the daityas in battle. Then the heroes among men were
bewildered in the battle, their minds and strength struck by the bewildering
weapon. All the Kurus ran on every side, with their horses, elephants and
chariots, when they saw your sons bewildered and senseless, like men whose time
had come.

"At that very time Drona, the best of those who bear weapons, came up to Drupada
and pierced him with three cruel arrows. Badly wounded by Drona in the battle,
king, King Drupada withdrew, remembering their old enmity. Having beaten
Drupada, mighty Drona blew his conch, and all the Somakas were terrified at the
sound. Then splendid Drona, the best of those who bear weapons, heard that your
sons had been bewildered in the battle by the bewildering weapon. Then Drona,
eager for the king's sake, came quickly from the fight. There the great archer,
mighty Bharadvaja's son, saw Dhrishtadyumna and Bhima moving through the great
battle. The great chariot fighter saw your sons lost in bewilderment. Then he
took up the weapon of understanding and destroyed the bewildering weapon.

"Then your sons, the great chariot fighters, their life breath back, went into
battle again against Bhima and Prishata's son. Then Yudhishthira called his own
soldiers and said, 'Let them follow the track of Bhima and Prishata's son in the
battle as best they can. Let twelve chariot fighters in armour, heroes led by
Subhadra's son, find out what has happened. My mind is not at ease.' Given leave
like this, the brave men, valiant fighters, all proud of their manhood, said,
'Yes!' When the sun was at midday, they all set out. The Kekayas, the sons of
Draupadi and brave Dhrishtaketu, with Abhimanyu in front, were surrounded by a
great army. Those tamers of enemies made the needle-mouth array in the battle
and broke through the chariot division of the sons of Dhritarashtra.

"Your army, lord of men, possessed by fear of Bhimasena and bewildered by
Dhrishtadyumna, could not hold back those great archers led by Abhimanyu as they
came on. It stood like a woman on the road, reeling and faint with drink. The
great archers, with their gold-worked banners, came on and charged, wanting to
rescue Dhrishtadyumna and Vrikodara. When those two saw the great archers led by
Abhimanyu, they were glad, and went on killing your army.

"But when the heroic Panchala, Prishata's son, saw his own teacher suddenly
coming, he no longer hoped to kill your sons. He put Vrikodara into the Kekaya's
chariot and charged in great anger at Drona, who had mastered arrows and
weapons. As he came at him quickly, mighty Bharadvaja's son, killer of enemies,
in anger cut his bow with a broad arrow. And he sent hundreds of other arrows at
Prishata's son, for Duryodhana's good, remembering the bread he had eaten from
his master. Then Prishata's son, killer of enemy heroes, took another bow and
pierced Drona with seventy arrows with golden feathers, whetted on stone. Drona,
destroyer of enemies, cut his bow again, and quickly sent his four horses to
Vaivasvata's terrible house with four fine arrows. With a broad arrow the brave
man sent his charioteer to Death. The strong-armed great chariot fighter leapt
quickly down from his chariot with its horses killed and climbed into
Abhimanyu's great chariot.

"Then the army, with its chariots, elephants and horses, shook, while Bhimasena
and Prishata's son looked on. Seeing the army broken by Drona of boundless
power, all those great chariot fighters together could not stop it. Killed by
Drona with sharp arrows, the army whirled about here and there like a churning
ocean. Seeing the army like that, your forces were glad. Seeing the teacher in
his anger burning up the enemy army, the warriors on every side cried out, 'Well
done! Well done!' Bharata.

"Then King Duryodhana, come back from his bewilderment, again held back the
unfallen Bhima with showers of arrows. Your sons, the great chariot fighters,
came together again and fought Bhima in the battle, set on it. Strong-armed
Bhimasena too found his own chariot again in the battle, climbed into it and
went where your son was. Taking up his bright bow of great speed, strong, that
takes away life, he pierced your sons with arrows in the battle.

"Then King Duryodhana struck mighty Bhimasena hard in a vital place with a very
sharp iron arrow. Badly wounded by your son the bowman, the great archer, his
eyes red with anger, swiftly raised his bow. He struck Duryodhana with three
arrows in the arms and the chest. Struck like that, the king did not move, like
the king of mountains. Seeing those two angry men striking each other in the
battle, all Duryodhana's younger brothers, heroes ready to give up their lives,
remembered their old plan to seize Bhima of the terrible deeds. They made up
their minds and set about seizing him.

"As they came on in the battle, mighty Bhimasena went out to meet them, great
king, like an elephant against rival elephants. In great anger the splendid,
famous man struck your son Chitrasena with an iron arrow, great king. And he
struck your other sons in the battle, Bharata, with many kinds of swift arrows
with golden feathers.

"Then the twelve great chariot fighters led by Abhimanyu, sent by the Dharma
king to follow Bhimasena's track, set all their own divisions in order and went
out against your mighty sons, great king. Your mighty sons saw those heroes in
their chariots, bright as the sun and fire, great archers every one, shining
with splendour, blazing in the great battle and bright with golden armour. They
left Bhima in the battle. Kunti's son could not bear that they should go away
alive. He followed all your sons again and harried them.

"Then the great chariot fighters in your army, led by Duryodhana, saw Abhimanyu
joined with Bhimasena and Prishata's son in the battle. They took up their bows
and drove hard with swift horses to where those chariots were. Then in the
afternoon, king, a great battle began between your mighty men and the enemy,
Bharata.

"Abhimanyu killed Vikarna's swift horses and then filled him with twenty-five
small arrows. Leaving his chariot with its horses killed, Vikarna, the great
chariot fighter, climbed into Chitrasena's shining chariot, king. As the two
brothers, increasers of the Kurus, stood in one chariot, Arjuna's son covered
them with a net of arrows, Bharata. Durjaya and Vikarna pierced Arjuna's son
with five iron arrows, and Arjuna's son did not tremble, as Mount Meru does not
move. Duhshasana fought the five Kekayas in the battle, sir, lord of kings, and
it was a marvel.

"The sons of Draupadi, in anger, held back Duryodhana in the battle. Each struck
your son with three arrows, lord of the people. And your son, so hard to beat,
struck each of Draupadi's sons in the battle with sharp arrows, king. Pierced by
them and drenched in blood, he shone like a mountain with its streams mixed with
red ores.

"Mighty Bhishma too, king, drove the Pandava army in the battle as a herdsman
drives his herds. Then the twang of the Gandiva rose to the south of the army,
lord of the people, as Partha killed his enemies. Headless bodies rose on every
side of the field there, in the armies of the Kurus and of the Pandavas,
Bharata. The tigers among men crossed the ocean of the armies in boats of
chariots. Its water was blood, its whirlpools chariots, its islands elephants,
its waves horses. The best of men were seen fallen there by the hundred and the
thousand, with their hands cut off, without armour, without bodies. With dead
elephants in rut, bathed in floods of blood, the earth shone as if heaped with
mountains, best of the Bharatas. We saw a wonder there, among yours and theirs,
Bharata: there was not a man there who did not want to fight. So the heroes,
yours and the Pandavas', fought, seeking great glory and longing for victory in
battle.

"Then, as the sun turned red, King Duryodhana, eager for battle, charged Bhima,
wanting to kill him. Seeing that hero among men, his sworn enemy, coming at him,
Bhimasena in great anger said, 'The time I have longed for through a host of
years has come. Today I will kill you, if you do not leave the battle. Today,
when you are killed, I will drive away all Kunti's suffering and our life in the
forest, and Draupadi's suffering. You were a trickster at the dice, and you
despised the Pandavas. Son of Gandhari, see the disaster that has come from that
evil. Long ago you followed Karna's counsel and Subala's son's, and you did as
you liked, without a thought for the Pandavas, out of greed. In your delusion
you despised the Dasharha when he came to plead, and you gladly gave Uluka his
message. Today I will kill you with your followers and your kin. I will settle
the account for the evil you did long ago.'

"Having said this, he drew and whirled his terrible bow again and again, took up
terrible arrows as bright as great thunderbolts, and in his anger swiftly loosed
twenty-six at Suyodhana. They were like blazing flames of fire, like the
thunderbolt, and flew straight. Then he pierced his bow with two and his
charioteer with two, and with four he sent his swift horses to Yama's house.
With two well-drawn arrows the destroyer of enemies cut the king's umbrella from
his fine chariot in the battle. With three he cut his blazing, splendid banner,
and when he had cut it, he roared aloud while your son watched. The splendid
banner, adorned with many jewels, fell suddenly from the chariot to the ground,
like lightning from a cloud. All the kings saw the Kuru lord's banner cut down,
the blazing, fine jewelled serpent, bright as the sun. Then Bhima, the great
chariot fighter, almost smiling, struck him with ten arrows in the battle, as
men strike a great elephant with goads.

"Then the king of the Sindhus, Jayadratha, best of chariot fighters, took up the
guard of Duryodhana's rear, as good men do. Kripa, best of chariot fighters,
took the unforgiving Kaurava, Duryodhana of boundless power, up into his
chariot, king. Deeply pierced and in pain from Bhimasena in the battle, King
Duryodhana sank down on the floor of the chariot. Then Jayadratha, wanting to
kill Bhima, surrounded him with many thousands of chariots and closed off every
direction to him.

"Then, king, Dhrishtaketu and brave Abhimanyu, the Kekayas and the sons of
Draupadi fought your sons. Chitrasena, Suchitra, Chitrashva and Chitradarshana,
Charuchitra, Sucharu, and Nanda and Upanandaka: these eight great archers,
delicate and famous, surrounded Abhimanyu's chariot on every side, king. Then
great-hearted Abhimanyu quickly struck them, piercing each with five straight
arrows like the thunderbolt and Death, loosed from his bright weapon. Unable to
bear it, they all rained sharp arrows on Subhadra's son, the best of chariot
fighters, as clouds rain on Mount Meru. Abhimanyu was hard pressed in the
battle. But the master of weapons, mad in battle, made your men tremble, great
king, as the Wielder of the Thunderbolt made the great asuras tremble in the war
of the gods and the asuras.

"Then the best of chariot fighters sent fourteen terrible broad arrows like
venomous snakes at Vikarna, Bharata. He cut his banner, his charioteer and his
horses, seeming to dance in the battle. Then mighty Subhadra's son sent more
arrows at Vikarna, yellow, unblunted, whetted on stone. Feathered with heron and
peacock plumes, they reached Vikarna, pierced his body and went into the ground
like blazing snakes. Those arrows with golden feathers and heads were seen on
the ground, wet with Vikarna's blood, as if they were vomiting blood.

"Seeing Vikarna pierced, his own brothers charged the chariots led by Subhadra's
son in the battle. Coming quickly on against those men in their chariots, bright
as the sun, they pierced one another in the battle, furious and mad for battle.
Durmukha pierced Shrutakarman with seven swift arrows, cut his banner with one
and struck his charioteer with seven. Coming up close, he killed his horses,
swift as the wind and covered with golden nets, with six, and brought down his
charioteer. Standing in his chariot with its horses killed, Shrutakarman, the
great chariot fighter, in anger hurled a spear like a great blazing meteor.
Shining and sharp, it split famous Durmukha's broad armour, tore through him and
went into the ground. Seeing him without a chariot there, mighty Sutasoma took
him up into his own chariot while all the armies watched.

"Heroic Shrutakirti went against your famous son Jayatsena in the battle, king,
wanting to kill him. As great Shrutakirti drew his bow, your son Jayatsena,
almost laughing, cut it in the battle with a very sharp razor arrow, Bharata.
Seeing his brother's bow cut, splendid Shatanika came on, roaring again and
again like a lion. Shatanika stretched his bow hard in the battle and quickly
pierced Jayatsena with ten arrows. Then with another very sharp arrow that could
split any armour, Shatanika pierced Jayatsena hard in the heart.

"As that went on, Dushkarna, near his brother, in a fury of anger cut the bow of
Nakula's son in the battle. Then mighty Shatanika took another fine bow that
could bear any load and took up sharp arrows. Calling, 'Stand! Stand!' to
Dushkarna in front of his brother, he loosed sharp arrows like blazing snakes.
Then with one he cut his bow, and with two his charioteer, sir, and quickly
pierced him with seven. The blameless man quickly killed all his dappled horses,
swift as the mind, with twelve sharp arrows. Then with another well-loosed broad
arrow that brings men down, in anger, he pierced Dushkarna hard in the heart in
the battle.

"Seeing Dushkarna struck down, king, five great chariot fighters surrounded
Shatanika on every side, wanting to kill him. The five Kekaya brothers charged
in fury at famous Shatanika as he was covered with flights of arrows. Seeing
them come on, your sons, great chariot fighters, went out to meet them, great
king, like elephants against great elephants. Durmukha and Durjaya, young
Durmarshana, Shatrunjaya and Shatrusaha, all angry and famous, went out
together, great king, against the Kekaya brothers. Their chariots were like
cities, yoked with horses swift as the mind, adorned with flags of many colours.
The heroes, bearing fine bows, with bright armour and banners, went into the
enemy's army like lions from one forest into another.

"Between them there was a tumultuous and most cruel battle, the chariots and
elephants tangled together, each killing the other, each wronged by the other,
king, filling Yama's kingdom. When the sun had just set, they fought a very
cruel fight, and chariot fighters and horsemen were scattered by the thousand.
Then Bhishma, son of Shantanu, in anger, destroyed the army of those great men

**Divergences.**

* **Not killed here.** Vikarna is pierced through by Abhimanyu's arrows (6.75.29–30), Durmukha by Shrutakarman's spear (6.75.36), and Jayatsena and Dushkarna are pierced "in the heart" by Shatanika (6.75.42, 48). The text does not say that any of them dies. Vikarna, Durmukha and Dushkarna all fight again in Book 7 (7.24.34; 7.19.27; 7.130.29–34). At 6.75.49 *duṣkarṇaṃ nihataṃ dṛṣṭvā* is therefore "seeing Dushkarna struck down". The rejected \*318 has him fall "like a tree broken by the thunderbolt".
* **The bewildering weapon.** Dhrishtadyumna uses the *pramohana* weapon on Duryodhana's brothers, and Drona undoes it with the *prajñāstra*, the weapon of understanding (6.73.42–49). The rejected \*306 says that Drona had given it to him.
* **Bhima's charioteer.** Vishoka (6.73.18–22), as on day 4.
* **"Karna" among Duryodhana's brothers.** The list at 6.73.6–7 includes a son of Dhritarashtra named Karna (as at 1.108.3), not Kunti's son, who is not fighting while Bhishma commands.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*293–\*321.

**Choices.**

* 6.71.33 *samadeva varāṅganā*: "like a fine woman drunk", and 6.73.57 *pramadevādhvani sthitā*: "like a woman on the road, reeling and faint with drink". Both are the text's similes.
* 6.72.14 *apakṣaiḥ pakṣasaṃkāśaiḥ*: "wingless but look like winged things".
* 6.73.35 and 6.73.36–38 are one movement in the text. Dhrishtadyumna reaches Bhima, draws out the arrows and embraces him.
* 6.73.49 *prajñāstra*: "the weapon of understanding".
* 6.73.55 *sūcīmukha*: "the needle-mouth array".
* 6.73.63 *bhartṛpiṇḍam anusmaran*: "remembering the bread he had eaten from his master".
* 6.75.15 *nāgaṃ maṇimayam*: Duryodhana's banner is "the fine jewelled serpent".
* 6.75.17 *pārṣṇiṃ … jagrāha*: "took up the guard of Duryodhana's rear".

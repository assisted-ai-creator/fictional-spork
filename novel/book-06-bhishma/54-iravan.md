# Iravan

Dhritarashtra said, "Seeing so many of my sons killed by one man, Sanjaya, what
did Bhishma, Drona and Kripa do in battle? Day after day my sons go to
destruction, Sanjaya. I think, charioteer, that they are struck down by fate in
every way, since all my sons are beaten and never win. They were in the middle
of Bhishma, Drona and the great-souled Kripa, of Somadatta's brave son and
Bhagadatta, of Ashvatthama, son, and of other great-souled heroes, and still
they were killed in battle. What is it but fate? Foolish Duryodhana did not
understand what was said before. I held him back, son, and Bhishma and Vidura,
and Gandhari too, always wishing him well. The dull-witted one did not
understand it then, out of folly, and this fruit has come to him: Bhimasena in
anger leads my senseless sons to Yama's home day after day in battle."

"The fine words of the steward have come home to you," said Sanjaya. "You did
not understand them, lord, when that good advice was given: 'Keep your sons from
the dice game. Do not wrong the Pandavas.' You would not listen to the words of
your friends, who wished you well and kept saying the same thing, any more than
a dying man listens to wholesome medicine. Those words, well spoken, have come
home to you. Because they did not follow the wholesome words of Vidura, Drona,
Bhishma and others who wished them well, the Kauravas are going to destruction.
All that has long gone by, lord of the people. So hear from me, truly, how the
battle went.

"At midday a very fierce battle broke out that brought destruction to the world,
king. Hear it as I tell it. Then at the command of the son of Dharma all the
troops ran in fury at Bhishma, wanting to kill him. Dhrishtadyumna, Shikhandi
and the great chariot fighter Satyaki, with their ranks in order, went against
Bhishma, great king. Arjuna, the sons of Draupadi and Chekitana went in battle
against all the kings Duryodhana had appointed. The hero Abhimanyu, the great
chariot fighter Hidimba's son, and Bhimasena in anger ran at the Kauravas. The
Kauravas were killed in battle by the Pandavas, divided three ways, and in the
same way, king, the enemy were killed in battle by the Kauravas.

"Drona, the best of chariot fighters, in anger ran at the Somakas with the
Srinjayas, to send them to Yama's home. There was a great cry from the
great-souled Srinjayas, king, as they were killed in battle by Bharadvaja's son,
the bowman. Many kshatriyas killed by Drona in battle were seen writhing there
like men tormented by sickness. A sound was heard without pause in battle, of
men moaning, crying out and groaning, like men wasted by hunger. In the same way
the very strong Bhimasena in anger made a terrible slaughter of the Kauravas,
like a second Death. As the armies killed one another there in the great battle,
a terrible river began to flow, running with floods of blood. That battle of the
Kurus and the Pandavas, great king, was great and terrible in form, swelling the
kingdom of Yama.

"Then Bhima, angry in battle and above all impetuous, reached the force of
elephants and sent them to death. There, Bharata, struck by Bhima's iron arrows,
the elephants fell, sank down, trumpeted and ran in all directions. Great
elephants with their trunks cut and their feet cut, sir, cried out in fear like
herons and lay down on the earth. Nakula and Sahadeva ran at the force of
horses. Those horses with golden headbands and trappings of gold were seen
killed in hundreds and thousands. The earth was strewn with falling horses,
king. It shone, best of men, with horses of many forms, with their tongues out,
breathing, crying, lifeless. The earth was terrible to see here and there, lord
of the people, with horses killed in battle by Arjuna, Bharata. It was covered
with broken chariots, cut banners and very bright parasols, with necklaces,
golden ornaments and armlets, and with heads with earrings. Turbans were thrown
off and flags lay on every side, with fine chariot bottoms and yoke-ropes and
reins. So the earth shone, king, like spring with flowers. So this destruction
came on the Pandavas too, Bharata, with Bhishma son of Shantanu angry, and
Drona, best of chariot fighters, Ashvatthama, Kripa and Kritavarma. And with the
others angry, there was destruction of your men too.

---

"While that terrible destruction of the best heroes went on, king, the glorious
Shakuni, son of Subala, ran at the Pandavas. So too the Satvata son of Hridika,
killer of enemy heroes, ran at the Pandava army in battle, king.

"Then the strong son of the Pandava, the scorcher of enemies, went gladly
against that army with the finest horses, swift as the wind, adorned with gold,
armoured and well equipped. There were the chief horses of the Kambojas, horses
born by rivers, horses of Aratta, of Mahi and of Sindhu. There were fine white
horses of Vanayu, mountain horses, and other swift horses born in Tittira, fast
as the wind.

"This was Arjuna's son, the mighty Iravan by name, born to wise Partha by the
daughter of the snake king. She had been given by the great-souled Airavata when
she was childless and wretched, her spirit low, after her husband was killed by
Suparna. Partha took her as his wife, for she was in the power of desire. So
Arjuna's son was born in another man's field. He grew up in the world of the
snakes, protected by his mother, abandoned by his evil-hearted paternal uncle
out of hatred for Partha. He was handsome, full of courage, endowed with good
qualities, of true courage. Hearing that Arjuna had gone to Indra's world, he
went there quickly. Coming up to his great-souled father, of true courage, he
greeted him calmly and humbly with joined palms. 'I am Iravan, bless you, and I
am your son, lord.' He told him all about his meeting with his mother, and the
Pandava remembered it all as it had happened. Embracing his son, so like himself
in his qualities, Partha was glad in the dwelling of the king of the gods. Then
Arjuna gave the strong-armed one an affectionate command in the world of the
gods, king, about his own task, Bharata: 'When the time of war comes, you must
give us help, lord.' He said, 'Very well,' and when the time of war came he
arrived, surrounded by many horses, king, of whatever colour and speed he
wished.

"Those horses with golden headbands, of many colours, swift as the mind, flew up
at once, king, like swans on the great ocean. They reached your very swift
troops of horses, striking chest against chest and nose against nose, and fell
at once to the ground, king, struck with great force. As those troops of horses
fell against one another, a cruel sound was heard, like the swoop of Suparna. In
the same way, great king, the horsemen met in battle and made a terrible
slaughter of one another. While that very tumultuous, crowded fight went on, the
troops of horses on both sides were destroyed on every side. Their arrows spent,
their horses killed, worn out with toil, the brave men went to their end,
cutting one another down.

"Then, with the force of horses destroyed and only a little left, Bharata, the
brave sons of Subala came out at the head of the battle. They rode the finest
horses, of good breeding and in their prime, swift as the wind and with the
wind's force in their touch. Gaja, Gavaksha, Vrishaka, Charmavan, Arjava and
Shuka: these six, full of strength, came out of the great army. Though Shakuni
and their own very strong warriors held them back, they were in armour, skilled
in battle, fierce in form and very strong. Splitting that very unconquerable
force, strong-armed one, with a great army behind them, seeking heaven and
wanting victory, the Gandharas, mad for battle, went in gladly.

"Seeing them come in, the mighty Iravan said to his warriors with their bright
ornaments and weapons, 'Make a plan so that all these warriors of
Dhritarashtra's son, with their followers and mounts, are killed in battle.'
'Very well,' said all Iravan's warriors, and they killed the enemy force, which
others could not conquer in battle. Seeing that force brought down by force in
battle, all the sons of Subala could not bear it. They ran at Iravan and
surrounded him on every side. Striking with sharp spears and urging one another
on, the brave men ran about, making a great confusion.

"Then Iravan, split by the sharp spears of those great-souled men, was drenched
in streaming blood like an elephant pierced with goads. Struck hard in the
chest, the back and both sides, one man against many, he did not falter, king,
because of his firmness. Then Iravan in anger pierced them all with sharp arrows
in battle and stunned them, the conqueror of enemy cities. The tamer of enemies
pulled all the spears out of his own body and struck the sons of Subala with
them in battle. He drew a sharp sword, took up a shield, and came quickly on
foot, wanting to kill the sons of Subala in battle. Then all the sons of Subala
came to again and, possessed by anger, ran at Iravan once more. Iravan too,
proud of his strength, showing the lightness of his hands with his sword, went
against all the sons of Subala. Moving with lightness, all those sons of Subala,
though they moved swiftly, could find no opening. Then, seeing him again on the
ground in battle, they all surrounded him closely and set about seizing him. As
they came close, the destroyer of enemies cut their limbs with his sword, with
forehand and backhand strokes. Their weapons and their adorned arms fell, and
with their limbs cut they went to the ground, lifeless. Only Vrishaka, great
king, much wounded, escaped from that very terrible cutting down of heroes.

"Seeing them all fallen, Duryodhana was afraid. In anger he spoke to the
rakshasa of terrible looks, Rishyashringa's son, a great archer, a master of
illusion, tamer of enemies, an enemy of Bhimasena from of old because of the
killing of Baka. 'See, hero, how this strong son of Phalguna, a master of
illusion, has done me the terrible harm of destroying my army. You, son, go
where you wish and are skilled in the weapon of illusion, and you have a feud
with Partha. So kill him in battle.'

"'Very well,' said the rakshasa of terrible looks, and he went with a lion roar
to where Arjuna's young son was. He was surrounded by his own ranks of brave
hard hitters, well mounted, skilled in battle, fighting with clean spears,
wanting to kill the very strong Iravan in battle. Iravan too in anger, hurrying,
mighty, the killer of enemies, held off the rakshasa who wanted to kill him.
Seeing him come, the very strong rakshasa hurried to use illusion. He made as
many horses out of illusion, ridden by terrible rakshasas with tridents and axes
in hand. Those two thousand hard hitters met in fury and soon sent one another
to the world of the dead. When that army was killed, the two of them, mad for
battle, stood against each other in battle like Vritra and Vasava. Seeing the
rakshasa, mad for battle, running at him, the very strong Iravan, furious with
anger, ran at him. As the evil-minded one came close in battle, he cut his
blazing bow and his five arrows with his sword. Seeing his bow cut, he went at
speed into the sky, as if bewildering the angry Iravan with illusion. Then
Iravan too flew up into the sky. Bewildering the rakshasa with illusions, he cut
his limbs with arrows, knowing all his vital points, taking whatever form he
wished, hard to approach. Cut again and again by arrows like that, the best of
the rakshasas grew whole again, great king, and regained his youth. For illusion
is inborn in them, and their age and form are as they wish. So the rakshasa's
body, cut again and again, grew back. Iravan too, in anger, cut that very strong
rakshasa again and again with a sharp axe. Being cut by that strong one like a
tree, the brave rakshasa roared terribly, and the sound was tumultuous. Wounded
by the axe, the rakshasa streamed much blood. Then the strong one grew angry and
put on speed in battle.

"Then Rishyashringa's son, seeing his enemy so strong in battle, took on a great
and terrible form and set about seizing him at the head of the battle, while
everyone there watched. Seeing such an illusion of the great-souled rakshasa,
Iravan too in anger began to create an illusion. As he was overcome by anger,
never turning back in battle, his mother's kin came to him. Surrounded on every
side in battle by many snakes, king, he took on a very great form like the
coiled Ananta. Then he covered the rakshasa with snakes of many kinds. Covered
by the snakes, the bull among rakshasas thought for a moment, took on the form
of Suparna and ate the serpents. When his mother's kin had been eaten by
illusion, the rakshasa killed the bewildered Iravan with his sword. The rakshasa
brought down to the ground Iravan's head, with its earrings and crown, bright as
a lotus or the moon.

"When the hero, Arjuna's son, had been killed by the rakshasa, the sons of
Dhritarashtra and their kings were freed from grief. In that great and fearful
battle a great and terrible confusion of the two armies came about again.
Horses, elephants and foot soldiers, mixed together, were killed by elephants.
Chariots and elephants were killed there by foot soldiers. Floods of foot
soldiers and chariots, and many horses, were killed in battle by chariot
fighters in that crowd of yours and theirs, king. Not knowing that his own true
son had been killed, Arjuna killed the brave kings guarding Bhishma in battle.
In the same way your men, king, and the very strong Srinjayas killed one
another, offering up their lives in battle. With hair loose, armour gone,
chariots lost and bows cut, they fought one another with their bare arms. So too
the very strong Bhishma killed great chariot fighters with arrows that went past
the vital points, making the Pandava army tremble in battle. By him many men in
Yudhishthira's army were killed, elephants, horsemen, chariot fighters and
horses. There, Bharata, seeing Bhishma's courage in battle, we saw something
most wonderful, like Shakra's courage.

"So too was Bhimasena's, and Prishata's grandson's, Bharata, and the battle of
the Satvata bowman was fierce. Seeing Drona's valour, fear came over the
Pandavas. 'He alone in battle could kill us with our soldiers,' they said, great
king, hard pressed by Drona in battle. 'How much more when he is surrounded by
hosts of warriors, heroes of the earth?' While that terrible battle went on,
bull of the Bharatas, the heroes of both armies could not bear one another. Like
men possessed, become rakshasas, the very strong bowmen, yours and the
Pandavas', fought in fury, son. We saw no warrior there, lord of men, who
guarded his life in that battle like the battle of the daityas."

# The Northern Kurus

"Tell me in full, wise Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "about the northern and
eastern sides of Meru, and the mountain Malyavat."

"South of Nila, king, and on the northern side of Meru," said Sanjaya, "are the
holy Northern Kurus, where the perfected ones live. There the trees have sweet
fruit and always bear flowers and fruit. The flowers are fragrant and the fruits
full of juice. Some of the trees there, lord of the people, bear the fruit of
every wish. Other trees there, lord of men, called milk trees, always pour out
milk of six tastes, like amrita, and clothes are born in their fruits, and
ornaments. All the ground is made of jewels, with fine golden sand, pleasant to
touch everywhere and free of mud, lord of the people. All the men born there
have fallen from the world of the gods. They have the same beauty and virtues,
on level ground or uneven. Couples are born there, and the women are like
apsarases. They drink the milk of those milk trees, like amrita. A couple is
born together and grows up equally, with the same beauty, virtues and dress,
each devoted to the other like a pair of chakravaka birds, lord. Free of
sickness and grief, always glad at heart, they live ten thousand years and ten
hundred years, great king, and they never leave each other. Birds called
bharunda, with sharp beaks and great strength, carry off the dead and throw them
into caves. I have told you of the Northern Kurus in brief, king.

"Now I will tell you truly of the eastern side of Meru. Its first consecration
is that of Bhadrashva, lord of the people, where there is the forest of
Bhadrasala and the great tree Kalamra. The Kalamra, great king, always bears
flowers and fruit and is lovely. It is a yojana high and served by the perfected
ones and the charanas. The men there are white, full of energy and very strong,
and the women are the colour of white water lilies, beautiful and lovely to see.
They have the light of the moon, the colour of the moon, faces like the full
moon and bodies as cool as the moon, and they are skilled in dance and song.
Life there lasts ten thousand years, bull of the Bharatas. They drink the juice
of the Kalamra and stay young for ever.

"South of Nila and north of Nishadha is the great, eternal Jambu tree called
Sudarshana. It bears the fruit of every wish, it is holy and served by the
perfected ones and the charanas. After its name the eternal Jambudvipa is
called. The king of trees is eleven hundred yojanas high, bull of the Bharatas,
touching the sky, lord of men. Its girth is two thousand five hundred cubits,
and its fruits burst with juice. As they fall to the ground they make a great
noise and pour out a juice like silver, king. The juice of the fruit of that
Jambu becomes a river, lord of the people, goes round Meru keeping it on the
right, and flows to the Northern Kurus. The people there always drink that juice
gladly, lord of the people, and when they have drunk the juice of the fruit, old
age does not trouble them. There is the gold called Jambunada, the ornament of
the gods, and the men born there are the colour of the young sun.

"On the peak of Malyavat burns the fire called Samvartaka, the fire of time,
bull of the Bharatas. On the eastern peak of Malyavat is Purvantagandika.
Malyavat stretches fifty thousand yojanas. The men born there are like great
silver. All of them have fallen from the world of Brahma, and all speak of
Brahman. Practising austerity, they keep their seed drawn up, and to protect
beings they enter the sun. Sixty thousand and sixty hundred of them go before
Aruna, surrounding the sun. For sixty thousand and sixty hundred years, burned
by the heat of the sun, they then enter the circle of the moon."

---

"Tell me truly, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "the names of the lands and the
mountains, and those who live on the mountains."

"South of Shveta and north of Nila," said Sanjaya, "is the land called Ramanaka,
and the men born there are all of pure birth and lovely to see, and devoted
above all to love. They live eleven thousand five hundred years, great king,
always glad at heart. South of Shringin and north of Shveta is the land called
Hairanvata, where the river Hairanvati flows. They follow the yakshas, great
king, rich and lovely to see, very strong and always glad at heart. They live
eleven thousand five hundred years, lord of the people. Shringavat has three
peaks, lord of men: one made of jewels, one of wonderful gold, and one made of
every jewel and adorned with palaces. There the self-shining goddess Shandili
always lives.

"North of the peak, at the ocean's edge, lord of the people, beyond Shringavat,
is the land called Airavata. The sun does not burn there, and the men do not
grow old. The moon with the stars is covered there, as if it had become light
itself. The men born there have the splendour and the colour of lotuses, eyes
like lotus petals and the fragrance of lotus leaves. They do not blink, they are
fragrant, they eat nothing and have mastered their senses. All of them have
fallen from the world of the gods and are free of passion, king. They live
thirteen thousand years, lord of the people, best of the Bharatas.

"North of the ocean of milk the lord Hari, Vaikuntha, lives on a chariot made of
gold. That carriage has eight wheels, is drawn by beings, swift as thought, the
colour of fire, very fast and adorned with gold. He is the lord of all beings
and all-pervading, bull of the Bharatas. He is the short and the long of it, the
maker and the one who makes others act. He is earth, water and space, wind and
fire, lord of the earth. He is the sacrifice of all beings, and fire is his
mouth."

When Sanjaya had said this, high-minded King Dhritarashtra fell to brooding
about his sons, lord of the people. When he had thought it over, great king, he
spoke again. "No doubt, son of a suta, time gathers up the world and creates it
all again. Nothing here is lasting. Nara and Narayana, the all-knowing, who
upholds all beings: the gods call that lord Vaikuntha, and the Vedas call him
Vishnu."

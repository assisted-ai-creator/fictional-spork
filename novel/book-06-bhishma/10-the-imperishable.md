summary: Chapters 8 to 10 of the Bhagavad Gita, told simply, with footnotes. Krishna explains the words he used, says that what a person thinks of at death decides where they go, and describes the day and night of Brahma and the bright and dark paths of the dead; he reveals the royal secret, that everything rests in him though he is not bound by it, that he accepts even a leaf or a flower offered with love, and that no one who loves him is lost; then he names his powers, the best of every kind of thing, and says the whole world is held up by a single part of him.

# The Imperishable

"'Krishna, best of persons,' said Arjuna, 'what is Brahman? What is the self?
What is action? What is it that is said to be "about beings", and what is called
"about gods"? Who is "about sacrifice" here in this body, and how? And how are
you to be known at the hour of death by those who have controlled themselves?'

"'Brahman is the highest, the Imperishable,' said Krishna. 'Its own nature is
called the self. The creative force that brings beings into existence is called
action. What is "about beings" is everything that perishes. What is "about gods"
is the Person. And I myself am the one "about sacrifice", here in the
body.[^b6c10-1]

"'Whoever leaves the body at the time of death thinking of me alone comes to my
being. There is no doubt of that. Whatever a person thinks of when he leaves the
body at the end, that is what he goes to, Arjuna, because he has always been
shaped by thinking of it.[^b6c10-2] So think of me at all times, and fight. With
your mind and understanding given to me, you will surely come to me.

"'With a mind trained by practice, which does not wander off to anything else, a
person who keeps thinking of the highest divine Person goes to him, Arjuna. He
is the seer, the ancient one, the ruler, smaller than the smallest, the maker of
all, whose form cannot be imagined, bright as the sun, beyond the darkness. At
the hour of death, with an unmoving mind, joined in love and by the power of
yoga, a person who draws his breath fully up between his eyebrows reaches that
highest divine Person.

"'Now I will tell you briefly about the place that those who know the Veda call
the Imperishable, which self-controlled people free from passion enter, and for
which people keep the vow of celibacy. He shuts all the gates of the body, holds
the mind in the heart, draws his breath up into his head, and stays firm in
yoga. Saying the single syllable Om, which is Brahman, and thinking of me, the
one who leaves his body and goes reaches the highest goal.

"'I am easy to reach, Arjuna, for the yogi who is always joined, who thinks of
me constantly and of nothing else. When great souls have come to me, they are
not born again into this passing world, which is a home of sorrow. They have
reached the highest perfection. All the worlds, even the world of Brahma, come
round again and again, Arjuna. But when a person has come to me, there is no
more rebirth.

"'People who know day and night know that a day of Brahma lasts a thousand ages,
and his night lasts a thousand ages too.[^b6c10-3] When his day begins, all
things that can be seen come out of what cannot be seen. When his night falls,
they melt back into that same thing, the unseen. This same crowd of beings comes
into being again and again, and melts away helplessly when night falls, Arjuna,
and comes out again when day begins.

"'But beyond that unseen there is another unseen being, eternal, which does not
perish when all beings perish. It is called the unseen, the Imperishable. They
say it is the highest goal. Those who reach it do not come back. That is my
highest home. That highest Person, Arjuna, in whom all beings live and by whom
all this is filled, can be reached by love that turns to nothing else.

"'Now I will tell you the times at which yogis die and do not come back, and the
times at which they die and come back. Fire, light, day, the bright half of the
month, the six months when the sun moves north: the people who know Brahman and
die then go to Brahman.[^b6c10-4] Smoke, night, the dark half of the month, the
six months when the sun moves south: the yogi who dies then reaches the light of
the moon and comes back. These two paths, the bright and the dark, are thought
to be the world's eternal paths. By one, a person goes and does not return. By
the other, he comes back again. No yogi who knows these two paths is ever
confused, Arjuna. So be joined in yoga at all times.

"'Whatever reward is promised for the Vedas, for sacrifices, austerities and
gifts, the yogi who knows this goes beyond it all, and reaches the highest first
home.'

"'Now I will tell you, since you do not find fault,' said Krishna, 'the deepest
secret, knowledge together with wisdom. When you know it, you will be free from
evil. It is the royal knowledge, the royal secret,[^b6c10-5] the highest thing
that makes pure. It can be known directly. It is right, it is very easy to
practise, and it never fades. People who have no faith in this teaching do not
reach me, Arjuna. They come back to the road of death and rebirth.

"'This whole world is filled by me, in my unseen form. All beings rest in me,
but I do not rest in them. And yet beings do not rest in me. See my divine
power! My self supports beings and brings them into being, but it does not rest
in them. The great wind blows everywhere, yet always stays in space. In the same
way, understand, all beings rest in me.[^b6c10-6]

"'At the end of an age of the world, Arjuna, all beings go back into my nature.
At the beginning of an age, I send them out again. Resting on my own nature, I
send out this whole crowd of beings again and again. They are helpless, under
the power of nature. These actions do not bind me, Arjuna. I sit as if I were
not involved, and I do not cling to these actions. Under my watch, nature gives
birth to all that moves and all that does not. That is why the world goes round
and round.

"'Fools look down on me when I have taken a human body. They do not know my
higher being as the great Lord of beings. Their hopes are empty, their actions
are empty and their knowledge is empty. They have lost their minds and taken on
the confusing nature of rakshasas and asuras. But great souls, Arjuna, take on
the divine nature. They love me with minds that turn to nothing else, knowing me
as the unchanging source of beings. They always praise me, strive with firm
vows, bow to me with love, and worship me, always joined. Others worship me with
the sacrifice of knowledge, as one, as separate, and in many ways, facing every
way.

"'I am the ritual. I am the sacrifice. I am the offering to the ancestors, and I
am the healing herb. I am the sacred words. I am the butter poured on the fire.
I am the fire, and I am the offering. I am the father of this world, its mother,
its supporter and its grandfather. I am what is to be known, the thing that
makes pure, the syllable Om, and the Rig, Sama and Yajur Vedas. I am the goal,
the supporter, the lord, the witness, the home, the shelter and the friend. I am
the beginning, the end and the middle, the storehouse and the seed that never
dies. I give heat. I hold back the rain and I send it. I am deathlessness, and I
am death. I am what is and what is not, Arjuna.

"'People who know the three Vedas, who drink the soma and are cleansed of their
wrongs, worship me with sacrifices and pray to go to heaven. They reach the holy
world of the king of the gods, and enjoy the gods' divine pleasures in heaven.
When they have enjoyed that wide world of heaven, and their good deeds are used
up, they come back into the world of mortals.[^b6c10-7] So those who follow the
dharma of the three Vedas, wanting what they want, keep going and coming.

"'But those who think of me and nothing else, and worship me, always joined, I
bring them what they need and keep what they have. Even those who love other
gods and worship them with faith are really worshipping me, Arjuna, though not
in the proper way. For I am the one who enjoys all sacrifices, and their lord.
But they do not know me as I truly am, so they fall. Those who are devoted to
the gods go to the gods. Those devoted to the ancestors go to the ancestors.
Those who worship spirits go to the spirits. And those who worship me come to
me.

"'If someone offers me a leaf, a flower, a fruit or some water with love, I
accept that gift of love from a person whose heart is pure.[^b6c10-8] Whatever
you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer in the fire, whatever you give,
whatever austerity you practise, Arjuna, do it as an offering to me. In this way
you will be freed from the chains of action, with their good and bad results.
With yourself joined in the yoga of giving up, you will be set free, and you
will come to me.

"'I am the same to all beings. No one is hateful to me, and no one is dear. But
those who worship me with love are in me, and I am in them. Even if a person who
has done very wrong worships me and turns to nothing else, he should be thought
of as good, because he has made the right decision.[^b6c10-9] He soon becomes a
person of dharma and finds lasting peace. Know this for certain, Arjuna: no one
who loves me is ever lost.

"'Those who take shelter in me, Arjuna, even people born into low births, women,
vaishyas and shudras, also reach the highest goal.[^b6c10-10] How much more,
then, holy brahmins and devoted royal seers! You have come into this passing,
unhappy world. So love me. Fix your mind on me. Love me, worship me, bow to me.
With yourself joined like this, with me as your goal, you will come to me.'

"'Listen again, strong-armed one,' said Krishna, 'to my highest word, which I
will tell you for your good, because you delight in it. Neither the crowds of
gods nor the great seers know where I came from. For I am the beginning of the
gods and the great seers, in every way. The person who knows me as unborn,
without beginning, the great Lord of the world, is not confused among mortals,
and is set free from all wrongs.

"'Understanding, knowledge, clear thinking, patience, truth, self-control and
calm, pleasure and pain, being and not being, fear and no fear, not
harming,[^b6c10-11] evenness, contentment, austerity, giving, fame and shame:
all these different states of beings come from me alone. The seven great seers
of old, and the four Manus, came from my mind and share my nature. All the
creatures in the world come from them. The one who truly knows these powers of
mine and my yoga is joined to me by a yoga that does not waver. There is no
doubt of that.

"'I am the source of everything. Everything comes out of me. Knowing this, the
wise love me with all their feeling. With their thoughts on me and their lives
given to me, they teach one another, always talk of me, and are content and
happy. To those who are always joined, who love me with delight, I give the yoga
of understanding by which they come to me. Out of pity for them, I live in their
hearts and destroy the darkness born of ignorance with the shining lamp of
knowledge.'

"'You are the highest Brahman,' said Arjuna, 'the highest home, the highest
purifier, the eternal divine Person, the first god, unborn and all-pervading.
All the seers say this of you: the divine seer Narada, Asita, Devala and Vyasa.
And now you tell me so yourself. I believe everything you tell me is true,
Keshava. Neither the gods nor the danavas know how you show yourself, Lord. You
alone know yourself by yourself, best of persons, source of beings, lord of
beings, god of gods, lord of the world. Please tell me all your divine powers,
by which you fill these worlds. How can I know you, yogi, as I think of you
always? In what things am I to think of you, Lord? Tell me again, in full, about
your yoga and your powers, Janardana. I never have enough of hearing your
nectar.'

"'Very well,' said Krishna. 'I will tell you my divine powers, the main ones,
best of the Kurus. There is no end to all of them.[^b6c10-12]

"'I am the self, Arjuna, in the heart of every being. I am the beginning, the
middle and the end of beings.

"'Among the Adityas I am Vishnu. Among lights, I am the radiant sun. Among the
Maruts I am Marichi. Among the stars I am the moon. Among the Vedas I am the
Sama Veda. Among the gods I am Vasava. Among the senses I am the mind. In beings
I am awareness.

"'Among the Rudras I am Shankara. Among the yakshas and rakshasas I am the lord
of wealth. Among the Vasus I am fire. Among mountains I am Meru. Know that among
household priests I am the chief, Brihaspati. Among generals I am Skanda. Among
waters I am the ocean. Among the great seers I am Bhrigu. Among words I am the
single syllable. Among sacrifices I am the sacrifice of quiet prayer. Among
things that do not move I am the Himalaya.

"'Among all trees I am the ashvattha. Among divine seers I am Narada. Among
gandharvas I am Chitraratha. Among the perfected I am the sage Kapila. Among
horses, know that I am Ucchaihshravas, born from the nectar. Among lordly
elephants I am Airavata. Among men I am the king. Among weapons I am the
thunderbolt. Among cows I am the cow that gives all wishes. I am Kandarpa, the
god of love, who brings about birth. Among snakes I am Vasuki. Among nagas I am
Ananta. Among the creatures of the waters I am Varuna. Among the ancestors I am
Aryaman. Among those who hold others in check I am Yama.

"'Among the daityas I am Prahlada. Among those who count I am time. Among beasts
I am the king of beasts, the lion. Among birds I am Vinata's son. Among things
that purify I am the wind. Among those who bear weapons I am Rama. Among sea
creatures I am the makara. Among rivers I am the Jahnavi, the Ganga.

"'Among creations I am the beginning, the end and the middle, Arjuna. Among
kinds of knowledge I am the knowledge of the self. Among those who argue I am
the argument itself. Among letters I am the letter A. Among word-compounds I am
the pair. I alone am time that never ends. I am the maker who faces every way. I
am death, which takes everything away, and I am the birth of all that is to be.
Among feminine things I am fame, fortune, speech, memory, intelligence,
steadfastness and patience. Among the Sama chants I am the Brihatsaman. Among
metres I am the Gayatri. Among months I am Margashirsha. Among seasons I am the
spring, the maker of flowers.

"'I am the dice of those who cheat.[^b6c10-13] I am the brightness of the
bright. I am victory. I am effort. I am the goodness of the good. Among the
Vrishnis I am Vasudeva. Among the Pandavas I am Dhananjaya.[^b6c10-14] Among
sages I am Vyasa. Among poets I am the poet Ushanas. I am the rod of those who
punish. I am the good policy of those who want to win. Among secrets I am
silence. I am the knowledge of those who know.

"'Whatever is the seed of all beings, Arjuna, that is me. There is no being,
moving or still, that could exist without me. There is no end to my divine
powers, scorcher of enemies. What I have told you is only a sample of their full
extent. Whatever is powerful, glorious or mighty, know that it has come from
just a spark of my splendour. But what need is there for you to know all this,
Arjuna? I stand holding up this whole world with a single part of myself.'"

## Explanations

[^b6c10-1]: **The six words.** *What it means:* Arjuna asks about six terms
    Krishna used at the end of the last chapter. In simple words: *Brahman* is
    the highest, unchanging reality. *The self* is each being's own inner
    nature. *Action* is the power that brings things into being. *"About
    beings"* means everything that changes and dies. *"About gods"* means the
    divine Person behind the gods. *"About sacrifice"* is Krishna himself,
    living in the body and receiving every offering.

[^b6c10-2]: **The last thought.** *What it means:* what fills a person's mind at
    the moment of death decides where they go next, and that last thought
    depends on what they have thought about all their life. *Everyday example:*
    a person who practises music every day will hum a tune even when half
    asleep. That is why Krishna says, "Think of me at all times" (CE 6.30.7).

[^b6c10-3]: **A day of Brahma.** *What it means:* the world runs in great
    cycles. Four ages (Krita, Treta, Dvapara and the last) make up one great
    round, and a thousand such rounds make one "day" of Brahma, the creator.
    During his day the world exists. During his "night" of the same length,
    everything melts back into the unseen. *In the story:* Sanjaya describes the
    four ages and how life gets shorter in each (CE 6.11.3–14; see ch 3 of this
    Book).

[^b6c10-4]: **The sun's northern course.** *What it means:* for half the year
    the sun appears to move north (from midwinter towards midsummer,
    *uttarāyaṇa*). That half was thought to be the bright, holy time. Dying then
    was a sign of going on to Brahman and not being reborn. *In the story:* when
    Bhishma falls on his bed of arrows, he chooses to hold on to life "wanting
    the sun's northern course", and waits for it before he dies (CE 6.114.89,
    98).

[^b6c10-5]: **The royal secret.** *What it means:* Krishna calls this teaching
    the "king of knowledge" and the "king of secrets". Its heart is that God
    fills everything, and that loving him is the easiest and surest way.

[^b6c10-6]: **In me, but not in me.** *What it means:* a riddle. Everything
    exists in Krishna, as the wind moves inside the sky. But he is not caught up
    in things or limited by them, just as the sky is not blown about by the
    wind.

[^b6c10-7]: **Heaven is not for ever.** *What it means:* people who do good
    rituals go to heaven. But heaven is a reward that gets used up, like money
    in a bank. When the good deeds are spent, the person is born again on earth.
    Krishna points to something beyond heaven, from which there is no return.

[^b6c10-8]: **A leaf, a flower, a fruit, some water.** *What it means:* what God
    wants is love, not costly gifts. The smallest thing offered with love is
    accepted. *In the story:* when Krishna went to Hastinapura as an envoy, he
    refused Duryodhana's grand feast. "People eat another's food out of love, or
    when they are in need," he said, and "you show me no love, and I am in no
    need." He ate at Vidura's house instead (CE 5.89.25; see ledger L-178).

[^b6c10-9]: **Even a wrongdoer.** *What it means:* no one is shut out. Even a
    person who has done great wrong, if they turn to God with a whole heart, has
    made the right choice, and will soon become good.

[^b6c10-10]: **"Women, vaishyas and shudras."** *What it means:* in Krishna's
    time, society often shut women and the lower classes out of Vedic learning
    and rituals. Krishna says that none of this matters on the path of love:
    whoever takes shelter in him reaches the highest goal. The words "low
    births" reflect the ideas of that society. The verse's point is that
    everyone is welcome.

[^b6c10-11]: **Not harming (*ahiṃsā*).** *What it means:* not hurting any living
    being, in deed, word or thought. Krishna counts it among the good qualities
    that come from him.

[^b6c10-12]: **The powers (*vibhūti*).** *What it means:* Krishna says that the
    best of every kind of thing shows his power most clearly. A few of the names
    he uses: the *Adityas*, *Maruts*, *Rudras* and *Vasus* are groups of gods.
    *Vasava* is Indra. *Shankara* is Shiva. *Meru* is the golden mountain at the
    centre of the world (see ch 2). *Skanda* is the war god. *Bhrigu* is a great
    early seer. The *ashvattha* is the sacred fig tree. *Ucchaihshravas* and
    *Airavata* are the horse and elephant that rose from the churning of the
    ocean. *Vasuki* is the king of snakes. *Prahlada* is an asura prince famous
    for his goodness. *Vinata's son* is Garuda, king of birds. *Rama*, among
    warriors, is not identified further by the text. The *makara* is a sea
    monster. *Margashirsha* is a month in early winter.

[^b6c10-13]: **"I am the dice of those who cheat."** *What it means:* even in
    cheating, the most powerful thing is a sign of God's power. This does not
    make cheating good. It means that nothing, good or bad, has any power except
    from him. *In the story:* the whole war began with a game of dice, in which
    Shakuni played for Duryodhana and won throw after throw against
    Yudhishthira, saying each time, "I have won" (CE 2.53–54).

[^b6c10-14]: **"Among the Pandavas I am Dhananjaya."** *What it means:*
    Dhananjaya is Arjuna himself. Krishna is telling Arjuna that the best in him
    is Krishna's own power. Just before, he named himself as Vasudeva, the best
    of the Vrishnis, his own clan.

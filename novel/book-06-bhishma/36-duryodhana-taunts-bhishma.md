# Duryodhana Taunts Bhishma

"When the ranks were drawn up, yours and the others'," said Sanjaya,
"Dhananjaya, Bharata, killed your rank of chariots. The atiratha brought down
the leaders of chariot troops with arrows in battle. They were killed by Partha
as by Time at the end of an age. Yet the sons of Dhritarashtra, ready in battle,
fought the Pandavas back. They sought shining fame and made death their only
turning back. With single minds they broke the Pandava host many times, king,
and were broken in battle themselves. With the Pandavas and the Kauravas
running, broken and turning back, nothing could be made out. Earthly dust rose,
covering the sun. No one there knew the quarters or the points between them. By
guesswork, by signs, by names and family names, the battle went on here and
there, lord of the people.

"The Kauravas' array did not break at all, guarded by wise Bharadvaja's son,
true to his word. In the same way the Pandavas' great array, guarded by the
left-handed archer and well protected by Bhima, did not break. Coming out from
the front of the army, the men fought there, king, chariots and elephants of
both armies entangled. In the great battle horsemen were brought down by
horsemen with bright-pointed spears and lances. Chariot fighter met chariot
fighter and brought him down with arrows adorned with gold in that very fearful
battle. Elephant riders, engaged with elephant riders, brought them down in
crowds with iron arrows, darts and lances, yours and theirs. Crowds of foot
soldiers, glad, having wronged one another, brought down foot soldiers in battle
with slings and axes. Foot soldier brought down chariot fighter in battle, and
chariot fighter foot soldier, with sharp weapons, in both armies. Elephant
riders brought down horsemen, and horsemen elephant riders, and it was a wonder.
Here and there foot soldiers were seen brought down by the best elephant riders,
and elephant fighters by them. Crowds of foot soldiers were seen brought down by
horsemen, and crowds of horsemen by foot soldiers, in hundreds and thousands.

"Banners were thrown down there, with bows and lances, spears, clubs, iron bars
and kampanas. There were darts, bright armour, kanapas and goads, bright swords
and arrows with golden feathers, trappings, saddle-cloths and costly blankets.
With all these the ground shone, best of the Bharatas, as if painted with
garlands. With bodies of men and horses fallen, and elephants, in the great
battle, the earth became impassable, a mire of flesh and blood. The earthly dust
settled, sprinkled with the blood of battle, and all the quarters became clear,
lord of men. Countless headless trunks rose on every side, Bharata, signs of the
destruction of the world. In that very fierce, dreadful battle, chariot fighters
were seen running on every side.

"Then Drona and Bhishma, Jayadratha of Sindhu, Purumitra, Vikarna and Shakuni
son of Subala, hard to withstand in battle, with courage like lions, broke the
Pandavas' ranks again and again. So too Bhimasena, the rakshasa Ghatotkacha,
Satyaki, Chekitana and the sons of Draupadi, Bharata, drove off your men in
battle. They drove off your sons and all the kings with them, as the thirty gods
drove off the danavas. So those bulls among kshatriyas, killing one another in
battle, smeared with blood and terrible in form, shone like danavas. Having
conquered their enemies, the heroes of both armies were seen, great beings, like
planets in the sky.

"Then your son Duryodhana with a thousand chariots went against the Pandavas in
battle and against the rakshasa Ghatotkacha. So too all the Pandavas with a
great army went against Drona and Bhishma, brave in battle, tamers of enemies.
The diademed one went in anger against the able best of kings. Arjuna's son and
Satyaki went against Subala's son's force. Then again a battle began that made
the hair stand on end, between yours and the enemy, each wanting victory.

---

"Then the kings, angry, seeing Phalguna in the battle, surrounded him on every
side with many thousands of chariots. Making a pen round him with a mass of
chariots, Bharata, they held him off on every side with many thousands of
arrows. In their anger they hurled things at Phalguna's chariot: bright, sharp
darts, clubs and iron bars, lances, axes, hammers and pestles. That rain of
weapons, coming on like a swarm of locusts, Partha checked on every side with
arrows adorned with gold. Seeing Bibhatsu's more than human lightness there,
gods, danavas, gandharvas, pishachas, serpents and rakshasas praised Phalguna,
king of kings: 'Well done! Well done!'

"The brave Gandharas with Subala's son held back Satyaki and Abhimanyu with a
great army in battle. There the men of Subala in anger cut the Varshneya's fine
chariot into pieces like sesame seeds with weapons of many kinds in battle. In
that time of great danger Satyaki left his chariot and quickly climbed onto
Abhimanyu's chariot, scorcher of enemies. Yoked in one chariot, the two of them
quickly blew away the army of Subala's son with sharp arrows with bent joints.
Drona and Bhishma, ready in battle, destroyed the army of the King of Dharma
with sharp arrows fitted with heron feathers. Then the son of Dharma, the king,
and Madri's two Pandava sons, while all the troops watched, ran at Drona's rank.
There was a very great battle there, tumultuous and making the hair stand on
end, like the very dreadful battle of the gods and asuras long ago.

"Bhimasena and Ghatotkacha were doing great deeds, and Duryodhana came and held
them both off. There we saw Hidimba's son's wonderful courage, for he fought in
that battle beyond his father, Bharata. Bhimasena in his anger, as if laughing,
pierced impatient Duryodhana in the heart with an arrow. Then King Duryodhana,
stunned by that great blow, sat down on the chariot floor and fainted. Knowing
him to be senseless, his charioteer quickly carried him off from the battle,
king, and then the army broke. Then, killing that Kaurava army as it ran on
every side, Bhima followed behind with sharp arrows. Prishata's grandson, the
best of chariot fighters, and the Pandava son of Dharma killed the army while
Drona and Ganga's son watched. They used sharp arrows that destroy enemy ranks.
The great chariot fighters Bhishma and Drona could not stop your son's army
running in battle. Though held back by Bhishma and Drona, lord of the people,
that army kept running while Drona and Bhishma watched.

"Then, as thousands of chariots ran here and there, Subhadra's son and the bull
of the Shinis, standing in one chariot, cut down Subala's army in the battle on
every side. The bull of the Shinis and the bull of the Kurus shone then like the
moon and the sun come together in the sky at the new moon. Then Arjuna in anger
rained a shower of arrows on your army, lord of the people, like a cloud with
torrents. Killed by Partha's arrows in battle, the Kaurava army ran, trembling
with despair and fear. Seeing them run, the great chariot fighters Bhishma and
Drona, roused, wanting Duryodhana's good, held them back. Then King Duryodhana,
having recovered, lord of the people, turned back that army running on every
side. Wherever any of the great chariot fighters of the kshatriyas saw your son,
Bharata, they turned back there. Seeing them turned back, other people too stood
firm, some in rivalry with one another, king, others out of shame. The force of
those turning back, lord of the people, was like the ocean filling at moonrise.

"Seeing them turned back, King Suyodhana went quickly to Bhishma son of Shantanu
and spoke. 'Grandfather, hear what I will say to you, Bharata. I do not think it
fitting, Kaurava, that this army should run while you live. Drona lives too, the
best of those who know weapons, with his son and friends, and so does the great
archer Kripa. I do not think the Pandavas are any match for you at all, king,
nor for Drona in battle, nor for Drona's son, nor for Kripa. Surely the sons of
Pandu are to be favoured by you, grandfather, since you bear with this army
being killed, hero. You should have told me before the meeting, king, "I will
not fight the Pandavas in battle, nor Prishata's grandson and Satyaki." When I
had heard your words, and the teacher's and Kripa's, I would have thought then
with Karna about what to do. If I am not to be abandoned by you two in this
battle, let the two bulls among men fight with fitting courage.'

"Bhishma heard these words and laughed again and again. Rolling his eyes in
anger, he said to your son, 'Many times, king, I have told you true and good
words: the Pandavas cannot be conquered in battle even by the gods with Vasava.
But what I can do today, old as I am, best of kings, I will do as far as I can.
Now watch, with your kin. Today I alone will hold back all the sons of Pandu
with their armies and their kin, before the eyes of the whole world.' When
Bhishma had said this, lord of men, your sons blew their conches gladly and beat
their drums loudly. Hearing that great noise, king, the Pandavas too blew their
conches and sounded their drums and tabors."

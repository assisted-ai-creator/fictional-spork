The Kalinga army and the Nishadas surround Bhima; the Chedis fall back and leave him alone; he kills the Kalinga prince Shakradeva with his mace, leaps on to Prince Bhanuman's elephant with his sword, and fights on foot through the whole Kalinga host; with a new chariot he kills King Shrutayus of Kalinga, his wheel guards and Ketuman, until his enemies take him for Death itself; Dhrishtadyumna and Satyaki come to his side, and when Bhishma comes up, Satyaki kills Bhishma's charioteer and the horses carry Bhishma off; in the afternoon Abhimanyu fights Duryodhana's son Lakshmana, Arjuna comes to save his son and routs the Kaurava army, and at sunset Bhishma calls a halt.

# Bhima and the Kalingas

"When the king of Kalinga, commander of an army, had been sent like that," said
Dhritarashtra, "how did he fight mighty Bhimasena of wonderful deeds, that hero
going about with his mace like Death with his rod in hand, in the battle, with
his army?"

"When your son had spoken to him like that, lord of kings," said Sanjaya, "the
mighty king went towards Bhima's chariot, guarded by a great army. As that great
army of the Kalingas came on suddenly, crowded with chariots, elephants and
horses and carrying great weapons, Bhimasena went against it, Bharata, and
against Ketuman, son of the Nishada, who came on with the Chedis. Then
Shrutayus, in fury, with King Ketuman, met Bhima in the battle as the Chedis
stood in their ranks. The lord of the Kalingas with many thousands of chariots,
and Ketuman with ten thousand elephants and the Nishadas, surrounded Bhimasena
on every side in the battle, king. The Chedis, the Matsyas and the Karushas, led
by Bhimasena, charged the Nishadas and their kings.

"Then a terrible, fearful battle began. The warriors did not know their own
side, in their eagerness to kill one another. Bhima's fight with the enemy was
sudden and terrible, like Indra's with the great army of the daityas, great
king. As that army fought in the battle, Bharata, there was a huge noise like
the ocean roaring. The warriors cut each other down, lord of the people, and
made the whole earth like a field of blood, the colour of a hare's blood. In
their eagerness to kill, they did not know their own men from the enemy's. Brave
men, hard to beat in battle, struck down even their own. There was a great crush
of the few against the many, the Chedis against the Kalingas and the Nishadas,
lord of the people.

"When they had shown their manhood as far as they could, the mighty Chedis left
Bhimasena and fell back. With the Chedis gone, the Pandava faced all the
Kalingas. Trusting to the strength of his own arms, he did not turn back. Mighty
Bhimasena did not move from the floor of his chariot. He covered the Kalinga
army with sharp arrows.

"The king of Kalinga, a great archer, and his son, a great chariot fighter known
as Shakradeva, struck the Pandava with arrows. Then strong-armed Bhima, shaking
his fine bow, fought the Kalingas, trusting to the strength of his own arms.
Shakradeva, loosing many arrows in the battle, killed Bhimasena's horses. Then
he rained showers of arrows, like a cloud at the end of the hot season. Standing
in his chariot with its horses dead, mighty Bhimasena hurled at Shakradeva a
mace made all of Shaikya iron. Struck by it, king, the son of the Kalinga king
fell from his chariot to the ground, with his banner and his charioteer.

"When he saw his own son killed, the lord of the Kalingas closed every direction
to Bhima with many thousands of chariots. Then strong-armed Bhima threw away his
heavy mace and drew his sword, meaning to do a terrible deed. He took a
matchless shield of bull's hide, bull among men, studded with stars and
half-moons of gold. The king of Kalinga, in fury, wiped his bowstring, took up a
single terrible arrow like a snake's poison and, wanting to kill him, shot it at
Bhimasena. As the sharp arrow came at him, Bhimasena cut it in two with his
broad sword, king, and shouted with joy, terrifying the army. The king of
Kalinga, in fury, quickly hurled fourteen lances whetted on stone at Bhimasena
in the battle. Before they reached him, while they were still in the air, the
strong-armed Pandava cut them down, unshaken, with his fine sword.

"When he had cut down the fourteen lances in the battle, Bhima, bull among men,
saw Bhanuman and ran at him. Bhanuman covered Bhima with a shower of arrows and
gave a mighty roar that filled the sky. Bhima would not bear that lion roar in
the great battle, and he roared back with a great voice. At that sound the
Kalinga army was terrified. They did not think Bhima was a man, bull among the
Bharatas.

"Then Bhima, great king, gave a great roar, leapt with his sword by way of the
tusks on to a fine elephant, and climbed up on to the middle of that king of
elephants, sir. With his broad sword he cut Bhanuman in two. When the destroyer
of enemies had killed the prince, who was armed with his weapon, he brought his
sword down on the elephant's neck, which could bear great loads. With its neck
cut through, that leader of herds fell, trumpeting, like a mountain with its
ridges torn away by the rush of the sea.

"Then the Bharata leapt down from the elephant and stood on the ground in his
armour, Bharata, sword in hand, with a spirit that did not sink. He went many
ways, without fear, bringing down elephants, and he was seen on every side like
a whirling wheel of fire. Among the troops of horses, among the elephants, among
the chariot ranks and the crowds of foot soldiers, the lord moved in the battle
like a hawk, drenched in blood, fierce against the enemy's forces, killing as he
went. Very swift, he cut off the bodies and heads of the elephant fighters in
the battle with his sharp-edged sword. On foot, alone and in fury, increasing
his enemies' fear, he bewildered them, like Death at the end of time. And they,
bewildered, charged him in the battle with loud cries as he moved through the
great battle with his sword, at the greatest speed.

"Cutting the poles and yokes of the chariot fighters in the battle, that mighty
destroyer of enemies killed the chariot fighters too. Bhimasena was seen moving
in many ways. The Pandava showed them the circling, the swerving, the whirling,
the leap, the lunge, the sidestep, the charge and the spring. Some, cut by the
point of the great Pandava's sword, screamed with their vitals torn and fell
dead. Other elephants, with their tusks and trunks cut off and their temples
split, lost their riders and killed their own ranks, Bharata, and fell to the
ground with great cries. We saw falling and fallen, king, with their riders, cut
lances and bows, the heads of elephant drivers, bright saddle-cloths and girths
gleaming with gold, neck chains, spears, flags and darts, quivers, engines and
bright bows, shining fire-pots, goads and hooks, bells of many kinds, and sword
hilts inlaid with gold. With the elephants that had fallen, killed, with their
limbs, hind parts and trunks cut off, the ground there was strewn as if with
fallen hills.

"When he had crushed the great elephants like this, the bull among men crushed
the horses and brought down the best of the horsemen, Bharata. Terrible was the
battle between him and them, Bharata. Bridles, harness, whips gleaming with
gold, saddle-cloths, spears and costly lances, armour, shields and bright
coverlets were seen scattered here and there in the great battle. With bright
spiked weapons and shining swords, he made the ground strewn as if with
many-coloured flowers.

"The mighty Pandava leapt up, seized some chariot fighters, and brought them
down with his sword, with their banners. People were amazed in the battle as the
famous man leapt up again and again, ran in every direction and moved in so many
wonderful ways. He killed some with his foot. He seized others and crushed them.
He cut others down with his sword, and frightened others with his roar. He
brought others down to the ground with the rush of his thighs. Others, seeing
him, died of fear.

"So the large army of the swift Kalingas surrounded Bhishma in the battle and
then charged Bhimasena. Then, bull among the Bharatas, Bhimasena saw Shrutayus
at the head of the Kalinga army and went towards him. When he saw him coming,
the king of Kalinga, whose spirit had no measure, pierced Bhimasena between the
breasts with nine arrows. Struck by the Kalinga's arrows, Bhimasena blazed up in
anger like a fire with fuel, like an elephant struck with a goad.

"Then Ashoka, the charioteer, brought up a chariot adorned with gold and gave it
to Bhima. The son of Kunti, destroyer of enemies, climbed quickly into the
chariot and charged the Kalinga, crying, 'Stand! Stand!' Then mighty Shrutayus,
in fury, shot sharp arrows at Bhima, showing the speed of his hands. Struck hard
by the nine sharp arrows shot from the Kalinga's fine bow, king, famous Bhima
was as furious as a snake struck with a stick. In his fury, Bhima, son of
Pritha, the strongest of the strong, drew his bow hard and killed the Kalinga
with seven iron arrows. With two razor-headed arrows he sent the Kalinga's two
mighty wheel guards, Satyadeva and Satya, to Yama's house. Then Bhima, whose
spirit had no measure, sent Ketuman to Yama's house in the battle with three
sharp iron arrows.

"Then the Kalinga kshatriyas, in fury, held back the angry Bhimasena with ranks
many thousands strong. The Kalingas covered Bhimasena, king, with spears, maces,
swords, lances, javelins and axes. Holding off that terrible shower of arrows as
it rose, the mighty man took up his mace and leapt quickly down, and Bhima sent
seven hundred heroes to Yama's house. And again the destroyer of enemies sent
two thousand Kalingas to the world of death. It was a marvel.

"So the hero broke the Kalinga ranks again and again in the battle, looking
towards Bhishma of the great vow. Elephants whose riders the great Pandava had
killed ran wild through the ranks like clouds driven by the wind, trampling
their own troops and screaming in the pain of their wounds. Then mighty,
strong-armed Bhima blew his conch, and he made the hearts of all the Kalinga
soldiers tremble. Confusion came over the Kalingas, scorcher of enemies, and all
their troops and animals shook. As Bhima went many ways in the battle, like a
lord of elephants, running here and there and leaping up again and again, there
was bewilderment everywhere, king. Terrified of Bhimasena, the army shook, like
a great lake churned by a crocodile.

"When the heroes had been terrified by Bhima of wonderful deeds, and all the
Kalinga warriors were turning back and running in crowds, Prishata's son,
commander of the Pandava army, told his own ranks, 'Fight!' When they heard the
commander's words, the troops led by Shikhandi charged towards Bhima with ranks
of chariots and fighters. And the Pandava, the righteous king, supported them
all from behind with a great division of elephants the colour of clouds. When he
had urged on all his ranks like this, Prishata's son took up the guarding of
Bhimasena's rear, as a good man should. For there was no one in the world dearer
than life to the Panchala king's son than Bhima and Satyaki.

"That slayer of enemy heroes, Prishata's son, saw strong-armed Bhimasena, the
destroyer of enemies, going among the Kalingas. He roared again and again, king,
and the scorcher of enemies was glad. He blew his conch in the battle and gave a
lion roar. And when Bhimasena saw the kovidara banner on the gold-adorned
chariot drawn by dove-coloured horses, he took heart. Dhrishtadyumna, whose
spirit had no measure, seeing Bhimasena beset by the Kalingas, came up in the
battle to protect him.

"Satyaki saw from afar those two heroes of great spirit, Dhrishtadyumna and
Vrikodara, fighting the Kalingas in the battle. Shaineya, best of winners, went
there at speed, and that bull among men took up the guard of Partha's and
Prishata's son's rear. With his bow in hand, he made great slaughter there, and
taking on a fearful form, he killed the enemy in the battle.

"Bhima set flowing there a river of blood, its mud made of flesh and blood,
springing from the Kalingas. Mighty Bhimasena crossed that river, so hard to
cross, between the Kalinga army and the Pandava army. Seeing Bhimasena like
that, your men cried out, king, 'This is Death, in Bhima's shape, fighting the
Kalingas!'

"Then Bhishma, son of Shantanu, hearing that cry in the battle, came quickly at
Bhima, with his ranks drawn up all round him. Satyaki, Bhimasena and
Dhrishtadyumna, son of Prishata, charged Bhishma's gold-adorned chariot. They
surrounded Ganga's fierce son in the battle and quickly struck Bhishma with
three terrible arrows each. Your father Devavrata pierced back each of those
great archers, as they strove, with three straight arrows. Then, holding back
those great chariot fighters with a thousand arrows, he killed Bhima's horses,
in their golden harness, with his arrows. Standing in his chariot with its
horses dead, mighty Bhimasena hurled a spear swiftly at the chariot of Ganga's
son. Your father Devavrata cut the spear in three in the battle before it
reached him, and it fell shattered to the ground.

"Then Bhimasena took up a heavy mace of Shaikya iron and leapt quickly from his
chariot, bull among men. Satyaki too, to please Bhima, quickly brought down the
old Kuru's charioteer with his arrows. With his charioteer killed, Bhishma, best
of chariot fighters, was carried off from the battlefield by his horses, running
like the wind. When the man of the great vow had been carried off, king,
Bhimasena blazed up like a fire grown great, burning dry grass. He killed all
the Kalingas and stood in the middle of the army. None of your men dared to
attack him, bull among the Bharatas.

"Dhrishtadyumna, best of chariot fighters, took him up into his own chariot and
carried the famous man away, while all the armies watched. Honoured by the
Panchalas and the Matsyas, bull among the Bharatas, Bhima embraced
Dhrishtadyumna and met Satyaki. Then Satyaki, whose valour is true, the tiger of
the Yadus, cheering Bhimasena, said to him while Dhrishtadyumna looked on, 'By
good fortune the king of the Kalingas and Prince Ketuman, Shakradeva of Kalinga
and the Kalingas have been killed in battle. By the might of your own arms,
alone, you have crushed the great array of the Kalingas, crowded with elephants,
horses and chariots.' When he had said this, long-armed Satyaki, grandson of
Shini, destroyer of enemies, ran from his chariot to Bhima's and embraced the
Pandava. Then the great chariot fighter climbed back into his own chariot and,
in fury, killed your men, adding to Bhima's strength.

"When most of the afternoon of that day had passed, Bharata, in the great
destruction of chariots, elephants, horses, foot soldiers and horsemen, the
Panchala fought three great chariot fighters, Drona's son, Shalya and great
Kripa. The mighty heir of the Panchalas killed Drona's son's famous horses with
ten sharp, swift arrows. With his horses dead, Drona's son quickly climbed into
Shalya's chariot and rained arrows on the heir of the Panchalas. Seeing
Dhrishtadyumna engaged with Drona's son, Bharata, Subhadra's son rushed up
quickly, scattering sharp arrows. He pierced Shalya with twenty-five arrows,
Kripa with nine and Ashvatthama with eight, bull among men. Then Drona's son
quickly pierced Arjuna's son with a feathered arrow, Shalya with twelve, and
Kripa with three sharp ones.

"Your grandson Lakshmana, glad, came up against your grandson Abhimanyu, who
stood there, and they fought. Duryodhana's son, in fury, pierced Subhadra's son
with nine arrows in the battle, king. It was a marvel. Abhimanyu, quick of hand,
pierced his brother in fury with fifty arrows, bull among the Bharatas.
Lakshmana cut his bow at the grip with a feathered arrow, great king, and people
shouted. Throwing away the broken bow, Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes,
took another, bright and swifter. The two of them, glad in the battle and each
trying to pay the other back, bulls among men, struck each other with sharp
arrows.

"Then King Duryodhana, lord of men, seeing his son, the great chariot fighter,
hard pressed by your grandson, went there. When your son turned back, all the
lords of men surrounded Arjuna's son with a line of chariots on every side.
Surrounded by those heroes, so hard to beat in battle, the hero whose valour was
like Krishna's did not flinch, king.

"When Dhananjaya saw Subhadra's son engaged there, he charged in fury to save
his own son. Then the kings, led by Bhishma and Drona, charged Savyasachin
together, with their chariots, elephants and horses. A thick earthly dust,
raised suddenly by the elephants, horses, chariots and horsemen, was seen
reaching the path of the sun. Those thousands of elephants and hundreds of
kings, once they came within range of his arrows, could not go forward at all.
All beings cried out, and the quarters grew dark. The terrible, cruel ruin of
the Kurus was plain to see. Neither the sky, nor the quarters, nor the earth,
nor the sun could be seen, best of the Bharatas, for the floods of Kiritin's
arrows.

"Many chariot fighters were seen with their banners and elephants brought down
and their horses killed, and some leaders of chariot troops with their chariots
put to flight. Other chariot fighters without chariots were seen running in
every direction with their weapons, their arms still wearing armlets. Horsemen
left their horses and elephant riders their elephants, king, and ran in all
directions in fear of Arjuna. Kings were seen fallen and falling from chariots,
elephants and horses, struck by Arjuna. Taking on a fierce form, Arjuna cut down
with his terrible arrows the raised arms of men, with their maces and swords,
their spears and quivers, their arrows and bows, their goads and flags, here and
there. Heaps were seen scattered over the battlefield, sir, of great iron bars
and hammers, spears, javelins and swords, sharp axes and lances, armour thrown
away and coats of mail, banners, shields and fans, umbrellas with gold handles
and yak-tail whisks, Bharata, goads, whips and harness.

"There was not one man in your army, Bharata, who would go out to meet heroic
Arjuna in battle. Whoever went against Partha in the battle, lord of the people,
was carried off to the other world by his sharp arrows. As your warriors ran on
every side, Arjuna and Vasudeva blew their fine conches.

"When he saw that army broken, your father Devavrata, almost smiling, said to
heroic Bharadvaja's son in the battle, 'This mighty, heroic son of Pandu, with
Krishna, is treating the armies as only Dhananjaya could. He cannot be beaten in
battle today, in any way, since his form looks like Death at the end of time.
And this great army cannot be turned back. Look, the army is running, each man
watching the next. And there the sun is going down to the best of mountains, as
if drawing in the bodies of the whole world. I think the time for withdrawing
has come, bull among men. Our warriors are tired and afraid, and they will not
fight at all.'

"When Bhishma had said this to Drona, best of teachers, the great chariot
fighter withdrew your army. Then, as the sun set and the twilight came on, your
army and theirs withdrew, Bharata."

**Divergences.**

* **The Kalinga dead.** Bhima kills Shakradeva (6.50.22), Bhanuman (6.50.35), King Shrutayus of Kalinga with his wheel guards Satyadeva and Satya (6.50.68–69), and Ketuman (6.50.70). Satyaki sums them up (6.50.112).
* **Bhishma carried off.** Satyaki kills Bhishma's charioteer, and his horses carry him from the field (6.50.105–106).
* **Abhimanyu and Lakshmana.** Duryodhana's son Lakshmana fights Abhimanyu for the first time here (6.51.8–13).
* **The end of the second day.** Bhishma calls the withdrawal because the Kaurava warriors are "tired and afraid" (6.51.41–42). A rejected passage (\*186) has the Pandavas march back to camp in triumph, dancing.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*165–\*186. \*179 has Bhima, "in the fourteenth year", delighting at the sight of elephants; \*184 has the monkey on Arjuna's banner howling.

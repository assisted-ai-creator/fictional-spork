# The Highest Person

"'They speak of an undying ashvattha tree,' said the Blessed One, 'with its
roots above and its branches below. Its leaves are the Vedic hymns. One who
knows it knows the Veda. Its branches spread below and above, swollen by the
qualities, with the objects of the senses for their shoots. Below, its roots
stretch on and on, binding to action in the world of men. Its form is not
grasped here, nor its end, nor its beginning, nor its foundation. One must cut
down this ashvattha, its roots grown strong, with the firm axe of detachment.
Then one must seek that place from which those who reach it do not return again,
thinking, "I take refuge in that first Person, from whom the ancient activity
has flowed forth." Free of pride and confusion, the fault of attachment
conquered, always intent on the self, desires turned away, freed from the pairs
called pleasure and pain, the unconfused go to that undying place. The sun does
not light it, nor the moon, nor fire. Those who go there do not return. That is
my highest home.

"'An eternal part of me, become the living soul in the world of the living,
draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, which rest in nature.
When the lord takes a body, and when he leaves it, he takes these and goes, as
the wind takes scents from their place. Presiding over the ear, the eye, touch,
taste and smell, and the mind, he enjoys the objects of the senses. The confused
do not see him as he departs, or stays, or enjoys, joined to the qualities.
Those with the eye of knowledge see him. Yogins who strive see him standing in
the self. But the unwise, whose selves are not made ready, do not see him even
when they strive.

"'The splendour that is in the sun and lights the whole world, that which is in
the moon and in fire, know that splendour as mine. Entering the earth, I support
beings by my strength. Becoming soma, the essence of sap, I nourish all plants.
Becoming the fire of digestion, I dwell in the bodies of living beings and,
joined to the in-breath and out-breath, I cook the four kinds of food. I am set
in the heart of all. From me come memory, knowledge and their loss. I am what is
to be known by all the Vedas. I am the maker of the Vedanta and the knower of
the Veda.

"'There are two persons in the world, the perishable and the imperishable. The
perishable is all beings. The one standing firm like an anvil is called the
imperishable. But the highest Person is another, called the highest self, the
undying lord, who enters the three worlds and supports them. Since I am beyond
the perishable and higher even than the imperishable, I am known in the world
and in the Veda as the highest Person. One who, unconfused, knows me like this
as the highest Person knows all, and worships me with his whole being, Bharata.
So I have told you this most secret teaching, sinless one. Knowing this,
Bharata, one becomes wise and has done what must be done.'"

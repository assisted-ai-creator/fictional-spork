# Shikhandi Goes First

"How did Shikhandi come against Ganga's son in the battle, Sanjaya?" said
Dhritarashtra. "And how did the Pandavas come against Bhishma? Tell me that."

"Then, in the clear morning, towards sunrise," said Sanjaya, "as drums, tabors
and kettledrums were beaten and conches the colour of curds were blown on every
side, the Pandavas went out to battle with Shikhandi in front. They made an
array that destroys all enemies, great king, and Shikhandi stood at the head of
all the armies, lord of the people. Bhimasena and Dhananjaya guarded his wheels.
Behind him were the sons of Draupadi and brave Subhadra's son. Satyaki and
Chekitana, the great chariot fighter, guarded them. After them came
Dhrishtadyumna, protected by the Panchalas. Then came lord King Yudhishthira
with the twins, making the air ring with a lion roar, bull of the Bharatas.
Behind him was Virata with his own army, and behind him Drupada came on, great
king. The five Kekaya brothers and brave Dhrishtaketu guarded the rear of the
Pandava army, Bharata. So the Pandavas drew up their great army and charged your
army in the battle, giving up their own lives.

"In the same way the Kurus, king, put mighty Bhishma at the head of all the
armies and went against the Pandavas. He was guarded by your very mighty sons,
whom no one could face. Then came the great archer Drona and his son, the great
chariot fighter. Behind them was Bhagadatta with his elephant division, and
Kripa and Kritavarma followed Bhagadatta. Then came mighty Sudakshina, king of
the Kambojas, and Jayatsena of Magadha, and Brihadbala, Subala's son. The other
great archers, the kings led by Susharma, guarded the rear of your army,
Bharata. Day after day, as each day came, Bhishma, son of Shantanu, made arrays
in battle, the arrays of the asuras, of the pishachas and of the rakshasas.

"Then the battle began between yours and theirs, Bharata, killing each other and
filling Yama's kingdom, king. The Parthas led by Arjuna, with Shikhandi in
front, came against Bhishma in the battle, scattering arrows of many kinds.
There, Bharata, your men, harried by Bhima with arrows, went to the other world,
drenched in floods of blood. Nakula, Sahadeva and Satyaki, the great chariot
fighter, came up to your army and pressed it hard. Being killed in the battle,
bull of the Bharatas, your men could not hold back the great army of the
Pandavas. Then your army, killed on every side, ran in all directions, king,
driven by the great chariot fighters. Your men found no rescuer, bull of the
Bharatas, as they were killed with sharp arrows by the Pandavas and the
Srinjayas."

"When valiant Bhishma saw the army hard pressed by the Parthas," said
Dhritarashtra, "what did he do in the battle in his anger? Tell me that,
Sanjaya. And how did the scorcher of enemies go against the Pandavas in the
battle, killing the heroic Somakas? Tell me, Sanjaya."

"I will tell you, great king," said Sanjaya, "what the grandfather did when your
son's army was hard pressed by the Pandavas and the Srinjayas. Glad at heart,
the brave Pandavas, elder brother of Pandu, came on, killing your son's army.
Bhishma could not bear that destruction of men, elephants and horses, lord of
men, the killing of the army in battle by the enemy. The great archer whom no
one could face charged the Pandavas, the Panchalas and the Srinjayas, giving up
his own life.

"He held back with arrows the five foremost great chariot fighters of the
Pandavas, king, as they stood in the battle with their weapons raised, set on
it. With iron arrows, calf-tooth arrows and sharp crescent-headed arrows he
killed in the battle, in his anger, countless elephants and horses. The bull
among men brought down chariot fighters from their chariots, king, horsemen from
their horses' backs and foot soldiers who came against him, and elephant riders
from their elephants, putting fear into the enemy. The Pandavas came against
that one great chariot fighter Bhishma in the battle as he moved fast, as the
asuras came against the Wielder of the Thunderbolt. Loosing sharp arrows that
struck like Shakra's thunderbolt, he was seen in every direction, taking on a
terrible form. His great bow, like Shakra's bow, was always seen drawn into a
circle as he fought in the battle. Seeing that deed in the battle, lord of the
people, your sons were full of wonder and honoured the grandfather. The Parthas,
dismayed, looked at your father as he fought in the battle, the hero, as the
immortals looked at Viprachitti. They could not hold him back, like Death with
his mouth open.

"When the tenth day came, he burned Shikhandi's chariot division with sharp
arrows, as fire burns a forest. Shikhandi pierced him with three arrows in the
middle of the chest, as he was like an angry venomous snake, like Death sent by
Time. Badly wounded by him, Bhishma looked at Shikhandi. Unwilling, though
angry, he laughed and said, 'Strike me or not, as you please. I will never fight
you. For you are the same Shikhandini the Maker made.'

"Hearing those words of his, Shikhandi, beside himself with anger, licked the
corners of his mouth and said to Bhishma in the battle, 'I know you,
strong-armed one, destroyer of kshatriyas. I have heard of your battle with
Jamadagni's son, and I have heard many times of your divine power. Though I know
your power, I will fight you today. Doing what pleases the Pandavas, and myself,
best of men, I will fight you today in battle, best of beings. And I will surely
kill you. I swear it to you by the truth. Having heard what I say, do what is
fitting. Strike me or not, as you please, you will not get away from me alive.
Take a good look at this world, Bhishma, winner of battles.'

"Having said this, he pierced Bhishma in the battle with five straight arrows,
king, Bhishma who had already been struck by his arrows of words. Hearing those
words of his, Savyasachin, scorcher of enemies, thought, 'This is the time,' and
urged Shikhandi on. 'I will follow you, driving the enemy back with arrows.
Charge Bhishma of terrible valour in your fury. The mighty man cannot hurt you
in battle. So today, strong-armed hero, charge Bhishma. If you go back without
killing Bhishma in battle, sir, you and I will be laughed at by the world. Make
the effort in battle, hero, so that we are not laughed at in this great battle.
Bring down the grandfather. I will guard you in the battle, scorcher of enemies,
holding off all the chariot fighters. Bring down the grandfather. Drona and
Drona's son, Kripa and Suyodhana, Chitrasena, Vikarna and Jayadratha the
Saindhava; Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, Sudakshina of Kamboja, brave Bhagadatta
and the great chariot fighter of Magadha; Somadatta's brave son and the
rakshasa, son of Rishyashringa; the Trigarta king with all the great chariot
fighters. I will hold them all back in the battle, as the shore holds back the
ocean. I will hold back all the Kurus together, and their soldiers standing
there, in the battle. Bring down the grandfather.'"

"How did Shikhandi, the Panchala, charge Ganga's son, the grandfather, in the
battle in his anger," said Dhritarashtra, "the righteous man of the strict vow?
Which great chariot fighters in the Pandava army guarded Shikhandi as he raised
his weapons, hurrying at the time for hurry, wanting victory? How did mighty
Bhishma, son of Shantanu, fight on that tenth day against the Pandavas and the
Srinjayas? I cannot bear it, Bhishma confronted by Shikhandi in the battle.
Surely his chariot did not break, or his bow split, as he shot?"

"His bow did not split," said Sanjaya, "and his chariot did not break, bull of
the Bharatas, as Bhishma fought in the battle, killing the enemy with straight
arrows. Many hundreds of thousands of your great chariot fighters, king, and
troops of chariots and elephants, and horses well equipped, went into battle
with the grandfather in front. And he, the winner of battles, Kauravya, made a
constant slaughter of the Parthas in the battle, as he had promised. As the
great archer fought, killing the enemy with arrows, the Panchalas with the
Pandavas all tried to hold him back. When the tenth day came, he scorched the
enemy's army, scattering it with sharp arrows by the hundred and the thousand.
The Pandavas, elder brother of Pandu, could not beat the great archer Bhishma in
the battle, as if he were Death with his noose in his hand.

"Then, great king, Savyasachin, scorcher of enemies, the unbeaten Bibhatsu, came
on, terrifying all the chariot fighters. Roaring loudly like a lion, drawing his
bowstring again and again, loosing floods of arrows, Partha moved through the
battle like Time. Terrified at his noise, bull of the Bharatas, your men ran in
great fear, king, like deer from a lion. Seeing the Pandava winning and your
army hard pressed, Duryodhana, in great pain, said to Bhishma, 'Father, this son
of Pandu with the white horses, whose charioteer is Krishna, is burning up all
my men, as fire burns a forest. Look, son of Ganga, at the armies running on
every side, driven in the battle by the Pandava, best of fighters. As a herdsman
drives his herds of cattle in the forest, so this army of mine is driven,
scorcher of enemies. Broken by Dhananjaya's arrows, it runs this way and that.
And unassailable Bhima is putting my army to flight. Satyaki and Chekitana, the
two Pandava sons of Madri and valiant Abhimanyu are burning up my army. Brave
Dhrishtadyumna and the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, those two mighty men, have suddenly
put my army to flight. As my army is killed by all these mighty men, I see no
other refuge, Bharata, no other place to stand in battle, but you, tiger among
men, of godlike valour. You are equal to it. Be the refuge of the hard pressed,
quickly.'

"Told this, great king, your father Devavrata thought for a moment and made up
his mind. Steadying your son, Shantanu's son said, 'Duryodhana, understand this.
Be steady, lord of the people. Long ago, mighty one, I promised you that I would
kill ten thousand great kshatriyas and then withdraw from the battle, and that
this would be my daily work. I have done it as I said, bull of the Bharatas.
Today too I will do a great deed in the great battle. Either I shall lie here
killed, or I shall kill the Pandavas today. Today, tiger among men, I will pay
you the great debt of the bread I have eaten from my master, king, killed at the
head of the army.'

"Having said this, the best of the Bharatas, whom no one could face, scorching
the kshatriyas with arrows, came up to the Pandava army. As Ganga's son stood in
the middle of the army, bull of the Bharatas, the Pandavas surrounded him like
an angry venomous snake. On that tenth day, showing his power, king, joy of the
Kurus, he killed a hundred thousand. He took the strength of the best of the
Panchalas, mighty princes, as the sun takes up water with its rays. He killed
ten thousand swift elephants with their riders, great king, and ten thousand
horses again. The best of men killed two full hundred thousand foot soldiers,
and Bhishma blazed in the battle like a fire without smoke. None of the Pandavas
could look at him, like the sun burning on its northern course.

"The Pandavas, hard pressed by the great archer, charged Bhishma in fury to kill
him, and so did the great chariot fighters of the Srinjayas. Fighting many men,
strong-armed Bhishma, son of Shantanu, was covered like a mountain by dark
clouds. Your sons surrounded Ganga's son on every side with a great army, and
the battle went on.

"Arjuna, seeing Bhishma's valour in the battle, king, said to Shikhandi, 'Come
up to the grandfather. You must not be afraid of Bhishma today in any way. I
will bring him down from his fine chariot with sharp arrows.' Told this by
Partha, Shikhandi, bull of the Bharatas, hearing Partha's words, charged Ganga's
son. Dhrishtadyumna too, king, and Subhadra's son, the great chariot fighter,
hearing Partha's words, charged Bhishma gladly. Old Virata and Drupada, and
Kuntibhoja in his armour, charged Ganga's son while your son watched. Nakula,
Sahadeva and the brave Dharma king, and all the other armies too, lord of the
people, hearing Partha's words, charged Ganga's son.

"Your men came together and went out against those great chariot fighters, each
as far as his strength and spirit allowed. Hear as I tell it. Chitrasena, great
king, went against Chekitana as he went into battle wanting to reach Bhishma,
like a tiger cub against a bull. Kritavarma, great king, quickly held back
Dhrishtadyumna in the battle as he came near Bhishma, set on it. Somadatta's son
quickly held back Bhimasena in his great anger, great king, as he sought the
death of Ganga's son. Vikarna, wanting Bhishma's life, held back heroic Nakula
as he scattered many arrows. Kripa, Sharadvat's son, in anger held back Sahadeva
in the battle as he went, set on it, towards Bhishma's chariot. Mighty Durmukha
charged the rakshasa of cruel deeds, Bhimasena's mighty son, who was seeking
Bhishma's death. Rishyashringa's son held back Satyaki, angry in the battle.
Sudakshina of Kamboja, great king, held back Abhimanyu, great king, as he went
towards Bhishma's chariot. Ashvatthama in anger, Bharata, held back old Virata
and Drupada, those crushers of enemies, together. Bharadvaja's son, set on it,
held back in the battle the eldest son of Pandu, the son of Dharma, who wanted
Bhishma's death. The great archer Duhshasana held back Arjuna in the battle,
fierce in battle, with Shikhandi in front, seeking Bhishma, scorching the ten
directions, great king. And your other warriors held back the great chariot
fighters of the Pandavas in the battle as they went towards Bhishma.

"Dhrishtadyumna shouted to the armies again and again, 'Charge that one mighty
man Bhishma in your fury! Here is Arjuna, joy of the Kurus, going against
Bhishma in the battle. Charge! Do not be afraid! Bhishma will not reach you.
Even Vasava would not dare fight Arjuna in battle, let alone Bhishma, heroes,
whose courage is gone and whose life is short!' Hearing the commander of the
army, the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas charged Bhishma's chariot
gladly. As they came on in the battle like strong floods of water, your bulls
among men gladly held them back.

"Duhshasana, great king, the great chariot fighter, putting away his fear,
charged Dhananjaya, wanting Bhishma's life. In the same way the brave Pandavas
charged your sons, the great chariot fighters, in the battle, going towards
Ganga's son's chariot. There we saw a marvel, strange to see, lord of the
people: when Partha reached Duhshasana's chariot, he did not get past it. As the
shore holds back the troubled great ocean, so your son held back the angry
Pandava. Both were the best of chariot fighters, both were hard to beat,
Bharata. Both were like the moon and the sun in beauty and in brightness,
Bharata. Both full of fury and wanting to kill each other, they met in the great
battle as Maya and Shakra met long ago.

"Duhshasana, great king, struck the Pandava with three arrows in the battle, and
Vasudeva with twenty. Then Arjuna, angry at seeing the Varshneya hurt, planted a
hundred iron arrows in Duhshasana in the battle. They split his armour and drank
his blood in the battle. Then Duhshasana in anger pierced Partha in the forehead
with five straight arrows, best of the Bharatas. With those arrows in his
forehead, the best of the Pandavas shone, great king, like Mount Meru with its
very tall peaks. Badly wounded by your son the bowman, the great archer Partha
shone in the battle like a flowering kimshuka tree. Then the Pandava in anger
harried Duhshasana as angry, fierce Rahu harries the moon at the new moon. Hard
pressed by the mighty man, your son, lord of the people, pierced Partha in the
battle with arrows feathered with heron plumes and whetted on stone. Partha
quickly cut his bow, the valiant man, and then struck your son with nine arrows.
He took another bow, standing in front of Bhishma, and planted twenty-five
arrows in Arjuna's arms and chest.

"The Pandava, destroyer of enemies, in anger, great king, sent many terrible
arrows at him like Yama's staff. Your son cut those arrows before they reached
him, and it was a marvel, for Partha was trying hard. Your son pierced Partha
with sharp arrows. Then Partha in anger fitted arrows to his bow and sent them
at him in the battle, gold-feathered and whetted on stone. They sank into the
great man's body, great king, as swans settle on a lake, Bharata. Hard pressed
by the great Pandava, your son left Partha in the battle and quickly took refuge
at Bhishma's chariot. Bhishma was then an island for him as he sank in
bottomless water. Coming to, your brave, valiant son held back Partha again. He
pierced Purandara with very sharp arrows, as Vritra did, the man of great
courage, and was not shaken by Arjuna.

"Rishyashringa's son, the great archer, held back Satyaki in the battle as he
went in his armour against Bhishma. Madhava in great anger struck the rakshasa
with nine arrows in the battle, king, almost laughing, Bharata. In the same way
the rakshasa in anger harried Madhava, bull of the Shinis, with sharp arrows,
lord of kings. Then Shaineya, killer of enemy heroes, in great anger, sent a
flood of arrows at the rakshasa in the battle. Then the rakshasa pierced
strong-armed Satyaki, whose valour was true, with sharp arrows, and roared a
lion roar. Badly wounded by the rakshasa in the battle, the splendid Madhava
held firm, and laughed, and roared.

"Then Bhagadatta in anger struck Madhava with sharp arrows in the battle, as men
strike a great elephant with goads. Leaving the rakshasa in the battle,
Shaineya, best of chariot fighters, shot straight arrows at the lord of
Pragjyotisha. The king of Pragjyotisha cut Madhava's great bow with a
sharp-edged broad arrow, like a man of practised hand. The killer of enemy
heroes took another swift bow and in anger pierced Bhagadatta with sharp arrows
in the battle. Badly wounded, the great archer, licking the corners of his mouth
again and again, hurled at Satyaki a strong iron spear adorned with gold and
beryl, terrible, like Yama's staff. As it came suddenly at him, driven by the
strength of his arm, Satyaki cut it in three with arrows in the battle, king,
and it fell to the ground like a great meteor that has lost its light.

"Seeing the spear struck down, your son, lord of the people, held back Madhava
with a great line of chariots. Seeing the great chariot fighter of the
Varshneyas surrounded like that, Duryodhana, very glad, said to all his
brothers, 'Kauravas, see to it that Satyaki does not come out of this great line
of chariots alive. When he is killed, I think the great army of the Pandavas is
killed.' Taking up his words, 'So be it,' the great chariot fighters fought
Shaineya then, in front of Bhishma.

"The mighty king of Kamboja held back Abhimanyu in the battle as he came against
Bhishma. Arjuna's son pierced the king with straight arrows, and then again with
sixty-four, king. Sudakshina pierced the son of Arjuna with five arrows in the
battle, and his charioteer with nine, wanting Bhishma's life. The battle between
those two was very great in its valour, as Shikhandi, scorcher of enemies,
charged Ganga's son.

"Old Virata and Drupada, great chariot fighters, holding back the great army and
charging Bhishma in fury in the battle, were met by angry Ashvatthama, best of
chariot fighters, and the battle went on between yours and theirs, Bharata.
Virata struck the great archer Drona's son, who shone in battle, with ten broad
arrows as he strove, scorcher of enemies. Drupada too pierced him with three
sharp arrows, meeting the teacher's son where he stood in front of Bhishma. Then
Ashvatthama pierced both of them, old Virata and Drupada, with ten arrows as
they rose against Bhishma. There we saw a great deed of the two old men, that
they held back Drona's son's terrible arrows in the battle.

"Kripa, Sharadvat's son, went against Sahadeva as he came, like a mad elephant
in the forest charging a mad elephant. And Kripa, king, quickly struck the great
chariot fighter, the son of Madri, with seventy arrows adorned with gold in the
battle. The son of Madri cut his bow in two with arrows, and then pierced him
with nine arrows when his bow was cut. He took another bow that could bear any
load, and very glad, in anger, he struck the son of Madri in the chest with ten
sharp arrows, wanting Bhishma's life. In the same way the Pandava in anger
struck unforgiving Sharadvat's son in the chest, king, wanting Bhishma's death.
The fight between them was terrible and full of fear.

"Vikarna, scorcher of enemies, pierced Nakula, angry in the battle, with sixty
arrows, guarding Bhishma's life. Nakula too, badly wounded by your son the
bowman, split Vikarna with seventy-seven arrows. There those two tigers among
men, scorchers of enemies, heroes, struck each other for Bhishma's sake like two
bulls in a cowpen.

"Mighty Durmukha went in the battle for Bhishma's sake against Ghatotkacha, who
was fighting hard, killing your army. Then Hidimba's son, king, in anger struck
Durmukha, scorcher of enemies, in the chest with ninety sharp arrows. And the
hero Durmukha, roaring with joy, pierced Bhimasena's son with sixty well-pointed
arrows at the head of the battle.

"Hridika's son held back Dhrishtadyumna in the battle as he went, wanting
Bhishma's death, guarding Bhishma's life. The Varshneya pierced brave Prishata's
son with five iron arrows, and then quickly struck him in the middle of the
chest with fifty. In the same way, king, Prishata's son pierced Hridika's son
with nine sharp arrows feathered with heron plumes. Their battle in the great
fight for Bhishma's sake was full of each outdoing the other, like that of
Vritra and great Indra.

"Bhurishravas came quickly against mighty Bhimasena as he came against Bhishma,
and said, 'Stand! Stand!' Then Somadatta's son struck Bhima in the middle of the
chest in the battle with a very sharp iron arrow with golden feathers. With it
in his chest, mighty Bhimasena shone, best of kings, like Mount Krauncha long
ago with Skanda's spear. In anger they hurled at each other again and again in
the battle arrows bright as the sun, burnished by the smith. Bhima, wanting
Bhishma's death, fought the great chariot fighter, Somadatta's son, and
Somadatta's son, eager for Bhishma's victory, fought the Pandava, each set on
returning blow for blow.

"Bharadvaja's son held back Yudhishthira, great king, surrounded by a great
army, as he went against Bhishma. Hearing the rumble of Drona's chariot, like
the roar of Parjanya, the Prabhadrakas trembled, sir, king. That great army of
the son of Pandu, set on it and held back in the battle by Drona, did not move a
step. Your son Chitrasena held back Chekitana, lord of men, as he came in anger
against Bhishma, looking angry. The great chariot fighter Chitrasena, valiant
for Bhishma's sake, fought Chekitana with all his strength, Bharata. And
Chekitana fought Chitrasena, and the battle between them was very great in its
valour.

"Arjuna, though your son held him back again and again, turned your son away and
crushed your army. Duhshasana too held back Partha with all his strength,
determined, Bharata, that the enemy should not kill Bhishma. Your son's army,
being killed in the battle, was churned up here and there by the best of the
chariot fighters, Bharata.

"Then the hero, the great archer with the valour of an elephant in rut, took his
great bow that could stop an elephant in rut. Shaking his fine bow, putting
great chariot fighters to flight, the great chariot fighter bringing down the
Pandava army, the brave man who knew omens looked all round at the omens. Drona
then said to his son as he scorched the ranks:

"'This is the day, my son, when great Partha will make his greatest effort to
kill Bhishma in battle. My arrows leap out, my bow seems to throb, my weapons
come to my hand, and my mind is cruel. In quarters that are calm, beasts and
birds cry terribly. Vultures fly low over the army of the Bharatas. The sun
seems to have lost its light, and the quarters are red all round. The earth
groans and trembles, and the mounts moan. Herons, vultures and cranes cry again
and again, and she-jackals with ill-omened howls tell of great fear. A great
meteor fell from the middle of the sun's disc, and an iron bar with a headless
trunk stands covering the sun. There is a terrible halo round the moon and the
sun, telling of the cutting of kings' bodies. The gods of the Kaurava king in
their shrines shake and laugh, dance and weep. The planets have moved to the
left of the moon, leaving it without its marks, and the blessed moon has risen
head down. I see the bodies of the kings as if their life had gone. In the
armies of the son of Dhritarashtra those in armour do not shine. From both
armies on every side we hear the great noise of Panchajanya and the twang of the
Gandiva.

"'Surely Bibhatsu has taken his finest weapons in the battle, thrust the other
fighters aside in the battle and is going for the grandfather. The hairs on my
body stand up, and my mind seems to sink, strong-armed one, when I think of the
meeting of Bhishma and Arjuna. Partha has gone into battle against Bhishma with
that cunning, evil-minded Panchala in front. Bhishma said long ago, "I will not
kill Shikhandi. The Maker made her a woman, and by fate she became a man again.
And the great chariot fighter, Yajnasena's son, is an inauspicious banner."
Ganga's son would not strike one with an inauspicious banner. As I think of
this, my mind sinks deeply. Today, in the battle, Partha has charged the old man
of the Kurus. Yudhishthira's anger, the meeting of Bhishma and Arjuna, and my
own fury with weapons are surely ill for the people.

"'The Pandava is high-minded, mighty, brave, a master of weapons, of firm
valour. He shoots far and his arrows are firm, and he knows omens. He cannot be
beaten in battle even by the gods with Vasava. He is mighty, wise, the best of
fighters, one who has conquered hardship. The Pandava always wins in battle, and
his weapons are terrible. Keep out of his way and go quickly to the man of the
strict vow. See, strong-armed one, this slaughter that has come. The great,
fine, gold-worked armour of brave men is being split by straight arrows. Banner
tops, javelins and bows are being cut, and bright, sharp lances, spears shining
with gold, and the pennants of elephants, by angry Kiritin. This is no time for
those who live on the son of a king to guard their lives. Go, putting heaven
first, for fame and victory. The monkey-bannered one is crossing in his chariot
the very terrible river of battle, so hard to cross, its whirlpools horses,
elephants and chariots.

"'Reverence for brahmins, self-control, giving, austerity and great good conduct
are seen here, in the king whose brother is Dhananjaya, and mighty Bhimasena,
and the two Pandava sons of Madri, and whose protector is Vasudeva the
Varshneya. It is the anger of that man, whose body is burned pure by austerity,
born of the evil-minded son of Dhritarashtra's wrongs, that is burning up the
Bharatas. Here is Partha, relying on Vasudeva, to be seen tearing apart all the
armies of the son of Dhritarashtra on every side. This army is seen being
churned by Kiritin, like a great river mouth bound with great waves, churned by
a whale. Cries of "Alas!" and yells are heard at the head of the army. Go to the

"'It is hard to find an opening in the array of the king of boundless power. It
is like the belly of the ocean, with chariot fighters beyond the rest stationed
all round. Satyaki and Abhimanyu, Dhrishtadyumna and Vrikodara guard the king,
and the twins guard the lord of men. Dark like Upendra, like a great sal tree
risen up, here goes through the ranks one like a second Phalguna. Take your
finest weapons and another great bow, go to the king's side and fight Vrikodara.
Who would not want his dear son to live for endless years? But putting the
dharma of kshatriyas first, we give you this charge. And here is Bhishma in the
battle, burning up the great army, my son, in battle like Yama and like
Varuna.'"

**Divergences.**

* **"The tenth day."** The text names the day at 6.104.38 (*daśame 'hani saṃprāpte*).
* **Bhishma and Shikhandi.** Pierced by Shikhandi, Bhishma laughs: "Strike me or not, as you please. I will never fight you. For you are the same Shikhandini the Maker made" (6.104.41). Shikhandi swears to kill him anyway (6.104.43–47).
* **Ten thousand a day.** Bhishma reminds Duryodhana of his promise to kill ten thousand kshatriyas each day (6.105.25), made before the war (5.153.21; ledger L-182).
* **Drona's words about Shikhandi.** He calls him "that cunning, evil-minded Panchala" (6.108.17), and repeats that Bhishma will not strike "one with an inauspicious banner" (6.108.19).
* **Bhishma's arrays.** "Day after day" Bhishma made "the arrays of the asuras, of the pishachas and of the rakshasas" (6.104.16). The text does not say what these were.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*435–\*454. \*451 adds the monkey's roar from Arjuna's banner; \*453 has Drona recall Arjuna's austerity before the Kirata.

**Choices.**

* 6.104.3 *dadhivarṇeṣu jalajeṣu*: "conches the colour of curds".
* 6.104.30 *vatsadanta*: "calf-tooth arrows", a kind of arrowhead.
* 6.104.47 *sudṛṣṭaḥ kriyatāṃ … loko 'yam*: "Take a good look at this world."
* 6.105.27 *bhartṛpiṇḍakṛtaṃ ṛṇam*: "the debt of the bread I have eaten from my master".
* 6.107.44 *skandaśaktyā yathā krauñcaḥ*: "like Mount Krauncha long ago with Skanda's spear".
* 6.108.1–3: the grammar makes Drona himself the archer "with the valour of an elephant in rut" who reads the omens; the rendering keeps that.
* 6.108.12 *apasavyaṃ grahāś cakruḥ*: "the planets have moved to the left of the moon".
* 6.108.28 *putropajīvibhiḥ*: "those who live on the son of a king", that is, on Duryodhana's pay.
* 6.108.38: "one like a second Phalguna" is not named in the text; it is probably Abhimanyu.

# Bhima Among the Elephants

Dhritarashtra said, "I think fate is stronger even than human effort, Sanjaya,
since my son's army is being killed by the Pandava army. You always tell me,
son, that my men are killed. You always tell me that the Pandavas are calm and
glad. Today, Sanjaya, you tell me that my men are lacking in manly effort, that
they have fallen, are falling and are killed. They fight as hard as they can and
strive for victory, yet the Pandavas win and my men are beaten. Son, I have
heard again and again of bitter griefs, many of them and hard to bear, all
brought about by Duryodhana. I see no means by which the Pandavas might be
beaten, Sanjaya, or by which my men might win victory in battle."

"Hear of the destruction of men's bodies, king," said Sanjaya, "and of
elephants, horses and chariots. Steady yourself. The great wrong was yours.

"Dhrishtadyumna, struck by Shalya with nine arrows, angrily struck the lord of
the Madras with iron arrows. There we saw a wonderful show of courage from
Prishata's grandson, for he quickly held off Shalya, who shines in battle. No
one could see any difference between the two of them as they fought in fury. For
a while the fight was even. Then, great king, Shalya cut Dhrishtadyumna's bow
with a sharp yellow broad arrow. Then he covered him with a shower of arrows,
Bharata, as rain clouds cover a mountain in the rains.

"With Dhrishtadyumna hard pressed, Abhimanyu in anger ran at speed against the
Madra king's chariot. Krishna's grandson reached it in great anger. His self was
beyond measure. He pierced Artayani with three arrows. Then your men, king,
wanting to catch Arjuna's son in battle, quickly surrounded the Madra king's
chariot and stood there. Duryodhana and Vikarna, Duhshasana and Vivimshati,
Durmarshana, Duhsaha, Chitrasena and Durmukha, Satyavrata, bless you, and
Purumitra, Bharata, stood in battle guarding the chariot of the lord of the
Madras.

"Bhimasena in anger, and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, the sons of
Draupadi, Abhimanyu and Madri's two Pandava sons went against them gladly, lord
of the people. They loosed weapons of many kinds. Each side wanted to kill the
other. They met in battle, king, because of your bad counsel. In that
frightening battle of ten chariot fighters against ten, the other chariot
fighters, yours and the enemy's, became onlookers. Those great chariot fighters
loosed weapons of many kinds, roaring at one another, and fought. They were
alert and furious, all wanting to kill one another. In their impatience they
fell on one another, loosing great weapons.

"Duryodhana in anger quickly pierced Dhrishtadyumna hard in that great battle
with four sharp arrows. Durmarshana pierced him with twenty, Chitrasena with
five, Durmukha with nine arrows, Duhsaha with seven, Vivimshati with five and
Duhshasana with three. Prishata's grandson, scorcher of enemies, pierced each of
them in return with twenty-five, king of kings, showing the lightness of his
hands. Abhimanyu pierced Satyavrata and Purumitra in battle with ten arrows
each, Bharata. Madri's two sons, the joy of their mother, covered their uncle in
battle with floods of arrows, and it was a wonder. Then Shalya, great king,
struck his sister's sons, the best of chariot fighters, with many arrows, as
they sought to pay him back blow for blow. Though covered with arrows, Madri's
sons did not flinch.

---

"Then the very strong Bhimasena, seeing Duryodhana and wanting to put an end to
the quarrel, took up his club. Seeing the strong-armed Bhimasena with his club
raised, like Kailasa with its peaks, your sons ran away in fear. But Duryodhana
in anger urged on the Magadha king with a force of ten thousand swift elephants.
Putting the Magadha in front, he went against Bhimasena. Seeing that force of
elephants coming, Wolf-belly, club in hand, got down from his chariot, roaring
like a lion. He took up his great heavy club, made of the hardest metal, and ran
at the elephants like Death with his mouth open. The strong-armed, mighty
Bhimasena went about the battle killing elephants with his club, like Vasava
with the thunderbolt. At the great roar of Bhima, which made minds and hearts
shake, the elephants huddled together and could not move.

"Then the sons of Draupadi came to guard Bhima's back, with the great chariot
fighter Subhadra's son, Nakula, Sahadeva and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's
grandson. They ran at the elephants and rained arrows on them, as clouds rain on
mountains. With razor arrows, razor-headed arrows, broad arrows and yellow
crescent-headed arrows the Pandavas brought down the heads of the elephant
fighters. With heads falling, and adorned arms, and hands still holding their
goads, it looked like a rain of stones. Elephant fighters were seen on the
elephants' shoulders with their heads cut off, like trees with their tops broken
on mountain peaks. We saw other great elephants killed by Dhrishtadyumna, fallen
or falling, brought down by the great-souled grandson of Prishata.

"Then the Magadha king sent an elephant like Airavata in battle against the
chariot of Subhadra's son. Seeing the Magadha's fine elephant coming, the hero,
Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, killed it with one arrow. When the
king's elephant was down, Krishna's grandson, conqueror of enemy cities, carried
off the king's head with a silver-feathered broad arrow.

"Bhimasena the Pandava plunged into that force of elephants and went about the
battle crushing them, as Indra crushes mountains. We saw elephants struck down
by Bhimasena with a single blow in that battle, like mountains struck by the
thunderbolt. Elephants like mountains lay killed, their tusks broken, their
temples broken, their legs broken, their backs broken, their frontal globes
broken. Others in the battle roared and sank down, or turned away. Others,
broken and terrified, let go of their urine and their dung. In Bhimasena's path
we saw elephants like mountains, dead or killed, and others groaning. Other
great elephants, their frontal globes split, vomited blood and, staggering, fell
to the earth like mountains.

"His limbs smeared with fat and blood, drenched with marrow and grease, Bhima
went about the battle like Death with his staff in his hand. Carrying that club
wet with the blood of elephants, Wolf-belly was terrible and fearful, like
Pinaka's bearer with the Pinaka bow. Crushed by the angry Bhimasena, the
elephants that were left ran off at once, trampling your army. The great
archers, the chariot fighters led by Subhadra's son, guarded that hero as he
fought, as the immortals guard the bearer of the thunderbolt. Carrying his
bloody club, drenched with the blood of elephants, Bhimasena looked like Death,
fierce of soul. We saw him swinging his club in all directions, Bharata, dancing
like Shankara dancing. We saw his terrible killing club, great king, like Yama's
staff, heavy, sounding like Indra's thunderbolt. Mixed with hair and marrow and
smeared with blood, it was like the Pinaka of Rudra when he strikes down beasts
in anger. As a herdsman drives a herd of beasts with a stick, so Bhima drove the
force of elephants with his club. Struck by the club and by arrows on every
side, your elephants ran, trampling their own ranks. Having blown the elephants
away as a great wind blows the clouds, Bhima stood in that tumult like the
bearer of the trident in a burning ground."

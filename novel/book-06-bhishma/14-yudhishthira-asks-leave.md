# Yudhishthira Asks Leave

"When they saw Dhananjaya with his arrows and the Gandiva," said Sanjaya, "the
great chariot fighters roared again. The Pandavas, the Somakas and their
followers blew their sea-born conches with joy. Then drums and tabors, saws and
cow-horns sounded all at once, and there was a great noise. The gods came to
watch, lord of men, with the gandharvas and the ancestors, and hosts of siddhas
and charanas. The fortunate seers came together too, with Shatakratu at their
head, to see that great slaughter.

"Then Yudhishthira saw the two armies, like two oceans, ready for battle and
surging again and again. The hero took off his armour and put down his fine
weapon. He got down quickly from his chariot, and on foot, with joined palms,
looking towards the grandfather, the righteous king went silently east, towards
the enemy army.

"When Dhananjaya, son of Kunti, saw him going, he got down quickly from his
chariot and followed him with his brothers. The blessed Vasudeva followed
behind, and the chief kings followed eagerly.

"'What have you decided, king,' said Arjuna, 'that you leave us and go on foot,
east, towards the enemy army?'

"'Where are you going, lord of kings,' said Bhima, 'with your armour and weapons
laid down, leaving your brothers, when the enemy's soldiers are in their
armour?'

"'Bharata,' said Nakula, 'when you, my eldest brother, go like this, fear burns
in my heart. Tell me, where are you going?'

"'In this terrible clash of armies,' said Sahadeva, 'when we must fight, where
are you going, king, towards the enemy?'

"Though his brothers spoke to him like this, joy of the Kurus, Yudhishthira said
nothing, and kept on walking. Then wise and great-minded Vasudeva, almost
laughing, said to them, 'I know what he means to do. He will ask leave of
Bhishma, Drona, Gautama and Shalya, all his elders, and then the king will fight
his enemies. It is heard from the old times that whoever fights without asking
the leave of his elders is plainly cursed by those greater than him. But whoever
asks the leave of his elders as the teachings say, and then fights those greater
than him, is sure of victory in the battle. That is what I think.'

"As Krishna spoke, a great cry went up in Dhritarashtra's son's army, while
others fell silent. When Dhritarashtra's son's soldiers saw Yudhishthira from
afar, they said to one another, 'That shame of his family has no fight in him.
Plainly the king is frightened, and coming close to Bhishma. Yudhishthira is
coming with his brothers to beg for shelter. With Dhananjaya, the Pandava
Vrikodara, Nakula and Sahadeva to protect him, how can the Pandava come to us in
fear? Surely he was not born in a kshatriya family famous on earth, since his
heart is so afraid in battle. There is no courage in him.'

"Then all those kshatriyas praised the Kauravas. Glad and cheerful, they each
waved their clothes. All the warriors there, lord of the people, jeered at
Yudhishthira with his brothers, and with Keshava. When the Kaurava army had
jeered at Yudhishthira, it quickly fell silent again, lord of the people. 'What
will the king say? What will Bhishma answer? What will Bhima say, who glories in
battle? What will Krishna and Arjuna say? What does he mean to say?' In both
armies, king, there was great doubt about Yudhishthira.

"He plunged into the enemy army, bristling with arrows and spears, and went
quickly straight to Bhishma, surrounded by his brothers. He clasped the feet of
Bhishma, son of Shantanu, with his hands, as Bhishma stood ready for battle, and
spoke.

"'I greet you, you whom no one can face. I will fight you, sir. Give me your
leave, sir, and give me your blessings.'

"'If you had not come to me like this in battle, lord of the earth,' said
Bhishma, 'I would have cursed you to be defeated, great king. I am pleased, my
son. Fight, and win, Pandava. And whatever else you wish for, may you have it in
battle. Choose a boon, Partha. What do you want from me? Since you have come
like this, great king, there is no defeat for you. A man is the slave of wealth,
but wealth is no one's slave. That is the truth, great king, and I am bound to
the Kauravas by wealth. That is why I speak to you like a eunuch, joy of the
Kurus. Wealth has taken me, Kauravya. What do you want, apart from my fighting?'

"'Wise one,' said Yudhishthira, 'always give me counsel, wishing me well. Fight
for the Kaurava. That is always my wish.'

"'King, joy of the Kurus,' said Bhishma, 'how can I help you here? I shall fight
for the other side, as I must. Tell me what you want to say.'

"'How can I beat you in battle,' said Yudhishthira, 'when you are unbeaten? Give
me good counsel on this, if you see what is best.'

"'I see no man, son of Kunti,' said Bhishma, 'who could beat me in battle when I
am fighting, not even Shatakratu himself.'

"'Then I ask you this, grandfather,' said Yudhishthira. 'I bow to you. Tell me
the way the enemy can defeat you in battle.'

"'My son,' said Bhishma, 'I see no enemy who could beat me in battle. The time
of my death has not yet come. Come to me again.'

"Then Yudhishthira accepted Bhishma's words with a bow of the head, joy of the
Kurus, and greeted him again. Then the strong-armed man went on to the teacher's
chariot with his brothers, through the middle of the armies, while all the
armies watched. He greeted Drona, walked round him keeping him to his right, and
spoke to that unconquerable man for his own good.

"'I greet you, lord. I will fight free from sin. Give me your leave, brahmin,
and I will beat all my enemies.'

"'If you had not come to me,' said Drona, 'when you had made up your mind to
fight, I would have cursed you to be utterly defeated, great king. So,
Yudhishthira, I am pleased, and you have honoured me, blameless one. I give you
leave. Fight, and win. I will do what you wish. Tell me what you want. Since you
have come like this, great king, what do you want, apart from my fighting? A man
is the slave of wealth, but wealth is no one's slave. That is the truth, great
king, and I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth. That is why I speak to you like
a eunuch. What do you want, apart from my fighting? I shall fight for the
Kaurava, but I shall wish for your victory.'

"'Wish me victory, brahmin,' said Yudhishthira, 'and counsel me for my good.
Fight for the Kaurava. That is the boon I choose.'

"'Your victory is certain, king,' said Drona, 'since Hari is your counsellor. I
too know that you will beat your enemies in battle. Where dharma is, there is
Krishna, and where Krishna is, there is victory. Go and fight, son of Kunti. Ask
me what I should tell you.'

"'Best of brahmins,' said Yudhishthira, 'I ask you: hear what I want to say. How
can I beat you in battle, when you are unbeaten?'

"'There is no victory for you,' said Drona, 'as long as I am fighting in the
battle. Strive for my quick death, king, with your brothers.'

"'Then tell me, strong-armed one,' said Yudhishthira, 'the way you can be
killed. Teacher, I bow down and ask you. I salute you.'

"'I see no enemy, my son,' said Drona, 'who could kill me as I stand in battle,
fighting in fury and pouring out floods of arrows, unless, king, I had given
myself up to death, laid down my weapons and lost my senses. Then a warrior
could kill me in battle. I tell you the truth. And I would lay down my weapons
in battle if I heard something very painful from a man whose word I trust. I
tell you this truly.'

"When he had heard these words of wise Bharadvaja's son, great king,
Yudhishthira took leave of the teacher and went to the son of Sharadvat. The
king, skilled in speech, greeted Kripa, walked round him keeping him to his
right, and spoke to that unconquerable man.

"'I ask your leave, teacher. I will fight free from sin. With your leave,
blameless one, I will beat all my enemies.'

"'If you had not come to me,' said Kripa, 'when you had made up your mind to
fight, I would have cursed you to be utterly defeated, great king. A man is the
slave of wealth, but wealth is no one's slave. That is the truth, great king,
and I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth. I think I must fight for them, great
king. That is why I speak to you like a eunuch. What do you want, apart from my
fighting?'

"'Then I ask you, teacher,' said Yudhishthira, 'hear what I say.' But when the
king had said this, he was so distressed that he lost his wits and could not go
on. Gautama understood what he meant to say, and answered him. 'I cannot be
killed, lord of the earth. Fight, and win. I am pleased that you came to me,
lord of men. I shall rise every day and wish for your victory. I tell you this
truly.'

"When he had heard Gautama's words, great king, the king took leave of Kripa and
went to the lord of the Madras. He greeted Shalya, walked round him keeping him
to his right, and spoke to that unconquerable man for his own good.

"'I ask your leave, elder. I will fight free from sin. With your leave, great
king, I will beat my enemies.'

"'If you had not come to me,' said Shalya, 'when you had made up your mind to
fight, I would have cursed you to be defeated in battle, great king. I am
pleased, and you have honoured me. Let it be as you wish. I give you leave.
Fight, and win. Tell me, hero, what else you need. What shall I give you? Since
you have come like this, great king, what do you want, apart from my fighting? A
man is the slave of wealth, but wealth is no one's slave. That is the truth,
great king, and I am bound to the Kauravas by wealth. I will do what you want,
my sister's son. That is why I speak to you like a eunuch. What do you want,
apart from my fighting?'

"'Always give me your best counsel for my good, great king,' said Yudhishthira.
'Fight for the other side, as you must. That is the boon I choose.'

"'Tell me, best of kings,' said Shalya, 'how I can help you here. I must fight
for the other side, for the Kauravas have bound me with wealth.'

"'The boon you promised me in the preparations for war is my boon,' said
Yudhishthira. 'In battle you must sap the spirit of the charioteer's son.'

"'This wish of yours will come true, son of Kunti, as you want,' said Shalya.
'Go and fight without fear. I promise you victory.'

"Then the son of Kunti took leave of his uncle, the lord of the Madras, and came
out of the great army, surrounded by his brothers.

"But Vasudeva went to Radheya on the battlefield. Then Gada's elder brother
spoke to him for the Pandavas' sake. 'I have heard, Karna, that you will not
fight, because you hate Bhishma. Take our side, Radheya, until Bhishma is
killed. When Bhishma is killed, Radheya, you can come back into the battle on
the side of Dhritarashtra's son, if you think it fair.'

"'I will not do anything that Dhritarashtra's son would not like, Keshava,' said
Karna. 'Know that I have given up my life, and that I want Duryodhana's good.'

"When he heard these words, Bharata, Krishna turned back and joined the
Pandavas, led by Yudhishthira.

"Then, in the middle of the army, the eldest of the Pandavas called out,
'Whoever chooses us, I choose him to help us.'

"Then Yuyutsu looked at them, and with a glad heart he said to Kunti's son, the
righteous king Yudhishthira, 'I will fight the sons of Dhritarashtra in battle
for you, great king, while they look on, if you will choose me, blameless one.'

"'Come, come,' said Yudhishthira. 'We will all fight your foolish brothers.
Yuyutsu, Vasudeva and all of us say this. I choose you, strong-armed one. Fight
for me. In you, it seems, lie Dhritarashtra's line and the offering of rice to
his spirit. Join us, prince, as we join you, you of great splendour. The
evil-minded son of Dhritarashtra, so full of anger, will be no more.'

"Then Yuyutsu the Kauravya left your sons and went over to the army of the sons
of Pandu, to the sound of drums.

"Then King Yudhishthira, with his younger brothers, full of joy, put on his
bright armour again, gleaming with gold. All those bulls among men got back into
their chariots, and they formed their array again as before. They beat drums and
kettledrums by the hundred, and those bulls among men roared their many lion
roars. When the kings, Dhrishtadyumna and the rest, saw the Pandavas, tigers
among men, in their chariots, they were glad again. When the lords of the earth
saw how the sons of Pandu had honoured those who deserved honour, they praised
them highly. The kings talked of the great men's friendship and compassion,
shown at the right time, and of their deep kindness to their kin. Everywhere the
cry went up, 'Well done, well done!', holy words of praise for those famous men,
which gladdened hearts and minds. The mlecchas and noble men who saw and heard
what the sons of Pandu had done wept, their voices choked. Then the
high-spirited warriors, full of joy, beat great drums and kettledrums by the
hundred, and blew conches white as cow's milk."

**Divergences.**

* **Bhishma does not yet tell how he can be killed.** Here he says only, "The time of my death has not yet come. Come to me again" (6.41.43). A rejected passage (\*125) has him list those he will not fight: the unarmed, the fallen, the fleeing, a woman, one with a woman's name, and so on. It is not narrated. He tells the Pandavas how he can be brought down on the ninth night (6.103).
* **Drona's condition.** Drona says he can be killed only if he has given himself up to death, laid down his weapons and lost his senses, and that he would lay down his weapons "if I heard something very painful from a man whose word I trust" (6.41.59–61). How this comes true belongs to Book 7.
* **Shalya's promise.** Yudhishthira reminds Shalya of the boon given "in the preparations for war", to sap Karna's spirit in battle (6.41.81–82; 5.8). See ledger L-172.
* **Karna.** Krishna invites Karna to fight for the Pandavas until Bhishma falls; Karna refuses (6.41.84–87). See ch 6, where Karna has laid down his weapons (6.17.13).
* **Yuyutsu** is the only son of Dhritarashtra to change sides (6.41.89–95). Yudhishthira says that in him lie Dhritarashtra's line and the offering of rice to his spirit (6.41.93).
* **"Where dharma is, there is Krishna."** Drona's words (6.41.55) echo 6.2.14 and 6.21.12–14.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*115–\*133. \*115 has Arjuna take up his weapons after seeing Vishnu's form; \*117 has Bhima think Yudhishthira afraid.

**Choices.**

* 6.41.3 *krakacāḥ*: "saws", read as a kind of rattle or noise-maker; the sense is uncertain. *Goviṣāṇikāḥ*: "cow-horns".
* 6.41.21 *neśo 'sti*: "has no fight in him".
* 6.41.36–37 *arthasya puruṣo dāsaḥ*: "A man is the slave of wealth, but wealth is no one's slave." *Klībavat*: "like a eunuch", the text's own word for his helplessness. The same words are repeated by Drona, Kripa and Shalya, and are kept each time.
* 6.41.60 *prāyagatam*: "given myself up to death".
* 6.41.69: Yudhishthira is too distressed to finish; Kripa answers what he means to ask.
* 6.41.84 *gadāgrajaḥ*: "Gada's elder brother", Krishna.
* 6.41.93 *piṇḍaś ca tantuś ca*: "the offering of rice … and the line".

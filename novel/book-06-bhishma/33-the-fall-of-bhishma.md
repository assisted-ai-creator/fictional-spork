summary: The rest of the tenth day. Bhima holds off ten Kaurava champions, and Arjuna joins him. Duhshasana fights Arjuna alone and is beaten, and the kings who guard Bhishma are driven back. Weary of life after ten days of slaughter, Bhishma tells Yudhishthira to put Arjuna in front and try to kill him. With Shikhandi in front, the Pandavas close in. Bhishma kills Virata's brother Shatanika and seven great chariot fighters, but Arjuna cuts his bow again and again. Bhishma decides not to fight the Pandavas, for two reasons: they cannot be killed, and Shikhandi was born a woman. The seers and the Vasus in the sky approve, and only Bhishma and Sanjaya hear them. Pierced all over, Bhishma tells Duhshasana that these are not Shikhandi's arrows but Arjuna's. A little before sunset he falls from his chariot, his head to the east, held off the ground by the arrows. Since the sun is in its southern course, he holds on to life. Ganga sends seers in the form of swans, and he tells them he will wait for the sun to turn north. The Kurus stand stunned, and Bhima dances.

# The Fall of Bhishma

"Bhagadatta, Kripa, Shalya, Kritavarma the Satvata, Vinda and Anuvinda of
Avanti, Jayadratha the Saindhava, Chitrasena, Vikarna and young Durmarshana,"
said Sanjaya, "these ten warriors of yours fought Bhimasena. They had a great
army from many lands, king, and they were seeking great glory in Bhishma's
battle. Shalya struck Bhimasena with nine arrows, Kritavarma with three and
Kripa with nine. Chitrasena, Vikarna and Bhagadatta, sir, struck Bhimasena with
ten broad arrows each. The Saindhava struck him at the collarbone with three
arrows, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti with five each, and Durmarshana struck the
Pandava with twenty sharp arrows.

"Mighty Bhimasena pierced each of them in turn, great king, those shining great
chariot fighters, the sons of Dhritarashtra's side, heroes of the whole world,
with many arrows. He pierced Shalya with fifty and Kritavarma with eight, and
cut Kripa's bow with its arrow in the middle, Bharata. When his bow was cut, he
pierced him again with five. He struck Vinda and Anuvinda with three each,
Durmarshana with twenty and Chitrasena with five. Having pierced Vikarna with
ten arrows and Jayadratha with five, Bhima roared with joy, and pierced the
Saindhava again with three.

"Then Gautama, best of chariot fighters, took another bow and in fury pierced
Bhima with ten sharp arrows. Pierced with many arrows like a great elephant with
goads, mighty, strong-armed Bhimasena grew angry and struck Gautama with many
arrows in the battle. With three arrows he sent the Saindhava's horses and
charioteer to the world of the dead, bright as Time the Ender. The great chariot
fighter leapt quickly down from his chariot with its horses killed and shot
sharp arrows at Bhimasena in the battle. With two broad arrows Bhima cut the
great Saindhava's bow in the middle, best of the Bharatas. His bow cut, without
his chariot, his horses and charioteer killed, he quickly climbed into
Chitrasena's chariot, king. The Pandava did a most marvellous deed in the battle
there: he pierced great chariot fighters with arrows, held them back and left
the Saindhava without his chariot while the whole world watched.

"Shalya could not bear Bhimasena's valour. He fitted sharp arrows burnished by
the smith, pierced Bhima with seventy and said, 'Stand! Stand!' Kripa,
Kritavarma and Bhagadatta, sir, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, Chitrasena,
Durmarshana, Vikarna and the mighty Sindhu king, tamers of enemies, quickly
pierced Bhima for Shalya's sake. He pierced them back with five arrows each, and
pierced Shalya with seventy and again with ten. Shalya pierced him with nine and
again with five, and pierced his charioteer deep in a vital spot with a broad
arrow. Seeing Vishoka pierced, mighty Bhimasena planted three arrows in the
Madra king's arms and chest. And he struck the other great archers with three
straight arrows each in the battle, and roared like a lion.

"Set on it, those great archers struck the Pandava, mad in battle, hard in his
vital points with three unblunted arrows each. Badly wounded, the great archer
Bhimasena did not flinch, like a mountain under raining clouds. The famous man
pierced Shalya hard with nine arrows, and pierced the lord of Pragjyotisha
firmly in the battle with a hundred, king. Then with a very sharp razor arrow he
cut the great Satvata's bow with its arrow, like a man of practised hand.
Kritavarma took another bow and struck Vrikodara between the brows with an iron
arrow, scorcher of enemies. Bhima pierced Shalya in the battle with nine iron
arrows, Bhagadatta with three and Kritavarma with eight, and pierced Gautama and
the other chariot fighters with two each. They pierced him with sharp arrows in
the battle, king. Hard pressed on every side by those great chariot fighters, he
thought them no better than straw and moved about without distress. Calmly,
those best of chariot fighters sent sharp arrows at Bhima by the hundred and the
thousand.

"Brave Bhagadatta, the great chariot fighter, hurled at him in the battle a
costly spear of great speed with a golden shaft. King Jayadratha, the
strong-armed, hurled a javelin and a battle-axe, Kripa a hundred-killer, king,
and Shalya an arrow in the battle. The other great archers sent five arrows each
at Bhimasena with force. The son of the Wind cut the javelin in two with a razor
arrow, and cut the battle-axe with three arrows like a sesame stalk. He split
the hundred-killer with nine heron-feathered arrows, and the mighty man cut the
arrow the Madra king had loosed. He suddenly cut the spear hurled by Bhagadatta
in the battle, and the other terrible arrows too, with straight arrows.
Bhimasena, who gloried in battle, cut each of them in three, and struck all
those great archers with three arrows each.

"Then, as that great battle went on, Dhananjaya came there in his chariot,
seeing the great chariot fighter Bhima killing the enemy in the battle and
fighting them with arrows. Seeing those two great Pandavas together, your men,
bull among men, gave up hope of victory there. Then Arjuna, fighting Bhishma,
the great chariot fighter, in the battle, wanting Bhishma's death, with
Shikhandi in front, came up to your ten warriors who stood fighting Bhima in the
battle, king, and Bibhatsu pierced them, to please Bhima. Then King Duryodhana
urged Susharma to kill both Arjuna and Bhimasena. 'Susharma, go quickly with
your floods of troops and kill these two sons of Pandu, Dhananjaya and
Vrikodara.' Hearing his command, the Trigarta, lord of Prasthala, ran at the two
bowmen, Bhima and Arjuna, in the battle, and surrounded them on every side with
many thousands of chariots. Then the battle of Arjuna with the enemy began.

"Arjuna covered Shalya, the great chariot fighter, with straight arrows in the
battle as he strove. He struck Susharma and Kripa with three each, and the lord
of Pragjyotisha, Jayadratha the Saindhava, Chitrasena, Vikarna, Kritavarma,
Durmarshana and the two great chariot fighters of Avanti, lord of kings, each
with three arrows feathered with heron and peacock plumes. The chariot fighter
beyond the rest harried your army in the battle. Jayadratha, standing in
Chitrasena's chariot, pierced Partha with arrows, Bharata, and quickly pierced
Bhima too. Shalya and Kripa, best of chariot fighters, pierced strong-armed
Jishnu in the battle again and again with arrows that split the vital points.
Your sons led by Chitrasena, lord of the people, quickly struck Arjuna and
Bhimasena with five sharp arrows each in the battle, sir.

"Those two best of chariot fighters, the sons of Kunti, bulls of the Bharatas,
harried the great army of the Trigartas in the battle. Susharma too pierced
Partha in the battle with many iron arrows and roared a mighty roar, making the
sky ring. The other brave chariot fighters pierced Bhimasena and Dhananjaya with
sharp, straight arrows with golden feathers. In the middle of those chariot
fighters the two sons of Kunti, best of chariot fighters, noble in their
chariots, wonderful to see, shone as they played, like two mighty lions among
cattle, hungry for flesh. Cutting the heroes' bows and arrows in many ways in
the battle, the two heroes brought down the heads of men by the hundred. Many
chariots were broken, and horses killed by the hundred, and elephants with their
riders fell to the ground in the great battle. Chariot fighters and horsemen,
killed here and there, were seen writhing in many ways on every side, king. The
earth was strewn with the killed masses of elephants and foot soldiers, with
slain horses and many broken chariots, with umbrellas cut to pieces and banners
brought down, with goads thrown away and elephant cloths, Bharata, with armlets,
bracelets, necklaces and trampled deerskins, with turbans thrown down and
yak-tail fans, and here and there with the arms, smeared with sandal, and the
thighs of kings. There we saw Partha's marvellous valour in the battle: holding
back those heroes with arrows, he killed your army.

"Seeing that meeting of Bhima and Arjuna, your son went, in great fear, to the
side of Ganga's son's chariot. Kripa, Kritavarma, Jayadratha the Saindhava and
Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti came into the battle then. Then Bhima, the great
archer, and Phalguna, the great chariot fighter, drove hard at the terrible army
of the Kauravas in the battle. Then the feathered arrows of tens of thousands
and hundreds of millions fell quickly on Dhananjaya's chariot in the battle.
Holding back those great chariot fighters with a net of arrows, Partha sent them
to Death on every side in the battle.

"Shalya, the great chariot fighter, seeming to play, struck Jishnu in the chest
in the battle in anger with straight broad arrows. Partha cut his bow and his
hand-guard with five arrows, and then pierced him hard in a vital spot with
sharp arrows. The lord of the Madras took another bow that could bear any load
and in anger struck Jishnu in the battle with three arrows, great king, Vasudeva
with five, and planted nine in Bhimasena's arms and chest. Then, great king,
Drona and the great chariot fighter of Magadha came, at Duryodhana's command, to
where Partha and Bhimasena the Pandava, great king, those great chariot
fighters, were killing the Kaurava's great army. Young Jayatsena pierced Bhima
of the terrible weapons in the battle with eight sharp arrows, bull of the
Bharatas. Bhima pierced him with ten and again with seven, and took his
charioteer off the chariot seat with a broad arrow. His horses bolted and ran in
all directions, and the Magadha king was carried away while the whole army
watched.

"Drona, finding an opening, pierced Bhimasena with sixty-five sharp iron arrows.
Bhima, who gloried in battle, pierced his teacher, who was like a father to him,
with nine broad arrows and then sixty, Bharata. Arjuna pierced Susharma with
many iron arrows and blew away his army as the wind blows away great clouds.
Then Bhishma, the king and Brihadbala, Subala's son, charged Bhimasena and
Dhananjaya in anger. In the same way the brave Pandavas and Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's son, charged Bhishma in the battle, who was like Death with his mouth
open. Shikhandi came up to the grandfather of the Bharatas and charged the man
of the strict vow gladly, throwing off fear. The Parthas led by Yudhishthira,
with Shikhandi in front, fought Bhishma in the battle, joined with the
Srinjayas. In the same way all your men, putting the man of the strict vow in
front, fought the Parthas led by Shikhandi in the battle.

"Then a battle began that was full of fear for the Kauravas, as they fought the
sons of Pandu for Bhishma's victory. For your side, lord of the people, Bhishma
was the stake in the battle. There the dice had come down for victory or for the
other thing. Dhrishtadyumna, great king, urged on all the armies: 'Charge
Ganga's son! Do not be afraid, best of men!' Hearing the words of the commander,
the army of the Pandavas went quickly against Bhishma himself, giving up their
lives in the great battle. And Bhishma, best of chariot fighters, took that army
as it came on, great king, as the ocean takes the shore."

"How did mighty Bhishma, son of Shantanu, fight on the tenth day against the
Pandavas and the Srinjayas, Sanjaya?" said Dhritarashtra. "And how did the Kurus
hold back the Pandavas in the battle? Tell me of the great battle of Bhishma,
who shone in battle."

"I will tell you how the Kurus fought the Pandavas, Bharata, and how that battle
went," said Sanjaya. "Listen.

"Day after day your troops of chariots that came against him were sent to the
other world by Kiritin's supreme weapons. And the Kauravya, the winner of
battles, made a constant slaughter of the Parthas, as he had promised. Seeing
Bhishma, the great chariot fighter, fighting with the Kurus, and Arjuna with the
Panchalas, people were in doubt. On that tenth day, at the meeting of Bhishma
and Arjuna, a very cruel, constant slaughter went on. There, king, Bhishma,
scorcher of enemies, who knew the supreme weapons, killed warriors by the tens
of thousands and more. Heroes whose names and families were unknown, all brave
men who never turned back, were killed there by Bhishma.

"Having scorched the Pandava army for ten days, righteous Bhishma, scorcher of
enemies, was weary of life. Wanting his own death quickly in the battle,
strong-armed Devavrata, your father, thought, 'I will not kill the best of men
who face me in battle,' and spoke to the Pandava who was near him, great king.
'Yudhishthira, wise one, skilled in every science, listen, my son, to what I
say, which is right and leads to heaven. I am very weary of this body, my son,
Bharata. My time has passed in killing a great many living things in battle. So,
if you want to please me, put Partha in front, with the Panchalas and the
Srinjayas, and try to kill me.'

"Understanding his mind, the Pandava, who saw the truth, went against Bhishma in
the battle, set on it, with the Srinjayas. Hearing Bhishma's words, king,
Dhrishtadyumna and Yudhishthira the Pandava urged on the army: 'Charge! Fight!
Beat Bhishma in battle! You are guarded by Jishnu, true to his word, conqueror
of enemies. And this great archer, Prishata's son, the commander of the army,
and Bhimasena will surely protect you in the battle. You must have no fear of
Bhishma in battle, Srinjayas. We will surely beat Bhishma, with Shikhandi in
front.' Having made that agreement on the tenth day, the Pandavas, set on the
world of Brahma, came together, beside themselves with anger. With Shikhandi and
Dhananjaya the Pandava in front, they made the greatest effort to bring Bhishma
down.

"Then the lords of many lands, sent by your son, with Drona and his son, with
their armies, mighty men, and mighty Duhshasana with all his brothers, guarded
Bhishma, standing in the middle of the battle. Then your brave men, putting the
man of the strict vow in front, fought the Parthas led by Shikhandi in the
battle. The one with the monkey banner, with the Chedis and the Panchalas, went
against Bhishma, son of Shantanu, with Shikhandi in front. The grandson of Shini
fought Drona's son, Dhrishtaketu fought the Paurava, and Yudhamanyu fought
Duryodhana with his ministers. Virata, scorcher of enemies, with his division,
came up against Jayadratha with his army, the heir of Vriddhakshatra.
Yudhishthira went against the great archer, the Madra king, with his army, and
Bhimasena, well guarded, charged the elephant division. The Panchala, set on it,
with the Somakas, went against Drona, the best of all who bear weapons,
unassailable and unstoppable. Prince Brihadbala, tamer of enemies, with the lion
banner, went out against Subhadra's son with the karnikara banner. Your sons
with the kings attacked Shikhandi and Dhananjaya the Pandava in the battle,
wanting to kill them.

"In that most fearful display of valour by the armies, as the divisions ran at
each other, the earth shook. Seeing Shantanava in the battle, the divisions of
yours and theirs locked together, Bharata. As they strove and charged each
other, a great noise rose in every direction, Bharata. With the sound of conches
and drums, the trumpeting of elephants and the lion roars of the armies, it
became fearful. The splendour of all the kings, like the moon and the sun, grew
dim on the heroes' armlets and crowns. Clouds of dust rose, lit by the lightning
of weapons, and the twang of the bows became fearful. The sound of arrows and
conches, the great noise of drums and the rumble of chariots of both armies came
together. The sky between the armies, crowded with masses of lances, spears and
javelins and floods of arrows, seemed to lose its light. Chariot fighters and
horsemen fell on each other in the great battle. Elephants killed elephants, and
foot soldiers foot soldiers. That great battle of the Kurus and the Pandavas was
fought for Bhishma, tiger among men, like the fight of two hawks for a piece of
meat. Their meeting in battle was terrible, Bharata, as they fought to kill each
other, wanting victory.

"Abhimanyu, great king, fought your son with a great army, valiant for Bhishma's
sake. Duryodhana in anger struck Arjuna's son in the battle with nine straight
arrows and then with three more. In anger Arjuna's son hurled at Duryodhana's
chariot a spear, terrible as the sister of Death. As it suddenly came at him,
terrible in form, lord of the people, your son, the great chariot fighter, cut
it in two with a razor arrow. Seeing the spear fall, Arjuna's son, in great
fury, planted three arrows in Duryodhana's arms and chest, and struck the
unforgiving Duryodhana again in the middle of the chest with ten terrible
arrows, best of the Bharatas. That battle was terrible and wonderful to see,
Bharata, a joy to those who watched it, honoured by all the kings. The two
heroes, Subhadra's son and the bull of the Kurus, fought in the battle for
Bhishma's death and for Partha's victory.

"Drona's son, bull among brahmins, scorcher of enemies, in anger struck fierce
Satyaki in the chest with an iron arrow in the battle. Shaineya, whose spirit
had no measure, struck the teacher's son in every vital point with nine
heron-feathered arrows, Bharata. Ashvatthama struck Satyaki in the battle with
nine arrows, and quickly planted thirty more in his arms and chest. Badly
wounded by Drona's son, the famous great archer, the Satvata, struck Drona's son
with three arrows.

"The Paurava came at Dhrishtaketu, the great archer, the great chariot fighter,
in the battle and tore him in many places with arrows. In the same way mighty
Dhrishtaketu, the great chariot fighter, pierced the Paurava in the battle with
thirty sharp arrows. The great chariot fighter, the Paurava, cut Dhrishtaketu's
bow, roared a mighty roar and pierced him with ten arrows. He took another bow
and struck the Paurava with seventy-three sharp arrows, great king. Those two
great archers, great men, great chariot fighters, rained a great shower of
arrows on each other. Each cut the other's bow and killed his horses, Bharata,
and without chariots the two great chariot fighters met to fight with swords.
Taking bright shields of bull's hide adorned with a hundred moons, and very
bright swords adorned with a hundred stars, king, they ran at each other, like
two lions in a great forest set on it at the meeting with a lioness in heat.
They moved in bright circles, advancing and falling back, showing their skill
and seeking each other. The Paurava in anger struck Dhrishtaketu on the temple
with his great sword and said, 'Stand! Stand!' And the Chedi king struck the
Paurava, bull among men, at the collarbone with his great sharp-pointed sword.
Meeting each other in the great battle, great king, struck by each other's
force, the two tamers of enemies fell. Then your son Jayatsena took the Paurava
up into his chariot, king, and carried him from the field. And Sahadeva, the
valiant son of Madri, scorcher of enemies, carried Dhrishtaketu away from the
battle, king.

"Chitrasena pierced Susharma with nine swift arrows, then again with sixty and
again with nine. Susharma in anger in the battle pierced your son, lord of the
people, with ten sharp arrows and ten more. Chitrasena in anger struck him in
the battle with thirty straight arrows, king, and he struck back, increasing
Bhishma's glory and honour in the battle, king. Subhadra's son fought Prince
Brihadbala. The lord of Kosala pierced Arjuna's son with five iron arrows and
again with twenty straight ones. Subhadra's son pierced Brihadbala with nine
iron arrows and did not shake him in the battle, and pierced him again and
again. Phalguna's son cut the Kosala's bow again and struck him with thirty
heron-feathered arrows. Prince Brihadbala took another bow and in anger pierced
Phalguna's son with many arrows in the battle. Their battle for Bhishma's sake,
scorcher of enemies, great king, those two angry, wonderful fighters, was like
that of Maya and Vasava in the war of the gods and the asuras.

"Bhimasena, fighting the elephant division, shone greatly, like Shakra,
thunderbolt in hand, splitting the finest mountains. Killed by Bhima, the
elephants like mountains fell together to the ground, making the earth ring. As
tall as mountains, like heaps of broken collyrium, those elephants lay scattered
on the earth like mountains. Yudhishthira, the great archer, met the Madra king
in the battle, guarded by a great army, and pressed him hard. And the lord of
the Madras in the battle, in fury, valiant for Bhishma's sake, pressed the son
of Dharma, the great chariot fighter. The Sindhu king pierced Virata with nine
sharp straight arrows, and harried him again with thirty. Virata, great king,
struck the Saindhava in the middle of the chest at the head of the army with
thirty sharp arrows. With bright bows and swords, with bright armour, weapons
and banners, the Matsya and the Saindhava shone in the battle, wonderful to see.

"Drona met the son of the Panchala in the great battle and made a great display
with straight arrows. Then Drona, great king, cut Prishata's son's great bow and
pierced him with fifty arrows. Prishata's son, killer of enemy heroes, took
another bow and sent arrows at Drona in the battle while he watched. The great
chariot fighter Drona held off those arrows with masses of arrows and sent five
arrows at Drupada's son. Prishata's son, killer of enemy heroes, in anger, great
king, hurled at Drona in the battle a mace like Yama's staff. As it came
suddenly at him, adorned with gold bands, Drona held it back in the battle with
fifty arrows. Cut many times by the arrows from Drona's bow, king, it fell to
the earth, crumbling into dust. Seeing his mace struck down, Prishata's son,
destroyer of enemies, hurled at Drona a beautiful spear all of iron. Drona cut
it with nine arrows in the battle, Bharata, and pressed the great archer,
Prishata's son, in the battle. So there was that great battle between Drona and
Prishata's son, great king, terrible and full of fear, for Bhishma's sake.

"Arjuna reached Ganga's son and, harrying him with sharp arrows, charged him as
he stood set on it, like a mad elephant in the forest. Mighty Bhagadatta came
out against Partha on an elephant blind with rut, streaming rut from three
places. As it came suddenly on, like great Indra's elephant, Bibhatsu made the
greatest effort and met it. Then mighty King Bhagadatta, on his elephant, held
Arjuna back in the battle with a shower of arrows. Arjuna pierced the elephant
as it came on, like silver, with bright, sharp iron arrows in the great battle.
And the son of Kunti urged Shikhandi on, great king, towards Bhishma: 'Go! Go!
Kill him!' Then the king of Pragjyotisha, elder brother of Pandu, left the
Pandava and went quickly to Drupada's chariot, king. Then Arjuna, great king,
charged Bhishma quickly, with Shikhandi in front, and the battle went on.

"Then all your brave men charged the fierce Pandava in the battle, shouting, and
it was a marvel. Arjuna blew away the various divisions of your sons, lord of
men, in good time, as the wind blows away clouds in the sky. Shikhandi came up
to the grandfather of the Bharatas and calmly heaped him quickly with many
arrows. Bhishma killed the Somakas who followed Partha in the battle, and the
great chariot fighter held back the army of the Pandavas. The fire of Bhishma,
with chariots for its hearth and bows for its flames, swords, spears and maces
for its fuel, great flames of masses of arrows, burned up the kshatriyas in the
battle. As a great fire driven by the wind moves through dry brush, so Bhishma
blazed, calling up divine weapons. With straight, sharp arrows feathered with
gold, the famous Bhishma made the quarters and the points between ring. Bringing
down chariot fighters, king, and elephants with their riders, he made the lines
of chariots look like groves of palm trees with their tops cut off. Bhishma, the
best of all who bear weapons, left chariots, elephants and horses without men in
the battle, king. Hearing the slap of his bowstring on his arm-guard, like the
crack of a thunderbolt, the soldiers trembled on every side, king. Your father's
arrows fell without missing, lord of men. The arrows loosed from Bhishma's bow
did not stop in bodies. We saw chariots without men, king, well yoked to swift
horses, being carried about as if by the wind, lord of the people.

"Fourteen thousand great chariot fighters of the Chedis, Kashis and Karushas,
famous, of good families, ready to give up their bodies, brave men who never
turned back, with banners worked with gold, met Bhishma in the battle, like
Death with his mouth open, and went to the other world with their horses,
chariots and elephants. There was no great chariot fighter of the Somakas there,
great king, who, having come up to Bhishma in the battle, set his mind on
living. Seeing Bhishma's valour, people thought all those warriors in the battle
had been taken to the city of the king of the dead. No great chariot fighter
went out against him in the battle except the heroic son of Pandu with the white
horses, whose charioteer is Krishna, and Shikhandi, the Panchala of boundless
power.

"Shikhandi came up to Bhishma in the battle, bull of the Bharatas, and struck
him with ten arrows at a time in the great battle. Ganga's son looked at
Shikhandi sideways with an eye blazing with anger, Bharata, as if to burn him.
Remembering that womanhood of his, king, Bhishma did not strike him in the
battle while the whole world watched, and Shikhandi did not understand it.
Arjuna, great king, said to Shikhandi, 'Hurry, quickly, and kill the
grandfather. Why talk, hero? Kill Bhishma, the great chariot fighter. I see no
one else in Yudhishthira's army who could fight the grandfather Bhishma in
battle except you, tiger among men. I tell you the truth.' Told this by Partha,
bull of the Bharatas, Shikhandi quickly charged the grandfather with arrows of
many kinds. Your father Devavrata, not caring about those arrows, held back
angry Arjuna in the battle with arrows. And in the same way, sir, the great
chariot fighter sent the whole army of the Pandavas to the other world in the
battle with sharp arrows. In the same way the Pandavas, king, surrounded by a
great army, covered Bhishma as clouds cover the sun. Surrounded on every side,
bull of the Bharatas, the Bharata burned up the heroes in the battle like a
blazing fire in a forest.

"There we saw a marvel of manhood in your son: he fought Partha and guarded the
man of the strict vow. All the worlds were pleased with that deed in the battle
of your great son Duhshasana, the bowman. For alone he fought the Parthas and
their followers in the battle, and the Pandavas could not hold him back in the
battle, fierce as he was. Chariot fighters were made chariotless in the battle
by Duhshasana, great king, and horsemen and mighty elephants, split by sharp
arrows, fell to the ground, and other elephants, hurt by arrows, ran in all
directions. As a fire finding fuel blazes with fierce flames, so your son
blazed, burning up the Pandavas. No great chariot fighter of the Pandavas dared
to beat that great man of the Bharatas, or even to go out against him, except
the son of great Indra with the white horses, whose charioteer is Krishna. And
even he, Vijaya Arjuna, having beaten him in the battle, king, charged Bhishma
while the whole army watched. And your son, though beaten, relying on Bhishma's
arms, came to again and again and fought on, fierce in battle. Fighting Arjuna
in the battle, king, he shone.

"But Shikhandi, king, pierced the grandfather in the battle with arrows that
struck like the thunderbolt, like snake venom. They gave your father no pain,
lord of men. Ganga's son, smiling, took those arrows then. As a man tormented by
heat welcomes streams of rain, so Ganga's son took the streams of Shikhandi's
arrows. The kshatriyas, great king, saw him terrible in the battle, Bhishma,
burning up the armies of the great Pandavas.

"Then your son said to all the armies, sir, 'Charge Phalguna in the battle with
chariots on every side! Bhishma, who knows dharma, will protect all of you in
the battle. Throw off your very great fear and fight the Pandavas. Here stands
Bhishma with his blazing palm banner, guarding, the shelter and the armour of
all the sons of Dhritarashtra in battle. Even the thirty gods, all together,
could not stand up to Bhishma, let alone the Parthas, who are mortal and so
weak. So charge, warriors, when you reach Phalguna in the battle. Today in the
battle I myself, set on it, will fight Phalguna with you, lords of the earth,
all set on it on every side.'

"Hearing those words of your son the bowman, king, the mighty great chariot
fighters set themselves against Arjuna. The Videhas and Kalingas with the hosts
of the Dasherakas, the Nishadas and Sauviras charged in the great battle. The
Bahlikas and Daradas, the easterners, northerners and Malavas, the Abhishahas,
Shurasenas, Shibis and Vasatis, the Shalvashrayas and Trigartas, the Ambashthas
with the Kekayas charged Partha in the battle like moths into a fire.
Dhananjaya, calling to mind divine weapons and fitting them, great king, quickly
burned up all those great chariot fighters with their divisions. With those
weapons of great speed the mighty Bibhatsu burned them with the heat of his
arrows, as fire burns moths. As the strong bowman loosed thousands of arrows,
the Gandiva was seen as if blazing in the sky. Hurt by the arrows, great king,
their chariots and banners scattered, the kings did not come on together against
the monkey-bannered one. Chariot fighters fell with their banners, horsemen with
their horses, elephants with their riders, struck by Kiritin's arrows. The earth
was covered on every side with the armies of the kings running in many ways,
struck by the arrows from Arjuna's arms.

"Then strong-armed Partha, having put that army to flight, sent arrows at
Duhshasana in the battle. The iron-headed arrows split your son Duhshasana and
all went into the ground like snakes into an anthill. Then he killed his horses
and brought down his charioteer. With twenty arrows he made Vivimshati
chariotless, lord, and struck him hard with five straight ones. The son of Kunti
with the white horses pierced Kripa, Shalya and Vikarna with many iron arrows
and made them chariotless. So the five of them, without chariots, Kripa and
Shalya, sir, Duhshasana, Vikarna and Vivimshati, ran from the battle, beaten by
Savyasachin.

"Having beaten the great chariot fighters so in the forenoon, king, Partha
blazed in the battle like a fire without smoke. With a shower of arrows, like
the sun with its rays, he brought down other kings too, great king. Having
turned the great chariot fighters away with showers of arrows, he set flowing in
the battle a great river whose water was blood, through the middle of the armies
of the Kurus and the Pandavas, Bharata. Elephants and masses of chariots were
killed in many ways by chariot fighters, chariots were killed by elephants, and
elephants by horses and foot soldiers. Bodies and heads, cut in between, of
fighters on elephants, horses and chariots fell in all directions. The field,
covered with princes, great chariot fighters, wearing earrings and armlets,
fallen and falling, shone. Foot soldiers were seen cut by chariot wheels and
crushed by elephants, and horses with their riders. Masses of elephants, horses
and chariots fell on every side, and chariots lay broken on the ground with
their wheels, yokes and banners broken. Soaked with the blood of the floods of
elephants, horses and chariots, the covered field shone like a red cloud in
autumn. Dogs, crows and vultures, wolves and jackals, and misshapen beasts and
birds cried out as they found food. Winds of many kinds blew in every direction,
as rakshasas and ghosts were seen roaring. Gold garlands and costly flags were
seen suddenly smoking, blown by the wind. Thousands of white umbrellas and great
chariot fighters with their banners were seen scattered by the hundred and the
thousand, and elephants with their flags ran in all directions, hurt by arrows.
Kshatriyas, lord of men, holding maces, spears and bows, were seen fallen on the
ground on every side.

"Then Bhishma, great king, calling up a divine weapon, charged Kunti's son while
all the bowmen watched. Shikhandi in his armour charged him as he came on, set
on it, in the battle. Then Bhishma drew back that weapon, which was like fire.
At that very time the son of Kunti with the white horses bewildered the
grandfather and killed your army.

"So, as the armies were drawn up, most of them following on, they all became set
on the world of Brahma, Bharata. In the confusion, division did not meet
division, nor chariots chariot fighters, nor foot soldiers foot soldiers. Horses
did not fight horses, nor elephants elephant fighters. A great and fearful
confusion came over the armies. As men, elephants and chariots were scattered
everywhere in that most fearful destruction, there was no telling one from
another.

"Then Shalya, Kripa, Chitrasena, Duhshasana and Vikarna, brave men, mounted
chariots quickly, Bharata, and made the army of the Pandavas tremble in the
battle. Being killed in the battle by those great men, the Pandava army found no
rescuer, like a ship sinking in the water. As the cold season cuts the vitals of
cattle, so Bhishma cut the vitals of the sons of Pandu. And great Partha brought
down very many elephants of your army, like mountains or clouds. Leaders of men
were seen crushed by Partha, struck by arrows and iron arrows by the thousand.
Great elephants fell here and there, crying out in pain. With the bodies of dead
great men, still in their ornaments, and with heads with earrings, the field
shone, king, in that most fearful destruction of the best heroes, while Bhishma
and Dhananjaya the Pandava showed their valour in the battle.

"Seeing the grandfather's valour in the battle, king, the Kauravyas did not turn
back, with the world of Brahma before them. Wanting death in battle, making
heaven their goal, they came against the Pandavas in that destruction of the
best heroes. The brave Pandavas too, great king, remembering the many kinds of
wrongs done to them by you and your son long ago, threw off fear and fought your
men and your sons gladly, with the world of Brahma before them.

"The commander of the army, the great chariot fighter, said to his army in the
battle, 'Charge Ganga's son, Somakas, with the Srinjayas!' Hearing the
commander's words, the Somakas with the Srinjayas charged Ganga's son with a
shower of weapons on every side. Being struck, king, your father, son of
Shantanu, came under the power of his anger and fought the Srinjayas. The famous
man had long ago been given by wise Rama a training in weapons that destroyed
the enemy's armies. Relying on that training and destroying the enemy's forces,
day after day the old grandfather of the Kurus, Bhishma, killer of enemy heroes,
killed ten thousand of the Parthas. When that tenth day came, bull of the
Bharatas, Bhishma alone killed countless elephants and horses among the Matsyas
and Panchalas in the battle, and seven great chariot fighters were killed. The
great-grandfather killed five thousand chariot fighters and fourteen thousand
men in the great battle, and a thousand elephants and ten thousand horses, lord
of the people, killed by your father's power of training.

"Then, having shaken the army of all the kings, he brought down Shatanika,
Virata's dear brother. Having killed Shatanika in the battle, mighty Bhishma
brought down thousands of kings with broad arrows, great king. Whoever of the
Parthas' kings went against Dhananjaya went to Yama's house when they met
Bhishma. So Bhishma covered the ten directions on every side with nets of
arrows, passed beyond the army of the Parthas and stood at the head of the army.
Having done that very great deed on that tenth day, he stood between the two
armies with his bow in hand. None of the kings could look at him, king, like the
burning sun at midday in summer in the sky. As Shakra scorched the army of the
daityas in battle, so Bhishma scorched the Pandavas, Bharata.

"Seeing him show such valour, Madhusudana, the son of Devaki, said gladly to
Dhananjaya, 'Here stands Bhishma, son of Shantanu, between the two armies.
Unless you kill him by force, you will have no victory. Stop him, set on it,
where this army is breaking. No one else can bear Bhishma's arrows, lord.' Then,
at that moment, king, urged on, the monkey-bannered one hid Bhishma with arrows,
with his banner, chariot and horses. And the bull among the chiefs of the Kurus
drove off with masses of arrows, in many ways, the masses of arrows the Pandava
had loosed. The king of the Panchalas, brave Dhrishtaketu, Bhimasena the
Pandava, Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, the twins, Chekitana and the five
Kekayas, Satyaki, great king, Subhadra's son and Ghatotkacha, the sons of
Draupadi, Shikhandi and brave Kuntibhoja, Susharma and Virata, the mighty men of
the Pandava side, these and many others, hard pressed by Bhishma's arrows and
sunk in an ocean of sorrow, were rescued by Phalguna.

"Then Shikhandi, taking up a supreme weapon, charged Bhishma himself at speed,
guarded by Kiritin. Then unbeaten Bibhatsu, who knew the divisions of the
battle, killed all his followers and charged Bhishma himself. Satyaki,
Chekitana, Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, Virata, Drupada and the two Pandava
sons of Madri, guarded by the strong bowman, ran at Bhishma in the battle.
Abhimanyu and the five sons of Draupadi ran at Bhishma in the battle with great
weapons raised. All those strong bowmen, who never ran from battle, struck
Bhishma in many ways with arrows that found their mark. Shaking off the masses
of arrows loosed by those best of kings, the grandfather, undaunted, plunged
into the army of the Pandavas, striking down their arrows as if at play. He
would not fit an arrow against the Panchala, smiling again and again,
remembering Shikhandi's womanhood, and he killed seven great chariot fighters in
Drupada's division. Then in a moment there were yells from the Matsyas, the
Panchalas and the Chedis as they charged that one man. With masses of fine
horses and chariots, with elephants and foot soldiers, they covered that one
man, as clouds cover the sun, Bhishma, son of Bhagirathi, burning his enemies in
the battle. Then, in their battle, like the war of the gods and the asuras,
Kiritin came at Bhishma, with Shikhandi in front.

"So all the Pandavas, with Shikhandi in front, surrounded Bhishma in the battle
and pierced him on every side. All the Srinjayas together struck Bhishma in the
battle with very terrible hundred-killers, spears and battle-axes, hammers,
pestles, lances and slings on every side, with arrows with golden feathers, with
javelins, lances and shaking spears, with iron arrows, calf-tooth arrows and
bhushundis, Bharata. His armour split, hard pressed by many, Ganga's son did not
flinch, though his vital points were being pierced.

"To his enemies Bhishma became like the fire at the end of an age. His blazing
arrows and bow were its flames, the spread of his weapons its wind, the rumble
of his wheels its roar, the rising of great weapons its heat, his bright bow its
great blaze and the destruction of heroes its great fuel. Falling on the masses
of chariots, coming out between them, he was seen again moving in the middle of
the kings. Then, passing the Panchala king and Dhrishtaketu, he reached the
middle of the Pandava army at speed. Then he struck these six, Satyaki, Bhima
and Dhananjaya the Pandava, Drupada, Virata and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son,
with six arrows bright as the sun, of terrible sound and great speed, arrows
that split enemy elephants. Holding off those sharp arrows of his, those great
chariot fighters struck Bhishma with force, with ten arrows each. The arrows
that Shikhandi loosed in the battle at the man of the great vow went quickly
into Bhishma, with their golden feathers, whetted on stone.

"Then Kiritin in fury charged Bhishma himself, with Shikhandi in front, and cut
his bow. The great chariot fighters could not bear the cutting of Bhishma's bow.
Drona and Kritavarma, Jayadratha the Saindhava, Bhurishravas, Shala, Shalya and
Bhagadatta, these seven, in great fury, charged Kiritin. Showing supreme divine
weapons, the great chariot fighters fell on him in great anger and covered the
Pandavas. The sound of them as they fell on Phalguna was heard like the sound of
the oceans rising at the end of an age. 'Kill! Bring them! Seize! Fight! Cut!'
There was a tumultuous noise round Phalguna's chariot. Hearing that tumultuous
noise, the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas charged, wanting to rescue
Phalguna, bull of the Bharatas: Satyaki, Bhimasena and Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's son, Virata and Drupada both, the rakshasa Ghatotkacha and angry
Abhimanyu. These seven, beside themselves with anger, holding bright bows,
charged quickly. Their battle was tumultuous and hair-raising, best of the
Bharatas, like the gods' battle with the danavas.

"Shikhandi, the best of chariot fighters, guarded by Kiritin, pierced Bhishma
with ten arrows in the battle when his bow was cut. He pierced his charioteer
with ten and cut his banner with one. Ganga's son took another, swifter bow, and
Phalguna cut that too with three sharp broad arrows. So the angry Pandava,
Savyasachin, scorcher of enemies, cut Bhishma's bow again and again as he took
it up. With his bow cut, in anger, licking the corners of his mouth, he took in
anger a spear that could split even mountains, and in anger hurled it at
Phalguna's chariot. Seeing it come like a blazing thunderbolt, the joy of the
Pandavas took five sharp broad arrows. In anger, best of the Bharatas, he cut
that spear, driven by the strength of Bhishma's arm, into five pieces with five
arrows. Cut by angry Kiritin, it fell like lightning broken loose from a mass of
cloud.

"Seeing the spear cut, Bhishma was filled with anger. The hero, conqueror of
enemy cities, thought in his mind in the battle, 'With a single bow I could kill
all the Pandavas, if mighty Vishvaksena were not their guardian. For two reasons
I will not fight the Pandavas: because the sons of Pandu cannot be killed, and
because Shikhandi was a woman. Long ago, when my father married Kali, he was
pleased and gave me death at my own choosing, and that I could not be killed in
battle. So I think my own death has come, its time arrived.'

"Knowing this decision of Bhishma of boundless power, the seers and the Vasus in
the sky said to Bhishma, 'What you have decided, hero, is very dear to us too.
Do it, great archer. Turn your mind away from battle.' At the end of those words
a kindly wind rose, favourable and fragrant, with drops of rain. The drums of
the gods sounded with a great noise, and a shower of flowers fell on Bhishma,
lord of the earth. No one heard them speaking, king, except strong-armed
Bhishma, and I too, by the power of the sage. There was great alarm among the
thirty gods, lord of the people, that Bhishma, dear to all the worlds, would
fall from his chariot. Hearing those words of the hosts of the gods, the
great-hearted Bhishma, son of Shantanu, did not go against Bibhatsu then, though
he was being split with sharp arrows that split all armour.

"Shikhandi, great king, in anger, struck the grandfather of the Bharatas in the
chest with nine sharp arrows. Struck by him in the battle, Bhishma, the
grandfather of the Kurus, did not tremble, great king, like a mountain in an
earthquake. Then Bibhatsu, laughing, drawing the Gandiva bow, planted
twenty-five small arrows in Ganga's son. Again Dhananjaya, in a hurry, in anger,
struck him in every limb and every vital point with a hundred arrows. So, though
badly struck by others too in the great battle, the gold-feathered arrows
whetted on stone gave him no pain.

"Then Kiritin in fury charged Bhishma himself, with Shikhandi in front, and cut
his bow. He pierced him with ten arrows and cut his banner with one, and shook
his charioteer with ten arrows. Ganga's son took another, stronger bow, and he
drove that off too with three sharp broad arrows, cutting it in three, in the
blink of an eye, as each was taken up in the great battle. So he cut very many
bows of his in the battle. Then Bhishma, son of Shantanu, did not go against
Bibhatsu.

"Then he harried him with twenty-five small arrows. Badly wounded, the great
archer said to Duhshasana, 'Here is Partha, the great chariot fighter of the
Pandavas, angry in battle, aiming many thousands of arrows at me alone in the
battle. He cannot be beaten in battle even by the Wielder of the Thunderbolt.
Nor could all the heroes together, gods, danavas and rakshasas, beat me, let
alone mortals, who are very weak.'

"As those two were talking, Phalguna, with Shikhandi in front, pierced Bhishma
in the battle with sharp arrows. Then, badly wounded by the Gandiva bowman's
sharp arrows, he spoke to Duhshasana again, smiling. 'These arrows,
sharp-pointed, striking like the thunderbolt and lightning, coming in without a
break, are not Shikhandi's arrows. These that cut my vitals and split strong
armour, striking me like pestles, are not Shikhandi's arrows. These that touch
like Brahma's staff, with the force of the thunderbolt, unbearable, that tear at
my life, are not Shikhandi's arrows. These that go into my vitals like angry
snakes swollen with poison, licking, are not Shikhandi's arrows. These that seem
to destroy my life like Yama's messengers sent against me, that touch like a
mace or an iron bar, are not Shikhandi's arrows. They cut my limbs as the month
of Magha cuts cattle. These are Arjuna's arrows. These are not Shikhandi's
arrows. All the other kings together could not give me pain, except the hero
with the Gandiva bow, Jishnu with the monkey banner.'

"Saying this, Shantanu's son, as if he would burn up the Pandava, hurled a spear
with sparks and a blazing point, Bharata. He cut it with arrows and brought it
down in three pieces with three, while all the heroes of the Kurus watched,
Bharata. Then Ganga's son took a shield adorned with gold, and a sword, wanting
either death first or victory. But the other shattered that armoured man's
shield into a hundred pieces before he could get down from his chariot, and it
was a marvel. Roaring loudly like a lion, he urged on his own divisions: 'Charge
Ganga's son! Let there not be the least fear among you!'

"Then with javelins, lances and floods of arrows on every side, with battle-axes
and swords and weapons of many kinds, with calf-tooth arrows and broad arrows,
they ran at that one man. A terrible lion roar went up from the Pandavas. In the
same way your sons, king, wanting Bhishma's victory, came against that one man
and gave lion roars. There was a tumultuous battle there between your men and
the enemy, on the tenth day, lord of kings, at the meeting of Bhishma and
Arjuna. For a moment there was a whirlpool like Ganga's in the ocean, as the
armies fought, killing one another. The earth, soaked with blood, became
impassable, and nothing could be told, level or uneven. Having killed ten
thousand warriors on that tenth day, Bhishma stood in the battle, his vital
points pierced.

"Then Partha Dhananjaya, standing at the head of the army, put your army to
flight through the middle of the Kuru ranks. We, afraid of Kunti's son
Dhananjaya with his white horses, hard pressed by sharp weapons, ran from the
great battle. The Sauviras, Kitavas, easterners, westerners, northerners and
Malavas, the Abhishahas, Shurasenas, Shibis and Vasatis, the Shalvashrayas,
Trigartas and Ambashthas with the Kekayas, these twelve peoples, hurt by arrows
and pained by wounds, did not leave Bhishma in the battle as he fought Kiritin.

"Then many surrounded that one man on every side, drove off all the Kurus and
covered him with showers of arrows. 'Bring him down! Seize him! Pierce him! Drag
him!' There was a tumultuous noise round Bhishma's chariot, king. Striking him
with floods of arrows by the hundred and the thousand, they left not a finger's
breadth of his body unpierced.

"So your father, lord, cut to pieces with sharp-pointed arrows by Phalguna in
the battle, fell from his chariot with his head to the east, a little before the
sun set, while your sons watched. As Bhishma fell from his chariot there was a
very great cry of 'Alas! Alas!' in the sky, from the gods, and from the kings on
every side. Seeing the great grandfather falling, all our hearts fell with
Bhishma. The strong-armed man fell, making the earth ring, like Indra's banner
thrown down, the banner of all bowmen. And he did not touch the ground, covered
as he was with masses of arrows.

"As the great archer, the bull among men, lay on his bed of arrows, fallen from
his chariot, a divine state came over him. Parjanya rained, and the earth
trembled. As he fell he saw that the sun was diminished. The hero kept his
senses, thinking of the time, Bharata. And he heard a divine voice from the sky
on every side: 'How will great Ganga's son, the best of all who bear weapons,
tiger among men, make his end when the sun has come to its southern course?'
Hearing that, Ganga's son said, 'I am here.' Fallen though he was on the ground,
Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, held on to his life, waiting for the northern
course.

"Knowing his mind, Ganga, daughter of Himavat, sent great seers there in the
form of swans. Then the swans that live on Lake Manasa came flying swiftly
together to see Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, where the best of men lay on
his bed of arrows. Those sages in the form of swans came to Bhishma and saw
Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, lying on his bed of arrows. Seeing that great
man, Ganga's son, best of the Bharatas, they walked round him with their right
sides towards him, and round the sun in the south too. Addressing one another,
the wise ones said there, 'Will Bhishma, great as he is, make his end in the
southern course?'

"Having said this, the swans set off towards the southern quarter. Seeing them
go, the man of great understanding thought, Bharata, and Shantanu's son said to
them, 'I will never go while the sun has turned south. That is fixed in my mind.
I will go to my own old place when the sun has turned north, swans. I tell you
the truth. I will hold on to my life, longing for the northern course. For I
have power over the giving up of my life, and I am bound to that. So I will hold
on to my life, wanting to die in the northern course. And the boon my great
father gave me, "Death at your choosing," let that boon be true. So I will hold
on to my life, since the giving it up is in my control.' Having said this to the
swans, he lay on his bed of arrows.

"When Bhishma of great power, the peak of the Kurus, had fallen, the Pandavas
and the Srinjayas gave lion roars. When that man of great strength, the one not
in the middle among the Bharatas, was struck down, your sons, bull of the
Bharatas, did not know what to do. A tumultuous bewilderment came over the Kurus
then. The kings led by Duryodhana sighed and wept. In their despair they stood
for a long time with their senses gone. They brooded, great king, and did not
set their minds on battle. As if seized by the thighs, they did not charge the
Pandavas. When the son of Shantanu, who could not be killed, Bhishma of great
power, was struck down, a very great ruin, king, came upon the Kurus,
unsleeping. We, our best heroes killed, cut by sharp arrows, beaten by
Savyasachin, did not know what to do.

"But the Pandavas, having won victory, and the highest place in the next world
too, all the brave men with arms like iron bars, blew their great conches, and
the Somakas with the Panchalas rejoiced, lord of men. Then, as thousands of
instruments sounded, mighty Bhimasena slapped his arms hard, and danced. When
Ganga's son was brought down, the heroes of both armies laid down their weapons
and brooded all round. Some cried out, others fell, others fainted. Some blamed
the way of the kshatriyas, and some honoured Bhishma. The seers and the
ancestors praised the man of the great vow, and those of the Bharatas who had
gone before praised him.

"And wise, valiant Shantanu's son, holding to the yoga of the great Upanishad,
murmuring, stayed waiting for his time."

**Divergences.**

* **Bhishma asks to be killed.** Weary of life after ten days of killing, Bhishma tells Yudhishthira to put Arjuna in front and try to kill him (6.111.10–15).
* **"Two reasons."** Bhishma decides not to fight the Pandavas "because the sons of Pandu cannot be killed, and because Shikhandi was a woman" (6.114.32), and recalls his father's boon of death at his own choosing (6.114.33; ledger L-05). The seers and the Vasus in the sky approve, and "no one heard them … except Bhishma, and I too, by the power of the sage" (6.114.34–38).
* **Not Shikhandi's arrows.** Bhishma says six times, "These are not Shikhandi's arrows", and "These are Arjuna's arrows" (6.114.55–60). The text says he fell "cut to pieces with sharp-pointed arrows by Phalguna" (6.114.81). See ledger L-198.
* **The fall.** He falls "with his head to the east, a little before the sun set", held off the ground by the arrows (6.114.81–84). Since the sun is in its southern course, he holds on to life until it turns north. Ganga sends seers in the form of swans (6.114.87–100). The CE here does not give a count of the nights he will lie there.
* **Shatanika.** Bhishma kills Virata's brother Shatanika (6.113.24), not Nakula's son of the same name.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*455–\*481. \*478 has Bhishma say that he could not beat all the great chariot fighters "except Arjuna".

**Choices.**

* 6.110.43 *glaha*: "the stake", in the dicing sense, followed through in "the dice had come down".
* 6.111.21 *brahmalokaparāḥ*: "set on the world of Brahma", that is, ready to die in battle.
* 6.111.31: Brihadbala's lion banner and Abhimanyu's karnikara banner are named as the text names them.
* 6.113.19–20: Bhishma's training by Rama Jamadagnya is the text's own explanation of his power.
* 6.114.3 *bhuśuṇḍī*: a weapon, left as "bhushundis".
* 6.114.38 *munitejasā*: "by the power of the sage", Vyasa's gift of sight to Sanjaya (6.2.9–13).
* 6.114.60 *māghamāse gavām iva*: "as the month of Magha cuts cattle", with its cold.
* 6.114.84 *indradhvaja*: "Indra's banner", the pole raised and lowered at Indra's festival.
* 6.114.102 *bharatānām amadhyame*: "the one not in the middle among the Bharatas", that is, the foremost of them.
* 6.114.112 *mahopaniṣadaṃ … yogam*: "the yoga of the great Upanishad", kept as the text names it.

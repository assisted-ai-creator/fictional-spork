# The Other Islands

"You have told me properly of Jambukhanda, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra. "Tell
me truly its breadth and its extent, and the measure of the ocean, you whose
sight is clear and flawless. Tell me of Shaka island and Kusha island, Sanjaya,
and truly of Shalmala, and of Krauncha island too. Tell me everything, son of
Gavalgana, and about Rahu, the moon and the sun."

"King, there are a great many islands spread across this world," said Sanjaya.
"But I shall tell you of seven, and of the moon, the sun and the planets. The
breadth of Jambu, lord of the people, is eighteen thousand six hundred yojanas.
The breadth of the salt ocean is said to be twice as much. It is crowded with
many lands and bright with gems and coral, adorned with mountains veined with
many minerals and full of siddhas and charanas. The ocean is round.

"I shall tell you properly of Shaka island, lord of the earth. Hear me in due
order, joy of the Kurus. It is twice the breadth of Jambudvipa, lord of men,
great king, and its ocean is larger in proportion. The ocean of milk surrounds
it, best of the Bharatas. The lands there are holy, and no one dies there. How
could there be famine? The people are full of patience and energy. That, great
king, bull among the Bharatas, is Shaka island in brief. What else do you want
to hear?"

"You have told me Shaka island in brief, fortunate Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra.
"Now tell me of it truly and at length."

"There are seven mountains there too, king," said Sanjaya, "adorned with jewels
and full of gems, and there are rivers. Hear their names from me. Everything
there is rich in virtue and holy, lord of the people. The first is called Meru,
and gods, seers and gandharvas are on it. Running east, great king, is the
mountain called Malaya, where the clouds start and from which they spread
everywhere. Beyond it, Kauravya, is the great mountain Jaladhara, from which
Vasava always draws the finest water, and from which the rain comes in the rainy
season, lord of men. There is the high mountain Raivataka, over which the star
Revati always stands fixed in the sky, by the Grandfather's ordering. To the
north, lord of kings, is the great mountain called Shyama, the Dark, from which
the people there have become dark, lord of the land."

"A great doubt comes to me now, Sanjaya, from what you have said," said
Dhritarashtra. "How did the people there come to be dark, son of a suta?"

"In all the islands, wise joy of the Kurus," said Sanjaya, "there are two
colours, fair and dark, and between them a mixed colour, king. I will tell you
where the dark comes from, Bharata. The blessed Krishna lives there, and it
became dark by his radiance.

"Beyond it, lord of the Kauravas, is the great and lofty Durgashaila, and
Kesarin, with its filaments, from which the wind blows. Their breadth doubles in
turn, Kauravya, and the wise have named the varshas on them: Mahameru,
Mahakasha, Jalada and Kumudottara. The one beyond Jaladhara, king, is known as
Sukumara. Raivata's is Kaumara, and Shyama's is Manichaka. Kesara's is Modaki,
and the one beyond is Mahapuman.

"All round it, Kauravya, in length and breadth, it is as famous as Jambudvipa.
In its middle is a great tree called Shaka, great king, in the middle of that
island. The lands there are holy, and Shankara is worshipped there. Siddhas,
charanas and gods go there. The people are righteous, king, and the four
classes, Bharata, are all devoted to their own work. No thief is seen there.
They live long, great king, and they are free of age and death. The people there
increase as rivers do in the rains. The rivers have holy water, and the Ganga
runs there in many streams. There are the Sukumari, the Kumari, the Sita and the
Kaveraka, the Mahanadi, Kauravya, and the river Manijala, and the
Ikshuvardhanika, best of the Bharatas. From them flow holy rivers by the hundred
thousand, joy of the Kuru line, from which Vasava sends the rain. Their names
and their size cannot be counted, for they are the best of holy rivers.

"There are four holy lands there, honoured by the world: the Magas, the
Mashakas, the Manasas and the Mandagas. The Magas are mostly brahmins, king,
devoted to their own work. Among the Mashakas are the kshatriyas, righteous, who
grant every wish. Among the Manasas, great king, are the vaishyas, who live by
their work. They have all they wish for, and they are brave and set on dharma
and wealth. In Mandaga are the shudras, men whose nature is always to keep
dharma. There is no king there, lord of kings, no punishment and no one to
punish. They guard dharma by their own dharma, and they guard one another. That
is all that can be told of that island, and all that is to be heard of mighty
Shaka island.

"A tale is told of the northern islands, Kauravya. Hear me tell it as I have
heard it, great king. Here is an ocean whose water is ghee, and another whose
water is the whey of curds. There is an ocean of wine, and another ocean of hot
water. Each island is twice the size of the one before, lord of men, and all of
them are surrounded by mountains on every side, great king. In the middle island
is a great fair mountain of red arsenic. The western mountain is dark, king,
like Narayana. Keshava himself guards the divine jewels there. Attending on
Prajapati, he made happiness for all creatures.

"In Kusha island a clump of kusha grass stands in the middle of the land, and in
Shalmalika island, king, the shalmali tree is worshipped. In Krauncha island,
great king, the great mountain Krauncha, a mine of heaped jewels, is always
worshipped by the four classes. There is the very great mountain Gomanda, king,
full of every mineral, where the glorious lord Narayana Hari, the lotus-eyed,
always lives, praised by those who seek release.

"In Kusha island, lord of kings, is a mountain covered with coral, called
Sudhaman, which no one can overcome. The second is a mountain of gold. The
third, Kauravya, is the Kumuda mountain, called Dyutimat. The fourth is called
Pushpavat, the fifth Kusheshaya and the sixth Harigiri. These are the six great
mountains, and the distance between them doubles in turn. The first varsha is
Audbhida, the second Venumandala, the third Rathakara and the fourth Palana. The
fifth varsha is Dhritimat, the sixth Prabhakara and the seventh Kapila. These
are the seven groups of varshas. In them gods, gandharvas and people roam and
take their pleasure, lord of the world, and no one dies there. There are no
robbers there, king, and no mleccha peoples. The people are mostly fair and
delicate, lord of the earth.

"I shall tell you of the remaining varshas as I have heard, lord of men, great
king. Listen with an undistracted mind. In Krauncha island, great king, is the
great mountain called Krauncha. Beyond Krauncha is Vamanaka, and beyond Vamana
is Andhakaraka. Beyond Andhakara, king, is Mainaka, the best of mountains, and
beyond Mainaka, king, is the fine mountain Govinda. Beyond Govinda, king, is the
mountain called Nibida. Each one beyond has twice the breadth of the one before,
you who make the line prosper. I shall tell you of the lands there. Hear me as I
name them. Krauncha's land is Kushala, and Vamana's is Manonuga. Beyond Manonuga
is the land Ushna, joy of the Kuru line. Beyond Ushna is Pravaraka, and beyond
Pravara is Andhakaraka. Beyond the land of Andhakaraka is Munidesha, they say.
And beyond Munidesha is the one called Dundubhisvana, full of siddhas and
charanas, where the people are mostly fair, lord of the people. These lands,
great king, are visited by gods and gandharvas.

"In Pushkara is the mountain called Pushkara, full of gems and jewels. The god
Prajapati himself always lives there. All the gods and the great seers always
attend on him, lord of the people, and worship him with words that please his
heart. Jewels of every kind go out from Jambudvipa to the people in all those
islands, joy of the Kurus. In them, through the brahmins' chastity, truth and
self-control, health and length of life double and double again in turn. In
these islands, king, the lands that have been named are one land, Bharata, and a
single dharma is seen in them. The lord Prajapati himself, raising the rod of
punishment, stands always guarding these islands, great king. He is their king,
he is their good, king. He is their father and their grandfather. He protects
the people, best of men, the dull and the wise alike. Food comes to the people
there of itself, Kauravya, and they eat it always ready-made, great king.

"Beyond that is seen the world's boundary, called Sama. It is square, great
king, with a circuit of thirty-three. There stand the four elephants of the
quarters, Kauravya, honoured by the world, best of the Bharatas: Vamana,
Airavata and the others, and Supratika, king, whose temples and mouth stream
with rut. I cannot count its measure, for it is beyond counting, across, above
and below. There, great king, the winds blow from every quarter without
hindrance, and those elephants seize them with their trunks, which are like
lotuses, huge and shining. Then slowly, and again swiftly, they always let the
winds go. Let loose by the breathing of the elephants of the quarters, the winds
come here, great king, and so creatures live."

"You have told me at very great length, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra. "You have
shown me how the islands lie. Now tell me the rest, Sanjaya."

"I have told you of the islands, great king," said Sanjaya. "Now hear from me
truly of the planets. Hear how great Svarbhanu is, best of the Kauravas.
Svarbhanu is said to be a round planet, great king. His diameter is twelve
thousand yojanas, his circumference thirty-six thousand, and his breadth,
blameless one, six thousand, as the wise who know the ancient lore say. The
moon, king, is said to be eleven thousand in diameter, best of the Kurus,
thirty-three thousand in circumference, and fifty-nine hundred in breadth, that
great cool-rayed one. The sun, joy of the Kurus, is eight thousand and two
thousand more in diameter, king, thirty thousand in circumference, and
fifty-eight hundred in breadth, blameless king. So it is told of that most
generous bird, the bright one. That is the measure of the sun given here,
Bharata. By his greatness, Rahu covers these two, the moon and the sun, when the
time comes, great king. This is told in brief.

"So, great king, I have told you all this as it is, as you asked, with the eye
of the teachings. So be at peace. I have told you this world as I have seen it,
with how it goes. So take comfort, Kauravya, about your son Duryodhana.

"A kshatriya who hears this section of the earth, which pleases the heart, best
of the Bharatas, becomes glorious, gains his ends and is honoured by the good.
His life, strength, valour and energy increase. Whoever hears it on the days of
the moon's change, keeping his vows, lord of the earth, pleases his fathers and
his grandfathers. This is Bharata, where we live, where holiness first began.
You have heard it all."

**Divergences.**

* **Why the people of Shaka are dark.** "The blessed Krishna lives there, and it became dark by his radiance" (6.12.20). This is Sanjaya's answer as the text gives it.
* **The chapter's own name.** Sanjaya calls this section *bhūmiparva*, "this section of the earth" (6.13.48), the Bhumi sub-parva named in 1.2.55.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*54–\*58.

**Choices.**

* 6.12.5 *jambuparvataḥ*: "the breadth of Jambu".
* 6.12.19 *varṇāntaram*: "a mixed colour".
* 6.12.23–24: the names of the varshas are given in the text's order. Which varsha belongs to which mountain is not clear in every case.
* 6.12.36 *na daṇḍo na ca daṇḍikāḥ*: "no punishment and no one to punish".
* 6.13.2 *gharmasāgaraḥ*: "an ocean of hot water".
* 6.13.4 *mānaḥśilaḥ*: "of red arsenic".
* 6.13.10 *dyutimān nāma … tṛtīyaḥ kumudo giriḥ*: "The third … is the Kumuda mountain, called Dyutimat"; the reading is uncertain.
* 6.13.28 *eko janapadaḥ*: "are one land".
* 6.13.30 *sa śivaḥ*: "he is their good".
* 6.13.32 *trayas triṃśat tu maṇḍalam*: "with a circuit of thirty-three". The text gives no unit.
* 6.13.41–44: the text gives the diameters in thousands of yojanas and the other figures as bare numbers. The circumferences are taken as thousands and the breadths as hundreds, following the pattern of 6.13.41 (*ṣaṣṭim … śatāni*, "sixty hundred").
* 6.13.44 *pataṃgaḥ … vibhāvasuḥ*: "that most generous bird, the bright one", names of the sun.
* 6.13.47 *saniryāṇam*: "with how it goes"; the sense is uncertain.

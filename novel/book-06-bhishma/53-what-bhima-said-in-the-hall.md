# What Bhima Said in the Hall

"Having passed that night," said Sanjaya, "and slept well, the lords of the
people, Kurus and Pandavas, went out to battle again. Then a great noise like
the ocean rose from both armies as they went out to battle. Then King
Duryodhana, Chitrasena, Vivimshati, Bhishma, the best of chariot fighters, and
Bharadvaja's son, the twice-born, together and well prepared, in armour, king,
arranged the great army of the Kauravas in an array against the Pandavas.

"Bhishma, your father, lord of the people, made a great array like the ocean,
terrible, its waves and ripples the mounts. Bhishma son of Shantanu went in
front of all the troops with the Malavas, the southerners and the men of Avanti.
Next after him was the mighty son of Bharadvaja with the Pulindas, the Paradas,
and the Kshudrakas and Malavas. After Drona, ready, came the mighty Bhagadatta
with the Magadhas, the Kalingas and the pishachas, lord of the people. After the
Pragjyotisha came Brihadbala, king of Kosala, with the Mekalas, the Traipuras
and the Chicchilas. After Brihadbala came the brave Trigarta, lord of Prasthala,
with many Kambojas and Yavanas in thousands. After the Trigarta, Bharata, went
Drona's son, bold and brave, making the earth resound with lion roars. Then King
Duryodhana went after Drona's son with the whole army, surrounded by his
brothers. After Duryodhana went Kripa, son of Sharadvat. So that great array
like the ocean went out. Flags and white parasols shone there, lord, and bright
armlets and costly bows.

"Seeing that great array of yours, the great chariot fighter Yudhishthira
quickly said to Prishata's grandson, commander of the army, 'See the array that
has been made like the ocean, great archer. You too make a counter-array,
Prishata's grandson, without delay.' Then the brave Prishata's grandson made the
very fierce horned array, great king, which destroys enemy arrays. At the horns
were Bhimasena and the great chariot fighter Satyaki, with many thousands of
chariots, horses and foot soldiers. At the navel was the best of men with white
horses and the monkey banner. In the middle were King Yudhishthira and Madri's
two Pandava sons. Then the other kings, great archers with their armies, skilled
in the science of arrays, filled up that array. At the back were Abhimanyu, the
great chariot fighter Virata, the glad sons of Draupadi and the rakshasa
Ghatotkacha. Having drawn up this great array, Bharata, the brave Pandavas stood
in the battle wanting to fight, seeking victory.

"The tumultuous sound of drums mixed with the noise of conches, with roars, the
slapping of arms and shouting, very terrible, in every direction. Then the brave
men came up to one another in battle and looked at one another, enraged, with
unblinking eyes, king. In their minds, lord of men, the warriors had already
challenged one another and come together to fight. Then a battle broke out,
terrible in form and frightening, as yours and the enemy killed one another.

"Sharp iron arrows fell in battle, Bharata, in swarms, like frightening snakes
with their mouths open. Clean, oil-washed, very sharp spears flew out, king,
like flashes of lightning from the clouds. Clubs bound with clean bands and
adorned with gold were seen falling there, fine, like mountain peaks. Swords
shone like the clear sky. Shields of bull's hide with a hundred moons, Bharata,
shone as they fell on every side in battle, king. The two armies fighting each
other in battle, lord of men, shone like the armies of the daityas and the gods
rising against each other. They ran at one another in battle on every side.
Chariots were quickly driven by their chariot fighters in the great battle, and
bulls among kings fought with yoke locked against yoke. From the clash of
fighting elephants fire with smoke arose from their tusks on every side, best of
the Bharatas. Some elephant fighters, struck by spears, were seen falling on
every side, like trees from mountain peaks. Foot soldiers too were seen killing
one another, brave men of wonderful form, fighting with claws and spears. Coming
up against one another, the soldiers of the Kurus and Pandavas sent one another
to Yama's home in battle with terrible weapons of many kinds.

"Then Bhishma son of Shantanu came against the Pandavas in battle, making it
resound with the rumble of his chariot and bewildering them with the sound of
his bow. The Pandavas' chariots too, roaring with a terrible sound, ran at him,
ready, led by Dhrishtadyumna. Then a battle broke out between yours and theirs,
Bharata, with men, horses, chariots and elephants tangled together.

---

"The Pandavas could not look at Bhishma, angry in battle, scorching on every
side, like the burning sun. Then at the command of the son of Dharma all the
troops ran at Ganga's son, who was crushing them with sharp arrows. But Bhishma,
who gloried in battle, brought down the Somakas with the Srinjayas, and the
Panchalas, great archers, with arrows. Being killed by Bhishma, the Panchalas
with the Somakas went quickly at Bhishma himself, giving up the fear that death
brings. The hero Bhishma son of Shantanu at once cut off the arms and heads of
those chariot fighters in battle, king. Your father Devavrata made chariot
fighters chariotless. The heads of horsemen fell from their horses. We saw
riderless elephants like mountains lying bewildered by Bhishma's weapon, great
king. There was no man among the Pandavas there, lord of the people, except
Bhimasena, the very strong, the best of chariot fighters.

"He came up against Bhishma and struck him in battle. Then at the meeting of
Bhishma and Bhima a terrible, frightening groan rose from all the armies. The
Pandavas, glad, roared a lion roar. Then King Duryodhana, surrounded by his
brothers, guarded Bhishma in battle as the destruction of people went on. Bhima,
the best of chariot fighters, killed Bhishma's charioteer. The chariot ran about
everywhere with its horses bolting. With a very sharp razor-headed arrow, the
killer of enemies quickly cut off Sunabha's head. Killed, he fell to the ground.

"When that son of yours, a great chariot fighter, was killed, great king, seven
of his brothers, brave men, could not bear it in battle. Adityaketu, Bahvashin,
Kundadhara, Mahodara, Aparajita, Panditaka and Vishalaksha, very hard to
conquer, crushers of enemies, in bright harness, with bright armour and banners,
ran at the Pandava in battle, wanting to fight. Mahodara pierced Bhima in battle
with nine feathered arrows like thunderbolts, as the killer of Vritra pierced
Namuchi. Adityaketu struck him with seventy, Bahvashin with five, Kundadhara
with ninety and Vishalaksha with seven. Aparajita, great king, the great chariot
fighter, eager to conquer, struck the very strong Bhimasena with many arrows.
Panditaka hurt him in battle with three arrows. Bhima could not bear being
struck by enemies in battle.

"The destroyer of enemies gripped his bow with his left hand and with an arrow
with bent joints cut off in battle the head, with its fine nose, of your son
Aparajita. Beaten by Bhima, his head fell to the earth. Then with another broad
arrow he sent the great chariot fighter Kundadhara to the world of death while
the whole world watched. Then he whose self is beyond measure fitted another
arrow and sent it in battle at Panditaka, Bharata. That arrow killed Panditaka
and went into the ground, like a snake driven by Time that has quickly killed a
man. With three arrows, high in spirit, remembering the old suffering, he cut
off Vishalaksha's head and brought it down to the ground. He pierced the great
archer Mahodara between the breasts with an iron arrow in battle, king, and
killed, he fell to the ground. The killer of enemies cut Adityaketu's banner
with an arrow in battle and cut off his head with a very sharp broad arrow. Then
Bhima in anger sent Bahvashin to Yama's home with an arrow with bent joints.

"Then your other sons fled, lord of the people, thinking that what he had said
in the assembly hall was true. Then King Duryodhana, worn down by the disaster
to his brothers, said to your warriors, 'Kill this Bhima in battle!' So these
great archers, your sons, lord of the people, seeing their brothers killed,
remembered those words. The words that the very wise steward spoke, good and
wholesome, the words of one with divine sight, have come about. Possessed by
greed and folly, out of love for your son, lord of the people, you did not
understand the great words spoken long ago in truth. Surely the strong-armed,
mighty Pandava was born to kill your sons, so does he kill the Kauravas.

"Then King Duryodhana came up to Bhishma, sir, overcome with great grief, and
lamented, worn down. 'My brave brothers have been killed in battle by Bhimasena.
Others too, all the soldiers, are being killed though they strive. And you are
always neutral and neglect us. I have taken a bad road. See this fate of mine.'

"Hearing these cruel words, your father Devavrata, his eyes full of tears, said
this to Duryodhana. 'I told you this before, and so did Drona and Vidura and the
glorious Gandhari, and you did not understand the truth of it, son. And I made a
pact with you before, destroyer of enemies: neither I nor the teacher is to be
let off from the battle at all. Whichever of Dhritarashtra's sons Bhima sees in
battle, he will kill in battle. I tell you this truly. So, king, steady
yourself, make your mind firm in battle and fight the Parthas, making heaven
your goal. The Pandavas cannot be conquered by gods and asuras with Indra. So
make your mind firm for battle and fight, Bharata.'"

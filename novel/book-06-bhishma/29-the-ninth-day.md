summary: The morning of the ninth day. Stung by Duryodhana's words in the night, Bhishma broods, longing to fight Arjuna. Duryodhana tells Duhshasana to guard him with every chariot, repeating Bhishma's word that he will never shoot at Shikhandi, who was born a woman; a wolf must not be let kill a lion. Arjuna has Shikhandi placed in front of Bhishma and guards him himself. Bhishma forms the all-sided array, and terrible omens fill the sky. Abhimanyu breaks the Kaurava ranks until the rakshasa Alambusa comes against him. Alambusa overwhelms Draupadi's five sons, but Abhimanyu scatters his darkness with the sun weapon and drives him off. Satyaki fights Ashvatthama, Arjuna fights Drona and the Trigartas, and Drona stills Arjuna's wind weapon with a mountain weapon. Bhima, on foot among the elephants, tears out a tusk and kills its owner with it.

# The Ninth Day

"When the night had turned to morning," said Sanjaya, "the king rose early and
ordered the kings, 'Yoke the army! Today Bhishma in his anger will kill the
Somakas.'

"Bhishma, son of Shantanu, had heard Duryodhana's long lament in the night, and
took it as a rebuke to himself, king. He fell into deep despair, blamed his
being in another's service, and thought for a long time, wanting to fight Arjuna
in battle. Duryodhana, great king, understood from his looks what Ganga's son
was thinking, and urged on Duhshasana.

"'Duhshasana, have the chariots that guard Bhishma yoked quickly. Urge on all
thirty-two divisions. What we have thought of for a host of years has come: the
killing of the Pandavas with their armies, and the winning of the kingdom. I
think the thing to do now is to guard Bhishma above all. If he is guarded, he
will be our happiness, and he will kill the Parthas in battle. The pure-hearted
man said to me, "I will not kill Shikhandi. He was born a woman first, so I must
avoid him in battle. The world knows that long ago, to please my father,
strong-armed one, I gave up a rich kingdom, and women. And I would never kill a
woman in battle, best of men, or anyone who was a woman before. I tell you the
truth. This Shikhandi was a woman first, king, as you may have heard. What was
told at the time of the preparations for war, that is how Shikhandini was born.
Having been a girl, she became a man, and she will fight, Bharata. I will never
loose arrows at her face. But I will kill all the other kshatriyas of the
Pandavas who seek victory in battle, my son, when they come within range of my
arrows."

"'So said Ganga's son, best of the Bharatas, who knows the sciences. So I think
we must guard Bhishma with our whole selves. In the great forest a wolf may kill
an unguarded lion. Let us not have the tiger killed by Shikhandi as by a wolf.
Let our uncle Shakuni, Shalya, Kripa, Drona and Vivimshati guard Ganga's son
with care. If he is guarded, victory is sure.'

"Hearing Duryodhana's words, the kings surrounded Ganga's son on every side with
a line of chariots. And your sons went gladly, surrounding Ganga's son, shaking
the earth and the sky and troubling the Pandavas. With those well-yoked chariots
and elephants the great chariot fighters stood in their armour round Bhishma in
the battle. As the thirty gods stood round the Wielder of the Thunderbolt in the
war of the gods and asuras, they all stood guarding that great chariot fighter.
Then King Duryodhana spoke to his brother again. 'Yudhamanyu guards the left
wheel of Arjuna's chariot and Uttamaujas the right. These two guard Arjuna, and
Arjuna guards Shikhandi. Duhshasana, see to it that Shikhandi, guarded by Partha
and avoided by us, does not kill Bhishma.' Hearing his brother's words, your son
Duhshasana set out with the army, putting Bhishma in front.

"Seeing Bhishma surrounded by that line of chariots, Arjuna, best of chariot
fighters, said to Dhrishtadyumna, 'Tiger among men, blameless Panchala, place
Shikhandi in front of Bhishma today. I will guard him myself.'

"Then Bhishma, son of Shantanu, went out with the army and drew up a great array
for battle, the all-sided array. Kripa and Kritavarma, the great chariot fighter
Shaibya, Shakuni, the Saindhava and Sudakshina of Kamboja stood with Bhishma and
with your sons, Bharata, in front of all the armies, at the head of the array.
Drona, Bhurishravas, Shalya and Bhagadatta, sir, stood in their armour on the
right wing of the array. Ashvatthama, Somadatta and the two great chariot
fighters of Avanti guarded the left wing with a great army. Duryodhana, great
king, surrounded on every side by the Trigartas, stood in the middle of the
array facing the Pandavas, Bharata. Alambusa, best of chariot fighters, and
Shrutayus, the great chariot fighter, stood in their armour at the back of the
array, behind all the armies. So your men drew up the array, Bharata, and were
seen in their armour like blazing fires.

"In the same way King Yudhishthira, Bhimasena the Pandava, and Nakula and
Sahadeva, the two sons of Madri, stood in their armour at the head of the array,
in front of all the armies. Dhrishtadyumna, Virata and Satyaki, the great
chariot fighter, stood with a great army, destroyers of the enemy's ranks.
Shikhandi and Vijaya, the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, strong-armed Chekitana and brave
Kuntibhoja stood in the battle, great king, surrounded by a great army. The
great archer Abhimanyu, Drupada, the great chariot fighter, and the five Kekaya
brothers stood in their armour for battle. So the brave Pandavas too drew up a
great counter array, very hard to beat, and stood ready for battle, sir.

"Your kings, set on it, with their armies, went out against the Parthas in the
battle, putting Bhishma in front, king. In the same way the Pandavas, king, led
by Bhimasena, wanting victory in the battle, went against Bhishma, who was eager
for battle. With yells, war cries, saws and cow horns, sounding drums, tabors,
kettledrums and pushkaras, the Pandavas charged, roaring terrible roars. With
the noise of drums, tabors, conches and kettledrums, with loud lion roars and
leaps of many kinds, we answered their roars and went at them quickly, all at
once in great anger. Then there was a great tumult. Charging each other, they
fought, and the earth shook with the great noise.

"Birds wheeled about, screaming very terribly. The sun, which had risen bright,
lost its light. Tumultuous winds blew, telling of very great fear. Terrible
jackals howled there with terrible howls, great king, telling of the great
slaughter that had come. The quarters blazed, king, and a shower of dust fell,
and a shower of bones mixed with blood. Tears fell from the eyes of the weeping
mounts, and they let fall dung and urine, lord of the people, brooding. Great
roars were heard, bull of the Bharatas, from rakshasas and man-eaters who could
not be seen, roaring terrible roars. Jackals, herons and crows were seen
swooping down, and dogs stood there barking with cries of many kinds. Great
blazing meteors struck the sun and suddenly fell to the ground, telling of great
fear.

"When the Pandavas and the sons of Dhritarashtra met in that great mustering,
the great armies shone with the sound of conches and tabors, like forests shaken
by the wind. As the armies, crowded with kings, elephants and horses, came on at
that ill-omened hour, there was a tumultuous roar from them like seas stirred by
the wind.

"Abhimanyu, a great chariot fighter, full of fire, charged the great army of
Duryodhana with his fine tawny horses, scattering showers of arrows like a cloud
pouring streams. Your bulls of the Kurus could not hold back in the battle the
angry son of Subhadra, destroyer of enemies, as he plunged with his floods of
weapons into that endless ocean of an army. The arrows he loosed in the battle,
king, killers of enemies, took brave kshatriyas to the house of the king of the
dead. In anger Subhadra's son loosed arrows in the battle like Yama's staff,
terrible, like blazing venomous snakes. Phalguna's son quickly brought down
chariot fighters from their chariots, horsemen from their horses' backs, and
elephant riders with their elephants. As he did that great, wonderful deed in
the battle, the kings were glad, honoured him and praised Phalguna's son.

"Subhadra's son, putting those ranks to flight, shone greatly, like the wind
scattering a heap of cotton in every direction. Put to flight by him, your
armies, Bharata, found no rescuer, like elephants sunk in mud. Having put all
your armies to flight, Abhimanyu, best of men, stood, king, blazing like a fire
without smoke. None of your men could bear that killer of enemies, as moths
driven by fate cannot bear a blazing fire. Striking at all his enemies, the
great archer, the great chariot fighter of the Pandavas, looked like the Wielder
of the Thunderbolt with his thunderbolt. His gold-backed bow was seen as he
moved in every direction, like flashing lightning in the clouds, king. Sharp,
yellow arrows flew from it in the battle, king, like swarms of bees from a
forest of flowering trees. As great Subhadra's son moved about like that in his
chariot, rumbling like a cloud, people could find no opening in him.

"Bewildering Kripa, Drona, Drona's son, Brihadbala and the great archer the
Saindhava, he moved about quickly and well. We saw his bow drawn always into a
circle, sir, like the circle of the sun, as he scorched your army. Seeing him
scorch them with the flames of his arrows, the brave kshatriyas thought from his
deeds that this world had two Phalgunas. Harried by him, great king, the great
Bharata army reeled here and there like a woman drunk. Having put that army to
flight and made the great chariot fighters tremble, he gladdened his friends, as
Vasava did when he beat Maya.

"As your armies were put to flight by him in the battle, they cried out terribly
in pain, like the roar of Parjanya. King Duryodhana heard that terrible cry from
your army, sir, like the sea at the full moon when its force is whipped up by
the wind. He said to Rishyashringa's son, 'This son of Krishna's sister, a great
archer, like a second Phalguna, is putting the army to flight in his anger, as
Vritra did the army of the gods. I see no other great remedy for him in battle
except you, best of rakshasas, master of all learning. Go quickly and kill
heroic Subhadra's son in the battle. We, led by Bhishma and Drona, will kill the
Parthas.'

"Told this, the mighty, powerful lord of rakshasas went quickly into the battle
at your son's command, roaring a great roar like a cloud in the rains. At his
great noise the great army of the Pandavas heaved on every side, king, like the
sea filling. Many men, king, terrified by his roar, gave up their dear lives and
fell to the ground. And Arjuna's son, glad, bow in hand, seeming to dance on the
floor of his chariot, charged that rakshasa. Then the angry rakshasa, reaching
Arjuna's son in the battle, put his army to flight not far from him. Being
killed in the battle, the great army of the Pandavas went out against the
rakshasa, as the army of the gods went against Bali. There was a very great
slaughter, sir, of that army as it was killed in the battle by the rakshasa of
terrible form. Then the rakshasa put the great army of the Pandavas to flight in
the battle with thousands of arrows, showing his valour. Killed like that by the
rakshasa of terrible form, the Pandava army ran in fear in the battle.

"Having crushed that army as an elephant crushes a lotus pond, he then charged
the mighty sons of Draupadi in the battle. But the sons of Draupadi, angry great
archers who struck hard, all ran at the rakshasa like five planets at the sun.
Hard pressed by those men of courage, the best of rakshasas was like the moon
pressed by five planets at the terrible end of an age. Then mighty Prativindhya
quickly split the rakshasa with sharp, unblunted arrows made all of iron. With
his armour split by them, the best of rakshasas shone like a great cloud
threaded with the rays of the sun. With the golden-feathered arrows stuck in
him, Rishyashringa's son shone, king, like a mountain with blazing peaks. Then
the five brothers pierced the lord of rakshasas in the great battle with sharp
arrows adorned with gold. Split by those terrible arrows, like angry snakes,
Alambusa grew very angry, king, like a lordly serpent.

"Badly wounded, great king, hard pressed by those great chariot fighters, he
went for a moment into a long darkness, sir. Coming to, doubled in his anger, he
cut their banners and their bows with arrows. Alambusa, the great chariot
fighter, seeming to dance on the floor of his chariot, struck each of them with
three arrows, almost smiling. In a hurry and in great anger, the mighty rakshasa
killed the horses of those great men and their charioteers. Glad, he pierced
them again with well-sharpened arrows of many shapes, by the hundred and the
thousand. Having made those great archers chariotless there, the night-walker
charged them at speed, wanting to kill them.

"Seeing them hard pressed in the battle by that evil-hearted rakshasa, Arjuna's
son charged the rakshasa in the battle. Between them there was a battle like
that of Vritra and Vasava. All your men and the great chariot fighters of the
Pandavas watched it. The two mighty men met in the great battle, blazing with
anger at each other, great king, their eyes red with fury, and looked at each
other, like the fire of Time in battle. Their meeting was terrible and bitter,
like that of Shakra and Shambara in the war of the gods and asuras."

"Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "how did Alambusa fight in the battle against
Arjuna's brave son, the great chariot fighter, who was killing my men? And how
did Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, fight Rishyashringa's son? Tell me
truly how it went in the battle. And what did Dhananjaya do among my armies,
Sanjaya, or Bhima, best of the mighty, or the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, or Nakula,
Sahadeva, or Satyaki, the great chariot fighter? Tell me all this, Sanjaya, for
you are skilled."

"Then I will tell you, sir," said Sanjaya, "of the hair-raising battle between
the lord of rakshasas and Subhadra's son. I will tell of the valour that Arjuna,
Bhimasena the Pandava, Nakula and Sahadeva showed in the battle. And I will tell
of the marvellous and wonderful deeds that all your men, led by Bhishma and
Drona, did without fear.

"Alambusa, roaring a very great roar in the battle and threatening again and
again, charged the great chariot fighter Abhimanyu at speed, saying, 'Stand!
Stand!' Subhadra's son too, king, roaring like a lion again and again in the
battle, went against Rishyashringa's son, the great archer, his father's bitter
enemy. Then the man and the rakshasa met quickly in the battle in their
chariots, best of chariot fighters both, like a god and a danava. One was the
best of rakshasas, a master of magic. The other was Phalguna's son, who knew
divine weapons.

"Then Arjuna's son, great king, pierced Rishyashringa's son in the battle with
three sharp arrows, and again with five. Alambusa too, in anger, pierced
Arjuna's son in the heart with nine swift arrows at speed, as men strike a great
elephant with goads. Then the quick-acting night-walker harried Arjuna's son in
the battle with a thousand arrows, Bharata. Then Abhimanyu in anger shot ninety
sharp, straight arrows into the rakshasa's broad chest. They quickly entered his
body, splitting his vital points. With all his limbs split by them, the best of
rakshasas shone, king, like a mountain covered with flowering kimshuka trees.
Bearing those golden-feathered arrows, the mighty best of rakshasas shone like a
mountain in flames.

"Then, great king, Rishyashringa's mighty son in anger covered Arjuna's son, who
was like great Indra, with feathered arrows. The sharp arrows he loosed, like
Yama's staff, split Abhimanyu and went into the ground. In the same way the
arrows adorned with gold loosed by Arjuna's son split Alambusa and went into the
ground. In the battle Subhadra's son came up to the rakshasa and turned him away
with straight arrows, as Shakra turned Maya away in battle.

"Turned away, and being killed in battle by his enemy, the rakshasa, scorcher of
enemies, made a great magic of darkness. Then they were all taken by darkness on
the ground. They could not see Abhimanyu, nor their own men, nor the enemy in
the battle. Seeing that great darkness, terrible in form, Abhimanyu, joy of the
Kurus, brought out the very fierce sun weapon. Then the whole world became
light, lord of the earth, and he destroyed the evil-hearted rakshasa's magic.
The best of men, of great courage, in anger covered the lord of rakshasas in the
battle with straight arrows. Many other kinds of magic the rakshasa used too,
and Phalguna's son, who knew all weapons and whose spirit had no measure, held
them all back. His magic destroyed and being killed by arrows, the rakshasa left
his chariot right there and ran in great fear. When that crooked fighter, the
rakshasa, had quickly been beaten, Arjuna's son crushed your army in the battle,
like a wild lordly elephant, blind with rut, crushing a lotus pond with its
lotuses.

"Then Bhishma, son of Shantanu, seeing the army put to flight, surrounded
Subhadra's son with a great line of chariots. Making a pen round that hero, the
great chariot fighters, the sons of Dhritarashtra, many against one, cut him
hard with arrows in the battle. That hero was equal to his father in valour and
like Vasudeva in courage and strength. The best of all who bear weapons did many
kinds of deeds in the battle worthy of them both, his father and his mother's
brother.

"Then, king, Dhananjaya, killing your soldiers and unforgiving, wanting to reach
his son, came up to Bhishma in the battle. In the same way, king, your father
Devavrata reached Partha in the battle, as Svarbhanu reaches the sun. Then your
sons, lord of the people, with chariots, elephants and horses, surrounded
Bhishma in the battle and guarded him on every side. In the same way, king, the
Pandavas surrounded Dhananjaya, ready for a great battle, in their armour, bull
of the Bharatas.

"Then, king, Sharadvat's son heaped twenty-five arrows on Arjuna, who stood in
front of Bhishma. Satyaki went out to meet him and pierced him with sharp
arrows, to please the Pandava, like a tiger an elephant. Gautama too, quickly,
in anger, pierced Madhava in the heart with nine arrows feathered with heron
plumes. Then Shaineya too, the great chariot fighter, badly wounded and angry,
took up a terrible arrow that would end Gautama. As it came at speed, bright as
Shakra's thunderbolt, Drona's son, easily angered, cut it in two in his anger.
Then Shaineya left Gautama, best of chariot fighters, and charged Drona's son in
the battle, as Rahu charges the moon in the sky. Drona's son cut his bow in two,
Bharata, and then struck him with arrows when his bow was cut. He took another
bow, which killed enemies and could bear any load, and planted sixty arrows in
Drona's son's arms and chest, great king. Pierced and in pain, faint for a
moment, he sat down on the floor of his chariot, leaning on his flagstaff.

"Coming to, Drona's mighty son in anger struck the Varshneya in the battle with
an iron arrow. It split Shaineya and went into the ground, like a young snake
going into its hole in spring. Then with another broad arrow Drona's son cut
down Madhava's fine banner in the battle and roared a lion roar. Again he
covered him with terrible arrows, Bharata, great king, as a cloud covers the sun
at the end of the hot season. But Satyaki, great king, struck down that net of
arrows and quickly fell on Drona's son with nets of arrows of many kinds.
Shaineya, killer of enemy heroes, scorched Drona's son like the sun freed from a
net of clouds. The mighty Satyaki covered him again with a thousand arrows as he
rose up, and roared.

"Seeing his son swallowed like that, like the moon by Rahu, mighty Bharadvaja's
son charged Shaineya. Wanting to rescue his son, who was being scorched by the
Varshneya, king, he pierced him with a very sharp arrow in the great battle. But
Satyaki, having beaten the teacher's son, the great chariot fighter, in the
battle, pierced Drona with twenty arrows made all of iron. At that very moment
the son of Kunti with the white horses, whose spirit had no measure, the great
chariot fighter, charged Drona in anger in the battle. Then Drona and Partha met
in the great battle, great king, like Budha and Shukra in the sky."

"How did those two heroes meet in battle," said Dhritarashtra, "Drona the great
archer and Dhananjaya the Pandava? Tell me that, Sanjaya. For the Pandava was
always dear to wise Bharadvaja's son, and the teacher always dear to Partha in
battle, Sanjaya. Both chariot fighters are proud in battle, fierce as lions. How
did Bharadvaja's son and Dhananjaya meet in battle?"

"In battle Drona does not know Partha as dear to him," said Sanjaya, "nor does
Partha know his teacher, holding to the dharma of kshatriyas. Kshatriyas do not
spare each other in battle, king. They fight without limits, even with fathers
and brothers.

"In the battle, Bharata, Drona was pierced by Partha with three arrows, and he
paid no heed to the arrows loosed from Partha's bow in the battle. Again Partha
covered him with a shower of arrows in the battle, and he blazed up with anger
like a fire rising in a thicket. Then Drona soon held Arjuna back in the battle
with straight arrows, lord of kings, Bharata. Then King Duryodhana urged on
Susharma, king, to guard Drona's flank in the battle. The Trigarta king too, in
anger, drew his bow hard and covered Partha with iron-headed arrows in the
battle. The arrows loosed by those two shone in the sky, king, like swans in the
sky in autumn, great king. Those arrows reached Kunti's son and all went into
him, lord, as birds go into a sweet tree bent low with fruit.

"But Arjuna, best of chariot fighters, roared in the battle and pierced the
Trigarta king and his son with arrows. Being killed by Partha like Time at the
end of an age, they turned on Partha himself, set on death, and loosed a shower
of arrows at the Pandava's chariot. The Pandava took that shower of arrows with
a rain of arrows, lord of kings, as a mountain takes a shower of rain. There we
saw a marvel of speed in Bibhatsu's hands: alone he held back the shower of
weapons, so hard to withstand, loosed by many brave men, as the wind holds back
masses of cloud. Gods and danavas were pleased with that deed of Partha's.

"Then Partha in anger, Bharata, loosed the wind weapon at the Trigartas at the
head of the army, great king. A wind rose up, stirring the sky, bringing down
clumps of trees and killing soldiers. Then Drona, seeing that very cruel wind
weapon, loosed another terrible weapon, the mountain weapon, great king. When
Drona had loosed that weapon in the great battle, the wind died down and the
quarters became clear. Then the heroic son of Pandu made the chariot troops of
the Trigarta lose heart, turn away and lose their valour in the battle.

"Then King Duryodhana and Kripa, best of chariot fighters, Ashvatthama, Shalya
and Sudakshina of Kamboja came on. With Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti and Bahlika
with the Bahlikas, they closed off every direction to Partha with a great line
of chariots. In the same way Bhagadatta and mighty Shrutayus closed off every
direction to Bhima with the elephant division. Bhurishravas, Shala and Subala's
son quickly held back the two sons of Madri with floods of arrows of many kinds,
lord of the people. And Bhishma, with all the soldiers of Dhritarashtra's son,
came up to Yudhishthira and surrounded him on every side.

"Seeing the elephant division coming, Vrikodara, the son of Pritha, licked the
corners of his mouth like the king of beasts in the forest. Then the best of
chariot fighters took his mace in the great battle, leapt quickly down from his
chariot and terrified your army. Seeing him with his mace in his hand, the
elephant riders, set on it, surrounded Bhimasena on every side in the battle.
Having reached the middle of the elephants, the Pandava shone like the sun in
the middle of a great net of clouds. The bull of the Pandavas blew away that
elephant division with his mace, as the wind blows away a great, unbroken,
matchless net of cloud. Being killed by mighty Bhimasena, the elephants cried
out in pain in the battle, roaring like clouds. Torn in many places by the
elephants' tusks, Partha shone at the head of the battle like a flowering ashoka
tree. He seized an elephant by the tusk and made it tuskless. With that same
tusk he struck the elephant on the forehead and brought it down in the battle,
like Death with his staff in his hand. Carrying his mace smeared with blood, his
skin coloured with fat and marrow, with blood for his armlets, he was seen like
Rudra. Being killed like that, the great elephants that were left ran in every
direction, king, crushing their own army. As those great elephants ran on every
side, bull of the Bharatas, Duryodhana's whole army turned away again."

**Divergences.**

* **Bhishma's word about Shikhandi.** Duryodhana repeats it: Bhishma will not shoot at anyone "who was a woman before", and says Shikhandi was born a woman, "as told at the time of the preparations for war" (6.95.8–12; see 5.188–193).
* **Shikhandi in front.** Here Arjuna first asks Dhrishtadyumna to place Shikhandi in front of Bhishma and says "I will guard him myself" (6.95.25). The plan to kill Bhishma this way is made the next night (6.103).
* **Drona and Arjuna.** Asked how teacher and pupil could fight, Sanjaya answers that kshatriyas do not spare each other in battle, "even fathers and brothers" (6.98.4–5).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*382–\*392. \*382 has Duryodhana say "as a lion killed by a crow".

**Choices.**

* 6.95.3 *vinindya paravācyatām*: "blamed his being in another's service".
* 6.95.11 *udyoge kathitaṃ yat tat*: "what was told at the time of the preparations for war", Bhishma's own reference to his story in Book 5.
* 6.95.26 *sarvatobhadra*: "the all-sided array".
* 6.95.41 *krakacān goviṣāṇikāḥ … puṣkarān*: "saws and cow horns … pushkaras", instruments; *puṣkara* is kept as a name.
* 6.96.22, 97.11 *kārṣṇi*: "son of Krishna", that is, of Arjuna, who is also called Krishna. It is rendered "Arjuna's son".
* 6.97.23 *bhāskaram astram*: "the sun weapon".
* 6.97.57 *budhaś ca śukraś ca*: "Budha and Shukra", the planets Mercury and Venus.
* 6.98.20 *śailam astram*: "the mountain weapon".
* 6.98.34 *pārthaḥ*: Bhima, as a son of Pritha.

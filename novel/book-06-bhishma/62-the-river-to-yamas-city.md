# The River to Yama's City

"At midday, great king," said Sanjaya, "a fierce battle broke out between
Bhishma and the Somakas that brought destruction to the world. Ganga's son, the
best of chariot fighters, blew away the Pandava army with sharp arrows in
hundreds and thousands. Your father Devavrata trampled that army as herds of
cattle trample a heap of cut grain. Dhrishtadyumna, Shikhandi, Virata and
Drupada came up against Bhishma in battle and struck the great chariot fighter
with arrows. Bhishma pierced Dhrishtadyumna and Virata with three arrows each,
and sent an iron arrow at Drupada, Bharata. Pierced by Bhishma, destroyer of
enemies, the great archers grew angry in battle, king, like snakes trodden on.

"Shikhandi pierced the grandfather of the Bharatas, but the unfalling one,
thinking of him in his mind as a woman, did not strike at him. Dhrishtadyumna in
battle, blazing with anger like fire, fixed three arrows in the grandfather's
arms and chest. Drupada pierced Bhishma with twenty-five arrows, Virata with ten
and Shikhandi with twenty-five. Badly pierced in battle by those great-souled
men, great king, Bhishma looked like a red ashoka in spring, dappled with
flowers. Ganga's son pierced them back with three straight arrows each, and cut
Drupada's bow with a broad arrow, sir. He took up another bow and pierced
Bhishma with five arrows and his charioteer with three very sharp ones at the
head of the battle.

"Then Bhima, great king, the five sons of Draupadi, the five Kekaya brothers and
Satyaki the Satvata ran at Ganga's son, wanting Yudhishthira's good, to protect
the Panchala and those led by Dhrishtadyumna in battle. So too all your men,
ready to guard Bhishma, went out against the Pandava army with their troops,
lord of men. There was a very great and crowded battle between yours and theirs,
of men, horses, chariots and elephants, swelling the kingdom of Yama. Chariot
fighter met chariot fighter and sent him to Yama's home. So too they met others,
men, elephants, horses and horsemen. They sent them to the other world here and
there with arrows with bent joints and with terrible weapons of many kinds, lord
of the people.

"Chariots without their chariot fighters, their charioteers killed, their horses
bolting, went in all directions in the battle. Crushing many men and horses in
battle, king, they were seen rushing like the wind, like cities of the
gandharvas. There were chariot fighters without their chariots, in armour, full
of splendour, all wearing earrings and turbans and adorned with golden ornaments
and armlets. They were like the sons of gods in beauty and like Shakra in
bravery in battle. They surpassed Vaishravana in wealth and Brihaspati in
policy, brave lords of all the world. Yet they were seen here and there, lord of
the people, running like common men. Elephants without their fine riders, best
of men, crushed their own ranks and ran at every sound. Their armour, yak-tail
fans, parasols, flags, girths, goads, bells and lances were torn apart. They
were seen running in the ten directions, like mountains or clouds, roaring like
rain clouds rising. So too, lord of the people, we saw elephant riders without
their elephants running in the crowd, yours and theirs. I saw horses from many
countries, adorned with gold, rushing like the wind in hundreds and thousands.
We saw horsemen with their horses killed, swords in hand, running on every side
and being driven off in battle. Elephant met fleeing elephant in the great
battle and went on, crushing foot soldiers and horses with force. So too an
elephant crushed chariots in battle, king, and a chariot, meeting a foot soldier
or a horse, crushed horses and men in battle, king. So they crushed one another
in many ways, king.

"While that fierce and very dangerous battle went on, a terrible river began to
flow, its waves blood and entrails. Its rafts were heaps of bones, its moss and
grass hair. Chariots were its pools, arrows its whirlpools, horses its fish, and
it was hard to approach. It was strewn with heads for stones and crowded with
elephants for crocodiles. It was rich in foam of armour and turbans, bows were
its islands, swords its tortoises. It was rich in flags and banners for trees,
carried away banks of men, was crowded with flocks of flesh-eaters, and swelled
the kingdom of Yama. Many brave kshatriyas crossed that river in the great
battle, king, on rafts of horses, elephants and chariots, giving up fear. It
carried away the fearful, overcome with faintness, as the Vaitarani carries the
dead to the city of the king of the dead.

"Seeing that great slaughter, the kshatriyas there cried out, 'Through
Duryodhana's offence the Kauravas are going to destruction. Why did the
evil-souled son of Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, deluded by greed, feel
hatred for the sons of Pandu, who have good qualities?' Many such words were
heard there, Bharata, joined with praise of the Pandavas, very harsh to your
sons. Hearing those words spoken by all the warriors, your son Duryodhana, who
has wronged the whole world, said to Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and Shalya, Bharata,
'Fight without pride! Why do you delay?' Then the battle of the Kurus with the
Pandavas went on, king, the very terrible slaughter brought about by the dice
game. Son of Vichitravirya, see this fruit of that which you did not check long
ago, though great souls held you back. For neither the sons of Pandu, king, with
their armies and followers, nor the Kauravas guard their lives in battle, lord
of the people. For this reason a terrible destruction of people goes on, tiger
among men, whether through fate or through your bad policy, king.

---

"Arjuna, tiger among men, sent the kings led by Susharma to the house of the
king of the dead with sharp arrows. Susharma too pierced Partha with arrows in
battle, Vasudeva with seventy and Partha again with nine. Warding them off with
a flood of arrows, the great chariot fighter, Shakra's son, sent Susharma's
warriors in battle to Yama's home. Being killed by Partha like Time at the end
of an age, the great chariot fighters ran in battle, king, as fear arose. Some
left their horses, sir, some their chariots, others their elephants, and ran in
the ten directions. Others, goading their horses, elephants and chariots, ran
from the battle in the greatest haste, lord of the people. Foot soldiers too
threw down their weapons in the great battle and ran heedless this way and that,
Bharata. Though Susharma of Trigarta held them back many times, and other
excellent kings too, they did not stand firm in battle.

"Your son Duryodhana saw that army running. He put Bhishma in front, with the
whole army behind him, and with all his great effort ran at Dhananjaya to save
the life of the lord of the Trigartas, lord of the people. He alone stood firm
in battle with all his brothers, scattering arrows of many kinds. The rest of
the men had fled. So too the Pandavas, king, in armour, with all their effort,
went for Phalguna's sake to where Bhishma stood. Though they knew the terrible
bravery of Gandiva's bowman in battle, they went to Bhishma on every side, their
spirits raised by cries. Then the brave one with the palm banner covered the
Pandava army in battle with arrows with bent joints. Then all the Kurus, united,
fought the Pandavas, great king, when the sun had reached midday.

"Satyaki pierced Kritavarma with five iron arrows and stood firm in battle, the
brave one, scattering arrows in thousands. So too King Drupada pierced Drona
with sharp arrows, and pierced him again with seventy and his charioteer with
seven. Bhimasena pierced King Bahlika, the great-grandfather, and roared a great
roar, like a tiger in the forest. Arjuna's son, pierced by Chitrasena with many
swift arrows, pierced Chitrasena hard in the heart with three. Meeting in
battle, the two great ones shone, king, like Mercury and Saturn, very terrible,
in the sky. Killing his four horses and his charioteer with nine arrows,
Subhadra's son, killer of enemy heroes, roared a strong roar. Leaping quickly
from the chariot with its horses killed, the great chariot fighter quickly
climbed onto Durmukha's chariot, lord of the people. Drona pierced Drupada with
arrows with bent joints and, hurrying, the mighty one pierced his charioteer.
Hard pressed at the head of the army, King Drupada went away with swift horses,
remembering the old enmity. In a moment, as it seemed, Bhimasena left King
Bahlika without horses, charioteer or chariot while the whole army watched. In
alarm, great king, in the greatest danger, Bahlika, best of men, the great
chariot fighter, leapt down from the carriage and quickly climbed onto
Lakshmana's chariot.

"Satyaki, having held off Kritavarma, the great chariot fighter, came up against
the grandfather with arrows of many kinds, king. He pierced the Bharata with
sixty sharp feathered arrows and seemed to dance on the chariot floor, shaking
his great bow. Then the grandfather hurled at him a great iron spear, bright
with gold, of great speed, beautiful as a serpent maiden. As it came at once,
like death, very sharp, the glorious Varshneya destroyed it with lightness. Not
reaching the Varshneya, the very cruel spear fell to the ground like a great
meteor that has lost its light. Then the Varshneya, king, took his own spear,
terrible to see, and hurled it with force at the grandfather's chariot. Driven
by the force of the Varshneya's arm in the great battle, it rushed at him with
speed, like the night of Time at a man. As it came at him at once, Bharata, he
cut it in two with two very sharp razor-headed arrows, and it was scattered on
the ground. Having cut the spear, Ganga's son, destroyer of enemies, in anger,
laughing, struck Satyaki in the chest with nine arrows. Then the Pandavas, elder
brother of Pandu, with chariots, elephants and horses, surrounded Bhishma in
battle to protect Madhava. Then a tumultuous and hair-raising battle began
between the Pandavas and the Kurus, each wanting victory."

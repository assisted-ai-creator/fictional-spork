# Shankha Falls

"So the battle began," said Sanjaya. "Susharma had turned back, and the
great-souled Pandava had broken the heroes. Your ocean-like army was quickly
thrown into turmoil, and Ganga's son hurried out to win victory. Duryodhana saw
Partha's courage in battle, king. He hurried up and spoke to all those kings in
the middle of the whole army, and above all to the brave, very strong Susharma
at their head, greatly cheering them. 'Here is Bhishma son of Shantanu, best of
the Kurus, wanting to fight Dhananjaya with his whole self, giving up his own
life. As the Bharata goes against the enemy army with the whole host, all of
you, ready in battle, guard the grandfather.'

"'Very well,' they said, and all the ranks of the kings went with the
grandfather, great king. Then Bhishma son of Shantanu went quickly and met the
very strong Arjuna, the Bharata, coming in battle. He shone in his great
chariot, yoked to great white horses, with its terrible monkey banner, rumbling
like a great cloud. When Dhananjaya, the diademed one, came up in battle, a
tumultuous cry of fear arose from all the armies at the sight of him. Seeing
Krishna, reins in hand, like a second sun at midday, they could not look at him
in battle. In the same way the Pandavas could not look at Bhishma son of
Shantanu, with his white horses and his white bow, like the white planet risen.
He was surrounded on every side by the great-souled Trigartas, his brothers,
your sons and other great chariot fighters.

"Bharadvaja's son pierced the Matsya in battle with a feathered arrow. With one
arrow he cut his banner in the fight, and his bow with one. Throwing away the
cut bow, Virata, lord of an army, took up at speed another bow, firm and able to
bear the load, and arrows like venomous snakes, like blazing serpents. He
pierced Drona with three, his horses with four, his banner with one and his
charioteer with five. He pierced his bow with one arrow, and the bull among
brahmins grew angry. Drona killed his horses with eight arrows with bent joints,
best of the Bharatas, and his charioteer with one feathered arrow. Leaping down
from the chariot with its horses and charioteer killed, the best of chariot
fighters quickly climbed onto Shankha's chariot.

"Then father and son, standing on the chariot, held off Bharadvaja's son by
force with a great shower of arrows. Then Bharadvaja's son in anger quickly
loosed at Shankha in battle an arrow like a venomous snake, lord of the people.
It split his heart, drank his blood in battle, and went into the earth, its
sheen wet with blood. Struck by Bharadvaja's son's arrow, he fell at once from
the chariot, letting go of his bow and arrows, right beside his father. Seeing
his own son killed, Virata fled in fear, leaving Drona in battle like Death with
his mouth open. Then Bharadvaja's son quickly tore apart the great Pandava army
in battle, in hundreds and thousands.

"Shikhandi too, great king, met Drona's son in battle and struck him between the
brows with three swift iron arrows. With the three set in his forehead, the
tiger among men looked like Meru with three golden peaks raised high. Then
Ashvatthama grew angry. In half a blink he aimed many arrows and brought down
Shikhandi's charioteer, banner, horses and weapons in battle, king. Leaping down
from the chariot with its horses killed, the best of chariot fighters took up a
sharp sword and a clean shield. Shikhandi, scorcher of enemies, moved about in
anger like a hawk. As he moved about in battle with his sword, great king,
Drona's son could find no opening, and it was a wonder. Then Drona's son, very
angry, sent many thousands of arrows in battle, bull of the Bharatas. The best
of the strong cut down that very cruel rain of arrows in battle with his
sharp-edged sword. Then Drona's son cut his clean, lovely shield with its
hundred moons. He broke his sword in pieces in battle, king, and pierced him
with many sharp feathered arrows. Shikhandi whirled the sword that his arrows
had broken and quickly hurled it like a blazing snake. As it came at him, bright
as the fire of Time, Drona's son cut it in battle, showing the lightness of his
hands, and pierced Shikhandi with many iron arrows. Hard struck by sharp arrows,
king, Shikhandi quickly climbed onto the great-souled Madhava's chariot.

"Then Satyaki in anger, the strong one, pierced the cruel, strong rakshasa
Alambusa with terrible arrows in battle. The lord of the rakshasas cut his bow
with a crescent arrow, Bharata, and pierced him with arrows in battle. He made a
rakshasa illusion and covered him with showers of arrows. There we saw the
Shaineya's wonderful courage: he was not flustered in battle, though struck by
sharp arrows. The Varshneya brought forth Indra's weapon, Bharata, which the
glorious Madhava had received from Vijaya. That weapon burned the rakshasa
illusion to ashes, and he covered Alambusa on every side with terrible arrows,
as a cloud covers a mountain with torrents of water in the rains. Pressed like
that by the great-souled Madhava, the rakshasa fled in fear, leaving Satyaki in
battle. Having conquered the lord of the rakshasas, whom even Maghavat could not
conquer in battle, the Shaineya roared while your warriors watched. Satyaki, of
true courage, killed your men too with many sharp arrows, and they ran,
tormented by fear.

"At that very time, great king, the strong son of Drupada, Dhrishtadyumna,
covered your son, the lord of men, with arrows with bent joints in battle.
Covered with arrows by Dhrishtadyumna, Bharata, your son, the lord of men, did
not falter, king of kings. He quickly pierced Dhrishtadyumna in battle with
sixty arrows and thirty, and it was a wonder. The commander of the army, sir, in
anger cut his bow, and the great chariot fighter quickly killed his four horses
and quickly pierced him with seven very sharp arrows. The strong-armed, strong
one leapt down from the chariot with its horses killed and ran on foot at
Prishata's grandson with his sword raised. The very strong Shakuni, eager for
the king, came up and took the king of the whole world up onto his own chariot.
Then Prishata's grandson, killer of enemy heroes, having beaten the king, killed
your army as the wielder of the thunderbolt kills the asuras.

"Kritavarma struck the great chariot fighter Bhima with arrows in battle and
covered him as a great cloud covers the sun. Then Bhimasena, scorcher of
enemies, laughed in battle and in anger sent arrows at Kritavarma. Struck by
them, the atiratha, the Satvata skilled in weapons, did not tremble, great king,
and struck Bhima with sharp arrows. The very strong Bhimasena killed his four
horses and brought down his charioteer and his well-made banner. The killer of
enemy heroes covered him with arrows of many kinds, and with every limb cut he
looked like a porcupine. From his chariot with its horses killed he quickly went
to the chariot of Vrishaka, your brother-in-law, great king, while your son
watched. Bhimasena too in anger ran at your army and killed it in his anger,
like Death with his staff in his hand."

---

Dhritarashtra said, "I have heard you tell of many wonderful single combats here
between the Pandavas and my men, Sanjaya. But you never tell me that any of mine
is glad, Sanjaya. You always tell me that Pandu's sons are glad and unbroken.
You tell me, charioteer, that my men in battle are beaten, dispirited, their
strength gone. This is fate, no doubt."

"Your men strive in battle as hard as they can and with all their spirit," said
Sanjaya, "showing the highest manhood with their strength, bull among men. The
water of the Ganga, river of the gods, is sweet, yet it turns salty from mixing
with the great ocean. In the same way, king, the manhood of your great-souled
men comes to nothing in battle when it meets the heroic sons of Pandu. You
should not find fault with the Kauravas, best of the Kurus, when they strive as
hard as they can and do deeds that are hard to do. Through your offence, and
your son's, lord of the people, this very great and terrible destruction of the
earth has come, swelling the kingdom of Yama. It arose from your own fault, and
you should not grieve, king. For the kings do not guard all their wealth, nor
even their lives. Wanting the worlds of those who have done good, the lords of
the earth plunge into the army and fight, always intent on heaven.

"In the morning, great king, the destruction of people went on. Hear it from me
with a single mind, like the battle of the gods and asuras.

"The two great-souled princes of Avanti, very strong great archers, fierce in
battle, saw Iravan and met him. A tumultuous battle broke out between them that
made the hair stand on end. Iravan in great anger quickly pierced the two
brothers, godlike in form, with sharp arrows with bent joints. The two,
wonderful fighters, pierced him back in battle. As they fought, king, trying to
destroy the enemy and wanting to pay back blow for blow, no difference could be
seen between them. Then, king, with four arrows Iravan took Anuvinda's four
horses to Yama's home. With two very sharp broad arrows he cut his bow and
banner in battle, sir, and it was a wonder. Leaving his chariot, Anuvinda stood
on Vinda's chariot and took up a new bow, a fine one, able to bear the load.
Standing on one chariot, the two heroes of Avanti, the best of chariot fighters,
quickly loosed arrows at the great-souled Iravan. The very swift gold-adorned
arrows they loosed reached the path of the sun and covered the sky. Then Iravan
in anger rained a shower of arrows on the two great chariot fighters and brought
down their charioteer. When the charioteer had fallen lifeless to the ground,
the chariot ran off in all directions, its horses bolting. Having beaten them
both, great king, the son of the snake king's daughter, making his manhood
known, quickly blew away your army. Being killed in battle, the great army of
Dhritarashtra's sons went through convulsions of many kinds, like a man who has
drunk poison.

"The very strong lord of the rakshasas, Hidimba's son, ran at Bhagadatta in a
chariot the colour of the sun, with its banner. Then the king of Pragjyotisha
mounted on his lordly elephant, like the wielder of the thunderbolt long ago in
the battle over Taraka. The gods, gandharvas and seers who had gathered there
could see no difference between Hidimba's son and Bhagadatta. As Shakra, lord of
the gods, terrified the danavas, so he terrified the Pandavas in battle, king.
Driven off by him on every side, the Pandavas found no protector in their own
ranks, Bharata. There we saw Bhima's son standing on his chariot, Bharata. The
other great chariot fighters lost heart and ran. When the Pandava troops turned
back again, Bharata, there was a terrible groaning in your armies in battle.

"Then, king, Ghatotkacha covered Bhagadatta with arrows in the great battle, as
a cloud covers Mount Meru. Striking down the arrows loosed from the rakshasa's
bow, the king quickly struck Bhima's son in all his vitals in battle. Struck by
many arrows with bent joints, the lord of the rakshasas did not falter, like a
mountain being split. The king of Pragjyotisha in anger sent fourteen lances at
him in battle, and the rakshasa cut them. Having cut those lances with sharp
arrows, the strong-armed one pierced Bhagadatta with seventy heron-feathered
arrows. Then, king, the lord of Pragjyotisha, as if laughing, Bharata, brought
down his four horses in battle with arrows. Standing on his chariot with its
horses killed, the mighty lord of the rakshasas hurled a spear at speed at the
Pragjyotisha's elephant. As that very swift gold-handled spear came at him, the
king cut it in three, and it was scattered on the ground. Seeing the spear
destroyed, Hidimba's son fled in fear, as Namuchi, the best of the daityas, fled
from Indra's battle long ago. He had beaten that brave, bold hero of famous
manhood, whom even Yama and Varuna could not conquer in battle, king. Then he
crushed the Pandava army with his elephant, as a wild elephant crushes a lotus
pond as it moves about.

"The lord of the Madras met the twins in battle and covered his sister's sons,
the joy of Pandu, with floods of arrows. Sahadeva, seeing his uncle engaged in
battle, held him off with a flood of arrows, as a cloud holds off the sun.
Covered with a flood of arrows, he became all the gladder, and they too felt a
matchless affection for him because of their mother. Then the great chariot
fighter laughed in battle and with four fine arrows sent Nakula's four horses to
Yama's home, king. Leaping quickly from the chariot with its horses killed, the
great chariot fighter climbed onto his glorious brother's carriage. Standing on
one chariot, the two brave men drew their firm bows and in anger covered the
Madra king's chariot in a moment. Though covered by his sister's sons with many
arrows with bent joints, the tiger among men did not tremble, like a mountain.
As if laughing, he struck down that shower of arrows.

"Then the mighty Sahadeva in anger raised an arrow and, looking at the Madra
king, sent it at him, Bharata. Sent by him, the arrow, swift as Garuda, split
the Madra king and fell to the ground. Badly wounded and in pain, the great
chariot fighter sat down on the chariot floor, great king, and fainted. Seeing
him fallen senseless in battle, hard pressed by the twins, his charioteer
carried him off on the chariot. Seeing the Madra lord's chariot turned away, all
the sons of Dhritarashtra lost heart and thought, 'This is the end.' Having
beaten their uncle in battle, the great chariot fighters, Madri's two sons, blew
their conches gladly and roared lion roars. Glad, lord of the people, they ran
at your army, king, as the two immortals Indra and Upendra ran at the army of
the daityas."

# Arjuna's Grief

"Hearing that his son Iravan had been killed," said Sanjaya, "Dhananjaya was
overcome with great grief and sighed like a snake. In the battle, king, he said
this to Vasudeva. 'Surely the very wise Vidura, great-minded, foresaw long ago
this terrible destruction of the Kurus and the Pandavas, and so he held back
Dhritarashtra, lord of the people. Many heroes who should not have been killed,
Killer of Madhu, have been killed in battle by the Kauravas, and they have been
killed by us. For the sake of wealth, best of men, a despicable deed is done.
Shame on wealth, for whose sake kin are destroyed like this! For the man without
wealth, death is better than wealth won by killing kin. What will we gain,
Krishna, by killing our gathered kin? Through Duryodhana's offence, and Shakuni
son of Subala's, and through Karna's bad counsel, kshatriyas are going to their
deaths. Now I understand, Killer of Madhu, that the king did well, strong-armed
one, when he begged Suyodhana for half the kingdom or five villages, and the
evil-minded one would not give them. Seeing the brave kshatriyas lying on the
ground, I blame myself bitterly. Shame on the life of a kshatriya! These
kshatriyas would think me unable to fight. Fighting with these kin is not to my
liking, Killer of Madhu. Drive the horses quickly at the army of Dhritarashtra's
son. I will cross this ocean of battle, with its far shore, with my two arms.
This is no time at all for weakness, Madhava.'

"So addressed by Partha, Keshava, killer of enemy heroes, drove those pale
horses, swift as the wind. Then there was a great sound from your army, Bharata,
like the ocean at the full or new moon when its force is stirred by the wind. In
the afternoon, great king, a battle broke out of Bhishma with the Pandavas,
roaring like Parjanya.

"Then, king, your sons ran at Bhimasena, surrounding Drona in battle as the
Vasus surround Vasava. Then Bhishma son of Shantanu, Kripa the best of chariot
fighters, Bhagadatta and Susharma ran at Dhananjaya. The son of Hridika and
Bahlika ran at Satyaki. The king of the Ambashthas held off Abhimanyu. The rest,
great king, met the rest of the great chariot fighters. Then a battle broke out,
terrible in form and frightening.

"Seeing your sons, lord of the people, Bhimasena blazed with anger in battle,
like the carrier of oblations fed with oblations. Your sons covered the Kaunteya
with arrows, great king, as clouds cover a mountain in the rains. Covered in
many ways by your sons, lord of the people, the hero licked the corners of his
mouth like a proud tiger. Then, lord of the earth, Bhima brought down
Vyudhoraska with a very sharp razor-headed arrow, and he lost his life. With
another yellow, sharp broad arrow he brought down Kundalin, as a lion brings
down a small deer. Then he took up sharp, yellow arrows and, hurrying, sent them
at seven of your sons, sir. Sent by firm-bowed Bhimasena, those arrows brought
down your sons, very great chariot fighters, from their chariots: Anadhrishti,
Kundabheda, Vairata, Dirghalochana, Dirghabahu, Subahu and Kanakadhvaja. As they
fell, the heroes shone, bull of the Bharatas, like flowering mango trees falling
in spring. Then your remaining sons fled, lord of the people, thinking the very
strong Bhimasena was Death.

"Drona covered the hero on every side with arrows in battle, as he burned up
your sons, as torrents of water cover a mountain. There we saw the Kaunteya's
wonderful manhood, for though Drona held him back, he killed your sons. As a
bull bears the rain falling from the sky, so Bhima bore the shower of arrows
loosed by Drona. And Wolf-belly did a wonderful thing there, great king, for he
killed your sons in battle and held off Drona at the same time. Arjuna's elder
brother played among your heroic sons, great king, like a very strong tiger
moving among deer. As a wolf among cattle would drive the cattle off, so
Wolf-belly drove off your sons in battle.

"Ganga's son, Bhagadatta and the great chariot fighter Gautama held off the
Pandava Arjuna, impetuous in battle. Warding off their weapons with his weapons,
the atiratha sent the heroes of your armies to death in battle. Abhimanyu with
his arrows left the world-famous king of the Ambashthas, the best of chariot
fighters, without a chariot. Without his chariot, being struck by the glorious
son of Subhadra, the lord of men leapt quickly from his chariot in shame. He
hurled his sword in battle at the great-souled son of Subhadra and climbed onto
the great-souled son of Hridika's chariot. Subhadra's son, killer of enemy
heroes, skilled in the ways of battle, dodged the sword with lightness as it
came. Seeing the sword dodged by Subhadra's son in battle, a cry of 'Well done!
Well done!' rose from the troops, lord of the people.

"Others, led by Dhrishtadyumna, fought your army. In the same way all your men
fought the Pandava army. There was a great outcry there, yours and theirs,
Bharata, as they killed one another hard and did deeds that are hard to do.
Brave men seized one another by the hair in battle, sir, and fought with nails
and teeth, with fists and knees, with arms and palms and with well-sharpened
swords. Finding an opening, they sent one another to Yama's home. Father killed
son and son killed father in battle. The men there fought with their purposes
thrown into confusion.

"On the battlefield, Bharata, lay the fine bows of the killed, with golden
backs, thrown aside, and costly quivers. Sharp, oil-washed arrows with golden
and silver feathers shone like snakes that have cast their skins. There were
swords with ivory hilts adorned with gold, and the shields of bowmen thrown
aside, with golden backs. There were spears worked with gold, axes adorned with
gold, lances made of gold and darts shining with gold, sir. Heavy pestles lay
cut off and fallen, and iron bars, axes and slings. There were fallen javelins,
bright and adorned with gold, saddle-cloths of many shapes, and yak-tail fans.
Men lay fallen, having let go of weapons of many kinds. Great chariot fighters,
lifeless, looked as if alive. Men lay on the ground with limbs crushed by clubs
and heads split by pestles, trampled by elephants, horses and chariots. The
earth, king, covered on every side with the bodies of horses, men and elephants,
looked as if covered with mountains.

"The earth was strewn with spears, swords, arrows and lances fallen in battle.
Swords, axes, spears, iron lances and hatchets lay there, with iron bars, slings
and shataghnis, and bodies split by weapons. The earth, destroyer of enemies,
shone covered with the lifeless, some silent, some faintly moaning, drenched in
floods of blood. The earth shone, Bharata, with arms in finger guards and
armlets, smeared with sandal paste. There were the cut thighs of the swift, like
elephants' trunks. There were the fallen heads of men with eyes like bulls',
with earrings and crest jewels bound in place. With golden armour smeared with
blood and scattered about, the ground shone brightly as if with fires whose
flames had died. Quivers were thrown aside and bows had fallen, and
gold-feathered arrows lay scattered on every side. There were many broken
chariots hung with nets of little bells, and killed horses strewn about, their
tongues hanging out and bloody. There were chariot bottoms, flags, quivers and
banners, and the great white conches of heroes scattered about. Elephants lay
with their trunks limp. So the earth shone like a young woman adorned with
ornaments of many kinds.

"With other elephants there, deeply hurt, with lances in them, letting out sound
and spray from their trunks again and again, that battlefield looked like
mountains being blown upon. There were blankets of many colours and elephants'
coverings. Fine goads with handles of beryl had fallen, and the bells of lordly
elephants lay fallen on every side. There were bright saddle-cloths torn apart,
deerskin rugs, bright neck chains and golden girths, fittings cut in many ways,
and lances with kampanas. There were horses' breastplates covered in gold and
tawny with dust, and the cut arms of horsemen fallen with their armlets. There
were clean, sharp spears and clean swords, turbans cut and thrown here and
there, and bright crescent arrows adorned with gold. There were trampled horse
blankets and deerskin rugs, the bright, costly crest jewels of kings, and
parasols and yak-tail fans thrown aside. There were the faces of heroes bright
as lotuses or the moon, with fine earrings and neatly trimmed beards, and
earrings thrown aside shining with gold. With all these, great king, the earth
was like the sky dappled with planets and stars.

"So these two great armies, yours and theirs, Bharata, were crushed there,
coming up against each other in battle. When they were tired, broken and
crushed, Bharata, a terrible night came on, and then we could not see the
battle. Then the Kurus and the Pandavas made the withdrawal of their troops, as
the terrible, fierce and very cruel start of night came on. Having withdrawn
together, the Kurus and the Pandavas went to their own camps at the proper time
and rested there."

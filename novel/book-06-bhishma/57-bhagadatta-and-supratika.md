# Bhagadatta and Supratika

"In that great uproar," said Sanjaya, "King Duryodhana went up to Ganga's son,
greeted him humbly, and began to tell him all that had happened, Ghatotkacha's
victory and his own defeat. The unassailable one told it, sighing again and
again. Then, king, he said to Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, 'Relying on
you, lord, as the enemy rely on Vasudeva, I began this terrible war with the
Pandavas. These eleven famous akshauhinis of mine stand at your command, with
me, scorcher of enemies. Yet I have been beaten in battle by the Pandavas led by
Bhimasena, relying on Ghatotkacha, tiger of the Bharatas. That burns my limbs as
fire burns a dry tree. So, fortunate one, scorcher of enemies, I wish by your
grace to kill that outcast of a rakshasa myself, grandfather, relying on you,
the unassailable. You should do that for me.'

"Hearing these words of the king, best of the Bharatas, Bhishma son of Shantanu
said this to Duryodhana. 'Hear my words, king, what I will tell you, Kaurava, of
how you should act, great king, scorcher of enemies. You must guard yourself in
battle, son, in every situation, tamer of enemies. You should always fight the
King of Dharma, faultless one, or Arjuna, or the twins, or Bhimasena again.
Putting the dharma of kings first, a king goes against a king. I, Drona, Kripa,
Drona's son, Kritavarma the Satvata, Shalya, Somadatta's son, the great chariot
fighter Vikarna and your brave brothers led by Duhshasana will fight that very
strong rakshasa for your sake. Or if your heart is set on that terrible lord of
the rakshasas, let King Bhagadatta here, like Purandara in battle, go and fight
the evil-minded one.'

"Having said so much to the king, Bhishma, skilled in speech, spoke to
Bhagadatta in front of the lord of kings. 'Go quickly, great king, against
Hidimba's son, mad for battle. Ready in battle, hold off the rakshasa of cruel
deeds while all the bowmen watch, as Indra held off Taraka long ago. You have
divine weapons and courage, scorcher of enemies, and long ago you met many
asuras in battle. You are his match in the great battle, tiger among kings.
Surrounded by your own force, king, kill the bull among rakshasas.'

"Hearing these words of Bhishma, the commander of the army, he went out quickly
with a lion roar to face the enemy. The great chariot fighters of the Pandavas
saw him come on, roaring like a cloud. They went out in anger against him:
Bhimasena, Abhimanyu, the rakshasa Ghatotkacha, the sons of Draupadi,
Satyadhriti and Kshatradeva, sir, the Chedi king, Vasudana and the lord of the
Dasharnas. Bhagadatta too ran at them on Supratika. Then a terrible and
frightening battle broke out between the Pandavas and Bhagadatta, swelling the
kingdom of Yama.

"Arrows of terrible speed, very sharp, loosed by chariot fighters, fell on
elephants and chariots, great king. Great elephants in rut, trained by their
riders, came at one another and fell on one another without fear. Blind with
rut, roused with fury, they came together in the great battle and split one
another with the points of their tusks, their tusks like pestles. Horses with
whisk headbands, ridden by men with spears in their hands, urged on by their
riders, quickly fell on one another. Foot soldiers, struck with spears and
lances by floods of foot soldiers, fell to the ground in hundreds and thousands.
Chariot fighters, king, killed heroes in battle with barbed and tubular arrows
and roared lion roars.

"While that hair-raising battle went on, the great archer Bhagadatta ran at
Bhimasena on his elephant in rut, streaming rut in seven places like a mountain
streaming water on every side. Seated on the head of Supratika, he scattered
thousands of arrows, faultless one, as Maghavat on Airavata scatters torrents of
rain. The king struck Bhima with torrents of arrows, as a cloud in the rains
strikes a mountain with torrents of water. But Bhimasena in anger, the great
archer, killed more than a hundred of his foot guards with showers of arrows.
Seeing them killed, the mighty Bhagadatta in anger drove his lordly elephant at
Bhimasena's chariot. Sent by him like an arrow driven by the bowstring, the
elephant ran at speed at Bhimasena, tamer of enemies.

"The great chariot fighters of the Pandavas saw it come. Led by Bhimasena, they
ran at speed, all of them angry: the Kekayas, Abhimanyu and all the sons of
Draupadi, the brave lord of the Dasharnas and Kshatradeva, sir, and Chitraketu
the Chedi king. Showing supreme divine weapons, the very strong ones in anger
surrounded that one elephant on every side. Pierced by many arrows, the great
elephant shone, gushing blood, like the king of mountains bright with minerals.
The lord of the Dasharnas too, mounted on an elephant like a mountain, ran at
Bhagadatta's elephant. But Supratika, lord of elephants, held off that elephant
as it came at him in battle, as the shore holds off the sea, the home of sea
monsters. Seeing the lordly elephant of the great-souled Dasharna king held off,
the Pandava troops cried out in praise, 'Well done! Well done!'

"Then the Pragjyotisha in anger sent fourteen lances at that elephant's face,
best of kings. They split its gold-adorned armour and face guard and quickly
went in, like snakes into an anthill. Deeply pierced and in pain, best of the
Bharatas, the elephant quickly lost its rut and turned back at speed. It ran off
at speed, trumpeting a fearful sound, crushing its own army with force as the
wind crushes trees.

"When that elephant was beaten, the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas
roared a loud lion roar and stood ready for battle. Then, putting Bhima in
front, they ran at Bhagadatta, scattering arrows and weapons of many kinds.
Hearing the terrible roar of those angry, impatient men as they came, the great
archer Bhagadatta, his fear gone in his anger, drove his own elephant on. Urged
by the goad and the toe, that best of elephants became at once like the fire of
dissolution in battle. In great anger it crushed crowds of chariots, elephants,
horses with their riders and foot soldiers in hundreds and thousands, running
here and there in battle, king. Churned up by it, that great army of the
Pandavas shrank, great king, like a hide laid in the fire.

"Seeing his own army broken by the wise Bhagadatta, Ghatotkacha in anger ran at
Bhagadatta. He took on a frightening form, king, a monstrous man with a blazing
mouth and blazing eyes, as if burning with rage. The very strong one took up a
great trident that could split even mountains. Wanting to kill the elephant, he
hurled it at once, surrounded on every side with sparks and flames. Seeing it
come at once, wreathed in flames in battle, the king shot a bright, sharp
crescent arrow and with that swift arrow cut the very great trident. The
gold-adorned trident fell, cut in two, like a great thunderbolt hurled by Shakra
and dropping from the sky. Seeing the trident fallen, cut in two, the king took
up a great gold-handled spear like a flame of fire. He hurled it at the rakshasa
and said, 'Stand! Stand!' Seeing it come like a thunderbolt in the sky, the
rakshasa leapt up quickly, caught it and roared. Hurrying, he set it on his knee
and broke it, Bharata, while the lord of kings watched, and it was a wonder.
Seeing that deed done by the very strong rakshasa, the gods in heaven with the
gandharvas and the sages were amazed. The Pandava great archers led by Bhimasena
made the earth resound with the cry, 'Well done! Well done!'

"Hearing that great roar of the glad, great-souled men, the mighty great archer
Bhagadatta could not bear it. Drawing his great bow, which sounded like Indra's
thunderbolt, he ran at speed at the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas,
loosing clean, sharp iron arrows bright as fire. He pierced Bhima with one, the
rakshasa with nine arrows, Abhimanyu with three and the Kekayas with five. With
a gold-feathered arrow drawn to the full he split Kshatradeva's right arm in
battle, and his fine bow fell at once with its arrow. He struck the five sons of
Draupadi with five arrows, and in anger he killed Bhimasena's horses. With three
arrows he cut his lion banner, and with three other feathered arrows he split
his charioteer. Vishoka was deeply pierced and in pain from Bhagadatta in
battle, best of the Bharatas. He sat down on the chariot floor. Then Bhima,
great king, the best of chariot fighters, without a chariot, took up his club
and leapt at speed from the great chariot. Seeing him with his club raised, like
a mountain with its peak, a terrible fear came over your men, Bharata.

"At that very time, great king, the Pandava with Krishna as his charioteer came
up, killing enemies in thousands. He came to where those two tigers among men,
father and son, were engaged with the Pragjyotisha: Bhimasena and Ghatotkacha,
scorchers of enemies. Seeing the great chariot fighters fighting, king, the
Pandava hurried there, best of the Bharatas, scattering arrows. Then King
Duryodhana, the great chariot fighter, hurried and quickly urged on his army,
crowded with chariots, elephants and horses. As that great army of the Kauravas
came on at once, the Pandava with white horses ran at it at speed. Bhagadatta
too, with that elephant, Bharata, crushing the Pandava army in battle, ran at
Yudhishthira. Then there was a tumultuous battle of Bhagadatta, sir, with the
Panchalas, the Srinjayas and the Kekayas, their weapons raised. And in the
battle Bhimasena told Keshava and Arjuna both the whole story of how Iravan had
been killed."

# Like Pearls on a Thread

"'Hear, Partha,' said the Blessed One, 'how, with your mind clinging to me,
practising yoga, taking refuge in me, you will know me entirely, without doubt.
I will tell you, without leaving anything out, this knowledge with discernment.
When you know it, nothing else remains here to be known. Among thousands of men,
one perhaps strives for perfection. And of those who strive and are perfected,
one perhaps knows me truly.

"'Earth, water, fire, wind, space, mind, understanding and self-regard: this is
my nature, divided eightfold. This is the lower. But know my other, higher
nature, strong-armed one, which has become life, by which this world is held.
Understand that all beings have their womb in these. I am the source of the
whole world, and its dissolution. There is nothing higher than me, Dhananjaya.
All this is strung on me like heaps of pearls on a thread.

"'I am the taste in water, son of Kunti. I am the light of the moon and the sun,
the sacred syllable Om in all the Vedas, the sound in space, the manhood in men.
I am the pure smell in the earth and the brightness in fire. I am the life in
all beings and the austerity in ascetics. Know me, Partha, as the eternal seed
of all beings. I am the understanding of the understanding, the splendour of the
splendid. I am the strength of the strong, free of desire and passion. In beings
I am desire that does not go against dharma, bull of the Bharatas.

"'And know that the states of goodness, passion and darkness come from me alone.
But I am not in them; they are in me. This whole world, confused by these three
states made of the qualities, does not know me, who am beyond them and undying.
For this divine power of mine, made of the qualities, is hard to cross. Those
who take refuge in me alone cross over this power. Evil-doers, the confused, the
lowest of men, whose knowledge is carried off by that power, who have taken to
the nature of the asuras, do not take refuge in me.

"'Four kinds of men who do good worship me, Arjuna: the man in distress, the man
who wants to know, the man who wants wealth, and the man of knowledge, bull of
the Bharatas. Of them the man of knowledge, always disciplined, devoted to one
alone, is best. For I am very dear to the man of knowledge, and he is dear to
me. All of these are noble, but the man of knowledge I hold to be my very self.
For, his self disciplined, he stands in me alone as the highest end. At the end
of many births the man of knowledge takes refuge in me, thinking, "Vasudeva is
everything." Such a great soul is very hard to find.

"'Those whose knowledge is carried off by this desire or that take refuge in
other gods, keeping this rule or that, held by their own nature. Whatever form
any devotee wants to worship with faith, I make that faith of his unshakeable.
Joined to that faith, he seeks to win that god's favour, and from it he gets his
desires, which are given by me alone. But the fruit for those of small
understanding has an end. Those who sacrifice to the gods go to the gods. My
devotees come to me.

"'The unwise think that I, the unmanifest, have come into manifestation, not
knowing my higher state, undying and unsurpassed. I am not revealed to all,
covered by the power of my yoga. This confused world does not know me, the
unborn, the undying. I know the beings that have passed, Arjuna, those that are,
and those that will be, but no one knows me. All beings, scorcher of enemies,
fall into confusion at birth, Bharata, through the confusion of the pairs of
opposites, which rises from desire and hatred. But men of good deeds, whose sin
has come to an end, freed from the confusion of the pairs, worship me, firm in
their vows. Those who take refuge in me and strive for release from old age and
death know that Brahman entirely, and the self, and all action. Those who know
me as the ground of beings, of the gods and of sacrifice, know me with
disciplined thought even at the hour of departure.'"

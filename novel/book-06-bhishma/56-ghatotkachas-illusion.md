# Ghatotkacha's Illusion

"Having turned back all your men in battle," said Sanjaya, "the rakshasa ran at
Duryodhana, best of the Bharatas, wanting to kill him. Seeing him rushing at the
king, your men, mad for battle, ran at him wanting to kill him. Drawing bows as
long as palm trees, the very strong men ran at that one man, roaring like a
pride of lions. They surrounded him on every side with a shower of arrows, as
clouds in autumn surround a mountain with torrents of water. Deeply pierced and
in pain, like an elephant hurt by goads, he flew up then into the sky, like
Vinata's son. He roared a very great roar like an autumn cloud, filling the
quarters, the sky and the points between them with his fearful voice.

"Hearing that sound of the rakshasa, King Yudhishthira, best of the Bharatas,
said this to Bhimasena. 'The rakshasa is surely fighting the great chariot
fighters of Dhritarashtra's sons, to judge from the sound we hear of him roaring
his fearful roar. I see too great a burden laid on him there, son. And the
grandfather, angry, has set out to kill the Panchalas, and Phalguna is fighting
the enemy to protect them. You have heard this, strong-armed one: two tasks have
come up. Go and protect Hidimba's son, who is in the greatest danger.'

"Taking his brother's command, Wolf-belly hurried off with a lion roar,
terrifying all the kings, with great speed, king, like the ocean at the full or
new moon. After him went Satyadhriti, Suchitta's son, mad for battle, Shreniman,
Vasudana and Abhibhu, son of the Kashi king, the great chariot fighters led by
Abhimanyu, the sons of Draupadi, the bold Kshatradeva and Kshatradharma too, and
Nila, lord of Anupa, with his own force. With a great line of chariots they
surrounded Hidimba's son. With six thousand hard-hitting elephants, always in
rut, they guarded together Ghatotkacha, the lord of the rakshasas. With a great
lion roar, with the rumble of chariot rims and the noise of hooves, they made
the earth shake.

"Hearing the sound of them coming, your army, shaken by fear of Bhimasena, its
faces pale, turned back, great king, leaving Ghatotkacha. Then a battle broke
out here and there between those great-souled men, yours and the enemy, who
never turned back in battle. Loosing weapons of many forms, the great chariot
fighters ran at one another and fought, a tangled, very fierce battle that
frightened the timid. Horses met elephants, and foot soldiers chariot fighters,
seeking great glory against one another in battle, king. At once a thick, great
dust arose from the clash, raised by the feet and wheel rims of chariots,
horses, elephants and foot soldiers. The thick dust, the colour of smoke and
dawn, covered the battlefield. Neither our own men nor the enemy, king, knew one
another. Father did not know son, nor son father, in that lawless and
hair-raising slaughter. There was a very great sound of weapons and of men
shouting, best of the Bharatas, like bamboos burning. A river of the blood and
entrails of elephants, horses and men began to flow there, with hair for its
moss and grass. A very great sound was heard of the heads of men falling from
their bodies in battle, like stones falling. The earth was strewn with headless
men, with elephants with their limbs cut and horses with their bodies split.

"The great chariot fighters, loosing weapons of many kinds, ran at one another
and fought. Horses driven by horsemen met horses, struck one another in battle
and fell lifeless. Men met men, their eyes very red with anger, and killed one
another, clasping chest to chest. Elephants driven by their riders struck enemy
elephants in battle with the points of their tusks. Streaming blood, adorned
with flags, locked together, they looked like clouds with lightning. Some, split
by the points of tusks, their frontal globes split by lances, ran trumpeting
like thundering clouds. Some, with their trunks cut in two and others with their
limbs cut, fell in that tumult like mountains with their wings cut off. Other
fine elephants, their flanks torn by elephants, shed much blood, as mountains
shed minerals. Others, struck by iron arrows and pierced by lances, their riders
killed, looked like mountains without peaks. Some, possessed by anger, blind
with rut and out of control, crushed chariots, horses and foot soldiers in
hundreds in battle. So too horses struck with spears and lances by horsemen
rushed this way and that, throwing the quarters into confusion. Chariot
fighters, sons of good families who would give up their bodies, fought with
chariot fighters, using their utmost strength and doing their deeds as if
without fear. As if at a bridegroom choice, in that crush, the warriors who
gloried in battle struck at one another, seeking glory or heaven, king. While
that hair-raising battle went on, the great army of Dhritarashtra's son was
mostly turned back.

---

"Seeing his own army killed, King Duryodhana himself ran in anger at Bhimasena,
tamer of enemies. Taking up his very great bow, which sounded like Indra's
thunderbolt, he covered the Pandava with a great shower of arrows. In anger he
fitted a very sharp feathered crescent arrow and cut Bhimasena's bow. Seeing
that opening, the great chariot fighter hurried and fitted a sharp arrow that
could split mountains, and with it the strong-armed one struck Bhimasena in the
chest. Deeply pierced and in pain, licking the corners of his mouth, the
splendid one leaned on his gold-adorned banner.

"Seeing Bhimasena so dispirited, Ghatotkacha blazed with anger like a fire about
to burn. The great chariot fighters of the Pandavas, led by Abhimanyu, ran
shouting at the king, alarmed. Seeing them coming, angry and alarmed,
Bharadvaja's son said to your great chariot fighters, 'Go quickly, bless you,
and protect the king. He is in the greatest danger, sinking in an ocean of
disaster. These angry great archers, the great chariot fighters of the Pandavas,
have put Bhimasena in front and rushed at Duryodhana. They are loosing weapons
of many kinds, intent on victory, roaring fearful roars and terrifying this
earth.'

"Hearing the teacher's words, your men led by Somadatta went against the Pandava
army. Kripa, Bhurishravas, Shalya, Drona's son, Vivimshati, Chitrasena, Vikarna,
the Sindhu king, Brihadbala and the two great archers of Avanti surrounded the
Kaurava. Having gone twenty paces, the Pandavas and the sons of Dhritarashtra
fought, wanting to kill one another. Having spoken so, the strong-armed son of
Bharadvaja drew his great bow and struck Bhima with twenty-six arrows. Again the
strong-armed one quickly covered him with arrows, as a cloud in autumn covers a
mountain with torrents of water. The mighty great archer Bhimasena, hurrying,
pierced him back with ten arrows in the left side. Deeply pierced and in pain,
and old in years, Bharata, he lost consciousness at once and sat down on the
chariot floor.

"Seeing his teacher hurt, King Duryodhana himself and Drona's son ran in anger
at Bhimasena. Seeing the two coming, like Time, the ender, Yama, the
strong-armed Bhimasena quickly took up his club. He leapt quickly from his
chariot and stood like an unmoving mountain in battle, his heavy club raised,
like Yama's staff. Seeing him with his club raised, like Kailasa with its peaks,
the Kaurava and Drona's son ran at him together. As those two, the best of the
strong, came at him together and quickly, Wolf-belly hurried and ran at them
with speed. Seeing him come, angry and terrible to see, the great chariot
fighters of the Kauravas ran at him quickly. All of them, led by Bharadvaja's
son, wanting to kill Bhimasena, brought weapons of many kinds down on Bhima's
chest. Together they pressed the Pandava on every side.

"Seeing that great chariot fighter in danger and hard pressed, the great chariot
fighters of the Pandavas, led by Abhimanyu, ran up wanting to save him, giving
up the lives that are so hard to give up. The brave lord of Anupa, Bhima's dear
friend, Nila, like a dark cloud, went in anger at Drona's son. The great archer
always rivalled Drona's son. Drawing his great bow, he pierced Drona's son with
a feathered arrow, as Shakra long ago, great king, pierced the danava
Vipracitti, hard to attack, the terror of the gods, who frightened the three
worlds with his anger and his own splendour. Split like that by Nila with a fine
feathered arrow, Drona's son, gushing blood, was filled with anger. The best of
the wise drew his bright bow, which sounded like Indra's thunderbolt, and set
his mind on destroying Nila. Then he fitted clean broad arrows, tempered by the
smith, killed his four horses and brought down his banner. With a seventh broad
arrow he pierced Nila in the chest. Deeply pierced and in pain, he sat down on
the chariot floor.

"Seeing King Nila, like a mass of clouds, stunned, Ghatotkacha too, surrounded
by his brothers, ran in anger at speed at Drona's son, who shines in battle. So
did the other rakshasas, mad for battle. Seeing the rakshasa of terrible looks
coming, the splendid son of Bharadvaja hurried to meet him. In anger he killed
the rakshasas of terrible looks who were in front, the angry ones going ahead of
the rakshasa. Seeing them turned away by the arrows loosed from Drona's son's
bow, the huge-bodied Ghatotkacha, Bhimasena's son, grew angry. The lord of the
rakshasas, master of illusion, brought forth a great illusion, terrible in form
and very cruel, bewildering Drona's son in battle.

"Then all your men were turned away by the illusion. They saw one another cut
down on the ground, writhing, wretched, drenched in blood: Drona, Duryodhana,
Shalya and Ashvatthama, and most of the great archers who were the leading
Kauravas. All the chariot fighters were destroyed, the elephants brought down,
and the horses with their riders cut to pieces in thousands. Seeing that, your
army ran towards the camp. I shouted, king, and so did Devavrata: 'Fight! Do not
run! This is a rakshasa illusion in battle, made by Ghatotkacha!' But they did
not stop, bewildered. In their fear they did not believe what we two said.
Seeing them run, the Pandavas, having won the victory, roared lion roars
together with Ghatotkacha, and the sounds of conches and drums rang out loudly
on every side. So at the time of sunset your whole army was broken by the
evil-hearted son of Hidimba and fled in all directions."

# Drona Reads the Omens

"Then the brave great archer," said Sanjaya, "with the stride of an elephant in
rut, took up his great bow, which could stop an elephant in rut. The great
chariot fighter shook his fine bow, drove off great chariot fighters and brought
down the army of the Pandavas. Mighty Drona, who knew omens, looked at the omens
on every side, and said to his son, who was scorching the ranks:

"'This is the day, son, on which Partha, the great chariot fighter, wanting to
kill Bhishma in battle, will make his greatest effort. My arrows are flying up
of themselves, and my bow seems to throb. My weapons are coming to hand, and my
mind is set on cruelty. In the quarters, though they are calm, beasts and birds
cry terribly. Vultures are settling low over the army of the Bharatas. The sun
seems to have lost its light, and the quarters are red on every side. The earth
roars and trembles, and the mounts groan. Herons, vultures and cranes cry again
and again. Ill-omened jackals howl, announcing great fear. A great meteor has
fallen from the middle of the sun's disc. A bar of cloud with a headless trunk
stands covering the sun. A terrible halo has come round the moon and the sun,
announcing terrible fear, the cutting down of the bodies of kings. The gods in
the temples of the lord of the Kurus tremble and laugh, dance and weep. The
planets have moved against the moon from right to left, and the blessed moon has
risen with its horns pointing down. I see the bodies of the kings as if their
light had gone. In the armies of Dhritarashtra's son, those in armour do not
shine. And on every side, in both armies, the great sound of Panchajanya and the
twang of Gandiva are heard.

"'Surely Bibhatsu, having taken up the supreme weapons in battle, will set aside
the other warriors and go for the grandfather. My hair stands on end and my mind
seems to sink, strong-armed one, when I think of the meeting of Bhishma and
Arjuna. Partha has put in front in battle that evil-minded Panchala, wise in
deceit, and gone to fight Bhishma. Bhishma said long ago, "I will not kill
Shikhandi. The Creator made her a woman, and by fate she became a man again."
And Yajnasena's son, the great chariot fighter, is an ill-omened banner, and the
son of the river will not strike at one with an ill-omened banner. Thinking of
this, my understanding sinks badly. Today Partha has rushed at the eldest of the
Kurus in battle. Yudhishthira's anger, the meeting of Bhishma and Arjuna, and my
own eagerness with weapons are surely ill-omened for the people.

"'The Pandava is high-minded, strong, brave, skilled in weapons, of firm
courage. He shoots far, his arrows strike hard, and he knows omens. He cannot be
conquered in battle even by the gods with Vasava. He is strong and wise, he has
conquered hardship, he is the best of fighters. The Pandava is always victorious
in battle, and his weapons are terrible. Keep out of his way and go quickly to
him of the controlled vow. See, strong-armed one, the slaughter that has come.
The great, fine armour of the brave, bright with gold, is being split by arrows
with bent joints. Banner tops, lances and bows are being cut, and clean, sharp
spears, darts shining with gold, and the elephants' banners, by the angry
diademed one. This is no time for those who live on a master's bread to guard
their lives, son. Go, putting heaven first, for glory and for victory. The
monkey-bannered one is crossing in his chariot the river of battle, very
terrible and very hard to cross, whose whirlpools are horses, elephants and
chariots.

"'Reverence for brahmins, self-control, giving, austerity and great conduct are
seen here in the king whose brother is Dhananjaya, and the strong Bhimasena, and
Madri's two Pandava sons, and whose protector is Vasudeva the Varshneya. It is
his anger, born of the evil-minded son of Dhritarashtra's anger, the anger of
one whose body is burned by austerity, that is burning the Bharatas. Here Partha
is seen, relying on Vasudeva, tearing apart all the armies of Dhritarashtra's
son on every side. This army is seen being stirred up by the diademed one, like
a great river mouth bound with great waves stirred up by a whale. Cries of
"Alas!" and "Kilakila!" are heard at the head of the army. Go to the heir of the
Panchalas. I will go to Yudhishthira. It is hard to find a gap in the array of
the king of measureless splendour. It is like the belly of the ocean, held on
every side by atirathas. Satyaki, Abhimanyu, Dhrishtadyumna and Wolf-belly, and
the twins, guard the king, the lord of men. Dark, like Upendra, risen like a
great sala tree, here goes through the ranks one like a second Phalguna. Take
the supreme weapons, take another great bow, and go to the king's side, and
fight Wolf-belly. For who would not want his dear son to live for endless years?
But putting the dharma of the kshatriya first, we send you. And here is Bhishma
in battle burning up the great army, son, in battle much like Yama or Varuna.'"

**Divergences.** The rejected lines (\*450–\*454) are not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 6.108.17: Drona calls Shikhandi "wise in deceit" and "evil-minded" (*nikṛtiprajñaṃ ... pāpacetasam*). These are his words.
* 6.108.18: Drona quotes Bhishma's words, which use the feminine for Shikhandi.
* 6.108.32: "It is his anger ... that is burning the Bharatas": Yudhishthira's anger, born of Duryodhana's.
* 6.108.38 *upendrasadṛśaḥ śyāmaḥ*: the one "like a second Phalguna" is not named. He is dark like Upendra (Vishnu). The text leaves him unnamed.

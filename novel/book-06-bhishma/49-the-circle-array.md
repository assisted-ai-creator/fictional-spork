# The Circle Array

"Then the heroes, great king," said Sanjaya, "having wronged one another, went
to their own camps drenched in blood. Having rested as was proper and honoured
one another, they were seen in armour again, wanting to fight.

"Then your son, king, overwhelmed with worry, his limbs smeared with flowing
blood, asked the grandfather this. 'Their terrible, frightening armies, well
arrayed, with many banners, the swift floods of the Pandavas' chariots, those
brave and famous men, have split our ranks, killed and crushed them. They have
bewildered everyone in battle and gone into that crocodile array like a
thunderbolt. Bhima has struck me down with terrible arrows like the staff of
death. Seeing him angry, king, I fainted with fear, and I have found no peace
today. Through your grace, you who are true to your word, I wish to win victory
and kill the Pandavas.'

"So addressed, the great-souled one laughed. Knowing that Duryodhana was angry,
the high-minded son of Ganga, the best of those who bear weapons, answered him
without being troubled. 'Plunging into the army with the greatest effort, with
my whole self, prince, I wish to give you victory and happiness. I am not hiding
myself from you. But these many terrible great chariot fighters who are the
Pandavas' allies in battle are glorious, the bravest of the brave and skilled in
weapons. They have conquered weariness and they vomit the poison of anger. They
cannot be conquered quickly here, swollen with courage and made enemies by you.
I will fight them, king, with my whole self, giving up my life, hero. For your
sake in battle, great one, my life is not what I most want to keep today. For
your sake I could burn all the worlds with the gods and the daityas, let alone
your enemies here. So I will fight the Pandavas, king, and I will do all that
you want.'

"When he heard this, Duryodhana was very glad and pleased at heart. Then,
delighted, he said to all the troops and all the kings, 'Go out!' At his command
they went out quickly, in tens of thousands of chariots, horses, foot soldiers
and elephants. Full of joy, great, with weapons of many kinds, with elephants,
horses and foot soldiers, your forces stood shining in the battle, king. Herds
of elephants stood in groups, well handled, shining on every side. The hosts of
your army were manned by warriors who knew arms and weapons, god among men. With
chariots and crowds of foot soldiers, elephants and horses going into battle,
driven forward by the rule, the dust rose the colour of the young sun and shone,
hiding the sun's rays. Flags on chariots and elephants shone, blown by the wind
and whirling on every side, of many colours, king, like lightning in the sky
among clouds. As the kings drew their bows, a tumultuous and very terrible sound
arose, like that of the ocean being churned by the hosts of gods and great
asuras at the first age. So your sons' army, with its fierce roars, of many
forms and colours, risen up to kill the enemy army, looked then like the masses
of clouds at the end of an age.

---

"Then Ganga's son, best of the Bharatas, spoke again to your son, who was lost
in thought, words that gladdened him. 'I, Drona, Shalya, Kritavarma the Satvata,
Ashvatthama, Vikarna, Somadatta, the Sindhu king, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti,
Bahlika with the Bahlikas, the strong king of the Trigartas, the Magadha king,
very hard to defeat, Brihadbala of Kosala, Chitrasena and Vivimshati are here.
There are many thousands of shining chariots with great banners, and horses of
the country well ridden by horsemen. There are lordly elephants maddened with
rut, their temples and mouths running, and brave foot soldiers with weapons of
many kinds, born in many countries. All are ready to fight for you. These and
many others have given up their lives for your sake. My view is that they could
conquer even the gods in battle. But I must always tell you what is good for
you, king. The Pandavas cannot be conquered even by the gods with Vasava. They
have Vasudeva as their ally, and their courage is like great Indra's. Still,
king of kings, I will do what you say in every way. Either I will defeat the
Pandavas in battle, or the Pandavas will defeat me.'

"Having said this, he gave him a fine and potent herb that draws out arrows, and
he was freed from his arrows then.

"Then at clear dawn the mighty Bhishma, skilled in arrays, drew up the array
himself with his own army. It was a circle, best of men, crowded with weapons of
many kinds, full of chief warriors, elephants and foot soldiers. It was
surrounded on every side by many thousands of chariots and by great troops of
horses carrying spears and lances. By each elephant there were seven chariots,
and by each chariot seven horses. Behind each horse were ten archers, and by
each archer seven men with shields. So your army was arrayed, great king, with
great chariot fighters, standing ready for the great battle, protected by
Bhishma in battle. Ten thousand horses, as many elephants, ten thousand chariots
and your sons in armour, brave men led by Chitrasena, guarded the grandfather.
Guarded by those heroes, and themselves protected by him, the very strong kings
were seen in armour. Duryodhana, in armour, standing on his chariot in the
battle, shone, filled with glory, like Shakra in heaven. Then there was a great
noise from your sons, Bharata, a tumultuous rumble of chariots and the sound of
instruments. The great circle array of Dhritarashtra's sons, drawn up by Bhishma
facing west, hard for the enemy-killers to break, shone on every side, king,
hard for enemies to reach in battle.

"Seeing that most terrible circle array, King Yudhishthira himself drew up the
thunderbolt array. With the ranks drawn up so, the chariot fighters and horsemen
standing in their places roared lion roars. Then the brave hard hitters, wanting
battle and wanting to break the array, went out against one another with their
troops. Bharadvaja's son went against the Matsya, and Drona's son against
Shikhandi. King Duryodhana himself ran at Prishata's grandson. Nakula and
Sahadeva, king, went against the lord of the Madras. Vinda and Anuvinda of
Avanti ran at Iravan. All the kings fought Dhananjaya in battle. Bhimasena,
ready in battle, held off the son of Hridika. Arjuna's son fought your sons
Chitrasena, Vikarna and Durmarshana in battle, lord, king. Hidimba's son, the
best of rakshasas, ran at speed at the great archer of Pragjyotisha, like a
maddened elephant at a maddened elephant. Then the rakshasa Alambusa, king, ran
in anger at Satyaki, mad for battle, and his army. Bhurishravas, ready in
battle, fought Dhrishtaketu. Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, fought King
Shrutayu. Chekitana fought Kripa in battle. The rest, ready, went against the
great chariot fighter Bhima.

"Then thousands of kings, with spears, lances, iron arrows, clubs and iron bars
in their hands, surrounded Dhananjaya. Then Arjuna, very angry, said to the
Varshneya, 'Madhava, see the armies of Dhritarashtra's son in battle, drawn up
by the great-souled son of Ganga, who knows arrays. See the brave men in armour,
eager for battle, Madhava. See the king of the Trigartas with his brothers,
Keshava. Today I will bring them down while you watch, Janardana, these men who
want to fight me on the battlefield, best of the Yadus.'

"Having said this, the Kaunteya wiped his bowstring and rained showers of arrows
on the crowds of kings. Those great archers too filled him with showers of
arrows, as clouds fill a pond with their torrents in the rains. There was a
great cry of grief in your army, lord of the people, when they saw the two
Krishnas heavily covered with arrows in the great battle. The gods and divine
seers, the gandharvas and great serpents, were filled with the greatest wonder
at seeing the two Krishnas like that.

"Then Arjuna in anger brought forth Indra's weapon, king. There we saw Vijaya's
wonderful courage, for he held off with floods of arrows the rain of weapons the
enemy loosed, and not one of them was left unpierced there, lord of the people.
Partha pierced those thousands of kings, and their horses and elephants, sir,
some with two arrows and some with three. Being killed by Partha, they went to
Bhishma son of Shantanu. Bhishma became the saviour then of those drowning in
the bottomless deep. With them falling back on it, your army broke and was
thrown into turmoil, great king, like the great ocean by the winds."

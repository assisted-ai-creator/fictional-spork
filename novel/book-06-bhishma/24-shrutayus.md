summary: The afternoon and evening of the seventh day. Yudhishthira blazes up in an anger that frightens even the gods, then masters it and drives King Shrutayus from the field. Chekitana and Kripa fight on foot with swords until both fall senseless. Abhimanyu leaves Duryodhana's brothers alive, remembering Bhima's vow, and Arjuna kills Susharma's companions and their rear guards. Yudhishthira rebukes Shikhandi for not keeping his vow to kill Bhishma, and Shikhandi turns Shalya's weapon with the Varuna weapon. Bhima smashes Chitrasena's chariot with his mace. Bhishma kills Yudhishthira's horses, turns away from Shikhandi again, and cuts down chariot fighters like ripe palm fruit. The armies part at nightfall, and for a while the camps are like heaven.

# Shrutayus

"When the sun had reached midday," said Sanjaya, "King Yudhishthira saw
Shrutayus and urged on his horses. The king charged Shrutayus, tamer of enemies,
striking him with nine sharp, straight arrows. The great archer King Shrutayus
held off the arrows the son of Dharma sent in the battle, and planted seven
arrows in Kunti's son. They split his armour and drank his blood in the battle,
as if searching for the life in the great man's body. Badly wounded by that
great king, the Pandava pierced the king in the heart with a boar-eared arrow.
Then with another broad arrow Partha, best of chariot fighters, quickly brought
the great man's banner down from his chariot to the ground. Seeing his banner
fallen, King Shrutayus pierced the Pandava with seven sharp arrows, king.

"Then Yudhishthira, son of Dharma, blazed up in anger, like fire at the end of
an age about to burn up all beings. Seeing the Pandava angry, the gods,
gandharvas and rakshasas trembled, great king, and the world was thrown into
turmoil. This was in the minds of all beings: 'This king, in his anger, will
burn up the three worlds today.' The seers and the gods made great prayers for
blessing, king, for the peace of the worlds, while the Pandava was angry. Filled
with anger, licking the corners of his mouth, he took on a terrible form, like
the sun at the end of an age. Then all your armies there, lord of the people,
lost hope of life, Bharata.

"But the famous man held back his anger with firmness and cut Shrutayus's great
bow at the grip. When his bow was cut, the king pierced him in the middle of the
chest with an iron arrow while the whole army watched. Then the mighty man
quickly killed the great man's horses and his charioteer with arrows, king.
Seeing the king's manhood, Shrutayus left his chariot with its horses killed and
ran swiftly from the battle. When that great archer had been beaten by the son
of Dharma in battle, king, Duryodhana's whole army turned away. Having done
this, great king, Yudhishthira, son of Dharma, killed your army like Time with
his mouth open.

"Chekitana the Varshneya covered Gautama, best of chariot fighters, with arrows
while all the armies watched. Kripa, Sharadvat's son, held off those arrows in
the battle and pierced Chekitana, who was fighting hard, with feathered arrows,
king. Then with another broad arrow he cut his bow, sir, and with his quick hand
he brought down his charioteer in the battle. He killed his horses too, king,
and both his wheel guards. The Satvata leapt quickly down from his chariot and
took up a mace. With that mace, which killed heroes, the best of mace fighters
killed Gautama's horses and brought down his charioteer. Standing on the ground,
Gautama shot sixteen arrows at him. They split the Satvata and went into the
earth. Then Chekitana in anger hurled that mace again, wanting to kill Gautama,
as Purandara hurled at Vritra. Gautama held off the bright, great mace, with its
core of stone, with many thousands of arrows as it came at him.

"Then Chekitana drew his sword from its sheath, Bharata, and with the greatest
speed ran at Gautama. Gautama too threw down his bow, took a sharp sword and ran
at Chekitana with great speed, king. Both mighty men, holding fine swords, cut
each other with their very sharp blades. Struck by the force of the swords,
those two bulls among men fell to the earth, where all beings live, their limbs
overcome with faintness, dazed by the effort. Then Karakarsha, his friend, ran
up fast. Seeing Chekitana, mad in battle, in that state, he lifted him into his
chariot while the whole army watched. In the same way brave Shakuni, your
brother-in-law, lord of the people, quickly lifted Gautama, best of chariot
fighters, into his chariot.

"Mighty Dhrishtaketu in anger quickly pierced Somadatta's son in the chest with
ninety arrows, king. With the arrows standing in his chest, Somadatta's son
shone brightly, great king, like the sun at midday with its rays. Bhurishravas
with his best arrows made the great chariot fighter Dhrishtaketu chariotless in
the battle, his charioteer and horses killed. Seeing him without a chariot, his
horses and his charioteer killed, he covered him with a great shower of arrows
in the battle. Great-hearted Dhrishtaketu left that chariot, sir, and climbed
into Shatanika's.

"Chitrasena, Vikarna and Durmarshana, king, chariot fighters in golden armour,
charged Subhadra's son. Then there was a terrible fight between Abhimanyu and
them, king, like the fight in the body between wind, bile and phlegm. The tiger
among men left your sons without chariots in the great battle, king, but he did
not kill them, remembering then what Bhima had said.

"Then, king, Bhishma, whom even the gods could hardly come at, went quickly to
rescue your sons, surrounded by many hundreds of kings riding elephants, horses
and chariots. Seeing him go, the son of Kunti with the white horses thought of
Abhimanyu, a boy, a great chariot fighter, alone, and said to Vasudeva, 'Drive
the horses, Hrishikesha, to where those many chariots are. These are many
heroes, masters of weapons, mad in battle. Drive, Madhava, so that they do not
kill our army.'

"Told this by Kunti's son of boundless power, the Varshneya drove the chariot
yoked with white horses into the battle. There was a great groan from your army,
sir, when Arjuna came on in anger against your men in the battle. Reaching the
kings who guarded Bhishma, Kunti's son said to Susharma, king, 'I know you, best
in battle, and my enemy of old. See the most cruel fruit of it now, come round
in its turn. Today I will show you your forefathers who have died before you.'

"As Bibhatsu, killer of enemies, spoke like this, Susharma, leader of chariot
troops, heard the harsh words but said nothing to him, good or bad. He went
against heroic Arjuna, surrounded by many kings, in front, behind and at the
sides, on every side. Surrounding Arjuna in the battle with your sons, blameless
one, he covered him with arrows as clouds cover the sun. Then a very great
battle began between yours and the Pandavas in the fight, Bharata, with blood
for water.

"Stung by the arrows, mighty Dhananjaya hissed like a snake trodden underfoot.
With one arrow after another he forcibly cut the bows of the great chariot
fighters in the battle. In a moment he cut the bows of those mighty kings in the
battle, and the great man pierced them all at once with arrows, meaning to leave
none. Struck by Shakra's son, king, they fell in the battle smeared with blood,
their limbs split, their heads fallen, lifeless, their armour and bodies cut.
Overcome by Partha's might, they went to the earth, those men of many forms, and
perished together. Seeing the princes killed in battle, the Trigarta king went
forward at once.

"Then thirty-two others, the rear guards of those chariots, fell on Partha. They
surrounded Partha as the others had, drew their loud bows and rained a great
flood of arrows on him, as clouds rain floods of water on a mountain. Hard
pressed by the flood of arrows, Dhananjaya grew angry in the battle and killed
those rear guards too with sixty arrows washed in oil. Having beaten those sixty
chariots in the battle, famous Dhananjaya was glad at heart. Then Jishnu, having
killed the forces of the kings in the battle, hurried on to kill Bhishma.

"The Trigarta king saw those great chariot fighters killed, his own kin, and
went quickly at Partha to kill him, putting those kings in front in the battle.
Seeing Dhananjaya, the best of those who bear weapons, attacked, the fighters
led by Shikhandi came out with sharp weapons in their hands, wanting to guard
Arjuna's chariot. Partha too, seeing those heroes among men coming on with the
Trigarta king, scattered them in the battle with sharp arrows loosed from the
Gandiva. As the bowman went to meet Bhishma, he saw Duryodhana, the Saindhava
and the other kings in the battle, ready to hold him back. The hero fought them
hard for a moment. Then the man of boundless valour and great power left the
king and the kings led by Jayadratha. Strong as terror, high-minded, bow and
arrows in hand, he went on towards Ganga's son in the battle.

"Great-hearted Yudhishthira, of fierce strength and endless fame, came up
quickly in his anger. He left behind in the battle the lord of the Madras, who
was his share, and went to fight Bhishma, son of Shantanu, with the sons of
Madri and Bhimasena. Attacked by those foremost great chariot fighters, Ganga's
son, the wonderful fighter, great Shantanava, did not flinch at all the sons of
Pandu together.

"Then King Jayadratha came up in the battle, true to his word, of very fierce
strength, high-minded, and forcibly cut the bows of those great chariot fighters
with his fine bow. Great-hearted Duryodhana, whose anger is poison, grew angry
in the battle and struck Yudhishthira, Bhimasena, the twins and Partha with
arrows bright as fire. Kripa, Shalya, Shala and Chitrasena too, lord, pierced
them with arrows in their great anger, as the hosts of daityas, joined together,
pierced the gods.

"Seeing Shikhandi with his weapons cut by Shantanava, great Ajatashatru grew
angry in the battle and spoke angrily to Shikhandi. 'You said to me in front of
your father, "I will kill him, the man of the great vow, Bhishma, with floods of
arrows bright as the clear sun. I speak the truth." That was the promise you
made. You are not making it good, since you do not kill Devavrata in battle. Do
not be false to your promise, hero among men. Guard your dharma, your family and
your fame. Look at Bhishma in battle, of terrible speed, scorching all my troops
with floods and nets of very fiery arrows, like Time made by Death, in a moment.
Your bow cut, careless of the battle, beaten by King Shantanava, where will you
go, leaving your kin and your brothers? This is not worthy of you. Surely you
are afraid, son of Drupada, seeing Bhishma of endless valour and the army broken
and running like this. Your face has no joy in it. Even with Dhananjaya caught
up in the great battle, hero among men, how can you, famous on earth, be afraid
of Bhishma today, hero?'

"Hearing the Dharma king's words, harsh in their syllables and full of reproach,
the great man took them as a rebuke, king, and hurried to kill Bhishma. As
Shikhandi came at great speed, charging Bhishma, Shalya held him back with a
terrible weapon very hard to overcome. But when he saw the weapon rising, with
the power of the fire at the end of an age, Drupada's son, king, whose might was
like great Indra's, was not bewildered. The great archer stood right there,
checking that weapon with arrows. Then Shikhandi took another weapon, the fierce
Varuna weapon, to strike it down. The gods in the sky and the kings saw that
weapon split by the weapon.

"But heroic Bhishma, great king, cut the bright bow and the banner of the son of
Pandu, King Yudhishthira of the line of Ajamidha, in the battle, and roared.
Then Bhimasena, seeing Yudhishthira overcome by fear, threw down his bow and
arrows, took up his mace and went on foot at Jayadratha in the battle. As
Bhimasena came at great speed with his mace, Jayadratha pierced him all over
with five hundred terrible sharp arrows like Yama's staff. Not caring for those
arrows, swift Vrikodara, his mind possessed by anger, killed all the horses of
the Sindhu king in the battle, horses bred in Aratta.

"Then your son, of matchless power, like the king of the gods, seeing this, came
quickly in his chariot with his weapons raised to kill Bhimasena. Bhima suddenly
roared and went out to meet him, threatening him with his mace. When they saw
that mace raised like Yama's staff, all the Kurus round about left your fierce
son, wanting to escape the fall of the mace. They fell back in that tumultuous,
most cruel, bewildering fight, Bharata. But Chitrasena kept his head. Seeing the
great mace coming, he left his chariot and went on foot in the battle with a
bright sword and shield. He leapt down like a lion from a mountain top and went
to another part of the ground. The mace reached the bright chariot and struck it
down in the battle with its horses and its charioteer, and went into the ground
like a great blazing meteor falling from the sky to earth. Seeing that great
wonder, Bharata, your men were glad. All of them roared together on every side,
and with their troops they honoured your son.

"Your son Vikarna came up to high-minded Chitrasena without his chariot and
lifted him into his own. As that tumultuous, confused fight went on, Bhishma,
son of Shantanu, quickly charged Yudhishthira. Then the Srinjayas, with their
chariots, elephants and horses, trembled. They thought Yudhishthira had reached
the mouth of Death. Lord Yudhishthira of the Kurus, with the twins, went against
the great archer Bhishma, son of Shantanu, a tiger among men. Then the Pandava,
loosing thousands of arrows in the battle, covered Bhishma as a cloud covers the
sun. Ganga's son received the nets of arrows he sent so well, by the hundred and
the thousand, Bharata. In the same way the nets of arrows Bhishma shot, sir,
were seen in the sky like flocks of flying birds. In half the blink of an eye
Bhishma, son of Shantanu, made Kunti's son invisible in the battle with a net of
arrows, part by part.

"Then King Yudhishthira in anger sent an iron arrow like a venomous snake at the
great Kaurava. Before it reached him, king, the great chariot fighter Bhishma
cut down the arrow loosed from his bow with a razor arrow in the battle. Having
cut that iron arrow, which was like Time, Bhishma killed the Kuru lord's horses
adorned with gold. Leaving his chariot with its horses killed, Yudhishthira, son
of Dharma, quickly climbed into great Nakula's chariot. Bhishma, conqueror of
enemy cities, in great anger, came up to the twins too in the battle and covered
them with arrows.

"Seeing them hard pressed by Bhishma's arrows, great king, Yudhishthira fell
into deep thought, wanting Bhishma killed. Then Yudhishthira urged on the kings
who were his, his hosts of friends: 'All of you, kill Bhishma, son of Shantanu!'
Hearing Partha's words, all those kings surrounded the grandfather with a great
line of chariots. Surrounded on every side, your father Devavrata played with
his bow, king, bringing down great chariot fighters. The Parthas saw the Kaurava
moving in the battle like a lion cub that has gone in among deer in the forest.
Seeing him threaten the heroes in the battle and terrify them with arrows, they
were terrified, great king, as herds of deer are by a lion. The kshatriyas saw
the lion of the Bharatas move in the battle like fire, with the wind to help it,
eager to burn dry brush. Bhishma brought down the heads of chariot fighters in
the battle as a skilled man brings down ripe fruit from palm trees. With the
heads falling to the ground, great king, there was a tumultuous noise like
falling stones.

"As that tumultuous, most cruel battle went on, there was a great confusion
among all the armies. When the arrays were broken, the kshatriyas challenged
each other one to one and stood up to fight. Shikhandi reached the grandfather
of the Bharatas, charged him fast and said, 'Stand! Stand!' Bhishma paid no heed
to Shikhandi in the battle. Thinking of Shikhandi's womanhood, he went in anger
against the Srinjayas. Seeing Bhishma, the great chariot fighter, the Srinjayas
were glad, and gave many kinds of lion roars mixed with the sound of conches.

"Then a battle began, the chariots and elephants tangled together, when the sun
had gone to the western quarter, lord. Dhrishtadyumna the Panchala and Satyaki,
the great chariot fighter, pressed hard on the army with showers of spears and
javelins, and killed your men in the battle with many weapons, king. Though they
were being killed in the battle, bull among men, your men set their minds nobly
on fighting and did not leave the battle. The great chariot fighters struck at
the enemy as hard as their spirit allowed. There was a great cry from your great
men, king, as they were killed in battle by great Prishata's son.

"Hearing that terrible cry from your men, the two great chariot fighters of
Avanti, Vinda and Anuvinda, stood up against Prishata's son. Those great chariot
fighters quickly killed his horses and both covered Prishata's son with a shower
of arrows. The mighty Panchala leapt quickly down from his chariot and climbed
into the chariot of great Satyaki. Then King Yudhishthira, surrounded by a great
army, went against the two angry princes of Avanti, scorchers of enemies. In the
same way your son too, sir, surrounded Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti with all his
strength and stood by them. And Arjuna in anger, bull of the kshatriyas, fought
the kshatriyas in the battle as the Wielder of the Thunderbolt fought the
asuras. Drona too, in anger, doing what your son wanted, blew away all the
Panchalas as fire blows away a heap of cotton. Your sons, lord of the people,
led by Duryodhana, surrounded Bhishma in the battle and fought the Pandavas.

"Then, as the sun turned red, King Duryodhana said to all your men, 'Hurry!'
Bharata. As they fought on like that, doing hard deeds, the sun climbed the
western mountain and no longer shone. In a moment, at the mouth of the night, a
terrible river began to flow, its waves floods of blood, crowded with packs of
jackals. The field became terrible, filled with hosts of ghosts, with ill-omened
she-jackals howling their fearful howl. Rakshasas and pishachas and other eaters
of flesh were seen on every side, by the hundred and the thousand.

"Then Arjuna, having beaten the kings led by Susharma with their followers in
the middle of the army, went towards his own camp. King Yudhishthira of the
Kurus too, with his two brothers, went to his own camp at night, surrounded by
his army. Bhimasena too, lord of kings, having beaten the chariot fighters led
by Duryodhana in the battle, went towards his own camp. King Duryodhana too
surrounded Bhishma, son of Shantanu, in the great battle and went quickly
towards the camp. Drona, Drona's son, Kripa, Shalya and Kritavarma the Satvata
surrounded the whole army and went towards the camp. In the same way Satyaki,
king, and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's son, gathered the warriors in the battle
and went towards the camp. So, great king, your men and the Pandavas, scorchers
of enemies, turned back together at nightfall.

"Then the Pandavas and the Kurus went to their own camps, great king, and
settled there, honouring one another. The heroes set guards for themselves and
posted their troops as was proper. They drew out the arrows and bathed in many
kinds of water. Blessings were said for them all, and bards praised them. The
famous men took their pleasure to the sound of songs and instruments. For a
while all of it was like heaven. The great chariot fighters told no stories of
the battle there. As the army slept there, king, with its people tired out, the
two camps, full of elephants and horses, were a sight to see."

**Divergences.**

* **Yudhishthira's anger.** When he is wounded, Yudhishthira blazes up so fiercely that gods and seers pray for the worlds' safety, and then he "held back his anger with firmness" (6.80.8–14).
* **Abhimanyu spares the princes.** He leaves Chitrasena, Vikarna and Durmarshana chariotless but does not kill them, "remembering what Bhima had said" (6.80.40), Bhima's vow to kill all the sons of Dhritarashtra himself.
* **Yudhishthira and Shikhandi.** Yudhishthira reminds Shikhandi of his vow to kill Bhishma, made "in front of your father" (6.81.18). Bhishma again turns away from Shikhandi, "thinking of his womanhood" (6.82.26).
* **Arjuna's "sixty".** Arjuna kills the thirty-two rear guards "with sixty arrows", and the text then speaks of "those sixty chariots" he has beaten (6.81.5–7). The numbers are kept as they stand.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*327–\*334. \*327 has Arjuna say "Let us go quickly to protect the boy."

**Choices.**

* 6.80.5 *varāhakarṇa*: "a boar-eared arrow", an arrow with a broad head.
* 6.80.30 *sarvabhūtaniṣevitām*: the earth "where all beings live".
* 6.80.39 *vātapittakaphaiḥ*: "like the fight in the body between wind, bile and phlegm", the three humours of old medicine.
* 6.81.12 *svabhāgam āptam*: Shalya was Yudhishthira's "share", the opponent allotted to him.
* 6.81.28 *ājamīḍha*: "of the line of Ajamidha", a Kuru ancestor.
* 6.81.31 *āraṭṭajān*: "horses bred in Aratta".
* 6.82.21: "as a skilled man brings down ripe fruit from palm trees".

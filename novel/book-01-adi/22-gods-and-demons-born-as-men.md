# Gods and Demons Born as Men

"Blessed one," said Janamejaya, "I want to hear the truth about the gods, the
danavas, the yakshas and the rakshasas, and all the other beings: the births and
the deeds of these great ones among men, one after another."

Lord of men, I will tell you all of the dwellers of heaven who were born among
men. First I will tell you of the danavas.

The bull among the danavas who was known as Viprachitti became the bull among
men known as Jarasandha. The son of Diti who is remembered as Hiranyakashipu,
king, was born in the world of men as Shishupala, a bull among men. The one known
as Samhrada, Prahrada's younger brother, was born as the famous Shalya, the bull
of the Bahlikas. Bright Anuhrada, who was known as the youngest, became the king
known as Dhrishtaketu. The daitya named Shibi, king, became a lord of the earth
famous as Druma. The one named Bashkala, the best of the asuras, became the lord
of men known as Bhagadatta.

Ayahshiras, Ashvashiras, mighty Ayahshanku, Gaganamurdhan and Vegavat the fifth:
these five brave great asuras, king, were born among the Kekayas as great
princes, the best of the bulls among kings. Another, the mighty one known as
Ketumat, became a king on earth known as Amitaujas. The glorious great asura known
as Svarbhanu became the king of fierce deeds known as Ugrasena. The glorious great
asura known as Ashva became a king named Ashoka, of great valour and courage. His
younger brother, king, who is remembered as Ashvapati, a daitya, became a king,
Hardikya, a bull among men.

The glorious great asura known as Vrishaparvan became a king on earth known as
Dirghaprajna. Ajaka, king, the younger brother of Vrishaparvan, became a king on
earth known as Malla. The strong great asura known as Ashvagriva became a king on
earth known as Rochamana. Sukshma, king, who was called wise and famous, became a
king on earth known as Brihanta. The best of the asuras known as Tuhunda became a
lord of men known as Senabindu. The one named Isripa, the strongest of those
asuras, became a king named Papajit, of famous courage on earth. The great asura
known as Ekachakra became the famous Prativindhya on earth. Virupaksha, a daitya,
a great asura of many ways of fighting, became a king on earth known as
Chitravarman. The hero Hara, the best of the danavas, who took away his enemies,
was born as a bull among men known as Suvastu. Ahara, of great splendour, who
destroyed the side of his enemies, became a famous king on earth named Bahlika.

Nichandra, the best of the asuras, whose face was like the moon, became a
glorious king known as Munjakesha. Nikumbha, unconquered in battle, was born on
earth as a king of great mind, the best of kings, remembered as Devadhipa. The one
named Sharabha, the great asura among those daityas, became a royal seer among men
here named Paurava. The second, Shalabha, among those asuras, became a lord of men,
a Bahlika named Prahrada. Chandra, the best of the sons of Diti, like the lord of
the stars in the world, became a royal seer named Rishika, the best of kings.

The best of the asuras known as Mritapa, know him, best of kings, as the king
Pashchimanupaka. The great asura of great splendour, known as Gavishtha, became a
king on earth known as Drumasena. The glorious great asura known as Mayura became a
lord of the earth known as Vishva. His younger brother, known as Suparna, became a
king on earth known as Kalakirti. The chief asura who was called Chandrahantri
became a royal seer, a lord of men, named Shunaka. The great asura called the
Destroyer of the Moon became a royal seer, a lord of men, named Janaki. The bull
among the danavas who was called Dirghajihva, Kauravya, became a lord of the earth
known as the king of Kashi. The planet that Simhi bore, the tormentor of the moon
and the sun, became a lord of men known as Kratha.

The eldest asura of the four sons of Anayu, bright Vikshara, became the king
Vasumitra. The second, the great asura after Vikshara, lord of men, became a king
famous as the lord of the Pamsu kingdom. The best of the asuras known as Balavira
became a lord of men, Paundramatsyaka. The great asura known as Vritra, king,
became a royal seer, a lord of men, named Manimat. His younger brother, the asura
Krodhahantri, became a king on earth known as Danda. The other, called
Krodhavardhana, became a lord of men known as Dandadhara.

Of the sons of Kalaka, eight became lords of men, tiger among kings, with the
courage of tigers. In Magadha the glorious Jayatsena became king, the eldest of
those eight Kaleyas, a great asura. The second of them, as glorious as Indra,
became the lord of men named Aparajita. The third, great king, a strong-armed
great asura, was born as the king of the Nishadas, of terrible valour on earth.
Another of them, called the fourth, became famous on earth as Shrenimat, the best
of royal seers. The fifth, the best of them, a great asura, became famous here as
Mahaujas, the scorcher of enemies. The sixth, a wise great asura among them, became
famous on earth as Abhiru, the best of royal seers. And King Samudrasena came from
that same host, famous on the earth that ends at the sea, who knew the truth of
dharma and of wealth. The eighth of the Kaleyas, named Brihat, king, was a
righteous scorcher of enemies, devoted to the good of all beings.

---

I named to you, king, the host called the Krodhavashas. From them heroic lords
of men were born here on earth. They were Nandika, Karnaveshta, Siddhartha and
Kitaka, Suvira and Subahu, and the great hero Bahlika. They were Krodha,
Vichitya, Surasa and glorious King Nila, Viradhaman, Kauravya, and the one named
Bhumipala. There was the one named Dantavaktra, and the one named Durjaya. There
was Rukmin, tiger among kings, and King Janamejaya. There were Ashadha, Vayuvega
and Bhuritejas, Ekalavya, Sumitra, Vatadhana and Gomukha. There were the kings of
the Karushas, and Kshemadhurti, Shrutayus, Uddhava and Brihatsena. There were
Kshemogratirtha, and Kuhara, lord of men among the Kalingas, and Matimat, lord of
men, and the one known as Ishvara. So a host of kings was born on earth long ago
from the Krodhavashas, great king, of great fame and great strength.

The one called Devaka, as bright as the king of the gods, the chief lord of the
gandharvas, was born on earth as a lord of men.

Know, Bharata, that Drona, the son of Bharadvaja, came from a portion of the
famous divine seer Brihaspati. He was not born from a womb. Among archers, tiger
among kings, he knew all weapons best. That famous man, of great splendour, was
born here among men. Those who know the Veda know Drona as the best in the Veda
and in the science of the bow. His deeds were like Indra's, and he made his own
family great.

Mahadeva and Death, desire and anger, were joined together as one, Bharata. From
them was born the hero Ashvatthaman, the scorcher of enemies. He lived on earth,
lord of men, a hero of great courage with eyes like lotus petals, a destroyer of
the enemy's side.

The eight Vasus were born to Ganga as the sons of Shantanu, by the curse of
Vasishtha and by the command of Vasava. The youngest of them was Bhishma, who made
the Kurus safe. He was wise and eloquent, he knew the Veda, and he destroyed the
enemy's side. He was the best of all the wise, of great splendour. He fought with
the great Rama Jamadagnya, the son of Bhrigu.

There was a brahmin seer on earth named Kripa, king, of great manhood. Know that
he came from the host of the Rudras. There was a king named Shakuni, a great
warrior in the world, the crusher of enemies. Know him, king, as Dvapara born.
Truthful Satyaki carried on the line of the Vrishnis. That crusher of enemies was
born from the side of the gods called the Maruts. And the royal seer Drupada came from that same host,
in this world of men, king, the best of all who bear weapons. From there too, know,
king, came King Kritavarma, the best of bulls among the kshatriyas. His deeds had
no equal. And the royal seer named Virata, who scorched the kingdoms of his
enemies, was born from the host of the Maruts.

The son of Arishta known as Hamsa was the lord of the gandharvas. He was born to
make the Kuru line grow, as Dhritarashtra, the son of Krishna Dvaipayana. He was a
long-armed lord of men of great splendour, whose eyes were his wisdom. Because of
his mother's fault and the seer's anger, he was born blind.

Know that the very fortunate son of Atri, the best of those who have sons, was
born in this world as Vidura, the best of the wise.

From a portion of Kali was born King Duryodhana on earth, of wicked mind and evil
thoughts, the disgrace of the Kurus. That man of Kali was hated by the whole
world. That lowest of men had the whole earth killed. By him the enmity was kindled
that made an end of beings.

All the brothers born of Pulastya were born among men here: the hundred, with
Duhshasana first, all of them cruel in their deeds. Durmukha and Duhsaha, and the
others not named, were Duryodhana's helpers, the sons of Pulastya, bull of the
Bharatas.

Know, king, that King Yudhishthira was a portion of Dharma, Bhimasena of the Wind,
and Arjuna of the king of the gods. And in the same way Nakula and Sahadeva, of
beauty without equal on earth, who charmed the whole world, were portions of the
Ashvins.

The mighty son of Soma known as Suvarchas became Abhimanyu, of great fame, the
son of Arjuna. Know that Dhrishtadyumna, the great warrior, was a portion of
Agni, and know, king, that Shikhandi, both woman and man, was a rakshasa. The
five sons of Draupadi, bull of the Bharatas, know them, king, as the hosts of
the Vishvadevas.

Karna, the great warrior, who was born wearing his armour, know him as a portion of
the god Divakara, the Sun, beyond compare.

He who is called Narayana, the god of gods, the eternal: his portion among men was
mighty Vasudeva. Mighty Baladeva was a portion of the naga Shesha. And know, king,
that powerful Pradyumna was Sanatkumara. So, lord of men, many other portions of
the dwellers of heaven were born in the family of Vasudeva, making it grow.

I named to you, king, the host of the apsarases. A share of them was born on
earth by Vasava's command. Those sixteen thousand goddesses, lord of men, were born in the
world of men as the wives of Narayana.

A share of Shri was born on the surface of the earth for love, in Drupada's
family, from the middle of the altar: a girl without fault. She was neither too
short nor too tall, and she smelled of the blue lotus. Her eyes were long like
lotuses, her hips were lovely, and her hair was black and long. She had every good
mark, and she shone like beryl. In private she stirred the hearts of five lords
among men.

The two goddesses Siddhi and Dhriti were born as Kunti and Madri, the mothers of
the five. And Mati was born as the daughter of Subala.

So, king, I have told you how portions of the gods and the asuras came down to
earth, and of the gandharvas, the apsarases and the rakshasas. I have told you of
the kings born on earth, mad for battle, and of the great ones born in the wide
family of the Yadus. This account
of the descent of the portions should be heard without envy. It brings wealth, fame,
sons, long life and victory. The wise man who has heard the descent of the portions
of the gods, the gandharvas and the rakshasas knows the origin and the end of
things, and he does not sink down in hard times.

**Choices and puzzles kept.**

* **Dhritarashtra** is the gandharva Hamsa, son of Arishta (1.61.77–78). His
  blindness is "because of his mother's fault and the seer's anger". The story
  is told at 1.100.
* **Vidura** is here "the son of Atri" (1.61.79, *atres tu … putram*), while
  1.57.81 makes him Dharma born by Animandavya's curse. The CE has both. The
  novel keeps both and does not reconcile them.
* **Another Janamejaya.** A King Janamejaya is named among the Krodhavashas
  (1.61.57). He is not the listener of this story.
* **Ekalavya** is also among the Krodhavashas (1.61.58).
* **Shikhandi** is "both woman and man, a rakshasa" (1.61.87, *strīpuṃsaṃ …
  rākṣasam*).
* **Duryodhana** is a portion of Kali (1.61.80–81), the spirit of the losing
  throw at dice and of the last age. **Shakuni** is Dvapara (1.61.72). The
  hundred brothers are "born of Pulastya", i.e. rakshasas (1.61.82–83).
* **Ashvatthaman** is from Mahadeva and Death, desire and anger joined (1.61.66).
* **Draupadi** is a share of Shri, "for love" (*ratyartham*, 1.61.95).
* **The mothers.** Kunti and Madri are Siddhi and Dhriti. Gandhari, "the
  daughter of Subala", is Mati (1.61.98).
* 1.61.29 *prahrādo nāma bāhlīkaḥ*: a Bahlika king named Prahrada, not the
  asura Prahrada.
* 1.61.36 *vināśanas tu candrasya*: "the destroyer of the Moon", rendered as a
  title.
* 1.61.38: Rahu, "the planet that Simhi bore", became Kratha.
* 1.61.94: the sixteen thousand goddesses as Narayana's wives. This is the
  text's statement, and the novel keeps it.
* Some kings here share names with other people in the epic (Bahlika,
  Prativindhya, Ugrasena). They are different men. The novel does not merge
  them.

# The Blindfold and the Mantra

"This famous family of ours is rich in every virtue," said Bhishma. "It has held
rule over the earth, above all other kings. It was protected by kings before us,
great men who knew dharma, and it has never died out here. Satyavati and I and
the great Krishna have set it up again in you, the threads of the family. It
must grow like the ocean, son. I must see to that, and you above all.

"I hear of a Yadava girl who would suit our family, and of a daughter of Subala,
and one of the lord of Madra. They are all well born and beautiful, and they
have protectors. Those bulls among kshatriyas are fit to be joined to us. I
think we should ask for them, wisest of the wise, for the sake of this family's
line. Or what do you think, Vidura?"

"You are our father," said Vidura, "and our mother, and our highest teacher. So
think it over yourself, and do what is good for this family."

Then Bhishma heard from brahmins about Gandhari, Subala's daughter. She had
worshipped Hara, the giver of boons, who took away Bhaga's eyes. And the lovely
Gandhari, so they said, had won the boon of a hundred sons. When Bhishma,
grandfather of the Kurus, had heard this for certain, he sent messengers to the
king of Gandhara, Bharata.

Subala hesitated, because Dhritarashtra was blind. But he weighed the family,
its fame and its conduct in his mind, and he gave Gandhari, who kept to dharma,
to Dhritarashtra.

Gandhari heard that Dhritarashtra was blind, and that her father and mother
meant to give her to him, Bharata. Then the lovely girl took a cloth, folded it
many times, and bound it over her own eyes, king. She was wholly devoted to her
husband. She had made up her mind: *I will not have more than my husband has.*

Then the king of Gandhara's son, Shakuni, came to the Kauravas, bringing his
sister, who was richly adorned. The hero gave his sister away with a fitting
train of servants, and then, honoured by Bhishma, he went back to his own city.

Gandhari of the lovely hips pleased all the Kurus by her character, her conduct
and her ways, Bharata. She won them all by her behaviour, and she was wholly
devoted to her husband. She kept her vows so well that she would not even speak
of other men.

---

There was a chief of the Yadus named Shura, the father of Vasudeva. He had a
daughter named Pritha, whose beauty had no equal on earth. Shura had promised
his first child to his cousin, his father's sister's son, who had no children.
So the mighty man gave the girl, as his first-born, to the great Kuntibhoja, a
friend to a friend, for Kuntibhoja had longed for the favour of that first
child.

In her father's house she was given the task of honouring the gods and the
guests. There she waited on a fierce and terrible brahmin of strict vows, whose
purposes in dharma were hidden. People know him as Durvasas. She pleased that
fierce, disciplined man with every effort. Looking to the dharma for times of
trouble, the sage gave her a mantra, joined with the power of a spell. And he
said to her, "Whatever god you call with this mantra, by his grace you will have
a son."

When the brahmin had said this, the famous girl was curious. While she was still
a virgin, she called the god, the Sun. She saw him coming, the Sun who makes the
world come to be. And the girl of flawless limbs was amazed to see that great
wonder.

The Sun, the Heater, whose work is light, put a child in her. Then she bore a
hero, the best of all who bear weapons. He was a god's child, glorious and
wrapped in splendour, and he wore armour. It was armour he was born with, and
his face was lit by earrings. That son was Karna, famous in all the worlds.

Then the Sun, of the highest brilliance, gave her back her virginity. And when
he had given it, the best of givers went up to heaven.

For fear of her family, Kunti hid what she had done. She let the boy, with all
his lucky marks, go on the water. The famous husband of Radha, a son of the
sutas, took the abandoned child, and he and his wife made him their son. The two
of them named the boy. "He was born with wealth, *vasu*," they said. "Let him be
Vasushena."

As he grew he became strong, and he gave himself to every weapon. The mighty
youth worshipped the Sun until it burned his back. And at the time when that
hero, true to his word, sat reciting his prayers, there was nothing the great
man would not give to a brahmin.

Indra, who makes all beings, took the form of a brahmin, and came to beg of him.
The splendid god asked for the earrings and the armour. Karna, dismayed, cut the
armour from his own body, streaming with blood. He cut off the earrings too, and
he gave them with joined hands.

Shakra was amazed. He gave him a spear, and said, "Whoever you throw this at in
anger, god, asura or man, gandharva, snake or rakshasa, he will not live."

Before, his name had been Vasushena, as we have heard. Then, because of that
deed, he became Vaikartana, the Cutter: Karna.

**Divergences and the CE's own doublets.**

* **The blindfold's reason.** The CE gives it in her own resolve: *nātyaśnīyāṃ patim
  aham*, "I will not surpass my husband" (1.103.13). See ledger L-74.
* **No ten sisters.** The vulgate has Subala give Gandhari's ten sisters to
  Dhritarashtra as well (\*1113). The CE does not. Dhritarashtra's other wife, the
  vaishya woman who bears Yuyutsu, comes at 1.107.35–36.
* **Karna's story in brief.** Book 1 tells Karna's birth and the loss of his armour in
  a few verses. The long telling, with the Sun's argument with Pritha, the basket on
  the river, the Sun's warning in a dream and Indra's bargain, is in Book 3
  (3.284–294). The vulgate adds pieces of it here (App. I Nos. 58–60, \*1119, \*1125);
  the novel leaves them for Book 3.
* **Radha's husband unnamed.** The CE here calls him only "Radha's husband, a son of
  the sutas" (1.104.14). His name, Adhiratha, comes later.

**Choices.**

* 1.103.3 *kṛṣṇena*: Krishna Dvaipayana, that is, Vyasa.
* 1.103.6 *nāthavatyaḥ*: "who have protectors", i.e. families able to defend them.
* 1.103.9 *bhaganetraharaṃ haram*: "Hara, who took away Bhaga's eyes", an epithet of
  Shiva.
* 1.103.14 *parayā lakṣmyā yuktām*: "endowed with the highest splendour"; rendered
  "richly adorned".
* 1.104.2 *paitṛṣvaseyāya*: "to his father's sister's son", Kuntibhoja.
* 1.104.6 *abhicārābhisaṃyuktam*: a mantra "joined with the power of compelling", a
  spell that summons.
* 1.104.10 *prakāśakarmā tapanaḥ*: "the Heater, whose work is light".
* 1.104.16 *ā pṛṣṭhatāpāt*: "until the heat on his back", i.e. from morning until the
  sun had moved round behind him.
* 1.104.19 *vimanāḥ*: "dismayed", "downcast". Kept.
* 1.104.21 *vaikartana*: from *vi-kṛt*, "to cut off". The CE's etymology.

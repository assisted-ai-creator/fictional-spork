# Vidura's Riddle

The Pandavas yoked their chariots with fine horses as swift as the wind. As they
mounted, they clasped Bhishma's feet in sorrow, and the feet of King
Dhritarashtra, of great Drona, of the other elders, of Vidura and of Kripa. So
those men of strict vows bowed to all the Kuru elders. They embraced their
equals, and the children bowed to them. They took leave of all the mothers,
walked round them with respect, and took leave of all the people. Then they set
out for Varanavata.

Wise Vidura, the other bulls of the Kurus and the townspeople followed the
tigers among men, worn thin with grief. There some of the brahmins spoke out
fearlessly, deeply grieved for the sons of Pandu, bull of the Bharatas.

"The king does not see straight," they said. "He is lost in darkness.
Dhritarashtra's mind is wicked, and he has no eye for dharma. The sinless
Pandava will never take to wrongdoing, nor Bhima, the strongest of the strong,
nor Kunti's son Dhananjaya. How much less would Madri's two wise sons?
Dhritarashtra cannot bear that they should have the kingdom that came to them
from their father. How can Bhishma allow such a wrong, when the Kaunteyas, bulls
of the Bharatas, are sent away for no reason? Shantanu's son Vichitravirya, the
royal seer, was like a father to us, and so was Pandu, the joy of the Kurus. Now
that tiger among men has gone the way of fate, and Dhritarashtra cannot bear
these young princes. We cannot bear it either. Let us all leave our homes in
this finest of cities and go where Yudhishthira goes."

Yudhishthira, the king of dharma, was worn thin with grief himself. But he was
deeply moved, and he said to the grieving townspeople, "The king, the lord of
the earth, is our father, to be honoured, our teacher and our elder. Whatever he
says, we must do without suspicion. That is our vow. You are our friends. Walk
round us, bless us, and go back to your homes. When there is something we need
from you, then you will do what is dear and good to me."

"So be it," they promised. They walked round them, blessed them, and went back
to the city.

---

When the townspeople had turned back, Vidura, who knew every dharma, spoke to
the eldest Pandava to warn him. The wise man, who knew the talk of riddles,
spoke to the wise one, seeing clearly what dharma and prudence required.

"A man who understands this should act so as to escape danger. There is a sharp
weapon, not made of iron, that cuts the body. Enemies cannot kill the man who
knows it, for he knows how to ward it off. The eater of dry grass and the
destroyer of cold does not burn the creature that lives in a hole in the great
thicket. The man who protects himself like that lives. A man without eyes does
not know the road. A man without eyes does not find the directions. A man
without steadiness does not win good fortune. You have been warned. Understand.
A man may take a weapon not made of iron from those he cannot trust. The
porcupine escapes the fire by going into its burrow. By wandering, a man comes
to know the paths. By the stars he finds his directions. He who masters his five
by himself is not overcome."

When he had instructed them and gone some way with them, Vidura walked round
them, gave the Pandavas leave to go, and went home.

When Vidura, Bhishma and the townspeople had turned back, Kunti spoke to
Ajatashatru. "The steward said something in the middle of the crowd, as if he
were not saying it, and you answered 'So be it.' We did not understand it. If we
may hear it, and there is no harm in it, I want to hear all that passed between
you and him."

"Vidura told me to beware of poison and of fire," said Yudhishthira. "He said
that no road should be unknown to us. And he said that if I master my senses I
will win the earth. I told Vidura that I had understood it all."

They set out on the eighth day of Phalguna, under the constellation Rohini. And
when they reached Varanavata, they saw the people of the town.

---

When they heard that the Pandavas had come, all the people of the town of
Varanavata, with every lucky sign, as the books lay down, eagerly came out to
meet the best of men, in thousands, in every kind of carriage, full of joy. The
people of Varanavata came up to the Kaunteyas, cried blessings of victory over
them, and stood round them. Surrounded by them, Yudhishthira, the king of
dharma, the tiger among men, shone like a god, like Indra with the thunderbolt
in his hand among the immortals.

The sinless ones were honoured by the townspeople, and they honoured them in
turn. Then they entered crowded Varanavata, decked out for them. When the heroes
had entered the town, they went quickly to the houses of the brahmins who were
devoted to their duties, lord of the earth. The best of men visited the houses
of the town's officers, and of the chariot fighters, and the houses of the
vaishyas and shudras too.

When the townsfolk had honoured them, the Pandavas, bulls of the Bharatas, went
to their lodging, with Purochana leading the way. Purochana gave them food,
drink and delicacies, fine beds and the best seats. They stayed there, honoured
by him, with very costly furnishings, waited on by the people of the town.

When they had stayed there ten nights, Purochana told them about the house
called Auspicious, which was not auspicious. At Purochana's word the tigers
among men went into it with their household, like guhyakas going into Kailasa.

But Yudhishthira, who knew every dharma, looked the house over, and said to
Bhimasena, "It is made to burn. I can smell fat, gentle brother, and ghee mixed
with lac. This house has clearly been built to burn, tamer of enemies. Hemp and
resin have plainly been brought in to build it. The grass, the rushes, the
bamboo and everything are soaked in ghee. It has been built well by trusted
craftsmen, skilled in building. This wicked Purochana wants to burn me when I
trust him. Vidura, the wise, saw this danger then, Partha, and that is why he
warned me before. We have been warned by him, and we know the house is evil. It
has been built by skilled men in secret, obeying Duryodhana."

"If you think this house is made to burn," said Bhima, "let us go back to where
we were staying before."

"I think we should stay here," said Yudhishthira, "on our guard, showing
nothing, as if we were lost, while we look for a sure way to get out. If
Purochana sees any sign in us, he will act quickly and burn us even by force.
Purochana has no fear of blame or of wrongdoing. That is how the fool behaves,
keeping to Suyodhana's plan.

"And if we were burned here, would our grandfather Bhishma be angry? Why would
he anger the Kauravas? Perhaps he and the other bulls of the Kurus would be
angry for dharma's sake. But if we ran away for fear of burning, Suyodhana,
greedy for the kingdom, would have us all killed by his spies. He is in power
and we are not. He has allies and we have none. He has a great treasury and we
have nothing. He would surely have us killed by his schemes.

"So we must deceive this wicked man, and wicked Suyodhana too, and live in
hiding here and there. We will go about the earth as hunters. Then, if we have
to flee, we will know the paths. And today we will dig a tunnel in the ground,
well hidden. If we keep our breathing hidden there, the fire will not burn us.
We must work tirelessly, so that neither Purochana nor any of the townspeople
knows that we are living here like this."

**Divergences.**

* **"Mleccha speech."** At 1.133.18 the CE says only that Vidura, "who knew riddling
  talk" (*pralāpajña*), spoke to Yudhishthira. Later the miner says that Vidura spoke
  "in mleccha speech" (*mlecchavācā*, 1.135.6). Ganguli puts "the Mlechchha tongue"
  here as well (G 1.147). The novel follows each CE passage where it stands.
* The vulgate has Bhima propose to burn Purochana and leave (\*1467) and gives him a
  longer speech (App. I No. 84). The CE's Bhima says only "let us go back".

**Choices.**

* 1.133.19 *alohaṃ niśitaṃ śastram*: "a sharp weapon not of iron", i.e. fire.
* 1.133.20 *kakṣaghnaḥ śiśiraghnaḥ*: "the destroyer of dry grass and of cold", i.e.
  fire; *bilaukasaḥ*, "the hole-dweller", who escapes it underground.
* 1.133.23 *ātmanā cātmanaḥ pañca pīḍayan*: "restraining one's own five", the senses.
* 1.133.26 *janamadhye 'bruvann iva*: "in the midst of the people, as if not speaking".
* 1.133.30: "the eighth day (of the bright or dark half) of Phalguna, under Rohini".
  The text's date, as it stands.
* 1.134.11 *śivākhyam aśivam*: "called Auspicious, but inauspicious".
* 1.134.12 *kailāsam iva guhyakāḥ*: the guhyakas are Kubera's attendants, whose home
  is Mount Kailasa.
* 1.134.19 *nirākāraiḥ*: "without showing any sign".
* 1.134.24: the three contrasts (position, allies, treasury) are Yudhishthira's.

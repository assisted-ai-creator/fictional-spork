# The Line of Puru

"Blessed one," said Janamejaya, "I want to hear about the kings who carried on
Puru's line: how brave they were, what kind of men, how many, and how valiant.
For in this line there has never been a king without good character, or without
courage, or without children. I want to hear in full, rich in austerity, the
lives of those famous kings, so rich in wisdom."

Then I will tell you, since you ask, of the heroes who carried on Puru's line,
as bright as Shakra.

Three sons were born to Puru by Paushti, all great warriors: Pravira, Ishvara
and Raudrashva. Of them, Pravira carried on the line. From him was born the lord
Manasyu, the son of Shyeni, a hero with lotus eyes, who protected the earth to
its four ends. Manasyu had three sons by Sauviri, all heroes and great warriors:
Subhru, Samhanana and Vagmin.

Raudrashva had ten sons by an apsaras. They were great archers, heroes, men who
offered sacrifices, with many children and much learning, all of them skilled in
every weapon and all devoted to dharma: Richepu, Kakshepu and mighty Krikanepu,
Sthandilepu, Vanepu and the great warrior Sthalepu, strong and wise Tejepu,
Satyepu with the courage of Indra, Dharmepu, and Samnatepu, the tenth, with the
courage of a god. These sons of Anadhrishti, my friend, performed the royal
consecration and the horse sacrifice.

Then the learned King Matinara was born, the son of Richepu. Matinara had four
sons, king, of boundless courage: Tamsu, Mahat, Atiratha and Druhyu of matchless
splendour. Of them, mighty Tamsu carried on the Paurava line, won blazing fame
and conquered the earth. Mighty Tamsu fathered a son, Ilina, and he too, the
best of conquerors, conquered this whole earth. Then Ilina fathered five sons on
Rathantari, like the five elements, king, with Dushyanta the first: Dushyanta,
Shura and Bhima, Prapurva and Vasu. The eldest of them, Dushyanta, became king,
Janamejaya.

From Dushyanta was born the learned King Bharata, the son of Shakuntala. From
him the great fame of the Bharata line spread. Bharata fathered nine sons on
three wives. The king did not welcome them. "They are not like me," he said.
Then Bharata offered great sacrifices, and he won a son, named Bhumanyu, from
Bharadvaja, Bharata. Then, knowing that he had a son, the joy of the Pauravas
consecrated Bhumanyu as crown prince, best of the Bharatas.

Then that great king had a son, Vitatha. Then Vitatha was born as the son of
Bhumanyu. Bhumanyu had sons by Pushkarini: Suhotra and Suhotri, Suhavis and
Suyajus. The eldest of them, Suhotra, won the kingdom of the lords of the earth.
He sacrificed with many offerings, the royal consecration, the horse sacrifice
and others. Suhotra enjoyed the whole earth, clothed by the ocean, full of
elephants, cattle and horses, and crowded with jewels. The earth almost sank
under him, weighed down by its great load, full of elephants, horses and
chariots, and crowded with people. While King Suhotra ruled his people by
dharma, the land was marked by shrines and sacrificial posts by the hundred
thousand. With her people and her crops thriving, and her gods with her, the
earth shone.

Aikshvaki bore to Suhotra, the lord of the earth, Ajamidha, Sumidha and
Purumidha, Bharata. Ajamidha was the best of them, and the line rested on him.
He too fathered six sons, on three wives, Bharata. Dhumini bore Riksha. Nili
bore Dushyanta and Parameshthin. Keshini bore Jahnu, and the two, Jana and
Rupina. All these Panchalas come from Dushyanta and Parameshthin, and the
Kushikas come from Jahnu of boundless splendour, king. They call Riksha the
eldest, the lord of the people, older than Jana and Rupina. From Riksha was born
Samvarana, king, who carried on your line.

---

While Samvarana, the son of Riksha, ruled the earth, king, there was a very
great destruction of his people. So we have heard. Then the kingdom broke apart,
struck by ruin of many kinds, by hunger and death, by drought and by sickness.
The armies of enemies struck at the Bharatas. Shaking the earth with his army of
four divisions, the king of the Panchalas, who had conquered the earth by force,
marched against him, and defeated him in battle with ten akshauhinis.

Then King Samvarana fled from that great danger, with his wives, his ministers,
his sons and his friends. He lived then in a thicket by the great river Sindhu,
near a mountain at the edge of the river country. The Bharatas lived there for a
long time, sheltered in a stronghold.

When they had lived there a thousand years, the blessed seer Vasishtha came to
the Bharatas. They all went out eagerly to meet him and bowed to him, and the
Bharatas brought him the guest offering. They offered everything to the bright
seer with honour. When he had lived there eight years, the king himself chose
him. "Be our family priest. We are striving for our kingdom."

"Om," said Vasishtha, and he agreed to serve the Bharatas. Then, so we have
heard, he consecrated the Paurava as emperor over all the kshatriyas, the horn
of the whole earth. He took again the fine city where Bharata had lived before,
and he made all the kings of the earth pay him tribute once more. Then the
mighty descendant of Ajamidha, having won back the earth, sacrificed again with
many great sacrifices, with rich gifts to the priests.

Then Tapati, the daughter of the sun, bore Kuru to Samvarana. All his people
chose him as king, for he knew dharma. The land of Kurujangala on earth is
famous by his name. That great ascetic made Kurukshetra holy by his austerity.
We have heard that his clever wife Vahini bore him five sons: Ashvavat,
Abhishvat, Chitraratha, Muni and the famous Janamejaya.

Abhishvat's sons were Parikshit and mighty Shabalashva, Abhiraja and Viraja, and
mighty Shalmala, Uccaihshravas, Bhadrakara, and Jitari, remembered as the
eighth. In their line there were men famous for the virtues their deeds brought
them. Parikshit's sons were Janamejaya and six more, mighty men, all of them
skilled in dharma and in wealth: Kakshasena and Ugrasena, mighty Chitrasena,
Indrasena, Sushena, and the one named Bhimasena.

Janamejaya's sons were mighty and famous on earth: Dhritarashtra, the
first-born, Pandu and Bahlika, Nishadha of great splendour, and mighty
Jambunada, Kundodara, Padati, and Vasati, remembered as the eighth. All of them
were skilled in dharma and in wealth, and all of them delighted in the good of
all beings. Then Dhritarashtra became king. His sons were Kundika, Hastin,
Vitarka, Kratha and Kundala the fifth, Havihshravas, Indrabha, and Sumanyu the
unconquered.

Pratipa had three sons, bull of the Bharatas: Devapi, Shantanu, and Bahlika, the
great warrior. Devapi went forth from the world, seeking dharma. Shantanu and
the great warrior Bahlika won the earth.

Many strong and great warriors were born in the line of Bharata, king, many of
the best of kings, like divine seers. And there were others like them, like
gods, great warriors, born in the line of Manu, who made the line of Ila grow.

**Puzzles the CE leaves as they are.**

* **Earlier Dhritarashtra, Pandu, Parikshit and Janamejaya.** This line includes
  a Parikshit, a Janamejaya and a Dhritarashtra, Pandu and Bahlika who lived long
  before the heroes of the story (1.89.45–51). They are different men who share
  the names.
* **Bharata's heir.** Here Bharata rejects his nine sons and gets Bhumanyu "from
  Bharadvaja" by sacrifice (1.89.17–18). The next chapter's prose genealogy makes
  Bhumanyu Bharata's son by Sunanda (1.90.34). Both are kept.
* 1.89.10 *anādhṛṣṭisutāḥ*: the ten are called "sons of Anadhrishti", apparently
  another name of Raudrashva.
* 1.89.11 *carceputaḥ*: read as *ṛceputaḥ*, "son of Richepu".
* 1.89.15 *janamejaya*: a vocative. Vaishampayana is addressing his listener.
* 1.89.20: Vitatha is named twice, once as "that great king's son" and once as
  Bhumanyu's son. Kept.
* 1.89.21 *puṣkariṇyām ṛcīkasya*: "by Pushkarini, (of) Richika". The genitive is
  unclear, and the novel leaves out Richika rather than guess.
* 1.89.28: Ajamidha's six sons are counted as Riksha, Dushyanta, Parameshthin,
  Jahnu, Jana and Rupina.
* 1.89.38 *samām aṣṭamīm uṣṭam*: Vasishtha is chosen "when he had stayed eight
  years".
* 1.89.39 *viṣāṇabhūtam*: the Paurava as "the horn" (the chief) of the earth.
* 1.89.51–52: the text does not say how Pratipa descends from the kings just
  named. The next chapter gives a different route (1.90.43–46).

# The Teacher's Fee

Then Drona called together all his pupils, every one of them, lord of the earth,
and asked them for his teacher's fee. "Capture Drupada, the king of the
Panchalas, at the head of his army, and bring him to me. Bless you. That will be
my highest fee."

"So be it," they all said. And those fighters quickly set off in their chariots
with Drona to pay their teacher's fee. The bulls among men marched on the
Panchalas, killing as they went, and they crushed the city of mighty Drupada.
Those bulls of the Bharatas captured Yajnasena Drupada at the head of his army,
with his ministers, and brought him to Drona.

His pride was broken, his wealth taken, and he was in Drona's power. Drona,
thinking of their old quarrel, said to Drupada, "I have crushed your kingdom by
force and trampled your city. Your life is in your enemy's hands. Do you want
our old friendship now?"

When he had said this, he laughed at him. Then he made up his mind and spoke
again. "Do not be afraid for your life, king. We brahmins are forgiving. When we
were boys, you played with me in the hermitage, and that made my love for you
grow, bull of the kshatriyas. I ask again for your friendship, bull among men. I
give you a gift, king. Take half the kingdom. A man who is not a king, it seems,
cannot be the friend of a king. So I have made an effort for your kingdom,
Yajnasena. You are king on the south bank of the Bhagirathi, and I am king on
the north. Know that I am your friend, Panchala, if you think fit."

"This is no wonder, brahmin," said Drupada, "in great men who are brave. I am
pleased with you, and I want your affection for ever."

When he said this, Drona set him free, Bharata. With a glad heart, he honoured
him and gave him half the kingdom. Drupada, cast down in spirit, lived in
Makandi, on the bank of the Ganga, a land full of people, and in Kampilya, the
finest of cities, and ruled the southern Panchalas as far as the river
Charmanvati.

But Drupada never forgot his quarrel with Drona, and it gave him no peace. He
could not see how to beat him with a kshatriya's strength. He knew he was weaker
than the brahmin in strength, and he longed for the birth of a son, and he held
to that. And Drona took the country of Ahicchatra. So, king, the city of
Ahicchatra, with its lands, was conquered in battle by Partha and handed over to
Drona.

---

When Duryodhana saw how strong Bhimasena was, and how learned Dhananjaya, the
wicked man burned with jealousy. Then Karna, the son of Vikartana, and Shakuni,
Subala's son, tried to kill the Pandavas by many means. And the Pandavas, tamers
of enemies, knew all about it. But they kept to Vidura's advice, and did not
bring it out into the open.

When the townspeople saw how the sons of Pandu shone with every good quality,
they gathered in the squares and the halls and talked about them.
"Dhritarashtra, whose eyes are his wisdom, did not get the kingdom before,
because he was blind. How can he be king now? And Bhishma, son of Shantanu, true
to his word and his great vow, gave up the kingdom long ago. He will never take
it now. So let us consecrate the eldest Pandava today, and rightly. He is young,
but he has the ways of the old. He is truthful, and he knows pity. He knows
dharma, and he will honour Bhishma, son of Shantanu, and Dhritarashtra and his
sons, and give them every comfort."

The wicked Duryodhana heard what they said, those people devoted to
Yudhishthira, and he was sick at heart. The evil man burned and could not bear
their words. Burning with jealousy, he went to Dhritarashtra. He found his
father alone, bowed to him, and then, burning with the people's love for the
Pandavas, he said:

"Father, I have heard the people of the city talking, and what they say bodes
ill. They pass over you, and Bhishma, and want the Pandava for their lord.
Bhishma agrees, for he does not want to be king. But the people in the city mean
to do us the greatest harm. Pandu won the kingdom from his father by his own
merits, long ago. You did not get the kingdom that came to you, because of a
defect. If the Pandava gets Pandu's inheritance, his son will surely get it
after him, and then that son's son, and so on. Then we and our sons will be cut
out of the royal line, lord of the earth, and despised by the world. Act
quickly, king, so that we do not fall for ever into hell, living on others'
food. If you had held the kingdom firmly before, king, we would surely have got
it, even if the people did not want us."

---

When he heard his son speak like this, Dhritarashtra, whose eyes were his
wisdom, thought for a moment. Then he said to Duryodhana:

"Pandu always kept to dharma. He was always kind to me, and good to me, as he
was to all his kinsmen, and to me above all. He hid nothing from me. I knew
everything he meant to do, down to his meals. Firm in his vows, he always
reported the kingdom to me. His son keeps to dharma just as Pandu did. He has
every good quality, he is famous in the world, and the townspeople think well of
him. How can we drive him out of his father's and grandfather's kingdom by
force, when he has so many to help him? Pandu always supported the ministers and
the army, and their sons and grandsons too. The people of the city were treated
well by Pandu, son. Why would they not kill us and all our kin for
Yudhishthira's sake?"

"I have thought of this danger myself, father," said Duryodhana. "I have seen to
it that all the subjects have been won over with money and honours. They will
surely be on our side, the leading men above all. The treasury and the ministers
are in my hands today, lord of the earth. So you should send the Pandavas away
quickly, by some gentle means, to the town of Varanavata. When the kingdom is
secure in my hands, king, then Kunti will come back with her children, Bharata."

"Duryodhana," said Dhritarashtra, "this is in my heart too. But because it is
wicked, I do not speak it aloud. And neither Bhishma, nor Drona, nor the
steward, nor Gautama will ever allow the Kaunteyas to be sent away. To the
Kauravas, son, we and they are equal. Those wise men, who keep to dharma, would
not want it to be unequal. So why would the Kauravas and these great men, and
the whole world, not want to put us to death, son?"

"Bhishma is always in the middle," said Duryodhana. "Drona's son is on my side.
Where the son is, there Drona will be, without doubt. And Kripa, Sharadvat's
son, will go where these three go. He will never leave Drona and his sister's
son. The steward is bound to us by money, though secretly he is with the others.
And alone he cannot stop us for the Pandavas' sake. Have no fears. Send Pandu's
sons away to Varanavata with their mother, this very day. No harm will come of
it. This sorrow is like a dagger planted in my heart. It is terrible, it keeps
me from sleeping, and it has lit a fire of grief in me. Put it out with this
deed."

**Divergences.**

* **No battle of the Kauravas first.** The vulgate expands the attack on Drupada:
  Duryodhana, Karna and the rest rush in first, are beaten back, and then Arjuna, who
  has held back with his brothers, captures Drupada (App. I No. 78). In the CE "they"
  capture him (1.128.5), and only at the end is Arjuna named as the conqueror
  (1.128.18).
* **No coronation of Yudhishthira as crown prince, and no Kanika.** The vulgate has
  Dhritarashtra install Yudhishthira as heir, the Pandavas win victories abroad, and
  the minister Kanika give Dhritarashtra his long lesson in crooked policy, with the
  fable of the jackal (App. I Nos. 80–81; G 1.139–142). None of this is in the CE.
  See ledger L-86.
* **No sacrifice for a son yet.** The vulgate tells here how Drupada gets
  Dhrishtadyumna and Draupadi from the fire (App. I No. 79). The CE tells it later, at
  1.155.

**Choices.**

* 1.128.11: Drona echoes Drupada's own words, "one who is not a king cannot be a king's
  friend" (1.122.9).
* 1.128.12 *bhāgīrathyāḥ*: the Ganga. Drona takes the northern Panchala country.
* 1.128.17 *brāhmaṇena balena*: "in brahmin's strength", i.e. than Drona. Drupada
  "held to" (*adhārayat*) his wish for a son.
* 1.129.2: repeats 1.119.42–43 almost word for word. Kept.
* 1.129.14 *aguṇasaṃyogāt*: "because of a defect", Dhritarashtra's blindness.
* 1.129.17 *parapiṇḍopajīvinaḥ*: "living on another's rice-balls", on others' charity.
* 1.130.3 *bhojanādi*: "down to his meals". Pandu kept nothing from Dhritarashtra.
* 1.130.17 *bhāgineyam*: Ashvatthama, Kripa's sister's son.
* 1.130.18 *arthabaddhaḥ*: Vidura is "bound by wealth" to the Kauravas, that is, he
  depends on them for his living.

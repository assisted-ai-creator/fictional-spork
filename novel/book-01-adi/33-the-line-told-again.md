# The Line Told Again

"I have heard from you, brahmin," said Janamejaya, "the great story of how my
forebears came to be, and I have heard of the noble kings in this line. But a
lovely tale told briefly does not satisfy me. So tell it again, in full. Who
would not delight in this heavenly story of their holy births, from the lord of
creatures and from Manu? Their fame stands, wide and great, holding up the three
worlds, grown by the greatness of their true dharma and their virtues. They had
virtue and power, courage and strength, goodness and spirit, and as I hear their
story, which tastes like the nectar of immortality, I cannot have enough of it."

Listen, king, to the whole of this fine account of your own line, as I heard it
rightly long ago from Dvaipayana.

From Daksha came Aditi. From Aditi came Vivasvat. From Vivasvat came Manu. From
Manu came Ila. From Ila came Pururavas. From Pururavas came Ayus. From Ayus came
Nahusha. From Nahusha came Yayati.

Yayati had two wives, Devayani, the daughter of Ushanas, and Sharmishtha, the
daughter of Vrishaparvan. Here there is a verse of the line:

*Devayani bore Yadu and Turvasu. Sharmishtha, daughter of Vrishaparvan, bore
Druhyu, Anu and Puru.*

From Yadu came the Yadavas. From Puru came the Pauravas.

Puru's wife was named Kausalya. On her he fathered Janamejaya, who performed
three horse sacrifices, and after he had offered the sacrifice called Vishvajit,
went into the forest.

Janamejaya married Ananta, a woman of the Madhavas. On her he fathered
Prachinvat, who conquered the eastern quarter as far as the place where the sun
rises. That is why he is called Prachinvat, the Conqueror of the East.

Prachinvat married a woman of the Ashmakas. On her he fathered Samyati.

Samyati married Varangi, the daughter of Drishadvat. On her he fathered
Ahampati.

Ahampati married Bhanumati, the daughter of Kritavirya. On her he fathered
Sarvabhauma.

Sarvabhauma won Sunanda, a Kaikeyi princess, by conquest and carried her off. On
her he fathered Jayatsena.

Jayatsena married Sushuva, a princess of Vidarbha. On her he fathered Arachina.

Arachina too married a princess of Vidarbha, another, named Maryada. On her he
fathered Mahabhauma.

Mahabhauma married Suyajna, the daughter of Prasenajit. On her he fathered
Ayutanayin, who performed ten thousand human sacrifices. That is why he is
called Ayutanayin, the Bringer of Ten Thousand.

Ayutanayin married Bhasa, the daughter of Prithushravas. On her he fathered
Akrodhana.

Akrodhana married Karandu, a princess of Kalinga. On her he fathered Devatithi.

Devatithi married Maryada, a princess of Videha. On her he fathered Richa.

Richa married Sudeva, a princess of Anga. On her he fathered a son, Riksha.

Riksha married Jvala, the daughter of Takshaka. On her he fathered a son named
Matinara.

Matinara held a twelve-year sacrifice on the Sarasvati. When the sacrifice was
over, the river Sarasvati came to him and chose him as her husband. On her he
fathered a son named Tamsu. Here there is a verse of the line:

*Sarasvati bore Tamsu, the son of Matinara. Tamsu fathered a son, Ilina, on
Kalindi.*

Ilina fathered five sons on Rathantari, with Dushyanta the first.

Dushyanta married Shakuntala, the daughter of Vishvamitra. On her he fathered
Bharata. Here there are two verses:

*The mother is only the bellows. The son belongs to the father. The one who
fathers him, he is that one. Support your son, Dushyanta. Do not despise
Shakuntala. The man who places the seed raises his son up from the house of
Yama, god among men. You placed this child in the womb. Shakuntala speaks the
truth.*

That is why he was called Bharata.

Bharata married Sunanda, the daughter of Sarvasena of Kashi. On her he fathered
Bhumanyu.

Bhumanyu married Jaya, a woman of the Dasharhas. On her he fathered Suhotra.

Suhotra married Suvarna, a princess of the Ikshvakus. On her he fathered Hastin,
who laid out this city of Hastinapura. That is why it is called Hastinapura.

Hastin married Yashodhara, a princess of Trigarta. On her he fathered
Vikunthana.

Vikunthana married Sudeva, a woman of the Dasharhas. On her he fathered
Ajamidha.

Ajamidha had a hundred and twenty-four sons, by Kaikeyi, Naga, Gandhari, Vimala
and Riksha. They were kings who each founded a line of his own. Of them,
Samvarana carried on this line.

Samvarana married Tapati, the daughter of Vivasvat. On her he fathered Kuru.

Kuru married Shubhangi, a woman of the Dasharhas. On her he fathered Viduratha.

Viduratha married Samprija, a princess of Magadha. On her he fathered a son
named Arugvat.

Arugvat married Amrita, a princess of Magadha. On her he fathered Parikshit.

Parikshit married Suyasha, a woman of the Bahudas. On her he fathered Bhimasena.

Bhimasena married Sukumari, a Kaikeyi princess. On her he fathered Paryashravas,
whom they call Pratipa.

Pratipa married Sunanda, a princess of the Shibis. On her he fathered sons:
Devapi, Shantanu and Bahlika.

Devapi went into the forest while he was still a boy, and Shantanu became king.
Here there is a verse of the line:

*Whatever old man he touches with his hands is made happy and becomes young
again. That is why they know him as Shantanu.*

That is why he was called Shantanu.

Shantanu married Ganga, the river Bhagirathi. On her he fathered Devavrata, whom
they call Bhishma.

Bhishma, to do what would please his father, brought Satyavati to be his mother,
whom they call Gandhakali. She had a son born to her as a maiden, Dvaipayana, by
Parashara. And she had two sons by Shantanu, Chitrangada and Vichitravirya. Of
them, Chitrangada was killed by a gandharva before he reached his youth, and
Vichitravirya became king.

Vichitravirya married Ambika and Ambalika, the daughters of the king of Kashi by
a princess of Kosala. But Vichitravirya died without children.

Then Satyavati thought, *The line of Dushyanta is being cut off.* She called
Dvaipayana, the seer, to mind. He stood before her. "What shall I do?" he said.

She said to him, "Your brother Vichitravirya has gone to heaven without
children. Father children for him, as is right."

"Very well," he said, and he fathered three sons: Dhritarashtra, Pandu and
Vidura.

King Dhritarashtra had a hundred sons by Gandhari, through a boon from
Dvaipayana. Four of Dhritarashtra's sons were the chief: Duryodhana, Duhshasana,
Vikarna and Chitrasena.

Pandu had two wives, Kunti and Madri, both of them jewels among women.

Once, when Pandu was hunting, he saw a seer mating with a doe. The seer was in
the midst of it and had not yet tasted the pleasure of love, and Pandu shot him
with an arrow. Pierced by the arrow, the seer said to Pandu, "You know dharma,
and yet you struck me down when I had not yet tasted the pleasure of love. So
you too, when you come to this state, before you have tasted the pleasure of
love, will quickly die."

Pandu went pale, and to escape the curse he did not go near his wives. And he
said, "I have brought this on myself by my own rashness. And I hear that there
are no worlds for a man without children." So he said to Kunti, "Bear sons for
me."

She bore sons there: Yudhishthira by Dharma, Bhimasena by the Wind, and Arjuna
by Shakra. Pandu was glad, and he said to her, "Your fellow wife has no
children. Let her have children too, as is right."

"So be it," said Kunti. Then Nakula and Sahadeva were born to Madri by the two
Ashvins.

One day Pandu saw Madri adorned, and desire rose in him. As soon as he touched
her, he died. Madri climbed onto his funeral pyre with him. She said to Kunti,
"Noble one, watch carefully over the twins."

Then the ascetics brought the five Pandavas, with Kunti, to Hastinapura, and
presented them to Bhishma and Vidura. There too, when an attempt was made to
burn them in the house of lac, it failed, through Vidura's counsel. Then, on the
way, they killed Hidimba and went to Ekachakra. In Ekachakra too they killed a
rakshasa named Baka, and then they went to the city of the Panchalas. There they
won Draupadi as their wife, and came home to their own country safely.

And they fathered sons. Yudhishthira fathered Prativindhya, Wolf-belly Sutasoma,
Arjuna Shrutakirti, Nakula Shatanika, and Sahadeva Shrutakarman.

Yudhishthira won Devika, the daughter of Govasana the Shaibya, at her own
choosing of a husband. On her he fathered a son named Yaudheya. Bhimasena
married Baladhara, a princess of Kashi, whom he won as the prize for his
strength. On her he fathered a son named Sarvaga. Arjuna went to Dvaravati and
married Subhadra, the sister of Vasudeva. On her he fathered a son named
Abhimanyu. Nakula married Karenumati, a princess of Chedi. On her he fathered a
son named Niramitra. Sahadeva married Vijaya, a Madra princess, at her own
choosing of a husband. On her he fathered a son named Suhotra. And Bhimasena,
before all this, had fathered a son named Ghatotkacha on the rakshasi Hidimba.
These are the eleven sons of the Pandavas.

Abhimanyu married Uttara, the daughter of Virata. She bore a child that was
dead. At the command of Vasudeva, the best of men, Pritha took him in her lap.
"I will bring this six-month child back to life," Vasudeva said. And when he had
brought him back to life, he said, "He was born when the line was wasted away.
Let him be named Parikshit."

Parikshit married Madravati. On her he fathered Janamejaya. Janamejaya had two
sons by Vapushtama, Shatanika and Shanku. Shatanika married a princess of
Videha. On her he fathered a son, Ashvamedhadatta.

This is the line of Puru, and of the Pandavas. Whoever hears this line of Puru
is freed from all his sins.

**Form.** This adhyaya is in prose, with a few verses of the line (*anuvaṃśa*).
The novel keeps the prose list, sets the verses in italics, and keeps the text's
pattern of "X married Y. On her he fathered Z."

**Differences from other tellings, kept as they are.**

* **Bharata's heir.** Here Bhumanyu is Bharata's son by Sunanda (1.90.34). In
  1.89.18 he comes "from Bharadvaja". Both are kept.
* **Hastinapura** is named after Hastin, who founded it (1.90.36). 1.89 does not
  mention him.
* **The route to Pratipa** differs between 1.89.51–52 and 1.90.43–45.
* **Sahadeva's son by Draupadi** is Shrutakarman here (1.90.82), but Shrutasena at
  1.57.103.
* **The Pandavas' other wives and sons** (Devika and Yaudheya, Baladhara and
  Sarvaga, Karenumati and Niramitra, Vijaya and Suhotra) are named only here in
  Book 1.
* **Parikshit.** The CE says only that Uttara bore a dead child, that Kunti took
  him in her lap at Vasudeva's command, and that Vasudeva revived "this six-month
  child" (1.90.90–92). The weapon that killed him in the womb is told in Book 10.
* **The seer who curses Pandu** is not named here, and 1.90.64 says only that he
  was mating "with a doe". The full story, in which the seer has taken a deer's
  form, is told at 1.109.

**Choices.**

* 1.90.12 *mādhavīm*: Ananta is "a Madhavi", a woman of the Madhava people.
* 1.90.19 *puruṣamedhānām ayutam*: "ten thousand human sacrifices" (the Vedic
  *puruṣamedha*). The text says it, and the novel keeps it without comment.
* 1.90.26: the river Sarasvati "chose him as husband". Kept.
* 1.90.39 *caturviṃśaṃ putraśatam*: "a hundred and twenty-four sons".
* 1.90.48: the name Shantanu is explained from his touch that restores youth.
* 1.90.51 *gandhakālī*: Satyavati's name here, "the dark one of fragrance".

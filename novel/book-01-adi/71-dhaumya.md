# Dhaumya

"When great Vasishtha said this," said the gandharva, "the brahmin seer held
back his anger from destroying all the worlds. But the splendid seer Parashara,
Shakti's son, the best of all who know the Veda, offered a rakshasa sacrifice.
Remembering Shakti's death, the great sage burned rakshasas, old and young, in
the wide-spread sacrifice. Vasishtha did not stop him from killing the
rakshasas. He had decided, *I will not break his second vow.* In that sacrifice
the great sage sat in front of the three fires, blazing, like a fourth fire.
With that bright sacrifice properly offered, the sky blazed as if with the sun
at the end of the rains. All the sages there, Vasishtha and the rest, thought
him a second sun blazing in the sky with his splendour.

"Then the generous seer Atri came, wanting to bring that sacrifice, so hard for
others to perform, to its end. So too Pulastya, Pulaha and Kratu came to the
great sacrifice, tamer of enemies, wanting the rakshasas to live. Pulastya,
wishing to save the rakshasas from death, bull of the Bharatas, said to
Parashara, the tamer of enemies, 'I hope there is nothing to hinder you, son. Do
you take joy, child, in the death of all these rakshasas, who knew nothing and
did no wrong? You, the best of those who drink soma, a man of the highest
standing, are doing this wholly unrighteous thing, destroying my offspring,
Parashara. King Kalmashapada wants to go to heaven. And all great Vasishtha's
sons, the younger brothers of Shakti, are rejoicing together with the gods.
Great Vasishtha knows all this, great sage. This destruction of the rakshasas is
ascetics' work, son, and you have been only its instrument in this sacrifice,
joy of Vasishtha. Let the sacrifice go, bless you. Let it be finished for you.'

"When Pulastya and wise Vasishtha said this to him, Shakti's son Parashara
finished the sacrifice. The sage let the fire he had gathered for the sacrifice
of all the rakshasas go in a great forest on the northern flank of the Himalaya.
There, to this day, that fire can be seen eating rakshasas, trees and stones, at
every change of the moon."

---

"Why did King Kalmashapada set his own wife to lie with his teacher," asked
Arjuna, "the best of those who know Brahman? And why did great Vasishtha, who
knew the highest dharma and the ways of the world, lie with a woman forbidden to
him? Tell me everything that happened then, for I ask."

"Dhananjaya," said the gandharva, "listen to what you ask me about Vasishtha,
whom none can overcome, and King Mitrasaha. I told you before, best of the
Bharatas, how the king was cursed by great Shakti, Vasishtha's son. Fallen into
the power of the curse, his eyes troubled with anger, the king, the tamer of
enemies, left the city with his wife. He went into an empty forest with his wife
and wandered through it, gripped by the curse. It was full of herds of every
kind of beast and creatures of every kind. It was covered with every kind of
shrub and creeper and tree, and full of terrible noises.

"Once, overcome by hunger and looking for food for himself, he saw by a forest
stream, in great distress, a brahmin woman and a brahmin who had come together
to make love. When they saw him they were terrified and ran, before they had
done. As they ran, the king seized the brahmin by force. When she saw her
husband seized, the brahmin woman said, 'Listen to what I say to you, king, you
of good vows. You were born in the line of the Sun, and you are famous in the
world. You have stood carefully by dharma, and you delight in serving your
teachers. You have been cursed, unconquerable one, but you should not do evil.
My season has come, and I came to my husband today. I have not had what I came
for from my husband, and I long for a child. Be kind, best of kings. Let my
husband go.'

"As she cried out like this, he, doing a most cruel thing, ate her husband, as a
tiger eats the beast it wants. The tears that fell to the ground from her,
overcome by anger, became a blazing fire and set that place ablaze. Then the
brahmin woman, burning with grief and pain at her husband's fate, cursed the
royal seer Kalmashapada in her anger. 'Base one, you cruelly ate my husband, my
great and famous lord, before my eyes, before I had had what I came for. So you
too, fool, wounded by my curse, will die as soon as you come to your wife in her
season. And the seer Vasishtha, whose sons you have destroyed, will lie with
your wife, and she will bear a son. That son will carry on your line, lowest of
kings.' When she had cursed the king like this, that good woman of the Angiras
line went into the blazing fire before his eyes.

"And the fortunate Vasishtha saw all this by his great yoga of knowledge and by
his austerity, tamer of enemies. Long afterwards, when the royal seer was freed
from his curse, he went to Madayanti in her season, and she held him back.
Bewildered by the curse, the king had not remembered it. When he heard the
queen's words, the best of kings remembered the curse and was deeply grieved.
That is why the king, marked by the fault of the curse, set Vasishtha to his own
wife, best of the Bharatas."

---

"Tell us of a family priest who would suit us," said Arjuna, "one who knows the
Veda, gandharva, for you know everything."

"Devala's younger brother, Dhaumya, is practising austerity in the forest at the
bathing place of Utkochaka," said the gandharva. "Choose him, if you wish."

Then Arjuna, pleased, gave the gandharva the fire weapon as the rules require,
and said to him, "Let the horses stay with you for now, best of gandharvas. I
will take them when the time for action comes. Farewell."

The gandharva and the Pandavas honoured each other and set out from the lovely
bank of the Bhagirathi wherever they wished. Then the Pandavas went to the
bathing place of Utkochaka, to Dhaumya's hermitage, and chose Dhaumya as their
family priest, Bharata. Dhaumya, the best of all who know the Veda, welcomed
them with water for their feet, with fruit and roots, and with his priesthood.
With that brahmin at their head, the Pandavas hoped to win back their glory and
their kingdom, and the Panchala princess at her bridegroom choice. Joined with
that teacher, the bulls of the Bharatas and their mother felt as if they had a
protector. For he knew the truth of the meaning of the Veda, their generous
teacher, and that wise man who knew dharma made the Parthas his patrons. He
thought the heroes, by their own dharma, had already won their kingdom, joined
as they were with intelligence, courage, strength and energy, like gods. When he
had blessed them, the lords of men thought they could go together to the
bridegroom choice of the Panchala princess.

**The CE's own doublets.**

* This adhyaya names the queen Madayanti (1.173.22), as Pandu did (1.113.21), where
  1.168 left her unnamed. It gives a second reason for her going to Vasishtha: the brahmin
  woman's curse that the king would die if he lay with his wife (1.173.18). Both kept.

**Choices.**

* 1.172.4 *dvitīyām … pratijñām*: Parashara's "second vow", after his first, to destroy the
  worlds, was set aside.
* 1.172.12 *prajocchedam … mahyam*: "the destruction of my offspring". Pulastya is the
  ancestor of the rakshasas.
* 1.172.17 *parvaṇi parvaṇi*: "at every change of the moon".
* 1.173.2 *agamyāgamanam*: "going to one not to be approached". Arjuna's word.

# Purochana

Then King Duryodhana and his brothers slowly won over all the people with gifts
of money and honours. And some clever counsellors, set on by Dhritarashtra,
began to talk about the lovely town of Varanavata. "A great festival of
Pashupati has begun in the town of Varanavata, the loveliest on earth. It is
full of every kind of jewel, a place that delights men's hearts." So they
talked, at Dhritarashtra's bidding.

As they talked about lovely Varanavata, the sons of Pandu began to think of
going there, king. When the king saw that their curiosity was roused, Ambika's
son spoke to the Pandavas.

"My men keep telling me, again and again, that Varanavata is the loveliest town
in the world. If you would like to go to the festival at Varanavata, my sons, go
with your followers and your trains, and enjoy yourselves like immortals. Give
jewels to the brahmins and to the singers, as many as you like, shining like
gods. When you have enjoyed yourselves for a while and had your fill of
pleasure, you will come back happy to this Hastinapura."

Yudhishthira understood what Dhritarashtra wanted, and he knew that he himself
had no one to help him. "So be it," he answered.

Then, sadly, Yudhishthira spoke quietly to wise Bhishma and very wise Vidura, to
Drona, to Bahlika, to Somadatta the Kaurava, to Kripa, to the teacher's son, and
to famous Gandhari. "At Dhritarashtra's command, we are going to live with our
followers in the lovely, crowded town of Varanavata. Let all of you, with kind
hearts, speak words of blessing over us. Made strong by your blessings, no evil
will overcome us."

When the son of Pandu said this, all the Kauravas turned to the Pandavas with
kindly faces. "May you always be safe on the road, sons of Pandu, from all
creatures. May no harm come to you from anywhere."

Then, when the blessings had been said, the Pandavas did all they had to do, and
set out for Varanavata, for the sake of the kingdom.

---

When the king had spoken to the great Pandavas like this, the wicked Duryodhana
was overjoyed. He took Purochana aside, bull of the Bharatas, and grasping his
minister by the right hand, he said to him:

"Purochana, this earth, full of wealth, is mine. As it is mine, so it is yours.
You must guard it. I have no one I trust more than you, no ally to plan with as
I plan with you. Keep my counsel, friend, and root out my rivals. Do what I tell
you, by some clever means.

"Dhritarashtra has sent the Pandavas to Varanavata. At his command they will
enjoy the festival. You must go to Varanavata today, in a swift chariot drawn by
donkeys. When you get there, have a large, costly house built, with four halls
and well enclosed, backing onto the armoury. Have every kind of thing that burns
brought there, hemp, resin and the rest. Mix earth with ghee, oil and plenty of
lac, and have the walls plastered with it. Put hemp, bamboo, ghee, wood and all
kinds of contraptions all through that house. Do it so that the Pandavas do not
suspect you, even when they look closely, nor anyone else think *This house is
made to burn.*

"When the house is built, honour the Pandavas greatly and settle them there, and
Kunti, with their friends. Have fine seats, carriages and beds made ready for
the Pandavas, so that my father is pleased. See to everything so that they enjoy
themselves in the town of Varanavata without suspicion, until the time has come
round. Then, when you know that they trust you completely and are sleeping
without fear, set fire to the house at its door. When they have been burned in
their own house, people will say they were burned, and their kinsmen will never
be able to blame us for the Pandavas."

"So be it," Purochana promised the Kaurava. And he set off for the town of
Varanavata in a chariot drawn by donkeys. He went quickly, king, and keeping to
Duryodhana's plan, Purochana did everything the prince had told him.

**Choices.**

* 1.131.3 *paśupateḥ samājaḥ*: "a gathering (festival) of Pashupati", i.e. Shiva.
* 1.131.6 *ambikāsutaḥ*: Dhritarashtra, "Ambika's son".
* 1.131.11 *ātmanaś cāsahāyatvam*: Yudhishthira sees that "he had no one to help
  him", and so agrees.
* 1.131.18 *rājyalābhāya*: they set out "for the gaining of the kingdom", i.e. with an
  eye to it, or for luck in winning it. The phrase is compressed.
* 1.132.7 *rāsabhayuktena*: a chariot "drawn by donkeys", fast.
* 1.132.8 *catuḥśālam*: a house "with four halls" round a court.
* 1.132.9–11: the materials are named as in the text: *śaṇa* (hemp), *sarjarasa*
  (resin), *lākṣā* (lac), ghee, oil, bamboo, wood, and "contraptions" (*yantrāṇi*).
* 1.132.13 *sasuhṛjjanām*: Kunti "with their friends", i.e. their companions and
  household.
* 1.132.15 *yāvat kālasya paryayaḥ*: "until the time comes round".

# The Wedding

"Great seer," said Drupada, "it was before I heard these words of yours that I
tried to do what I did. What has been ordained cannot be turned aside, so this
arrangement is fitting. The knot of fate cannot be untied. Nothing here is
settled by our own doing. What was arranged to win one husband has turned out to
be for many. As Krishna said before, again and again, 'Lord, give me a husband,'
so the god gave her that boon. The god knows what is highest here. If Shankara
has ordained this, whether it is right or wrong, the fault is not mine. Let them
take her hand as the rites require, as they wish. Krishna is ordained for them."

Then the blessed Vyasa said to the Dharma King, "Today is a holy day, son of
Pandu. Today the moon enters the constellation Pushya. Take Krishna's hand
today, you first."

Then King Yajnasena and his son brought together everything needed for the
wedding party, many things of the finest kind. He had his daughter Krishna
bathed and decked with many jewels. Then all the king's friends came there, with
his counsellors and ministers. The brahmins and the townspeople came too, in
order of rank. They were all very happy to see the wedding. His palace, graced
with guests, its courtyard strewn with lotuses and water lilies, bright with
heaps of costly jewels, shone like the sky studded with clear stars.

Then came those young princes of the Kurus, adorned and wearing earrings,
dressed in costly clothes and sprinkled with fine sandal water. They had bathed,
and the blessing rites had been performed. With their family priest Dhaumya,
bright as fire, they went into the hall one after another, lord, in due order.
They went in gladly, like great bulls into a cow pen.

Then Dhaumya, who had mastered the Veda, laid the fire and offered into its
flames with mantras. The priest, who knew the mantras, brought Yudhishthira and
joined him with Krishna. The master of the Veda led the two of them, hand in
hand, round the fire, keeping it on their right. Then the priest took leave of
Yudhishthira, the ornament of battle, and went out of the palace.

In this way, day after day, the princes took the hand of that fine woman, each
in his turn. They were great warriors who made the Kuru line grow, and each wore
his finest form. And the brahmin seer told of a wonderful thing there, beyond
the human: that great-spirited woman with the lovely waist, they say, became a
virgin again as each day passed.

When the wedding was done, Drupada gave the great warriors wealth of many fine
kinds. He gave a hundred chariots decked with the best gold, each drawn by four
horses, with golden bridles and garlands. He gave a hundred elephants with lotus
marks on their foreheads, like a hundred mountains with golden peaks. He gave a
hundred maidservants in the first bloom of youth, with costly dresses,
ornaments, clothes and garlands. With the fire as witness, Drupada of the
Somakas gave each of them, separately, wealth to the count of a hundred
thousand. He gave them clothes and ornaments too, of great power and great
price.

When the wedding was done, and they had gained that Shri, rich in jewels, the
mighty Pandavas, like Indras, lived happily in the city of the Panchala king.

---

Joined with the Pandavas, Drupada had no fear of anyone, not even of the gods.

The women of great Drupada came to Kunti. Each told her name and touched Kunti's
feet with her head. Krishna, dressed in linen, with the wedding thread tied and
the blessings spoken, greeted her mother-in-law and stood before her, bowing,
with her hands joined.

Pritha spoke a blessing with love to her daughter-in-law Draupadi, who had
beauty and every good mark, and good character and conduct.

"As Indrani is to Indra of the bay horses, and Svaha to the fire, as Rohini is
to the moon and Damayanti to Nala, as Bhadra is to Vaishravana and Arundhati to
Vasishtha, as Lakshmi is to Narayana, so be you to your husbands. Be the mother
of living sons, good lady, the mother of heroes, blessed with many joys,
fortunate, rich in pleasures, a wife at the sacrifice, true to your vows. May
your years pass for ever in honouring guests who come, as is right. Honour good
men, children, the old and your elders. In the kingdoms and cities with
Kurujangala at their head, may you be consecrated after your husband, the king
who loves dharma. Give all the earth, won by your mighty husbands' courage, to
the brahmins at the horse sacrifice, the great rite. Whatever jewels of worth
there are on earth, virtuous one, win them, lovely one, and be happy for a
hundred autumns. And as I greet you today, my son's wife, dressed in linen, so I
will greet you again, virtuous one, when you have borne a son."

---

Then Hari sent the married Pandavas golden ornaments inlaid with pearls and
lapis. Madhava sent costly clothes from many lands, and blankets, skins and
jewels, soft to the touch and fine. He sent beds, seats and carriages of many
kinds, and large, and vessels by the hundred, inlaid with lapis and diamond.
Krishna gave them thousands of serving women from many lands, with beauty, youth
and skill, finely adorned. He gave well-trained elephants of good breed, fine
horses well adorned, and chariots with trained horses, decked with fine golden
cloths. And Madhusudana, whose spirit is beyond measure, sent them gold by the
ten million, both raw and worked, in heaps.

The Dharma King Yudhishthira accepted it all with the greatest joy, wishing to
please Govinda.

**Divergences.**

* **A virgin again each day.** This is the CE's own text: "she became a virgin again as each
  day passed" (1.190.14), told by the brahmin seer. See ledger L-99.
* The vulgate adds a verse on how Draupadi stood to each husband (\*1929), and that the brothers
  were all pleased with her conduct (\*1932). Neither is in the CE.
* Kunti's list of model wives in the CE has no Sita, Parvati or Lopamudra. They are added in the
  vulgate (\*1934–\*1936).

**Choices.**

* 1.190.2 *kṛtaṃ nimittaṃ hi varaikahetoḥ*: "what was arranged to win one husband", as in
  Ganguli.
* 1.190.5 *pauṣyaṃ yogam*: the moon's conjunction with the constellation Pushya.
* 1.190.12 *ājiśobhinam*: "the ornament of battle", Yudhishthira.
* 1.190.13 *uttamarūpadhāriṇaḥ*: "wearing their finest form".
* 1.190.14 *viprarṣiḥ*: "the brahmin seer", Vyasa. *kila*: "they say".
* 1.190.17 *daśāyutānvitam*: "ten ayutas", a hundred thousand. The unit is not given.
* 1.190.18 *śriyam*: Draupadi as Shri, after 1.189.
* 1.191.3 *kṛtakautukamaṅgalā*: with the wedding thread (*kautuka*) tied and the blessings
  spoken.
* 1.191.9 *kurujāṅgala*: the Kuru country.
* 1.191.12 *sūtaputrām*: "one who has borne a son". This has nothing to do with *sūta*,
  charioteer.
* 1.191.18 *akṛtakaṃ … vītīkṛtam*: "raw and worked, in heaps". The sense of *vītīkṛta* is
  uncertain. Ganguli has "in separate heaps".

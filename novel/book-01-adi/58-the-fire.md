# The Fire

A friend of Vidura's, a skilled miner, came to the Pandavas in private, king,
and said, "Vidura has sent me. I am a very skilled miner. He said, 'Do the
Pandavas a service.' What can I do for you? Vidura told me in secret, 'Win the
Pandavas' trust, and do them good there.' What can I do for you?

"On the fourteenth night of the dark fortnight, Purochana will set fire to the
door of your house. The Pandavas, the bulls among men, are to be burned with
their mother. That is what Dhritarashtra's son has decided, Partha. I have heard
it. And Vidura said something to you in mleccha speech, Pandava, and you said to
him, 'So be it.' Let that be the sign by which you trust me."

Kunti's son Yudhishthira, steadfast in truth, answered him. "I know you, gentle
friend, for a friend of Vidura's, honest and trusted and dear to him, always
firm in your loyalty. The wise man has no need of a sign. As he is to us, so are
you to us. We make no difference between you. We are yours as we are his.
Protect us, as the wise man would.

"I believe this refuge is made to burn, and made for me. Purochana built it at
the command of Dhritarashtra's son. That wicked, evil-minded man has a treasury
and allies, and the villain troubles us all the time. So save us from this fire,
with all your skill. If we are burned here, Suyodhana will have what he wants.
This is the well-stocked armoury of that wicked man. This great house has been
built up against it, at the edge of the rampart, so that there is no escape.
This is surely the evil deed he meant to do. Vidura knew of it from the start,
and so he warned us. This is the danger the steward saw long ago. Get us out
without Purochana knowing."

"So be it," the miner promised, and he set to work. Clearing out a moat, he made
a very large tunnel. In the middle of the house he made its mouth, not very
wide, fitted with a door, hidden and level with the ground, Bharata. For fear of
Purochana he kept the mouth covered, for that evil-minded man always lived at
the door of the house.

The Pandavas spent their nights there with their weapons, king, and by day they
went hunting from forest to forest. They trusted no one, but they seemed to
trust. They were deeply unhappy, but they seemed content, king, deceiving
Purochana. The people of the town knew nothing of them, except that best of
miners, Vidura's friend.

---

When Purochana saw them living there happily for a whole year, seeming to trust
him, he was delighted. And when he saw Purochana so pleased, Kunti's son
Yudhishthira, who knew dharma, said to Bhimasena, Arjuna and the twins:

"This wicked Purochana thinks we trust him completely. The cruel man has been
deceived. I think it is time to run. Let us set fire to the armoury, burn
Purochana, leave six living beings here, and escape without being seen."

Then Kunti, on the pretext of giving gifts, gave a great feast for brahmins one
night, king, and women came to it. When they had enjoyed themselves, eaten and
drunk as they liked, Bharata, they took leave of the lady of the Madhavas and
went home that night. But by chance a Nishada woman with five sons came to the
feast looking for food, driven by Time, with her sons. She drank wine and was
drunk, and with all her sons, king, reeling from drink, she fell asleep in that
very house, senseless, like the dead, lord of men.

Then, when everyone was asleep and a strong wind was blowing in the night, Bhima
set fire to the place where Purochana was sleeping. A great heat and the roar of
the fire rose up, and with it the crowd of people woke.

"This house was built by that wicked, witless man at Duryodhana's bidding,"
cried the townspeople, "and he has burned it, to his own destruction. Shame on
Dhritarashtra, whose mind is so crooked! He has had the pure young Pandavas
burned by his minister. But by good fortune the wicked, evil-minded man has been
burned too, who burned these innocent best of men when they trusted him."

So the people of Varanavata lamented. And they stood round the house on every
side all night.

But the Pandavas and their mother, in deep distress, came out through the
tunnel, king, and went away in secret, unseen. They were held back by lack of
sleep and by fear, and the tamers of enemies could not move fast with their
mother. But Bhimasena, lord of kings, whose speed and courage were terrible,
went on, carrying all his brothers and his mother. The mighty man put his mother
on his shoulder and the twins on his hips, and took the two strong Parthas by
the hands. Breaking the trees with his speed and splitting the earth with his
feet, splendid Wolf-belly went on fast, as swift as the wind.

**Divergences.**

* **The six were planned.** Yudhishthira himself proposes "leaving six living beings
  here" (1.136.4) before the Nishada woman and her five sons arrive "by chance,
  driven by Time" (1.136.7). The vulgate makes her Purochana's spy and Kunti's
  familiar (\*1474–\*1476). The CE does neither. See ledger L-87.
* **Who lights the fire.** The CE says Bhima set fire to "the place where Purochana was
  sleeping" (1.136.9). The vulgate adds that he fired the door and the whole house
  and then entered the tunnel last (\*1477–\*1478).
* **No miner from heaven.** The vulgate makes the miner a being sent by Indra
  (\*1472). In the CE he is simply "a friend of Vidura's".

**Choices.**

* 1.135.4 *kṛṣṇapakṣe caturdaśyām*: "on the fourteenth of the dark fortnight".
* 1.135.6 *mlecchavācā*: "in mleccha speech". The CE's own label for Vidura's riddle.
* 1.135.13 *vaprānte*: "at the edge of the rampart".
* 1.135.16 *parikhām utkiran*: "digging out a moat" (or "throwing up earth as for a
  moat"), as cover for the tunnel.
* 1.136.1 *parisaṃvatsaroṣitān*: "having lived there a full year".
* 1.136.6 *mādhavīm*: Kunti, "the Madhava lady", of the Yadava line.
* 1.136.7 *kālacoditā*: "driven by Time", i.e. by fate.
* 1.136.16 *sahasā*: "quickly". Rendered "fast".

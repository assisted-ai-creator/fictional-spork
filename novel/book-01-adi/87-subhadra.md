# Subhadra

Arjuna, of boundless courage, went in order to all the holy bathing places and
shrines in the western lands. He went to all the bathing places and shrines by
the western sea, and then he came to Prabhasa.

Madhusudana heard that Bibhatsu, the unconquered, had come to Prabhasa and was
visiting the bathing places. Then Madhava came, unannounced, to Kunti's son, and
Krishna and the Pandava saw each other at Prabhasa. They embraced, and asked
each other how they fared in the forest. They were dear friends, those two, the
seers Nara and Narayana.

Then Vasudeva asked Arjuna about his travels. "Why are you going round these
bathing places, Pandava?"

Arjuna told him everything, just as it had happened, and when he had heard it,
the lord, the Varshneya, said, "So it is."

When Krishna and the Pandava had enjoyed themselves at Prabhasa as they wished,
they went to Mount Raivataka to stay. At Krishna's word, men had already
decorated that mountain and brought food. The Pandava accepted it all and ate,
and with Vasudeva he watched the actors and dancers. When he had given them all
leave to go, and honoured them, the splendid Pandava went to a fine and heavenly
bed. He told the Satvata about the bathing places he had seen, Bharata, and the
mountains, rivers and forests. As he told his stories, Janamejaya, sleep stole
over Kunti's son on that bed, fit for heaven. He was woken by sweet singing and
the sound of the lute, with praises and blessings.

When he had done what had to be done, the Varshneya greeted him, and he went in
a chariot with golden fittings to Dvaraka. Dvaraka was adorned in honour of
Kunti's son, Janamejaya, even in its gardens. The people of Dvaraka, eager to
see Kunti's son, came quickly to the royal road by the hundred thousand. At the
windows there were hundreds and thousands of women, and there was a great crowd
of Bhojas, Vrishnis and Andhakas. He was honoured by all the sons of the Bhojas,
Vrishnis and Andhakas. He greeted those he should, and they all greeted him in
return. All the young men greeted him with honour, and he embraced those of his
own age again and again. He stayed many nights with Krishna, in Krishna's lovely
house, which was full of jewels and good things to eat.

---

Some days later, king, there was a great festival of the Vrishnis and Andhakas
on Mount Raivataka. At that mountain's festival the heroes, the Bhojas, Vrishnis
and Andhakas, gave gifts to thousands of brahmins. The whole place round the
mountain was bright with palaces inlaid with jewels, king, and with trees of
lamps. Musicians played their instruments there, dancers danced and singers
sang. The young men of the Vrishnis, adorned and full of energy, went about
everywhere in carriages with golden fittings. The townspeople came on foot and
in carriages of every kind, with their wives and their followers, by the hundred
and the thousand.

Then the lord Haladhara, drunk, went about there with Revati, Bharata, followed
by gandharvas. So did the mighty Ugrasena, king of the Vrishnis, sung to by
gandharvas, with a thousand women in his company. Rukmini's son and Samba,
drunk, fierce in battle, wearing heavenly garlands and clothes, enjoyed
themselves like immortals. Akrura and Sarana, Gada, Bhanu and Viduratha,
Nishatha and Charudeshna, Prithu and Viprithu, Satyaka and Satyaki, Bhangakara's
companions, Kritavarma, Hridika's son, and others not named here, each with his
own women and gandharvas round him, graced that festival on Raivataka.

While that great and happy uproar went on, Vasudeva and Partha went about
together. As they strolled there, they saw Vasudeva's lovely daughter Bhadra,
adorned, among her friends. As soon as he saw her, love rose in Arjuna. Krishna
saw that Partha's mind was fixed on her.

Then lotus-eyed Krishna said, as if laughing, "What is this, Bharata? Is a
forest wanderer's mind being churned by love? She is my sister, Partha, Sarana's
sister by the same mother. If your mind is set on it, I will speak to my father
myself."

"She is Vasudeva's daughter," said Arjuna, "and Vasudeva's sister, and she is
beautiful. Whom would she not bewitch? Everything good would surely have come to
me, if this Vrishni girl, your sister, were my queen. Tell me, Janardana, what
way there might be to win her. I will do all of it, if a man can."

"The bridegroom choice is the kshatriya way of marriage, bull among men," said
Vasudeva. "But it is uncertain, Partha, for there is no knowing which way a
woman's heart will turn. Carrying a bride off by force is also praised, for
kshatriyas and heroes who seek a wife. So say those who know dharma. So, Arjuna,
carry off my lovely sister by force. For who knows what she would do at a
bridegroom choice?"

Then Arjuna and Krishna decided what to do, king, and sent swift men to tell the
Dharma King, in Indraprastha, all about it. As soon as he heard, the
strong-armed Pandava gave his consent.

---

When that had been arranged, and he had been given leave, Dhananjaya learned
that the girl had gone to Raivataka, Janamejaya. He told Vasudeva what he meant
to do, and with his leave, knowing Krishna's mind, the bull of the Bharatas set
out. He rode a chariot with golden fittings, harnessed as the rules require,
yoked with Sainya and Sugriva, hung with nets of little bells. It was furnished
with every weapon, it thundered like a cloud, and it shone like blazing fire,
killing the joy of enemies. He was armed, in his armour, with his sword, his
leather guard and finger guards bound on. He went under the pretext of hunting,
Bharata, at the same moment as the girl.

Subhadra had worshipped the lord of mountains, Raivataka, and all the gods, and
had the brahmins speak blessings. She walked round the mountain, keeping it on
her right, and set out towards Dvaraka. Kunti's son ran at her and forced her
onto his chariot. Then that tiger among men took the brightly smiling girl and
drove towards his own city in his chariot, which could move through the sky.

When the guards saw Subhadra being carried off, they all ran shouting towards
the city of Dvaraka. They came together to the assembly hall Sudharma, and told
the keeper of the hall all that Partha had dared to do. When he heard them, the
keeper of the hall beat the great war drum, decorated with gold, with its
thundering sound. Stirred by that sound, the Bhojas, Vrishnis and Andhakas left
their food and drink and gathered in the hall. The tigers among men, the great
warriors of the Vrishnis and Andhakas, took their seats on hundreds of thrones,
decorated with gold and costly covers, inlaid with jewels and coral, bright as
blazing fire, like fires on their altars. When they were seated, like a meeting
of the gods, the keeper of the hall and his men told them what Jishnu had done.

When they heard it, the Vrishni heroes, their eyes red at the corners with
drink, sprang up in pride. They could not bear what Partha had done.

"Yoke the chariots, quickly!"

"Bring the lances!"

"Bring the costly bows and the great coats of mail!"

Some shouted to their charioteers, "Yoke the chariots!" Some led up the
gold-decked horses themselves. As the chariots, armour and banners were brought,
there was a great confusion in the uproar of the heroes.

Then Balarama, wearing his garland of wild flowers, drunk, like the peak of
Kailasa, dressed in blue, swollen with pride, spoke. "What are you doing, you
fools, while Janardana sits silent? You do not know what he thinks, and you are
angry, and your roaring is empty. Let the great-minded one tell us his view
first. Then do, without fail, what he thinks right."

When they heard those words of Halayudha's, which they could accept, they all
fell silent, and said, "Good! Good!" Hearing the wise Baladeva's even-handed
words, they all sat down again in the middle of the hall.

Then Balarama, Kamapala, said to Vasudeva, the tamer of enemies, "Why do you sit
without a word, Janardana, only watching? It was for your sake, Achyuta, that we
all honoured Partha. And he does not deserve that honour, the fool, the disgrace
to his family. What man who thinks himself well born would eat a meal and then
break the dish? Who, wanting an alliance, and honouring the one that already
exists, would act so rashly? He has scorned us, disregarded Keshava, and taken
Subhadra by force today, to his own death. How can I bear it, Govinda, that he
has set his foot on the middle of my head, as a snake bears being trodden on?
Today I will rid the earth of the Kauravas, alone. I cannot bear this offence of
Arjuna's."

And all the Bhojas, Vrishnis and Andhakas agreed with him as he roared like a
cloud or a kettledrum.

**Divergences.**

* **No ascetic disguise, and Subhadra does not drive.** In the popular story Arjuna comes to
  Dvaraka disguised as a wandering ascetic, is lodged near Subhadra, and she herself takes the
  reins when he carries her off. All of this is in the long Appendix passage App. I No. 114, with
  \*2053 and \*2046. In the CE Arjuna rides into Dvaraka openly, as Krishna's honoured guest
  (1.210.15–21), and seizes Subhadra onto his own chariot (1.212.7). See ledger L-105.
* The vulgate has Balarama threaten to kill Arjuna "with my brothers" (\*2063). Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.210.5 *naranārāyaṇāv ṛṣī*: "the seers Nara and Narayana", Arjuna and Krishna.

  lists "Bhangakara" as a name.

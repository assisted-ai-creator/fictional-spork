# The Hundred Sons

Then a hundred sons were born to Dhritarashtra by Gandhari, Janamejaya, and one
more besides the hundred by a vaishya woman. And five sons, great warriors, were
born to Pandu's wives, Kunti and Madri, from the gods, to carry on the line.

"How were a hundred sons born to Gandhari, best of brahmins?" asked Janamejaya.
"How long did it take, and how long did they live? How did Dhritarashtra come to
have a son by a vaishya woman? How could he pass over Gandhari, a wife worthy of
him, who kept to dharma and did all he wished? And how were five sons, great
warriors, born to Pandu from the gods, when that great seer had cursed him? You
know it all, rich in austerity. Tell it to me as it happened, and in full. I
never have enough of hearing about my family."

Once Dvaipayana came to Gandhari worn out with hunger and toil. She made him
welcome, and Vyasa gave her a boon. She chose a hundred sons, each like her
husband. In time she conceived by Dhritarashtra.

Gandhari carried that child in her womb for two years, and still she did not
give birth. Then sorrow came over her. She heard that Kunti's son had been born,
as bright as the rising sun. She felt how hard and unmoving her own belly was,
and she brooded. Then, without Dhritarashtra knowing, Gandhari, faint with
grief, struck her own womb with great force until it gave up what it held. Out
came a lump of flesh, hard as a ball of iron. She had carried it in her belly
for two years, and now she began to throw it away.

But Dvaipayana knew of it, and he came in haste. The best of those who pray saw
that lump of flesh. "What is this you mean to do?" he said to Subala's daughter.

She told the great seer the truth of what she had in mind. "I heard that Kunti's
son was born first, as bright as the sun," she said, "and in my great sorrow I
struck down my womb. You granted me a hundred sons, did you not? And this lump
of flesh is what has been born to me, for a hundred sons!"

"It is so, daughter of Subala," said Vyasa, "and it cannot be otherwise. I have
never said anything untrue, even in jest. Why would I now? Have a hundred pots
filled with ghee made ready at once. And sprinkle this lump with cold water."

As the lump was sprinkled, it split into a hundred pieces. Each part was a
separate embryo, the size of the top joint of a thumb. A full hundred and one
were formed from the lump of flesh, lord of the people, one after another, as
time went by, king. Then he put those embryos in the pots, in places well
guarded, and set a watch over them. And the blessed one told Subala's daughter
that the pots should be opened only after the same length of time again. When
the blessed Vyasa had said this and made all these arrangements, the wise one
went to the Himalaya, the mountain of rock, to practise austerity.

In the order in which they had been placed, King Duryodhana was born first of
them. But by the reckoning of birth, King Yudhishthira was the eldest.

As soon as that son was born, Dhritarashtra called many brahmins, and Bhishma
and Vidura too. "Prince Yudhishthira is the eldest, the one who makes our line
grow," he said. "He has won the kingdom by his own merit, and we have nothing to
say against it. But will this one, born next after him, be king too? Tell me the
truth of this, what will surely come to pass."

As he finished speaking, Bharata, fierce beasts of prey howled in every
direction, and jackals cried out, foretelling evil. When the brahmins and wise
Vidura saw these dread signs on every side, they said, "It is plain, king, that
this son of yours will be the end of the family. Peace lies in giving him up.
There is great harm in rearing him. Let there be a hundred sons less one, lord
of the earth. By giving up one, bring safety to the world and to the family. For
the family, give up one. For the village, give up a family. For the country,
give up a village. For yourself, give up the whole earth."

So Vidura and all those fine brahmins told him. But the king did not do it, for
he loved his son.

Then within a month all Dhritarashtra's hundred sons were born, king, and one
daughter over the hundred.

While Gandhari was suffering, with her belly growing, a vaishya woman waited on
strong-armed Dhritarashtra, so they say. In that year, king, the wise and famous
Yuyutsu was born to her by Dhritarashtra, a son of mixed birth.

So a hundred sons were born to wise Dhritarashtra, great warriors and heroes,
and one daughter, Duhshala.

---

"Tell me the names of Dhritarashtra's sons in order, lord," said Janamejaya,
"from the eldest down."

Duryodhana and Yuyutsu, king, and Duhshasana, Duhsaha and Duhshala, Jalasandha,
Sama and Saha. Vinda and Anuvinda, Durdharsha, Subahu, Dushpradharshana,
Durmarshana, Durmukha, Dushkarna and Karna. Vivimshati and Vikarna, Jalasandha,
Sulochana, Chitra and Upachitra, Chitraksha, Charuchitra and Sharasana. Durmada
and Dushpragaha, Vivitsu, Vikata and Sama, Urnanabha and Sunabha, Nanda and
Upanandaka. Senapati and Sushena, Kundodara and Mahodara, Chitrabana and
Chitravarma, Suvarma and Durvimochana.

Ayobahu and Mahabahu, Chitranga and Chitrakundala, Bhimavega and Bhimabala,
Balaki and Balavardhana. Ugrayudha and Bhimakarma, Kanakayu and Dridhayudha,
Dridhavarma and Dridhakshatra, Somakirti and Anudara. Dridhasandha and
Jarasandha, Satyasandha and Sadahsuvak, Ugrashravas and Ashvasena, Senani and
Dushparajaya. Aparajita and Panditaka, Vishalaksha and Duravara, Dridhahasta and
Suhasta, Vatavega and Suvarchas.

Adityaketu and Bahvashi, Nagadanta and Ugrayayi, Kavachi, Nishangi and Pashi,
Dandadhara and Dhanurgraha. Ugra and Bhimaratha, Vira and Virabahu, Alolupa and
Abhaya, Raudrakarma and Dridharatha. Anadhrishya and Kundabhedi, Viravi and
Dirghalochana, Dirghabahu and Mahabahu, Vyudhoru and Kanakadhvaja. Kundashi and
Virajas. And Duhshala, over the hundred.

These are the hundred and one, king, and the one daughter, as they are told.
Know that the order of their names is the order of their birth, lord of men. All
of them were great warriors and heroes, all skilled in war. All knew the Vedas,
and all were learned in the books of kingship. All were skilled in the arts of
company, and splendid in their learning and their birth. When the time came,
Dhritarashtra looked into it carefully and found fitting wives for them all, as
the rules require. And when the time came, Bharata, the king gave Duhshala to
Jayadratha, king of Sindhu, with Subala's son's consent.

**Divergences.**

* **No braying at Duryodhana's birth.** The popular story has the newborn Duryodhana
  bray like a donkey, answered by donkeys, vultures and jackals, with Bhima born the
  same day (\*1142, \*1143). In the CE the jackals and beasts of prey howl at the end
  of *Dhritarashtra's* question (1.107.28). See ledger L-76.
* **No wish for a daughter.** In the vulgate Gandhari silently wishes for a daughter
  and Vyasa makes the extra piece into Duhshala (App. I No. 63). The CE simply says
  the lump divided into "a full hundred and one" (1.107.20) and that there were a
  hundred sons "and one daughter over the hundred" (1.107.34).

**The CE's own puzzles.**

* **A hundred, or a hundred and one?** The lump makes 101 embryos (1.107.20). At
  1.108.14 the list is summed up as "this hundred and one, and one daughter". The list
  includes Yuyutsu, the vaishya woman's son, second after Duryodhana (1.108.2).
* **Repeated names.** Jalasandha (1.108.2, 4), Sama (1.108.2, 5) and Mahabahu
  (1.108.7, 13) each appear twice. There is a son named Duhshala (masculine, 1.108.2)
  as well as the daughter Duhshala (1.108.14), and a son named Karna (1.108.3) who is
  not Kunti's son. Kept as they stand.
* **Pandu's sons first.** Yudhishthira is born before Duryodhana (1.107.24), though
  Kunti's sons are not narrated until 1.113–115. The book tells the two families side
  by side.

**Choices.**

* 1.107.11 *sodaraṃ pātayām āsa*: "she made her womb fall", i.e. she forced the
  birth. "Struck her own womb with great force" follows *yatnena mahatā*.
* 1.107.12 *lohāṣṭhīlā*: "a ball of iron".
* 1.107.19 *aṅguṣṭhaparvamātra*: "the size of the top joint of a thumb".
* 1.107.22 *kālenaitāvatā punaḥ*: "after the same length of time again", i.e.
  another two years.
* 1.107.32: the proverb is given in full.
* 1.108.18 *saubalānumate*: "with the consent of Subala's son", Shakuni. It could
  also be read as Gandhari's consent.
* 1.107.36 *karaṇaḥ*: Yuyutsu is "of mixed birth", a kshatriya's son by a vaishya
  woman.
* **Name forms.** Stems in *-in* and *-an* are given in their familiar nominative
  (Balaki, Kavachi, Chitravarma, Bhimakarma), as the house rules require.

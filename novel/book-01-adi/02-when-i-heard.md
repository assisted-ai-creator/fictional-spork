# When I Heard

Then the blind king told Sanjaya what he had heard, one thing after
another. And after each one, he said the same words.

"Arjuna drew the wonderful bow and pierced the target and brought it down
to the ground. He carried off Draupadi while all the kings looked on. When I
heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"In Dvaraka, Arjuna took Subhadra of the Madhu clan by force. The two heroes
of the Vrishnis came to Indraprastha. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no
hope of victory.

"Indra, king of the gods, poured down his rain, and Arjuna held it back with
his divine arrows. He gave the fire god his fill in the Khandava forest. When
I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Yudhishthira was beaten at the dice by Shakuni, the son of Subala, and lost
his kingdom. And his brothers, whose strength has no measure, still followed
him. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Draupadi was brought into the hall, choking with tears, suffering, in a
single garment, in her monthly season. She had husbands to protect her, and
no one protected her. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Those righteous men set out for the forest, and I heard of all they did as
they went. The Pandavas suffered for love of their eldest brother. When I
heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Thousands of brahmins who had finished their studies went with the king of
dharma into the forest, and great-souled brahmins who lived on alms. When I
heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"The three-eyed god of gods, Shiva, came to Arjuna in the form of a mountain
hunter. Arjuna fought him and pleased him, and won the great weapon of
Pashupata. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Arjuna, true to his word and praised by all, went up to heaven. There he
learned the divine weapons from Indra himself, in the proper way. When I
heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Bhima and the other sons of Pritha met Kubera, lord of riches, in a country
where no human being can go. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"My sons, who followed Karna's advice, went out to see the cattle. The
gandharvas took them prisoner, and Arjuna set them free. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Dharma came to the king of dharma in the form of a yaksha and asked him
questions, and he answered them rightly. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no
hope of victory.

"While Arjuna was living in Virata's kingdom, he broke my greatest warriors,
alone on a single chariot. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"The king of the Matsyas offered Arjuna his daughter Uttara, with every
honour, and Arjuna accepted her for his son. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I
had no hope of victory.

"Yudhishthira was beaten and penniless, driven out and cut off from his own
people, and still he had seven full armies behind him. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Narada said that Krishna and Arjuna are Nara and Narayana, and that he
himself has always seen them so in the world of Brahma. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Krishna of the Madhu clan, Vasudeva, whose single stride, they say, is this
whole earth, gave himself heart and soul to the Pandavas' cause. When I heard
that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Karna and Duryodhana made up their minds to seize Krishna, and he showed
himself in many forms. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"As Krishna was leaving, Pritha stood alone in front of his chariot in her
grief, and he comforted her. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"Krishna was their counsellor. Bhishma, son of Shantanu, and Drona gave them
their blessing. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Karna said to Bhishma, 'I will not fight while you are fighting.' And he
left the army and went away. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"Krishna and Arjuna and the Gandiva bow, whose power has no measure, those
three fierce powers, came together. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope
of victory.

"Arjuna sank down on his chariot, overcome with despair, and Krishna showed
him all the worlds inside his own body. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no
hope of victory.

"Bhishma, the scourge of his enemies, was killing ten thousand
chariot-fighters in the battle, and not one of the Pandavas' great men was
killed. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Bhishma, bravest of the brave, whom no one could defeat, was struck down in
battle by Arjuna, who put Shikhandi in front of him. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"The old hero Bhishma lay on a bed of arrows, brought down by
bright-feathered shafts, after he had cut the Somakas down until few were
left. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"As Bhishma lay there, he asked for water. Arjuna split the earth and gave
him water to drink. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"The planet Venus and the sun stood in their places for the victory of
Kunti's sons, and the beasts of prey howled at us day after day. When I
heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Drona, that wonderful fighter, showed every way of fighting with weapons,
and still he did not kill any of the greatest of the Pandavas. Then,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Our great chariot-fighters, the Samshaptakas, swore themselves to Arjuna's
death, and Arjuna killed them. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"Drona himself, weapons in hand, guarded a battle array that no one else
could break, and Subhadra's heroic son broke into it alone. When I heard
that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Our great chariot-fighters could not stand against Arjuna. So they all
surrounded the boy Abhimanyu and killed him, and they were glad. Then,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"When they had killed Abhimanyu, Dhritarashtra's sons were shouting, drunk
with joy, and Arjuna turned his anger on Jayadratha. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Arjuna swore to kill Jayadratha, and he made his vow come true in the
middle of his enemies. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Arjuna's horses were exhausted. Krishna unyoked them, let them drink,
brought them back, yoked them again and drove on. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"While his horses rested, Arjuna stood on his chariot with the Gandiva bow
and held off every warrior. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"Satyaki of the Vrishnis crushed Drona's army, with its elephant troops that
no one could withstand, and went to where Krishna and Arjuna were. When I
heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Karna had Bhima in his power. He insulted him and prodded him with the tip
of his bow, and let him go alive. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope
of victory.

"Drona and Kritavarma and Kripa, Karna and Drona's son and the brave king of
Madra let Jayadratha be killed. Then, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Indra had given Karna a divine spear, and Krishna turned it aside onto
Ghatotkacha, the terrible rakshasa. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope
of victory.

"In the fight between Karna and Ghatotkacha, Karna threw the spear that was
meant to kill Arjuna in battle. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"The teacher Drona sat alone on his chariot, and he had given himself up to
death. Dhrishtadyumna stepped beyond dharma and cut him down. When I heard
that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Nakula, the son of Madri, fought Drona's son chariot to chariot, in front of
everyone, and held his own. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"After Drona was killed, his son used the divine weapon of Narayana, and
still he did not make an end of the Pandavas. Then, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"Karna, bravest of the brave, whom no one could defeat, was killed by Arjuna
in that war between brothers, which was the gods' secret. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Drona's son and Kripa, Duhshasana and fierce Kritavarma could not overpower
Yudhishthira, though he stood alone. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no
hope of victory.

"The brave king of Madra, who was always Krishna's rival in battle, was
killed by the king of dharma. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of
victory.

"Shakuni, root of the quarrel and of the dice, whose strength was trickery,
was killed in battle by Sahadeva the Pandava. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I
had no hope of victory.

"Duryodhana, worn out and alone, went into a lake and stilled its waters and
lay there, with no chariot, his pride broken. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I
had no hope of victory.

"The Pandavas stood at the lake of the Ganga with Krishna and insulted my
son, who never could bear an insult. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had no
hope of victory.

"My son moved round the circle in the mace fight, trying every skill, and he
was killed unfairly, by Krishna's design. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I had
no hope of victory.

"Drona's son and the others killed the Panchalas and the sons of Draupadi in
their sleep. It was a foul and shameful deed. When I heard that, Sanjaya, I
had no hope of victory.

"Ashvatthama, chased by Bhimasena, in his anger let loose the supreme
weapon, the Aishika, and it struck down the child in the womb. When I heard
that, Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Arjuna let loose the Brahmashiras weapon, crying 'Peace!', and quieted that
weapon with his own, and Ashvatthama gave up his jewel. When I heard that,
Sanjaya, I had no hope of victory.

"Drona's son was bringing his great weapon down on the child in the womb of
Virata's daughter. And Dvaipayana and Krishna laid their curses on him.

"Gandhari is to be pitied, robbed of her sons and her grandsons. So are the
wives of my sons, robbed of fathers and of brothers. The Pandavas have done a
thing that was very hard to do. They have won back their kingdom, and no one
stands against them.

"Oh, the pity of it! I hear that only ten are left alive after the war: three
of ours, and seven of the Pandavas. Eighteen full armies of warriors were
killed in that battle.

"Darkness has spread over me, and a kind of madness seems to be coming into
me. I cannot find my senses, son of a suta. My mind is reeling."

When he had said this, Dhritarashtra wept and grieved bitterly, and he
fainted. When he came to himself again, he spoke to Sanjaya.

"Sanjaya, things being as they are, I want to give up my life, and soon. I
cannot see the smallest good in staying alive."

---

The king was grieving and talking like this, broken. Then Sanjaya, the wise
son of Gavalgana, spoke to him, and his words were full of meaning.

"You have heard Dvaipayana and the wise Narada tell of kings of great
courage and great strength. They were born in great royal lines rich in
virtue. They knew the divine weapons and blazed like Indra. They won the
earth by dharma, held sacrifices and gave the priests their fees, and won
fame in this world. And then they fell into the power of Time.

"There was Vainya, the great chariot-fighter, and Srinjaya, the best of
victors. There were Suhotra, and Rantideva, and Kakshivat, son of Ushij.
There were Bahlika, Damana, Shaibya, Sharyati, Ajita and Jita. There were
Vishvamitra, the slayer of enemies, and mighty Ambarisha, and Marutta, and
Manu, and Ikshvaku, and Gaya, and Bharata. There were Rama, son of Dasharatha,
and Shashabindu, and Bhagiratha. There was Yayati, whose deeds were good,
for whom the gods themselves made sacrifice, whose earth was marked all over with shrines and sacrificial posts, and
was full of sacrifices. These are the
twenty-four kings of whom the divine seer Narada told King Shaibya long ago,
when grief for his son was burning him.

"And before them other kings have gone, stronger still: great chariot-fighters
and great souls, rich in every virtue. There were Puru, Kuru, Yadu and Shura,
and Vishvagashva of great strength of mind. There were Yuvanashva,
Kakutstha, and brave Raghu. There were Ushinara, and Vena, and Sagara, and
Nimi, and Nala of the Nishadhas, and many more besides. These and many others
we hear of, by hundreds and by thousands, and still others by the ten
thousand, in numbers too vast to name.

"Great kings, wise and strong, far greater than your sons, left their vast
pleasures behind them and met their deaths. Their deeds
were divine, and so were their courage and their giving. Great poets of old
tell of their greatness and their faith, their truth, their purity and their
honesty. They had every blessing and every virtue, and they died too.

"Your sons were wicked. They burned with anger. They were greedy, and most of
what they did was wrong. You should not grieve for them.

"You are learned and clever and wise, and wise men respect you. Those whose
minds follow the scriptures do not lose their senses, Bharata. You know how to
punish and how to show mercy, lord of men. And it is not taught that a father should go to any length to shield a
son.

"It was to be, and so you must not grieve. Who can turn back fate by any
cleverness? No one steps outside the path the Maker has laid down. All this
has its root in Time: being and not being, joy and sorrow.

"Time ripens all creatures. Time destroys them. When Time burns up the
creatures, Time again puts out the fire. Time makes all things in the
world, good and bad. Time draws all creatures in, and sends them out again.
Time moves through all beings, and no one holds it back, and it treats all
alike.

"All that has been, all that will be, and all that is now: know that Time
has made them, and do not let go of your senses."

---

Here Ugrashravas turned back to the sages in the forest.

"In this," he said, "Krishna Dvaipayana spoke a holy and secret teaching.
If a man with faith learns even a quarter of a verse of the holy Bharata,
every one of his sins is washed away.

"Here the holy divine seers are praised, and the brahmin seers and the royal
seers whose deeds were good, and the yakshas and the great serpents too.
And here the blessed Krishna, son of Vasudeva, is praised, the eternal one.
He is truth and right. He is pure and holy. He is Brahman, eternal and
supreme, the unchanging, everlasting light, whose divine deeds the wise
tell over. From that god come what is and what is not, and what is both.
From him come the flow of life and all its striving, birth and death and
birth again. What the scriptures call the self, made of the qualities of the
five elements, and what lies beyond the unseen: that is he, and it is of him
that they sing. The best of ascetics, steady in yoga, strong in meditation,
see him standing inside themselves, like a reflection in a mirror.

"A man who has faith, who is always diligent and holds to truth and dharma,
and who keeps to this chapter, is set free from sin. Whoever listens always,
with faith, to this first chapter of the Bharata, which sets out its whole
course, does not sink down when troubles come. Whoever recites some of it at
dawn and at dusk is freed at once from any sin gathered by day or by night.

"This is the body of the Bharata. It is truth and it is nectar. As butter is
the best of curds, as the brahmin is best of those who walk on two feet, as
the ocean is best of all waters and the cow the best of beasts that walk on
four, so the Bharata is called the best.

"If a man has even a quarter of a verse of it recited to brahmins at a rite
for the dead, the food and drink he offers will reach his ancestors and
never fail them.

"Let a man make the Veda larger with the histories and the old stories. The
Veda is afraid of a man with little learning. 'He will do me harm,' it
thinks.

"A learned man who recites this Veda of Krishna Dvaipayana to others wins his
purpose. He is freed even from the sin of killing an unborn child, of that
there is no doubt. I believe that a man who reads this chapter, pure, at
every change of the moon, has read the whole Bharata. And whoever listens to
this work of the seer every day, with faith, will win long life and fame and
the road to heaven.

"Long ago the gods and the divine seers came together and set up a pair of
scales. They put the four Vedas on one side and the Bharata alone on the
other. The Bharata was heavier, and greater. Because of its greatness and
its weight it is called the Mahabharata, the *Great* Bharata. Whoever knows
what its name means is freed from every sin.

"Austerity is not wrong. Study is not wrong. The rites that the Veda lays
down are not wrong. Winning wealth by force is not wrong. But all of these,
done with a spoiled heart, are wrong."

**Divergences.**

* Ganguli's vulgate lament has extra verses (for example, Arjuna defeating
  the Kalakeyas and Paulomas) that are not in the Critical Edition. Only the
  CE verses are given here.
* In 1.1.147 the vulgate reads "Yudhishthira overcame Drona's son,
  Duhshasana and Kritavarman". The CE reads that these warriors *could not
  overpower* Yudhishthira though he was alone (*śūnyam adharṣayantam*).

**Choices.**

* The refrain *tadā nāśaṃse vijayāya saṃjaya* is kept in every verse, as in
  the Sanskrit. It is the passage's drumbeat. The CE's 1.1.156 has no refrain,
  and the sentence runs into the next verse. The English leaves it
  unfinished, which is how it reads in the Sanskrit.
* 1.1.151 *gaṅgāhrade* is kept literally as "the lake of the Ganga". The
  lake itself is described in Book 9.
* 1.1.156 *paraspareṇābhiśāpaiḥ śaśāpa*: rendered "laid their curses on
  him". The mutual curses are told in full in Book 10.
* 1.1.166–169: Narada's kings are listed as the CE gives them. *Kakshivat,
  son of Ushij* reads *kakṣīvantaṃ tathauśijam* as one person. *Sharyati,
  Ajita and Jita* reads *śaryātim ajitaṃ jitam* as three names. The text
  calls them twenty-four, and the novel keeps its number.
* 1.1.172–178, a long list of about sixty earlier kings, is shortened to the
  names that recur elsewhere in the epic, followed by the text's own
  "these and many others… by hundreds and by thousands" (1.1.179), as the
  Canon Policy allows for long lists.
* "Bharata" in Sanjaya's speech is a form of address to Dhritarashtra, a
  descendant of Bharata.

# Indraprastha

"Wise Bhishma, Shantanu's son, and the blessed seer Drona," said Dhritarashtra,
"have told me what is best, and you tell me the truth. As those heroes, Kunti's
sons, the great warriors, are Pandu's sons, so by dharma they are all my sons,
without a doubt. As this kingdom is meant for my sons, so it is meant for
Pandu's sons, without a doubt. Steward, go and bring them here with their
mother, with all honour, and with godlike Krishna, Bharata. By good fortune the
Parthas are alive. By good fortune Pritha is alive. By good fortune the great
warriors have won Drupada's daughter. By good fortune we all prosper. By good
fortune Purochana is at rest. By good fortune my great sorrow has been taken
away, glorious one."

Then Vidura went, at Dhritarashtra's command, to Yajnasena and the Pandavas,
Bharata. When he arrived there, Vidura, who knew dharma and was skilled in all
learning, went to Drupada, who was now joined with them, as was proper. Drupada
received Vidura as dharma requires, and the two of them asked each other, in the
proper way, how they were. Vidura saw the Pandavas there, and Vasudeva, Bharata.
He embraced them warmly and asked them how they were. They honoured him in turn,
in due order. And the man of boundless understanding asked the sons of Pandu,
again and again, at Dhritarashtra's word and with affection, how they were,
king. He gave jewels and wealth of many kinds to the Pandavas, to Kunti and
Draupadi, and to Drupada's sons, as the Kauravas had given them.

Then that man of boundless wisdom, modest and courteous, spoke to Drupada, in
front of the sons of Pandu and of Keshava.

"King, hear what I have to say, with your ministers and your sons.
Dhritarashtra, with his sons, his ministers and his kin, sends you his
greetings, king, again and again, with great pleasure. He is very pleased with
the alliance, lord of men. So too Bhishma, Shantanu's son, and all the Kauravas,
wise one, ask after your welfare in every way. Drona of Bharadvaja's line, the
great archer, your dear friend, embraces you and asks how you are.

"Dhritarashtra, Panchala, now that he has made this alliance with you, thinks he
has gained all he wished, and so do all the Kauravas. Winning a kingdom would
not please them as much as winning this alliance with you, Yajnasena. Knowing
this, please send the Pandavas. The Kurus are very eager to see Pandu's heirs.
These bulls among men have long been away from home, and they and Pritha too
will be eager to see the city. All the best women of the Kurus, and our city and
our country, wait eagerly to see Krishna of Panchala. So please give the sons of
Pandu leave to go with their wife, without delay. That is why I have come. When
you have let the great Pandavas go, king, I will send swift messengers to
Dhritarashtra: 'The Kaunteyas are coming, and Kunti with Krishna.'"

"It is as you say today, wise Vidura," said Drupada. "My joy too is great at
this alliance, lord. It would be right for these great men to go home. But it is
not right for me to say so myself. When the hero Yudhishthira, Kunti's son,
thinks fit, and Bhimasena and Arjuna and the twins, bulls among men, and Rama
and Krishna, who know dharma, then let the Pandavas go. For these two tigers
among men are devoted to their good."

"We all depend on you, king," said Yudhishthira, "with all our people. What you
tell us, in your love, we will do."

Then Vasudeva said, "I think they should go. Or as King Drupada thinks, who
knows all dharma."

"As the hero thinks, the Dasharha, the best of men," said Drupada, "strong-armed
Krishna, that the time has come, that is my firm view. For as the fortunate
Kaunteyas are now to me, so the sons of Pandu are to Vasudeva, without a doubt.
Kunti's son Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, does not think as much of their
good as Keshava, the tiger among men, does."

---

Then, with great Drupada's leave, the Pandavas and Krishna and wise Vidura took
Draupadi Krishna and glorious Kunti, and went happily, at their ease, to the
city named after the elephant.

When Dhritarashtra the Kaurava heard that the heroes had come, he sent Kauravas
to welcome the Pandavas: Vikarna, the great archer, and Chitrasena, Bharata, and
Drona, the supreme archer, and Kripa of the Gautama line. Surrounded by them,
the heroes, great warriors, shining, slowly entered the city of Hastinapura. The
city seemed to burst with eagerness to see those tigers among men, who put an
end to sorrow and grief. Then the Pandavas heard all kinds of pleasing words,
words that went to their hearts, from people who wanted to please them.

"Here is that tiger among men come back again, who knows dharma, and who
protects us by dharma as if we were his own kin."

"Today it is as if great King Pandu, who loved the forest, has come back from
the forest, wanting to please us. There is no doubt of it."

"What is there that has not been done today to please us all, when Kunti's
heroic sons, our masters, have come back?"

"If we have given, if we have offered, if we have any austerity, by that let the
Pandavas stay in the city for a hundred autumns."

Then they bowed at the feet of Dhritarashtra, of great Bhishma, and of the
others who deserved it. They asked the whole city how it fared, and then, at
Dhritarashtra's command, they went into their houses.

When the great and mighty men had rested for a while, King Dhritarashtra and
Bhishma, Shantanu's son, sent for them.

"Kaunteya," said Dhritarashtra, "listen, with your brothers, to what I say. Let
there be no quarrel between you again. Go and live in Khandavaprastha. No one
will be able to harm you there, protected by Partha as the gods are protected by
the wielder of the thunderbolt. Take half the kingdom and go and live in
Khandavaprastha."

They accepted his words, and all of them bowed to the king. Then the bulls among
men set out for that terrible forest. Taking half the kingdom, they went to live
in Khandavaprastha.

---

The Pandavas, the unfallen, went there with Krishna at their head, and they made
that city as fine as heaven. In a holy and auspicious place the great warriors
performed a rite of peace, and then, with Dvaipayana at their head, they had a
city measured out. It was adorned with moats like the sea, and surrounded by a
wall that stood hiding the sky. The wall gleamed like white clouds, like a heap
of snow, and the finest of cities shone like Bhogavati with its snakes.

It was guarded by fearsome gates, like Garuda with his two wings spread, and by
towers like banks of cloud, like Mount Mandara. It was hedged with weapons of
many kinds, well placed and well covered, and with spears like two-tongued
snakes. It had its practice grounds, and it was guarded by fighting men. It was
bright with sharp hooks and hundred-killers, with nets of machines, and with
great iron wheels. So that finest of cities shone. Its great roads were well
laid out. It was free of harm from the gods, and bright with many fine white
houses. That Indraprastha shone like heaven, like a swollen mass of clouds in
the sky, wrapped in lightning. In a lovely, auspicious place in it the Kaurava's
palace shone, full of wealth, like the house of the lord of wealth.

Brahmins came there, king, the best of all who know the Veda, and liked to live
there, men who knew every language. Merchants came to that place from every
direction, looking for wealth, and men who knew every craft came there to live.

All round the city there were lovely gardens, with mango and hog plum, nipa and
ashoka and champak trees, punnaga and nagapushpa, lakucha and jackfruit, sal,
palmyra, kadamba, bakula and ketaka. They were lovely, in flower, and bent low
with fruit. There were amalaka, lodhra and ankola trees in full flower, rose
apple and patala, kubjaka and atimuktaka, oleander and coral trees, and many
other trees, always in flower and fruit, full of flocks of birds, loud with
peacocks in rut and cuckoos always in song. There were houses as clear as
mirrors, and bowers of creepers of many kinds. There were lovely painted halls,
and little hills, and wells of many kinds full of the best water. There were
very lovely lakes, fragrant with lotuses and water lilies, full of geese and
ducks, bright with chakravaka birds. There were lovely pools of many kinds with
woods round them, and fine, broad, great tanks.

As they lived in that great kingdom, full of good people, the Pandavas' joy grew
all the time, great king. So, when Bhishma and the king had done what was just,
the Pandavas came to live in Khandavaprastha. With those five great archers,
like Indras, the finest of cities shone like Bhogavati with its snakes.

When he had settled them there, the hero Keshava, with Rama, went to Dvaravati,
with the Pandavas' leave, king.

**Divergences.**

* **Who built the city?** In the CE the Pandavas perform a rite of peace and "have a city
  measured out, with Dvaipayana at their head" (1.199.28). The vulgate brings in Vishvakarman,
  the gods' architect, summoned by Indra at Krishna's wish, to build it (\*1987, \*1989, App. I
  No. 109). In the CE Vishvakarman plays no part. See ledger L-101.
* The vulgate adds that Duryodhana and Shakuni proclaimed that no citizen should follow the
  Pandavas (\*1986). It also adds Vidura's meeting with Kunti (App. I No. 105), and a welcome for
  Draupadi by Duryodhana's queen, the Kashi princess (App. I No. 107). None of these is in the CE.
* The vulgate has Dhritarashtra recall how Pandu enlarged the kingdom (\*1985). Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.198.6 *śāntaḥ purocanaḥ*: "Purochana is at rest", that is, dead.
* 1.198.18: Vidura calls Drona Drupada's "dear friend". The diplomatic words are his.
* 1.199.9: Drupada means that Krishna cares for the Pandavas' good even more than Yudhishthira
  himself does.
* 1.199.11 *nāgasāhvayam*: "the city named after the elephant", Hastinapura.
* 1.199.26 *ghoraṃ vanam*: "that terrible forest", Khandavaprastha.
* 1.199.30 *bhogavatī*: the city of the snakes in the underworld.
* 1.199.32 *talpaiś cābhyāsikaiḥ*: "practice grounds". The sense is uncertain.
* 1.199.33 *śataghnī*: "hundred-killer", a weapon mounted on walls.
* 1.199.34 *devatābādhavarjitam*: "free of harm from the gods".
* 1.199.35: the first time the story names the city Indraprastha. Dhritarashtra's lament
  had named it in advance (1.1.103).
* 1.199.36 *kauravyasya*: "the Kaurava's", Yudhishthira's.
* 1.199.39–42: the trees are named as in the text. Common English names are used where they
  exist.
* 1.199.48 *dharmapraṇayane kṛte*: "when (Bhishma and the king) had done what was just".
* 1.199.50 *dvāravatī*: Dvaraka.

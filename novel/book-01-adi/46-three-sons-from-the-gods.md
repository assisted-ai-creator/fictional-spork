# Three Sons from the Gods

When Gandhari had been with child for a year, Janamejaya, Kunti called on
Dharma, the unfailing, to give her a child. The queen quickly brought an
offering to Dharma, and she recited as the rules required the prayer that
Durvasas had given her long before. She lay with Dharma, who had taken on a body
by his yogic power, and the lovely woman won a son, the best of all living
beings.

It was the eighth hour of the day, the one called Abhijit, when the sun stands
at noon. The moon was in Indra's constellation, and it was a holy day, held in
honour. Then Kunti bore a son of abundant fame. As soon as that son was born, a
voice without a body spoke.

"This one will be the best of those who uphold dharma. There is no doubt of it.
Pandu's first-born son will be called Yudhishthira. He will be a famous king,
known in the three worlds, full of glory, splendour and good conduct."

When he had won that righteous son, Pandu spoke to her again. "They say a
kshatriya is greatest in strength. Choose a son who is greatest in strength."

So when her husband told her this, she called on Vayu, the Wind. From him was
born the strong-armed Bhima, of terrible courage. When he was born, so very
strong, the voice spoke of him too, Bharata. "This one is born the strongest of
all the strong."

And there was a great wonder as soon as Wolf-belly was born. He fell from his
mother's lap and smashed a rock with his body. Kunti, so they say, had leaped up
suddenly in fear of a tiger. She did not notice Wolf-belly asleep in her lap.
The boy, as hard as a thunderbolt, fell on the mountain. As he fell, his body
broke the rock into a hundred pieces. When Pandu saw the rock broken to powder,
he was amazed.

On the very day that Bhima was born, best of the Bharatas, Duryodhana too was
born, lord of the earth.

---

When Wolf-belly was born, Pandu thought again. *How can I have a son who is
excellent, the best in the world? This world rests on fate and on human effort.
And fate is won by rites performed in their proper time. Indra is the king of
the gods, the first of them, so we have heard. His strength and energy cannot be
measured. He is mighty, and his splendour has no end. I will win him over with
austerity and get a son of great strength. The son he gives me will be the best.
So I will practise great austerity, in deed, in mind and in word.*

Then the splendid Pandu, the Kaurava, took counsel with the great seers. He told
Kunti to keep an auspicious vow for a year. And the strong-armed king himself
stood on one foot. He practised fierce austerity in deep concentration. To win
over the lord of the thirty gods, the righteous man turned with the sun from its
rising to its setting, Bharata.

After a long time Vasava, Indra, spoke to him. "I will give you a son famous in
the three worlds, who will bring about what gods, brahmins and friends want. I
will give you a son who is the best, and who will destroy all his enemies."

When great Vasava had said this to him, the righteous Kaurava king remembered
the words of the king of the gods and spoke to Kunti. "Bear a son, you of the
lovely hips, wise in policy, great, as bright as the sun, hard to overcome,
active and wonderful to see, the home of a kshatriya's splendour. We have won
the favour of the lord of the gods. Call him, you of the bright smile."

When he said this, the famous woman called on Shakra. The king of the gods came,
and he fathered Arjuna.

As soon as the boy was born, a voice without a body spoke, filling the sky with
its deep, great roar. "Kunti, this one will be the equal of Kartavirya and of
Shibi in courage. He will be as unconquerable as Shakra, and he will spread your
fame. As Aditi's joy grew because of Vishnu, so Arjuna, the equal of Vishnu,
will make your joy grow. He will bring the Madras under his power, and the Kurus
with the Kekayas, the Chedis, the Kashis and the Karushas, and he will uphold
the fortune of the Kurus. By the strength of his arms the Fire at Khandava will
be utterly sated with the fat of all creatures. This mighty leader will conquer
the kings of the earth, and with his brothers he will offer three great
sacrifices. Kunti, he will be the equal of Jamadagni's son, and as brave as
Vishnu, the best of the brave, unconquered. He will win every divine weapon.
This bull among men will bring back lost fortune."

The Wind spoke these wonderful words in the sky at the birth of Kunti's son, and
Kunti heard them. When the ascetics who lived on Shatashringa heard that voice
speaking aloud, they were filled with the greatest joy. And there was a mighty
sound of drums in the sky from the divine seers and the dwellers in heaven with
Indra. A great roar rose up, with showers of flowers. The companies of the gods
gathered and honoured Partha.

Kadru's sons and Vinata's sons were there, the gandharvas and the apsarases, all
the lords of creatures and the seven great seers. Bharadvaja, Kashyapa and
Gautama came, and Vishvamitra, Jamadagni and Vasishtha. The blessed Atri, who
rose when the sun was lost, came too. Marichi and Angiras, Pulastya, Pulaha and
Kratu came, and Daksha the lord of creatures, and the gandharvas and the
apsarases. They wore heavenly garlands and clothes and were decked with every
ornament. They sang to Bibhatsu, and the apsarases danced around him.

Glorious Tumburu sang with the gandharvas. There were Bhimasena and Ugrasena,
Urnayu and Anagha, Gopati, Dhritarashtra and Suryavarchas the seventh. There
were Yugapa, Trinapa, Karshni, Nandi and Chitraratha, Shalishiras the
thirteenth, Parjanya the fourteenth, Kali the fifteenth and Narada the
sixteenth. There were Brihatta, Brihaka and famous Karala, Brahmacharin,
Bahuguna and the famous Suparna, Vishvavasu, Bhumanyu and Suchandra the tenth.
And there were the two famous for the sweetness of their song, Haha and Huhu.
These divine gandharvas sang to that bull among men.

The apsarases too, happy and decked with every ornament, danced and sang, great
ladies with long eyes. Anuna and Anavadya, Priyamukhya and Gunavara, Adrika and
Sachi, Mishrakeshi and Alambusha danced. Marichi and Shichuka, Vidyutparna and
Tilottama, Agnika, Lakshana, Kshema, Devi, Rambha and Manorama danced. Asita and
Subahu, Supriya and Suvapu, Pundarika and Sugandha, Suratha and Pramathini,
Kamya and Sharadvati all danced there in troupes. Menaka, Sahajanya, Parnika and
Punjikasthala, Kratusthala and Ghritachi, Vishvachi and Purvachitti, the famous
Umlocha and Pramlocha, these ten, sang. And Urvashi the eleventh sang with them,
long-eyed like the rest.

The Adityas stood in the sky, blazing like fire, and made the greatness of
Pandu's son grow: Dhatri and Aryaman, Mitra and Varuna, Amsha and Bhaga, Indra,
Vivasvat and Pushan, Tvashtri and Savitri, Parjanya and Vishnu. The Rudras stood
there: Mrigavyadha and Sharva and famous Nirriti, Ajaikapad, Ahirbudhnya and
Pinakin the scorcher of enemies. Dahana and Ishvara and Kapalin were there, lord
of the people, and Sthanu and the blessed Bhava. The two Ashvins, the eight
Vasus, the mighty Maruts, the All-gods and the Sadhyas stood round about.
Karkotaka and Shesha came, and the serpent Vasuki, Kacchapa and Apakunda, and
the great snake Takshaka. They were full of splendour, great in anger and great
in strength. These and many other nagas stood there. Tarkshya and Arishtanemi,
Garuda and Asitadhvaja, Aruna and Aruni, the sons of Vinata, stood there.

When they saw that great wonder, the best of sages were amazed. And from then on
they treated the Pandavas with even more care.

---

But famous Pandu, greedy for sons, urged his lovely wife again. Kunti said to
him, "They do not speak of a fourth birth even in time of trouble. A woman who
goes beyond that is loose, and with a fifth she is a whore. You know this
dharma, and you understand it. So how can you pass it over for the sake of
children, and speak to me as if you had forgotten it?"

**The CE's own puzzles.**

* **How long Gandhari was pregnant.** Kunti calls Dharma "when Gandhari's pregnancy
  had lasted a year" (1.114.1). At 1.107.9 Gandhari carries for two years before
  Kunti's first son is born. Both kept.
* **Bhima and Duryodhana.** The CE says here, at Bhima's birth, that Duryodhana was
  born the same day (1.114.14). See ledger L-76.
* **Kunti's count.** Kunti says a fourth birth is not allowed (1.114.65), counting the
  three sons by Dharma, Vayu and Indra. Karna, her son before marriage, is not
  mentioned.
* **The tiger.** 1.114.12 says only that Kunti leaped up "in fear of a tiger". The
  vulgate adds Pandu shooting the tiger (\*1209).

**Choices.**

* 1.114.3 *yogamūrtidhareṇa*: Dharma "bearing a body made by yoga".
* 1.114.4: the time is given as the text gives it: the eighth *muhūrta*, Abhijit, at
  noon, with the moon in the constellation of Indra (Jyeshtha).
* 1.114.12 *sahasā*: "suddenly".
* 1.114.11 *vṛkodara*: Wolf-belly, Bhima's nickname.
* 1.114.21 *sūryeṇa saha paryavartata*: "turned with the sun", i.e. stood facing it
  from sunrise to sunset.
* 1.114.36: the voice is the Wind's (*vāyuḥ*).
* 1.114.41: Atri is "the one who rose when the sun was lost", an allusion to a myth
  of Atri and the eclipse. Kept as an allusion.
* 1.114.43 *bībhatsu*: one of Arjuna's names.
* 1.114.44–62: all the names are given in the text's order. Where the text numbers
  them ("the seventh", "the tenth") the numbers are kept, even where they do not add
  up.
* 1.114.46 *sad vā bṛhad vā bṛhakaḥ*: an unclear line; Ganguli reads the names
  "Vrihatta, Vrihaka". The novel follows that reading.
* 1.114.51 *kṣemā devī*: read as two names, Kshema and Devi.
* 1.114.65 *svairiṇī*, *bandhakī*: "a loose woman", "a whore". Kunti's words, kept
  plain.

# The Burden of the Earth

"Brahmin," said Janamejaya, "I want to hear properly about those you have named,
and about the others you have not named, and about the other kings of great
splendour. Tell me properly, fortunate one, why those great warriors, who were
like gods, were born here on earth."

This, king, is a secret of the gods, or so we have heard. But I will tell it to
you, bowing first to the Self-born.

Long ago, Jamadagni's son made the earth empty of kshatriyas three times over
seven. Then he went to practise austerity on Mahendra, the best of mountains.
When the son of Bhrigu had emptied the world of kshatriyas, king, the kshatriya
women came to the brahmins, wanting children. The brahmins kept strict vows.
They lay with them, tiger among men, only in their seasons. They never did it
from desire, and never out of season.

The kshatriya women conceived by them in their thousands, king. They bore
kshatriya sons of honoured courage, boys and girls, so that the kshatriya class
would grow again. So the kshatriyas were born to the kshatriya women by the
ascetic brahmins. They flourished in dharma, and they lived very long. And then
all four classes had the brahmins at their head.

Men went to women in their season, not from desire, and never out of season. The
other creatures did the same, even those born as animals. They went to their
mates in their season, bull of the Bharatas. So the people grew in dharma, lord
of the earth, devoted to dharma and their vows. They lived for a hundred
thousand years. All men were free of cares and of sickness.

Then the kshatriyas ruled again over this earth, with its mountains, its woods
and its forests. She was the cow whose edges are the oceans, where the great
elephants roam. The kshatriyas ruled the earth again by dharma, and all the
classes, the brahmins first, were filled with the greatest joy. The kings cast
out the faults that are born of desire and anger. They protected their people,
and brought the rod of punishment by dharma down on those who deserved it.

While the kshatriyas kept to dharma like this, Indra rained sweetly, the
thousand-eyed god of the hundred sacrifices. He rained in the right place and at
the right time, and the people flourished. No one died as a child then, lord of
men. No one knew a woman before he was grown. So the earth, as far as the
oceans, filled up with long-lived people, bull of the Bharatas.

The kshatriyas offered great sacrifices with rich gifts to the priests, and the
brahmins studied the Vedas with their limbs and their Upanishads. The brahmins
did not sell the sacred word then, king, nor recite the Vedas within hearing of
shudras. The vaishyas worked the land here with oxen. They did not yoke weak
cattle, and they kept even the lean ones alive. Men did not milk the cows while
the calves were still sucking, and traders did not sell their wares with false
measures.

People did their work, tiger among men, in keeping with dharma, looking only to
dharma, devoted to dharma. All the classes kept to their own work, lord of men.
So, tiger among men, dharma did not decline anywhere then. Cows gave birth at
their proper time, and so did women, bull of the Bharatas, and trees bore
flowers and fruits in their seasons.

So, king, while the Krita age went on as it should, the whole earth filled up
with a great many living beings.

---

Then, while the world of men was flourishing, bull of the Bharatas, asuras were
born in the fields of kings, best of men. For the Adityas had defeated the
daityas many times in battle. Thrown down from their power, they were born here
on earth. Those proud asuras wanted to be gods here among men. So they were born
on earth, lord, among all kinds of beings. They were born among cows and horses,
king of kings, among donkeys, camels and buffaloes, among flesh-eating beasts,
elephants and deer. So many had been born, and so many more were being born,
lord of the earth, that the earth could no longer bear her own weight.

Some of the sons of Diti and of Danu fell from their world and were born here as
mighty lords of the earth. They were brave and proud, and they took many forms.
Those crushers of enemies overran this earth to the ocean's edge. They oppressed
brahmins, kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras, and by their strength they oppressed
other creatures too. By the hundred thousand they roamed the earth on every
side, terrifying and killing all kinds of creatures. They were hostile to
brahmins, drunk with their own courage and maddened by the pride of their
strength. They attacked the great seers in their hermitages everywhere.

Those great asuras were swollen with courage and strength, and they oppressed
the Earth, lord of the earth. So the Earth went to Brahma. For neither the wind,
king, nor the nagas, nor the mountains could hold up the earth then, overrun by
the danavas' might.

Weighed down and stricken with fear, lord of the earth, the Earth went for
refuge to the god who is the grandfather of all beings. She saw the god Brahma,
the maker of the worlds, who never passes away. The blessed gods, the brahmins
and the great seers were all around him. Gandharvas and apsarases praised him,
glad in their work of praise. She came close and bowed to him.

Then the Earth, seeking refuge, told him her trouble, in the presence of all the
guardians of the world, Bharata. But the Self-born, the highest, knew long
before what the Earth had come to do, king. For how could the creator of the
world not know, Bharata, all that is in the minds of the gods, the asuras and
the worlds?

Then he spoke to the Earth, great king: the lord of the earth, the
all-pervading, the source of all beings, the lord, the kindly one, the lord of
creatures. "Earth, the reason you have come to me is the reason I will set all
the dwellers of heaven to work."

When he had said this and sent the Earth away, king, the god Brahma, the maker
of beings, gave his orders to all the gods himself. "To take the burden off this
earth, be born on her, each of you, with a portion of yourselves, to make war on
them." And in the same way the blessed one called together all the troops of
gandharvas and apsarases, and said to them these excellent words: "Be born among
men with your own portions, as you wish."

Shakra and all the others heard the words of the teacher of the gods. They were
true and useful and wholesome, and the gods took them to heart. Then all of
them, ready to go to earth with their portions, went to Narayana, the slayer of
enemies, in Vaikuntha. He holds the discus and the mace. He is dressed in
yellow, dark and shining, with the lotus at his navel. He is the slayer of the
gods' enemies, and his eyes are wide and beautiful and long.

Indra said to that highest of persons, "Go down with a portion of yourself, to
make the earth clean."

And Hari said to him, "So be it."

**Frame.** Vaishampayana narrates. Janamejaya's question is given as dialogue
(see STYLE_GUIDE §7).

**Divergences.**

* **The Earth goes to Brahma.** Popular tellings often have the Earth, as a cow,
  go to Vishnu. In the CE she goes to Brahma (1.58.37–40), and it is the gods,
  led by Indra, who go to Narayana (1.58.49–51).
* **"Parashurama".** The CE says *jāmadagnya* and *bhārgava* (1.58.4–5). See
  ledger L-62.

**Choices.**

* 1.58.4 *triḥsaptakṛtvaḥ*: "three times seven", i.e. twenty-one times.
  Rendered "three times over seven" to keep the text's way of counting.
* 1.58.6, 1.58.9 *ṛtāv ṛtau … na kāmāt*: "in the season, not from desire". Kept
  as the text states it.
* 1.58.11 *gāṃ … sāgarāpāṅgām*: the earth as "the cow whose edges are the
  oceans". Kept.
* 1.58.18 *na ca śūdrasamābhyāśe vedān uccārayanti*: "nor did they recite the
  Vedas near shudras". This is the text's own statement, and the novel keeps it.
* 1.58.19 *na gām ayuñjanta dhuri kṛśāṅgāś cāpy ajīvayan*: read with
  1.57.11 ("they do not yoke lean cattle") as "they did not yoke weak cattle".
* 1.58.20 *phenapān … vatsān na duhanti*: literally "they did not milk (cows
  with) calves still drinking the foam". Rendered "while the calves were still
  sucking".
* 1.58.43 *śaṃbhuḥ*: here an epithet of Brahma, "the kindly one".
* 1.58.49 *vaikuṇṭham*: Narayana is called Vaikuntha. Rendered as the place-name
  "in Vaikuntha", which is how most readers will know it. This is a small
  liberty, noted here.
* 1.58.50: the description of Narayana is given in full.

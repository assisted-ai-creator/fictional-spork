# The Girl in the Well

When Kacha had finished his vow, and his teacher had let him go, and he was
setting out for the home of the thirty gods, Devayani said to him,

"Grandson of the seer Angiras, you shine with good conduct and birth, with
learning, austerity and self-control. As the famous seer Angiras is honoured by
my father, so Brihaspati is honoured and revered by me even more. Knowing this,
understand what I say, you who are rich in austerity. Remember how I behaved
towards you while you kept your vows and your rules. Now that you have finished
your learning, you should love me, for I love you. Take my hand by the rite,
with the mantras before you."

"As the blessed one, your father, is to be honoured and revered by me," said
Kacha, "so you, faultless one, are to be honoured by me still more. You are
dearer than his own life to the great Bhargava, and by dharma you are always to
be honoured by me as my teacher's daughter, good lady. As my teacher Shukra,
your father, is always to be revered, Devayani, so are you. You should not speak
to me like this."

"You are the son of my father's teacher's son," said Devayani, "but you are not
my father's son. So I honour and revere you too, best of brahmins. When the
asuras killed you again and again, Kacha, remember the love I showed you from
that time on. You know my devotion, the highest, in friendship and in love. You
know dharma. You should not abandon me, when I love you and have done no wrong."

"You are urging me to something I must not do, you of the good vows," said
Kacha. "Be gracious to me, lovely one. You are more to be revered than my
teacher, fair one. Where you once lived, wide-eyed one with the face like the
moon, there I too have lived, good lady, in Kavya's belly, proud one. By dharma
you are my sister. Do not speak so, lovely one. I have lived here happily, good
lady, and I have no anger. I take my leave of you, and I will go. Wish me well
on my way. Think of me when you talk, without going against dharma. And always
serve my teacher, watchful and ready."

"If you refuse me," said Devayani, "when I ask you for the sake of dharma and
love, then, Kacha, this knowledge of yours will not bear fruit."

"I refuse you because you are my teacher's daughter," said Kacha, "not for any
fault of yours. And my teacher has given me leave to go. Curse me, then, if you
will. I spoke to you of the dharma of the seers, Devayani, and you have cursed
me. I did not deserve a curse. You cursed me out of desire today, not out of
dharma. So your desire will not come true as you wish. No seer's son will ever
take your hand. My knowledge will not bear fruit for me, since you have said so.
But whoever I teach, for him that knowledge will bear fruit."

When he had said this to Devayani, Kacha, the best of brahmins, went quickly to
the home of the lord of the thirty gods. The gods, with Indra at their head, saw
that he had come. They honoured Brihaspati, and joyfully they said to Kacha,
"You have done a most wonderful deed for our good. Your fame will never perish.
You will have a share with us."

---

The dwellers of heaven were glad when Kacha came back having mastered the
knowledge. They learned that knowledge from Kacha, and they had what they
wanted, bull of the Bharatas. They all came together and said to Indra of the
hundred sacrifices, "Now is the time for your courage. Kill our enemies, breaker
of cities."

When the thirty gods said this to him together, the bountiful Indra said, "So be
it," and set out. In a forest he saw some women. The girls were playing in a
forest like the garden of Chitraratha. Indra became the wind and mixed up all
their clothes. Then the girls came out of the water together and took their
clothes, whichever lay nearest. Then Sharmishtha, the daughter of Vrishaparvan,
took Devayani's garment, not knowing they had been mixed up. And a quarrel broke
out between the two of them, king of kings, Devayani and Sharmishtha, over it.

"Why are you taking my garment, asura girl," said Devayani, "when you are my
pupil? You have no manners, and no good will come of it for you."

"When my father is sitting or lying down," said Sharmishtha, "your father
praises my father and bows to him, standing low, like a humble man, again and
again. You are the daughter of one who begs, and praises, and accepts gifts. I
am the daughter of one who is praised, and gives, and does not accept. You have
no weapon, and I have one. You are empty-handed, and you shake with rage, beggar
girl. You will find someone to fight you. I do not count you as anything."

Devayani stood up to her, and held on to the garment. Sharmishtha threw her into
a well, and then went back to her own city. Sharmishtha, set on evil, thought,
*She is dead*, and in the rush of her anger she went home without looking back.

Then Yayati, the son of Nahusha, came to that place. His horses and his team
were tired. He was hunting deer, and he was thirsty. Looking into the well,
which had no water in it, the son of Nahusha saw the girl there, bright like a
flame of fire. As soon as he saw the girl, as fair as an immortal, the best of
kings spoke to her very gently, to comfort her.

"Who are you, dark young woman, with your copper-coloured nails and your
polished jewelled earrings? You have been thinking for a long time, and you are
sighing, in distress. Why? And how did you fall into this well, overgrown with
creepers and grass? Whose daughter are you? Tell me everything, you of the
slender waist."

"I am the daughter of Shukra," said Devayani, "who raises up with his knowledge
the daityas the gods have killed. Surely he does not know what has happened to
me. Here is my right hand, king, with its copper-nailed fingers. Take it and
pull me out, for I think you are of good family. I know you are calm and brave
and famous. So you should pull me out of this well I have fallen into."

The son of Nahusha knew she was a brahmin woman, and he took her by the right
hand and lifted her out of the pit. When he had pulled her quickly out of the
well, King Yayati took his leave of the lovely woman and went back to his own
city.

"Go quickly, Ghurnika," said Devayani, "and tell my father everything. I will
not set foot in Vrishaparvan's city now."

Ghurnika went quickly to the asura's palace. When she saw Kavya, she said, her
mind in a whirl, "I tell you, great sage, Devayani has been killed in the forest
by Sharmishtha, fortunate one, Vrishaparvan's daughter."

When Kavya heard that his daughter had been killed there by Sharmishtha, he went
out in haste, in sorrow, to look for his daughter in the forest. When he saw his
daughter Devayani in the forest, Kavya took her in his arms, and said in his
grief, "All people bring sorrow and happiness on themselves by their own faults.
I think you have done some wrong, and this has been your penance."

"Penance or no penance," said Devayani, "listen carefully to me. Hear what
Sharmishtha, Vrishaparvan's daughter, said to me. She says it is true that you
are the daityas' singer of praises. This is what Sharmishtha, the daughter of
Vrishaparvan, says to me, in sharp and cruel words, her eyes red with anger:
'You are the daughter of one who praises, and begs, and accepts gifts. I am the
daughter of one who is praised, and gives, and does not accept.' So Sharmishtha,
the daughter of Vrishaparvan, told me again and again, her eyes red with anger,
full of pride. If I am the daughter of one who praises and accepts gifts,
father, then I will beg Sharmishtha's pardon. So I told my friend."

"You are not the daughter of one who praises, my dear," said Shukra, "nor of one
who accepts gifts. You are the daughter of one who is praised and does not
praise, Devayani. Vrishaparvan himself knows it, and Shakra, and King Yayati,
the son of Nahusha. My power is Brahman, beyond thought, beyond all opposites,
the power of a lord."

**Choices.**

* 1.72.9 *guruputrasya putraḥ … na tu … me pituḥ*: "you are the son of (my
  father's) teacher's son, but not my father's son". Devayani means Kacha is not
  her brother. Kacha answers that he lived in the same belly (1.72.13).
* 1.72.16–20: the curses as the CE gives them. Kacha's knowledge will not work
  for himself, only for those he teaches. No seer's son will marry Devayani.
* 1.73.4: Indra "became the wind" and mixed up the clothes. His purpose is not
  stated beyond the gods' call to action.
* 1.73.8 *śiṣyā bhūtvā*: literally "being a pupil", since Sharmishtha's father
  is the patron of Devayani's father. Kept as "when you are my pupil".
* 1.73.12 *samucchrayaṃ … gatām*: Devayani "rose up" or "grew proud". Rendered
  "stood up to her".
* 1.73.17 *śyāmā*: "dark", or "a young woman". Rendered "dark young woman".
* 1.73.24 *ghūrṇikā*: Ghurnika, Devayani's maid. The CE names her here without
  introducing her.
* 1.73.36 *acintyaṃ brahma nirdvandvam aiśvaraṃ … balam*: Shukra's boast is given
  in full.

# Share It All Together

The two great Parthas, the best of men, went to the potter's workshop and came
to Pritha. Full of joy, they presented Yajnasena's daughter to her as "alms".

Kunti was inside the hut, and she did not look at her sons. "Share it, all of
you together," she said.

Afterwards Kunti looked at the girl, and she said, "What a terrible thing I have
said!"

Afraid of doing wrong, and ashamed, Kunti took Yajnasena's daughter, who was
full of joy, by the hand, and went to Yudhishthira and said, "This is King
Drupada's daughter. Your younger brothers gave her to me. And without thinking,
son, I said what I always say: 'Share it together,' king. Tell me, bull of the
Kurus, how what I said today may not become a lie, and how no wrong, such as has
never been before, may fall on the daughter of the Panchala king."

King Yudhishthira, of the highest energy, the hero of the Kurus, thought for a
moment. He comforted his mother Kunti, and he said to Dhananjaya, "You won
Yajnasena's daughter, Pandava, and the princess will be happy with you. Let the
fire be lit and the offerings made. Take her hand as the rites require."

"Do not make me a sharer in wrong, lord of men," said Arjuna. "This is not a
dharma that others follow. You should marry first. Then this strong-armed Bhima,
of unimaginable deeds. Then I. Then Nakula after me, and Madri's son Sahadeva
last. Vrikodara and I and the twins, king, and this girl, we all belong to you.
Things being as they are, think, and do what should be done here, what is right
and brings honour, and what would please the Panchala king. Tell us. We all
stand under your command."

They all looked at glorious Krishna standing there. Then, sitting there, they
looked at one another, and each held her in his heart. For as they looked at
Draupadi, love rose up in all of them, men of boundless energy, and threw their
senses into turmoil. The Panchala princess's lovely beauty had been made by the
Creator himself. It was greater than that of other women, and it stole the
hearts of all beings.

Kunti's son Yudhishthira understood their looks and their feelings. Remembering
all of Dvaipayana's words, bull among men, and afraid of a split among them, the
king said to his brothers, "Lovely Draupadi will be the wife of us all."

---

When they heard their elder brother's words, the sons of Pandu, men of boundless
energy, sat there, all of them turning the matter over in their minds.

Meanwhile the hero of the Vrishnis, who had guessed that these were the heroes
of the Kurus, came with Rohini's son to the potter's workshop where those heroes
were sitting. There Krishna and Rohini's son saw long-armed Ajatashatru seated,
and the others sitting round him, shining like fire. Vasudeva went up to Kunti's
son, the best of those who uphold dharma. Pressing the feet of King Yudhishthira
of the Ajamidha line, he said, "I am Krishna." Rohini's son did the same after
him. The Kurus welcomed the two of them with joy. And the two heroes of the
Yadus clasped the feet of their father's sister, chief of the Bharatas.

Ajatashatru, the hero of the Kurus, told Krishna that all was well, and asked
after him. "How did you find us out, Vasudeva," he asked, "when we were all
living here in hiding?"

Vasudeva laughed. "Fire is known even when it is hidden, king. Who among men
could do such a deed of courage, except the Pandavas? By good fortune all of you
Pandavas, the crushers of enemies, have escaped from that fire. By good fortune
the wicked son of Dhritarashtra and his ministers have not had their wish. Bless
you. Grow, like a hidden fire that is fed. Let none of the kings here know you.
We two will go back to our camp now."

With the Pandava's leave, he of unfading glory went quickly away with Baladeva.

---

Dhrishtadyumna, the Panchala, had followed the two joys of the Kurus as they
went to the potter's house. Without being recognised, he set men all round, and
he himself stayed hidden near the potter's house.

In the evening Bhima, the crusher of enemies, Jishnu, and the great twins went
out for alms, and in good spirits they brought what they had been given to
Yudhishthira.

Then generous Kunti, when the time came, spoke to Drupada's daughter. "Take the
first portion of this, good lady, and make the offering to the gods, and give
alms to a brahmin. Give to those who want food, the people who have come to
shelter round us. Then quickly divide what is left. Half is for the four and for
me and for you. Give the other half, good lady, to Bhima, the one who looks like
a bull in rut, the dark young man, strong of build. This hero always eats a
great deal."

The princess, delighted, and trusting that what she said was right, did just as
she was told, the good woman, and they all ate.

Madri's son Sahadeva, the swift, made a bed of kusha grass on the ground. Each
of the heroes spread his own deerskin, and they slept on the ground. The best of
the Kurus lay with their heads to the south, the quarter ruled by Agastya. Kunti
lay above their heads, and Krishna lay across at their feet. She lay on the
ground with the sons of Pandu on the kusha grass, as if she had been made a
pillow for their feet. There was no sorrow in her, and she did not think less of
those bulls of the Kurus.

The heroes told one another wonderful stories about armies, about heavenly
weapons, chariots and elephants, swords, maces and axes.

Then the son of the Panchala king heard the stories they told, and he saw
Krishna lying there, and all his men saw them too. Prince Dhrishtadyumna went
quickly to tell King Drupada everything that had happened and everything they
had said in the night.

The Panchala king was downcast, because he had not found the Pandavas. The great
man questioned Dhrishtadyumna. "Where has Krishna gone? Who has taken her?
Surely she has not gone to a shudra, or to a man of low birth, or to a vaishya
who pays tribute? Surely no foot smeared with dirt has been set on my head?
Surely the garland has not fallen on a burning ground? Is he a man of the best
of our own class, or even of a higher class? Surely no one has set his left foot
on my head today, son, by touching Krishna? Shall I make offerings in great joy,
joined with Partha, the bull among men? Tell me truly, great one: who is this
who has won my daughter today? Are the sons of that hero of the Kurus,
Vichitravirya's son, alive today? Was it Partha, the younger brother, who took
up the bow today and brought down the target?"

**Divergences.**

* **"Share it all together."** In the CE, Kunti's unseeing words (1.182.2) are only the start.
  She herself calls them a mistake and asks Yudhishthira to find a way out (1.182.4–5).
  Arjuna declines to marry before his elder brothers (1.182.8–9). Yudhishthira sees that all
  five have fallen in love with her and fears a split among them. Remembering Vyasa's words
  (see 1.157), he decides (1.182.14–15). Drupada's objections, and Vyasa's account of her past
  lives, come in the next adhyayas. See ledger L-96.
* The vulgate has Bhima and Arjuna call out "Mother, we have brought alms!" as the other three
  brothers are already inside (\*1887). The CE says only that they presented her "as alms"
  (1.182.1).
* The vulgate has Krishna also say, "By good fortune Krishna was won by courage" (\*1892).

**Choices.**

* 1.182.1 *bhārgavakarmaśālām*: "the potter's workshop", as at 1.176.6.
* 1.182.2 *bhuṅkta*: "enjoy, share, eat". Kunti's word for alms is also the word for enjoying a
  wife.
* 1.182.4 *yathocitam*: "as usual", what she always says of alms.
* 1.182.8 *na dharmo hy ayam īpsito 'nyaiḥ*: "this is not a dharma others follow".
* 1.182.15 *mithobhedabhayāt*: "for fear of a split among them".
* 1.183.4 *ājamīḍha*: "of the Ajamidha line", a Kuru epithet.
* 1.183.9 *nihitaṃ yad guhāyām*: literally "what is laid in a cave", a hidden fire.
* 1.184.9 *agastyaśāstām … diśam*: "the quarter ruled by Agastya", the south.
* 1.184.15: Drupada's images of disgrace (a foot smeared with dirt on his head, the garland on a
  burning ground, a left foot on his head) are his.
* 1.184.18: *vicitravīryasya … putrāḥ* is taken as "the sons of Vichitravirya's son", that is
  Pandu's sons. *yavīyasā pārthena*, "the younger Partha", is Arjuna.

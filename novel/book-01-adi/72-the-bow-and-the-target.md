# The Bow and the Target

Then the five Pandava brothers, tigers among men, set out to see Draupadi and
that great festival of the gods. As the tigers among men, the tamers of enemies,
went on with their mother, they saw many brahmins on the road, travelling in
groups. The brahmins, king, said to the Pandavas, who looked like brahmin
students, "Where are you going, sirs, and where have you come from?"

"You should know that we are brothers who have come from Ekachakra," said
Yudhishthira, "travelling together with our mother. We worship the gods."

"Go today to the Panchalas, to Drupada's palace," said the brahmins. "There is
to be a great bridegroom choice there, with very great wealth. We are going
there too, in one company. There will be a great, wonderful festival. The
daughter of great Drupada Yajnasena was born from the middle of the altar. Her
eyes are like lotus petals. She is lovely, flawless in every limb, delicate and
spirited, the sister of mighty Dhrishtadyumna, Drona's enemy, who was born in
the blazing fire, bright as fire, strong-armed, with armour, sword, arrows and
bow. His sister is Draupadi, flawless in every limb, slender-waisted, and the
scent of blue lotuses comes from her two miles away. We are going to see
Yajnasena's daughter, whose bridegroom choice is at hand, and that great
festival of the gods.

"Kings and princes will come, men who sacrifice with rich gifts to the priests,
who study the Veda, pure, great-hearted and strict in their vows, young and
handsome, from many lands. Great warriors skilled in weapons will come, lords of
the earth. Those lords of men will give gifts of every kind to win, wealth and
cows, and food of every sort. We will take it all, see the bridegroom choice,
enjoy the festival, and go where we like. Actors, bards and dancers, singers and
heralds will gather from many lands, and mighty wrestlers. So when you have
satisfied your curiosity, seen it all and taken your gifts, you great men will
come back with us. Krishna may see you all standing there, handsome and godlike,
and choose one of you by chance for her husband. This brother of yours is
handsome and strong-armed. If he wrestles, he may win a great deal of wealth by
chance."

"We will gladly go with you, sirs," said Yudhishthira, "to see that festival of
the gods, the girl's bridegroom choice."

---

When they had been told this, Janamejaya, the Pandavas went on towards the
southern Panchalas, protected by King Drupada. Then on the road the Pandavas saw
great Dvaipayana, pure of soul and without stain, king. They honoured him
properly, and he comforted them. When their talk was over, he gave them leave,
and they went on towards Drupada's home. Looking at the lovely forests and
lakes, and staying here and there, the great warriors went slowly on. Studying
the Veda, pure, gentle and kindly spoken, the joys of the Kurus came in time to
the Panchalas.

When the Pandavas saw the city and the camp, they made their lodging in a
potter's workshop. There they lived on alms, following the way of life of
brahmins. And no one anywhere knew that the heroes had come.

Yajnasena's wish was always, *I would give Krishna to Kiritin, the Pandava.* But
he did not say this aloud. Looking for the Kaunteyas, Janamejaya, the Panchala
had a stiff bow made that no one could bend, Bharata. He also had an artificial
device made, hung in the air, and with that device the king set up a golden
target.

"Whoever strings this bow," said Drupada, "and with it strung shoots arrows past
the device and hits the target, will win my daughter."

King Drupada proclaimed this everywhere, and when they heard it, all the kings
came there, Bharata. Great seers came to see the bridegroom choice, and the
Kurus led by Duryodhana, with Karna, king. Fortunate brahmins came from many
lands. Great Drupada honoured the companies of kings. Then all the townspeople,
roaring like the ocean, came to Shishumarapura, and the kings took their places.

North-east of the city, on level, fair ground, the arena shone, surrounded on
every side by buildings. It had a wall and a moat, and was decked with gates and
arches. It was covered all over with a bright canopy. It rang with hundreds of
trumpets, was scented with costly aloe smoke, sprinkled with sandal water and
adorned with garlands. It was enclosed on every side by fine tall palaces like
the peaks of Kailasa, scraping the sky. They were hung with nets of gold,
floored with gems, with easy stairs and great seats, covered with fine cloths,
scented with the best aloe, white as swans, fragrant for miles around. They had
a hundred wide doors, with beds and seats, their walls inlaid with many metals,
like the peaks of the Himalaya.

There, on stands of many kinds, well adorned, all the kings sat vying with one
another. People saw them sitting there, of great spirit and courage, lions among
kings, fortunate, decked with black aloe, generous, good to brahmins, protectors
of their kingdoms, loved by all the world for their good deeds. The people of
the city and the country sat on costly platforms all round, to delight in seeing
Krishna. The Pandavas sat with the brahmins, looking at the matchless wealth of
the Panchala king.

The gathering went on for many days, king, with many gifts of jewels, and lit up
by actors and dancers. On the sixteenth day, while the lovely gathering was
going on, Draupadi came down into the arena, bull of the Bharatas. She had
bathed, and she was beautifully dressed and decked with every ornament. She
carried the golden bowl for the hero, finely adorned. The Somakas' family
priest, a pure brahmin who knew the mantras, spread the grass and poured ghee
into the fire as the rules require. When he had satisfied the fire and had the
brahmins speak blessings, he silenced all the instruments on every side.

When it was silent, lord of the people, Dhrishtadyumna went into the middle of
the arena, and in a voice as deep as a cloud he spoke these words aloud, gently,
full of meaning.

"Here is the bow, here is the target, and here are the arrows. Hear me, all you
kings. Shoot the target through the opening of the device with five arrows that
fly through the air. Whoever does this very hard deed, and has family, beauty
and strength, today this sister of mine, Krishna, will be his wife. I do not
lie."

When he had said this to them, Drupada's son turned to Draupadi, and named for
her the kings who had gathered, by name, by family and by deeds.

**Divergences.**

* **No fish, no water.** In the popular story the target is a fish turning on a wheel, which
  Arjuna must hit by looking at its reflection in water or oil below. The CE has "an
  artificial device hung in the air" and "a golden target" (1.176.10), to be shot "through the
  opening of the device with five arrows" (1.176.34). Neither the CE, its rejected lines, nor
  Ganguli (G 1.187) has a fish or a reflection. See ledger L-94.
* **No Krishna among the guests yet.** The vulgate names the Yadavas with Vasudeva here
  (\*1810). The CE brings Krishna into the list of kings in the next adhyaya.
* The vulgate adds that Drupada made the bow to be bent only by Arjuna, and names it (\*1809).
  The CE says only that it was stiff, "not to be bent" (1.176.9).

**Choices.**

* 1.175.4 *devadarśinaḥ*: "who see (worship) the gods". Yudhishthira's evasive answer.
* 1.176.8: Drupada's secret wish to give Krishna to Arjuna is the first time the CE mentions it.
  See ledger L-92.
* 1.176.10 *yantraṃ vaihāyasam*: "a device in the air".
* 1.176.15 *śiśumārapuram*: the place where the kings assembled, named as in the text.
* 1.176.30 *vīrakāṃsyam*: "the hero's vessel", a golden bowl, perhaps for the garland.
* 1.176.34 *daśārdhaiḥ*: "with half of ten", five arrows.

# Pandu's Conquests

Kuntibhoja's daughter had beauty, strength of spirit and every good quality. She
delighted in dharma, and she kept great vows. Her father held a bridegroom
choice for her. And there, among thousands of kings, she found Pandu, with teeth
like a lion's, shoulders like an elephant's and eyes like a bull's, a man of
great strength. The joy of the Kurus, whose good fortune had no measure, was
joined with Kuntibhoja's daughter as Indra, the generous, was joined with
Paulomi.

Then Bhishma, the keeper of the god's vow, went to the capital of the Madras.
Madri, the daughter of the lord of Madra, was famous in the three worlds and
renowned among all kings, and her beauty had no equal on earth. She was bought
for Pandu with a great deal of wealth, and Bhishma had her married to the great
Pandu. When they saw Pandu, that tiger among men, with his lion's chest, his
elephant's shoulders, his bull's eyes and his proud spirit, men on earth were
amazed.

---

When he was married, Pandu, full of strength and energy, set out to conquer the
earth, against many enemies. First that lion among men, who carried the fame of
the Kauravas, went against the Dasharnas, who had wronged the Kurus before, and
he beat them in battle.

Then Pandu took his army, with its banners of every kind, crowded with
elephants, horses and chariots and thick with companies of foot soldiers. Darva,
the protector of the kingdom of Magadha, was killed in Rajagriha. He had wronged
all the heroes and was the enemy of every king.

Then Pandu took his treasure, his beasts and his troops and went to Mithila, and
he beat the Videhas in battle. So too among the Kashis, the Suhmas and the
Pundras, bull of the Bharatas, he won fame for the Kurus by the strength of his
own arms.

Pandu was a fire. His flood of arrows was its great flame, and his weapons were
its rays. The kings who came against that tamer of enemies were burned up. He
shattered their armies with his own. He brought the kings and their armies under
his control and set them to paying tribute. All the kings on earth were beaten
by him, and they thought him the one hero, like Indra among the gods.

All the lords of the earth came to him with joined hands, bowing, bringing
wealth and jewels of every kind. They brought gems, pearls and coral, gold and
silver, the finest cattle, the finest horses, the finest chariots, and
elephants. They brought donkeys, camels and buffaloes, and all the goats and
sheep they had. The king of the City of the Elephant accepted it all.

Pandu took it and went home again, his beasts glad. He wanted to delight his own
kingdom and the City of the Elephant. "The fame of Shantanu, that lion among
kings, and of wise Bharata had been lost," said the kings and the royal
ministers when they came together, "and Pandu has raised it up again. Those who
once took the Kurus' kingdoms and the Kurus' wealth have been made to pay
tribute by Pandu, the lion of the City of the Elephant."

They were glad and confident, and so were the people of the city and the
country. All of them, with Bhishma at their head, went out to meet him when he
came. They had not gone far from the City of the Elephant when they saw the
whole world before them filled with happy people of every kind. And the
Kauravas, with Bhishma, could see no end to the jewels, high and low, brought on
vehicles of every kind, and the finest elephants, horses and chariots, the
cattle, camels and sheep.

Pandu, who swelled the joy of Kausalya, bowed at the feet of Bhishma, who had
been a father to him. And he honoured the people of the city and the country,
each as they deserved. Bhishma met the son who had crushed the kingdoms of
others and come home with his purpose achieved, and he wept for joy. Then, to
the great sound of hundreds of trumpets and drums, Pandu entered the City of the
Elephant, delighting the townspeople everywhere.

---

With Dhritarashtra's leave, Pandu presented the wealth he had won by his own
arms to Bhishma, to Satyavati and to his mother. The righteous man sent some of
that wealth to Vidura too, and he satisfied his friends with wealth as well.
Then Bhishma gladdened Satyavati and famous Kausalya with the fine jewels Pandu
had won, Bharata. His mother Kausalya rejoiced in her son of matchless
splendour. She put her arms round that bull among men as Paulomi embraced
Jayanta.

With the wealth that the hero's courage had won, Dhritarashtra offered a hundred
great horse sacrifices, with a hundred thousand gifts to the priests at each.

Then Pandu, bull of the Bharatas, with Kunti and Madri, shook off all idleness
and went to live in the forest. He left his palace and its fine beds, and lived
always in the wilds, given up to hunting. He roamed the lovely southern slopes
of the Himalaya mountains, and lived on the mountain heights and in forests of
great sal trees.

Living in the forest with Kunti and Madri, Pandu shone like a splendid elephant
of Indra's between two cow elephants. The forest people saw the Bharata king
roaming with his two wives, a hero carrying arrows, sword and bow, in bright
armour, a master of the great weapons. "He is a god," they thought. At
Dhritarashtra's command, men tirelessly brought him everything he wanted and
enjoyed, to the edges of the forest.

Then Bhishma, the son of the river, heard of a daughter of King Devaka, born of
a brahmin father and a shudra mother. She was young and beautiful. That bull
among men asked for her and had her brought, and he married her to the wise
Vidura. On her Vidura, the joy of the Kurus, fathered sons who were well
disciplined and like him in their virtues.

**Divergences and the CE's own doublets.**

* **Madri's brother not named here.** The CE says only that Madri was "bought with
  great wealth" (1.105.5). The vulgate adds a long passage in which Bhishma goes to
  Shalya, who explains that his family's custom is a bride price, and Bhishma pays
  it (App. I No. 61). The novel keeps to the CE's two verses.
* **No garland.** The vulgate has Kunti put a garland on Pandu's shoulder (\*1131).
  The CE says only that she "found" him among the kings (1.105.2).
* **Two Kausalyas.** At 1.99–100 "Kausalya" is Ambika, Dhritarashtra's mother. Here
  "Kausalya" is Pandu's mother (1.105.25; 1.106.3–4), who is Ambalika. Both sisters
  seem to share the name, from their Kosala mother. The novel uses it as the text
  does.

**Choices.**

* 1.105.3 *paulomyā maghavān iva*: "as Maghavan (Indra) with Paulomi (Shachi)".
* 1.105.4 *devavratena*: Bhishma by his birth name, Devavrata. Rendered "the keeper
  of the god's vow".
* 1.105.10 *dārvaḥ*: the CE's reading, taken as the name of the lord of Magadha,
  Darva. Ganguli has "Dhirga" (G 1.113), apparently from a reading *dīrgha*. The CE says
  only that he "was killed in Rajagriha", in the middle of Pandu's campaign;
  Ganguli says Pandu killed him.
* 1.105.25 *pituḥ pādau*: "his father's feet". Bhishma is meant: he raised the
  princes "as sons" (1.102.15) and weeps at the meeting in the next verse. The novel
  says so.
* 1.106.4 *jayantam iva paulomī*: Jayanta is Indra's son by Paulomi.
* 1.106.6 *jitatandrīḥ*: "having conquered sloth".
* 1.106.12 *pāraśavīm*: "a pārashavi", the daughter of a brahmin by a shudra woman,
  like Vidura himself. Her name is not given.

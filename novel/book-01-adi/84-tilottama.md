# Tilottama

"Then all the divine seers, the perfected ones and the great seers," said
Narada, "were in great distress when they saw that great slaughter. Having
conquered anger, having mastered themselves and their senses, they went to the
Grandfather's house, out of pity for the world. There they saw the Grandfather
seated with the gods, surrounded on every side by perfected ones and brahmin
seers. The god Mahadeva was there. Agni was there with Vayu, and the moon and
the sun, Dharma, Parameshthin and Budha. The Vaikhanasas, the Valakhilyas, the
forest-dwellers, the Marichipas, the Ajas, the Avimudhas and the Tejogarbhas,
ascetics all, and all these seers, sat in attendance on the Grandfather.

"Then all the great seers went to him together and told him everything that
Sunda and Upasunda had done. They told the Grandfather all of it, how it was
done, what was done, and in what order. Then all the hosts of the gods, and the
great seers, pressed the Grandfather on that very matter.

"When the Grandfather had heard what they all said, he thought for a moment to
decide what should be done. Then, with a view to killing the two, he sent for
Vishvakarman. When he saw Vishvakarman, the Grandfather, of great austerity,
gave him this order: 'Create a woman here whom all will desire.'

"Vishvakarman bowed to the Grandfather and welcomed his words. After careful
thought, he made a heavenly woman. Whatever was beautiful to look at in the
three worlds, moving or still, he gathered with care from here and there. He set
jewels in her body by the ten million. So he made her of heaps of jewels, in the
form of a goddess. Made by Vishvakarman with great care, she had no equal among
the women of the three worlds in beauty. There was no part of her body, however
small, that did not have its fill of beauty, and no place where the eyes of
those who looked at her did not linger. Like Shri in bodily form, lovely and
beautiful in shape, she stole the eyes and the hearts of all beings. Because she
had been made by gathering the jewels grain by grain, *tila* by *tila*, the
Grandfather named her Tilottama.

"'Go, Tilottama,' said the Grandfather, 'to the asuras Sunda and Upasunda, and
tempt them with your desirable beauty, good lady. Make it so that, as soon as
they see you, the wealth of your beauty sets them at odds with each other.'

"She promised, 'So be it,' and bowed to the Grandfather. Then she walked round
the circle of the gods, keeping them on her right. The blessed Brahma sat facing
east. Maheshvara was on the south, the gods sat on the north, and the seers were
all round. As she walked round the circle, Indra and the blessed Sthanu held
firm and kept their composure. But Sthanu wanted very much to see her. When she
went to his side, another face, with curling lashes, came out on the south. When
she moved round behind, a face came out on the west. When she went to the north
side, a face came out on the north. And great Indra too grew eyes, a thousand of
them, large and red at the corners, all over him, at the sides, behind and in
front. That is how Sthanu, the great god, came to have four faces long ago, and
how the slayer of Bala came to have a thousand eyes.

"So too, among the companies of the gods and the seers, faces turned everywhere
she went. The eyes of those great ones fell on her body, the eyes of all of
them, except the god Grandfather alone. As she went, all the gods and the great
seers thought, from the wealth of her beauty, that the work was as good as done.
When Tilottama had gone, the maker of the worlds sent away all the gods and the
companies of seers.

---

"Meanwhile the two daityas, having conquered the earth, with no rivals and no
cares, had put the three worlds at their feet, and they had done all they meant
to do. They took all the jewels of the gods, the gandharvas and the yakshas, of
the snakes, the kings and the rakshasas, and they were very content. When there
was no one left to stand against them, they gave up all effort and enjoyed
themselves like immortals. With women, garlands and perfumes, with plenty of
food of every kind, and with drinks of every sort to delight the heart, they
took the greatest pleasure. They amused themselves like immortals wherever they
liked, in the women's quarters, in forest parks and gardens on the mountains.

"Then once they went to amuse themselves on a ridge of the Vindhya, on a level
stretch of rock, among sal trees with flowering tops. All kinds of heavenly
pleasures were brought to them there, and they sat happily on fine seats with
their women. Then the women came to entertain them with music and dancing, and
came to please them with songs full of praise.

"Then Tilottama, picking flowers in the forest, in an alluring dress of a single
red cloth, picking the karnikara flowers that grew along the river banks, came
slowly to the place where the two great asuras were. They had drunk the best
liquor, and the corners of their eyes were red with drink. As soon as they saw
that woman with the lovely hips, they were overcome. They sprang up, left their
seats, and went to where she stood. Both of them were mad with desire, and both
of them begged her. Sunda took the lovely-browed woman by the right hand, and
Upasunda took Tilottama by the left.

"They were drunk with the boon they had won and with their own strength, drunk
with pride in their wealth and jewels, and drunk with wine. Drunk with all
these, they frowned at each other, and, filled with passion and desire, they
spoke to each other.

"'She is my wife,' said Sunda, 'and so she is your elder, to be honoured.'

"'She is my wife,' said Upasunda, 'and so she is your daughter-in-law.'

"'She is not yours. She is mine.' So anger took hold of them. For her sake both
seized terrible maces. Seizing the terrible maces, deluded by desire for her,
they struck each other, crying, 'Me first! Me first!' Struck down by the maces,
the two terrible ones fell to the ground, their bodies smeared with blood, like
two suns fallen from the sky. Then the women fled, and the whole host of daityas
went to the underworld, trembling with grief and fear.

"Then the pure-souled Grandfather came there with the gods and the great seers,
to honour Tilottama. When Brahma offered her a boon, she asked only for his
goodwill. Pleased, the Grandfather said to her, 'You will move through the
worlds where the sun moves, lovely one. And because of your splendour, no one
will be able to look at you for long.' When he had given her this boon, the
Grandfather of all the worlds put the three worlds in Indra's charge, and the
lord went back to the world of Brahma.

"So those two, who were always together and of one mind in all things, killed
each other in their anger over Tilottama. That is why I tell you this, out of
love for you all, best of the Bharatas. Bless you. If you want to please me, do
what you must so that there is no quarrel among any of you over Draupadi."

---

When the great seer Narada had said this to them, king, those great men came
together and made an agreement among themselves, in the presence of Narada, the
divine seer of boundless power.

"Whichever of us sees another sitting with Draupadi shall live in the forest for
twelve years as a celibate student."

When the Pandavas, who kept to dharma, had made this agreement, the great sage
Narada was pleased, and he went away wherever he wished. So, long ago, urged on
by Narada, they made the agreement, and they did not quarrel with one another,
Bharata.

**Divergences.**

* **No year with each husband.** In the popular telling, Draupadi lived with each brother in
  turn for a year. The CE's agreement is only this: "whichever of us sees another sitting with
  Draupadi shall live in the forest for twelve years as a celibate student" (1.204.28). The
  year-by-year rotation is in a rejected line (\*2012). See ledger L-16.
* **Shiva's four faces, Indra's thousand eyes.** In this CE passage they come from gazing at
  Tilottama (1.203.23–26). See ledger L-102.
* The vulgate adds that Brahma and Vishnu stayed unmoved (\*2006). The CE names Indra and Sthanu
  as holding firm (1.203.22), and only Brahma as not looking (1.203.28).

**Choices.**

* 1.203.5: the classes of ascetics are named as in the text: the Vaikhanasas, Valakhilyas,
  forest-dwellers, Marichipas ("drinkers of sunbeams"), Ajas, Avimudhas and Tejogarbhas.
* 1.203.17 *tilaṃ tilam*: "grain by grain". The name Tilottama, "best of *tila*", is the text's
  own etymology.
* 1.203.22 *sthāṇuḥ*: "the Pillar", Shiva.
* 1.203.26 *balasūdanaḥ*: "the slayer of Bala", Indra.
* 1.204.9 *veṣam ākṣiptam*: "an alluring dress".
* 1.204.16 *guruḥ … vadhūḥ*: each brother claims her by placing her in a relation that forbids her
  to the other.
* 1.204.20 *pātālam*: "the underworld".
* 1.204.23: the boon reads literally "no one will look at you well because of your splendour".
* 1.204.28 *brahmacārī*: "as a celibate student".

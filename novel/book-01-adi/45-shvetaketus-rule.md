# Shvetaketu's Rule

When she had said this, the king, who knew dharma, spoke again to the queen,
with these fine words full of dharma.

"It is as you say, Kunti. Vyushitashva did this long ago, lovely one, for he was
like an immortal. But now I will tell you about dharma. Listen to me. It is the
ancient dharma, seen by the seers, great men who knew dharma.

"In the old days, so they say, women were not kept shut away, lovely one. They
went where they pleased and enjoyed themselves as they liked. They were free,
you of the lovely eyes. From girlhood they went from their husbands to other
men, fortunate one, and it was not against dharma. That was dharma in those
days. Even now, creatures born as animals follow that ancient dharma, free of
desire and hatred. This dharma, seen in ancient times, is honoured by the great
seers. And among the northern Kurus it is still the way, you whose thighs are
like plantain stems. For that eternal dharma favours women.

"But the present rule was set up in this world not long ago. Hear from me in
full who set it up, and why.

"There was a great seer named Uddalaka, so we have heard. He had a son, a sage,
named Shvetaketu. It was he who made this rule among men, so we have heard, out
of anger, lotus-eyed one. Hear why.

"Once, in front of Shvetaketu's father, a brahmin took his mother by the hand,
and said, 'Let us go.' The seer's son was furious and could not bear it, when he
saw his mother being led away as if by force. When his father saw that
Shvetaketu was angry, he said to him, 'Do not be angry, son. This is the eternal
dharma. The women of all classes on earth are unconfined. As cows are, son, so
are all creatures, each in its own kind.'

"But the seer's son, Shvetaketu, would not accept that dharma. And he made this
rule for men and women on earth, noble lady, for human beings, but not for other
creatures. From that time on this rule has stood, so we have heard.

"'From today, a woman who strays from her husband will commit a sin, the same
evil as killing an unborn child, and it will bring her misery. A man who strays
from his wife, when she has been chaste since girlhood and true to him, will
commit the same sin on earth. And a wife who is appointed by her husband to bear
a child, and will not do it, will commit the same sin.'

"So Uddalaka's son, Shvetaketu, set up this rule of dharma by force long ago,
timid one. And we have heard, lovely one, that when Saudasa appointed his wife
Madayanti to bear a child, she went to the seer Vasishtha. From him the lovely
woman got a son named Ashmaka. She was Kalmashapada's wife, and she did it to
please her husband. And you know how we ourselves were born, lotus-eyed one,
from Krishna Dvaipayana, timid one, to make the Kuru line grow.

"So, looking at all these reasons, you should do what I tell you. It is in
keeping with dharma, blameless one. Those who know dharma know this as dharma,
princess: at each of her seasons, a woman must not pass over her husband, you of
firm vows. At all the other times, so they say, a woman is entitled to her
freedom. Good people call this the ancient dharma. Those who know dharma know
that whatever a husband tells his wife, princess, whether it is in keeping with
dharma or not, she should do it. And all the more when he longs for a son and
cannot beget one himself, as I long to see a son, you of flawless limbs.

"So, lovely one, to win your favour, I raise my joined hands to my head. My
palms are red, and my fingers are like lotus petals. At my bidding, you of the
lovely hair, you should bear sons with good qualities by a brahmin great in
austerity. Through you, you of the full hips, I may go the way of those who have
sons."

When he said this, the lovely Kunti, who delighted in what was dear and good to
her husband, answered Pandu, the conqueror of enemy cities.

"When I was a girl in my father's house, I was given the task of honouring the
guests. There I waited on a fierce brahmin of strict vows, whose purposes in
dharma were hidden. People know him as Durvasas. I pleased that disciplined man
with every effort. The blessed one gave me a boon joined with the power of a
spell. He gave me a set of mantras and said to me, 'Whatever god you call with
this mantra, willing or unwilling, he will come into your power.'

"That is what the brahmin told me in my father's house, Bharata, and it is true.
Now its time has come. With your leave, king, I can call a god with that mantra,
royal seer, so that we may have children, lord. Tell me which god I should call,
you who know the truth best. Know that I am ready for this work, waiting only
for your leave."

"Try this very day, lovely one, as the rules require," said Pandu. "Call Dharma,
lovely one, for among the gods he has the greatest share of merit. Dharma could
never be joined with what is against dharma. And the world will think, lovely
one, *This is dharma.* He will surely be the most righteous of the Kurus. And a
son given by Dharma will never set his mind on what is against dharma. So put
dharma first, you of the bright smile. Restrain yourself, and win Dharma over
with worship and with the spell."

When her husband said this, the lovely woman said, "So be it." She bowed to him,
and with his leave she walked round him, keeping him on her right.

**Divergences.**

* **Kunti says nothing of Karna.** She tells Pandu about the mantra (1.113.32–37) but
  not that she has already used it. The CE never says she told him.
* The vulgate's fuller version of Shvetaketu's story, with his father's austerities
  and his words to the brahmin (App. I Nos. 64–65), and a verse on women's desire
  (\*1189), are not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.113.4 *anāvṛtāḥ*: "uncovered", "not shut away", i.e. unconfined.
* 1.113.6 *kāmadveṣavivarjitāḥ*: "free of desire and hatred". Kept literal.
* 1.113.14: the father's words, "as cows are … so are creatures, each in its own
  kind", are given as spoken.
* 1.113.17 *bhrūṇahatyā*: "killing an embryo", reckoned among the worst sins.
* 1.113.21–22: Saudasa and Kalmashapada are the same king. Madayanti's son is Ashmaka.
* 1.113.27: Pandu's claim that a wife should obey "whether it is in keeping with
  dharma or not" is his, given as he says it.
* 1.113.29: the joined hands raised to the head, with the red palms, are the text's.
* 1.113.35 *akāmo vā sakāmo vā*: "willing or unwilling". This version of the mantra's
  power is Kunti's. At 1.104.7 Durvasas promised only "a son by his grace".
* 1.113.42 *upacārābhicārābhyām*: "by service and by spell".
* 1.113.43 *pradakṣiṇam*: she walked round him keeping him on her right, a mark of
  respect.

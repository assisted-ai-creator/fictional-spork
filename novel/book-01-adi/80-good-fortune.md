# Good Fortune

Then the kings learned from their trusted spies that lovely Draupadi had been
joined to the Pandavas as her husbands. The great man who had drawn the bow and
pierced the target was Arjuna, the best of victors, who carries the great bow
and arrows. The strong man who had lifted Shalya, the king of Madra, and whirled
him round, and in his anger had frightened men in battle with a tree, and had
not been flustered at all, that great man was Bhima, terrible to meet, the
feller of the ranks of enemy armies.

When they heard that the sons of King Pandu, the Kaunteyas, had come in the form
of brahmins, the lords of men were amazed. For they had heard that Kunti and her
sons had been burned to death in the house of lac, and all the kings thought of
them as born again. They cursed Bhishma, and Dhritarashtra the Kaurava, for the
cruel deed that Purochana had done.

When the bridegroom choice was over, all the kings went back as they had come,
knowing that the Pandavas had been chosen.

---

King Duryodhana, downcast, turned back with his brothers, with Ashvatthama, his
uncle, Karna and Kripa, when he saw that Draupadi had chosen the man of the
white horses. Duhshasana, ashamed, spoke to him quietly.

"If he had not been a brahmin, he would not have won Draupadi. No one knew him
for what he was, king, for Dhananjaya. I think fate is supreme, and a man's
effort is useless. Shame on our effort, brother, when the Pandavas are alive."

Talking like this, and blaming Purochana, they went into Hastinapura, downcast
and dismayed. They were frightened, and their plans had come to nothing, for
they had seen the mighty Parthas free of the fire and allied with Drupada. And
they thought of Dhrishtadyumna, and of Shikhandi, and of Drupada's other sons,
all skilled in every kind of fighting.

But Vidura had heard that Draupadi had chosen the Pandavas, and that the sons of
Dhritarashtra had come back ashamed, their pride broken. Then the steward, glad
at heart and full of wonder, said to Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, "By good
fortune the Kurus prosper!"

When King Dhritarashtra, Vichitravirya's son, heard Vidura's words, he was
delighted, Bharata. "Good fortune!" he said. "Good fortune!" For the king, whose
eyes were his wisdom, in his ignorance thought that Drupada's daughter had
chosen his eldest son, Duryodhana. Then he ordered many ornaments for Draupadi,
and told his son Duryodhana, "Have Krishna brought here."

Afterwards Vidura told him that the Pandavas had been chosen, and that all the
heroes were well, and honoured by Drupada, and that they had many other kinsmen
through the marriage, men of strength.

"Pandu's sons are to me just as they were to him," said Dhritarashtra, "and even
more. This is a greater joy, Vidura, and I count it as prosperity, that the
heroic Pandavas are well and have friends. For who, steward, having won Drupada
and his kin as a friend, would not wish to prosper, even a king who has lost his
fortune and wants to thrive?"

"May your mind stay like this always, king," said Vidura, "for a hundred years."

---

Then Duryodhana and Radheya came to Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, and said
to him, "We could not speak to you, king, in front of Vidura. Now we are alone,
we ask: what is it you mean to do? You think your rivals' growth is your own,
father. You praise them to the steward, best of men. You are doing one thing,
sinless one, when another should be done. Their strength should be broken all
the time, father. So we have come to consider what should be done now, so that
they do not swallow us up, with our sons, our army and our kin."

"I think just as you two do," said Dhritarashtra. "But I do not want to show my
face to Vidura. So I praise their virtues above all, so that Vidura will not
read my purpose in my looks. Now tell me what you think fit, Suyodhana. And you,
Radheya, tell me what you think fit."

"Let us divide Kunti's sons today," said Duryodhana, "and the two Pandavas who
are Madri's sons, through clever brahmins, well trained and trusted. Or let King
Drupada, his sons and all his ministers be tempted with great hoards of wealth.
'Abandon King Yudhishthira, Kunti's son,' they will be told. Or let them make
the Pandavas want to live there. Let each of them describe to the Pandavas the
faults of living here, so that, divided from us, the Pandavas set their minds on
staying there. Or let some clever men, skilled in schemes, set the Parthas
against one another through their love for her. Or let them turn Krishna away
from them. That is easy, since there are so many. Or let them divide the
Pandavas over her, and then her from them.

"Or, king, let men skilled in schemes arrange Bhimasena's death in secret, for
he is the strongest of them. When he is dead, king, their spirit and their
strength will be broken, and they will not strive for the kingdom, for he is
their support. Arjuna cannot be beaten in battle while Vrikodara guards his
back. Without him, Phalguna is not worth a quarter of Radheya in battle. When
they know how weak they are without Bhimasena, and know that we are strong, they
will perish, for they will be the weaker side. Or when the Parthas have come
here, and are under our command, we will set about destroying them as we see
fit, king. Or let each of the Kaunteyas be tempted by beautiful women, one by
one, and then let Krishna grow cold to them. Or let Radheya be sent to bring
them here, and let them be killed on the way by trusted agents in league with
robbers.

"Whichever of these schemes you think free of fault, put it to use before the
time passes. While they have not yet put their trust in Drupada, bull among
kings, they can be overcome today. After that they cannot. This is my thinking,
father, bent on holding them down, whether it is good or bad. What do you think,
Radheya?"

"Duryodhana," said Karna, "I do not think your thinking is sound. The Pandavas
cannot be overcome by schemes, joy of the Kurus. You tried before to hold them
down by subtle schemes, hero, and you could not. When they were living here,
close to you, king, children not yet fledged, you could not harm them. Now they
are fledged, living abroad, grown in every way. The Kaunteyas cannot be overcome
by schemes. That is my view, Achyuta.

"They cannot be brought to ruin, for fate is with them. They are wary, and they
want the place of their father and grandfather. No split can be made among them.
Men who delight in one wife do not split from one another. Nor can others divide
Krishna from them. She chose them when they were poor, and how much less will
she leave them now they are prospering? For this is a thing women want, to be
one woman with many husbands. Krishna has it, and she cannot easily be divided
from them. The Panchala king is a man of noble conduct, and no lover of wealth.
He will surely not abandon the Kaunteyas, even if he is given kingdoms. His son
too is virtuous and devoted to the Pandavas. So I do not think they can be
overcome by schemes in any way.

"But this is what we can do now, bull among men. While the Pandavas have not yet
put down roots, lord of the people, they must be attacked. Let valour be your
choice. While our side is great and the Panchala small, attack them. Do not
hesitate. While they do not have many horses and chariots, and many friends, son
of Gandhari, strike quickly. While the Panchala king and his mighty sons have
not set their minds on war, strike quickly. Strike quickly, before the Varshneya
comes, drawing the Yadava army after him, to win the Pandavas their kingdom.
There is nothing, wealth, pleasures of every kind, or the kingdom itself, that
Krishna would not give up for the Pandavas, lord of the earth.

"Great Bharata won the earth by valour. By valour Indra, the chastiser of Paka,
won the three worlds. Valour is what men praise in a kshatriya, lord of the
people. Valour is the heroes' own dharma, bull among kings. Let us crush Drupada
quickly, king, with a great army of four branches, and bring the Pandavas here.
The Pandavas cannot be won by conciliation, or by gifts, or by division. So
destroy them by valour alone. Conquer them by valour and enjoy this whole earth.
I see no other way to our goal here, lord of men."

When he heard Radheya's words, mighty Dhritarashtra praised him, and then he
said, "This speech, so full of valour, is just what one would expect from you,
wise son of a suta, master of weapons. But let Bhishma, Drona and Vidura, and
the two of you, think again, and find the plan that will bring us happiness."

Then Dhritarashtra sent for all those very famous counsellors, great king, and
took counsel with them.

**Divergences.**

* **Dhritarashtra's mistake.** In the CE the blind king says "Good fortune!" because he thinks
  Duryodhana has won Draupadi (1.192.19). The vulgate makes him say it deliberately, to hide his
  feelings (\*1949). In the CE the pretence comes afterwards: "I do not want to show my face to
  Vidura" (1.193.1–2).
* The vulgate has Vidura's face light up "like a lotus at the sun" at the Kauravas' gloom
  (\*1943), and has Dhritarashtra swear that his love for Pandu's sons is greater than for any
  others (\*1952). Neither is in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.192.10 *śvetavāhanam*: "the man of the white horses", Arjuna.
* 1.192.12 *daivaṃ … paramaṃ … pauruṣaṃ tu nirarthakam*: Duhshasana's fatalism is his own.
* 1.192.17 *kṣattā*: "the steward", Vidura's title.
* 1.192.19 *prajñācakṣuḥ*: "whose eyes were his wisdom", the blind king.
* 1.192.21: *saṃbandhinaḥ*, the Pandavas' "kinsmen through the marriage".
* 1.193.1 *vivektuṃ … ākāram*: "to show my face", that is, to reveal his true feelings.
* 1.193.8 *anurāgataḥ*: "through their love", read as their love for Draupadi.
* 1.193.16 *loptrahāraiḥ*: "with robbers". Duryodhana means the killing to look like a robbery on
  the road.
* 1.194.4 *acyuta*: Karna calls Duryodhana "Achyuta", "unfallen".
* 1.194.7 *mṛjāvataḥ*: "prospering", literally "clean, cared for", as against *paridyūnān*,
  "wretched".
* 1.194.8: Karna's claim about what women want is his own. The narrator does not endorse it.
* 1.194.17 *pākaśāsanaḥ*: Indra, "chastiser of Paka".

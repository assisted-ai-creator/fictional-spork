# One Wife for Five

Then the glorious Panchala sent for Prince Yudhishthira and received him with
the welcome due to a brahmin. The great man, undaunted, questioned Kunti's
splendid son.

"How are we to know you? Are you kshatriyas or brahmins? Are you vaishyas of
fine qualities, or men born of shudra mothers? Or are you perfected beings who
wander everywhere by magic, and have come from heaven for Krishna's sake,
wanting to see her? Tell us the truth, for our doubt is great. Will our minds be
at rest when our doubt is ended? Will our fortune be good, tamer of enemies?
Tell the truth, of your own free will. Truth becomes kings. And for the sake of
the merit of sacrifices and good works, a lie should not be told. When I have
heard your answer, godlike tamer of enemies, I will certainly go ahead with the
marriage as the rules require."

"Do not be downcast, king," said Yudhishthira. "Be glad, Panchala. The wish you
cherished has surely come true, without any doubt. For we are kshatriyas, king,
the sons of great Pandu. Know that I am the eldest son of Kunti, and these are
Bhimasena and Arjuna, by whom your daughter was won in the assembly of kings.
The twins, king of kings, are where Krishna is. Let the sorrow in your heart go,
bull among men. We are kshatriyas. Your daughter has gone like a lotus from one
lake to another. This is the truth, great king. I tell you all of it. For you
are our elder and our highest refuge."

Then King Drupada's eyes rolled with joy, and for a while he could not answer
Yudhishthira properly. With an effort the tamer of enemies held back his joy,
and then the king gave Yudhishthira a fitting answer. The righteous king asked
him how they had fled before, and the Pandava told him everything in order. When
he had heard what Kunti's son said, King Drupada condemned Dhritarashtra, the
lord of the people. He comforted Yudhishthira, Kunti's son, and Drupada, the
best of speakers, promised to win him his kingdom.

Then Kunti and Krishna, Bhimasena and Arjuna and the twins went into a great
house, as the king had directed. They lived there, king, honoured by Yajnasena.

When they were at ease again, the king and his sons said to them, "Let the joy
of the Kurus, strong-armed Arjuna, take her hand today as the rites require. Let
him hold the celebration on a holy day."

Then Yudhishthira, the Dharma King, said to him, "I too must first take a wife,
lord of the people."

"Then take my daughter's hand yourself, as the rites require," said Drupada, "or
give Krishna to whichever of your brothers you think fit, hero."

"Draupadi will be the queen of all of us, king," said Yudhishthira. "So my
mother said before, lord of the people. I am not yet married, and neither is
Bhimasena the Pandava. And this daughter of yours, a jewel, was won by Partha.
This is our agreement, king, that we share a jewel together. We do not want to
give up that agreement, best of kings. By dharma Krishna will be the queen of
all of us. Let her take the hand of each of us in turn before the fire."

"Many queens are ordained for one man, joy of the Kurus," said Drupada, "but
never many men for one woman. You are righteous and pure, Kaunteya. You should
not do a wrong that is against the ways of the world and against the Veda. Why
is your mind set on this?"

"Dharma is subtle, great king," said Yudhishthira. "We do not know its way. We
follow the path that those before us have walked, one after another. My tongue
has never spoken a lie, and my mind does not turn to wrong. My mother says so,
and it is what I have in mind. This is firm dharma, king. Follow it without
hesitating. Do not have any misgivings about it at all, lord of the earth."

"You, Kaunteya, and Kunti and my son Dhrishtadyumna," said Drupada, "talk over
what should be done. Tomorrow we will do it at the proper time."

So they all met and talked it over, Bharata. And then Dvaipayana came there, by
chance, king.

---

All the Pandavas and the glorious Panchala rose to meet him, and when they saw
the great Krishna Dvaipayana they honoured him. The great-minded seer greeted
them all in turn, asked after their welfare, and sat down on a bright golden
seat. Then, at the word of Krishna of boundless splendour, all those best of men
sat down on costly seats.

After a moment Drupada, Prishata's son, the lord of the people, spoke sweetly,
and asked the great one about Draupadi. "How can one woman be the wife of many
men, and yet there be no confusion of dharma? Let the blessed one tell us all
this truly."

"This dharma has lapsed," said Vyasa, "and it is against the world and the Veda.
On this I want to hear what each of you thinks."

"I think it is wrong," said Drupada, "against the world and the Veda. There is
no one wife of many men, best of brahmins. Nor did the great men of old follow
this dharma. And a dharma that is held in so many ways should not be followed as
if it were eternal. So I cannot make up my mind to act in this way. It seems to
me clouded with doubt about dharma."

"How can an elder brother," said Dhrishtadyumna, "if he is a man of good
conduct, go to his younger brother's wife, bull of brahmins, rich in austerity?
Because dharma is subtle, we cannot tell its way at all. People like us cannot
decide whether this is wrong or right, brahmin. So I cannot resolve, in any way,
that Krishna should be the queen of five."

"My tongue has not spoken a lie," said Yudhishthira, "and my mind does not turn
to wrong. My heart goes this way, and it is not wrong in any way. We hear in the
Purana, too, that a woman of the Gautama line named Jatila lived with seven
seers, best of those who uphold dharma. They say that a teacher's word is
dharma, best of those who know dharma, and of all teachers the mother is the
highest. And she has said, 'Share it like alms.' So I think this is dharma, best
of brahmins."

"It is as righteous Yudhishthira says," said Kunti. "I am terribly afraid of a
lie. How can I be freed from a lie?"

"You will be freed from the lie, good lady," said Vyasa, "and this is eternal
dharma. But I will not tell it in front of everyone. Listen to me yourself,
Panchala, how this dharma was ordained and why it is eternal. As Kaunteya has
said, so is dharma. There is no doubt of it."

Then the blessed lord Vyasa Dvaipayana rose, took the king by the hand, and went
into the king's house. The Pandavas and Kunti, and Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's
grandson, stayed there, anxious, waiting for the two of them. Then Dvaipayana
told the great king how it could be dharma for many men to have one wife.

**Divergences.**

* **Other precedents.** In the CE Yudhishthira cites only Jatila of the Gautama line and her
  seven seers (1.188.14). The vulgate adds Varkshi, the sage's daughter who married the ten
  Prachetas brothers (\*1910), and has Yudhishthira cite Vyasa's own words (\*1908).
* The vulgate has Kunti say that she herself told Arjuna to share with his brothers (\*1911).
  Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.187.1 *parigraheṇa brāhmeṇa*: "with the welcome due to a brahmin". Drupada still does not
  know their class.
* 1.187.6 *iṣṭāpūrtena*: "the merit of sacrifices and good works", which a lie would forfeit.
* 1.187.10 *padminīva … hradād anyaṃ hradam*: "like a lotus from one lake to another".
* 1.187.19 *kṣaṇam*: "a celebration".
* 1.187.24 *ratnasya sahabhojanam*: "the sharing of a jewel", the brothers' agreement.
* 1.187.28: Yudhishthira's appeal to "the path those before us have walked" is his. The text
  gives no precedent at this point.
* 1.188.1 *kṛṣṇam*: Vyasa, Krishna Dvaipayana.
* 1.188.6 *vipralambhe*: "lapsed", following Ganguli's "hath become obsolete". The word can also
  mean "misleading".
* 1.188.8 *anekasthaḥ*: "held in so many ways". The sense is uncertain. Ganguli has "the wise
  should never commit a sin".
* 1.188.14 *jaṭilā nāma gautamī*: Jatila, of the Gautama line.
* 1.188.16 *bhaikṣavat*: "like alms". Yudhishthira's version of his mother's words.

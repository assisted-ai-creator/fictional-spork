# Abhimanyu

When all the Vrishnis had spoken again and again, Vasudeva spoke words full of
dharma and good sense.

"Gudakesha has not insulted this family. He has shown it greater honour, without
a doubt. Partha does not think that you Satvatas are greedy for wealth. And the
Pandava thinks a bridegroom choice cannot be counted on. Who would agree to a
girl being given away like cattle? And what man on earth would sell his child? I
think Kaunteya saw these faults, and so the Pandava carried the girl off by
force, as dharma allows. The alliance is a fitting one. Subhadra is glorious,
and Partha is the kind of man who would carry her off by force. He was born in
the line of Bharata and of great Shantanu, the son of Kuntibhoja's daughter. Who
would not want Arjuna?

"I see no one who could beat Partha by courage, sir, not in all the worlds,
Indra and Rudra included. And there is that chariot, and those horses of mine,
and Partha himself, the fighter, swift with his weapons. Who could be his equal?
Go after Dhananjaya and turn him back gladly, with the greatest friendliness.
That is my considered view. If Partha beats you and goes back to his own city by
force, your good name will be lost at once. But there is no defeat in making
friends."

When they heard Vasudeva, they did as he said, lord of the people. Arjuna turned
back, and he was married there. Kunti's son stayed there for a year, and then
the lord spent the rest of his time in Pushkara. When the twelfth year was
complete, he went back to Khandavaprastha.

He went to the king, humble and composed, and honoured the brahmins. Then Partha
went to Draupadi.

Out of her love, Draupadi spoke to the joy of the Kurus. "Go there, Kaunteya,
where that Satvata girl is. Tie a bundle as tight as you like, and the first
knot still slackens."

Dhananjaya comforted Krishna again and again as she went on complaining in many
ways, and he begged her pardon again and again.

Then Partha quickly sent Subhadra in, dressed in red silk, looking like a
cowherd girl. The glorious woman, the hero's wife, shone all the more in that
form. When she reached the great house, lovely Bhadra, with her large coppery
eyes, greeted Pritha. Then, with a face like the full moon, Bhadra went quickly
to Draupadi and greeted her. "I am your servant," she said.

Krishna rose and embraced Madhava's sister, and said with pleasure, "May your
husband have no enemies." And Bhadra, happy, said to her, "So be it."

Then the Pandavas, the great warriors, were glad at heart, and Kunti was very
pleased, Janamejaya.

---

When lotus-eyed Keshava heard that Arjuna, the best of the Pandavas, had come
back to his own fine city of Indraprastha, the pure-souled one came there with
Rama, and with the chief men of the Vrishnis and Andhakas, heroes and great
warriors. Shauri, the tamer of enemies, came surrounded by his brothers and
sons, and by hundreds of fighting men, and guarded by a great army.

Wise and glorious Akrura came there, the lord of gifts, the commander of the
Vrishni heroes, the tamer of enemies. There came Anadhrishti of great splendour,
and glorious Uddhava, of great wisdom and great fame, the pupil of Brihaspati
himself. There came Satyaka and Satyaki, Kritavarma the Satvata, Pradyumna and
Samba, Nishatha and Shanku, valiant Charudeshna, Jhilli and Viprithu,
strong-armed Sarana, and Gada, the best of the learned. These and many other
Vrishnis, Bhojas and Andhakas came to Khandavaprastha, bringing many bridal
gifts.

When King Yudhishthira heard that Madhava had come, he sent the twins to receive
Krishna. Received by them, that rich company of Vrishnis entered
Khandavaprastha, which was bright with flags and banners. Its streets were
sprinkled and swept and strewn with flowers. It was fragrant with cool sandal
water and holy scents, and with aloe burning here and there. It was crowded with
well-groomed people, and graced by merchants. The strong-armed Keshava, the best
of men, came in with Rama, surrounded by the Vrishnis, Andhakas and great
Bhojas. Honoured by the townspeople and by brahmins in their thousands, he
entered the king's palace, which was like the house of Purandara.

Yudhishthira met Rama as the rules require. He kissed Keshava on the head and
embraced him. Krishna honoured him humbly in return, for he was delighted, and
he honoured Bhima, the tiger among men, as was proper. The Dharma King
Yudhishthira received the best of the Vrishnis and Andhakas with honour, as the
rules require, each as he came. Some he honoured as elders, some as friends.
Some he greeted with love, and some greeted him.

Then glorious Vasudeva gave the finest wealth to the wedding party, as
Subhadra's bridal gift from her kin. Krishna gave a thousand chariots with
golden fittings, hung with nets of little bells, each drawn by four horses, with
skilled and trusted drivers, and ten thousand cows. Glorious Janardana gladly
gave a thousand milch cows from the country of Mathura, holy and bright, and a
thousand white mares, bright as moonbeams, with golden trappings. He gave five
hundred black-maned she-mules and five hundred white ones, tamed, as swift as
the wind. Lotus-eyed Krishna gave a thousand fair women, well dressed and
radiant, young and skilled at bathing and anointing, with golden necklaces of a
hundred pieces, healthy and finely clothed, skilled in service. The Dasharha
Janardana gave ten men's loads of the best gold, worked and unworked, bright as
fire. And Rama, the plough-bearer, who loved daring deeds, gave Partha a
thousand elephants as the gift for touching the bride's feet. They were in rut,
their ichor flowing in three streams, like mountain peaks, never turning back in
battle, well equipped, with loud bells and golden garlands, and with their
riders. Haladhara gave this gladly, to honour the new alliance.

That great river of wealth and jewels, with clothes and blankets for its foam,
great elephants for its crocodiles, and flags for its waterweed, poured into the
ocean of the Pandus. It filled that ocean to the brim, and it brought grief to
their enemies.

The Dharma King Yudhishthira accepted it all, and honoured those great warriors
of the Vrishnis and Andhakas. The great men, the best of the Kurus, Vrishnis and
Andhakas, came together and enjoyed themselves like men of good deeds in the
home of the immortals. With great drinking bouts and loud clapping, the Kurus
and Vrishnis enjoyed themselves as each thought fit and as each liked. So those
men of great courage enjoyed themselves for many days. Then, honoured by the
Kurus, they went back to the city of Dvaravati. The great warriors of the
Vrishnis and Andhakas went with Rama at their head, taking the fine jewels the
best of the Kurus had given them. But great-minded Vasudeva stayed there with
Partha, Bharata, in the lovely city of Shakraprastha, and he roamed the banks of
the Yamuna with Partha.

---

Then Subhadra, Keshava's dear sister, bore a radiant son, Saubhadra, as Paulomi
bore Jayanta. Subhadra bore a hero, a bull among men, long-armed,
great-spirited, with the eyes of a bull, a tamer of enemies. Because he was
fearless, *abhi*, and full of spirit, *manyumat*, they called Arjuna's son, the
bull among men, the crusher of enemies, Abhimanyu. That great warrior was born
to the Satvata woman from Dhananjaya, as fire is born from a shami stick,
churned at a sacrifice. When he was born, strong-armed Yudhishthira, Kunti's
son, gave ten thousand cows to brahmins, and as many gold coins.

He was dear to Vasudeva from his childhood, and to all his fathers, as the moon
is dear to all people. From his birth Krishna performed the rites for him, and
the boy grew like the moon in the bright half of the month. The tamer of enemies
learned the whole science of the bow, with its four parts and ten kinds, divine
and human, from Arjuna, who knew the Veda. The mighty man taught him special
skills in the knowledge of weapons, in deftness, and in every kind of practice.
In learning and in use he made him almost his own equal, and Dhananjaya was
delighted as he looked at his son Saubhadra. He was well made in every limb, and
had every good mark. He was hard to overcome, with shoulders like a bull's, like
a snake with its mouth open. He had a lion's pride, he was a great archer, he
moved like an elephant in rut, his voice was like thunder or a kettledrum, and
his face was like the full moon. In courage, in strength, in looks and in
bearing he was like Krishna. Bibhatsu looked on his son as Maghavan had looked
on him.

And the Panchala princess, who had every good mark, bore her five husbands five
heroic sons, fine men, like five mountains. To Yudhishthira she bore
Prativindhya, to Vrikodara Sutasoma, to Arjuna Shrutakarman, to Nakula
Shatanika, and to Sahadeva Shrutasena. The Panchala princess bore these five
heroes, great warriors, as Aditi bore the Adityas.

The brahmins told Yudhishthira, from the treatises, "Let this one be
Prativindhya, a match for the enemy in knowing his strokes." Bhimasena's son was
born when a thousand soma sacrifices had been pressed, and she bore that great
archer, bright as the moon and the sun, as Sutasoma. Because he was born after
Kiritin had done a great and famous deed and come back, and people said, "A son
is born to you," he became Shrutakarman. Nakula, the Kaurava, the joy of the
Kurus, who made his family's name grow, named his son after the royal seer
Shatanika, of the Kuru line. Then Krishna bore Sahadeva a son under the
constellation ruled by fire, and they knew him as Shrutasena.

The glorious sons of Draupadi were born one year apart, great king, and they
were devoted to one another's good. Dhaumya performed their birth rites, their
first haircuts and their initiations, as the rules require, one after another,
best of the Bharatas. When they had studied the Veda and kept their vows well,
they learned the whole art of archery, divine and human, from Arjuna.

With sons like children of the gods, broad-chested and mighty, the Pandavas were
happy, tiger among kings.

**Divergences.**

* **Subhadra's welcome.** In the CE Subhadra calls herself Draupadi's servant, and Draupadi
  blesses her (1.213.19–20). The vulgate adds Kunti's blessing and a long scene of rejoicing
  (\*2070–\*2072, App. I No. 116).
* The vulgate adds a thousand elephants, many horses, palanquins and buffaloes from Krishna
  (\*2079–\*2090), and Balarama's heavy drinking (\*2092). Not in the CE.
* The vulgate has Balarama entrust Subhadra to Draupadi's care as he leaves (\*2095). Not in the
  CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.213.3 *svayaṃvaram anādhṛṣyam*: "a bridegroom choice cannot be counted on".
* 1.213.5 *dharmeṇa*: "as dharma allows", with 1.211.22.
* 1.213.13 *saṃvatsaraparāḥ kṣapāḥ*: "for a year". The phrase is literally "nights ending in a
  year". *puṣkareṣu*: at Pushkara, the holy lakes.
* 1.213.15 *praṇayāt*: "out of her love", the love that gives a right to complain. Her proverb of
  the bundle's first knot is hers.
* 1.213.17 *gopālikāvapuḥ*: "looking like a cowherd girl".
* 1.213.20 *niḥsapatnaḥ*: "without enemies". The word can also suggest "without a rival".
* 1.213.25 *dānapatiḥ*: "lord of gifts", Akrura.
* 1.213.49 *pādagrāhaṇikam*: "the gift for touching the bride's feet".
* 1.213.57 *śakraprasthe*: Shakraprastha, another name for Indraprastha.
* 1.213.58 *paulomī*: Paulomi, that is Shachi, Puloman's daughter, mother of Jayanta.
* 1.213.60 *abhīś ca manyumāṃś ca*: the text's etymology of Abhimanyu.
* 1.213.65 *catuṣpādaṃ daśavidham*: the science of the bow "with four parts and ten kinds".
* 1.213.74: the sense of the brahmins' words about Prativindhya is uncertain.
* 1.213.78 *nakṣatre vahnidaivate*: "under the constellation ruled by fire", Krittika.

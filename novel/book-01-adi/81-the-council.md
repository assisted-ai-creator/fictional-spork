# The Council

"A quarrel with Pandu's sons does not please me at all," said Bhishma. "As
Dhritarashtra is to me, so is Pandu, without a doubt. As Gandhari's sons are to
me, so are Kunti's sons. I must protect them, Dhritarashtra, and so must you. As
they are to me and to the king, so they are to Duryodhana and to all the Kurus,
and to others too, Bharata. Since things are as they are, I do not want a
quarrel with them. Make peace with the heroes, and let land be given to them
today. This kingdom belongs to them too, the kingdom of their great-grandfathers
and of their father, those best of the Kurus.

"Duryodhana, my son, you look on this kingdom as your inheritance from your
father. So do the Pandavas. If the glorious Pandavas have no claim to the
kingdom, how can it be yours, or any Bharata's? And if you gained the kingdom by
dharma, bull of the Bharatas, then they gained it before you. That is what I
think. Let half the kingdom be given to them, kindly. That is for the good of
all the people, tiger among men.

"If it is done any other way, it will not be good for us, and you will be wholly
disgraced. There is no doubt of it. Guard your good name, for a good name is the
highest strength. The life of a man who has lost his good name is said to be
fruitless. As long as a man's good name does not perish, Kaurava, son of
Gandhari, he lives. When his good name is lost, he is lost. Follow the dharma
that suits the Kuru family. Do what is worthy of your forebears, strong-armed
one.

"By good fortune the heroes are alive. By good fortune Pritha is alive. By good
fortune the wicked Purochana has died without getting what he wanted. Ever since
I heard that Kunti had met that end, son of Gandhari, I have not been able to
look any living being in the face. The world does not blame Purochana as much as
it blames you, tiger among men. So you should welcome it, great king, that they
are alive and that we can see the Pandavas again. It wipes out your stain. While
those heroes live, joy of the Kurus, not even the wielder of the thunderbolt
himself could take their father's share from them. For they all stand by dharma,
and they are all of one mind, and they were wrongfully driven out, above all
from a kingdom that was equally theirs. If you want to do what is right, if you
want to do what pleases me, if you want what is best, give them half."

---

"We have always heard," said Drona, "that counsellors called in to advise, who
wish Dhritarashtra well, should say what is right and beneficial and brings
honour. My view, son, is the same as great Bhishma's. The Kaunteyas should be
given their share. This is the eternal dharma.

"Let some pleasant-spoken man be sent quickly to Drupada, Bharata, taking many
jewels for the Pandavas' sake. Let him take many gifts to Drupada, and speak of
the great good that will come of the alliance with him. Let him say, again and
again, to Drupada and to Dhrishtadyumna, that you and Duryodhana are well
pleased, king. Let him describe how fitting and how welcome the alliance is, and
speak kindly, again and again, to the Kaunteyas and to Madri's two sons. At your
word, king of kings, let him give Draupadi many bright golden ornaments, and
give what is fitting to all Drupada's sons, bull of the Bharatas, and to all the
Pandavas, and to Kunti.

"When he has spoken to Drupada and the Pandavas in this friendly way, let him
then speak of their coming here. When the heroes are given leave, let a splendid
escort go, and let Duhshasana and Vikarna bring the Pandavas here. Then, best of
kings, honoured by you always, they will take their father's place with the
consent of the people. This, great king, I think is the right way for you to
treat these sons of yours, and Bhishma thinks so too, Bharata."

---

"These two have always been given wealth and honour by you," said Karna, "and
they are close to you in all your affairs. Yet they do not advise what is good
for you. What could be stranger than that? When a man with a wicked mind and a
hidden heart speaks what he calls the best advice, how can he do what good men
approve? In hard times friends can bring neither good nor harm. For everyone's
sorrow and happiness come as they are ordained. Wise or foolish, young or old,
with allies or without, a man finds everything that is his, wherever he is.

"We hear that long ago there was a king named Ambuvicha, a king of the lords of
Magadha, in Rajagriha. He had lost the use of all his senses, and could do
little more than breathe. In all his affairs he depended on his minister. His
minister was Mahakarni, who became the sole master. Thinking he had won power
for himself, he despised the king. The fool took all the king's pleasures for
himself, his women, his jewels and his wealth, and his sovereignty too. As he
took these, the greedy man's greed grew with his gains. So, having taken
everything, he wanted to seize the kingdom. But though the king had lost the use
of all his senses, and could only breathe, the minister, for all his efforts,
could not take the kingdom. So we have heard. What else could it be but that the
man's kingship was ordained?

"If the kingdom is ordained for you, lord of the people, it will surely stay
with you while the whole world looks on. If it is ordained otherwise, you will
not get it, however hard you try. Knowing this, wise king, judge which of your
counsellors are good and which are not. Learn to tell the words of the wicked
from the words of those who are not."

"We know why you say this," said Drona. "It is because your heart is bad. You
are wicked, and because of the Pandavas you accuse us of wickedness. What I say,
Karna, is the highest good, and it will make the Kurus grow. If you think it
wicked, then say what the highest good is. If what I say is not done, though it
is the highest good, the Kurus will perish before long. That is my view."

---

"King," said Vidura, "your kinsmen have certainly told you what is best. But
words take no root in those who will not listen. Bhishma, Shantanu's son, the
best of the Kurus, has told you what is good for you, king, and you do not take
it. Drona too has said in many ways what is best and good, and Karna, Radha's
son, does not think it good for you. But though I think hard, king, I cannot see
a better friend to you than these two lions among men, or anyone who is wiser.
For these two are old in years, in wisdom and in learning, and they think of
you, king of kings, and of the sons of Pandu as equals. In dharma and in
truthfulness they are no lower than Rama, Dasharatha's son, or Gaya, Bharata,
without a doubt. They have never before advised you badly in anything. And you
can see that you have never done them any wrong. How could these two tigers
among men, whose courage is true, not advise you for your good, when you have
done them no wrong, king? They are wise, the best of men in this world, lord of
men. They will not say anything crooked for your sake. That is my firm belief,
joy of the Kurus. And they know dharma, and will not take sides for the sake of
wealth. They think this is the highest good for you, Bharata.

"As Duryodhana and the rest are your sons, king, so the Pandavas are your sons,
without a doubt. If your counsellors foolishly advise anything that would harm
them, they are surely not looking to your good. Or if there is favour in your
heart for your own sons, king, those who bring it into the open will surely not
do you good. That is why these two great and glorious men said nothing openly,
for that is not your resolve.

"And what these two bulls among men said, that the Pandavas cannot be beaten, is
just so, tiger among men. Bless you. How could the glorious Pandava Savyasachin,
the tamer of enemies, be beaten in battle, king, even by Maghavan? How could
strong-armed Bhimasena, great, with the strength of ten thousand elephants, be
beaten in war even by the immortals? And the twins, skilled in war, like the
sons of Yama himself: how could anyone who wants to live stand against them in
battle? How could the eldest Pandava be beaten in battle, in whom steadiness,
compassion, patience, truth and courage are always found? They have Rama on
their side, and Janardana as their counsellor, and Satyaki on their side. What
can they fail to win in battle? Drupada is their father-in-law, and the sons of
Prishata's line, Drupada's sons, the heroes led by Dhrishtadyumna, are their
brothers-in-law.

"So, knowing that they cannot be beaten, Bharata, and knowing that they have
first right to the inheritance by dharma, treat them well. Great is the disgrace
that Purochana has smeared on you. Wash it off today, king, by showing them
favour. King Drupada is also a great king, and he has been our enemy in the
past. To win him over, king, is to strengthen our own side. The Dasharhas are
strong and many, lord of the people. Where Krishna is, they will be. Where
Krishna is, there is victory. And who, unless he is cursed by fate, would try to
do by war what can be done by peace alone, king? The townspeople and the country
folk have heard that the Parthas are alive, and they long to see them. Do what
will please them, king.

"Duryodhana and Karna, and Shakuni, Subala's son, are unrighteous, foolish and
childish. Do not do what they say. I told you this long ago, virtuous king:
because of Duryodhana's wrongdoing these people will perish."

**Divergences.**

  brother" (\*1974). Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.195.4 *prapitāmahānām*: the kingdom of "their great-grandfathers".
* 1.195.11, 14 *gāndhāre*: Bhishma calls Duryodhana "son of Gandhari".
* 1.195.14 *tathāgatām*: "had met that end", the supposed death in the fire.

* 1.196.10: Duhshasana and Vikarna are named by Drona to escort the Pandavas.

  little more than breathe".
* 1.196.19 *mahākarṇiḥ*: the minister's name, given as in the text.

  and Drona did not voice it, is rendered as Ganguli reads it.

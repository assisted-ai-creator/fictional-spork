# The Cow Nandini

When he heard what the gandharva said, bull of the Bharatas, Arjuna shone with
the greatest delight, like the full moon. And the great archer, the best of the
Kurus, very curious about the power of Vasishtha's austerity, said to the
gandharva, "I want to hear about the seer whose name you spoke, Vasishtha. Tell
it to me as it is. Tell me, lord of the gandharvas, who was this blessed seer
who was the family priest of our forefathers?"

"Desire and anger," said the gandharva, "unconquerable even by the immortals,
were conquered for ever by his austerity, and they massaged his feet. He held
back his great anger at Vishvamitra's offence, and the noble man did not wipe
out the Kushikas. Though he burned with grief for his sons and had the power,
the lord did not think of a cruel deed to destroy Vishvamitra. Though he could
have brought his dead sons back from the house of Yama, he did not go past the
end appointed for them, as the ocean does not go past its shore. The kings of
the Ikshvaku line won this earth when they found that great man, who had
conquered himself. When they found that best of seers, Vasishtha, the best of
family priests, those kings offered sacrifices, joy of the Kurus. That brahmin
seer led all those best of kings in sacrifice, best of the Pandavas, as
Brihaspati leads the immortals.

"So find some brahmin with good qualities, who knows the Veda and dharma, whose
heart is set on dharma, a priest you would want. A kshatriya born to win the
earth should first appoint a family priest, Partha, for the growth of his
kingdom. A king who wants to conquer the earth should put brahmins in front. So
let some brahmin with good qualities be your family priest."

---

"How did the quarrel between Vishvamitra and Vasishtha begin?" said Arjuna.
"They both lived in holy hermitages. Tell us all of it."

"This story of Vasishtha is called ancient," said the gandharva, "in all the
worlds, Partha. Hear it as it happened.

"In Kanyakubja there was a great king, bull of the Bharatas, famous in the world
as Gadhi, devoted to truth and dharma. The righteous man had a son, rich in
armies and beasts of burden, known as Vishvamitra, the crusher of enemies. He
went hunting with his ministers in the deep forest, shooting deer and boar in
the lovely desert lands. Worn out with exertion, wanting game and thirsty, he
came, best of men, to Vasishtha's hermitage.

"When the seer Vasishtha, who had the best of fortunes, saw Vishvamitra, the
best of men, arrive, he received him with honour, with water for his feet, the
guest offering, water for rinsing and a welcome, Bharata, and with food of the
forest. Great Vasishtha had a cow that gave whatever was wished. When she was
told, 'Give what is wanted,' she yielded what was wanted. She yielded plants of
village and forest, and milk, and the finest elixir with six flavours and the
taste of nectar, food and drink and delicacies of many kinds, things to lick and
to suck, like nectar, Arjuna. The king was honoured with all these good things,
perfect in every way, and he was very pleased, with his ministers and his army.

"She was long in six parts, well rounded in her flanks and thighs, broad in
three and compact in five, with eyes like a frog's, handsome, with a full udder,
faultless, with a fine tail, ears like arrowheads, lovely horns, and a sturdy,
long head and neck. Vishvamitra looked at her in wonder. He praised Nandini,
Vasishtha's milch cow, and in his great pleasure he said to the sage, 'Give me
Nandini, brahmin, for ten million cows, or for my kingdom. Enjoy the kingdom,
great sage.'

"'This milch cow Nandini is for the gods, the guests and the ancestors, and for
the ghee of the offering,' said Vasishtha. 'I cannot give her away, sinless one,
even for your kingdom.'

"'I am a kshatriya,' said Vishvamitra, 'and you are a brahmin, whose means are
austerity and study. What strength is there in brahmins, calm and
self-controlled? If you will not give me what I want for ten million cows, I
will not give up my own dharma. I will take your cow by force.'

"'You stand on strength,' said Vasishtha. 'You are a king and a kshatriya,
strong in your arms. Do as you wish, quickly. Do not stop to think.'

"When he was told this, Partha, Vishvamitra took Nandini, the cow white as a
swan or the moon, as if by force. Beaten with whips and sticks and driven here
and there, Vasishtha's lovely Nandini lowed. She came back, Partha, and stood
facing the blessed seer, with her face turned up to him. Though they beat her
hard, she would not leave the hermitage.

"'I hear your cries, good lady,' said Vasishtha, 'as you low again and again.
You are being taken by force, Nandini. But I am a brahmin, and forbearing.'

"But Nandini, terrified of Vishvamitra and his troops, bull of the Bharatas,
came up to Vasishtha. 'Lord,' said the cow, 'I am struck with stones and sticks
by Vishvamitra's terrible troops, and I cry like one with no protector. Why do
you look on and do nothing?'

"While she was being mistreated like this, Partha, the great sage was not
shaken, and he did not waver from his steadiness, firm in his vow. 'The strength
of kshatriyas is their power,' said Vasishtha. 'The strength of brahmins is
forbearance. Forbearance holds me. So go, if you like.'

"'Have you abandoned me, lord,' said the cow, 'that you speak to me like this?
If you have not abandoned me, brahmin, I cannot be taken away by force.'

"'I do not abandon you, lovely one,' said Vasishtha. 'Stay, if you can. Here is
your calf, tied with a strong rope, being taken by force.'

"When the milch cow heard Vasishtha say 'Stay', she raised her head and neck and
became terrible to look at. With her eyes red with anger and her lowing like
thunder, the cow drove off Vishvamitra's whole army. Beaten with the tips of
whips and sticks and driven here and there, her eyes blazing with anger, she
grew angrier still. Her body blazing with anger, she shone like the sun at noon,
and poured out great showers of embers from her tail again and again. From her
tail she created the Pahlavas, from her dung the Shabaras and Shakas, and from
her urine, faint with fury, she created the Yavanas. From her foam she created
the Pundras, Kiratas, Dramidas, Simhalas and Barbaras, and the Daradas and
mlecchas. The great army of Vishvamitra was scattered by the hosts of mlecchas
of many kinds she let loose, in all kinds of armour, carrying all kinds of
weapons, furious, while Vishvamitra watched. Each of his fighters was surrounded
by five or seven of them. Driven off by a great rain of weapons, Vishvamitra's
army broke and fled in terror on every side, while Vishvamitra watched. Yet not
one of Vishvamitra's soldiers lost his life at the hands of Vasishtha's angry
men, bull of the Bharatas. Vishvamitra's army was driven three leagues, crying
out in terror, and found no one to save it.

"When Vishvamitra saw that great wonder, which came from brahmin power, he was
sick of being a kshatriya, and he said, 'Shame on strength, the strength of
kshatriyas! The strength of brahmin power is strength. Weighing strength and
weakness, austerity is the highest strength.'

"He gave up his rich kingdom and his blazing royal glory, turned his back on
pleasures, and set his mind on austerity alone. When he had reached perfection
by austerity, and held up the worlds by his power, the blazing Kaushika scorched
them all, and he became a brahmin. And the Kaushika drank the pressed soma with
Indra."

**Divergences.**

* **The cow's name.** Here the CE calls Vasishtha's cow Nandini (1.165.15–22). In the
  Vasus' story (1.93) the CE leaves her unnamed. See ledger L-69, now corrected.
* **No battle of weapons with Vasishtha.** The vulgate has Vishvamitra rain arrows on
  Vasishtha himself (App. I No. 97). In the CE the cow's armies do the fighting.
* The vulgate adds the saying that a king must never take a brahmin's two treasures, his
  fire-cow and his wife (\*1760). Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

* 1.164.5: desire and anger "massaged his feet", i.e. served him, conquered.
* 1.164.6–8: the gandharva alludes to the killing of Vasishtha's sons, told in the next
  adhyayas.
* 1.165.13–14: the points of a fine cow (*ṣaḍāyatā*, *tripṛthu*, *pañca saṃvṛtā*) are
  given as the text lists them.
* 1.165.21 *balād iva*: "as if by force".
* 1.165.35–36: the peoples created by the cow are named as in the text: Pahlavas,
  Shabaras, Shakas, Yavanas, Pundras, Kiratas, Dramidas, Simhalas, Barbaras, Daradas.
* 1.165.39: none of Vishvamitra's men was killed. The text stresses it.
* 1.165.44 *kauśikaḥ*: Vishvamitra, of Kushika's line.

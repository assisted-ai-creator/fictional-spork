# Aurva

"Then Adrishyanti, living in the hermitage," said the gandharva, "gave birth to
a son, king, who carried on Shakti's line, as if he were a second Shakti. The
blessed chief of sages himself performed his grandson's birth rites and the
others, best of the Bharatas. Because Vasishtha, who was set on dying, was
brought back by him while he was still in the womb, he is remembered in the
world as Parashara, He Who Brought Back the Dying.

"The righteous boy thought of Vasishtha as his father, and from his birth he
behaved towards him as a son to a father. He called the brahmin seer Vasishtha
'Father' in front of his mother Adrishyanti, Kaunteya, tamer of enemies. When
Adrishyanti heard his sweet word 'Father', full of meaning, her eyes filled with
tears, and she said to him, 'Do not say "Father, Father, Father", my son. The
great sage is not your father. Your father was eaten by a rakshasa in the
forest, son. The one you think of as your father is not your father, sinless
one. This noble man is the father of your great father.'

"When she said this, the best of seers, truthful and great-hearted, was stricken
with grief, and he set his mind on destroying all the worlds. As the great man
was set on it, the great ascetic Vasishtha held him back with an argument. Hear
it.

---

"'There was a king on earth named Kritavirya,' said Vasishtha. 'That bull among
kings was the patron in sacrifice of the Bhrigus, who knew the Veda. At the end
of the soma sacrifice the lord of the people satisfied those first-born, son,
with grain and wealth in plenty. When that tiger among kings had gone to heaven,
a time came when his descendants needed money. Knowing that the Bhrigus had
wealth, all those kings came to those best of the Bhargavas, son, to beg. Some
of the Bhrigus buried their endless wealth in the ground. Some, knowing there
was danger from the kshatriyas, gave it to brahmins. And some of the Bhrigus
gave the kshatriyas as much as they wanted, son, seeing another reason for it.

"'Then, son, a kshatriya happened to be digging in the ground and found wealth
in a Bhrigu's house, and all the bulls of the kshatriyas came together and saw
the wealth. Then in their anger they scorned the Bhrigus, though they had come
for shelter, and those great archers killed them all with sharp arrows. They
roamed the earth cutting them down, even to the unborn in the womb.

"'As the Bhrigus were being wiped out, the Bhrigu wives fled in fear to the
Himalaya, son. One of them, a woman with lovely thighs, in her fear held her
glowing child in one of her thighs, to make her husband's line grow. They saw
that brahmin woman shining with her own light. Then the child split the thigh
and came out of the brahmin woman, taking away the sight of the kshatriyas, like
the sun at noon. Then, robbed of their eyes, they wandered lost in the mountain
wilds. Their plans came to nothing, and the bulls of the kshatriyas, stricken
with fear, went to the blameless brahmin woman for shelter, to get their sight
back. Those kshatriyas, out of their wits, their light gone, in pain like fires
that have gone out, said to that noble lady, "By your grace, lady, let the
kshatriyas go with their sight. We will give up our wickedness and go away
together. You and your son should be kind to us all, and save the kings by
giving them their sight again."

"'"I have not taken your sight, children," said the brahmin woman. "I bear you
no anger. But surely this Bhargava, born from my thigh, is angry with you today.
The great one remembers his murdered kin, and surely it is he who has taken your
eyes in his anger, without doubt. When you were killing even the unborn of the
Bhrigus, my children, I carried this child in my thigh for a hundred years. The
whole Veda with its six limbs entered him while he was still in the womb, to do
a kindness again to the line of the Bhrigus. He surely wants to kill you, in
anger for his fathers' deaths. It is by his divine power that your eyes have
been stolen. So beg this fine son of mine, Aurva, children. If you bow down, he
will be pleased and will release your sight."

"'When she told them this, all those kings said to the one born from the thigh,
"Be gracious," and he was gracious. And because he was born by splitting a
thigh, *uru*, the good brahmin seer is famous in the worlds by the name of
Aurva. When they had got their eyes back, the kings went away. But the Bhargava
sage thought of the defeat of all the worlds. The great-hearted best of the
Bhrigus set his whole mind, son, on destroying all the worlds, every one.
Wanting to honour the Bhrigus, grown great by great austerity, he began to burn
all the worlds, with gods, asuras and men, by his fierce, great austerity, to
please his grandfathers.

"'Then the ancestors, knowing what the best of the Bhrigus was doing, son, all
came from the world of the ancestors and said to him, "Aurva, we have seen the
power of your fierce austerity, son. Be kind to the worlds. Restrain your anger.
It was not because we were powerless, son, that all we Bhrigus, perfected as we
were, overlooked our slaughter by the kshatriyas who were killing us. When
weariness came over us from our long lives, son, we ourselves wanted to be
killed by the kshatriyas. It was one of us who buried that wealth in the
Bhrigu's house, placing it there to cause hatred and to anger the kshatriyas.
What need have we of wealth, best of brahmins, when we long for heaven? When
death could not take us all, son, we saw this way, and we agreed on it. A man
who kills himself does not win the fair worlds, son. That is why we thought this
out, and did not destroy ourselves. What you want to do does not please us, son.
Hold back your mind from this evil, the defeat of all the worlds. No kshatriyas,
child, and no seven worlds, are spoiling your austerity or your power. Put away
the anger that has risen in you."

"'"The vow I made then in anger, ancestors," said Aurva, "to destroy all the
worlds, must not be made false. I cannot live with a vow of anger made in vain.
If my anger is not carried through, it will burn me as fire burns its
fire-stick. A man who forgives an anger that has arisen for a reason is not able
to guard the three aims of life properly. Kings who want to win heaven should
use anger in its place, restraining the wicked and protecting the good.

"'"When I lay in the bed of the womb, in the thigh, I heard the cries of my
mothers when the Bhrigus were killed by the kshatriyas. When the worlds with the
immortals let the lowest of the kshatriyas wipe out the Bhrigus down to the
unborn, anger entered me. My mothers and my fathers, so they say, filled with
fear, found no refuge in any of the worlds. When no one took in those wives of
the Bhrigus, then this good woman carried me in one thigh. When there is someone
in the worlds to stop a wrong, no wrongdoer is found in any world. But when the
wrongdoer finds no one to stop him anywhere, many in the world take to wicked
deeds. The man who knows of a wrong and has the power, yet does not stop it,
though he is master, is tied to that deed himself. The kings and the lords were
able to save my fathers, but they did not, thinking only of their own lives
here. That is why I am angry with these worlds, and I am their master now. But I
cannot go beyond your words. And if I, who have the power, look on and do
nothing, I shall again be in great danger from the sin of the worlds. This fire
born of my anger, which wants to take the worlds, will burn me myself if I hold
it back by my own power. I know you want the good of all the worlds. So do what
is best, lords, for the worlds and for me."

"'"This fire born of your anger, which wants to take the worlds," said the
ancestors, "let it go into the waters, bless you, for the worlds rest on the
waters. All the juices are made of water. The whole world is made of water. So
let this fire of anger go into the waters, best of brahmins. Let it stay in the
great ocean, brahmin, if you wish, the fire born of anger, burning the waters,
for the worlds are said to be made of water. So your vow will come true, sinless
one, and the worlds with the immortals will not be destroyed."

"'Then, son, Aurva let that fire born of anger go into the home of Varuna. And
it consumes the waters in the great ocean. It has become the great Mare's Head,
as those who know the Veda know, and, breathing fire from its mouth, it drinks
the waters of the great ocean. So you too, bless you, should not destroy the
worlds, Parashara, for you know the higher dharma, best of the wise.'"

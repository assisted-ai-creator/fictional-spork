# The Spies Return

When Kichaka had been killed with his younger brothers, lord of the people, the
common folk were amazed, thinking about that terrible deed. In that city and in
the country there was talk everywhere. "Kichaka was the king's favourite because
of his courage, a man of great strength. He struck men down, the evil-minded
fellow, and laid hands on other men's wives. And the wicked man has been killed
by the gandharvas, the villain." So people talked in land after land, great
king, of Kichaka, who could not be beaten, who broke up enemy armies.

Meanwhile the spies whom Dhritarashtra's son had sent out searched many
villages, kingdoms and cities. They did what they had been told, looking in each
place as they had been directed, and then, when they had done their thinking,
they turned back to the City of the Elephant. There they saw the king, the
Kauravya son of Dhritarashtra, with Drona, Karna and Kripa, and great-minded
Bhishma, with his brothers and the Trigartas, those great chariot fighters. They
said this to Duryodhana as he sat in the middle of the hall.

"We have made every effort, lord of men, in searching for the Pandavas in that
great forest, empty of people, full of beasts, covered with trees and creepers
of every kind, thick with tangled creepers and bushes. But we do not know which
way the Parthas of firm valour have gone. We looked for their footprints in
place after place, on high mountain peaks, in many countries, in crowded lands,
in hamlets and in cities. Again and again we looked, king, and we have not found
the Pandavas. They are lost for good. Bless you, bull among men.

"While we were looking for the tracks of their chariots, best of chariot
fighters, lord of men, we followed their charioteers for a time. When we had
searched properly, we learned the truth. The charioteers had reached Dvaravati
without the Parthas, scorcher of enemies. The Pandavas are not there, king, nor
is Krishna, who is true to her husbands. They are lost altogether. Homage to
you, bull of the Bharatas. We do not know which way they went, great-souled as
they are, nor where they live. We do not know the Pandavas' news, or anything
they have done. So give us your orders, lord of men, lord of the people, from
now on. What more shall we do in the search for the Pandavas?

"And hear this happy news of ours, pleasant and full of good fortune. The one
who brought the Trigartas low with his great army, king, the suta Kichaka,
great-souled, King Matsya's man, has been killed. He lies fallen, Bharata,
killed at night by gandharvas no one could see, the villain, with his brothers,
unshaken one. Now that you have heard this happy news, the defeat of your
enemies, you have what you wanted, Kauravya. Arrange what must be done next."

---

When King Duryodhana heard their words, he was lost in thought for a long time.
Then he said to those in the hall, "It is very hard to know in the end which way
things will go. So all of you look out for where the Pandavas may have gone.
Little of the time is left. Most of it is gone, of their life unrecognised in
this thirteenth year. If the Pandavas get through the rest of this year, their
pledge will be fulfilled, for they hold fast to their vow of truth. Then like
lordly elephants in rut, like venomous snakes, they will be dangerous, and
certainly enraged against the Kauravas. If they are recognised before the time,
they must go back to the forest again, in their wretched shapes, with their
anger held down. So find them quickly, so that our kingdom may be ours for a
long time, without end and without loss, without conflict, without trouble,
without rivals."

Then Karna said, "Let others go quickly, Bharata, cleverer, skilled, secret men
who do their work well. Let them go about disguised through rich and crowded
countries. There, in gatherings and elsewhere, among holy men and wandering
ascetics, in the kingdom's outposts, at bathing places and in mines of every
kind, men should find them out by well-trained reasoning. The Pandavas, living
hidden, should be sought out closely by many kinds of skilled men, devoted to
the task, who know the work and are well disguised. Let them look in thickets by
rivers, at bathing places, in villages and cities, in lovely hermitages, in the
mountains and in caves."

Then Duhshasana, the brother born next after the eldest, spoke to his eldest
brother, who delighted in wicked thoughts. "Let us see to everything Karna says,
just as he says it. Let all the spies search here and there as they are told,
these and many more, from country to country, in the proper way. But nothing is
to be found of where they went or where they live, or what they are doing.
Either they are hidden very deep, or they have gone across the sea with its
waves. Or those men, so proud of their courage, have been eaten by wild beasts
in the great forest. Or they have come into some danger and are lost for ever.
So set your mind at rest, joy of the Kurus, and do whatever you think best, as
your energy allows, lord of men."

---

Then Drona, of great valour, who saw the truth of things, said, "Men like them
do not perish, nor do they come to defeat. They are brave and learned, wise and
self-controlled, they know dharma, they are grateful, and they are devoted to
the Dharma King. He knows the truth of statecraft, dharma and profit. He stands
firm in dharma like a father, and he is steady in truth. He is the eldest, and
he honours his elders. His brothers are devoted to their great-souled brother,
king, and Ajatashatru, modest as he is, is devoted to his brothers. They are
obedient to him, and discreet, and great-souled. Why would the wise Partha not
do what is best for them? So they are carefully waiting for the time of their
rise to come. I see with my understanding that they will not come to ruin.

"What must be done now should be done quickly, without delay, after careful
thought. Let us think where they may be living. The sons of Pandu are steady in
every purpose, and those heroes, sinless and covered with austerity, are hard to
find out. The Partha is pure, virtuous, truthful, wise and clean, a mass of
energy beyond counting. He could blind the eyes of anyone who looked for him. So
let us think about this, and then act. And let us search for them again, through
brahmins, spies and holy men, and others who know such things."

---

Then Bhishma, Shantanu's son, grandfather of the Bharatas, who was learned, who
knew the right time and place, who knew the truth and all dharma, spoke when the
teacher had finished. Taking up his words, he spoke these words to the Bharatas
for their good. They were words about Yudhishthira, who knows dharma, words that
rest on dharma, always hard to find among the wicked and always valued by the
good. Bhishma spoke words there that the good honour.

"As this brahmin Drona, who knows the truth of all things, has said, the
Pandavas, who have every good mark, do not deserve to perish. They are rich in
learning and good conduct, keep good vows, are sunk deep in obedience to their
elders and hold fast to their vow of truth. They know the pledge, and they are
keeping it, pure in their vows. Carrying the burden of the good, they do not
deserve to be brought low. The Pandavas are protected by dharma and by their own
valour. My mind tells me they will not come to ruin.

"So I shall use my understanding about the Pandavas, Bharata. The ways of a man
of good policy cannot be traced by others. But what we can say, thinking about
the Pandavas, I shall say, with understanding and not with malice. Listen. What
I say should be said well, dear son, and never as bad advice, by a man who
stands by his elders' teaching and loves truth. A steady man, if he must speak
among the good, must speak as he thinks, in every way, for the sake of dharma.

"On this I do not think as other people do. In the city or the country where
King Yudhishthira is, the people will not be spiteful or jealous. They will not
speak too much or be envious. Each will keep to his own dharma. There will be
much sound of Vedic recitation, and full offerings, and many sacrifices with
rich fees. The rain-god will always rain there properly, no doubt of it. The
land will be full of crops and free from plague. The grain will be full of taste
and the fruits full of goodness. The garlands will be fragrant, and speech will
sound kind. The wind will be pleasant to touch, and nothing unpleasant will meet
the eye. Fear will not enter where King Yudhishthira is.

"The cows there will be many, not thin, and not hard to milk. The milk, curds
and ghee will be tasty and wholesome. The drinks will be good and the food full
of flavour in the country where King Yudhishthira is. Tastes and touches and
smells and sounds will all be good, and the sights will be clear, where King
Yudhishthira is. In that thirteenth year, dear son, everything will be full of
its own virtues in the country where the Pandavas are. The people there will be
affectionate, content, pure and steady, devoted to worshipping gods and guests,
loving to all creatures. They will give gifts gladly, full of energy, always
given to dharma, hating what is bad and wanting what is good, always
sacrificing, keeping good vows, where King Yudhishthira is. The people there
will have given up false speech, dear son. They will be full of what is good,
lucky and blessed. They will want good things and think good thoughts, where
King Yudhishthira is, and always keep vows that are dear and desired.

"That righteous one cannot be recognised even by the twice-born, dear son. How
much less can the Partha be found out by common people? In him are truth,
steadiness, giving, the highest calm, lasting patience, modesty, fortune, fame,
the highest energy, kindness and honesty. So the wise one's dwelling there is
hidden by a disguise. About his highest course I cannot say otherwise. Think
this over, and do quickly whatever you think will be good, Kauravya, if you
trust me."

---

Then Kripa, son of Sharadvat, said, "What the old man has said about the
Pandavas is fitting and timely. It is joined to dharma and profit, gentle, true
and well reasoned. It matches what Bhishma is. Hear my words too on this. Let us
think where they have gone and where they live, through spies at the bathing
places, and let us make whatever policy is good now.

"One who wants to prosper must not look down on an enemy, dear son, even an
ordinary one. How much less the Pandavas, dear son, skilled in every weapon in
battle. So now, while the great-souled Pandavas have gone into disguise, hiding
who they are, and when the time of their rise has come, you must learn your own
strength, in your own kingdom and in others'. When the time comes, the Pandavas
will surely rise. When their pledge is fulfilled, the great-souled, mighty
Parthas, the Pandavas, full of energy, will be full of great spirit. So make
ready your army, your treasury and your policy, so that when their time comes we
can make a proper treaty with them.

"I think all this is right, dear son. Learn your own strength, and how firmly it
holds among all your allies, strong and weak. Learn what strength is high and
what is low, and who stands in the middle, Bharata, who is glad and who is not,
and so we shall make terms with others. Bring enemies round by conciliation,
division, gifts, force and tribute, by fair means, and bend the weak by
strength. Win your allies with kind words and let your army be paid in comfort.
With your treasury and army grown, you will reach full success. Then you will
fight the strong enemies who face you, or others, or the Pandavas, when their
own strength and mounts are gone.

"When you have settled all this, and set to work by your own dharma, lord of
men, you will have happiness for a long time, when the time comes."

**Divergences.**

* **Bhishma's hint.** Many retellings have Bhishma deliberately point the Kauravas towards Virata's kingdom. In the CE he describes the kind of land where Yudhishthira must be living, and says he cannot say more (4.27.12–27). He names no kingdom. Rejected lines (\*532–\*535) add his remark that it is not right for him to speak, since the Kurus and Pandavas are equal to him. Not narrated.
* **The spies' account of Kichaka.** Rejected lines (\*505–\*509) add that Kichaka was Sudeshna's brother and that he was killed because of a sairandhri, the queen of the gandharvas. In the CE the spies say only that he was killed by unseen gandharvas at night, with his brothers (4.24.19–20). Not narrated.
* **Virata's grief.** A rejected line (\*495) says Virata grieved bitterly for Kichaka. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*494, \*496–\*504, \*510–\*531, \*536–\*544, @27) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 4.24.14–15: "we followed their charioteers … The charioteers had reached Dvaravati without the Parthas". The Pandavas sent their charioteers and empty chariots to Dvaravati (4.4.3).
* 4.24.19 *yena trigartā nikṛtāḥ*: "the one who brought the Trigartas low". This explains the Trigartas' raid in the chapters that follow.
* 4.25.6 *kṛcchrarūpadharāḥ*: "in their wretched shapes".
* 4.25.10 *paricāreṣu*: "in the kingdom's outposts". The sense is uncertain.
* 4.26.9 *gṛhṇīyād api cakṣuṣī*: "He could blind the eyes of anyone who looked for him", literally "he could seize even the eyes".
* 4.27.27 *channaṃ satreṇa*: "hidden by a disguise". *satra* here, as in 4.28.5, means disguise.
* 4.28.11 *sāmnā bhedena dānena daṇḍena*: "by conciliation, division, gifts, force", the four means of policy.

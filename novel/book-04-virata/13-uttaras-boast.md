# Uttara's Boast

While the Matsya was away with the Trigarta, trying to recover his herds,
Duryodhana marched against Virata with his counsellors. Bhishma, Drona, Karna,
Kripa who knew the highest weapons, Drona's son, Subala's son and lordly
Duhshasana, Vivimshati, Vikarna, valiant Chitrasena, Durmukha, Duhsaha and other
great chariot fighters, all came to the Matsyas. They drove off King Virata's
herdsmen by force and took the cattle with violence. The Kurus drove off sixty
thousand cattle, surrounding them on every side with a great mass of chariots.
In the herdsmen's stations, as those great chariot fighters struck them down,
there was a great outcry in that fearful fighting.

The overseer of the cattle, terrified, mounted a chariot in haste and went to
the city, crying out in distress. He entered the city and went to the king's
palace, got down quickly from the chariot and went in to tell the news. There he
saw the Matsya's proud son, named Bhuminjaya, and told him everything, the
carrying off of the kingdom's herds.

"The Kurus are driving off sixty thousand of your cattle. Rise up to win back
the herds, the kingdom's wealth. Prince, if you want what is good for us, march
out quickly yourself. For the Matsya, lord of the earth, left you here to guard
the city in his absence. The king boasts of you in the middle of the assembly.
'My son is worthy of me, a hero, the upholder of the family. My son is a
warrior, skilled with bow and arrow, always a hero.' Let the words of that lord
of men come true. Beat the Kurus and bring back the herds, best of cattle
owners. Burn up their divisions with the terrible fire of your arrows. With
gold-feathered arrows with smooth joints, loosed from your bow, break the enemy
divisions, as the leader of a herd breaks through elephants. Play the lute of
your bow in the middle of the enemy, with its bowstring for strings, its
bow-staff for a neck, its loud sound and its arrows for notes. Let white horses,
bright as silver, be yoked to your chariot, lord, and let them raise your golden
lion banner. Let your gold-feathered, bright-pointed arrows, loosed by your
skilled hand, cover the sun and stop the lives of kings. Beat all the Kurus in
battle, as the wielder of the thunderbolt beat the asuras, and win great fame,
and come back into this city. For you, son of the lord of the Matsyas, are the
kingdom's highest refuge. Let all who live in the land find a refuge today."

He said this in the middle of the women, words that should have banished fear.
And in the women's quarters, boasting, Uttara said this.

---

"Today I would follow the tracks of the cattle, with my strong bow," said
Uttara, "if only I had some charioteer skilled with horses. But I cannot find
any man who could drive for me. Find me a charioteer quickly, fit for me, since
I am setting out. My charioteer was killed in that great battle there was,
twenty-eight nights ago, or a month at the most. If I could get another man who
knew how to drive horses, I would go quickly today, with my great banner raised
high. I would plunge into that enemy army, crowded with elephants, horses and
chariots, beat the Kurus, robbed of their strength by the heat of my weapons,
and bring back the herds. Duryodhana, Shantanu's son, Karna Vaikartana, Kripa,
Drona with his son, great archers all gathered together. I would terrify them in
battle, as the thunderbolt-wielder terrified the danavas, and bring back the
herds this very hour. The Kurus found the place empty and are going away with
the herds. What could I do, when I was not there? Today the gathered Kurus would
see my valour. 'Can this be Partha Arjuna himself, attacking us?'"

As he said this again and again among the women, Panchali could not bear this
talk about Bibhatsu. The poor woman came out from among the women, went up to
him and, as if shy, said this quietly. "That young man like a great elephant,
very handsome to look at, known as Brihannada, was Partha's charioteer. He was
the great man's pupil in archery, and second to none. I saw him before, hero,
when I was in the service of the Pandavas. When the fire burned that great
forest of Khandava, it was he who held the reins of Arjuna's fine horses. With
him as his charioteer, Partha beat all creatures at Khandavaprastha. There is no
driver like him. This girl, your younger sister, with her lovely hips, he will
do what she says, hero, no doubt of it. If he were your charioteer, you would
surely beat all the Kurus, no doubt of it, take the cattle and come back."

When the sairandhri said this to him, he said to his sister, "Go, faultless one,
and bring that Brihannada." Sent by her brother, she went quickly to the dance
hall, where that strong-armed Pandava was living, hidden by his disguise.

---

He saw the large-eyed princess, his friend. Her friend laughed and said, "What
brings you here?"

The princess came up to that bull among men and said this among her friends,
showing her affection. "The Kurus are driving off the cattle of our kingdom,
Brihannada. My brother, the archer, will set out to win them back. Not long ago
his charioteer was killed in battle, and there is no driver equal to him who
could drive for him. As he was trying to find a charioteer, Brihannada, the
sairandhri told him of your skill with horses. So be a good friend and drive for
my brother, Brihannada, before the Kurus carry our cattle any further. If you
will not do what I tell you today, though I ask it as a friend, I shall give up
my life."

When his lovely-hipped friend said this to him, that scorcher of enemies went to
the prince, of boundless energy. The large-eyed girl followed him as he went
quickly, like an elephant in rut, as a young she-elephant follows.

When the prince saw him coming, he said to him from a distance, "With you as his
charioteer, Partha satisfied Agni at Khandava. Dhananjaya, son of Kunti,
conquered the whole earth. The sairandhri told me of you, and she knows the
Pandavas. So hold my horses in the same way, Brihannada, as I fight the Kurus to
win back the herds. They say you were Arjuna's beloved charioteer before. With
you to help him, the bull of the Pandavas conquered the earth."

When he said this, Brihannada answered the prince. "What power have I to drive a
chariot at the head of a battle? Singing, or dancing, or music of any kind, I
shall do that, bless you. But how can I drive a chariot?"

"Brihannada," said Uttara, "be a singer or a dancer again afterwards. Get on my
chariot quickly and hold these fine horses."

There, in front of Uttara, the Pandava made plenty of jokes, though he knew
everything perfectly, scorcher of enemies. He lifted the armour up and tried to
put it on upside down. The wide-eyed girls laughed when they saw him. When
Uttara saw him so confused, he himself put the costly armour on Brihannada. He
put on fine armour himself too, shining like the sun, and raised the lion
banner, and set Brihannada to drive. Taking costly bows and many bright arrows,
the hero set out with Brihannada as his charioteer.

Then the princess Uttara and the girls, her friends, called out, "Brihannada,
bring us back fine cloth, fine and bright-coloured and of many kinds, for our
dolls, when you have beaten the Kurus who have come to fight, with Bhishma and
Drona at their head."

When the girls said this together, the son of Pandu, Partha, answered them,
laughing, with a voice like a cloud or a drum. "If this Uttara beats the great
chariot fighters in battle, then I shall bring you heavenly, beautiful cloth."

With that, the hero Bibhatsu urged the horses on towards the Kurus with their
many banners and flags.

**Divergences.**

* **Arjuna prompts Draupadi.** A rejected passage (\*623) has Arjuna whisper to Draupadi to recommend him, since the time of the pledge is over. In the CE Draupadi speaks on her own, because she cannot bear Uttara's boasting about Arjuna (4.34.10–11).
* **Draupadi's praise.** A rejected appendix passage (@34) adds a longer speech about Arjuna's victories with Brihannada as charioteer. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*618–\*622, \*624–\*643) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 4.33.9 *bhūmiṃjayaṃ nāma*: "named Bhuminjaya". This is the prince Uttara's other name; he is called Uttara from 4.33.21 on.
* 4.33.16: the bow as a lute (*dhanurvīṇā*) is kept.
* 4.33.21 *strīmadhye … antaḥpure*: the herdsman speaks, and Uttara boasts, in the women's quarters.
* 4.34.3 *aṣṭāviṃśatirātraṃ vā māsaṃ vā*: "twenty-eight nights ago, or a month at the most". The battle is not named.
* 4.34.13: the sairandhri claimed to have served Draupadi (4.8.17); she speaks within her disguise.
* 4.35.1 *kutrāgamanam*: "What brings you here?" The *rājan* in this verse is Vaishampayana addressing Janamejaya.
* 4.35.7: the princess's threat to give up her life is a girl's pleading, kept as spoken.
* 4.35.18 *ūrdhvam utkṣipya kavacam*: "He lifted the armour up and tried to put it on upside down."
* 4.35.23 *pāñcālikārtham*: "for our dolls".
* The prince and the princess are both Uttara. The text is always marked to show which.

# Draupadi's Grief

Struck by the suta's son, the king's daughter blazed with anger. Wanting the
death of the commander of the army, lovely Krishna, Drupada's daughter, went to
her own quarters. Slender-waisted Krishna made herself pure as was proper,
washing her limbs and her clothes with water. Weeping, she thought how to end
her sorrow. "What shall I do? Where shall I go? How can my purpose be done?"

When she had thought like this, she went in her mind to Bhima. "No one but Bhima
can do what my heart wants today." Then she got up in the night, left her own
bed, and ran, wanting her protector, Krishna who had protectors, good woman,
high-minded, filled with great sorrow of mind.

In the kitchen the bright-smiling Panchali came to Bhimasena as a three-year-old
cow of the Mahi breed, all white, born in the forest, comes to her bull, as a
she-elephant in season comes to a great elephant. As a creeper clings to a great
sal tree in flower on the bank of the Gomati, the faultless one threw her arms
round him and woke him, as the wife of the king of beasts wakes a lion asleep in
a thick forest. Like a vina sweetly tuned in the gandhara note, faultless
Panchali spoke to Bhimasena.

"Get up! Get up! Why do you lie there like a dead man, Bhimasena? No wicked man
who has laid hands on the wife of a man who is not dead lives on. While that
worst of sinners, my enemy, the commander of the army, is alive, after what he
has done today, how can you sleep?"

Woken by the princess, he left his bed and sat up, like a cloud, on his couch
with its covers. Then the Kauravya said to the princess, his beloved queen, "Why
have you come to me in such a hurry? Your colour is not as it should be. You
look thin and pale. Tell me all of it, so that I may know everything. Happiness
or sorrow, hateful or dear, tell me all of it as it is, and when I have heard it
I shall know what to do next. For I am the one you can trust in all things,
Krishna. I am the one who frees you from dangers again and again. Tell me
quickly, as you wish, what you want to say, and go back to your bed before
anyone else wakes."

---

"How can a woman whose husband is Yudhishthira not have grief?" said Draupadi.
"You know all my sorrows. Why do you ask me? That the usher dragged me, calling
me a slave, into the middle of the hall, among the courtiers, burns me, Bharata.
What king's daughter like me would live, but Draupadi, after such suffering,
lord? And then, when I had gone to live in the forest, what woman could bear to
be laid hands on a second time, by the wicked Saindhava? And kicked by Kichaka
in front of the king of the Matsyas, before that dicer's eyes: what woman like
me would live? Tormented by so many kinds of suffering, Bharata, and you do not
know me, son of Kunti. What good is my life to me?

"This man named Kichaka, Bharata, King Virata's commander, tiger among men, his
wife's brother, is the most wicked of men. While I live in the king's house in
the dress of a sairandhri, the wicked man is always saying to me, 'Be my wife.'
As he keeps urging me, a man who deserves to die, slayer of enemies, my heart is
splitting like a fruit ripened by time.

"And blame your elder brother, the evil dicer, by whose deed I have come to this
endless sorrow. Who but an evil dicer would give up his kingdom and everything
he had, and himself too, and gamble only to go into exile? If he had gambled a
thousand gold pieces, or any other precious wealth, every evening and every
morning for many years, the gold and silver, the clothes and carriages, the
teams, goats and sheep, the herds of horses and mules would never have run out.
Thrown down from his fortune by talk of dice, he sits in silence like a fool,
thinking over his own deeds.

"He whom ten thousand elephants with lotus marks and golden garlands used to
follow as he went, now lives by dicing. So too hundreds of thousands of men of
boundless energy waited on the great king Yudhishthira in Indraprastha. A
hundred thousand slave women in his kitchen, plates in hand, fed guests day and
night. This best of givers, who gave away thousands of gold pieces, is now
surrounded by the great ruin that comes of dice. Many sutas and magadhas with
fine voices, wearing polished jewelled earrings, waited on him morning and
evening. A thousand seers, full of austerity and learning, sat in his hall every
day, provided with everything they wished. The blind, the old, the unprotected,
all those in trouble in his kingdoms, Yudhishthira supported always, untroubled,
out of his kindness.

"Now he has fallen into hell as the servant of the king of the Matsyas. In the
hall, as the king's dicer, Yudhishthira calls himself Kanka. He to whom all the
kings paid tribute while he lived in Indraprastha now asks others for wages. The
king whom the rulers of the earth obeyed is now in the power of others,
helpless. He who burned the whole earth with his splendour, like the sun, is now
King Virata's courtier, Yudhishthira. He whom kings attended in the hall with
the seers, Pandava, see now attending another. Who would not grieve to see
Yudhishthira, great in wisdom, whose self is dharma, not deserving it, taking
shelter with another for a living? He whom the whole earth attended in the hall,
hero, see now attending another, Bharata. Tormented by so many kinds of sorrow,
as if I had no protector, in the middle of an ocean of grief, why do you not see
me, Bhima?"

**Divergences.**

* **Draupadi's past.** Rejected passages (\*354–\*357) have Draupadi rehearse more of her past: Duhshasana dragging her by the hair, Jatasura, her walks through the wild country, her birth from the fire altar. Not narrated.
* **"Do not follow Yudhishthira."** A rejected passage (\*358) has Draupadi beg Bhima not to follow Yudhishthira, whom she calls idle and without spirit. Not narrated.
* **The seers of Yudhishthira's court.** A rejected appendix passage (@20) lists thousands of ascetics of many kinds in Yudhishthira's court, and another (\*360) the eighty-eight thousand snatakas. Not narrated here. Draupadi gave that last figure to Satyabhama (3.222.41).
* Other rejected lines (\*343–\*353, \*359, \*361–\*362) add details. Not narrated.

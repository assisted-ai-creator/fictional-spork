# The Wrestler

"Living like this in the city of the Matsyas," said Janamejaya, "what did the
mighty Pandavas do after that, brahmin?"

So the joys of the Kurus lived there hidden. Hear what they did as they served
the king.

Yudhishthira, the courtier, became dear to those in the hall, and to Virata and
his sons, lord of the people. For the Pandava knew the heart of the dice, and
made them play in the dicing hall as he pleased, like birds tied on strings.
Unknown to Virata, the Dharma King won wealth, and the tiger among men gave it
to his brothers as each deserved.

Bhimasena too sold to Yudhishthira meat and foods of many kinds that the king of
the Matsyas had given him. Arjuna sold the worn clothes he got in the women's
quarters, and gave the money to all the Pandavas. Sahadeva, in the dress of a
cowherd, gave the Pandavas curds, milk and ghee. Nakula too, when he got money
from the king for his work with the horses and the king was pleased, gave it to
the Pandavas. Krishna too, the ascetic, watching over all the brothers, went
about so that she would not be recognised, the lovely woman. So the great
chariot fighters looked after one another, and lived there hidden, watching over
Krishna, lord of men.

Then in the fourth month there was a great festival of Brahma, rich and much
honoured by the people among the Matsyas. Wrestlers came there in their
thousands from every direction, king, huge and of great strength, like the
Kalakhanja asuras. Swollen with courage, fierce in strength, honoured by the
king, with the shoulders, waists and necks of lions, clean and proud, they had
won many times in the arena before kings.

One of them was a giant, and he challenged all the wrestlers. No one would come
out to him as he leapt about in the arena. When all the wrestlers had lost heart
and had no spirit left, the king of the Matsyas set his cook to fight that
wrestler. Urged on, Bhima made up his mind, but with pain. For he could not
refuse the king in public.

Then that tiger among men came into the great arena, walking with a tiger's
loose stride, delighting Virata. The son of Kunti tied on his girdle, delighting
the people, and then Bhima challenged the wrestler, who was like Vritra. Both
had great spirit and fierce courage, like two huge rutting elephants sixty years
old. Bhima, slayer of enemies, tore him up with his arms and dragged him,
shouting at him as he roared, as a tiger drags an elephant. The strong-armed,
mighty one lifted him up and whirled him round, and the wrestlers and the
Matsyas were filled with wonder. When he had whirled him round a hundred times,
until he had no strength and no sense left, strong-armed Vrikodara crushed the
wrestler into the ground.

When the wrestler Jimuta, famous in the world, had been killed, Virata was
filled with the greatest joy, with his kinsmen. In his delight the great-minded
king gave Ballava much wealth in the great arena, like Vaishravana.

So he killed many wrestlers and men of great strength, and brought the king of
the Matsyas the highest pleasure. When there was no man there who was his equal,
the king made him fight tigers and lions and elephants. And again, in the
women's quarters, in the midst of the women, Vrikodara was made by Virata to
fight mighty, maddened lions.

Bibhatsu the Pandava too pleased Virata with his singing and fine dancing, and
all the women of the palace. Nakula pleased the king with the swift,
well-trained horses he brought together here and there, best of kings. When he
saw the well-trained bulls, the king was pleased with Sahadeva too, lord, and
gave him much wealth as a gift.

So the bulls among men lived there hidden, doing their work for King Virata.

**Divergences.**

* **Kichaka at the festival.** Rejected lines (\*248, \*250) bring Kichaka into the wrestling. Not narrated. In the CE Kichaka first appears in the next adhyaya (4.13).
* **Draupadi's grief.** A rejected line (\*262) says Draupadi sighed to see her husbands toil. Not narrated here; she tells her grief herself in 4.17–19.
* **The festival of Pashupati.** A rejected line (\*247) makes the festival one of Pashupati as well as Brahma. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*243–\*246, \*249, \*251–\*261, @10, @11) add details of the fight and the gifts. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 4.12.4 *sūtrabaddhān iva dvijān*: "like birds tied on strings".
* 4.12.6: Bhima "sold to Yudhishthira" the food the king gave him. The text uses the verb for selling (*vikrīṇāti*), as for Arjuna's clothes; the sense may be only "passed on".
* 4.12.12 *māse caturthe*: "in the fourth month" of their stay.
* 4.12.13 *kālakhañjāḥ*: "the Kalakhanja asuras".
* 4.12.18 *śārdūlaśithilaṃ caran*: "walking with a tiger's loose stride".
* 4.12.20 *ṣaṣṭihāyanau*: "sixty years old", elephants in their prime.
* 4.12.24: the wrestler's name, Jimuta, is given only after his death.

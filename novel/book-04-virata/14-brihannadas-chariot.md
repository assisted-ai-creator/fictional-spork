# Brihannada's Chariot

When he had driven out of the royal city, Virata's son Prithivinjaya said to his
driver, "Go where the Kurus have gone. I shall beat the gathered Kurus who want
victory, take the cattle back from them quickly and come back to my own city."

Then the son of Pandu urged on those fine horses. Urged on by that lion among
men, the horses, swift as the wind, wearing golden garlands, carried them as if
scratching the sky. When they had gone not very far, the Matsya's son and
Dhananjaya, killers of enemies, caught sight of the army of the mighty Kurus.
They went on towards the burning ground and came up with the Kurus.

That great army of theirs shone out with the sound of the sea, like a forest
thick with trees moving through the sky. The dust of the earth could be seen
raised by it as it moved, best of men, reaching the sky and blinding the eyes of
creatures.

Virata's son saw that great army, crowded with elephants, horses and chariots.
It was guarded by Karna, Duryodhana, Kripa and Shantanu's son, and by wise
Drona, the great archer, with his son. His hair on end and shaking with fear,
the prince said to Partha, "I dare not fight the Kurus. Look, my hair is
standing on end. I cannot fight that endless Kuru army, full of heroes, terribly
fierce, hard even for the gods to face. I do not hope to go into the Bharata
army with its terrible bows, crowded with chariots, elephants and horses,
thronged with foot soldiers and banners. Just seeing the enemy in battle makes
my heart shake. Drona is there, and Bhishma, Kripa, Karna, Vivimshati,
Ashvatthama, Vikarna, Somadatta, Bahlika, and the hero Duryodhana, the king,
best of chariot fighters, glorious great archers all, all skilled in war. Just
seeing these Kurus with their divisions drawn up, ready to strike, my hair
stands on end and I feel faint."

---

So the coward, in his folly, lamented in front of the Left-handed Archer, the
bold one, as that clever man watched.

"My father has gone to fight the Trigartas," said Uttara, "and left me in the
empty city. He took the whole army, and I have no soldiers here. I am a boy
alone, with no practice, and I cannot fight many men skilled in weapons. Turn
back, Brihannada."

"You look pitiful with fear," said Arjuna, "and you are making your enemies
glad. And the enemy have not done anything yet on the battlefield. You told me
yourself, 'Drive me towards the Kauravas.' So I shall take you there, where
those many banners are. I shall take you into the middle of the Kurus, who are
like vultures after meat, who have come ready to kill, strong-armed one, even if
they are fighting for the earth. You promised the women that, and your manhood
to the men. You set out boasting. Why do you not want to fight? If you go home
without winning back those cattle, hero, the men and women will all laugh at you
together. And I too have been praised by the sairandhri for my driving. I cannot
go back to the city without winning back the cattle. Because of the sairandhri's
praise, and because of what you said, why should I not fight all the Kurus?
Stand firm."

"Let the Kurus take all the wealth of the Matsyas they like," said Uttara, "and
let the women and men laugh at me, Brihannada."

With that, the prince in his earrings jumped down from the chariot in fear and
ran, throwing away his pride, the fool, and dropping his bow and arrows.

"This is not the dharma the ancients laid down for a kshatriya, running away,"
said Brihannada. "Death in battle is better for you than running away in fear."

With that, the son of Kunti leapt down from that fine chariot. Dhananjaya ran
after the prince as he ran, his long braid swinging, his two red garments
flying. Some of the soldiers, not knowing it was Arjuna, running with his braid
flying, laughed to see him in that shape.

When they saw him running fast, the Kurus said, "Who is this, hidden in that
dress, like a fire under ashes? He is partly like a man and partly like a woman.
He looks like Arjuna, but he has the form of a eunuch. That is the same head and
neck, and those are the two arms like iron bars, and his stride is the same.
This is no one but Dhananjaya. As the king of the gods is among the immortals,
so is Dhananjaya among men. Who else in the world but Dhananjaya would come
against us alone? Virata's only son was left in the empty city. He has come out,
they say, out of boyishness, not out of manhood. Surely Uttara has made Partha
Arjuna his charioteer, going about hidden in disguise, and come out of the city.
I think he is running away because he is frightened at the sight of our banners.
Surely that is Dhananjaya trying to catch him as he runs."

So all the Kurus kept turning it over, each in his own way, and they could not
decide anything, seeing the Pandava so hidden in his disguise, Bharata.

---

Dhananjaya ran after Uttara, and when he had quickly gone a hundred paces he
seized him by the hair. Seized by Arjuna, Virata's son cried out in distress,
long and pitifully.

"I shall give you a hundred pieces of pure gold, and eight bright beryls set in
gold, and a chariot with a golden pole and fine horses, and ten rutting
elephants. Let me go, Brihannada."

As he lamented, senseless, with these and other such words, that tiger among men
laughed and brought him to the chariot. Then Partha said to him, stricken with
fear, out of his wits, "If you dare not fight the enemy, slayer of enemies, come
and hold my horses while I fight them. Drive into that army of chariots, guarded
by the strength of my arms, fearful and hard to overcome, protected by heroic
great chariot fighters. Do not be afraid, best of princes. You are a kshatriya,
scorcher of enemies. I shall fight the Kurus and win back your cattle. Drive
into that army of chariots, unassailable and hard to face. Be my driver, best of
men. I shall fight the Kurus."

Talking to Virata's son like this, the unconquered Bibhatsu calmed Uttara for a
moment, bull of the Bharatas. Then Partha, best of fighters, put him on the
chariot, struggling, unwilling, stricken with fear.

---

The best of the Kurus' chariot fighters, with Bhishma and Drona at their head,
saw that bull among men on the chariot in the dress of a eunuch. He had set
Uttara on the chariot and was going towards the shami tree. They were all shaken
in mind with fear of Dhananjaya. Seeing their spirits fallen, and seeing
wondrous portents too, the teacher, Bharadvaja's son, best of those who bear
weapons, spoke.

"Harsh, unsteady winds are blowing with a rough sound. The sky is covered with a
darkness the colour of ash. Clouds of a rough colour, wondrous to see, can be
seen. Weapons of many kinds are slipping out of their sheaths. These jackals
howl terribly in the blazing quarter. The horses are shedding tears, and the
banners shake though nothing moves them. From the many signs that can be seen
here, be on your guard. There may be a battle coming. Protect yourselves, form
the army into battle order, and expect slaughter, and guard the cattle.

"This hero, the great archer, best of all who bear weapons, has come in the
dress of a eunuch. It is Partha, no doubt of it. This is that valiant Partha,
the Left-handed Archer, scorcher of enemies. He would not turn back without a
fight, not against all the hosts of the Maruts. The hero was made to suffer in
the forest, and he was trained by Vasava. He has given way to his anger, and he
will fight, no doubt of it. I see no one here, Kauravas, who can fight against
him. Even the Great God, they say, was pleased with Partha in battle."

"You are always praising Phalguna's virtues to us," said Karna. "But Arjuna is
not equal to a sixteenth part of me, or of Duryodhana."

"If this is Partha, son of Radha," said Duryodhana, "then my work is done. Once
they are recognised, they will have to wander for another twelve years. Or if
this is some other man in the dress of a eunuch, I shall bring him down to the
ground with sharp arrows."

As Dhritarashtra's son, scorcher of enemies, said this, Bhishma, Drona, Kripa
and Drona's son praised his manliness.

**Divergences.**

* **Uttara's comic pleas.** Rejected lines give the fleeing prince more to say: he has no use for cows and will live on sour gruel instead of milk and curds (\*653, \*656, \*657, @35); he calls himself Brihannada's slave and clutches her feet (\*668, \*670); he offers fourteen villages and sixty girls (\*672, \*675). None of this is in the CE, where he offers gold, gems, a chariot and ten elephants (4.36.39–40). Not narrated.
* **The Kurus' guesses.** Rejected lines have Drona and Karna speak during the chase, and Duryodhana wonder whether it is Arjuna, Vasudeva, Rama or Pradyumna (\*660, \*662, \*666). In the CE the Kurus talk among themselves and cannot decide (4.36.29–36). Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*644–\*652, \*654–\*655, \*658–\*659, \*661, \*663–\*665, \*667, \*669, \*671, \*673–\*674, \*676–\*682) add details. Not narrated.

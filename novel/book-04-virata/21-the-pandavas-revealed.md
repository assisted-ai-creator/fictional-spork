# The Pandavas Revealed

Then, on the third day, the five Pandava brothers bathed and put on white
clothes. They had kept their vow to the end of the agreed time. With
Yudhishthira at their head, adorned with every ornament, the great chariot
fighters shone like elephants in rut. They went to Virata's hall and sat down on
the seats of kings, all like blazing fires, like fires on their altars.

When they were seated there, Virata, lord of the earth, came to the hall to do
all the king's business. When he saw the glorious Pandavas, blazing like fires,
the Matsya spoke to Kanka, who sat there like a god, like the lord of the thirty
gods attended by the hosts of the Maruts. "You were a dice player. I made you a
courtier. So why are you sitting on the royal seat, dressed in ornaments?"

Hearing Virata's words, spoken as if in jest, Arjuna smiled and said this, king.

"This man is fit to sit even on Indra's throne, king. He honours brahmins, he is
learned, generous, devoted to sacrifice and firm in his vows. This is the bull
of the Kurus, Yudhishthira, son of Kunti. His fame stands in the world like the
light of the rising sun. The rays of his glory spread into every direction, like
the rays of the rising sun following its light. Ten thousand swift elephants
followed behind him, king, while he lived among the Kurus. Thirty thousand
chariots with golden garlands, drawn by fine horses, always went behind him.
Eight hundred sutas, with bright jewelled earrings, praised him with the
magadhas, as the seers praised Shakra long ago. The Kurus always waited on him
like servants, king, and all the kings, as the immortals wait on the lord of
wealth.

"He made all the rulers of the earth pay him tribute then, great king, like
vaishyas, whether they were willing or not. Eighty-eight thousand great-souled
snatakas lived on this king of good vows. He protected the old, the helpless,
the crippled and the lame like his own sons, and ruled his subjects by dharma,
lord. He keeps his vows in dharma, in self-control and in anger. He is most
gracious, devoted to brahmins, and truthful, this king. Suyodhana, with his
followers, with Karna and with Subala's son, is scorched by his glory and power.
His virtues cannot be counted, lord of men. This Pandava is always devoted to
dharma, and kind. How does such a great king, the Pandava, bull among kings,
lord of the earth, not deserve a royal seat?"

---

"If this is King Yudhishthira the Kauravya, son of Kunti," said Virata, "which
of these is his brother Arjuna, and which is mighty Bhima? Which are Nakula and
Sahadeva, and which is glorious Draupadi? Since the Parthas were beaten at dice,
they have been seen nowhere."

"This one who calls himself Ballava, your cook, lord of men," said Arjuna, "is
Bhima, strong-armed, of terrible speed and valour. He killed the Krodhavashas on
Mount Gandhamadana and brought the heavenly saugandhika flowers for Krishna. He
is the gandharva who killed the wicked Kichakas. He killed tigers, bears and
boars in your women's quarters. The one who was your horse-keeper is Nakula,
scorcher of enemies, and Sahadeva is the counter of your cattle. They are the
two sons of Madri, great chariot fighters, handsome and famous, in fine clothes
and ornaments. Those bulls among men are each a match for many thousands of
chariots. This lotus-eyed woman, slender-waisted, with the lovely smile, the
sairandhri, is Draupadi, king, for whose sake the Kichakas were killed. I am
Arjuna, great king. You must have heard of me. I am the Partha younger than
Bhima and older than the twins. We have lived happily in your house, great king,
living unrecognised, like children in the womb."

When Arjuna had named the five Pandava heroes, Virata's son told of Arjuna's
valour. "This is the one who moved among the enemy like a lion among deer, and
ranged among the masses of chariots killing the best of them. He shot a great
elephant with a single arrow and killed it, and it fell to the ground on its
tusks in the battle, with its golden girth. He won back the cattle, and he beat
the Kurus in battle. My ears were deafened by the blast of his conch."

When he heard those words, the mighty king of the Matsyas, who had wronged
Yudhishthira, said this to Uttara. "I think the time has come to win the
Pandava's favour. And I shall give the princess Uttara to Partha, if you agree."

"They are to be honoured, worshipped and respected," said Uttara, "and I think
the time has come. Let the fortunate Pandavas be honoured, as they deserve."

"I too was in the power of my enemies in battle," said Virata, "and was freed by
Bhimasena, and the cattle were won back too. It is through the strength of their
arms that we won the victory in battle. Let us all, with our counsellors, win
the favour of Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, bull of the Pandavas, with his
brothers, bless you. Whatever we may have said to the lord of men in ignorance,
he should forgive all of it, for the Pandava's soul is dharma."

---

Then Virata, full of joy, came to the king and made an agreement with him. The
great-souled one handed over to him his whole kingdom, with its sceptre, its
treasury and its city. And the mighty king of the Matsyas said to all the
Pandavas, Dhananjaya first, "How lucky! How lucky!" He smelled their heads and
embraced them again and again, Yudhishthira, Bhima and the two Pandava sons of
Madri. Virata, lord of the army, could not have enough of looking at them. In
his delight he said to King Yudhishthira, "How lucky that you have all come safe
from the forest. How lucky that you have got through that hard time,
unrecognised by the wicked. Let the sons of Kunti take this kingdom of ours,
Parthas, and any other wealth we have, all of it, without hesitation. Let the
Left-handed Archer, Dhananjaya, take Uttara. That best of men is a fitting
husband for her."

When he said this, the Dharma King looked at Partha Dhananjaya. Looked at by his
brother, Arjuna said to the Matsya, "I accept your daughter, king, as my
daughter-in-law. An alliance between us, the best of the Matsyas and the
Bharatas, is fitting."

"Why, best of the Pandavas," said Virata, "do you not want to take my daughter
as your wife, when I give her to you?"

"I have lived in the women's quarters, always seeing your daughter," said
Arjuna, "and she has trusted me like a father in private and in public. I was
dear to her and much respected as a dancer skilled in song, and your daughter
always thinks of me as her teacher. I have lived with her for a year, king, and
she is of age. You and the world would have good reason for suspicion, lord. So
I ask you for your daughter for my son, lord of the earth. I am pure,
self-controlled, restrained, and she has been kept pure by me. Between a
daughter-in-law and a daughter, as between a son and oneself, I see no cause for
suspicion. So her purity will be proved. I am afraid of slander and of false
conduct, scorcher of enemies. I accept your daughter Uttara as my
daughter-in-law, king. My son is strong-armed Abhimanyu, Vasudeva's sister's
son, like a child of the gods himself, dear to the bearer of the discus, skilled
in weapons while still a boy. He is a fit son-in-law for you, lord of the
people, and a fit husband for your daughter."

"This is worthy of the best of the Kurus, Dhananjaya son of Kunti," said Virata,
"a Pandava always devoted to dharma and born with wisdom. Do next whatever you
think should be done, Partha. All my wishes are fulfilled, since Arjuna is my
kinsman."

As the king of kings said this, Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, gave his consent to
the union, as the Matsya and the Partha had agreed.

---

Then the son of Kunti and King Virata sent word to all their friends and to
Vasudeva, Bharata. Then, when the thirteenth year was over, the five Pandavas
all came together at Virata's Upaplavya. While he lived there, Bibhatsu, the
Pandava, brought Janardana, and the Dasharhas from the Anartas, and Abhimanyu.
The king of Kashi and Shaibya came, fond of Yudhishthira, each with an army,
lord of the earth. So did mighty Yajnasena, full of energy, with an army, and
the heroic sons of Draupadi, unconquered Shikhandi, and Dhrishtadyumna, hard to
overcome, best of all who bear weapons. They were all lords of whole armies,
givers of sacrifices with rich fees, all armed with weapons, all heroes ready to
give up their bodies. When he saw them come, the Matsya, best of those who
uphold dharma, was pleased, and gave his daughter to Abhimanyu.

When the kings had come from here and there, Vasudeva came there, wearing his
forest garland, and the one armed with the plough. With them came Kritavarma,
son of Hridika, and Yuyudhana Satyaki, Anadhrishti, Akrura, Samba and Nishatha,
scorchers of enemies, bringing Abhimanyu with his mother. Indrasena and the
others came together with those chariots, well kept, after living there a full
year. Ten thousand elephants, a hundred myriads of horses, a full arbuda of
chariots and a nikharva of foot soldiers, and many Vrishnis, Andhakas and Bhojas
of great energy followed Vasudeva, tiger of the Vrishnis, of great splendour.
Krishna gave the great Pandavas wedding gifts, women, jewels and clothes, many
of each, for each of them. Then the wedding of the Matsya's daughter and the
Partha's son was held in due form.

Conches, kettledrums, gomukhas and adambaras sounded in the house of the Matsya
as he was joined with the Parthas. They killed deer of every kind, and
sacrificial animals by the hundred. They brought plenty of drinks, sura and
maireya. Singers and storytellers, actors and bards waited on them with praise,
and sutas with magadhas. The finest women of the Matsyas came with Sudeshna at
their head, lovely in every limb, with bright jewelled earrings. Those women
were of fine colour, beautiful and well adorned, but Krishna outshone them all
in beauty, fame and splendour. They surrounded the princess Uttara, adorned, and
came forward leading her, like the daughter of great Indra.

Then Dhananjaya, son of Kunti, received Virata's faultless daughter for his son,
the son of Subhadra. There the great king stood, bearing the form of Indra, and
Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, received her as his daughter-in-law. Having received
her, the Partha, with Janardana at their head, had the wedding of great-souled
Saubhadra performed. Then seven thousand horses swift as the wind and two
hundred of the finest elephants were given to him, and much wealth.

When the wedding was done, Yudhishthira, son of Dharma, gave the brahmins the
wealth that Achyuta had brought, thousands of cattle, jewels, clothes of many
kinds, the finest ornaments, carriages and beds. The city of the king of the
Matsyas, filled with glad and well-fed people, shone like a great festival, bull
of the Bharatas.

* **Draupadi's birth from the fire.** A rejected line (\*1134) has Bhima add that Draupadi was born from the fire altar. Not narrated.
* **The rites.** Rejected lines (\*1160–\*1165) have Dhaumya light the fire and perform the wedding rites, and Arjuna pour water from a golden jar. Not narrated.
* **Virata hands over his kingdom again.** Rejected lines (\*1169–\*1170) have Virata give the Pandavas his kingdom, army and treasury after the wedding. In the CE he does this once, after the revelation (4.66.21).

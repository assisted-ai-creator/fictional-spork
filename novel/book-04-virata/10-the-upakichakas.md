# The Upakichakas

At that time all Kichaka's kinsmen gathered there. When they saw Kichaka they
stood round him on every side and wept. Their hair stood on end, and they were
terrified to see Kichaka with every limb crushed in, like a tortoise pulled out
on to dry land. He had been smashed by Bhimasena, as a danava is smashed by
Indra. Wanting to perform his funeral rites, they began to carry him outside.

Then the gathered sons of the suta saw Krishna, faultless in every limb, not far
away, holding on to a pillar. When the sutas had gathered, the Upakichaka said
to them, "Kill the unchaste woman quickly. Kichaka was killed because of her. Or
rather, do not kill her here. Let her be burned with the man who desired her. In
every way we must do what would please the suta's son, even though he is dead."

Then they said to Virata, "Kichaka was killed because of her. Let her be burned
with him today. Please give your leave." Knowing the might of the sutas, lord of
the people, the king allowed the sairandhri to be burned with the suta's son.

The Kichakas went up to Krishna, lotus-eyed, terrified and fainting, and seized
her roughly. They lifted her, slender-waisted as she was, on to the bier, tied
her down, raised it up, and all set off towards the burning ground. As the sons
of the suta carried her away, king, faultless Krishna cried out, wanting her
protectors, she who had protectors.

"Jaya, Jayanta, Vijaya, Jayatsena, Jayadbala," cried Draupadi. "Let them hear my
voice. The sons of the suta are carrying me off. The slap of their bowstrings
has been heard in great battle, terrible, like the crash of a thunderbolt, and
the mighty roar of the chariots of the glorious gandharvas. Let those swift ones
hear my voice. The sons of the suta are carrying me off."

When he heard those pitiful words of Krishna's lament, Bhima leapt from his bed
without a second thought.

"I hear your voice, sairandhri, the words you have spoken," said Bhimasena. "So
you have nothing to fear from the sons of the suta, timid one."

With that, strong-armed Bhima stretched his body, wanting to kill. He made
himself huge, changed his dress, and, leaping out where there was no door, he
went outside. Bhimasena tore up a tree by force from beside the wall and made
for the burning ground, where the Kichakas had gone. Holding that tree, ten
fathoms long, with its trunk and branches, the mighty one ran at the sutas like
Death with his staff in his hand. With the force of his thighs as he ran,
banyans, fig trees and flame-of-the-forest trees fell to the ground and lay
there in heaps.

When the sutas saw the gandharva coming, angry as a lion, they were terrified on
every side, shaking with dismay and fear. The Upakichakas had been about to burn
their eldest brother. Seeing the gandharva coming towards them like Death, they
said to one another, shaking with dismay and fear, "A mighty gandharva is
coming, angry, with a tree raised high. Let the sairandhri go, quickly. A great
danger has come upon us."

When they saw the tree whirled round by Bhimasena, they let Draupadi go there
and ran for the city. As they ran, Bhima sent a hundred and five of them to
Yama's house, as the wielder of the thunderbolt did with the danavas.

Then he freed Krishna and comforted her, lord of the people. Strong-armed,
unconquerable Vrikodara said to Draupadi Panchali, whose face was wet with tears
and who was in distress, "This is how those who torment you, though you have
done no wrong, are killed, timid one. Go to the city, Krishna. You have nothing
to fear. I shall go to Virata's kitchen by another way."

The hundred and five lay there, killed, Bharata, like a great forest cut down,
its trees fallen. So the hundred and five Kichakas were killed, king, and the
commander before them: a hundred and six sutas in all. When they saw that great
wonder, men and women gathered there, so amazed that they could not say a word,
Bharata.

---

When they had seen the sutas killed, the people went to the king and told him.
"The sons of the suta have been killed by the gandharvas, king, more than a
hundred of them. The sutas lie scattered on the ground like the great peak of a
mountain split by a thunderbolt. And that sairandhri is free, and she is coming
back to your house. Your whole city will be in danger, king. For the sairandhri
is so beautiful, and the gandharvas are so strong, and there is no doubt that
men long to lie with a woman. Make a plan, king, so that this city of yours does
not quickly come to ruin because of the sairandhri."

When Virata, the lord of the army, heard what they said, he said, "Let the last
rites be done for these sutas. Let all the Kichakas be burned quickly on one
well-kindled fire, with jewels and perfumes of every kind."

And the king, full of fear, said to Queen Sudeshna, "When the sairandhri comes,
tell her this from me. 'Go, sairandhri, bless you. Go where you please, lovely
woman. The king is afraid, lovely-hipped one, of being overcome by the
gandharvas.' I cannot say it to her myself, since the gandharvas protect her.
But women are not at fault in saying it to her. That is why I am telling you."

Then, set free from fear, freed by Bhimasena from the sons of the suta whom he
had struck down, Krishna went towards the city, like a young doe frightened by a
tiger, proud woman. She washed her limbs and her clothes with water. When the
men saw her, king, they ran in all ten directions, terrified of the gandharvas,
and some shut their eyes.

Then, at the door of the kitchen, king, Panchali saw Bhimasena standing like a
great rutting elephant. Wondering at him, she spoke quietly, with signs. "Homage
to the king of the gandharvas, who has set me free."

"The men who live here under a woman's command," said Bhimasena, "when they hear
her word, go about free of their debt."

Then in the dance hall she saw Dhananjaya, strong-armed, teaching King Virata's
daughters to dance. The girls came out of the dance hall with Arjuna, and saw
Krishna coming, ill-used, though she had done no wrong.

"How lucky that you are free, sairandhri," said the girls. "How lucky that you
have come back. How lucky that the sutas who tormented you, though you had done
no wrong, have been killed."

"How did you get free, sairandhri?" said Brihannada. "And how were those wicked
men killed? I want to hear it all from you, just as it happened."

"Brihannada, what does the sairandhri matter to you now?" said the sairandhri.
"You live happily all the time in the maidens' quarters, lovely one. You do not
meet with the sorrow the sairandhri suffers. That is why you ask me, in my
misery, as if you were laughing at me."

"Brihannada too suffers the greatest sorrow, lovely one," said Brihannada. "She
has fallen into the state of a beast, girl, and you do not understand her."

Then Draupadi, with the girls, went into the royal house, to Sudeshna, not
running away. The king's daughter said to her, on Virata's orders, "Sairandhri,
go quickly, wherever you wish to go. The king is afraid, bless you, of being
overcome by the gandharvas. And you are young, lovely-browed one, and there is
no one on earth to match your beauty."

"Let the king bear with me for only thirteen days, lady," said the sairandhri.
"The gandharvas will have done what they must, no doubt of it. Then they will
take me away, and they will do what pleases you. And the king and his kinsmen
will surely meet with good fortune."

**Divergences.**

* **The tree.** Here, against the Upakichakas, Bhima does tear up a tree (4.22.18–19). The tree against Kichaka himself, in Virata's hall, is only in rejected passages. See L-164.
* **The number.** The CE gives a hundred and five Upakichakas (4.22.25, 4.22.28–29). The count is from the CE itself, not from a later addition.
* Rejected lines (\*458–\*493, @26) add speeches and details, among them a longer speech of comfort from Bhima, with the tale of a faithful wife (@26). Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 4.22.2 *kūrmaṃ sthala ivoddhṛtam*: "like a tortoise pulled out on to dry land". His limbs are pushed into his body, as a tortoise draws in its head and feet.
* 4.22.5 *upakīcakaḥ*: "the Upakichaka", one of Kichaka's younger brothers; the name covers them all at 4.22.22.
* 4.22.11 *nātham icchantī kṛṣṇā nāthavatī satī*: as at 4.16.5.
* 4.22.12: Draupadi calls the gandharvas by the Pandavas' secret names (4.5.30).
* 4.22.17 *vyāyataṃ kṛtvā veṣaṃ viparivartya ca*: "He made himself huge, changed his dress". *advāreṇa*: "where there was no door".
* 4.22.19 *daśavyāmam*: "ten fathoms long".
* 4.22.20 *nyagrodhāśvatthakiṃśukāḥ*: "banyans, fig trees and flame-of-the-forest trees".
* 4.22.29 *sūtaṣaṭśatam*: "a hundred and six sutas in all", the hundred and five with Kichaka.
* 4.23.4: the people's words about desire are kept, plainly.
* 4.23.15 *saṃjñābhiḥ*: "with signs".
* 4.23.16: Bhima's reply is a riddle, in keeping with their disguise.
* 4.23.23 *tiryagyonigatā*: "fallen into the state of a beast". Arjuna speaks of his state as a eunuch.
* 4.23.27 *trayodaśāhamātram*: "only thirteen days". At 4.20.13 Bhima said a month and a half; time has passed since.

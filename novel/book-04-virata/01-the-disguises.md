# The Disguises

"How did my great-grandfathers live unrecognised in Virata's city," said
Janamejaya, "troubled by their fear of Duryodhana?"

When he had won those boons from Dharma, Yudhishthira, the best of those who
keep dharma, went to the hermitage and told the brahmins all of it. When he had
told them everything, Yudhishthira gave the fire-sticks back to the brahmin.
Then King Yudhishthira, the great-minded son of Dharma, called all his younger
brothers back and said to them, Bharata.

"We have lived away from our kingdom for these twelve years. The thirteenth has
come, hard and very hard to live through. So, Arjuna son of Kunti, choose a
place to live from here, where we can spend all these days unknown to others."

"By that same Dharma's boon, lord of men," said Arjuna, "we shall go about
unrecognised by men, bull of the Bharatas. But I shall name some kingdoms to
live in, pleasant and hidden. Choose one of them. There are pleasant countries
rich in food all round the Kurus: the Panchalas, the Chedis and the Matsyas, the
Shurasenas, the Patachcharas, the Dasharnas, the Navarashtra, the Mallas, the
Shalvas and the Yugamdharas. Which of these do you like as our dwelling, king,
where we shall live out this year, king of kings?"

"It is as you say, strong-armed one," said Yudhishthira. "What that blessed
lord, the lord of all beings, said is so, and not otherwise. But we must all
consult together and find a place to live that is pleasant, kind and
comfortable, where we need fear nothing. Virata, king of the Matsyas, is strong,
and he would protect the Pandavas. He is devoted to dharma, generous, old and
very rich. Let us spend this year in Virata's city, my friend, doing his work,
Bharata. Tell me, joys of the Kurus, which of his tasks each of you can do."

"Lord of men," said Arjuna, "what work will you do in his kingdom? What work,
good king, will make you happy in King Virata's service? You are gentle,
generous, modest, devoted to dharma, and your valour is truth. You have been
wounded by misfortune, king. What will you do, Pandava? You have never known any
kind of hardship, as ordinary people do. How will you get through this terrible
misfortune?"

"Hear the work I shall do, joys of the Kurus," said Yudhishthira. "When I come
to King Virata, that bull among men, I shall be a courtier in the great king's
hall. I shall become a brahmin named Kanka, who knows the dice and loves to
play. I shall roll lovely dice of beryl, gold and ivory, with their dots and
their colours, black dice and red. If the king asks me, I shall tell him, 'I was
once Yudhishthira's friend, as dear to him as his own life.' That is how I shall
live, as I have told you. Vrikodara, what work will make you happy with Virata?"

---

"I shall call myself a cook named Ballava," said Bhima, "and go into King
Virata's service. That is my thought. I shall make his sauces, for I am skilled
in the kitchen. I shall surpass even the well-trained cooks who have made his
dishes before, and win his favour. I shall bring great heaps of wood too, and
when the king sees that great work he will be pleased. If there are strong
elephants or mighty bulls to be held down, I shall hold them down too. And
whatever wrestlers fight in the contests, I shall beat them and add to his
pleasure. But I will not kill them as they fight. I shall throw them so that
they do not die. If anyone asks, I shall say, 'I was Yudhishthira's cook, his
butcher, his maker of sauces and his wrestler.' I shall guard myself as I go
about, lord of the people. That is how I promise to live."

"This is the one to whom Agni came long ago as a brahmin," said Yudhishthira,
"the best of men, with Dasharha, wanting to burn the Khandava forest. He is
mighty and strong-armed, never beaten, the joy of the Kurus. What work will
Dhananjaya son of Kunti do? He is the one who went into that forest and
satisfied the fire. He beat Indra with a single chariot and killed the snakes
and rakshasas. He is called the best of those who fight back. What will Arjuna
do?

"The sun is the best of those that burn, the brahmin the best of two-footed
beings, the venomous snake the best of serpents, and fire the best of the
bright. The thunderbolt is the best of weapons, the humped bull the best of
cattle, the ocean the best of waters, and the rain cloud the best of those that
rain. Dhritarashtra is the best of the snakes, Airavata the best of elephants, a
son the dearest of the dear, and a wife the best of friends. As these are the
best of their kinds, Vrikodara, so is young Gudakesha the best of all archers.
He is no less than Indra or Vasudeva, Bharata, this bearer of Gandiva with his
white horses, Bibhatsu. What will he do? He lived five years in the house of the
thousand-eyed god, and won the divine weapons, shining in the form of a god. I
think him the twelfth Rudra and the thirteenth Aditya. His arms are long and
equal, their skin hardened by the blows of the bowstring, on the right and on
the left, like the hump of a bull. As the Himalaya among mountains, the ocean
among rivers, Shakra among the thirty gods and the carrier of offerings among
the Vasus, the tiger among beasts and Garuda among birds, so is Arjuna the best
of those who put on armour. What will he do?"

"I shall declare that I am a eunuch, lord of the earth," said Arjuna. "For the
great scars of the bowstring on my arms are hard to hide, king. I shall hang
earrings like fire in my ears, braid my hair, king, and call myself Brihannada.
Telling stories again and again in the manner of a woman, I shall entertain the
king and the others in the women's quarters. I shall teach the women in Virata's
palace song and dance, and many kinds of music, king. Speaking much of the ways
and doings of the people, I shall hide myself by my own art, son of Kunti. If
the king asks me, I shall say, 'I lived in Yudhishthira's house as Draupadi's
maid,' Bharata. Hidden in this way, as Nala was in disguise, I shall live
happily in Virata's palace, king of kings."

---

"What will you do, Nakula, my son, to live there?" said Yudhishthira. "You are
tender and brave, handsome and used to comfort."

"I shall be King Virata's keeper of horses," said Nakula, "and my name will be
Granthika. This is work I love. I am skilled in training horses and in treating
them, and horses have always been dear to me, as they are to you, king of the
Kurus. Whoever asks me in Virata's city, that is what I shall tell them. That is
how I shall live."

"Sahadeva," said Yudhishthira, "how will you live near him? What will you do, my
son, to go about hidden?"

"I shall be King Virata's counter of cattle," said Sahadeva, "skilled in
restraining and milking and counting cows. Let it be known that I am called
Tantipala by name. I shall do it well. Let the fever of your mind pass. For you
set me over the cows long ago, lord of the people, and I have learned that work
well. The marks of cattle, their ways and what brings them luck, I know all of
it well, and other things too, lord of the earth. I know the bulls too, king,
with their honoured marks, whose urine, when it is smelt, makes even a barren
cow conceive. That is how I shall live, for I always delight in it. No one else
will know me. Let it please you, lord of the earth."

"But this is our beloved wife," said Yudhishthira, "dearer than our lives, who
must be cherished like a mother and honoured like an elder sister. What work
will Krishna, Draupadi, do to live? For she does not know any of the work that
women do. She is tender and young, a glorious king's daughter, faithful to her
husbands and greatly blessed. How will she live? She knows only garlands and
perfumes, ornaments and clothes of many kinds, as one born to them, the lovely
one."

"There are sairandhris in the world, Bharata, maids who serve others and are
under no man's protection," said Draupadi. "Other women do not live like this.
That is what the world holds. So I shall call myself a sairandhri, skilled in
dressing hair, and I shall go about guarding myself, since you ask me. I shall
wait on Sudeshna, the king's glorious wife. She will protect me when I come to
her. Do not let such sorrow be yours."

"You speak well, Krishna," said Yudhishthira, "as one born in a good family
would speak. You know nothing of evil, good woman, keeping to the vow of a good
wife."

**Divergences.**

* **Urvashi's curse.** Rejected lines (\*53, \*62) have Arjuna say he will use the curse of the apsaras Urvashi, who made him a eunuch for a year. In the CE Arjuna simply declares that he will call himself a eunuch, because the bowstring scars on his arms are hard to hide (4.2.21). See L-25.
* **Opening invocations.** Several rejected passages (\*1–\*4) are benedictory verses placed before the Book in some manuscripts. Not narrated.
* **More roles.** Rejected passages add to the roles: Yudhishthira as an ascetic learned in astrology and omens (\*23), Bhima fighting lions for the king's sport (\*34, \*35), Nakula as a keeper of the harness (\*71), and others. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*5–\*22, \*24–\*33, \*36–\*52, \*54–\*61, \*63–\*100, @1) add phrases, speeches and repetitions. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 4.1.9: the countries are named as the text names them.
* 4.1.11: "what that blessed lord, the lord of all beings, said", Dharma's boon (3.298.17–18).
* 4.1.20 *kaṅkaḥ*: "Kanka". *sabhāstāraḥ*: "a courtier in the hall". *matākṣaḥ priyadevitā*: "who knows the dice and loves to play".
* 4.1.21: the dice are described as the text describes them. The sense of *phalair jyotīrasaiḥ* is uncertain; "with their dots and their colours" is a guess.
* 4.2.1 *paurogavaḥ … ballavaḥ*: "a cook named Ballava". *Paurogava* may mean a head of the kitchen.
* 4.2.7 *ārālikaḥ govikartā sūpakartā niyodhakaḥ*: "his cook, his butcher, his maker of sauces and his wrestler". *Govikartā* is literally "cow-carver"; the sense in this list is uncertain.
* 4.2.9 *dāśārhasahitam*: "with Dasharha", Krishna.
* 4.2.21 *ṣaṇḍhakaḥ*: "a eunuch".
* 4.2.22 *bṛhannaḍā*: "Brihannada".
* 4.2.27 *yathā nalaḥ*: "as Nala was in disguise", in the story told at 3.50–78.
* 4.3.2 *granthikaḥ*: "Granthika". 4.3.7 *tantipālaḥ*: "Tantipala".
* 4.3.16 *sairandhryaḥ … bhujiṣyāḥ*: "sairandhris … maids who serve others". Draupadi's point is that such women live under no man's protection.

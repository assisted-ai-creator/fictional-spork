# The Council in Virata's Hall

When they had held Abhimanyu's wedding, the heroes of the Kurus and all their
side were glad. They rested for four days. Then, in good spirits, they went to
Virata's hall.

The hall of the Matsya king was rich, bright with the finest gems and jewels.
Seats were set out in it, and garlands hung there, and it smelled sweet. The
best of the kings of men came into it. In the front seats sat the two kings,
Virata and Drupada. With Rama and Janardana sat the aged grandfather, honoured
among the kings of the earth. Near the king of the Panchalas sat the hero of the
Shinis, with Rohini's son. Close beside the Matsya king sat Janardana and
Yudhishthira.

All the sons of King Drupada were there, and Bhima and Arjuna, and the two sons
of Madri. So were Pradyumna and Samba, heroes in battle, and Virata's son with
Abhimanyu. Draupadi's sons, the young princes, sat on fine seats worked with
gold. They were all heroes, equal to their fathers in courage, looks and
strength. When the great chariot fighters sat there in their shining clothes and
ornaments, the crowded hall of kings looked like the sky full of clear stars.

The heroes talked together of many things. Then for a while they sat thinking,
with their eyes on Krishna. When the talk came to an end, Madhava turned them to
the business of the Pandavas. Together, those lions among kings listened to his
words, which were weighty and looked far ahead.

---

"You all know," said Krishna, "how Yudhishthira here was beaten at dice by
Subala's son through a trick, how his kingdom was taken, and how a pact was made
about his exile. The sons of Pandu could have conquered the earth by force. But
they stood by the truth, and these leaders of the Bharatas kept that harsh vow
to the letter for six years and seven. This thirteenth year was the hardest to
get through. They lived it near you, unrecognised, putting up with hardships no
one should have to bear. You know all that.

"Things being as they are, think about what would be good for the Dharma King
and for Duryodhana too. Think what is right, fitting and honourable for the
Kurus and the Pandavas alike. The Dharma King would not want even the kingdom of
the gods if it came with adharma. But he would be glad to rule even a single
village, if it came by dharma and by right.

"The kings know how the sons of Dhritarashtra took his father's kingdom, and how
by their lying conduct he came into great hardship that was hard to bear.
Dhritarashtra's sons never beat the Partha in battle by their own strength. Even
so, the king and his friends want nothing for them but their good health. What
the sons of Kunti and of Madri ask for is what they won themselves, by conquest,
by pressing the kings of the earth. You all know too how those wicked, savage
men tried by every kind of means to kill them when they were boys, because they
wanted their kingdom.

"Look how far their greed has grown. Look at Yudhishthira's devotion to dharma,
and at the ties of kinship between them. Then make up your minds, together and
each on your own. These men have always loved the truth. They have kept the pact
exactly. If they are treated any other way now, they would kill the sons of
Dhritarashtra, all of them together. And when their friends hear of the wrong
done to them, they will gather round them. If the others attack them in war,
they will fight, and kill them in battle.

"You may think they are too few to win. But they would all join together with
their friends and work for their enemies' destruction. Nor do we know for
certain what Duryodhana thinks, or what he will do. While the other side's mind
is unknown, what is the best thing for you to set about? So let a man go from
here as envoy, a man devoted to dharma, honest, of good family and careful. Let
him be able to bring them to peace, and to give half the kingdom to
Yudhishthira."

---

Janardana's words were in keeping with dharma and with good sense, gentle and
fair. When they had been heard, his elder brother spoke, and praised them first.

"You have heard the words of Gada's elder brother," said Baladeva. "They are as
true to dharma as they are to sense. They are good for Ajatashatru, and just as
good for King Duryodhana. The heroic sons of Kunti will give up half the kingdom
and work for the other half. Let Dhritarashtra's son give that half, and he can
live happily with us and be glad. Once these heroes have the kingdom, if the
others behave properly, they will surely be content and live in peace. And their
peace is good for the people.

"I would be glad if someone went there to make peace between the Kurus and the
Pandavas, to find out Duryodhana's mind and to speak for Yudhishthira. Let him
pay his respects to Bhishma, hero of the Kurus, and to great Vaichitravirya, to
Drona and his son, to Vidura and Kripa, to the king of Gandhara and to the
suta's son. Let him do the same to all the other sons of Dhritarashtra, strong
in arms and strong in learning, men who keep to their own duties, heroes of the
world, old in knowledge and in years. When they have all met, and the old men of
the city with them, let him speak humbly, in the way that will best serve the
son of Kunti.

"They should not be provoked in any way. What they have, they took by their own
strength. Yudhishthira was fond of the game. He was careless at the dice, and
his kingdom was taken. All the Kuru heroes, all his friends, tried to stop him.
But this descendant of Ajamidha, who knew nothing of the game, challenged the
son of the king of Gandhara, who was a master of the dice. There were thousands
of other gamblers there whom Yudhishthira could have beaten. He passed them all
over and challenged Subala's son, and he lost to him at the dice. He played
against him, and the throws kept going against him. He grew angry and was beaten
outright. Shakuni was in no way at fault there.

"So let the envoy bow down and speak to Vaichitravirya with every gentle word.
That is how Dhritarashtra's son can be brought round to what we want."

---

While the hero of the Madhus was still speaking, the hero of the Shinis leapt
up. He found fault with what had been said, and spoke angrily.

"A man speaks as his soul is," said Satyaki. "You speak like what you are
inside. There are brave men and there are cowards. People fall clearly into
these two kinds. A coward and a great chariot fighter can be born in one family,
just as one tree can have a branch that bears fruit and a branch that bears
none. I don't blame your words, you with the plough on your banner. I blame the
people who sit and listen to them, Madhava. How can anyone speak of the smallest
fault in the Dharma King, in the middle of an assembly, and not be afraid?

"Men who knew the dice challenged a great-souled man who did not know them, and
who trusted them, and beat him. How was that a victory by dharma? If they had
come to the son of Kunti while he was playing at home with his brothers, and had
beaten him, that would have been fair. But they challenged a king who always
delights in the dharma of a kshatriya, and they beat him by a trick. What good
is there in that? How can this man bow down to them here, now that he has kept
the terms of the wager? He is free of his exile in the forest. He has a right to
his ancestors' throne. Even if Yudhishthira wanted other people's wealth, he
still should not have to beg from his enemies.

"How can they call themselves dharmic, and say they don't want to seize the
kingdom, when they say the sons of Kunti were recognised before their time was
up? Bhishma and great Drona have pleaded with them, and still they won't agree
to give the Pandavas their father's wealth. I will plead with them in battle,
with sharp arrows. I will make them fall at the feet of the great son of Kunti.
If they won't agree to bow to that wise man, they will go to Yama's house with
their counsellors. When Yuyudhana is angry and wants to fight, they can no more
stand his charge than mountains can stand the thunderbolt.

"Who could face the Gandiva bowman in battle, or the one whose weapon is the
discus, or me? Who could face Bhima, whom no one can approach? Who that wants to
live would come near the twins, firm bowmen bright as Yama, or Dhrishtadyumna,
son of Prishata? Or these five sons of the Pandavas by Draupadi, who add to her
fame, as tall as the Pandavas, as brave, and fierce with pride? Or Subhadra's
son, the great archer, whom even the immortals could hardly withstand? Or Gada,
Pradyumna and Samba, who are like Time, the thunderbolt and fire?

"We will kill Dhritarashtra's son in battle, with Shakuni and with Karna, and we
will crown the Pandava. There is no adharma in killing enemies who come to kill
you. Begging from enemies is what is against dharma, and shameful. Do what he
has set his heart on, and don't be slow about it. Let the Pandava have the
kingdom Dhritarashtra gave up. Either Yudhishthira, son of Pandu, gets his
kingdom today, or they will all lie dead on the ground, killed in battle."

---

"Strong-armed one, this is how it will be, no doubt of it," said Drupada.
"Duryodhana will never give up the kingdom for sweet words. Dhritarashtra, who
loves his son, will go along with him. So will Bhishma and Drona, out of
weakness, and Radha's son and Subala's son, out of folly.

"Baladeva's advice does not sit with my judgment. True, a man who wants good
policy should try this first. But Dhritarashtra's son must never be spoken to
softly. I don't think that wicked man can be won by gentleness. Whoever speaks
softly to Duryodhana, with his wicked heart, is being gentle with a donkey and
harsh with cows. When the wicked man is spoken to gently, he thinks it comes
from weakness. The fool will think he has already won.

"We will do this too, but let us make our efforts here as well. Let us send to
our friends, and have them raise armies for us. Let swift messengers go to
Shalya, to Dhrishtaketu, to Jayatsena and to all the Kekayas. Duryodhana will
certainly send to them all. And good men side with whoever asks them first. So
hurry to be first in asking the kings. I believe a great task lies before us.

"Send quickly to Shalya and the kings who follow him, and to King Bhagadatta,
who lives by the eastern sea. Send to Amitaujas and to Ugra, to Hardikya and to
Ahuka, to the far-sighted king of the Mallas, and to Rochamana. Have Brihanta
brought, and King Senabindu, Papajit, Prativindhya, Chitravarman and Suvastuka,
Bahlika, Munjakesha and the lord of the Chedis, Suparshva, Subahu and the great
chariot fighter Paurava. Send to the kings of the Shakas, the Pahlavas and the
Daradas, to the Kambojas and the Rishikas, and to the kings of the western
shore. Send to Jayatsena and the king of Kashi, the kings of the land of the
five rivers, the unconquerable son of Kratha, and the kings of the mountains.

"Send to Janaki, to Susharma, to Maniman and Pautimatsyaka, to the lord of the
Pamsu kingdom, and to mighty Dhrishtaketu. Send to the Audra, to Dandadhara and
mighty Brihatsena, to Aparajita, Nishada, Shreniman and Vasuman. Send to
Brihadbala, great in strength, and Bahu, conqueror of enemy cities, and to King
Samudrasena with his mighty son. Send to Adari and Nadija and King Karnaveshta,
to Samartha and Suvira, Marjara and Kanyaka, to Mahavira and Kadru, Nikara,
Tumula and Kratha, to Nila and Viradharman and mighty Bhumipala. Send to Durjaya
and Dantavaktra, to Rukmin and Janamejaya, to Ashadha and Vayuvega and King
Purvapali, to Bhuriteja and Devaka and the son of Ekalavya, to the kings of the
Karushas and mighty Kshemadhurti. Send to Udbhava and Kshemaka and King
Vatadhana, to Shrutayu and Dridhayu and the mighty son of Shalva, and to the
prince who is lord of the Kalingas, mad for battle. Send to these quickly. That
is what I think best.

"And let this brahmin, my priest, be sent quickly to Dhritarashtra, king. Give
him the message. Tell him how Duryodhana is to be spoken to, how the king who is
Shantanu's son should be addressed, and Dhritarashtra, and Drona, the best of
the learned."

---

"These words are fitting for the leader of the Somakas," said Vasudeva. "They
will win success for the Pandava king, so full of energy. We want good policy,
and this is the first thing we must do. A man who acted otherwise would be a
great fool.

"But our tie to the Kurus and to the Pandavas is the same, however the Pandavas
and the others behave. We were all brought here for the wedding, you as much as
us. Now that the wedding is over, we shall be glad to go home. You are the
eldest of the kings in years and in learning. We are all like your pupils here,
no doubt of that. Dhritarashtra always holds you in high regard. You are the
friend of both the teachers, Drona and Kripa. So send a message today that will
serve the Pandavas. Whatever you send will be decided for us all.

"If the bull of the Kurus makes peace on fair terms, there will be no great
slaughter between brothers, the Kurus and the Pandavas. But if Dhritarashtra's
son is proud, and out of folly will not make peace, then send to the others, and
call us after them. Then slow-witted, deluded Duryodhana will meet his end, with
his counsellors and his kin, when the Gandiva bowman is angry."

Then Virata, lord of the earth, paid honour to the Vrishni and sent him home
with his followers and kin. When Krishna had gone to Dvaraka, Yudhishthira and
the others, with King Virata, made every preparation for war. Virata and his
kin, and King Drupada, sent word to all the kings of the earth. At the word of
the lions of the Kurus, and of the Matsya and the Panchala, the kings came,
mighty and glad. When the sons of Pandu heard that, they gathered a great army.
Dhritarashtra's son too gathered the kings of the earth.

The whole earth was in turmoil then, king, because of the Kurus and the
Pandavas, as the kings set out. The armies of those heroes came from every side.
They seemed to shake the goddess earth, with her mountains and her forests.

---

Then, with Yudhishthira's agreement, the Panchala king sent his own priest to
the Kurus, a man old in years and in wisdom.

"Among beings, those that breathe are best," said Drupada. "Among those that
breathe, those that live by their wits. Among those, men are best, and among
men, the twice-born. Among the twice-born, the learned are best, and among the
learned, those whose minds are made up. I think you are first among those. You
stand out in family, in years and in learning. In wisdom you are not less than
Shukra or Angirasa's son.

"You know how the Kaurava has behaved, and how Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, the
Pandava, has behaved. With Dhritarashtra's knowledge the Pandavas were cheated
by their enemies. Vidura pleaded with him, but he still follows his son. Shakuni
challenged the son of Kunti on purpose. Shakuni was a master of the dice, and
the son of Kunti was honest and kept to the ways of a kshatriya and knew nothing
of them. Having cheated Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, like that, they will
not give back the kingdom of their own accord, whatever happens.

"But when you speak words of dharma to Dhritarashtra, you will surely turn the
hearts of his fighting men. Vidura will back what you say. You will cause a
split among Bhishma, Drona and Kripa. Once the counsellors are divided and the
fighting men have turned away, their task will be to bring them together again.
Meanwhile the Parthas, at ease and of one mind, will raise their army and lay in
stores. While their own people are being divided, and while you are kept waiting
there, they will not be able to raise an army in the same way, no doubt of it.
This is the main purpose here. It may also be that when he meets you,
Dhritarashtra will act on your words of dharma.

"You are a man of dharma. Act by dharma among them. Tell the merciful ones of
the Pandavas' sufferings. Speak to the old men of the family dharma that their
forefathers kept. I have no doubt you will divide their hearts. You need have no
fear of them. You are a brahmin who knows the Vedas, you are on an envoy's
business, and above all you are old. So go quickly to the Kauravas, under the
star Pushya, at an hour of victory, to win success for the son of Kunti."

Instructed in this way by great Drupada, the priest, a man of good conduct, set
out for the city named after the elephant.

**Divergences.**

* **The opening.** The rejected lines \*1–\*6 add an invocation, a question from Janamejaya and a hymn to Vyasa. Not narrated.
* **Krishna's speech.** A rejected line (\*8) has Krishna say the Pandavas served others in the thirteenth year; another (\*9) adds that they were abandoned by their kin. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*7, \*10–\*31) add epithets and repeated details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.1.1 *viśramya catvāry uṣasaḥ*: "rested for four days" (literally "four dawns"). Ganguli has "that night".
* 5.1.3 *pitāmaho rāmajanārdanābhyām*: the verse does not name the "aged grandfather" who sits with Rama and Janardana. Ganguli reads "their father". He is left unnamed.
* 5.1.4 *saharauhiṇeyaḥ*: Rohini's son is Balarama. *śinipravīraḥ* is Satyaki.
* 5.2.1 *gadapūrvajasya*: "Gada's elder brother", Krishna.
* 5.2.11 *pratidevanena … akṣeṣu nityaṃ suparāṅmukheṣu*: "the throws kept going against him".
* 5.3.11: Satyaki refers to the Kauravas' claim that the Pandavas were recognised before the thirteen years were out (see 4.42, Book 4 ch 16).
* 5.4.3 *baladevasya vākyaṃ tu mama jñāne na yujyate*: "does not sit with my judgment". Ganguli reverses this ("commend themselves to my judgment").
* 5.4.5: the donkey and the cows are the text's own image. The verse is read as "to speak softly to Duryodhana is to be gentle with a donkey and harsh with cows", that is, to get things backwards.
* 5.4.11–25: the list of kings is kept in full and in the text's order. Readings follow the CE word division: *amitaujase tathogrāya* as two names, Amitaujas and Ugra; *dīrghaprajñāya mallāya* as "the far-sighted king of the Mallas"; *auḍra* as "the Audra", a king of the Odras.
* 5.6.3: "Angirasa's son" is Brihaspati.
* 5.6.17 *puṣyayogena*: "under the star Pushya", an auspicious conjunction.
* 5.6.18 *nāgasāhvayam*: "the city named after the elephant", Hastinapura.

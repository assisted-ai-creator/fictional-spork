# The Council in Virata's Hall

When the heroes of the Kurus and their glad allies had held Abhimanyu's wedding,
they rested for four days. Then, content, they went to Virata's hall.

The hall of the lord of the Matsyas was rich, bright with the finest gems and
jewels. The seats were set out, there were garlands, and the air was sweet. The
best of the kings of men came there. In the front seats sat the two kings,
Virata and Drupada, and the aged grandfather whom the kings honoured, with
Balarama and Krishna. Close to the king of the Panchalas sat Satyaki, the hero
of the Shinis, with Balarama, son of Rohini. Near to King Virata sat Krishna and
Yudhishthira. All of Drupada's sons were there, Bhima and Arjuna and the two
sons of Madri, Pradyumna and Samba, heroes in battle, and Virata's son with
Abhimanyu. And Draupadi's sons, the young princes, sat on fine seats worked with
gold. They were all heroes, like their fathers in courage, in looks and in
strength.

When those great chariot fighters had sat down, shining in their clothes and
ornaments, the hall full of kings glowed like the sky set with bright planets.
The heroes told one another many tales, as suited the gathering. Then they sat
thinking for a while, and all those kings looked at Krishna. When their talk was
over, Krishna drew them together to the Pandavas' business, and the lions among
kings listened as one to a speech that was full of meaning and full of promise.

---

"You all know," said Krishna, "how Yudhishthira here was beaten by Subala's son
at the dice, by trickery, how his kingdom was taken, and how the terms of the
exile were set. The sons of Pandu could have conquered the earth by force. But
they held to the truth, and for six years and seven more these first among the
Bharatas kept that cruel vow as it was set. The thirteenth year, the hardest to
get through, they spent unrecognised, close by you, bearing hardships no one
should have to bear. You know all this, and how they lived.

"Now that things stand so, think what would be good for Yudhishthira, the son of
Dharma, and for Duryodhana too. Think what is right and fitting for the Kurus
and the Pandavas, and what will bring them honour. The Dharma King would not
want even the kingdom of the gods if it came against dharma. But if it comes
with dharma and with its proper profit, he would be glad to rule even a single
village.

"The kings know how the sons of Dhritarashtra took his father's kingdom from
him. By that false dealing he fell into great hardship that no one could bear.
And yet it was not by their own strength that the sons of Dhritarashtra beat the
Partha in battle. Even so, the king and his friends wish them nothing but good.
What the Pandavas themselves won, when they conquered the kings of the earth and
pressed tribute from them, that is what the heroes ask for, the sons of Kunti
and the two sons of Madri.

"You know well how, when these unconquerable men were boys, those others tried
by every kind of means to kill them. They were cruel and wicked, and wanted the
kingdom for themselves. Look at their greed, grown great as it is. Look at the
goodness of Yudhishthira. Look at how they are related. Then make up your minds,
together and each on your own.

"These men have always loved the truth, and they have kept the agreement to the
letter. If they are dealt with in any other way, they will kill the sons of
Dhritarashtra, all of them together. When their friends hear of the wrong done
to the king, they will stand round them. If they are attacked in war, they will
fight back, and in the fighting they will kill their attackers. If you think
these few are not strong enough to win, know this: together, with their friends,
they would still strive to destroy them.

"And we do not know what Duryodhana thinks, or what he means to do. While the
other side's mind is unknown, what can you decide is best to begin with? So let
someone go from here as an envoy. Let him be a man of good conduct, honest, of
good family and careful, able to make peace between them, and let him ask for
half the kingdom for Yudhishthira."

---

When Balarama, Krishna's elder brother, had heard these words, which were right,
wise, gentle and fair, he praised them highly. Then he spoke.

"You have all heard the speech of Gada's elder brother," said Balarama. "It is
right and it is wise. It is good for Ajatashatru, and good for King Duryodhana
as well. The heroic sons of Kunti will give up half the kingdom and strive for
the other half. If Dhritarashtra's son gives them the half, he can live in
happiness, and be glad, with us. Once the heroes have their kingdom, and the
others behave rightly, they will surely be at peace and happy. And their peace
is good for the people.

"I would be glad if someone went there to make peace between the Kurus and the
Pandavas, to learn what Duryodhana thinks and to speak for Yudhishthira. Let him
greet Bhishma, the Kuru hero, and Vichitravirya's great son, and Drona with his
son, and Vidura and Kripa, and the king of Gandhara with the suta's son. Let him
greet all the other sons of Dhritarashtra too, those who are first in strength
and first in learning, who stand by their own duties, heroes of the world, old
in learning and in years. When they are all gathered, and the elders of the city
with them, let him speak words that bow low, words that will serve the son of
Kunti.

"Whatever happens, he must not be harsh with them. They took that wealth, and
they hold it by strength. Yudhishthira lost his kingdom at the dice, when he was
careless and too fond of the game. His friends, all the Kuru heroes, tried to
stop him. He did not know the game, and yet he challenged the son of the king of
Gandhara, who knew the dice, to play. There were thousands of other gamblers
there whom Yudhishthira could have beaten. He passed them all by and challenged
Subala's son himself, and at the dice he was beaten. He played against him, and
the dice went against him every time, and he grew heated and was beaten
outright. There is no fault in Shakuni in any of this.

"So let the envoy bow low and speak to Vichitravirya's son with every word of
peace. That is how a man can bring Dhritarashtra's son round to his purpose."

While the hero of the Madhus was still speaking, Satyaki, hero of the Shinis,
leapt to his feet. He spoke in anger, and scorned what Balarama had said.

---

"A man speaks as his heart is," said Satyaki. "You speak just as your heart is
made. There are brave men and there are cowards. Both kinds are fixed, and both
are found among men. A coward and a great chariot fighter can be born in the
same family, as one tree can have a branch with fruit and a branch without it.

"I do not blame you for what you say, you whose banner is the plough. I blame
the men who listen to it. How can anyone speak out in the middle of this
assembly, without fear, to lay even a little fault on the Dharma King?

"Men who were skilled at dice challenged a great-hearted man who did not know
the game, and who trusted them, and they beat him. Where is the dharma in that
victory? If they had come to the son of Kunti while he was playing at home with
his brothers, and beaten him there, it would have been a fair win. But they
challenged a king who always keeps the kshatriya's duty, and beat him by a
trick. What good is there in that?

"He has paid the stake in full. He is free of the forest exile, and he has come
into his grandfather's place. Why should he bow down here? Even if Yudhishthira
wanted other men's wealth, he still ought never to beg from his enemies. And how
can they claim to be righteous, and say they do not want to seize the kingdom,
when the sons of Kunti have finished their exile and they say, 'They were
recognised'? Bhishma and great Drona pleaded with them, and still they will not
make up their minds to give the Pandavas their father's wealth.

"I will plead with them too, with sharp arrows, in battle, by force. I will make
them fall at the feet of the great son of Kunti. If they will not make up their
minds to bow to the wise king, they will go with their ministers to the house of
Yama. When Yuyudhana is angry and eager to fight, they cannot stand against his
rush, any more than mountains can stand against the thunderbolt.

"Who can face the bearer of the Gandiva in battle, or the one whose weapon is
the discus, or me? Who can face Bhima, whom no one can come near? Who that wants
to live would come against the twins, with their strong bows, like Yama himself
in their brightness? Or against Dhrishtadyumna, son of Prishata? Or these five
sons of the Pandavas, who have added to Draupadi's fame, as tall as the Pandavas
and as strong, and proud in battle? Or against Subhadra's son, the great archer,
whom even the immortals could hardly withstand? Or against Gada, Pradyumna and
Samba, who are like Time, like the thunderbolt, like fire?

"We will kill Dhritarashtra's son in battle, with Shakuni and with Karna, and we
will consecrate the Pandava as king. There is no wrong in killing enemies who
come at you to kill. But begging from enemies is wrong, and it brings disgrace.
Do what the king wants in his heart, and do it without rest. Let Pandu's son
have back the kingdom Dhritarashtra gave up. Either Yudhishthira, son of Pandu,
gets his kingdom today, or they will all be killed in battle and lie asleep on
the ground."

---

"It will be as you say, strong-armed one," said Drupada. "I have no doubt of it.
Duryodhana will never give up the kingdom for sweet words. Dhritarashtra, who
dotes on his son, will follow him. So will Bhishma and Drona, out of weakness,
and Radha's son and Subala's son, out of folly.

"Baladeva's advice does not sit with my judgement, though a man who wants sound
policy should try that way first. But the son of Dhritarashtra must never be
spoken to softly. He cannot be won by gentleness. I think his mind is wicked. To
speak softly to Duryodhana, with his wicked heart, is like being gentle with a
donkey and harsh with cows. The wicked man thinks soft words are spoken out of
weakness. When he is treated gently, the fool thinks he has already won.

"So we will do this, and let the work start here. Let us send word to our
friends, and let them raise armies for us. Let swift envoys go to Shalya, to
Dhrishtaketu, to the lord Jayatsena, and to all the Kekayas. Duryodhana will
surely send to all of them. And good men who are asked first stand by the one
who asked them first. So hurry, and be first to ask the kings. A great task lies
ahead of us to carry. That is what I think.

"Send quickly to Shalya and the kings who follow him, and to King Bhagadatta,
who lives by the eastern ocean. Send to fierce Hardikya of boundless might, and
to Ahuka, to the wise king of the Mallas, and to Rochamana. Let Brihanta be
brought, and King Senabindu, Papajit, Prativindhya, Chitravarman and Suvastuka;
Bahlika and Munjakesha and the lord of the Chedis; Suparshva, Subahu, and the
great chariot fighter Paurava; the kings of the Shakas, the Pahlavas and the
Daradas; the Kambojas, the Rishikas, and the western Anupakas; Jayatsena, the
king of Kashi, and the kings of the Five Rivers; Kratha's son, who is hard to
beat, and the kings of the mountains; Janaki, Susharma, Maniman and
Pautimatsyaka; the lord of Pamsurashtra and brave Dhrishtaketu; Audra,
Dandadhara and brave Brihatsena; Aparajita, Nishada, Shreniman and Vasuman;
mighty Brihadbala, and Bahu, conqueror of cities; King Samudrasena with his
brave son; Adari, Nadija and King Karnaveshta; Samartha, Suvira, Marjara and
Kanyaka; Mahavira, Kadru, Nikara, Tumula and Kratha; Nila, Viradharman and the
brave Bhumipala; Durjaya, Dantavakra, Rukmin and Janamejaya; Ashadha, Vayuvega
and King Purvapalin; Bhuritejas, Devaka and Ekalavya's son; the kings of the
Karushas, and brave Kshemadhurti; Udbhava, Kshemaka and King Vatadhana;
Shrutayu, Dridhayu and Shalva's brave son; and the prince who rules the
Kalingas, fierce in battle. Send to them quickly. That is what I would do.

"And let this brahmin, my own priest, be sent quickly to Dhritarashtra, king.
Give him the message. Tell him what he should say to Duryodhana, what to the
king, Shantanu's son, what to Dhritarashtra, and what to Drona, the best of the
learned."

---

"What the leader of the Somakas says is fitting," said Krishna. "It will serve
the purpose of the Pandava king, whose power is great. We want sound policy, and
this is the first thing we must do. A man who acted otherwise would be a great
fool.

"But our tie to the Kurus and to the Pandavas is the same, however the Pandavas
and the others behave. We were all brought here for the wedding, and so were
you. Now the wedding is done, and we will go home glad.

"You are the eldest of the kings, in years and in learning. We are all like your
pupils here, no doubt of it. Dhritarashtra has always thought highly of you, and
you are a friend of the two teachers, Drona and Kripa. So send the message today
that will serve the Pandavas. Whatever you send, we are all agreed on it.

"If the bull of the Kurus makes peace on fair terms, then there will be no great
slaughter of Kurus and Pandavas, and they will live as brothers. But if
Dhritarashtra's son is proud and deluded and will not, then send to the others,
and after that call on us. Then, when the bearer of the Gandiva is angry, the
fool Duryodhana will come to his end, with his ministers and his kin."

King Virata honoured Krishna and sent him home with his people and his kin. When
Krishna had gone to Dvaraka, Yudhishthira and his brothers, and King Virata,
made every preparation for war.

Virata and his kin sent word to all the kings of the earth, and so did King
Drupada. At the word of the lions of the Kurus and of the kings of the Matsyas
and the Panchalas, the kings came, mighty and glad. When they heard of it, a
great army gathered for the sons of Pandu. And the son of Dhritarashtra, too,
brought kings together. The whole earth was crowded then with kings on the
march, for the sake of the Kurus and the Pandavas. The armies of those heroes
came from every side, and seemed to shake the goddess Earth, with her mountains
and her forests.

Then the Panchala king, with Yudhishthira's consent, sent his own priest to the
Kurus, a man old in wisdom and in years.

---

"Among all beings," Drupada told him, "living beings are the best. Among the
living, those who live by their minds. Among those with minds, men. Among men,
the twice-born. Among the twice-born, those learned in the Veda, and among the
learned, those whose minds are made up. I think you are first among those whose
minds are made up. You are high in family, in years and in learning. In wisdom
you are no less than Shukra, or Angiras' son Brihaspati.

"You know how that Kaurava has behaved, and how the Pandava Yudhishthira, the
son of Kunti, has behaved. The Pandavas were cheated by their enemies with
Dhritarashtra's knowledge. Vidura pleaded with him, and still he follows his
son. Shakuni knew the dice, and he challenged the son of Kunti on purpose. The
son of Kunti did not know the dice. He was honest and stood by the kshatriya's
way. Having cheated Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, like that, they will never
in any case give back the kingdom of their own accord.

"But when you speak words of dharma to Dhritarashtra, you will surely turn the
hearts of his fighting men. Vidura will back your words, and he will bring
division among Bhishma, Drona and Kripa. When the ministers are divided and the
fighters turn away, their work will be to bring them back to one mind. Meanwhile
the Parthas, of one mind and at ease, will build their army and gather stores.
While their own people are divided, and you stay on among them, they will
certainly not be able to build their army as well. This is the main purpose we
see in it. And Dhritarashtra, meeting you, may do the right thing you ask.

"You are a man of dharma. Deal rightly with them. Tell the kind-hearted among
them how the Pandavas have suffered. Tell the old men of the family's duty, as
the ancestors kept it. You will divide their hearts. I have no doubt of it. And
you need not fear them. You are a brahmin who knows the Veda, you have come on
an envoy's task, and above all you are old. So go quickly to the Kauravas, in
the conjunction of Pushya, at the hour called Victory, to win what the son of
Kunti needs."

So great Drupada instructed him, and the priest, a man of good conduct, set out
for the city named for the elephant.

**Divergences.**

* **The opening.** The vulgate opens the book with Janamejaya asking what the Pandavas did after the wedding (5.1.\*2, \*4, rejected). In the CE, Vaishampayana simply goes on. Other rejected opening lines (\*1, \*3, \*5, \*6) are invocations.
* **"That night."** Ganguli has the kings rest for a night. The CE has four dawns (5.1.1 *catvāry uṣasaḥ*).
* **Drupada and Balarama.** Ganguli has Drupada say that Balarama's words "commend themselves" to him. The CE reads *na yujyate* (5.4.3): they do not fit his judgement.
* **Satyaki's boasts.** Rejected lines (\*17) have Satyaki boast that he is Arjuna's pupil and could cut off the heads of Bhishma and the rest with his sword; another (\*19) adds the six kinds of "assailant" whom one may kill. Not narrated.
* **Drupada's boast.** Rejected lines (\*20, \*21) have Drupada say he could win alone. Not narrated.
* **The priest's pupils.** A rejected line (\*31) has the priest set out with his pupils. In the CE he simply goes.
* Other rejected lines (\*7–\*16, \*18, \*22–\*30) add or repeat details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.1.3 *vṛddhaś ca mānyaḥ pṛthivīpatīnāṃ; pitāmaho rāmajanārdanābhyām*: "the aged grandfather whom the kings honoured, with Balarama and Krishna". The verse does not name him. Ganguli reads "Balarama and Krishna along with their father". The novel keeps the text's words and does not guess.
* 5.1.4: Balarama (Rauhineya) is placed both beside the kings in 5.1.3 and beside Satyaki near Drupada in 5.1.4. Both are kept.
* 5.1.11 *varṣāṇi ṣaṭ sapta ca*: "six years and seven more", as the text counts the thirteen.
* 5.2.2: Balarama's "will give up half the kingdom and strive for the other half" follows the sense of *ardhaṃ hi rājyasya visṛjya … tasya kṛte yatante*.
* 5.2.8 *sarvāsv avasthāsu ca te na kauṭyād*: "whatever happens, he must not be harsh with them".
* 5.2.9 *ājamīḍhaḥ*: Yudhishthira, of Ajamidha's line.
* 5.4.3: Drupada's "though a man who wants sound policy should try that way first" reads *etad dhi puruṣeṇāgre kāryaṃ sunayam icchatā* as a concession.
* 5.4.5: the donkey and the cows is the text's own image of doing the wrong thing to the wrong creature.
* 5.4.12: *amitaujase tathogrāya hārdikyāya* is read as epithets of Hardikya (Kritavarma), and *dīrghaprajñāya mallāya* as "the wise king of the Mallas", as Ganguli reads them. They could also be read as separate names.
* 5.4.13–25: Drupada's list is kept in full, in order. *Kāśya* is "the king of Kashi", *pañcanadā nṛpāḥ* "the kings of the Five Rivers", *krāthaputraḥ* "Kratha's son". *Jānaki* here is a king's name, not Sita.
* 5.6.2: the ladder of "the best" is kept as the text gives it. The rejected line \*30 adds two more rungs.
* 5.6.17 *puṣyayogena muhūrtena jayena ca*: "in the conjunction of Pushya, at the hour called Victory".

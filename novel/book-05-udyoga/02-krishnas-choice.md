# Krishna's Choice

When Krishna and Baladeva Madhava had gone to Dvaravati, with all the Vrishnis
and Andhakas and the Bhojas in their hundreds, King Duryodhana, son of
Dhritarashtra, learned everything the Pandavas were doing through the envoys and
spies he had sent out. When he heard that Madhava had left, he set out for the
city of Dvaraka with fine horses as swift as the wind, and with a force that was
not very large. On that same day Dhananjaya, son of Kunti, the joy of Pandu,
also went quickly to the lovely city of the Anartas.

The two tigers among men, the two joys of the Kurus, reached Dvaraka and found
Krishna asleep. They went in to where he lay. Suyodhana entered first, while
Govinda was still sleeping, and sat down on a fine seat at Krishna's head. After
him came the great-minded wearer of the crown. He stood at Krishna's feet,
bowing, with his hands joined.

When the Vrishni woke, he saw the wearer of the crown first. Madhusudana
welcomed them both and honoured each of them as was fitting. Then he asked them
why they had come.

Duryodhana spoke to Krishna, half laughing. "In this war that is coming, you
should give me your help. Your friendship with me and with Arjuna is the same,
and our kinship with you is equal, Madhava. And today I came to you first,
Madhusudana. Good men take the side of the one who comes to them first,
following the old custom. Today you are the best of good men in the world,
Janardana, and always respected. Keep to the custom of the good."

"No doubt you came first, king," said Krishna. "But I saw the Partha Dhananjaya
first. Because you came first, and because I saw him first, I will help you
both, Suyodhana. But we are told that the younger should be given the first
choice. So the Partha Dhananjaya has the right to choose first.

"I have a great army, an arbuda, of cowherds as strong as I am, called the
Narayanas. They all fight in battle. Let those fighters, hard to withstand in
war, be the soldiers of one of you. On the other side I shall be alone, and I
shall not fight in the battle. I shall lay down my weapons. Partha, choose
whichever of the two your heart likes better. By dharma you have the first
choice."

When Krishna said this, Dhananjaya, son of Kunti, chose Keshava, who would not
fight in the battle. Duryodhana got the thousands upon thousands of fighting
men, Bharata. Knowing that Krishna had been taken by the other, he was delighted
all the same.

---

Then King Duryodhana, terribly strong, took that whole army and went to Rohini's
son, the mighty one. He told him all the reasons for his coming. Then Shauri
gave Dhritarashtra's son this answer.

"You must know everything I said long ago at Virata's wedding, tiger among men.
I held Hrishikesha back and spoke to him for your sake, joy of the Kurus. I told
him again and again, king, that our kinship with both sides was equal. Keshava
did not accept what I said. And I cannot stay apart from Krishna even for a
moment. Looking at Vasudeva, I have made up my mind. I shall help neither the
Parthas nor Duryodhana. You were born into the line of Bharata, honoured by all
the kings. Go, bull of the Bharatas, and fight by the dharma of a kshatriya."

When he was told this, Duryodhana embraced the one whose weapon is the plough.
Knowing that Krishna had been taken by the other, he still thought he had as
good as won the war.

Then King Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, went to Kritavarma, and Kritavarma
gave him an army, a full akshauhini. With that whole terrible army around him,
the joy of the Kurus went home, glad, and made his friends glad.

When Duryodhana had gone, Krishna spoke to the wearer of the crown. "Why did you
choose me, when I won't fight? What were you thinking?"

"You could kill them all, no doubt of it," said Arjuna, "and I too could kill
them on my own, best of men. But you are famous in the world, and that glory
will go to you. I want glory too, so I chose you. I have always had it in my
mind that you should be my charioteer. It is a wish I have held for many nights.
Please fulfil it."

"It is fitting that you should want to rival me, Partha," said Vasudeva. "I will
be your charioteer. Let your wish come true."

So the Partha, delighted, came back to Yudhishthira with Krishna, surrounded by
the best of the Dasharhas.

---

Shalya heard from the envoys, and set out for the Pandavas with a great army,
king, and with his sons, all great chariot fighters. His army's camp spread over
a yojana and a half, so great was the force that bull among men led. The leaders
of his army were bulls among kshatriyas, heroes with bright armour, bright
banners and bows, all with bright ornaments, bright chariots and horses.
Hundreds of thousands of heroes wore the dress and ornaments of their own
country. He marched slowly towards the Pandava, resting his army as he went,
seeming to trouble all creatures and to shake the earth.

When Duryodhana heard that the great chariot fighter was coming with his great
army, he hurried out to meet him and honoured him himself, Bharata. To honour
him, Duryodhana had halls built in pleasant places, bright with jewels and
finely decorated. Shalya came to those halls one after another. Duryodhana's
counsellors honoured him like a god in each place, as was fitting. Then he came
to another hall, as splendid as the dwelling of the gods. There he was given
pleasures fit for more than mortals. He thought himself greater than he was, and
looked down on Purandara.

Then the bull among kshatriyas asked the servants, in delight, "Which of
Yudhishthira's men built these halls? Let the hall builders be brought here. I
think they deserve a reward."

Duryodhana, who had been hidden, showed himself to the Pandavas' uncle. When the
Madra king saw him and understood what trouble he had taken, he embraced him and
said happily, "Take whatever you want."

"Be true to your word, good man," said Duryodhana. "Grant me a boon. You must be
the leader of my whole army."

"It is done," said Shalya. "What else is to be done?"

"It is done," said the son of Gandhari, again and again.

Then he took his leave of Shalya and went back to his own city. Shalya went on
to the sons of Kunti, to tell them what he had done.

---

Shalya came to Upaplavya, entered the camp and saw all the Pandavas there. The
strong-armed Shalya met the sons of Pandu and took the water for his feet, the
guest offering and the cow, as the rite lays down. Then the Madra king, slayer
of enemies, asked after their health and embraced Yudhishthira with the greatest
affection, and then the delighted Bhima and Arjuna, and the twins, his sister's
sons. When he had taken his seat, Shalya spoke to the Partha.

"Are you well, tiger among kings, joy of the Kurus? How lucky that you are free
of your life in the forest, best of victors. You have done something very hard,
king, living in the lonely forest with your brothers and with Krishna here. And
it was a hard thing to do, to live through the terrible year of hiding. A man
who has lost his kingdom has only sorrow. Where would he find happiness,
Bharata? You will find happiness, king, after this great sorrow that
Dhritarashtra's son has caused you, when you have killed your enemies. You know
the truth of the world, great king. Nothing you do comes from greed, my son."

Then the king told him of his meeting with Duryodhana, the service he had been
paid and the boon he had granted, Bharata.

"You did well, king," said Yudhishthira, "with your heart glad, to give
Duryodhana your word, hero. But I want one thing done. Bless you, lord of the
earth. Here, great king, you are Vasudeva's equal in battle. When Karna and
Arjuna come to fight chariot against chariot, best of kings, you will certainly
have to drive Karna's chariot. If you want to do what pleases me, king, you must
protect Arjuna there. You must break the suta's son's spirit, and bring victory
to us. Even though it is a thing that should not be done, uncle, you ought to do
it."

"Listen, Pandava, bless you," said Shalya. "You ask me to break the spirit of
the wicked suta's son in battle. I shall surely be his charioteer in the fight,
for he always thinks me Vasudeva's equal. When he is eager for battle, tiger of
the Kurus, I shall certainly speak to him in contrary and harmful words. Then
his pride and his spirit will be broken, Pandava, and he will be easy to kill. I
tell you this truly. I will do it as you say, my son. And anything else I can do
to please you, I will do.

"The sorrow you met at the dicing with Krishna, the harsh words of the suta's
son, the suffering from Jatasura and from Kichaka, all the misfortune Draupadi
has known, as Damayanti did, all this sorrow will end in happiness, hero. Do not
be angry about it. Fate is stronger. Great men meet with sorrows, Yudhishthira.
Even the gods have met with sorrows, lord of the earth. We are told that great
Indra, king of the gods, suffered great sorrow with his wife, Bharata."

**Divergences.**

* **Who was seen first.** Several rejected lines (\*33–\*37) have Krishna see Duryodhana sitting at his head as well, or afterwards. The CE says only that on waking he saw the wearer of the crown first (5.7.8). Ledger L-171.
* **Krishna's greeting.** A rejected passage (\*38–\*39) has Krishna say "Welcome, son of Gandhari, I did not notice you", and Duryodhana reply that he came first. Not narrated.
* **Krishna as Narayana.** A rejected line (\*41) calls the Krishna whom Arjuna chose "Narayana, unborn, born among men by his own will". Not narrated.
* **Shalya's welcome.** Rejected lines (\*46–\*51) describe the pleasures of the halls at length, Shalya's offer to give even his life, and the counsellors' praise of Duryodhana's service. Others (\*53–\*54) have Shalya say he will visit Yudhishthira first and come straight back. Not narrated. Ledger L-172.
* **Shalya's advice.** Rejected lines (\*59–\*60) add advice on the way of the ancient royal seers. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*32, \*40, \*42–\*45, \*52, \*55–\*58, \*61–\*67) add or repeat details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.7.6–7: Duryodhana "sat down on a fine seat at Krishna's head"; Arjuna "stood at Krishna's feet" (*paścārdhe*, the lower half of the bed), "bowing, with his hands joined".
* 5.7.15 *pravāraṇaṃ tu bālānāṃ pūrvaṃ kāryam iti śrutiḥ*: "the younger should be given the first choice". Arjuna is the younger of the two.
* 5.7.16 *arbudam*: a huge number, kept as the word.
* 5.7.20: the one who "was delighted all the same" is Duryodhana, who took the army.
* 5.7.23: Balarama refers to the council at Virata's court (ch 1).
* 5.7.26: Balarama's decision is his own. He does not say he will go on pilgrimage at this point.
* 5.8.11 *gūḍho duryodhanas tatra darśayām āsa mātulam*: Duryodhana, who had been hidden, showed himself to "the uncle". Shalya is the uncle of the twins (Madri's brother), not of Duryodhana, so the prose says "the Pandavas' uncle".
* 5.8.27 *tejovadhaḥ*: "break … spirit", literally "the killing of his fiery energy".
* 5.8.34 *jaṭāsurāt*: the rakshasa Jatasura, who carried off Draupadi and the brothers (3.154).

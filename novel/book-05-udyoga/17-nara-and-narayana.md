# Nara and Narayana

When all the kings had gathered, Bharata, Bhishma, Shantanu's son, said this to
Duryodhana.

"Brihaspati and Ushanas once waited on Brahma. With them were the Maruts with
Indra, the Vasus with the Ashvins, the Adityas and the Sadhyas, the seven seers
in the sky, the gandharva Vishvavasu, and the lovely hosts of apsarases. The
dwellers in heaven bowed to the old grandfather of the world and sat round the
lord of all. Then two seers of old, Nara and Narayana, first among the gods,
passed them by, taking to themselves the minds and the splendour of all those
dwellers in heaven.

"Brihaspati asked Brahma, 'Who are these two, who do not wait on you? Tell us,
grandfather.'

"'These two ascetics who light up earth and heaven,' said Brahma, 'blazing and
shining, who pervade all and go beyond it, of great strength, are Nara and
Narayana. They have gone from world to world. They are mighty in their own
austerity, great in spirit and in courage. These two, who stand firm, have
delighted the worlds by their deeds. The gods and gandharvas honour them, for
they are to bring about the end of the asuras.'

"When he heard that, Shakra went with all the hosts of gods, led by Brihaspati,
to where the two were practising austerity. At that time the gods had come into
great fear in the war of the gods and asuras, and Shakra asked the two great
ones for a boon. They said, 'Choose,' best of the Bharatas, and Shakra said to
them, 'Give us your help.' And they said to Shakra, 'We will do what you wish.'
And with the two of them Shakra conquered the daityas and danavas.

"Nara, scorcher of enemies, killed Indra's enemies in battle by hundreds and
thousands, the Paulomas and the Kalakhanjas. It was this Arjuna, standing in a
whirling chariot, who cut off the head of Jambha with a broad arrow in battle as
he was swallowing the sacrifice. It was he who broke down Hiranyapura beyond the
ocean and killed sixty thousand Nivatakavachas in battle. It was this
strong-armed Arjuna, conqueror of enemy cities, who beat the gods with Indra and
gave the fire its fill. And Narayana too killed many more there.

"So look at these two, of great courage, who have come together: the heroes
Vasudeva and Arjuna, great chariot fighters joined as one. They are the gods
Nara and Narayana, the ancient gods, as the tradition says, unconquerable in the
world of men even by gods and asuras with Indra. This Krishna is Narayana, and
Phalguna is known as Nara. Narayana and Nara are one being made two. By their
deeds these two win worlds that are eternal and never fade. They are born again
and again, here and there, when it is time for war. So work is what must be
done. That is what Narada said, who knows the Veda, and he told all this to the
Vrishnis.

"You will see Keshava with conch, discus and mace in his hands, and Arjuna of
the terrible bow taking up his weapons. You will see the two eternal great ones,
the two Krishnas, standing in one chariot. Then, Duryodhana, my son, you will
remember my words. If not, then this is the end of the Kurus that is coming.
Your mind has drifted from profit and from dharma, my son. If you will not take
my advice you will hear of very many killed. For all the Kurus follow your view.

"You alone, bull of the Bharatas, agree with the view of three: of Karna, who
was cursed by Rama, the low-born suta's son; of Shakuni, son of Subala; and of
your mean and wicked brother Duhshasana."

---

"You should not speak to me like that, grandfather," said Karna. "I stand by the
kshatriya's dharma and have not strayed from my own dharma. What other wrong is
in me, that you blame me? The sons of Dhritarashtra know of no wrong in me,
anywhere. I will do everything that pleases King Dhritarashtra, and Duryodhana
too, for he is set on the kingship."

When he heard Karna's words, Bhishma, Shantanu's son, spoke again, addressing
great King Dhritarashtra.

"This man is always boasting, 'I will kill the Pandavas.' He is not a sixteenth
part of the great Pandavas. Know that the ruin that is coming on your wicked
sons is the work of this evil-minded suta's son. Leaning on him, your foolish
son Suyodhana despised those heroes, sons of the gods, tamers of enemies. What
deed as hard to do has this man ever done, like the deeds each of the Pandavas
did in the past? In Virata's city he saw his dear brother killed by Dhananjaya's
courage. What did he do then? Dhananjaya attacked all the Kurus together,
crushed them and took the cattle. Was this man away then? When the gandharvas
carried off your son on the cattle expedition, where was the suta's son then,
who now bellows like a bull? Was it not the Partha, great Bhima and the twins
who came and beat the gandharvas there? These are many of his lies, bull of the
Bharatas, bless you, the lies of a boaster who always breaks dharma and profit."

When he heard Bhishma's words, the great-minded son of Bharadvaja spoke to
Dhritarashtra among the kings, honouring Bhishma.

"Do what Bhishma, best of the Bharatas, says, king. You should not do as those
who are greedy for gain would like. I think it is good to come to terms with the
Pandavas before there is war. The words Arjuna spoke, which Sanjaya has told us,
I know them all to be true, and the Pandava will do what he says. There is no
bowman like him in the three worlds."

But the king paid no heed to the wise words of Drona and Bhishma, and questioned
Sanjaya about the Pandavas. From that moment all the Kurus gave up hope of
living, when the king did not answer Bhishma and Drona as he should.

---

"What did the Pandava king, the son of Dharma, say," said Dhritarashtra, "when
he heard of these many armies gathered against him? What does Yudhishthira want,
in his eagerness to fight? Whose faces does he look to in his worries, among his
brothers and sons? Who holds him back, or tells him, 'Make peace,' or 'Fight,'
he who knows dharma and walks in dharma, and was angered by the fools with their
trickery?"

"The Panchalas and the Pandavas look to the king's face," said Sanjaya. "Bless
you, Yudhishthira commands them all. The hosts of chariots of the Pandavas and
the Panchalas, each on its own, greet Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, when he comes.
The Panchalas greet the blazing son of Kunti as darkness greets the rising sun,
like a heap of light lifted up. From the cowherds and shepherds upwards, the
Panchalas, the Kekayas and the Matsyas greet Yudhishthira the Pandava, who
greets them gladly. Brahmin women, princesses and the daughters of the vaishyas
come together at play to see the Partha in his armour."

"Sanjaya, tell me," said Dhritarashtra, "with whom have the Pandavas challenged
us? The Somakas, with Dhrishtadyumna as their general, what strength do they
have?"

When he was asked this in the hall, in the assembly of the Kurus, the son of
Gavalgana sighed long and deeply, again and again, as if thinking. Then, for no
reason that could be seen, by fate, a faint came over the suta. A man in the
hall, in the assembly of kings, said, "Great king, Sanjaya has fainted and
fallen to the ground. He says nothing. His wits are gone and he hardly knows
himself."

"Sanjaya must have seen the sons of Kunti, the great chariot fighters," said
Dhritarashtra. "His mind has been badly shaken by those tigers among men."

Sanjaya came to himself, and when he had recovered he spoke to Dhritarashtra in
the hall, in the assembly of the Kurus.

"I have seen the sons of Kunti, king of kings, the great chariot fighters, worn
thin by their confinement in the house of the king of the Matsyas. Hear, great
king, with whom the Pandavas have challenged you.

"There is the man who would never give up the truth, not from anger, fear or
desire, not for gain, not for any argument. He is the soul of dharma, the
measure of dharma, best of the upholders of dharma, great king. With that
Ajatashatru the Pandavas have challenged you.

"There is the man whose equal in strength of arm there is none on earth, the
bowman who brought all the kings under his power. The son of Kunti, Vrikodara,
was their island when they escaped from the house of lac and from Hidimba the
man-eater. He was their island when the king of Sindhu carried off Yajnaseni. He
saved all the Pandavas together when they were burning at Varanavata. Seeking
Krishnaa's pleasure, he went into the terrible, rough mountain Gandhamadana and
killed the Krodhavashas. The strength of ten thousand elephants has been put
into his arms. With that Bhimasena the Pandavas have challenged you.

"There is the hero who, with Krishna beside him, attacked and beat Purandara in
battle to please the fire. He pleased the great god himself in battle, Girisha,
trident in hand, god of gods, husband of Uma. The bowman brought all the
guardians of the world under his power. With that Vijaya the Pandavas have
challenged you in battle.

"There is Nakula the fighter, the brilliant fighter, who brought under his power
the western quarter, full of hosts of mlecchas. He stands ready there. With that
handsome hero, the great bowman, the son of Madri, the Pandavas have challenged
you, Kauravya.

"There is Sahadeva, who conquered in battle the Kashis, the Angas, the Magadhas
and the Kalingas. With him the Pandavas have challenged you. There are four men
on earth equal to him in courage: Ashvatthama, Dhrishtaketu, Pradyumna and
Rukmin. With that Sahadeva, the youngest, the hero among men who delights Madri,
the Pandavas have challenged you.

"Once the daughter of the king of Kashi practised terrible austerity, wanting
Bhishma's death even after she died, bull of the Bharatas. She was born the
daughter of the Panchala king, and by fate became a man again. He knows the
virtues and faults of both woman and man, tiger among men. The Panchala, wild in
battle, fell upon the Kalingas. With Shikhandi, skilled in weapons, the Pandavas
have challenged you, Kurus. A yaksha made her a man, they say, for Bhishma's
death. With that fierce great archer the Pandavas have challenged you.

"The five Kekaya brothers, princes and great archers, heroes in bright armour:
with them the Pandavas have challenged you. The long-armed one, quick with his
weapons, steady and true in courage, the Vrishni hero Yuyudhana: with him is
your war. The one who was the refuge of the great Pandavas in their time of
need: with Virata the Pandavas have challenged you in battle. The king of Kashi,
the great chariot fighter of Varanasi, has become their fighter: with him the
Pandavas have challenged you.

"The great sons of Draupadi, boys hard to beat in battle, whose touch is like a
snake's venom: with them the Pandavas have challenged you. The one who is like
Krishna in courage and like Yudhishthira in self-control: with Abhimanyu the
Pandavas have challenged you in battle. There is the famous Dhrishtaketu, whose
courage has no match, fierce and unbearable in battle when he is angry, the son
of Shishupala, the great chariot fighter. With that king of the Chedis the
Pandavas have challenged you.

"The one who is the Pandavas' refuge, as Vasava is the gods': with Vasudeva the
Pandavas have challenged you. Sharabha, the brother of the king of the Chedis,
bull of the Bharatas, with Karakarsha: with those two they have challenged you.
Jarasandha's son Sahadeva, and Jayatsena, both of them, and mighty Drupada, with
a great army around him, ready to give his life for the Pandavas, stand ready to
fight. These, and many other kings of the east and the north, by the hundred:
relying on them the Dharma King stands ready."

**Divergences.**

* **Take refuge in them.** A rejected line (\*318) has Bhishma tell Duryodhana to take refuge with Krishna and Arjuna and enjoy his fortune. Not narrated.
* **Karna's boast.** Rejected lines (\*319, \*320) add Karna's promise to kill all the Pandavas in battle. In the CE he protests his loyalty (5.48.29–31). Bhishma quotes his boast (5.48.33).
* **Dhrishtadyumna.** Rejected lines (\*324, \*325) add Dhrishtadyumna, born to destroy Drona, to Sanjaya's list. The CE's list does not name him, though Dhritarashtra asks about him (5.49.9).

# Nara and Narayana

When all the kings had gathered, Bharata, Bhishma, son of Shantanu, spoke to
Duryodhana.

"Brihaspati and Ushanas once waited on Brahma. So did the Maruts with Indra, the
Vasus with the Ashvins, the Adityas and Sadhyas, the seven seers in the sky, the
gandharva Vishvavasu and the lovely hosts of apsarases. They bowed to the
grandfather, the eldest of the worlds, and the dwellers in heaven sat down
around the lord of all. Then two seers, gods of old, Nara and Narayana, passed
by, taking away the minds and the splendour of the dwellers in heaven.
Brihaspati asked Brahma, 'Who are these two, who do not wait on you? Tell us,
grandfather.'

"'These two ascetics,' said Brahma, 'who light up earth and heaven, blazing and
shining, who have spread through all things and passed beyond, so mighty, are
Nara and Narayana. They have gone from world to world. They are strong with
their own austerity, great in being and in courage. These two, who never change,
have gladdened the worlds by their deeds. The gods and gandharvas honour them,
for the destruction of the asuras.'

"When he heard this, Shakra went with all the hosts of gods, led by Brihaspati,
to the place where those two were practising austerity. At that time a terrible
fear had come on the dwellers in heaven, in a war of the gods and asuras. Indra
asked the two great ones, Nara and Narayana, for a boon. 'Choose it,' they said,
best of the Bharatas. Then Shakra said to them, 'Give us your help.' They said
to Shakra, 'We will do what you want.' And with them Shakra conquered the
daityas and danavas.

"In Indra's battle Nara, scorcher of enemies, killed hundreds and thousands of
the Paulomas and the Kalakhanjas. This Arjuna, standing in his whirling chariot,
cut off with a broad-headed arrow the head of Jambha as he was swallowing the
sacrifice in battle. He broke Hiranyapura on the far shore of the ocean, and
killed sixty thousand Nivatakavachas in battle. Strong-armed Arjuna, conqueror
of enemy cities, beat the gods with Indra and fed the god of fire. And Narayana,
in the same way, killed many more there.

"See these two of great courage, who have come together, the heroes Vasudeva and
Arjuna, the great chariot fighters joined as one. They are the gods Nara and
Narayana, the gods of old, so we are told. In the world of men they cannot be
beaten, even by gods and asuras with Indra. This Krishna is Narayana, and
Phalguna is said to be Nara. Narayana and Nara are one being made two. By their
deeds these two win worlds that do not end and do not change. They are born
again and again, here and there, whenever there is a time of war. So their work
must be done. So Narada said, who knows the Veda, and he told all this to the
Vrishnis.

"When you see Keshava with conch, discus and mace in his hand, and Arjuna of the
terrible bow taking up his weapons, the two eternal great ones, the two
Krishnas, standing in one chariot, then, Duryodhana, my son, you will remember
my words. If not, this destruction has come upon the Kurus. Your mind, my son,
has drifted away from purpose and from dharma. If you will not take my words,
you will hear of very many killed. All the Kurus follow your opinion. You alone
accept as truth the opinion of just three, bull of the Bharatas. One is Karna,
cursed by Rama, the low-born son of a suta. One is Shakuni, son of Subala. And
one is your mean and wicked brother Duhshasana."

"You are old, grandfather," said Karna, "and you should not speak to me as you
have. I stand by the dharma of a kshatriya. I have not strayed from my own
dharma. What else is there in me so bad that you should blame me? The sons of
Dhritarashtra have never known any wrong in me. I do everything that pleases
King Dhritarashtra, and Duryodhana too, for he is set on the kingdom."

When he heard Karna's words, Bhishma, son of Shantanu, turned to the great king
Dhritarashtra.

"This man always boasts, 'I will kill the Pandavas.' He is not worth a sixteenth
part of the great Pandavas. Know that the bad policy that is coming on your
wicked sons is the work of this evil-minded son of a suta. Leaning on him, your
foolish son Suyodhana has despised those heroes, the sons of gods, tamers of
enemies. What deed has this man ever done, so hard to do, like the deeds each of
the Pandavas did in the past? When he saw his dear brother killed by
Dhananjaya's courage in Virata's city, what did he do then? Dhananjaya attacked
all the Kurus together, crushed them and took the cattle. Was this man away from
home then? When your son was being carried off by the gandharvas on the cattle
expedition, where was the suta's son then, who bellows like a bull now? Was it
not the Partha who came there with great Bhima and the twins, and beat the
gandharvas? Those are the lies of this boaster, bull of the Bharatas, bless you,
and there are many more. He always destroys dharma and purpose."

When he heard Bhishma's words, great-minded Drona, son of Bharadvaja, honoured
him and spoke to Dhritarashtra in the middle of the kings. "Do what Bhishma, the
best of the Bharatas, says, king. You should not do as those greedy for gain
would like. I think it good to make peace with the Pandavas before war. I know
the truth of everything Arjuna said, as Sanjaya reported it, and the Pandava
will do it. There is no bowman like him in the three worlds."

But the king ignored those words of Drona and Bhishma, full of sense as they
were. He went on to ask Sanjaya about the Pandavas. From that moment all the
Kurus gave up hope of living, when the king did not answer Bhishma and Drona
properly.

---

"What did the Pandava king, the son of Dharma, say," said Dhritarashtra, "when
he heard that these many armies had gathered against him? What does Yudhishthira
want, ready to fight, in his anger? Whose face among his brothers and sons does
he look to in his worries? Who holds him back, or says, 'Make peace,' or else,
'Fight'? He knows dharma and lives by it, and fools have angered him by their
tricks."

"The Panchalas and the Pandavas look to King Yudhishthira's face, bless you,"
said Sanjaya, "and he commands them all. The hosts of chariots of the Pandavas
and the Panchalas, each on its own, greet Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, when he
comes. The Panchalas welcome the son of Kunti, blazing with energy, as the
darkness welcomes the rising sun, a heap of energy coming up. Down to the
cowherds and the shepherds, the Panchalas, Kekayas and Matsyas welcome
Yudhishthira the Pandava, and he is glad. Brahmin women, princesses and the
daughters of the vaishyas come out in play to see the Partha in his armour."

"Sanjaya, tell me," said Dhritarashtra, "with whom the Pandavas have come
against us. Is it with Dhrishtadyumna as their general, and the Somakas, whose
strength is what?"

Questioned like this in the hall, in the assembly of the Kurus, the son of
Gavalgana sighed long and deep again and again, as if lost in thought. Then for
no reason, by the will of fate, the suta fainted.

Then a man in the hall announced to the assembly of kings, "Great king, this
Sanjaya has fainted and fallen on the ground. He cannot speak a word. His mind
is gone, and he has lost his senses."

"Sanjaya must have seen the sons of Kunti, those great chariot fighters," said
Dhritarashtra. "Those tigers among men have shaken his mind badly."

When Sanjaya came to and had recovered, he spoke to Dhritarashtra, great king,
in the hall, in the assembly of the Kurus.

"I have seen the sons of Kunti, great chariot fighters, king of kings, worn thin
from living shut up in the house of the Matsya king. Hear, great king, with whom
the Pandavas have come against you.

"The Pandavas have come against you with Ajatashatru. His soul is dharma. He
would never give up the truth, not for anger, fear or desire, nor for wealth,
nor for any argument. He is the measure of dharma, great king, the best of those
who keep it.

"The Pandavas have come against you with Bhimasena. There is no one on earth
equal to him in the strength of his arms, and that bowman brought all the kings
under his power. When they escaped from the house of lac, and from Hidimba the
man-eater, Vrikodara, son of Kunti, was their island. Where the king of Sindhu
dragged away Yajnaseni, Vrikodara, son of Kunti, was their island. He saved all
the Pandavas together when they were burning at Varanavata. They have come
against you with him. To please Krishna, he went into the rough and terrible
mountain Gandhamadana and killed the Krodhavashas. The strength of ten thousand
elephants is in his arms. The Pandavas have come against you with Bhimasena.

"With Krishna as his second, the hero once fought and beat Purandara, to please
the god of fire. He pleased the great god himself in battle, the mountain lord
with the trident in his hand, the god of gods, Uma's husband. That bowman
brought all the guardians of the world under his power. The Pandavas have come
against you with Vijaya.

"Nakula is there, the warrior, the wonderful fighter, who brought under control
the western quarter, crowded with hosts of mlecchas. The Pandavas have come
against you with that handsome hero, the great bowman, Madri's son, Kaurava.
Sahadeva conquered the Kashis, the Angas, the Magadhas and the Kalingas in
battle. The Pandavas have come against you with him. Only four men on earth are
his equal in courage: Ashvatthama, Dhrishtaketu, Pradyumna and Rukmin. The
Pandavas have come against you with that Sahadeva, the youngest, the hero among
men, who brings joy to Madri.

"There was a princess of Kashi who once practised terrible austerity, wanting
Bhishma's death even after her own, bull of the Bharatas. She was born the
daughter of the Panchala king, and by fate she became a man again. He knows the
virtues and faults of both woman and man, tiger among men. The Panchala, mad for
battle, who went against the Kalingas, Shikhandi, skilled in weapons, is the one
with whom they have come against you, Kurus. The Pandavas have come against you
with that terrible great archer whom a yaksha made a man, they say, for
Bhishma's death.

"The great archers, the princes, the five Kekaya brothers, heroes in bright
armour, are with them against you. You will have your battle with Yuyudhana, the
Vrishni hero, long-armed, quick with weapons, steady and truly brave. The
Pandavas have come against you in battle with Virata, who was the refuge of the
great Pandavas in their time of need. The king of Kashi, the great chariot
fighter of Varanasi, has become their fighter. They have come against you with
him. The Pandavas have come against you in battle with the great sons of
Draupadi, children hard to beat in battle, whose touch is like a poisonous
snake's. They have come against you in battle with Abhimanyu, who is like
Krishna in courage and like Yudhishthira in self-control.

"Dhrishtaketu, so famous, matchless in courage, is unbearable in battle when he
is angry, the great chariot fighter, Shishupala's son. The Pandavas have come
against you with that king of the Chedis. Vasudeva is the Pandavas' refuge, as
Vasava is the refuge of the gods. The Pandavas have come against you with
Vasudeva. Sharabha, brother of the king of the Chedis, bull of the Bharatas, is
there with Karakarsha. They have come against you with those two. Sahadeva, son
of Jarasandha, and Jayatsena are both there. Drupada of great energy, with a
great army round him, has given up his life for the Pandavas and stands ready to
fight. These and many other kings of the east and north, in hundreds, are the
ones the Dharma King relies on as he stands ready."

**Divergences.**

* **Karna and Rama's curse.** Bhishma speaks of Karna as "cursed by Rama" (*rāmeṇa caiva śaptasya*, 5.48.27). The story of the curse is told later, after the war (12.3). Ledger L-53.
* **Shikhandi.** Sanjaya's summary (5.49.31–34) gives Shikhandi's story in brief: the princess of Kashi reborn as Drupada's daughter, made a man by a yaksha. The full story is Bhishma's, in 5.170–197.
* Rejected lines (\*317–\*328) add detail. Not narrated.

# Sanjaya at Upaplavya

"They say the sons of Pandu have arrived, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra. "Go and
find them at Upaplavya. Greet Ajatashatru. Say to him, 'How lucky that you have
come to a village, faultless one.' Tell them all, Sanjaya, 'I hope you are well,
after living through a hard time you did not deserve.' They will soon be at
peace with us. They are good men, who have helped us even after being deceived.

"I have never seen any false conduct in the Pandavas, Sanjaya. They won all
their fortune by their own courage, and they brought it all to me. I have
searched, and I cannot find a single fault in them, ever, to blame the Parthas
for. They always act by dharma and by sound purpose. They do not love comfort,
and they do not give in to their desires. Heat and cold, hunger and thirst,
sleep and sloth, anger and delight and carelessness, the Parthas master them all
with steadiness and wisdom, and strive for what dharma and purpose require. When
the time comes, they give their wealth away to their friends. Their friendship
does not wear out with living together. The Parthas give honour and wealth to
each as he deserves. There is no one on Ajamidha's side who hates them, except
evil, crooked, slow-witted Duryodhana and meaner Karna. These two add to the
fire of those great men, who have given up comfort and pleasure.

"Duryodhana is energetic and brave. He grew up in comfort, and he thinks all
this well done. Only a child would think he can take away the Pandavas' share
while they live. Yudhishthira has Arjuna and Keshava in his train, and
Vrikodara, Satyaki, the sons of Madri and all the Srinjayas. It would be well to
give him his share before there is a war.

"The Left-handed Archer alone, the Gandiva bowman, standing in his chariot,
could sweep the earth. So could Vishnu, Keshava, whom none can withstand, the
great one, lord of the three worlds. What mortal could stand before him, the one
to be chosen and praised among all the gods, as he scatters floods of arrows
with the roar of the rain clouds, as fast as swarms of locusts? With the Gandiva
in his hand, alone in his chariot, the Left-handed Archer conquered the northern
quarter and the Northern Kurus. He took away their wealth and made them follow
his army and pay him tribute. At Khandava, the Gandiva bowman conquered the gods
with Indra, and Phalguna gave the forest to the god of fire, adding to the fame
and honour of the Pandavas.

"Today there is no one equal to Bhima among those who fight with the mace, and
no one equal to him riding an elephant. They say he is no less than Arjuna in a
chariot, and in the strength of his arms he has the strength of ten thousand
elephants. He is well trained, he has a grudge, and he is quick. In his anger he
would quickly burn up Dhritarashtra's sons. He is always quick to anger and he
is mighty. Not even Vasava himself could beat him in battle.

"The two sons of Madri are clever and strong and quick of hand, well trained by
Phalguna. Like two hawks tearing through flocks of birds, they would leave no
Kuru standing here. Among them is quick Dhrishtadyumna, counted as one of the
Pandavas, the chief of the Somakas, with his counsellors, ready to give his life
for the Pandavas' victory. I have heard that Virata, lord of the Shalveyas, who
has lived with them and had his wish fulfilled, who is in the prime of life, is
devoted to Yudhishthira, with his sons, and always on the Pandavas' side.

"There are five mighty brothers, great archers, who were kept out of their
kingdom by the Kekayas. They want the kingdom back from the Kekayas, and they
follow the Parthas, eager for war. All the heroes among the kings of the earth
who were brought together for the Pandavas are camped there. I hear they are
brave and devoted, bound by love to the Dharma King. Fighters who live in the
mountains and in strongholds, men of good family and pure, and mlecchas strong
with all kinds of weapons have gathered and camped there for the Pandavas. The
Pandya king, like Indra, has come for the Pandavas with many heroes of battle, a
great man, a hero of the world, of matchless strength and energy.

"I hear Satyaki learned weapons from Drona and Arjuna, from Vasudeva, Kripa and
Bhishma. They say he alone is the equal of Krishna's son. He is camped there for
the Pandavas. The Chedis and the Karushas have joined them, with kings ready to
give all they have. Among them, like the blazing sun, shone the king of the
Chedis in his glory. He thought himself unshakeable in battle, the best of all
bowmen on earth. Krishna struck down all the eagerness of the kshatriyas and
crushed him by force, at speed. To add to the fame and honour of the Yadavas, he
cut down Shishupala in the battle long ago, though all the kings, with the king
of the Karushas first, had added to his pride. Seeing that Keshava could not be
withstood, in his chariot yoked with Sugriva, they all left the king of the
Chedis and fled, like small animals at the sight of a lion. The one who rose
against him in his pride, hoping to meet Vasudeva in single combat, lay there
lifeless, killed by Krishna, like a karnikara tree uprooted by the wind.

"When I remember the courage of Keshava that they told me of, Sanjaya, and the
deeds of that Vishnu for their sake, I find no peace, son of Gavalgana. No enemy
could stand against those who have that lion of the Vrishnis at their head. My
heart trembles with fear when I hear that the two Krishnas are together in one
chariot. If my slow-witted son, whose mind is turned the wrong way, did not go
to war against those two, then all would be well. Otherwise those two would burn
up the Kurus, Sanjaya, as Indra and Vishnu burned the army of the daityas. I
think Dhananjaya is Shakra's equal, and the Vrishni hero is eternal Vishnu.

"Yudhishthira, son of Kunti and Pandu, delights in dharma and holds back for
shame. He is strong, he is proud, and he was wronged by Duryodhana. If he were
not what he is, in his anger he would burn up Dhritarashtra's sons. I am not as
afraid of Arjuna or Vasudeva, of Bhima or the twins, as I am always afraid of
the anger of the king when his wrath is ablaze, suta. He has austerity and the
celibate life to the full. Whatever he resolves in his mind would come true.
When I think of his anger in battle, Sanjaya, and know that it would be just, I
am terribly afraid today.

"So go quickly. I am sending you by chariot. Go to the camp of the Panchala
king. Ask Ajatashatru after his health, and speak to him warmly again and again.
And when you meet Janardana, my son, the greatest of the mighty, noble and
generous, ask after his health from me, and say that Dhritarashtra wants peace
with the Pandavas. There is nothing Vasudeva could say that the son of Kunti
would not do, suta. Krishna is dear to them, like their own selves. He is wise,
and always busy in their affairs. Gather the Pandavas and the Srinjayas,
Janardana, Yuyudhana and Virata, and ask after their health from me, and all the
five sons of Draupadi too. Whatever you think fitting at the time, and good for
the Bharatas, say it in the middle of the kings, Sanjaya, whatever will not
inflame them, and will not lead to war."

---

When Sanjaya heard King Dhritarashtra's words, he went to Upaplavya to see the
Pandavas, whose energy is boundless. He came to King Yudhishthira, whose soul is
dharma, and first bowed down to him. Then the suta's son, Sanjaya, son of
Gavalgana, spoke to Ajatashatru, glad at heart.

"How lucky that I see you well, king, with allies, like great Indra. The old
king, wise Dhritarashtra, son of Ambika, asks after your health. I hope Bhima,
first of the Pandavas, is well, and Dhananjaya, and the two sons of Madri. I
hope Krishna Draupadi, the princess, true to her vows, wife of heroes, is well
with her sons, that proud woman. I hope that wherever you wish for the things
you love, Bharata, you wish for them in peace."

"Welcome, Sanjaya, son of Gavalgana," said Yudhishthira. "I greet you with a
glad heart, suta. I tell you I am well, learned man, and so are my younger
brothers. After a long time, suta, I hear news of the health of the Bharata, the
old king of the Kurus. Seeing you, Sanjaya, out of my affection for him, I feel
I have seen the king himself.

"Is our grandfather well, my son, old Bhishma, the Kaurava, so proud, so wise,
so full of every kind of dharma? Is he still as he used to be? I hope great King
Dhritarashtra, son of Vichitravirya, is well with his sons. Is great King
Bahlika, son of Pratipa, the learned one, well, suta's son? Is Somadatta well,
my son, and Bhurishravas, true to his promise, and Shala? Are Drona and his son,
and Kripa the brahmin, well, those great archers? Those very wise men, pure in
every teaching, the chief bowmen on earth, are they given honour, my son? Are
those bowmen well? All the young bowmen on earth envy the Kurus, Sanjaya, in
whose kingdom lives Drona's son, so handsome, the great archer, so well behaved.

"Is the son of the vaishya woman well, my son, the very wise prince Yuyutsu? Is
the counsellor Karna well, my son, whom slow-witted Suyodhana obeys? Are the old
women of the Bharatas, the mothers, the cooks, the slave women, the
daughters-in-law, the sons, the nephews, the sisters and the daughters' sons all
free from harm, suta?

"Does the king keep up the brahmins' livelihood as he used to, my son? I hope
Dhritarashtra's son is not taking away my gifts to the twice-born, Sanjaya. I
hope King Dhritarashtra and his sons do not ignore wrongs done to brahmins. I
hope he does not stand by while their living is cut short. This is the brightest
light in the world of the living, made for creatures by the Creator. If the
slow-witted Kurus do not hold back their greed, the Kauravas will be destroyed
entirely.

"Do King Dhritarashtra and his sons try to provide for their ministers? I hope
there are no enemies posing as friends, who share a single ally, and want to
live by dividing them. I hope none of the Kurus speak ill of the Pandavas, my
son. When they see gangs of robbers gathered, do they remember the Partha, the
leader of battles? Do they remember the straight arrows, my son, sent from the
string and the tip of his arm, as the bowman shakes his bow, loosed from the
Gandiva, roaring like thunder? I have not seen or heard of anyone on earth equal
to Arjuna or better, who has sixty-one sharp, keen-edged, well-feathered arrows,
and whose arm guard is famous.

"Do they remember Bhimasena, quick and strong, with his mace in his hand,
shaking the hosts of his enemies in battle, striding about like an elephant in
rut among reeds? Do they remember mighty Sahadeva, son of Madri, who conquered
the gathered Kalingas at Dantakura, shooting with his left hand and his right?
Do they remember this Nakula, son of Madri, sent out before your eyes, Sanjaya,
son of Gavalgana, who brought the western quarter into my power?

"Do they remember the defeat at Dvaitavana, when they came on that badly planned
cattle expedition, and the fools fell into their enemies' hands, and Bhimasena
and Jaya set them free? I guarded Arjuna from behind. The sons of Madri and
Bhimasena guarded his wheels. The Gandiva bowman scattered the hosts of his
enemies and came back safe. Do they remember him?

"It is certain, Sanjaya, that one good deed is not enough to win here, if we
cannot win over Dhritarashtra's son with our whole selves."

---

"It is as you deserve, Pandava," said Sanjaya. "You ask after the Kurus and the
people, best of the Kurus. The best of the Kurus you ask about are well, Partha,
and in good heart. Know that there are old, good men around Dhritarashtra's son,
Pandava, and there are evil men around him too. Dhritarashtra's son would give
even to an enemy. How would he take away the brahmins' gifts?

"The way he treats you is not dharmic. To treat those who have done no harm as
if they had done harm is not good. Dhritarashtra and his sons would be betrayers
of friends, wicked men hating you who behave well. But he does not approve of
it. The old man grieves bitterly and mourns inside, Ajatashatru. When he meets
with brahmins, he hears that betraying a friend is worse than the deadly sins.

"In council they remember you, god among men, and the Victorious One, the leader
of battles. When the drums and conches sound loud, they remember Bhimasena, mace
in hand. They remember the two sons of Madri too, rushing in every direction in
the thick of battle, raining arrows on the army without end, great chariot
fighters no one can shake in battle.

"But I do not think a man can know what will come, king. You, Pandava, so full
of every dharma, have met with hard suffering. You, Ajatashatru, could set all
this right again with your wisdom. None of the sons of Pandu, all like Indra,
would give up dharma for the sake of pleasure or gain. You alone, Ajatashatru,
could make a peace with your wisdom by which all would find peace: the sons of
Dhritarashtra, the Pandavas, the Srinjayas, and the other kings who are camped
here.

"Ajatashatru, king, hear the words your father Dhritarashtra spoke to me in the
night, with his counsellors and his sons, when we had met together."

**Divergences.** Rejected lines (\*125–\*132) add detail and epithets. Not narrated. Ganguli's text has Arjuna make "the people of the Dravida land" part of his army (G 5.22). The CE says only that he made the Northern Kurus follow his army and pay tribute (5.22.12).

**Choices.**

* 5.22.1 *diṣṭyānagha grāmam upasthitas tvam*: "How lucky that you have come to a village". Upaplavya is a town of Virata's.
* 5.22.7: Duryodhana and Karna "add to the fire" (*saṃjanayanti tejaḥ*) of the Pandavas.
* 5.22.18 *śālveyānām adhipaḥ*: Virata is called "lord of the Shalveyas". *vayaḥsthaḥ*: "in the prime of life".
* 5.22.23 *kārṣṇipratimam*: "the equal of Krishna's son", Pradyumna.
* 5.22.27 *sugrīvayuktena rathena*: Krishna's chariot "yoked with Sugriva", one of his horses.
* 5.22.31: "the two Krishnas", Krishna and Arjuna (whose name Krishna is also).
* 5.23.16: read as "I hope he does not stand by while their living is cut short".
* 5.23.18 *suhṛdrūpā durhṛdaś caikamitrāḥ*: "enemies posing as friends, who share a single ally". The sense is uncertain.
* 5.23.21 *hastavāpaḥ*: Arjuna's "arm guard". Ganguli reads the sixty-one arrows as shot "by a single effort of his hand"; the CE verse says only that he has them.
* 5.23.25–26: the cattle expedition at Dvaitavana and Arjuna's rescue of the Kauravas from the gandharvas (Book 3). "Jaya" is Arjuna.
* 5.24.1–3: Sanjaya's reply is read as admitting that the treatment of the Pandavas is wrong, and placing the blame away from the old king.

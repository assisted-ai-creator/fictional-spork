# The Answer to Uluka

"When the bull among the Bharatas heard Duryodhana's words," said Sanjaya, "he
looked at the gambler's son with very red eyes. Famous Gudakesha looked at
Keshava, raised his great arm, and spoke to the gambler's son.

"'The man who relies on his own strength and challenges others, and fearlessly
makes good his power, is called a man. The man who relies on others' strength
and challenges others is a kshatriya in name only, because he cannot do it
himself. He is the lowest of men in the world. You think others' strength is
your own. You are a coward and a fool yourself, and you want to insult others.
You boast after consecrating the eldest of all the kings, whose mind is set on
good, who has mastered his senses and is very wise, to die. We know what you are
thinking, you fool, you disgrace to your family: "The Pandavas will not kill the
son of Ganga, out of pity." The man on whose strength you rely when you boast,
son of Dhritarashtra, is the one I shall kill first, Bhishma, while all the
bowmen watch.

"'Son of the gambler, go back to the Bharatas and tell Suyodhana, son of
Dhritarashtra, "Arjuna Savyasachin says: So be it. When the night is over there
will be a battle." The one whose spirit never droops, the one true to his word,
said among the Kurus to cheer you, "I shall kill the army of the Pandavas and
the Shalveyakas. This is my burden. I could kill the world, except Drona. You
have nothing to fear from the Pandavas." So you think the kingdom is surely
yours and the Pandavas are finished. You are full of pride and you do not see
the harm that is in you. So I shall kill the eldest of the Kurus first, in front
of you. At sunrise, with your army drawn up, with your banner and your chariot,
wait, and guard the one who is true to his word. For I shall throw Bhishma, this
island of yours, down from his chariot with my arrows while you all look on.
Tomorrow Suyodhana will learn what his boasting was worth, when he sees his
grandfather struck down by my net of arrows.

"'And what angry Bhimasena said in the hall to your brother Duhshasana, the
short-sighted man, who does not know dharma, who is always hostile, evil-minded
and cruel, you will soon see come true, Suyodhana. You will soon see the bitter
fruit of your pride and your arrogance, Suyodhana, of your anger and your
harshness, your hardness, your conceit and your self-regard. You will see the
fruit of your cruelty, your sharpness and your hatred of dharma, of your
adharma, your abuse and your scorn of your elders, your crooked looks and all
your bad policy. When I am angry, lord of men, with Vasudeva beside me, what
hope can you have of life or kingdom, fool? When Bhishma is at peace, and Drona
too, and the suta's son has fallen, you will lose hope of life, of kingdom and
of sons. When you see your brothers and your sons dead, Suyodhana, and you are
killed by Bhimasena, you will remember your wrongdoing. Keshava will not make a
second promise. I speak the truth, and all of it will come true.'

"When the gambler's son had been told this, king," said Sanjaya, "he took in the
words, and with their leave he went back as he had come. He returned from the
Pandavas to the son of Dhritarashtra and told it all, as he had been told, in
the Kuru assembly. When the bull among the Bharatas heard the words of Keshava
and Arjuna, he said to Duhshasana, Karna and Shakuni, 'Give orders to the kings,
to our army and to our allies' army, that the whole army must stand ready before
sunrise.' Then messengers sent by Karna hurried off in chariots, on camels and
she-camels, and on swift good horses, and went quickly round the whole army at
Karna's command, telling the kings, 'Be ready before sunrise.'

"When Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, heard Uluka's words, he had the army march out
with Dhrishtadyumna at its head. It was a terrible fourfold army of foot
soldiers, elephants, chariots and troops of horses, as unshakeable as the earth.
It was guarded by Bhimasena and the others, and by the great chariot fighters
with Arjuna. It was under Dhrishtadyumna's command, hard to attack, like the
still sea. At its head the great archer of the Panchalas, fierce in battle,
Dhrishtadyumna, who longed for Drona, led the divisions.

"He assigned the chariot fighters according to their strength and their spirit.
He assigned Arjuna to the suta's son and Bhima to Duryodhana, Nakula to
Ashvatthama and Shaibya to Kritavarma. He assigned Yuyudhana, the Varshneya, to
the Saindhava. He set Shikhandi in front, against Bhishma. He assigned Sahadeva
to Shakuni and Chekitana to Shala, Dhrishtaketu to Shalya, and Uttamaujas to
Gautama. He assigned the sons of Draupadi to the five Trigartas, and the son of
Subhadra to Vrishasena and the rest of the kings, for he thought him greater
than Partha in battle. When he had divided the fighters like this, separately
and together, the flame-coloured great archer took Drona as his own share.
Dhrishtadyumna, the great archer, the commander of the commanders, wise and set
on battle, arrayed the Pandava divisions as he had assigned them, and stood
ready on the field of battle for the victory of the sons of Pandu."

"When Phalguna has sworn to kill Bhishma, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "what
did my foolish sons, Duryodhana and the others, do? I see Ganga's son, my
father, already killed in battle by Partha, the strong bowman, with Vasudeva as
his ally. And what did Bhishma say when he heard Partha's words, the great
archer whose wisdom has no measure, the best of fighters? What did Ganga's son
do when he had taken command, the Kauravas' yoke-bearer, great in wisdom and
courage?"

Then Sanjaya told him everything, as the eldest of the Kurus, Bhishma, whose
power had no measure, had said it.

"When Bhishma, son of Shantanu, had taken command, king," said Sanjaya, "he
spoke to Duryodhana as if to cheer him. 'I bow to Kumara, the general of the
gods, who carries the spear. I shall be your commander now, no doubt of it. I
know the work of an army and all kinds of battle arrays, and how to get work
done by paid men and unpaid. I know marching and moving, battles and making
peace after victory, great king, as well as Brihaspati knows them. I know great
arrays of gods, gandharvas and men. With them I shall confound the Pandavas. Let
your fever go. I shall fight as I truly should, guarding your army, as the books
lay down, king. Let the fever of your mind go.'

"'I am not afraid of the gods and asuras together, strong-armed son of Ganga,'
said Duryodhana. 'I tell you the truth. How much less now that you, whom no one
can face, have taken command, and Drona, the tiger among men, who delights in
battle, stands with us? With you two, the foremost of men, standing by me,
victory is mine, best of the Kurus, and even the kingdom of the gods would
surely not be hard to win. But I want to know the full count of the chariot
fighters, Kaurava, the enemy's and ours, and of the great chariot fighters. The
grandfather knows about the enemy and about us. I want to hear it, with all
these kings.'

"'Son of Gandhari, king of kings,' said Bhishma, 'hear the count of the chariot
fighters in your own army, lord of the earth, and of the great chariot fighters.
There are many thousands, millions and hundreds of millions of chariot fighters
in your army. Hear from me the chief of them. First you are a splendid chariot
fighter yourself, with all your brothers, Duhshasana and the rest, a hundred of
them. All are skilled at striking, clever at cutting and piercing, on the
chariot seat, on an elephant's back, in the mace fight and with sword and
shield. They can drive and strike, they are skilled in weapons and can carry a
load. In archery they are pupils of Drona and of Kripa, son of Sharadvat. The
proud sons of Dhritarashtra, wronged by the Pandavas, will kill the Panchalas,
fierce in battle. And I, best of the Bharatas, the commander of your whole army,
shall destroy your enemies, treating the Pandavas as nothing. But I should not
speak of my own virtues. You know me.

"'Kritavarma the Bhoja, the best of fighters, is a great chariot fighter. He
will surely win you your aim in battle. Men skilled in weapons cannot overcome
him. He shoots far and his weapons are strong. He will kill your enemies as
great Indra kills the danavas. The great archer Shalya, king of the Madras, is
to my mind a great chariot fighter. He always rivals Vasudeva in battle after
battle. Shalya, the best of chariot fighters, has left his own sister's sons and
will fight Krishna, who bears the discus and the mace, in battle, flooding the
enemy with rushes like the waves of the sea. Bhurishravas, skilled in weapons,
your friend who wants your good, the great archer, son of Somadatta, leader of
leaders of chariot troops, will make a great slaughter of the enemy's forces.

"'The king of Sindhu, great king, is to my mind worth two chariot fighters. That
bold king, the best of chariot fighters, will fight in battle. He was tormented
by the Pandavas long ago when he carried off Draupadi, and remembering that
torment the killer of enemy heroes will fight. At that time, king, he practised
terrible austerity and won a boon hard to win, to fight the Pandavas in battle.
That tiger among chariot fighters will fight the Pandavas, my friend,
remembering that feud, giving up the life that is so hard to give up.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Arjuna's vow.** Arjuna swears to kill Bhishma first, "while all the bowmen watch", and to bring him down from his chariot at sunrise (5.160.8, 12–14). This is CE.
* **The other Pandavas' answers.** A long appendix passage (App. I No. 13, at 5.160.23) gives answers to Uluka by Yudhishthira, Bhima, Nakula, Sahadeva, Virata, Drupada, Shikhandi and Dhrishtadyumna, among them Bhima's vow to break Duryodhana's thighs. In the CE Arjuna speaks alone here. Not narrated.
* **The count begins.** Bhishma's count of the chariot fighters (the Rathatiratha) begins here, at Duryodhana's request (5.162.15–33), and continues in the next chapters.
* \*563 is not narrated.

* 5.160.4 *kṣatrabandhuḥ*: "a kshatriya in name only".
* 5.160.10 *śālveyakān*: the Shalveyakas, a people allied with the Pandavas. The words quoted are Bhishma's, as Arjuna repeats them.
* 5.160.13 *dvīpam enam*: "this island of yours", Bhishma as the Kauravas' refuge.
* 5.160.15 *hrasvadarśanaḥ*: "the short-sighted man", Duhshasana.

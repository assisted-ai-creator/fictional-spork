# Nahusha

"Then all the seers and the chief gods said, 'Let glorious Nahusha be crowned
king of the gods.' They all went to him and said, 'Be our king, lord of the
earth.'

"Nahusha, wanting what was good for himself, king, said to the gods, the hosts
of seers and the ancestors with them, 'I am weak. I have no power to protect
you. A king must be strong, and strength has always been in Shakra.'

"All the gods, led by the seers, spoke to him again. 'With our austerity behind
you, rule the kingdom in heaven. We are all terribly afraid of one another, no
doubt of it. Be crowned, king of kings. Be king in heaven. Whatever beings come
into your sight, gods, danavas, yakshas, seers, rakshasas, ancestors, gandharvas
and spirits, you will take their energy just by looking at them, and you will be
strong. Always put dharma first, and be lord of all the worlds. Protect the
brahmin seers and the gods in heaven.'

"So Nahusha won a boon that is very hard to win, and the kingdom of heaven. He
had always been devoted to dharma. Now he became devoted to desire. In all the
gardens of the gods, in the groves of Nandana, on Kailasa and the ridge of the
Himalaya, on Mandara and the White Mountain, on Sahya, Mahendra and Malaya, on
the oceans and the rivers, Nahusha, king of the gods, played in many ways,
surrounded by apsarases and the daughters of the gods. He listened to many kinds
of heavenly stories that charmed the ear, to every kind of music, and to songs
sung in sweet voices. Vishvavasu and Narada, the hosts of gandharvas and
apsarases, and the six seasons in bodily form waited on the king of the gods. A
fragrant breeze blew, pleasant, gentle and cool.

"While great Nahusha was playing in this way, the goddess who was Shakra's dear
queen came into his sight. When the wicked one saw her, he said to all those in
his hall, 'Why does Indra's queen not wait on me? I am Indra, lord of the gods
and of the worlds. Let Shachi come to my house today, quickly.'

"When she heard this, the goddess was distressed. She said to Brihaspati,
'Protect me from Nahusha, brahmin. I have come to you for refuge. You have told
me I have every lucky mark, brahmin, that I am the beloved of the king of the
gods and will have the greatest happiness. You told me before that I would never
be a widow, that I am a one-man woman, devoted to my husband. Make those words
true. You have never said anything false before, lord. So let what you said be
true, best of the twice-born.'

"Brihaspati said to Indrani, who was confused with fear, 'What I told you,
goddess, will certainly come true. You will see Indra, king of the gods, here
soon. Do not be afraid of Nahusha. I tell you truly, I shall bring you back
together with Shakra before long.'

"Then Nahusha heard that Indrani had taken refuge with Brihaspati, son of
Angiras, and the king was angry.

---

"When the gods and the seers saw that Nahusha was angry, they said to the king
of the gods, who was terrible to look at, 'King of the gods, let go of your
anger. When you are angry, lord of the world, the world is afraid, with its
asuras and gandharvas, its kinnaras and great serpents. Let go of this anger,
good king. People like you do not get angry. That goddess is another man's wife.
Be gracious, lord of the gods. Turn your mind away from the sin of touching
another man's wife. You are king of the gods, bless you. Protect the creatures
by dharma.'

"But he would not listen. He was blinded by desire. He spoke to the gods about
Indra. 'Long ago Indra violated Ahalya, the famous wife of a seer, while her
husband was alive. Why didn't you stop him? Indra did many cruel things long
ago, things against dharma, and tricks. Why didn't you stop him? Let the goddess
wait on me. That is what is best for her. And it will always go well for you
that way, gods.'

"'We will bring Indrani, as you wish, lord of heaven,' said the gods. 'Let go of
this anger, hero. Be pleased, lord of the gods.'

"When they had said this, Bharata, the gods went with the seers to give
Brihaspati and Indrani the evil message. 'We know that Indrani has come to your
house for refuge, and that you have promised her safety, best of brahmins and of
divine seers. The gods, the gandharvas and the seers beg you, glorious one, to
give Indrani to Nahusha. Nahusha, the glorious king of the gods, is greater than
Indra. Let this lovely woman choose him as her husband.'

"When they said this, the goddess burst into tears and cried aloud. Weeping and
wretched, she said to Brihaspati, 'I do not want Nahusha as my husband, lord. I
have come to you for refuge, brahmin. Save me from this great fear.'

"'I will not give up one who has come to me for refuge, Indrani,' said
Brihaspati. 'My mind is made up. I will not give you up, faultless one, for you
know dharma and keep it. I will not do what should not be done, above all
because I am a brahmin, I have learned dharma, I keep to the truth and I know
the teaching of dharma. I won't do it. Go, best of the gods. Hear what Brahma
sang long ago about this.

"'The man who hands over to his enemy one who comes to him in fear for
protection, his seed does not grow in the sowing season, and no rain falls for
him in the season of rain. When he himself needs protection, he finds none.
Witless, he eats food in vain. He falls from the world of heaven, and all his
efforts come to nothing. The gods will not accept his offerings. His children
die before their time, and his ancestors always quarrel with him. The gods with
Indra hurl the thunderbolt at him.

"'Knowing this, I will not give up Shachi, Indra's famous, beloved queen. Best
of the gods, do whatever is good for her and good for me too. But I will not
give up Shachi.'

"Then the gods said to that best of the Angirasas, their teacher, 'How can this
be handled well? Advise us, Brihaspati.'

"'Let the lovely goddess ask Nahusha for a little time,' said Brihaspati. 'That
will be good for Indrani, and for us. Time brings many obstacles. Time will
carry time along. Nahusha is proud, and strong because of his boon.'

"When he said this, the gods were pleased. 'Brahmin, what you say is good, and
good for all who live in heaven. So be it, best of the twice-born. Let the
goddess be won over.'

"Then all the gods, with Agni at their head, calmly said to Indrani, for the
good of the worlds, 'You hold up this whole world, moving and unmoving. You are
a one-man woman, and true. Go to Nahusha. The king who desires you will soon
perish, goddess, and Shakra will win back the lordship of the gods.'

"So, to gain her end, Indrani made up her mind. Shamed, she went to Nahusha, who
was terrible to look at. And when the wicked one saw her, young and beautiful,
he was delighted, his mind struck by desire.

---

"When Nahusha, king of the gods, saw her, he said, 'I am Indra of all three
worlds, you with the bright smile. Take me as your husband, lovely woman.'

"When Nahusha said this, the goddess, devoted to her husband, trembled with fear
like a plantain tree in a high wind. She bowed to Brahma, and with her joined
hands to her head, she said to Nahusha, the terrible-looking king of the gods,
'I want to be given a little time by you, lord of the gods. It is not known what
has happened to Shakra, or where he has gone. When I have found out the truth of
this, lord, if nothing is known of him, I will wait on you. I tell you this
truly.'

"When Indrani said this, Nahusha was pleased. 'Let it be as you say, lovely
woman,' said Nahusha. 'When you have found out, you must come. Remember this
promise.'

"Dismissed by Nahusha, the lovely ascetic woman left and went to Brihaspati's
house. When they heard what she said, the gods, led by Agni, took counsel
together for Shakra's sake, best of kings. They met with Vishnu, the mighty god
of gods, and those masters of speech, in great distress, said to him, 'Shakra,
lord of the hosts of gods, is overcome by the sin of killing a brahmin. You are
our refuge, lord of the gods, the first-born, lord of the world. You took the
form of Vishnu to protect all beings. When Vritra was killed by your power,
Vasava was gripped by the sin of killing a brahmin. Show him the way to be freed
of it, best of the gods.'

"When he heard the gods' words, Vishnu said, 'Let Shakra worship me in
sacrifice, and I will purify the wielder of the thunderbolt. When the chastiser
of Paka has worshipped me with a holy horse sacrifice, he will become Indra of
the gods again, with nothing to fear. And evil-minded Nahusha will be destroyed
by his own deeds. Bear this for a while, gods, and stay alert.'

"When they heard Vishnu's words, kind, true and like amrita, all the gods went
with their teacher and the seers to the place where Shakra was, troubled by
fear. There a great horse sacrifice was held for great Indra, to purify him and
take away the sin of killing a brahmin, king. The lord of the gods divided the
sin of killing a brahmin among the trees and the rivers, the mountains, the
earth and women, Yudhishthira. When he had shared it out among creatures and let
it go, Vasava's fever left him. His sin was cleansed, and he was himself again.

"But when the slayer of Bala saw that Nahusha could not be moved from his place,
and that through his boon he took the energy of all creatures and was hard to
bear, the hero, Shachi's husband, disappeared again. Unseen by all beings, he
wandered about, waiting for his time.

"When Shakra had disappeared, Shachi was overcome with grief. 'Oh Shakra!' the
goddess lamented, in deep sorrow. 'If I have given gifts, if I have made
offerings, if I have pleased my elders, if there is truth in me, then let me
have only one husband. I bow to this holy, divine goddess Night, who has begun
with the sun's northern course. Let my wish come true.'

"Pure in body and mind, she worshipped the goddess Night. By the truth of her
devotion to her husband, she called on Upashruti, the goddess who answers those
who listen for an omen. 'Show me the place where the king of the gods is,' the
goddess said to Upashruti. 'Let the truth be seen by truth.'

---

"Then Upashruti came to the good woman in bodily form. When Indrani saw the
goddess standing before her, young and beautiful, she was delighted. She
honoured her and asked, 'I want to know who you are. Tell me, lovely one.'

"'I am Upashruti, goddess,' said Upashruti. 'I have come to you. I am pleased
with your truth, and so you see me. You are devoted to your husband, and you
keep your vows and your rules. I will show you Shakra, the god who killed
Vritra. Follow me quickly, bless you, and you will see the best of the gods.'

"The goddess set out, and Indrani followed her. She passed the forests of the
gods and many mountains, crossed the Himalaya, and came to its northern side.
She came to the sea, many yojanas wide, and reached a great island covered with
trees and creepers of every kind. There she saw a heavenly lake, full of birds
of every kind, a hundred yojanas wide and as many long, beautiful. In it were
thousands of heavenly lotuses in bloom, Bharata, of five colours, with bees
humming over them.

"Upashruti split open the stalk of a lotus and went in, and Indrani with her.
There she saw Shatakratu, who had entered a fibre of the lotus stalk. When she
saw the lord there in a very tiny form, the goddess made herself tiny too, and
so did Upashruti. Indrani praised Indra with his famous deeds of old. Being
praised, the god Purandara said to Shachi, 'Why have you come? And how was I
found?'

"Then she told him what Nahusha was doing. 'Since he won the rule of Indra in
the three worlds, he has been drunk with his strength. He is full of pride and
wicked. He said to me, Shatakratu, "Wait on me." Cruel as he is, he has set me a
time. If you don't save me, lord, he will have me in his power. This is why I
have come to you, Shakra, burning with distress. Strong-armed one, kill Nahusha,
that cruel man set on evil. Show yourself, slayer of daityas and danavas. Take
back your energy, lord, and rule the kingdom of the gods.'

---

"When Shachi said this, the lord answered her. 'This is no time for courage.
Nahusha is stronger. The seers have made him grow with offerings to the gods and
the ancestors, my lady. I shall make a plan. You must carry it out, goddess.
Keep it secret, lovely one. Tell no one. Go to Nahusha alone, slender-waisted
one, and say to him, "Lord of the world, come to me in a heavenly carriage
carried by seers. Then I will gladly be yours." Tell him that.'

"When the king of the gods said this, his lotus-eyed wife said, 'So be it,' and
went to Nahusha.

"When Nahusha saw her, he was surprised. 'Welcome, lovely woman,' he said. 'What
can I do for you, you with the bright smile? Love me, for I love you, lovely
one. What do you want, proud lady? Whatever you want done, lovely one,
slender-waisted one, I will do. Don't be shy, lovely-hipped one. Trust me. I
swear by the truth, goddess, I will do as you say.'

"'I am waiting for the time you set me, lord of the world,' said Indrani. 'Then
you will be my husband, king of the gods. Hear what I have in my heart, king of
the gods. I will tell you. If you will do this to please me, a loving request,
then I will be in your power. Indra had horses, elephants and chariots to carry
him. I want you to have a carriage that no one has had before, lord of the gods,
one that neither Vishnu nor Rudra had, nor the asuras nor the rakshasas. Let the
seers together carry you, great king, all of them, in a litter, lord. That is
what I would like. You should not be equal to the asuras or the gods. Take the
energy of everyone by your own power, just by looking at them. No mighty one
will want to stand before you.'

"When she said this, they say, Nahusha was delighted. The lord of the gods said
to the faultless woman, 'This carriage you speak of has never been seen before,
lovely woman. I like it very much, goddess. I am in your power, lovely one. No
man of small power makes seers carry him. I am an ascetic, I am strong, I am
lord of what was, what will be and what is. If I were angry, the world would not
exist. Everything rests on me. Gods, danavas and gandharvas, kinnaras, serpents
and rakshasas, all the worlds together are no match for me when I am angry, you
with the bright smile. Whoever I look at, I take his energy. So I will do as you
say, goddess, no doubt of it. The seven seers and all the brahmin seers will
carry me. See my greatness and my power, lovely woman.'

"When he had said this, he dismissed the lovely goddess. Then he yoked to his
heavenly carriage the seers who were keeping their vows. He was hostile to
brahmins, full of strength, drunk with the pride of his boon, and he lived by
his desires. The wicked one made the seers carry him.

"When Nahusha had let her go, she said to Brihaspati, 'Only a little is left of
the time Nahusha set me. Search for Shakra quickly. Have pity on me, for I am
devoted to you.'

"'Very well,' said holy Brihaspati. 'You need not fear wicked Nahusha, goddess.
He won't last long. That lowest of men is already gone. He does not know dharma,
and making the great seers carry him has killed him, lovely one. I will perform
a rite for the destruction of that evil-minded one, and I will find Shakra.
Don't be afraid. Bless you.'

"Then mighty Brihaspati kindled the fire as the rite lays down and poured the
finest offering into it, to find the king of the gods. From it came the holy god
Agni himself, the eater of offerings. He took the wonderful form of a woman and
at once disappeared. Swift as thought, he searched the directions and the points
between them, the mountains and the forests, the earth and the sky. In the blink
of an eye he came back to Brihaspati.

"'Brihaspati, I can't see the king of the gods anywhere,' said Agni. 'Only the
waters are left, and I can never bring myself to enter the waters. I can't go
there, brahmin. What else can I do for you?'

"The teacher of the gods said to him, 'Enter the waters, glorious one.'

"'I can't enter the waters,' said Agni. 'I would perish there. I have come to
you for refuge, glorious one. Bless you. Fire was born from water, the kshatriya
from the brahmin, and iron from stone. Their energy goes everywhere, but in
their own source it dies down.'

---

"'You are the mouth of all the gods, Agni,' said Brihaspati. 'You carry the
offerings. You move hidden inside all beings, like a witness. The poets call you
one, and they also call you threefold. If you left this world, eater of
offerings, it would perish at once. Brahmins who bow to you go with their wives
and sons to the eternal place that their own deeds have won. You, Agni, are the
carrier of offerings, and you yourself are the highest offering. In the long
sacrifices and in the great rites, it is you they worship.

"'Carrier of offerings, you create these three worlds, and when the time comes
you blaze up and cook them again. You are the birthplace of this whole world,
Agni, and you are its resting place again. They say you are the clouds, Agni,
and you are the lightning. Flames go out of you and burn all creatures. All the
waters are placed in you, and this whole world is in you. There is nothing in
the three worlds you do not know, purifier. Everything goes back to its own
source. Enter the waters without fear. I will make you grow with the eternal
mantras of Brahma.'

"Praised in this way, the holy carrier of offerings, the best of poets, was
pleased, and he spoke to Brihaspati in excellent words. 'I will show you Shakra.
I tell you this truly.'

"Then the fire entered the waters, with their seas and their ponds, and came to
the lake where Shatakratu was hidden. Searching the lotuses there, bull of the
Bharatas, he found the king of the gods inside a lotus stalk. He came back
quickly and told Brihaspati of the lord, in a body the size of an atom, inside
the fibre of a lotus.

"Brihaspati went there with the divine seers and the gandharvas and praised the
god, the slayer of Bala, with his deeds of old. 'Shakra, you killed the terrible
great asura Namuchi, and Shambara and Bala, both fearful in their courage.
Shatakratu, grow, and kill all your enemies. Rise, wielder of the thunderbolt,
and see the divine seers who have come. Great Indra, you have killed the danavas
and saved the worlds, lord. Long ago you took the foam of the waters, filled
with Vishnu's energy, and killed Vritra, king of the gods, lord of the world.
You are the one to be chosen and praised among all beings. There is no being
here equal to you. All beings are upheld by you, Shakra. You made the greatness
of the gods. Protect the gods and the worlds, great Indra. Take back your
strength.'

"Praised like this, he grew little by little. He took on his own body and became
strong. Then the god said to his teacher Brihaspati, who stood before him, 'What
is left for you to do? The great asura, Tvashtri's son, is dead, and so is
Vritra, that huge one who wanted to swallow the worlds.'

"'King Nahusha, a man,' said Brihaspati, 'has won the kingdom of the gods by the
energy of the divine seers and the gods. He torments us all terribly.'

"'How did Nahusha win the kingdom of the gods, so hard to win?' said Indra.
'What austerity did he have, Brihaspati, or what power?'

"'The gods were afraid,' said Brihaspati. 'They wanted a Shakra, because you had
left the great place of Indra. Then the gods, the ancestors, the seers and the
hosts of gandharvas all came together. They went to Nahusha, Shakra, and said,
"Be our king, the guardian of the world." Nahusha said to them, "I am not able.
Fill me with your austerity and your energy." When he said this, the gods made
him grow, and Nahusha became king, terrible in his power. Now that he has the
kingdom of the three worlds, the wicked one has made ascetics his carriers, and
goes about the worlds. You must never look at Nahusha. His gaze is a poison that
takes away energy, and it is terrible. All the gods are terrified of Nahusha.
They go about in hiding, and do not look at him.'

"While Brihaspati, best of the Angirasas, was saying this, the guardian of the
world, Kubera, came there, and Yama, son of Vivasvat, the ancient one, and the
god Soma, and Varuna. They met great Indra and said to him, 'How lucky that
Tvashtri's son is dead, and Vritra too. How lucky that we see you well and
unhurt, Shakra, with your enemy killed.'

"Shakra answered them as was fitting, and urged them on against Nahusha. 'The
king of the gods, Nahusha, is terrible. Give me your help against him.'

"'Nahusha is terrible,' they said. 'His gaze is poison. We are afraid of him,
god. If you defeat Nahusha, king, then we deserve a share, Shakra.'

"'Let it be so,' said Indra. 'You shall be lord of the waters, and Yama and
Kubera too shall have the great consecration today, along with him. Let us
defeat our enemy Nahusha of the terrible gaze.'

"Then Agni said to Shakra, 'Give me a share too, and I will help you.'

"'You too shall have one, Agni,' said Shakra. 'In the great sacrifice there will
be one share for Indra and Agni together.'

"After thinking it over, the lord, great Indra, chastiser of Paka, the giver of
boons, honoured them and gave them lordship: to Kubera over all the yakshas and
over wealth, to the son of Vivasvat over the ancestors, and to Varuna over the
waters.

---

"While the wise king of the gods was thinking with the guardians of the world
about a way to kill Nahusha, the holy ascetic Agastya appeared there. He
honoured the king of the gods and said, 'How lucky that you prosper, by the
destruction of Vishvarupa and the killing of the asura Vritra. And how lucky
that Nahusha has fallen from the kingdom of the gods, Purandara. How lucky that
I see you with your enemy killed, slayer of Bala.'

"'Welcome, great seer,' said Indra. 'I am glad to see you. Take from me water
for your feet and for rinsing your mouth, a cow and the guest offering.'

"When the best of sages had been honoured and was seated, the lord of the gods
asked the bull among brahmins, in delight, 'I want you to tell me this, lord,
best of the twice-born. How did wicked Nahusha fall from heaven?'

"'Hear the good news, Shakra,' said Agastya, 'of how evil, wicked King Nahusha,
proud of his strength, fell from heaven. The fortunate divine seers and the
spotless brahmin seers were tired from carrying Nahusha, that doer of evil. They
asked Nahusha about a doubt they had, god, best of victors. "These mantras that
Brahma pronounced for sprinkling cows, are they authoritative or not, Vasava?"
Nahusha, his mind darkened, said, "No."

"'"You are set on adharma," said the seers. "You do not accept dharma. For us
these are authoritative. The great seers pronounced them long ago."

"'Then, as he argued with the sages, Vasava, he touched me on the head with his
foot, driven by adharma. At that he lost his energy and his glory, husband of
Shachi. Then I said to him, as he shook with fear, "You find fault with
faultless Vedic words, made by the ancients and practised by the brahmin seers,
and you have touched my head with your foot. And you have made the seers, fools,
who are like Brahma and hard to approach, into your carriers and made them carry
you. For that, your splendour is struck down, and you fall from heaven. Fall,
evil one, your merit used up. Fall to the earth. For ten thousand years you will
wander there in the form of a huge snake. When they are over, you will win
heaven again."

"'So the wicked one fell from the kingdom of the gods, scorcher of enemies. How
lucky that we prosper, Shakra. The thorn in the brahmins' side has been struck
down. Go to heaven, husband of Shachi, and protect the worlds, master of your
senses, conqueror of your enemies, praised by the great seers.'

"Then the gods were delighted, with the hosts of great seers about them, and the
ancestors, the yakshas, the serpents and the rakshasas, the gandharvas, the
daughters of the gods and all the hosts of apsarases, the lakes, the rivers, the
mountains and the oceans, lord of the people. They all came and said, 'How lucky
that you prosper, slayer of enemies. How lucky that wise Agastya has struck down
wicked Nahusha. How lucky that the evil-doer has been made a snake on the
earth.'

---

"Then Shakra, praised by the hosts of gandharvas and apsarases, mounted
Airavata, king of elephants, who bore all the lucky marks. Mighty Agni, the
great seer Brihaspati, Yama, Varuna and Kubera, lord of wealth, and all the gods
went round him. Shakra, slayer of Vritra, the lord, went to the three worlds
with the gandharvas and the apsarases. Shatakratu, king of the gods, met great
Indrani and ruled, in the greatest joy.

"Then the holy Angiras appeared there and worshipped the king of the gods with
mantras of the Atharva Veda. Lord Indra was delighted, and gave Atharvangiras a
boon. 'In this Veda you will be known by the name Atharvangiras. This will be
the proof of it, and you will receive a share in the sacrifice.' When he had
honoured Atharvangiras in this way, great king, Shatakratu, king of the gods,
sent him on his way. He honoured all the thirty gods and the seers rich in
austerity, and Indra ruled the creatures by dharma in delight, king.

"So Indra and his wife met with sorrow, and he lived in hiding, wanting to kill
his enemies. Do not be angry because you have suffered in the great forest with
Draupadi, king of kings, and with your great brothers. You too, king of kings,
will win your kingdom, Bharata, just as Shakra did when he had killed Vritra,
joy of the Kauravas. Wicked Nahusha, hater of brahmins, evil-minded, was struck
down by Agastya's curse and lost for endless years. In the same way your wicked
enemies, Karna, Duryodhana and the others, will quickly come to ruin, slayer of
enemies. Then you will enjoy this earth to the ocean's edge, hero, with your
brothers and with Draupadi, lord.

"This story of Shakra's victory is equal to the Veda. A king who wants victory
should hear it when the armies are drawn up. That is why I am telling you this
story of victory, best of victors. Great men grow greater when they are praised,
Yudhishthira. This destruction of great kshatriyas will come about,
Yudhishthira, through Duryodhana's offence and the strength of Bhima and Arjuna.
Whoever reads this story of Indra's victory with care is cleansed of sin, wins
heaven, and is happy here and hereafter. He has no fear of enemies, he will not
be without a son, he will meet no misfortune, and he will live long. He wins
victory everywhere, and is never defeated."

---

Comforted in this way by Shalya, bull of the Bharatas, the king, best of those
who keep dharma, honoured Shalya as is proper. When he had heard Shalya's words,
strong-armed Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, answered the Madra king. "You will
drive Karna's chariot, no doubt of it. When you do, you must break Karna's
spirit by praising us."

"I will do as you say," said Shalya. "And anything else I can do for you, I will
do."

Then Shalya, lord of the Madras, glorious slayer of enemies, took his leave of
the sons of Kunti and went with his army to Duryodhana.

**Divergences.**

* **Nahusha and Agastya.** Popular versions have Nahusha, in his hurry, kick Agastya and cry *sarpa, sarpa* ("move, move"), so that the sage curses him to be a *sarpa*, a snake. In the CE the seers ask Nahusha whether the Vedic mantras for sprinkling cows are authoritative; he says no, argues with them, and touches Agastya's head with his foot (5.17.9–11). The curse is ten thousand years as a huge snake, after which he will win heaven again (5.17.15). The pun is not in the CE. Ledger L-173.
* **The end of the curse.** Rejected lines (\*114–\*115) have Agastya add that Nahusha will be freed on seeing "Yudhishthira, born in your line". The CE curse sets only the ten thousand years. (Yudhishthira's meeting with Nahusha the snake is told in Book 3, CE 3.173–178.)
* Other rejected lines (\*92–\*113, \*116–\*117) add epithets and descriptive detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.11.13 *śakrasya mahiṣī priyā*: Shachi, also called Indrani, Indra's queen.
* 5.12.6: Ahalya, wife of the seer Gautama (her husband is not named here).
* 5.13.2: "like a plantain tree in a high wind" is the text's own image.
* 5.13.10 *viṣṇutvam upajagmivān*: "You took the form of Vishnu".
* 5.13.17: the sin is divided among trees, rivers, mountains, the earth and women. The text gives no more.
* 5.13.23 *uttarāyaṇe*: Night "begun with the sun's northern course".
* 5.13.24 *upaśruti*: a goddess of omens heard (literally "listening"). Explained in the sentence.
* 5.15.32: the saying "fire from water, the kshatriya from the brahmin, iron from stone" is Agni's.
* 5.16.2 *trividham*: "threefold", the three sacred fires.
* 5.16.31: "You shall be lord of the waters" is said to Varuna.
* 5.17.9 *prokṣaṇe gavām*: "for sprinkling cows", that is, consecrating them.
* 5.18.7: Atharvangiras is the holy Angiras who worships Indra with Atharva mantras.

# Nahusha

"Then all the seers and the gods, the lords of the thirty, said, 'Let the
glorious Nahusha be consecrated as king of the gods.' They all went to him and
said, 'King, be our king.'

"Nahusha, wanting what was good for himself, said to the gods, the hosts of
seers and the Ancestors, 'I am weak. I do not have the power to protect you. A
king must be strong, and strength has always been Shakra's.'

"All the gods, with the seers at their head, spoke to him again. 'Rule the
kingdom of heaven, armed with our austerity. We are all in terrible fear of one
another, no doubt of it. Be consecrated, king of kings. Be king in heaven.
Whatever gods, danavas, yakshas, seers, rakshasas, Ancestors, gandharvas and
spirits come within the range of your eyes, you will take their strength by
looking at them, and you will be strong. Keep dharma always before you and be
lord of all the worlds. Protect the brahmin seers and the gods in heaven.'

"So he won a boon very hard to win, and the kingdom of heaven. He had always
been a man of dharma. Now he became a man of desire. In all the gardens of the
gods and the groves of Nandana, on Kailasa, on the back of the Himalaya, on
Mandara and the White Mountain, on Sahya, Mahendra and Malaya, on the seas and
the rivers, Nahusha, now king of the gods, played in many ways, surrounded by
apsarases and by the daughters of the gods. He listened to many heavenly tales
that delighted the ear, and to every kind of music and sweet song. Vishvavasu
and Narada, and the hosts of gandharvas and apsarases, and the six seasons in
bodily form, attended the king of the gods. A fragrant wind blew, pleasing,
gentle and cool.

"While the great Nahusha was playing like this, the goddess, Shakra's dear
queen, came into his sight. When he saw her, the wicked one said to all who sat
with him, 'Why does the queen of Indra not wait on me? I am Indra of the gods,
and lord of the worlds. Let Shachi come to my house today, quickly.'

"When she heard that, the goddess was downcast, and she said to Brihaspati,
'Protect me from Nahusha, brahmin. I have come to you for refuge. You have told
me, brahmin, that I have every sign of good fortune, that I am the dear wife of
the king of the gods, and that great happiness is mine. You told me before that
I would never be widowed, that I am a wife of one husband, true to her lord.
Make those words come true. You have never spoken a false word before, lord. So
let what you said be true, best of brahmins.'

"Brihaspati spoke to Indrani, who was confused with fear. 'What I told you,
goddess, will surely come true. You will soon see Indra, the king of the gods,
come back here. You need not fear Nahusha. I tell you this truly. I will soon
bring you and Shakra together.'

"Then Nahusha heard that Indrani had gone to Brihaspati, of the line of Angiras,
for refuge, and the king was angry.

---

"When the gods and the seers saw that Nahusha was angry, they spoke to the king
of the gods, terrible to see. 'King of the gods, put away your anger. When you
are angry, lord of the world, the whole world trembles, with the asuras, the
gandharvas, the kinnaras and the great snakes. Put away this anger, good king.
Such as you do not grow angry. That goddess is another's wife. Be gracious, lord
of the gods. Turn your mind away from evil, from touching another man's wife.
You are king of the gods. Bless you, protect your subjects by dharma.'

"He did not take their words to heart, for he was deluded by desire. The lord of
the gods said this to the gods about Indra. 'Long ago Indra forced himself on
Ahalya, the famous wife of a seer, while her husband was alive. Why did you not
stop him? Many cruel deeds were done by Indra in the past, wrongs and deceits.
Why did you not stop him? Let the goddess wait on me. That is best for her, and
it will always go well with you too, gods.'

"'We will bring Indrani to you as you wish, lord of heaven,' said the gods. 'Put
away this anger, hero. Be pleased, lord of the gods.'

"Having said this, the gods and the seers went to Brihaspati to tell him and
Indrani this evil news. 'We know that Indrani has come to your house for refuge,
and that you have promised her safety, best of brahmins and first of divine
seers. The gods, the gandharvas and the seers all ask your favour, great one.
Let Indrani be given to Nahusha. Nahusha, the glorious king of the gods, is
greater than Indra. Let this lovely, fair woman choose him for her husband.'

"When they said this, the goddess burst into tears and wept aloud. Weeping and
wretched, she said to Brihaspati, 'I do not want Nahusha for my husband, lord. I
have come to you for refuge, brahmin. Save me from this great fear.'

"'I will not abandon one who has come to me for refuge, Indrani,' said
Brihaspati. 'That is my resolve. I will not abandon you, blameless one, who know
dharma and live by it. I do not want to do what should not be done, especially
as a brahmin, one who has heard the dharma, who lives by the truth and knows the
teaching of dharma. I will not do this. Go, best of gods. Hear what Brahma sang
long ago on this matter:

"'*His seed does not sprout in the sowing time, and his rain does not fall in
the rainy time, who gives up to his enemy one who comes to him afraid for
refuge. When he himself wants protection, he finds none. He eats his food in
vain, and his mind is lost. He falls from the world of heaven, and his efforts
come to nothing. The gods will not take the offerings of the man who gives up
one who comes to him afraid. His children die before their time, and his
Ancestors are always homeless. The gods, with Indra, hurl the thunderbolt at the
man who gives up to his enemy one who comes to him afraid.*'

"'Knowing this, I will not give up Shachi, Indrani, famous in the world, the
dear queen of Shakra. Best of gods, do whatever will be good for her, and good
for me too. But I will not give up Shachi.'

"Then the gods said to that teacher, the best of the Angirases, 'Brihaspati,
advise us. How can it be arranged well?'

"'Let the good goddess ask Nahusha for a little time,' said Brihaspati. 'That
will be good for Indrani, and for us too. Time brings many obstacles. Time will
carry time along. Nahusha is proud, and strong because of his boon.'

"The gods were pleased when he said this. 'Brahmin,' they said, 'you have spoken
well. This is good for all the dwellers in heaven. So be it, best of the
twice-born. Let the goddess be won over.'

"Then all the gods, with Agni at their head, spoke to Indrani calmly, for the
good of the worlds. 'You hold up this whole world, all that moves and all that
does not. You are a wife of one husband, and you are true. Go to Nahusha. The
king who desires you will soon perish, goddess, and Shakra will win back the
lordship of the gods.'

"So Indrani made up her mind, to bring about her purpose. Full of shame, she
went to Nahusha, terrible to see. When the wicked Nahusha saw her, young and
beautiful, he was delighted, and his mind was struck by desire.

---

"When he saw her, Nahusha, king of the gods, said to her, 'I am Indra of all the
three worlds, bright-smiling one. Take me for your husband, lovely lady.'

"When Nahusha said this, the goddess, true to her husband, trembled with fear
like a plantain tree in the wind. She bowed to Brahma and joined her palms over
her head. Then she said to Nahusha, the king of the gods, terrible to see, 'I
want a little time from you, lord of the gods. No one knows where Shakra is, or
what has become of him, or where he has gone. When I have found out the truth,
lord, if he is still not found, I will come to you. I tell you this truly.'

"Nahusha was pleased when Indrani said this. 'Let it be as you say, lovely one,'
said Nahusha. 'When you have found out, you must come. Remember this promise.'

"When Nahusha let her go, the good woman went out, and the ascetic went to
Brihaspati's house.

"When they heard what she had said, the gods, with Agni at their head, took
counsel together for Shakra's sake, best of kings. They went to the god of gods,
mighty Vishnu, and those skilled speakers spoke to him in great distress.
'Shakra, the lord of the hosts of gods, is overcome by the sin of killing a
brahmin. You are our refuge, lord of the gods, the first-born, the lord of the
world. You took on the form of Vishnu to protect all beings. When Vritra was
killed by your power, Vasava was seized by the sin of killing a brahmin. Best of
gods, show us the way to free him.'

"When Vishnu heard the gods, he said, 'Let Shakra sacrifice to me, and I will
make the bearer of the thunderbolt clean. When the chastiser of Paka has
worshipped me with the holy horse sacrifice, he will come back to be Indra of
the gods, with nothing to fear. And wicked Nahusha will be destroyed by his own
deeds. Bear with him for a while, gods, and do not tire.'

"When they had heard Vishnu's words, good and true and like nectar, all the
hosts of gods, with their teacher and the seers, went to the place where Shakra
was, troubled with fear. There a great horse sacrifice of great Indra was held,
to make him clean and to take away the sin of killing a brahmin. The lord of the
gods shared out the sin among the trees, the rivers, the mountains, the earth
and women, Yudhishthira. He shared it out among beings and let it go, and Vasava
was free of fever, his sin cleansed, and master of himself.

"But the slayer of Bala saw that Nahusha could not be moved from his place. He
saw that Nahusha took the strength of all beings, and was unbearable because of
his boon. So the heroic husband of Shachi vanished again. Unseen by any
creature, he went about waiting for his time.

---

"When Shakra had vanished, Shachi was full of grief. 'Oh, Shakra!' the goddess
cried, in her misery. 'If I have given gifts, if I have made offerings, if I
have pleased my elders, if there is truth in me, then let me have one husband
only. I bow to this holy, heavenly goddess Night, who has begun in the sun's
northern course. Let my wish come true.'

"Pure in body and mind, she worshipped the goddess Night. By the truth of her
faithfulness to her husband, she called up Upashruti, the goddess of the voice
that is overheard. 'Show me the place where the king of the gods is,' the
goddess said to Upashruti. 'Let truth be seen by truth.'

"Then Upashruti came to the good woman in bodily form. When Indrani saw the
goddess standing before her, young and beautiful, she was glad. She honoured her
and asked, 'I want to know who you are. Tell me, lovely one.'

"'I am Upashruti, goddess,' she said. 'I have come to you, and let you see me,
because I am pleased by your truth. You are true to your husband, and you are
disciplined in your vows and your restraint. I will show you Shakra, the god who
killed Vritra. Follow me quickly, bless you. You will see the best of gods.'

"So Indrani followed the goddess as she set out. They passed the forests of the
gods and many mountains. They crossed the Himalaya and came to its northern
side. They reached the ocean, many yojanas wide, and came to a great island
covered with trees and creepers of every kind. There she saw a heavenly lake,
full of birds of many kinds, a hundred yojanas wide and as long, and lovely. In
it were thousands of heavenly lotuses of five colours, in full flower, with the
bees humming over them.

"Upashruti split a lotus stalk and went into it, and Indrani with her. There,
inside a fibre of the stalk, she saw Shatakratu. When she saw the lord, who had
made himself very small, the goddess Upashruti took a tiny form too, and so did
Indrani.

"Indrani praised Indra with the famous deeds he had done before. As she praised
him, the god, the destroyer of cities, said to Shachi, 'Why have you come? And
how did you find me?'

"Then she told him what Nahusha had done. 'He won the lordship of Indra over the
three worlds, and he is drunk with power and full of pride. The wicked, cruel
man told me, "Wait on me," Shatakratu, and he gave me a time limit. If you do
not save me, lord, he will make me his. That is why I have come to you in my
distress, Shakra. Kill Nahusha, strong-armed one. He is cruel and set on evil.
Show yourself, slayer of daityas and danavas. Take back your strength, lord, and
rule the kingdom of the gods.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Nahusha's consecration.** Rejected lines (\*94, \*95) describe the consecration itself. In the CE the gods give him the boon (5.11.6–7) and "so he won" the kingdom (5.11.8).
* **The lotus in the lake.** A rejected line (\*102) describes a great lotus plant with a tall white stalk in the middle of the lake. Not narrated.
* **The end of Shachi's plea.** A rejected line (\*104) has her add "as you killed Vritra". Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*92, \*93, \*96–\*101, \*103) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* This chapter continues Shalya's tale to Yudhishthira. His narration stays inside double quotes, with speech in single quotes, as in the previous chapter.
* 5.11.6: the boon, that Nahusha will take the strength (*tejas*) of all who come within sight of his eyes, is kept in full. It is why Indra hides again (5.13.19).
* 5.11.9: the places are listed as the text gives them. *śvetaparvata* is "the White Mountain".
* 5.12.6: Nahusha's charge about Ahalya is his. The novel does not tell the story, which the CE only mentions here.
* 5.12.19–21: Brahma's verses on giving up a refugee are kept in full.
* 5.12.26 *bahuvighnakaraḥ kālaḥ kālaḥ kālaṃ nayiṣyati*: "Time brings many obstacles. Time will carry time along."
* 5.13.10: the gods tell Vishnu he "took on the form of Vishnu (*viṣṇutvam*) to protect all beings".
* 5.13.17–18: the sin of killing a brahmin shared out among trees, rivers, mountains, the earth and women is CE here, at the horse sacrifice. A different telling of the same sharing, earlier and with boons, is rejected at 5.9.\*75.
* 5.13.23 *pravṛttām uttarāyaṇe*: the Night "who has begun in the sun's northern course".
* 5.13.24 *upaśruti*: the goddess of the overheard word, the chance-heard voice taken as an answer. The novel explains her name once and then uses it.
* 5.13.25 *satyaṃ satyena dṛśyatām*: "Let truth be seen by truth."
* 5.14.9 *bisatantupraviṣṭaṃ*: Indra is hidden "inside a fibre of the stalk".

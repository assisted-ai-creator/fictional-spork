# Yayati's Fall

"'Then the king wanted to hold a bridegroom choice for her again," said Narada.
"He went to a hermitage where the Ganga and the Yamuna meet. Puru and Yadu set
their sister Madhavi, holding a garland, on a chariot and drove her round the
hermitage. Nagas, yakshas and men, birds, beasts and fowl, those who live in
mountains, trees and forests, all gathered there. The forest was crowded with
the lords of many peoples and lands, and ringed on every side by seers like
Brahma. But as the suitors were pointed out to her, the lovely woman passed over
all of them and chose the forest as her husband.

"'Yayati's daughter got down from the chariot, bowed to her kin, went to the
holy forest and practised austerity. She made herself light with fasts of many
kinds, with consecrations and rules, and she became one who lived like a deer.
She ate the young grass, soft and green, like shoots of beryl, bitter and sweet.
She drank the best of waters from holy streams, sweet, pure, cool and clear. In
forests full of the kings of beasts, where there were no lions, free of forest
fires, empty and deep, she wandered with the deer like a doe of the forest, and
practised great dharma, guarded by the celibate life.

"'Yayati too followed the way of the kings before him. After many thousands of
years of life he met the dharma of time. Puru and Yadu, two best of men, grew
two lines, and through them Nahusha's son was established in this world and the
next. King Yayati was honoured in heaven, like a great seer, a lord enjoying the
best fruits of heaven.

"'When many thousands of years of many virtues had passed, as he sat among the
royal seers and the great seers, Yayati, his understanding bewildered and his
mind filled with conceit, despised all men, gods and hosts of seers. Then the
god Shakra, slayer of Bala, noticed him, and all those royal seers said, "Shame!
Shame!" Looking at Nahusha's son, they began to wonder. "Who is this? Whose king
is he? How did he come to heaven? By what deed was he perfected? Where did he
heap up austerity? How is he known in heaven, and by whom?" So the kings who
lived in heaven wondered, looking at him, and asked one another about King
Yayati. The keepers of the heavenly cars in their hundreds, the guards of
heaven's gate and the keepers of the seats were asked, and they said, "We do not
know." Their knowledge was covered, and none of them knew the king. And in a
moment the king lost his splendour.

---

"'Then he was shaken from his place and fell from his seat. With a trembling
mind, overcome by the fire of grief, his garland faded and his understanding
lost, his crown and armlets fallen, reeling, all his limbs slack, his ornaments
and clothes fallen away, unseen, seeing them again and again and not seeing
them, empty, with an empty mind, he was about to fall to the earth. "What evil,
against dharma, did I think in my mind, that I have been shaken from my place?"
the king thought.

"'The kings there, the perfected ones and the apsarases, saw Yayati fallen and
without support. Then a man came, one who throws down those whose merit is
spent, and at the command of the king of the gods he said to Yayati, "You are
too drunk with pride. There is no one you do not despise. Through pride you have
lost heaven. You do not deserve it, son of a king. You are not known. Go. Fall."

"'Three times Nahusha's son said, "Let me fall among the good." As he was
falling, the best of those who have a goal thought about where he would go. At
that moment the king saw four bulls among kings in the Naimisha forest, and he
fell among them. Pratardana, Vasumanas, Shibi son of Ushinara and Ashtaka were
satisfying the lord of the gods with the Vajapeya sacrifice. Yayati smelled the
smoke of their sacrifice, which had come up to the door of heaven, and fell
towards the earth. The lord of the earth took hold of that river of smoke
joining earth and heaven, as if it were the flowing Ganga, and the king fell
among the four splendid kinsmen at their final bath, who were like guardians of
the world. The royal seer Yayati fell, his merit spent, among those four great
fires, lions among kings, like offerings poured in.

"'All the kings said to him, who was like the image of fortune, "Who are you?
Whose kinsman are you, of what land or city? Are you a yaksha, or a god, a
gandharva or a rakshasa? You do not have a man's form. What do you want?"

"'"I am Yayati, the royal seer," said Yayati. "My merit is spent, and I have
fallen from heaven. I fell among you because I thought, 'Let me fall among the
good.'"

"'"Let your wish come true, bull among men," said the kings. "Take the fruit of
all our sacrifices, and our dharma."

"'"I am not a brahmin, who lives on gifts," said Yayati. "I am a kshatriya. And
my mind does not lean to destroying the merit of others."

"'At that moment the kings saw Madhavi, who had come there in the course of her
deer's life. They greeted her and said, "Why have you come? What can we do?
Command us. We are all your sons, rich in austerity." When she heard their
words, Madhavi was overjoyed. She went to her father Yayati and bowed to him.
Seeing her sons bow their heads, the ascetic woman said, "These are your
daughter's sons, king of kings, my sons, not strangers to you. They will save
you. This was fated long ago. I am your daughter Madhavi, king, who lives like a
deer. I too have heaped up dharma. Take half of it. Since all men share in the
fruit of their offspring, king, they want daughters' sons, as you do, lord of
the earth."

"'Then all those kings bowed their heads to their mother, greeted her, and spoke
to their mother's father. With loud, matchless, loving voices filling the earth,
the kings set about saving their mother's father, fallen from heaven. Then
Galava too came there and said to the king, "Climb to heaven with an eighth part
of my austerity."

---

"'As soon as the good men recognised him, Yayati, the bull among men, took on a
heavenly form and his fever left him. He wore heavenly garlands and clothes, was
adorned with heavenly ornaments, had heavenly fragrance and virtues, and did not
touch the earth with his feet.

"'Then Vasumanas, famous in the world as a lord of giving, spoke first in a loud
voice to the king. "Whatever I have won in the world without blame from any
class, I will give that too. Be joined with it. The fruit of one who loves
giving, the fruit of one who loves patience, and the fruit of my sacrifices, be
joined with them."

"'Then Pratardana, the bull of the kshatriyas, said, "As I have always delighted
in dharma and always been devoted to war, the fame I have won in the world born
of the dharma of a kshatriya, and the fruit of the name of hero, be joined with
them."

"'Wise Shibi son of Ushinara spoke sweet words. "I have never spoken a lie, to
children or to women, in jest, in battles, in disasters, in troubles or
misfortunes. By that truth, go to the sky. As I would give up my life, my
kingdom, my work and my pleasures, king, but never truth, by that truth, go to
the sky. As dharma is pleased by my truth, as fire is pleased by my truth, and
Shakra too, by that truth, go to the sky."

"'Then the royal seer Ashtaka, son of Kaushika and Madhavi, who knew dharma,
spoke to the king who had sacrificed many hundreds of times. "I have piled up
Pundarikas, Gosavas and Vajapeyas in hundreds, lord. Take their fruit. I have no
jewels, no wealth, no other belongings that were not used in sacrifices. By that
truth, go to the sky."

"'As his daughter's sons spoke to the king, one after another, the king left the
earth and went to heaven. So all those kings together, by their good deeds,
quickly saved Yayati, who had fallen from heaven. Born in four royal lines,
adding to their families, the daughter's sons, by their own dharma done through
sacrifice and gifts, raised their wise mother's father to heaven.

"'"Rich in the virtues of a king's dharma, rich in the virtues of every dharma,"
said the kings, "we are your daughter's sons, king. Climb to heaven, lord of the
earth."

---

"'Raised to heaven by good kings who gave rich fees, Yayati took leave of his
daughter's sons and went to heaven. He was showered with a rain fragrant with
flowers of every kind, embraced by a holy wind with holy fragrance. He climbed
to the unshakeable place won by the fruit of his daughter's sons, and, gathered
up by his own deeds, he blazed with the highest splendour. The hosts of
gandharvas and apsarases sang and danced for him. He was gladly received in
heaven with the sound of drums. He was praised by the gods, royal seers and
bards of many kinds, worshipped with the finest guest offering and welcomed by
the gods.

"'When he had won the fruit of heaven, the grandfather spoke to him as he sat
content and calm, as if refreshing him with words. "You heaped up dharma of four
quarters by your deeds in the world. This world of yours is imperishable, and so
is your fame in heaven, again today, royal seer, through this good deed here.
The minds of all who live in heaven were covered with darkness, so that they did
not know you. Because they did not know you, you were thrown down. You have been
saved by your daughter's sons out of love, and you have come here. You have
reached the place won by your own deeds, unshakeable, eternal, holy, highest,
firm and imperishable."

"'"Lord, I have a doubt," said Yayati. "You should cut it, for I can ask no one
else, grandfather of the worlds. I won great fruit, grown through thousands of
years of protecting my people, by floods of sacrifices and gifts. How did it
waste away in so short a time, so that I fell? Lord, you know the eternal worlds
I won."

"'"The fruit you won," said the grandfather, "grown through thousands of years
of protecting your people, by floods of sacrifices and gifts, wasted away
through this one fault, which made you fall: pride, king of kings. The dwellers
in heaven cried shame on you. Royal seer, this world is not made eternal by
pride, nor by strength, nor by violence, nor by cunning, nor by tricks. You must
never despise the low, the high or the middle, king. For there is no one
anywhere so burnt as those burnt by pride. Those men who tell this story of
falling and rising will cross over even their misfortunes, no doubt of it."

"'"Long ago Yayati met this fault through pride," said Narada, "and Galava
through too much obstinacy, lord of the earth. Men should listen to friends who
want their good and wish them to prosper. A man should not be obstinate, for
obstinacy is the beginning of destruction. So you too, son of Gandhari, give up
pride and anger. Make peace with the Pandavas, hero. Give up your fury, king.
Whatever a man gives, king, whatever he does, whatever austerity he practises,
whatever he offers, is not destroyed or lessened. No one else enjoys it but the
doer. This great story is held to be supreme by the very learned, who are free
of anger and passion. Seeing it told in many ways in the world, one who keeps
the three aims of life in view enjoys the earth."'"

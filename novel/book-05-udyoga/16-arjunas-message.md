# Arjuna's Message

So the night passed while the king talked with Sanatsujata and wise Vidura. When
the night was over, all the kings came gladly into the hall, eager to see the
suta. Wanting to hear the words of the Parthas, full of dharma and profit, they
all went, with Dhritarashtra at their head, to the fine hall of the kings. It
was white as plaster, wide, and adorned with a golden courtyard. It shone like
the moon, and it was lovely and sprinkled with the best water. It was spread
with fine seats of gold and of wood, of stone and of ivory, well covered, with
cloths over them.

Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and Shalya, Kritavarma and Jayadratha, Ashvatthama and
Vikarna, Somadatta and Bahlika, wise Vidura and the great chariot fighter
Yuyutsu, all the heroic kings together, bull of the Bharatas, followed
Dhritarashtra into that fine hall. Duhshasana, Chitrasena, and Shakuni, son of
Subala, Durmukha, Duhsaha, Karna, Uluka and Vivimshati came in with the angry
Duryodhana, king of the Kurus, at their head, like the gods entering Shakra's
hall. As those heroes came in, with arms like iron bars, the hall shone like a
mountain cave full of lions. The great archers, who made any gathering splendid,
came into the hall and took their costly seats, shining like the sun.

When all the kings were seated, Bharata, the doorkeeper announced that the
suta's son had come. "Here is the chariot that went to the Pandavas," he said.
"Our envoy has come back quickly, with good horses from Sindhu."

Sanjaya came up quickly, jumped down from the chariot, and went in, wearing his
earrings, into the hall full of great kings.

"Kauravas, know that I went to the Pandavas and have come back," said Sanjaya.
"The Pandavas greet all the Kurus, each according to his age. The Parthas bow to
the elders, greet those of their own age as friends, and honour the young and
speak to them as their age deserves. Hear, kings, what I said to the Pandavas
when I went from here, as Dhritarashtra had instructed me."

---

"I ask you, Sanjaya, in the midst of the kings," said Dhritarashtra. "What did
Dhananjaya say, my son, the great-souled one whose spirit never fails, the
leader in battle, who cuts off the lives of the wicked?"

"Let Duryodhana listen," said Sanjaya, "to the words that Arjuna spoke, eager
for battle, with Yudhishthira's consent, in Keshava's hearing. The steady wearer
of the diadem, unafraid, knowing the strength of his arms, spoke to me eager for
battle, close to Vasudeva: 'Say this to the son of Dhritarashtra in the midst of
the Kurus. And let the kings who have been brought together to fight the
Pandavas hear it too. Make that king hear all that I have said, with his
ministers.'

"As all the gods listen to the king of the gods, thunderbolt in hand, so the
Pandavas and the Srinjayas listened to the weighty words the wearer of the
diadem spoke. And this is what Arjuna said, eager for battle, with the Gandiva
in his hand and his eyes red as lotuses.

"'If the son of Dhritarashtra will not give up the kingdom of King Yudhishthira,
of Ajamidha's line, then surely the sons of Dhritarashtra have some evil deed,
done before, that they have not yet paid for. For their war will be with
Bhimasena and Arjuna, with the Ashvins' two sons and with Vasudeva. It will be
with Shini's grandson, weapons in hand, with Dhrishtadyumna and Shikhandi, and
with Yudhishthira, who is like Indra, and who could burn earth and heaven with
an evil thought. If the son of Dhritarashtra thinks of fighting them, then the
Pandavas' whole purpose is achieved. Do not make peace for the Pandavas' sake.
Come to war, if that is what you think.

"'The Pandava, who walks in dharma, lay on a bed of pain in the forest when he
was exiled. A bed more painful and more wretched is waiting for the son of
Dhritarashtra, the last bed of all, when his life is gone. The wicked son of
Dhritarashtra, with his unjust ways, has trampled on the Kurus and the sons of
Pandu. They are men of modesty and wisdom, of austerity and self-control, of
anger held by the guard of dharma, and of wealth. Our king, met with trickery,
answered with honesty, with austerity and self-control, with dharma's guard and
strength. He spoke the truth, with love, even when he was met with lies. He
endured, and he suffered beyond measure.

"'The eldest Pandava, whose self is disciplined, has held a terrible anger for
years. When he lets it loose on the Kurus, with his heart stirred, then the son
of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. As a blazing fire burns up dry grass in
the hot season, so Yudhishthira, burning with anger, will burn up the army of
Dhritarashtra's son by looking at it.

"'He will see Bhimasena in the battle, mace in hand, spitting the poison of his
anger, the unbearable Pandava of terrible speed. Then the son of Dhritarashtra
will repent the war. Bhima, terrible to look at, the killer, mace in hand, will
come at the sons of Dhritarashtra and fall on them like a great lion among
cattle. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. He is fearless in
great danger, skilled in weapons, the crusher of enemy armies. He could meet the
hosts of chariots with a single chariot and strike down the crowds of foot
soldiers with his mace. He will crush many hosts by force and hurl back the army
of Dhritarashtra's son, like a hero cutting a forest with an axe. Then the son
of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. The son of Dhritarashtra will see his
great flood of an army overthrown, like a village of straw burned by fire, like
ripe grain burned by lightning. Its heroes will be killed, and it will turn
away, stricken with fear. Its fighters, mostly without courage, will flee,
burned by Bhimasena with the flame of his weapons. Then the son of Dhritarashtra
will repent the war.

"'Nakula, the finest of chariot fighters, a skilled and brilliant fighter, will
draw his arrows by the hundred from his quiver with his right hand. Then the son
of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. Nakula, used to comfort, lay so long on a
bed of pain in the forest. He will breathe hard like an angry snake. Then the
son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war.

"'The heroes are ready to give their lives, sent by the Dharma King to fight the
kings. When the son of Dhritarashtra sees them charging the army in their bright
chariots, he will repent afterwards. The Kaurava will see the five brave boys,
skilled in weapons, who do not look like boys, charging the Kekayas without a
thought for their lives. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war.

"'Sahadeva, the fierce one, will stand in his chariot, whose axles do not creak,
set with golden stars and drawn by well-trained horses. He will hurl away the
heads of kings with his floods of arrows. The son of Dhritarashtra will see him
in his chariot, skilled in weapons, wheeling in the battle as the great terror
begins, falling on every quarter. Then he will repent the war. Modest, skilful,
truthful, mighty, full of every virtue, swift to act, the fierce Sahadeva will
come at the son of Gandhari in the tumult and hurl his men aside.

"'He will see the sons of Draupadi coming like snakes with terrible poison,
great archers, heroes skilled in weapons and in chariot fighting. Then the son
of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. Abhimanyu, killer of enemy heroes, as
skilled in weapons as Krishna, will dive into the enemy, raining arrows on them
like a cloud. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. He will see
Subhadra's son, a boy with a man's courage, like Indra, skilled in weapons,
falling on the enemy army like Death. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent
the war. The Prabhadrakas, young and very swift, skilled, with the courage of
lions, will hurl back the sons of Dhritarashtra and their armies. Then the son
of Dhritarashtra will repent the war.

"'He will see the two old men, Virata and Drupada, great chariot fighters, each
with his own army, coming at the sons of Dhritarashtra. Then the son of
Dhritarashtra will repent the war. Drupada, skilled in weapons, standing in his
chariot, will pick out the heads of young men in the battle. In his anger he
will cut them off with arrows from his bow. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will
repent the war. Virata, killer of enemy heroes, will go into the heart of the
enemy army with the Matsyas, who are not cruel. Then the son of Dhritarashtra
will repent the war. He will see the eldest of the Matsyas, Virata's son, a
noble chariot fighter, armed at the front for the Pandavas' sake. Then the son
of Dhritarashtra will repent the war.

"'When the best of the Kurus, Shantanu's son, is killed in battle by Shikhandi,
our enemies will not stand. There is no doubt of it. I tell you this truly.
Shikhandi, in his armoured chariot, will pick out the chariot fighters and drive
at Bhishma, crushing the hosts of chariots with his heavenly horses. Then the
son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war.

"'Wise Drona taught the secret weapon to Dhrishtadyumna. When the son of
Dhritarashtra sees him shining at the head of the Srinjaya army, he will repent.
That commander, whose strength cannot be measured, will overwhelm the sons of
Dhritarashtra with his arrows and ride at Drona in battle, bearing down his
enemies. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. He is modest, wise,
strong, proud and glorious, the chief of the Somakas. No enemies could withstand
those whose leader is the lion of the Vrishnis.

"'And say this too: "Do not choose him." For we have chosen Shini's grandson,
Satyaki, mighty, fearless and skilled in weapons. He is the ally without an
equal in the world in battle, standing in his chariot. The lord of the Shinis,
sent by me, will rain arrows on the enemy like a cloud and cover the fighters
with a net of arrows. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. When
that great-souled one, long-armed, with his strong bow, makes up his mind to
fight, his enemies shrink from him. They shrink as cattle do when they catch the
smell of a lion, or as from fire. Long-armed, great-souled, with his strong bow,
he could split mountains and destroy all the worlds. Skilled in weapons, clever
and quick of hand, he shines like the sun standing in the sky. The Yadava, the
lion of the Vrishnis, has a wonderful, subtle and well-made mastery of weapons.
Satyaki has every quality of the mastery they call the best. Suyodhana will see
Madhava Satyaki's golden chariot yoked to four white horses in the battle. Then
that undisciplined fool will repent.

"'He will see my fierce chariot, shining with gold and jewels, drawn by white
horses, with the monkey banner, driven by Keshava in the battle. Then that
undisciplined fool will repent. I will shake the Gandiva in the great battle,
and the fool will hear the fierce slap of the bowstring on my palm, as loud as
the crash of the thunderbolt. Then the foolish son of Dhritarashtra, with his
bad mind and bad allies, will repent the war. He will see his army broken, dark
with the rain of arrows, like a herd of cattle at the front of the battle.

"'I will shoot arrows that cut bone and pierce the vitals, killing thousands of
enemies where the armies meet, like lightning leaping from a cloud. Then the son
of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. He will see the hosts of sharp arrows
falling from the Gandiva's string, carrying off elephants, horses and armoured
men. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. The fool will see the
enemy's arrows, once shot, carried back by my arrows, and cut across by my
razor-headed shafts. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war. My
broad-headed arrows, loosed from my arms, will cut off the heads of young men,
like birds taking fruit from the top of a tree. Then the son of Dhritarashtra
will repent the war. He will see the warriors falling from their chariots, from
their great elephants and from their horses, killed by arrows and thrown down in
the arena. Then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war.

"'I will drive back the enemy with blazing showers of arrows, the hosts of foot
soldiers and the hosts of chariots on every side, like Time with his mouth open
and his arrows drawn. Then the fool will repent. My chariot will rush in every
direction, and he will see his army bewildered, covered in dust and cut down by
the Gandiva. Then the fool will repent afterwards. Duryodhana will see his whole
army not knowing where to turn, its limbs cut, senseless. Its horses, heroes,
leaders, kings and elephants will be killed. It will be thirsty, its mounts
tired, in terror, crying out in pain, being killed and killed. It will be strewn
with hair, bones and skulls, like the unfinished work of the Creator. Then the
fool will see it and repent.

"'The son of Dhritarashtra will see the Gandiva in my chariot, and Vasudeva, and
the heavenly conch Panchajanya, and the horses. He will see the two quivers that
never empty, and my conch Devadatta, and me, all together in the battle. I will
burn up the Kauravas like fire, rolling up the hosts of robbers together, and
bringing in another age at the end of an age. Then Dhritarashtra and his sons
will repent. The foolish son of Dhritarashtra will fall from his power, with his
brothers, his sons and his army, angry and small-minded. He will tremble when
the end of his pride is brought about, and he will repent afterwards.

"'One morning, when I had said my prayers, a brahmin spoke to me pleasantly at
the water's edge. "Partha, you have a hard task to do. Left-handed Archer, you
must fight your enemies. Either Indra, with his bay horses and the thunderbolt
in his hand, will go before you in battle, killing your enemies. Or Krishna,
Vasudeva, will guard you from behind in his chariot drawn by Sugriva and the
others." And I chose Vasudeva over great Indra, thunderbolt in hand, as my ally
in this war. I have won Krishna for the killing of the robbers, and I think this
was arranged for me by the gods.

"'The man whose victory Krishna wishes for, even in his mind and without
fighting, will surely overcome all his enemies, even the gods with Indra. There
is no need to think of men. Krishna Vasudeva is full of power, a hero beyond
heroes. The man who wants to beat him in battle is like one who wants to swim
across the ocean with his arms, the great sea whose waters cannot be measured.
He is like one who wants to split with his palm a white mountain, a great mass
of rock. His hand will be torn, nails and all, and he will do nothing to the
mountain. He would put out a blazing fire with his arms, hold back the moon and
the sun, and seize the nectar of the gods by force, the man who wants to beat
Vasudeva in battle.

"'This is the one who crushed the kings' country by force with a single chariot.
He carried off Rukmini of the Bhojas to be his wife, blazing with glory, and of
her the great son of Rukmini was born. This is the one who crushed the Gandharas
by force, beat all the sons of Nagnajit, and freed Sudarshana, the pride of the
gods, as he lay bound and crying out. This is the one who struck down the Pandya
king at Kavata, and crushed the Kalingas at Dantakura. Burned by him, the city
of Varanasi was left without a protector for many years. Ekalavya, king of the
Nishadas, whom others thought unbeatable in battle, lies dead, killed by
Krishna, like Jambha dashed against a rock. With Baladeva beside him he struck
down Ugrasena's wicked son, who tormented the Vrishnis and Andhakas in their
midst. When he had killed him he gave the kingdom to Ugrasena.

"'This is the one who fought Saubha, standing in the sky, and the lord of the
Shalvas, terrible with his tricks. He caught the shataghni with his arms at the
gate of Saubha. What mortal could stand against him? There was a stronghold
called Pragjyotisha, a terrible city of the asuras that could not be attacked.
There mighty Naraka, the son of the Earth, carried off Aditi's beautiful
jewelled earrings. The gods with Shakra came together to take them back, and
they were afraid of him, and could not. They saw Keshava's courage, his strength
and his weapon that nothing can stop, and they knew his nature. So they set
Krishna to kill the robbers, and Vasudeva, lord of all powers of success, took
on that hard task. At Nirmochana the hero killed six thousand and cut through
the razor-edged nooses at a stroke. He killed Mura, struck down the host of
rakshasas, and went on to Nirmochana. There mighty Naraka fought Vishnu, whose
strength is greater still. He lies dead, killed by Krishna, like a karnikara
tree torn up by the wind. Wise Krishna brought back the earrings, and killed
Naraka, the son of the Earth, and Mura. Then he came home, full of fortune and
fame, his power without equal. There the gods gave him boons, when they saw the
terrible deed he had done in battle: "You will never tire when you fight. You
will move through the sky and the waters. Weapons will not pierce your body."
And so Krishna had all he wanted. Such is Vasudeva, immeasurable and mighty,
always rich in every virtue.

"'And the son of Dhritarashtra hopes to overcome him by force, him whom no one
can withstand, Vishnu of endless power. When the wicked one plots against him,
he bears that too, for our sake. The son of Dhritarashtra thinks that by
stirring up a quarrel he can take away what is the Pandavas' own, what has come
down to Krishna and to me. He will find out when he comes to the battle.

"'I will bow to King Bhishma, Shantanu's son, and to Drona with his son, and to
Sharadvat's son, who has no rival. Then I will fight, wanting the kingdom. I
think dharma has marked out a weapon for any man who fights the Pandavas, who
walk in dharma. The sons of Pandu were beaten at a false game by cruel men. For
twelve years they suffered hardship in the forest, and a long year in hiding.
The sons of Dhritarashtra, in their high place, cannot bear, for no reason, that
the Pandavas live. If they beat us in battle, even with the gods and Indra for
their allies, then surely what is against dharma, once done, is heavier than
dharma, and nothing good is ever done.

"'Whether or not a man is bound by his deeds, whether or not he thinks us his
betters, I hope, with Vasudeva beside me, to kill Duryodhana and all who hold
with him. Whether deeds bind men or not, whether a man has a work of his own or
not, look at both: the defeat of Dhritarashtra's son is sure and right. Kurus, I
tell you to your faces: if they fight, the sons of Dhritarashtra are no more. If
the Kurus hope to live, they must not fight. In a fight, no one here is left.
When I have killed the sons of Dhritarashtra, with Karna, I will win the whole
kingdom of the Kurus. Do whatever you have to do, each of you, and enjoy your
dear wives and your sons.

"'We have old brahmins with us, very learned, of good character and good family.
They reckon the years and know the stars, and are sure about the conjunctions of
the constellations. They foretell, high and low, the secret workings of fate,
from heavenly questions, the circles of the beasts, and the hours. They foretell
great destruction for the Kurus and the Srinjayas, and victory for the Pandavas.
And Ajatashatru thinks the same. He sees his purpose achieved, the subduing of
his enemies. And Janardana, the lion of the Vrishnis, who knows what is hidden,
has no doubt of it.

"'And I know the shape of what is to come. I see it myself with my
understanding, and I am not careless. My sight, which is old, does not waver. If
the sons of Dhritarashtra fight, they are no more. My bow, the Gandiva, yawns
without being touched. My bowstring trembles without being touched. My arrows,
again and again, want to fly from the mouth of my quiver. My bright sword comes
out of its sheath, as a snake leaves its old skin. On my banner voices speak,
terrible to hear: "When will your chariot be yoked, wearer of the diadem?" At
night packs of jackals howl, and rakshasas fall from the sky. When they see my
chariot yoked to white horses, deer, jackals, peacocks and crows, vultures,
cranes and hyenas, and birds of golden feathers, fall in behind.

"'I alone, raining arrows, could send all the kings and all their fighters to
the world of death. I will take up my weapons one after another, like a blazing
fire in a thicket in the hot season. I will loose them swiftly, set on killing:
the terrible Sthunakarna, the Pashupata, the brahma weapon, and the one Shakra
knew. I will leave nothing of those creatures alive. Then I will find peace.
This is my highest purpose, and it is firm. Tell them this, son of Gavalgana.

"'Look at the folly of the son of Dhritarashtra. With those ministers he thinks
he can start a quarrel, even with the gods and Indra as our allies, as he has
always said. Let it be as old Bhishma, Shantanu's son, and Kripa, Drona and his
son, and wise Vidura, all say. Let all the Kurus live long.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Karna in the message.** A rejected passage (\*307) has Arjuna tell Sanjaya to speak "in the hearing of that foul-mouthed, wicked suta's son, who always longs to fight me". The CE message names Karna only at its end (5.47.91).
* **Bhima and the elephants.** A rejected passage (\*312) adds Bhima at the head of the army and elephants falling like mountain peaks, "blood pouring from their broken temples". Not narrated.
* **Satyaki's description.** A rejected passage (\*313) adds that Satyaki is broad-chested and his weapon "the length of a palm tree". Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*303–\*306, \*308–\*311, \*314–\*316) add details or repeat verses. Not narrated.
* **Ekalavya killed by Krishna** (5.47.71) is CE. It is told again at 16.7.10, and at 7.156.17–20 Krishna says Drona took the thumb for Arjuna's sake. See ledger L-175.

**Choices.**

* 5.47.3 *upahvare vāsudevasya*: "close to Vasudeva".
* 5.47.7 *yo 'padhyānān nirdahed gāṃ divaṃ ca*: Yudhishthira "could burn earth and heaven with an evil thought".
* 5.47.10–11: two compressed verses. The novel reads the first as Duryodhana trampling on the Kurus and Pandavas, and the second as Yudhishthira's patience, following Ganguli.
* The refrain *tadā yuddhaṃ dhārtarāṣṭro 'nvatapsyat*, "then the son of Dhritarashtra will repent the war", is kept wherever the text has it.
* 5.47.23: the "five brave boys" are the five Kekaya brothers who fight for the Pandavas. Here they charge "the Kekayas", their kin on the other side (5.22.19, 5.19.25).
* 5.47.30: the Prabhadrakas are a people of the Panchalas.
* 5.47.37: the "secret weapon" Drona taught Dhrishtadyumna is not named.
* 5.47.40: "Do not choose him" (*mā pravṛṇīṣva*): Duryodhana should not hope to win Satyaki.
* 5.47.52 *vipāṭhā*: "broad-headed arrows".
* 5.47.57 *prajāpateḥ karma yathārdhaniṣṭhitam*: "like the unfinished work of the Creator".
* 5.47.61–63: the brahmin's prophecy and Arjuna's choice of Krishna over Indra are CE.
* 5.47.69: *sudarśanīyaṃ devatānāṃ lalāmam* is read, with Ganguli, as a king called Sudarshana, "the pride of the gods".
* 5.47.70 *kavāṭe*: "at Kavata", read as a place. Ganguli takes it as "by striking his breast".
* 5.47.71: Jambha is the asura killed by Indra.
* 5.47.72: "Ugrasena's wicked son" is Kamsa.
* 5.47.73: the shataghni, the "hundred-killer", is a heavy missile.
* 5.47.77: Nirmochana is named twice in the verse, as a place of battle and a city.
* 5.47.83 *paryāgataṃ mama kṛṣṇasya caiva*: difficult. Ganguli reads it as Duryodhana trying to divide Krishna and Arjuna. The novel reads it as "what has come down to Krishna and to me".
* 5.47.85 *dharmeṇāstraṃ niyataṃ tasya manye*: "I think dharma has marked out a weapon for" him.
* 5.47.88–89: two difficult verses about deeds and fate. The novel keeps both "whether or not" clauses.
* 5.47.93 *mṛgacakrāḥ*: "the circles of the beasts", a form of divination.
* 5.47.100: Sthunakarna, Pashupata and the brahma weapon are named weapons. "The one Shakra knew" is Indra's own.

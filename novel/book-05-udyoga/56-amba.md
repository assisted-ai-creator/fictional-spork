# Amba

"'Why, best of the Bharatas, would you not kill Shikhandi,'" said Duryodhana,
"'even if you saw him in battle with his arrow raised, coming at you to kill?
Before, strong-armed son of Ganga, you said, "I will kill the Pandavas with the
Somakas." Tell me that, grandfather.'

"'Listen, Duryodhana, with these lords of the earth,'" said Bhishma, "'to the
story of why I would not kill Shikhandi if I saw him in battle. My father, the
great king Shantanu, bull of the Bharatas, whose soul was dharma, reached his
appointed end in time, bull among men. Then, best of the Bharatas, keeping my
vow, I consecrated my brother Chitrangada in the great kingship. When he met his
death, I followed Satyavati's wish and consecrated Vichitravirya as king by the
rules. Consecrated by me by dharma, though he was the younger, king of kings,
Vichitravirya, whose soul was dharma, looked to me. Wanting to arrange his
marriage, my son, I set my mind on it, thinking it should be from a fitting
family.

"'Then I heard, strong-armed one, that there were three girls at a bridegroom
choice, all of matchless beauty, daughters of the king of Kashi: Amba, Ambika,
and Ambalika besides. The kings of the earth had been invited, bull of the
Bharatas. Amba was the eldest of them, Ambika the middle one, and the princess
Ambalika the youngest, king of kings. I went to the city of the lord of Kashi
with a single chariot. There I saw the three girls, strong-armed one, well
adorned, and the kings of the earth gathered there, lord of the earth. Then I
challenged all those kings standing ready for battle and had the girls get up
into my chariot, bull of the Bharatas. Knowing they were to be won by courage, I
put them in my chariot and said again and again to all the kings gathered there,
"Bhishma, Shantanu's son, is carrying off the girls. Strive with all your power,
all you kings, to set them free. For I am taking them by force, lords of men,
while you look on."

"'Then those lords of the earth sprang up with weapons raised. "Yoke! Yoke!"
they cried in anger, and urged on their charioteers. The lords of the earth
sprang up with weapons raised, in chariots like clouds, on elephants, the
elephant fighters, and on sturdy horses. Then all those lords of the earth
surrounded me on every side with a great mass of chariots, lord of the people. I
held them back with a great rain of arrows and beat all the kings, as the king
of the gods beat the danavas. With a single arrow each I brought down to the
ground their bright banners, worked with gold, as they came on. Laughing, I
brought down their horses, their elephants and their charioteers in battle with
blazing arrows, bull among men. They turned back, broken, when they saw my
lightness of hand. Then, having beaten the kings, I came to Hastinapura. So I
brought those girls for my brother's sake, Bharata, and told Satyavati of that
deed, strong-armed one.

---

"'Then, best of the Bharatas, I went to my mother, the mother of heroes, the
fisherman's daughter, clasped her feet and said, "These daughters of the lord of
Kashi I have won as the price of courage for Vichitravirya, by beating the
kings." Then, king, with tears in her eyes, Satyavati kissed my head and said
gladly, "Good fortune, my son. You have won."

"'When the wedding was at hand, with Satyavati's consent, the eldest daughter of
the lord of Kashi said this shyly. "Bhishma, you know dharma and are skilled in
every science. When you have heard my words, which are in keeping with dharma,
you should do as I ask. In my mind I chose the lord of the Shalvas as my husband
before, and he chose me before, in secret, without my father's knowledge. How
could you, a Kaurava above all, having studied the science, keep me in your
house, king, when I love another, Bhishma? Think this over and decide it in your
mind, bull of the Bharatas, and then do what is fitting for you, strong-armed
one. The king of the Shalvas is surely waiting for me, lord of the people. Have
pity on me, strong-armed one, best of those who uphold dharma. For we have
heard, hero, that you are true to your vows on earth."

"'Then I took leave of Kali Satyavati, and of the counsellors, the twice-born
and the priests, and gave the eldest girl, Amba, leave to go, lord of men. With
leave given, the girl went to the city of the lord of the Shalvas, guarded by
old twice-born men and followed by her nurse. She crossed that road and reached
the lord of men. When she reached King Shalva, she said, "I have come for you,
strong-armed one of great splendour."

"'The lord of the Shalvas said to her, as if smiling, lord of the people, "I do
not want you as my wife, lovely woman, since you were another's before. Go back
there, good lady, to the Bharata. I do not want you, whom Bhishma took by force.
For Bhishma beat you and took you away, and you were glad of it, when he had
seized you in a great battle and beaten the lords of the earth. I do not want
you as a wife, lovely woman, when you were another's before. How could a king
like me, who knows what is to be known and teaches dharma to others, take into
his house a woman who was another's? Go where you please, good lady. Do not let
this time pass for you."

"'Amba, tormented by the arrows of the bodiless god, said to him, king, "Do not
say so, lord of the earth. It is not so at all. I was not glad when Bhishma took
me, slayer of enemies. I was taken by force, weeping, when he had driven off the
lords of the earth. Love me, lord of the Shalvas, a young girl devoted to you
and without fault. For to abandon those who are devoted is not praised in any
dharma. I took leave of Ganga's son, who never turns back in battle, and with
his leave I came to your house. That strong-armed Bhishma does not want me, lord
of the people. I have heard that Bhishma's venture was for his brother's sake.
My two sisters who were taken, Ambika and Ambalika, Ganga's son gave to his
younger brother Vichitravirya, king. As I think of no other man at all but you,
lord of the Shalvas, tiger among men, so I touch my head in oath. I have not
come to you as one who was another's before, king of kings. I speak the truth,
Shalva, and I swear by my truth. Love me, large-eyed one, a girl who has come to
you herself, who was no one's before, king of kings, longing for your favour."

"'But Shalva cast off the daughter of the lord of Kashi as she spoke, best of
the Bharatas, as a snake casts off its old skin. Begged with many words like
this, sinless one, the lord of the Shalvas did not believe the girl, bull of the
Bharatas. Then the eldest daughter of the lord of Kashi, filled with anger,
spoke with tears in her eyes and a voice choked with weeping. "Cast off by you,
lord of the people, wherever I go, may the good be my refuge, as I have spoken
the truth." But as she spoke like this, the cruel king of the Shalvas cast her
off, Kauravya, as she wept piteously. "Go! Go!" Shalva said to her again and
again. "I am afraid of Bhishma, lovely woman, and you belong to Bhishma." Spoken
to like this by short-sighted Shalva, she went out of the city, miserable,
weeping like an osprey.'"

**Divergences.**

* **The abduction.** Book 1 tells it more briefly (1.96). Here Bhishma tells it himself, and his account is kept as he gives it.
* **Amba and Shalva.** In the CE Amba says she and Shalva had chosen each other in secret (5.171.6). Shalva refuses her as a woman Bhishma has taken (5.172.4–7, 22). Ledger L-182.
* Rejected lines (\*570–\*572) add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.170.5: Bhishma says he consecrated Chitrangada, and on his death Vichitravirya.
* 5.170.13 *vīryaśulkāḥ*: the girls were "to be won by courage".
* 5.171.1 *dāśeyī*: Satyavati, "the fisherman's daughter".
* 5.172.8 *anaṅgaśarapīḍitā*: "tormented by the arrows of the bodiless god", Kama.
* 5.172.14 *mūrdhānam ālabhe*: "I touch my head in oath".
* 5.172.23 *kurarī*: "an osprey", a bird whose cry is a byword for grief.

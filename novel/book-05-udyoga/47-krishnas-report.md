# Krishna's Report

The tamer of enemies came from Hastinapura to Upaplavya, and Keshava told the
Pandavas everything just as it had happened. When they had talked for a long
time and taken counsel again and again, Shauri went to his own lodging to rest.
The five Pandava brothers sent away all the kings, with Virata at their head.
When the sun had set they performed the evening worship, thinking of him, their
minds on him alone. Then they sent for Krishna the Dasharha and took counsel
again.

"Lotus-eyed one," said Yudhishthira, "you went to the City of the Elephant. What
did you say to Dhritarashtra's son in the assembly? Tell us that."

"I went to the City of the Elephant," said Vasudeva, "and in the assembly I told
Dhritarashtra's son what was true, wholesome and good, and the fool does not
take it."

"When he went off the path," said Yudhishthira, "what did the grandfather, the
eldest of the Kurus, say to impatient Duryodhana, Hrishikesha? Or what did the
teacher, Bharadvaja's son, say, strong-armed one? What did our younger father,
the steward, the best of those who uphold dharma, burning with grief for his
sons, say to Dhritarashtra's son? And what did all the kings sitting in the
assembly say? Tell me truly, Janardana. You have told me all that the two chief
Kurus said to that fool, overcome by desire and greed, who thinks himself wise.
Those unpleasant words do not stay in my heart, Keshava. I want to hear their
words, Govinda, lord. Act so that the time does not pass, my friend. You are our
refuge, Krishna, our protector and our teacher."

"Listen, king," said Vasudeva, "to what was said to King Suyodhana in the middle
of the Kurus in the assembly, king of kings. Hear it from me. When I had made my
speech heard, Dhritarashtra's son laughed. Then Bhishma, very angry, said this.

"'Duryodhana, hear what I say to you for the sake of the family, and when you
have heard it, tiger among kings, do what is good for your own family. My
father, king, was Shantanu, famous in the world, my son. I was his only son, the
best of those who have sons. The thought came to him, "How can there be a second
son?" The wise say a man with one son has no son. "The family must not be cut
off. How can its fame be spread?" Knowing what he wanted, I brought Kali to be
my mother. For my father's sake and the family's I made a vow that was hard to
keep: to be no king, and to live chaste, as you well know. I live by it still,
keeping my vow. Of her was born strong-armed, glorious Vichitravirya, whose soul
was dharma, the upholder of the Kuru family, my younger brother, a king. When my
father had gone to heaven, I set Vichitravirya on his own throne as king, and I
became his servant and stood below him. I brought him fitting wives, king of
kings, after I had conquered a host of kings. You have often heard of that too.
Then I fought a duel with Rama in battle. For fear of Rama he was driven out by
these citizens, and, too devoted to his wives, he fell ill with consumption.

"'When the kingdom had no king and the lord of the gods sent no rain, the
people, tormented by fear of hunger, ran to me. "All the people are wasting
away," they said. "Be king, for our good. Drive away the plagues, bless you, who
make Shantanu's family grow. All your people are tormented by terrible diseases,
son of Ganga, and few are left. You should save them. Drive away the diseases,
hero, and protect the people by dharma. While you live, do not let the kingdom
go to ruin."

"'My mind was not shaken, though the people cried out, for I was keeping my vow
and remembering good conduct. Then the citizens, great king, my lovely mother
Kali, the servants, priests and teachers, and brahmins of great learning, deeply
troubled, kept saying to me, "Be king. The kingdom Pratipa protected will perish
when it comes to you. So be king, wise one, for our good." When they said this,
I joined my hands, grieved and deeply troubled, and told them of my vow, out of
respect for my father, my son: for the family's sake I would be chaste and no
king. I said it again and again. Then I joined my hands and begged my mother
again and again, king. "Mother, I was born to Shantanu to uphold the line of the
Kurus. I cannot make my vow false. Above all, do not yoke me to this burden for
your own sake. I am your servant and your slave, mother, you who love your son."

"'When I had won over my mother and the people, I asked the great sage Vyasa for
my brother's wives. Together with my mother I won the seer's favour and asked
him for offspring, great king, and he granted it. He fathered three sons then,
best of the Bharatas. Your father was blind, lacking a sense, and so he was not
king. Great Pandu, famous in the world, became king. He was king, and his sons
are the heirs to their father's inheritance. Do not quarrel, my son. Let half
the kingdom be given. While I live, what man here can rule the kingdom? Do not
despise my words. I always want peace among you. I make no difference, my son,
between you and them, king. This is the view of your father, of Gandhari and of
Vidura. If the words of the old should be heard, do not mistrust mine. Do not
destroy everything, yourself and the earth too.'

---

"When Bhishma had spoken," said Vasudeva, "Drona, a master of speech, said this
to Duryodhana in the middle of the kings, bless you.

"'Just as Pratipa's son Shantanu stood up for the family, my son, so Devavrata
Bhishma stood firm for the family. Then King Pandu, true to his word, master of
his senses, king of the Kurus, whose soul was dharma, of good vows and steady
mind, gave the kingdom to wise Dhritarashtra, his elder, and to the steward, his
younger, he who made the Kuru line grow. When he had set the unfallen
Dhritarashtra on the throne, the Kaurava went to the forest with his two wives,
sinless one. Vidura stood below and served like a humble man, like a servant,
the tiger among men, lifting the yak-tail fan. Then all the people, my son,
accepted King Dhritarashtra by rule, as they had King Pandu. Leaving the kingdom
to Dhritarashtra and Vidura, Pandu, conqueror of enemy cities, roamed the whole
earth. Vidura, true to his promise, saw to raising the treasury, to gifts, to
watching over the servants and to supporting everyone. Bhishma of great energy,
conqueror of enemy cities, saw to peace and war and to the king's service.
Mighty King Dhritarashtra sat on the throne, always attended by great Vidura.

"'How can you, born in his family, set your mind on breaking the family? Join
with your brothers and enjoy your pleasures, lord of the people. I do not speak
out of meanness, nor at all for gain. I eat what Bhishma gives me, not what you
give, best of kings. I will not look to you for a living, lord of the people.
Where Bhishma is, there is Drona. Do what Bhishma says. Let half the kingdom be
given to Pandu's sons, tamer of enemies. I have always been an equal teacher to
you and to them, my son. As Ashvatthama is to me, so is the one with white
horses. What use is much talk? Where dharma is, there is victory.'

"When Drona of boundless energy had said this, great king, Vidura, true to his
promise, who knew dharma, spoke, turning and looking at his father's face.

"'Devavrata, listen to these words of mine. The Kaurava line was lost, and you
raised it again. You ignore my words when I lament. Who is this Duryodhana, a
disgrace to this family, whose mind you follow, overcome by greed, ignoble,
ungrateful, his mind struck by greed? He breaks the teaching of his father, who
sees dharma and purpose. These Kurus are perishing because of what Duryodhana
has done. Act, great king, so that they do not perish. Do not destroy me and
Dhritarashtra, whom you made before, glorious one, as a painter destroys his
picture, or as Prajapati makes creatures and then takes them back. Do not look
on, strong-armed one, as you watch the destruction of the family. Or if your
mind is lost today, now that destruction is at hand, go to the forest with me
and with Dhritarashtra. Or bind Dhritarashtra's evil-minded son, whose wisdom is
trickery, and let this kingdom be well ruled today, protected by the Pandavas.
Be gracious, tiger among kings. Great destruction is seen, of the Pandavas, of
the Kurus, and of kings of boundless energy.'

"When he had said this, Vidura fell silent, sad at heart, brooding and sighing
again and again.

"Then the daughter of King Subala, afraid that the family would be destroyed,
spoke words full of dharma and purpose in anger to her son Duryodhana,
evil-minded and cruel, in the presence of the kings.

"'Let the kings who have entered the royal hall, and the brahmin seers and the
other members of the assembly, hear. I will tell of your offence, you wicked
man, and of your counsellors and your following. The kingdom of the Kurus is
enjoyed in turn. That is our family's dharma, come down in order. You, with your
evil mind and your cruel deeds, are destroying the Kuru kingdom by your wrong
policy. Wise Dhritarashtra is on the throne, and his younger brother is
far-seeing Vidura. How can you pass over these two and want kingship today,
Duryodhana, in your folly? The king and the steward, great as they are, would be
under Bhishma while he stands. But this great man, the best of men, born of the
river, does not want the kingdom, because he knows dharma.

"'This unassailable kingdom is Pandu's. Today his sons are its masters, and no
others. This whole kingdom is the Pandavas', from their grandfather, passing to
sons and grandsons. Whatever great Devavrata, the chief of the Kurus, true to
his word and wise, says, all that must be accepted by us, without harming
dharma, as we keep our own dharma. And with the leave of that man of great vows,
the king should say what Vidura says, and it should be done by friends appointed
to it, putting dharma first, for a long time to come. Let Yudhishthira, the son
of Dharma, rule this kingdom of the Kurus that came to him by right, urged on by
King Dhritarashtra and honoured by Shantanu's son.'

---

"When Gandhari had said this," said Vasudeva, "Dhritarashtra, lord of the
people, said this to Duryodhana among the kings, lord of the people.

"'Duryodhana, hear what I will say to you, my son. If you have respect for your
father, do it, bless you. Soma Prajapati long ago made the Kuru line grow. Sixth
from Soma was Yayati, Nahusha's son. He had five sons, the best of royal seers.
Of them glorious Yadu was the eldest and their lord, and Puru, who made our line
grow, was the youngest. He was born to Sharmishtha, daughter of Vrishaparvan.
Yadu, best of the Bharatas, was Devayani's son, my son, the daughter's son of
Shukra Kavya of boundless energy. He founded the Yadavas, strong and honoured
for his courage. But he was full of pride and dull of mind, and he despised the
kshatriyas. Bewildered by pride in his strength, he did not keep to his father's
teaching. Unconquered, he despised his father and his brothers. On the
four-cornered earth Yadu alone was strong. He brought the kings under his power
and lived in the City of the Elephant. His father Yayati, Nahusha's son, was
furious and cursed his son, Gandhari's son, and put him out of the kingdom. And
in his anger Yayati cursed those sons of his too, the brothers who followed
Yadu, proud of his strength. Then the best of kings set his youngest son Puru on
the throne, who was obedient and did his will. So the eldest, if he is arrogant,
does not come to the kingdom, and the younger come to the kingdom by serving
their elders.

"'In the same way my father's grandfather Pratipa, who knew every dharma, ruled
the earth, famous in the three worlds. While that lion among kings ruled the
kingdom by dharma, three famous sons like gods were born to him. Devapi was the
eldest, then Bahlika, and third, my son, steadfast Shantanu, my grandfather.
Devapi, the best of kings, of great energy, had a disease of the skin. He was
devoted to dharma, truthful and eager to serve his father. The townsmen and
country people approved him, and the good honoured him. Devapi won the hearts of
all, young and old. He was wise and true to his word, devoted to the good of all
creatures, keeping to his father's teaching and to the brahmins'. He was the
dear brother of Bahlika and of great Shantanu, and the brotherhood of those
great men was very close.

"'Then, in the course of time, the old king, the best of kings, had the things
for the consecration prepared by the rules, and the lord had all the blessings
prepared. But the brahmins and the elders, with the townsmen and country people,
all forbade Devapi's consecration. When the king heard that the consecration had
been forbidden, his throat choked with tears and he grieved for his son. So
Devapi was generous, knew dharma and was true to his word, and dear to the
people, and yet he was flawed by his skin disease. "The gods do not welcome a
king who lacks a limb," the bulls among the twice-born said, and so they forbade
the best of kings. Then the king, deeply pained and full of grief for his son,
died. When he saw him dead, Devapi took refuge in the forest. Bahlika gave up
the kingdom and went to live in his mother's brother's family. He left his
father and brothers and came to a rich city. With Bahlika's leave, Shantanu,
famous in the world, ruled the kingdom as king when his father had died, king.

"'In the same way wise Pandu thought it over, and I, the eldest, was put out of
the kingdom because I lacked a limb, Bharata. Pandu came to the kingdom though
he was the younger, king. At his death the kingdom passes to his sons, tamer of
enemies. When I have no share in the kingdom, how can you want the kingdom?
Prince Yudhishthira is a great man, and this kingdom came to him by right. He is
the master of these Kaurava people and their ruler, a man of great power. He is
true to his word, always watchful, keeping to the teaching, good to his kin,
dear to the people, kind to his friends, master of his senses, the support of
good men. Patience, forbearance, self-control, uprightness, truth to his vows,
learning, watchfulness, compassion for creatures and the power to rule: all the
virtues of a king are in Yudhishthira. You are not a king's son, your conduct is
ignoble, you are greedy and evil-minded towards your kin. How can you, badly
trained, take this kingdom that came to others in order? Give up your folly and
give half the kingdom, with its mounts and its retinue. Then the rest of your
life may be left to you, lord of men, and to your younger brothers.'

---

"When Bhishma, Drona, Vidura, Gandhari and Dhritarashtra had spoken like this,
the fool still did not understand. He shook them off and rose in anger, his eyes
red with fury. The kings followed him, ready to give up their lives. He
commanded those evil-minded kings again and again, 'Go to Kurukshetra. Today the
moon is in Pushya.' Then those lords of the earth set out with their troops,
gladly, driven by time, having made Bhishma general. Eleven armies of kings have
gathered. At their head Bhishma shines, with his palm-tree banner. Do here what
is fitting and timely, lord of the people.

"I have told you, king, what Bhishma, Drona, Vidura, Gandhari and Dhritarashtra
said in my presence, Bharata, what happened in the Kuru assembly. First I used
conciliation, king, wanting brotherhood, so that the Kuru line would not be
divided and the people would prosper. When conciliation was not accepted, I used
division next, and told of deeds, divine and human. When Suyodhana did not
respect words that began with conciliation, I gathered all the kings and divided
them. I showed wonderful, terrible, dreadful deeds that were more than human,
lord. I rebuked those kings, treated Suyodhana like a straw, and frightened
Radha's son and Subala's son again and again. Again and again I spoke of the
meanness of Dhritarashtra's sons and blamed them. I divided all the kings, many
times, with words and counsel.

"Then again, with conciliation, I spoke of a gift, so that the Kuru line would
not be divided and for the work to be done. 'All the Pandavas will give up their
pride and stand below Dhritarashtra, Bhishma and Vidura, as children,' I said.
'Let them hand over the kingdom to you, and be without power. Let it be as King
Gangeya and Vidura said. Let all the kingdom be yours. Give up five villages,
best of kings. They surely must be supported by your father.'

"Spoken to like this, the wicked man did not give up his purpose. I see only the
fourth way, the rod, for those wicked men, and nothing else. The kings have set
out for Kurukshetra, to destruction. I have told you all that happened in the
Kuru assembly. They will not give you the kingdom without war, Pandava. They are
all causes of destruction, and their death is at hand."

**Divergences.**

* **Krishna's report.** The speeches Krishna reports here (5.145–147) are not the ones narrated in the assembly scenes (5.122–129). The text gives them as Krishna tells them, and the novel keeps both.
* **The offer of five villages.** Krishna says he offered that the Pandavas would serve under Dhritarashtra and give up the kingdom, if Duryodhana gave up five villages (5.148.14–16). Ledger L-174.
* **Bhishma's account.** Bhishma's account of his vow and of Vichitravirya's death differs in detail from Book 1, as the text has it here: Vichitravirya, "for fear of Rama", was driven out by the citizens (5.145.23). The line is hard; it is kept.
* Rejected lines (\*534–\*539) add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.145.18 *kālī*: Satyavati, called Kali.
* 5.145.30 *pratīparakṣitam*: "the kingdom Pratipa protected".
* 5.146.6 *vālavyajana*: "the yak-tail fan".
* 5.146.22: Vidura's image of the painter and his picture.
* 5.147.3: "Soma Prajapati", the moon as lord of creatures, the root of the lunar line.
* 5.147.17 *tvagdoṣī*: Devapi "had a disease of the skin".
* 5.148.3 *puṣyo 'dya*: "Today the moon is in Pushya", a lucky day for marching.
* 5.148.7–17: the four ways of policy (conciliation, division, gifts and the rod) are kept as Krishna names them.

# Krishna and Arjuna at Ease

"Tell me what those two great ones, Vasudeva and Dhananjaya, said," said
Dhritarashtra. "I want to hear your words, wise one."

"Listen, king, to how I saw Krishna and Dhananjaya," said Sanjaya, "and I will
tell you what the two heroes said, Bharata. Looking down at my toes, careful and
with my hands joined, I went into the inner rooms, king, to speak to those two
gods among men. Not even Abhimanyu or the twins go to the place where the two
Krishnas were, with Krishna Draupadi and lovely Satyabhama.

"They were both flushed with honey wine, both smeared with sandalwood. They wore
garlands and fine clothes and were adorned with heavenly ornaments. The two
tamers of enemies sat on a great golden seat, bright with many jewels and spread
with all kinds of coverings. I noticed that Keshava's feet were in Arjuna's lap,
and great Arjuna's feet were in the laps of Krishna and Satya. Then the Partha
pointed me to a golden footstool. I touched it with my hand, and then I sat down
on the ground.

"There I saw the Partha's lovely feet, taken from the footstool, with the upward
lines on their soles and lucky marks. When I saw those two sitting on one seat,
dark, tall and young, like shala trunks rising, a great fear came over me. The
fool does not understand that these two are equal to Indra and Vishnu, because
he relies on Drona and Bhishma and on Karna's boasting. Then I was sure that the
Dharma King's wish, whatever is in his mind, will be fulfilled, since these two
stand at his command.

"I was honoured with food and drink, covered with a cloth, and treated well.
Then I placed my joined hands on my head and gave them the message. The Partha,
moving Keshava's lucky foot with his hand, a hand used to the bow and arrows,
urged him to speak. Then Krishna rose up like Indra's banner, adorned with every
ornament, with the strength of Indra. Seated, he spoke to me. He is the best of
speakers, and his words were delightful and fit to be spoken, frightening to the
sons of Dhritarashtra, gentle at first and very harsh. I heard those words from
him, who deserves to speak, full of learning in every syllable, pleasing in
their purpose, and drying up the heart in the end.

"'Sanjaya, say these words to wise Dhritarashtra,' said Vasudeva, 'while the
chief of the Kurus listens, and Drona too. Sacrifice with great sacrifices. Give
gifts to brahmins. Enjoy your sons and wives. A great fear has come on you. Give
away your wealth to the worthy. Have the sons you desire. Do what pleases those
you love. The king is in a hurry for victory.

"'This debt has grown, and it does not leave my heart, that Krishna cried out
"Govinda!" to me when I was living far away. Your enmity is with the Left-handed
Archer, whose bow is the Gandiva, full of energy and hard to overcome, and who
has me as his second. Who would want to challenge the Partha when I am his
second, unless he were in the grip of time, even if he were Purandara himself?
The man who could beat Arjuna in battle could lift the earth with his arms, burn
up these creatures in his anger, and throw the gods down from heaven. Among
gods, asuras and men, yakshas, gandharvas and serpents, I see no one who could
stand against the Pandava in battle.

"'That great wonder that is told of in Virata's city, of one against many, is
proof enough. When they were broken by the single son of Pandu in Virata's city
and fled in every direction, that is proof enough. Strength, courage, energy,
speed, lightness of hand, freedom from despair and steadiness are found in the
Partha and nowhere else.'

"So Hrishikesha spoke, cheering the Partha with his words, thundering like the
chastiser of Paka raining in season in the sky. When he heard Keshava's words,
Arjuna, the wearer of the crown, whose horses are white, spoke great words that
made the hair stand on end."

---

When he had heard Sanjaya's words, the king whose eyes are his wisdom began to
weigh them for their strengths and faults. Wise as he was, he weighed the
strengths and faults finely, as they truly were, wanting victory for his sons.
He made up his mind about the strength and weakness of each side, as it truly
stood, and then the wise king of men began to count their power. He reckoned the
Pandavas in power and energy, divine and human, and the Kurus as weaker in
power. Then he spoke to Duryodhana.

"Duryodhana, this worry of mine never leaves me. I think this is true, and I see
it plainly, not by guessing. All creatures love their own children most, and do
what pleases them and what is good for them, as far as they can. In the same way
we see that, as a rule, the good wish to repay those who have helped them, with
great kindness in return.

"Fire will remember what was done for him at Khandava and be Arjuna's ally in
this terrible meeting of the Kurus and Pandavas. And many of the dwellers in
heaven, Dharma and the others, called on by the Pandavas, will come to them out
of their love. Out of fear of Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and the rest, I think they
will be roused to fury like the thunderbolt, wanting to protect them. With the
gods beside them, the Parthas, those mighty tigers among men who have mastered
weapons, cannot even be looked at by a man.

"His heavenly bow, the Gandiva, the best of bows, cannot be approached. He has
two great heavenly quivers from Varuna, which never empty and are full of
arrows. He has a heavenly monkey banner that catches on nothing and moves like
smoke. His chariot has no equal in brightness on the four-cornered earth. People
hear its roar like a great cloud, a sound like the great thunderbolt that
terrifies his enemies. The whole world takes him to be more than human in
courage. The kings know him as the conqueror even of the gods in battle. He is
seen loosing five hundred arrows as if sowing seed, in the blink of an eye, and
bringing them down far away.

"Bhishma and Drona, Kripa and Drona's son, the Madra king Shalya and those who
stand in the middle all say the same. They say that the Partha, standing ready
for battle, the tiger among chariot fighters, tamer of enemies, cannot be beaten
even by kings more than human. He shoots five hundred arrows at one stroke, and
in the strength of his arms he is like Kartavirya. I seem to see that Arjuna,
the great archer, guarded by great Indra and by Upendra, killing in this great
battle. Thinking of all this day and night, Bharata, I cannot sleep and I have
no happiness, worrying about peace for the Kurus. A great destruction has come
on the Kurus. There is no end to this quarrel except peace. I always want peace
with the Parthas, my son, not war. I always think the Pandavas are stronger than
the Kurus."

---

When he heard his father's words, Dhritarashtra's son, most impatient of men,
grew very angry and spoke again.

"You think the Parthas cannot be beaten because the gods are their allies, best
of kings. Let that fear of yours go. The gods became gods by being free of
desire and hatred, of treachery and greed, and by indifference to things. So
Dvaipayana Vyasa told us long ago, and Narada the great ascetic, and Rama, son
of Jamadagni. The gods never act as men do, out of desire, greed, pity or
hatred, bull of the Bharatas. If Agni and Vayu, Dharma, Indra and the Ashvins
acted out of desire, the Parthas would not have met with sorrow. So you should
never worry about this. The gods always look on with indifference at the things
of gods, Bharata. If desire, hatred and greed were seen in the gods, then their
authority as gods would not hold.

"When I chant mantras over him, the god of fire always quiets down, even when he
wants to burn all the worlds and has spread all round them. Know that I have
more of that highest energy that fills the dwellers in heaven, Bharata, beyond
compare, than the gods. When the earth is splitting apart, and the peaks of the
mountains, I make them firm again by my mantras, king, while the world looks on.
A terrible, roaring rain of stones and a wind rise to destroy all this, the
thinking and the unthinking, the moving and the still. I always calm them here,
out of pity for creatures, while the world watches. When I make the waters
solid, chariots and foot soldiers walk on them. I alone set going the affairs of
gods and asuras. To whatever lands I go on some business with my akshauhinis,
the waters flow for me wherever I wish.

"There are no dangers from snakes and the like in my kingdom, king. Terrible
creatures do not harm those who sleep, because of me. The rain cloud rains as
much as those who live in my kingdom want, king. All my people keep dharma, and
there are no plagues. The Ashvins, Vayu and Agni, the slayer of Vritra with the
Maruts, and Dharma cannot protect those I hate. If they could protect my enemies
by their power, the Parthas would not have met with sorrow for thirteen years.
Neither gods nor gandharvas, asuras nor rakshasas can protect one I hate. I tell
you this truly. Whatever I always think, good or bad, has never failed, for
friends or for enemies. When I say, 'This will happen,' scorcher of enemies, it
has never turned out otherwise. They know me as one whose word is true.

"This greatness of mine is witnessed by the world and famous in every direction.
I tell it to comfort you, king, not to boast. I have never been a boaster, king.
It is the way of the wicked to praise themselves. You will hear that the
Pandavas, the Matsyas, the Panchalas with the Kekayas, Satyaki and Vasudeva have
been beaten by me. As rivers reach the ocean and are lost entirely, so they and
their kin will perish when they meet me. The highest understanding, the highest
energy and the highest courage are in me. The highest learning and the highest
yoga are mine, beyond theirs. Whatever the grandfather, Drona, Kripa, Shalya and
Shala know of weapons, I have all of it."

When he had said this, scorcher of enemies, he questioned Sanjaya again,
Bharata, knowing what had to be done at that time, eager to fight.

**Divergences.**

* **"Govinda!"** Krishna says that Draupadi "cried out 'Govinda!' to me when I was living far away" (*yad govindeti cukrośa kṛṣṇā māṃ dūravāsinam*, 5.58.21), and calls it a debt. The CE dicing scene does not narrate this cry; the prayers to Krishna there are rejected passages (2.\*542–\*550). Ledger L-19, extended.
* **Arjuna's reply.** The chapter ends, as the CE adhyaya does, by announcing that Arjuna then spoke "great words that made the hair stand on end" (5.58.30). The CE does not give those words here.
* Rejected lines (\*346–\*354) add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.58.5 *madhvāsavakṣībau*: "flushed with honey wine". Kept as the text says.
* 5.58.7: Krishna's feet in Arjuna's lap, and Arjuna's in the laps of Draupadi and Satyabhama, as the text describes.
* 5.58.9 *ūrdhvarekhatalau*: "with the upward lines on their soles", a lucky mark.
* 5.58.15 *indraketuḥ*: "Indra's banner", a festival pole.
* 5.59.17 *madhyasthāḥ*: "those who stand in the middle", neutral men.
* 5.59.19: Kartavirya, the thousand-armed king of old.

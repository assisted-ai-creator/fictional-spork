# Sthunakarna

"'Then Shikhandi's mother told her husband the truth about the girl Shikhandini,
king, strong-armed one,'" said Bhishma. "'"I had no son, king, and out of fear
of my co-wives I gave it out that the girl Shikhandini, when she was born, was a
man. You approved it out of love for me, best of men, and the rites for a son
were done for the girl, bull among kings, and you married the Dasharna lord's
daughter as a wife for her. And you said before, seeing the meaning of the god's
words, 'Having been a girl, she will become a man,' and so this was let pass."

"'When he heard this, Drupada Yajnasena told the whole truth to his counsellors,
and the king took counsel on what was fitting for the protection of his people,
king. He thought it through in regard to his kinsman, the Dasharna king, lord of
men, and though he had himself made the deceit, he came to a decision, intent on
counsel. His city was naturally well guarded, but at the time of danger,
Bharata, he had it guarded on every side and fully fitted out, king of kings.
The king and his wife were in the greatest distress at the quarrel with the
Dasharna lord, bull of the Bharatas. Thinking in his mind, "How can I avoid a
great war with my kinsman?", he worshipped the gods.

"'Seeing him devoted to the gods and offering worship, his wife the queen said,
"Seeking the gods is always true and approved by the good. How much more so for
us who have come into an ocean of grief. Let all the gods be worshipped with
rich gifts and let offerings be poured into the fires to turn back the Dasharna.
And think in your mind of an end without war, lord. By the grace of the gods all
this will come about. What you have decided with your counsellors, large-eyed
one, for the safety of this city, do that too, king. For fate joined with human
effort succeeds best, lord of the earth. They do not succeed when they work
against each other. So make your arrangements in the city with your ministers
and worship the gods as you wish, lord of the people."

"'Seeing the two of them talking like this, given over to grief, the
high-spirited girl Shikhandini was ashamed. She thought, "These two are in grief
because of me," and she made up her mind to end her life. Having resolved this,
deep in grief, she left the palace and went to a deep, empty forest, guarded by
the rich yaksha Sthunakarna. For fear of him people kept away from that forest.
There Sthuna's house was plastered with lime and clay, rich with the smoke of
parched grain, with high walls and gateway. Drupada's daughter Shikhandi went in
there, king, and fasted for many days, wasting her body.

"'The yaksha Sthuna, with honey-coloured eyes, showed himself to her. "What is
this undertaking of yours for? Tell me, and I will do it at once." "It cannot be
done," she said to the yaksha again and again. "I will do it," the guhyaka
answered. "I am a follower of the lord of wealth and I give boons, princess. I
will give even what should not be given. Tell me what you want to say." Then
Shikhandi told it all, every part, to Sthunakarna, the chief of the yakshas,
Bharata. "My father is in trouble, yaksha, and will soon perish. For the
Dasharna lord will march against him in anger. That king in golden armour is
very strong and determined. So save me, yaksha, and my father and my mother. You
have promised to take away my grief. Let me become a man, yaksha, without fault,
by your grace. Grant this favour, great yaksha, guhyaka, before that king comes
to my city."

---

"'When he heard Shikhandi's words, the yaksha thought in his mind, bull of the
Bharatas, pressed by fate, "It is fated to be, to my grief, Kaurava." "Good
lady, I will do as you wish," he said, "but hear my terms. I will give you this
manhood of mine for some time. You must come back at the proper time. I tell you
this truly. I am a lord who gets what he wills, I change shape at will and I
fly. By my grace save your city and all your kin. I will bear your womanhood,
princess. Promise me truly, and I will do what pleases you."

"'"I will give your manhood back to you, lord," said Shikhandi. "Bear my
womanhood for some time, wanderer of the night. When the Dasharna king in golden
armour has gone back, I will be a girl again and you will be a man."

"'When they had said this, king, the two made their agreement there, each not to
betray the other, and made the exchange. The yaksha Sthuna took on the female
sex, lord of men, and Shikhandi took on the yaksha's shining form. Then
Shikhandi the Panchala prince, having won manhood, king, entered the city
joyfully and went to his father. He told Drupada all that had happened. When
Drupada heard it he felt the greatest joy, and he and his wife remembered the
great lord's words.

"'Then he sent word to the Dasharna lord, king: "This son of mine is a man.
Believe me." Then the Dasharna king came at once to the Panchala king Drupada,
full of grief and anger. When he reached Kampilya, the Dasharna lord honoured
and sent a messenger, the best of those who know Brahman. "Messenger, tell that
lowest of kings, the Panchala, from me: 'Evil-minded man, since you chose my
daughter for your daughter, you will see the fruit of your arrogance today, no
doubt of it.'" Sent by him, the brahmin messenger went to the city, best of
kings, sent by the Dasharna king. The chaplain came to Drupada in his city. The
Panchala king, with Shikhandi, had a cow and the guest water brought to him with
great honour, king of kings. He did not accept that honour and spoke the words
that the hero, the king in golden armour, had said. "Low-living man, since I
have been deceived by you over your daughter, reap the fruit of that evil deed,
evil-minded one. Give me battle today, lord of men, at the head of the fight. I
will root you out at once with your counsellors, sons and kin."

"'Hearing these reproaches spoken, they say, by the Dasharna lord's messenger,
the chaplain, among his counsellors, Drupada, bowing in friendship, best of the
Bharatas, said, "To what you have said to me, brahmin, as my kinsman's words, a
messenger will give the answer." Then Drupada too sent a brahmin who had
mastered the Veda as messenger to great Hiranyavarma. He met the Dasharna lord,
king, and gave the message Drupada had sent. "Let a plain test be made. The
prince is my son. Someone has said what is false, and it should not be
believed." Then the king, hearing Drupada, was in doubt, and he sent very lovely
young women, the finest, to find out whether Shikhandi was a woman or a man.
They were sent, learned the truth, and gladly told the king all of it: that
Shikhandi was a man, lord of the Kurus, a great one, they told the Dasharna
king. Then the king, having made the test, was pleased. He met his kinsman,
glad, and stayed there. Delighted, the lord of the people gave Shikhandi wealth,
elephants, horses, cows and slave women by the hundred. Honoured, he went back,
having rebuked his daughter, they say. When the Dasharna king Hemavarma had gone
back pleased, his anger gone, Shikhandini was glad.

---

"'Some time later Kubera, who is carried by men, making a tour of the world,
came to Sthuna's house. The guardian of wealth, passing over the house, looked
down at the dwelling of the yaksha Sthuna, well adorned and bright with strings
of garlands, worshipped with parched grain, perfumes and canopies, scented with
incense, adorned with flags and banners, with food, drink and meat given as
offerings. Seeing his place adorned on every side, the lord of the yakshas said
to the yakshas who followed him, "This house of Sthuna's is well adorned, you of
boundless courage. Why does the great fool not come to me today? Since the fool
knows and does not come to me, I think a heavy punishment should be laid on
him."

"'"King Drupada's daughter, king, was born Shikhandini," said the yakshas. "For
some reason he gave her the marks of a man. He took the marks of a woman and
stays in his house as a woman. So he does not come to you, ashamed, in a woman's
form. That is why Sthuna does not see you today, king. When you have heard this,
do what is right. Let the heavenly car stop here."

"'"Let Sthuna be brought," the lord of the yakshas said. "I will punish him," he
said again and again. Summoned, he came to the lord of the yakshas, lord of the
earth, and stood there in a woman's form, ashamed, great king. The giver of
wealth, very angry, cursed him, joy of the Kurus. "Let this wicked one's
womanhood stay as it is, guhyakas." Then the great lord of the yakshas said,
"Since you despised the yakshas and gave Shikhandi your sex, evil-minded one,
and took a woman's sex, doer of evil, since you did what had never been done,
fool, from today you are a woman and he is a man." Then the yakshas tried to
appease Vaishravana, they say: "Make an end to the curse, for Sthuna's sake,"
again and again. Then the great lord of the yakshas answered all the hosts of
yakshas who followed him, wishing to set an end to the curse, my son. "When
Shikhandi is killed in battle, Sthuna will take back his own form. Let the
yaksha Sthuna be free of care," said the great-minded one. When he had said
this, the blessed god, honoured by yakshas and rakshasas, went away with all
those who travel in the blink of an eye.

"'Sthuna, under the curse, stayed there. At the appointed time Shikhandi came to
that wanderer of the night. He went to him and said, "Here I am, lord." Sthuna
said to him again and again, "I am pleased." Seeing the prince Shikhandi come
back honestly, he told him all that had happened. "I have been cursed by
Vaishravana because of you, prince. Go now as you wish and travel the worlds
happily. I think this was fated long ago and cannot be passed by, your going and
the sight of Paulastya."

"'Spoken to like this by the yaksha Sthuna, Shikhandi went back to the city with
great joy, Bharata. He worshipped twice-born men, gods, shrines and crossroads
with many kinds of perfumes and costly garlands. Drupada the Panchala, with his
son Shikhandi whose aim was won, and with his kin, felt the greatest joy. He
gave his son Shikhandi, who had been a woman, to Drona as a pupil, bull of the
Kurus, great king. The prince Shikhandi learned the science of the bow with its
four parts, along with you, and so did Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson. My
spies, whom I had set on Drupada in the guise of idiots, the blind and the deaf,
told me all this as it was, my son.

"'So, great king, this son of Drupada, female and male, Shikhandi, the fine
chariot fighter, came to be, best of the Kauravas. The eldest daughter of the
lord of Kashi, famous by the name of Amba, was born in Drupada's family as
Shikhandi, bull of the Bharatas. Even for a moment I would not look at him when
he comes with a bow in his hand, wanting to fight, nor would I strike him. This
vow of mine has always been famous on earth, joy of the Kauravas: I will not
loose arrows at a woman, at one who was a woman before, at one with a woman's
name, or at one in a woman's form. For this reason I would not kill Shikhandi. I
know the truth of Shikhandi's birth, my son. So I will not kill him in battle,
even when he comes at me to kill. If Bhishma killed a woman, he would kill
himself as well. So I will not kill him, even if I see him standing in battle.'"

"When he heard this," said Sanjaya, "the Kaurava, King Duryodhana, thought for a
moment and judged it right in Bhishma."

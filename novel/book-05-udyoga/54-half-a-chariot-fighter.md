# Half a Chariot Fighter

"'Your uncle Shakuni, lord of men, is a chariot fighter worth one,'" said
Bhishma. "'He has bound himself in a feud with the Pandavas, and he will fight,
no doubt of it. His troops are hard to withstand and never turn back in battle,
mostly with strange weapons, as fast as the wind.

"'Drona's son, the great archer, surpasses all archers. He is a fighter of many
skills in battle, with strong weapons, a great chariot fighter. His arrows,
great king, loosed from his bow, fly in an unbroken line, like those of the
bearer of Gandiva. I cannot count this hero, the finest of chariot fighters. If
he wished, he could burn up the three worlds, he is so famous. His anger and
energy were gathered by austerity while he lived in the hermitage, and Drona
favoured him with heavenly weapons. He is noble-minded. But he has one great
fault, bull of the Bharatas, by which I do not think him a ratha or an atiratha,
best of kings. The brahmin loves his life too much and always wants to live
long. Yet there is no one like him in either army. He could kill even an army of
the gods with a single chariot. With the slap of his arm guard, mighty as he is,
he could split mountains. A hero of countless virtues, a striker of terrible
splendour, unbearable like the bearer of the rod, he will range like Time. Like
the fire at the end of the age in his anger, lion-necked, of great mind, he will
put an end to the rear of the war, Bharata.

"'His father, of great energy, is better than the young, though he is old. He
will do great deeds in battle. I have no doubt of it. Fanned by the wind of the
force of his weapons, rising from the dry grass and fuel of the armies, he will
burn up the armies of Pandu's son, set on victory. That bull among men,
Bharadvaja's son, leader of the leaders of chariot troops, will do fierce deeds
for your good. The teacher of all the anointed kings, old and venerable, would
make an end of the Srinjayas. But Dhananjaya is dear to him. That great archer
would never kill Partha, who does what others cannot, remembering his own
shining work as teacher, won by Partha's virtues. That hero always praises
Partha's many virtues. Bharadvaja's son regards him as more than his own son.
With heavenly weapons, mighty as he is, he could kill gods, gandharvas and
danavas in battle with a single chariot, even if they all came together.

"'The Paurava, a tiger among kings, a great chariot fighter, king, I think a
ratha, hero, a breaker of enemy heroes' chariots. With his own army he will
scorch the enemy host and burn up the Panchalas as the black-pathed fire burns
dry grass. Satyavrata, a fine chariot fighter, a prince and great chariot
fighter, will range like Time in the enemy's force, king. His fighters, king of
kings, in bright armour and weapons, will range in battle killing your enemies.
Vrishasena, Karna's son, a great chariot fighter, the foremost of your chariot
fighters and the best of the strong, will burn up your enemies' forces.
Jalasamdha of great energy, king, a fine chariot fighter of yours, of Magadha,
slayer of enemy heroes, will give up his life in battle. He will fight in
battle, strong-armed one, skilled on an elephant's neck or on a chariot, wasting
the enemy host. I think that bull among men a ratha, great king. He will give up
his life for you in the great battle with his army. A valiant fighter of many
skills in battle, he will fight your enemies without fear, king.

"'Bahlika is an atiratha and never turns back in battle. I think him a hero like
Vaivasvata in war, king. Once he comes to battle he will never turn back at all,
like the wind, king, until he has struck down the enemy. Your general Satyavat,
great king, is a great chariot fighter, a doer of wonderful deeds in battle, a
ratha who breaks the enemy's chariots. When he sees a battle he feels no fear at
all. Smiling, he goes up against the enemy standing in the chariot's path. That
best of men, valiant against enemies, will do a very great deed, fit for a good
man, for you in the clash. Alayudha, lord of the rakshasas, cruel in deeds and
very strong, will kill the enemy, king, remembering an old feud. He is the
finest chariot fighter of all the rakshasa armies, a master of magic, firm in
his feud, and he will range in battle.

"'Bhagadatta, lord of Pragjyotisha, a mighty hero, is the best of those who hold
the elephant goad and skilled on a chariot too. Long ago he fought with the
bearer of Gandiva for very many days, king, both of them greedy for victory.
Then, Gandhari's son, honouring his friend the chastiser of Paka, he made an
agreement with the great Pandava. That king, skilled on an elephant's neck, will
fight in battle like Vasava of the gods mounted on Airavata.

---

"'Achala and Vrishaka, two brothers together, are chariot fighters of yours,
hard to attack, who will destroy your enemies. They are strong, tigers among
men, firm in anger and ready to strike, chiefs of the Gandharas, young, handsome
and very strong.

"'And this friend of yours, always dear to you, rough in battle, who urges you
on to war with the Pandavas, king: your harsh, boastful, low Karna Vaikartana,
your counsellor, leader and friend, proud and very puffed up. He is not a full
ratha, nor an atiratha, king. He has been parted from the armour he was born
with, and he is a fool. He has been parted for ever from his heavenly earrings,
and he is soft-hearted. Because of Rama's curse and the brahmin's words, and
because he has lost his means, I think him half a chariot fighter. Once he meets
Phalguna, he will not get away alive.'"

"Then strong-armed Drona, the best of those who bear weapons, said, 'It is just
as you say. There is nothing false in it. Karna is too proud in every battle,
and he is seen to turn his back. He is soft-hearted and careless. So I think him
half a chariot fighter.'

"When he heard this, Radha's son, his eyes wide with anger, spoke to Bhishma,
king of kings, stinging him with words like a goad.

"'Grandfather, you cut me as you please with arrows of words, though I have done
no wrong, always out of hatred, like this at every step. And I bear all of it
for Duryodhana's sake. You think me powerless, like a coward. I have no doubt
that you are the half chariot fighter, son of Ganga. I do not speak falsely to
the whole world. You are always the enemy of the Kurus, and the king does not
know it. For who would destroy the spirit of his equals, all doing noble deeds
for their king, wanting to divide them on the eve of battle, as you mean to do
harm by naming virtues?

"'The greatness of a kshatriya cannot be counted by years, Kaurava, nor by grey
hair, nor by wealth, nor by kin. A kshatriya is held to be greatest by strength,
the twice-born by mantras, vaishyas by wealth, and shudras by age. You make
rathas and atirathas as you please, by your own choice, full of desire and
hatred, in your folly. Strong-armed Duryodhana, look at this well and rightly.
Get rid of Bhishma, whose mind is wicked and who does you harm. An army once
divided is hard to join again, king, even a native one, tiger among men, let
alone one raised from many lands. Doubt has arisen among these fighters in war,
Bharata, and our spirit is being destroyed, above all before our eyes.

"'What does Bhishma, of little sense, know of chariot fighters? I will hold back
the army of the Pandavas. When they meet me, whose arrows never fail, the
Pandavas and the Panchalas will run off in the ten directions, like bulls from a
tiger. What does Bhishma know of the clash of war, of counsels or of well-spoken
words? His years are gone, his mind is dull, and he is deluded by time. He is
always competing with the whole world, and he thinks no other man anything, his
sight is so useless. The scriptures teach that the old must be listened to, but
not the very old, for they are held to be children again. I alone will kill the
Pandavas, no doubt of it, tiger among kings, and in good fighting the fame will
go to Bhishma. You have made Bhishma general, lord of men, and the credit goes
to the general, never to the fighters. While Ganga's son lives I will not fight
at all, king. When Bhishma is killed, I will fight with all the great chariot
fighters.'

"'This great burden, like the ocean, is laid on me in the war of Dhritarashtra's
son, thought of for years on end,' said Bhishma. 'Now that this fearful time has
come, I must not make division among us. That is why you are alive, suta's son.
Old as I am, I would not today use my courage against a child like you and cut
short your hope of battle and of life, suta's son. When Rama son of Jamadagni
let loose his great weapons at me, I felt no pain at all. What will you do to
me? The good do not praise this, a man praising his own strength. But burning
with anger, I will tell you, low man, disgrace to your family. At the bridegroom
choice of the king of Kashi I beat the gathered kshatriyas of the earth with a
single chariot and carried off the girls by force. Thousands of such men, and
better, I alone drove back on the battlefield with their armies. Since you came,
a man of enmity, the great wrong policy of the Kurus is here to destroy them.
Make your effort. Be a man. Fight Partha in battle, the one you compete with. I
will see you got clear of this war, evil-minded man.'"

"Then great-minded King Duryodhana said to him, 'Look to me, son of Ganga, for a
great task is at hand. Think first of what is best for me. Both of you will do
great deeds for me. And I want to hear more about the enemy's finest chariot
fighters, and their atirathas, and the leaders of their chariot troops. I want
to hear of the enemy's strength and weakness, Kaurava. When the night has
passed, this war will begin.'

"'These are your rathas, counted,' said Bhishma, 'and your atirathas, king, and
the half chariot fighters. Now hear about the Pandavas, if you are curious about
the Pandavas' force today, king, the count of chariot fighters, strong-armed
one, with these lords of the earth.

"'The Pandava king himself, Kunti's son, is a fine chariot fighter. He will
range in battle like fire, my son, no doubt of it. Bhimasena, king of kings, is
a ratha worth eight. Proud, with the strength of ten thousand elephants, in
energy he is not human. Madri's two sons are chariot fighters, two bulls among
men, like the Ashvins in beauty and full of energy. At the head of the army,
remembering their own torment, they will range like Rudra. I have no doubt of
it. All those great men stand out like the trunks of sal trees. They are a span
taller than other men. All Pandu's sons are built like lions and very strong.
All of them have lived as celibate students, and all are great ascetics. Modest,
tigers among men, fierce in their strength like tigers, all of them are more
than human in speed, in striking and in the crush of battle. All of them
conquered kings, bull of the Bharatas, in their conquest of the quarters. No men
can ever bear their weapons, clubs and arrows, Kaurava, even to string their
bows, or to lift their heavy clubs or draw their arrows. In speed, in hitting a
target, in eating and in throwing dust, they beat all of you even as children.
When they meet your army, fierce in their strength like tigers, they will
destroy it in battle. Do not meet them. Each one of them could kill all the
kings in battle, as you saw for yourself at the Rajasuya, king of kings. They
will range like Time in battle, remembering Draupadi's torment and the harsh
words at the dice.

"'Red-eyed Gudakesha, with Narayana as his ally: there is no chariot fighter
like him in either army, hero. Neither among the gods before, nor the danavas or
serpents, nor the rakshasas or yakshas, let alone among men, have I heard of any
chariot, past or to come, so equipped as wise Partha's, great king. Vasudeva is
its driver and Dhananjaya its fighter. It has Gandiva, the heavenly bow, and
those horses swift as the wind, heavenly armour no one can pierce, and two great
inexhaustible quivers. It has the hosts of weapons of great Indra, of Rudra and
of Kubera, of Yama and of Varuna, clubs fearful to see, and the thunderbolt and
other chief weapons of many kinds. He killed thousands of the danavas who lived
in Hiranyapura in battle with a single chariot. What chariot fighter is his
equal? In his fury, strong-armed, mighty, true in courage, he could kill your
army while guarding his own. I could go out against Dhananjaya, or the teacher.
There is no third in either army, king of kings, no chariot fighter who could go
out against him as he pours down rains of arrows, like a cloud at the end of the
hot season driven by a great wind. Kunti's son has Vasudeva as his ally. He is
young and skilled, and we are both worn out.'"

"When the kings heard this from Bhishma, their stout arms, with golden armlets
and smeared with sandal, went limp, and so did their eager minds, as they
remembered the Pandavas' old power as they had seen it with their own eyes."

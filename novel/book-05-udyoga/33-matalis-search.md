# Matali's Search

When he had heard Jamadagnya's words, the holy seer Kanva spoke to Duryodhana in
the assembly of the Kurus.

"Brahma, the grandfather of the worlds, does not waste and does not decay. So
too the holy seers Nara and Narayana. Of all the Adityas, Vishnu alone is
eternal, unconquerable and undecaying, everlasting, the lord and master. Others
die by some cause: the moon and the sun, earth and water, wind and fire and sky,
the planets and the hosts of stars. At the end of the world they leave the three
worlds and all perish, and are created again and again. Others die in a moment:
men, beasts and birds, those born as animals and the others said to move in the
world of the living. Kings mostly enjoy their fortune, and at the end of their
lives go to death to reap their good and bad deeds.

"So you ought to make peace with the son of Dharma here. Let the Pandavas and
the Kurus protect the earth. You must not think, Suyodhana, 'I am strong.' The
strong are seen to meet those who are stronger, bull among men. Strength is no
strength among the strong, Kaurava. All the Pandavas are strong, with the
courage of gods.

"On this they tell an old story of Matali, who wanted to give his daughter away
and was looking for a husband for her. The king of the three worlds had a
charioteer named Matali. He had only one daughter in his family, famous in the
world for her beauty. She was called Gunakeshi, and she had the form of a
goddess. In grace and beauty she outshone other women. When Matali and his wife
knew that it was time to give her away, king, he pondered, thinking hard about
it.

"'Shame on the birth of a daughter in the family of men who are proud and
famous, of no light character, and rich in spirit. A daughter puts three
families in doubt for good men: her mother's family, her father's family and the
family she is given to. I have searched both the world of the gods and the world
of men with the eye of my mind, and no husband pleases me. I liked no god, no
son of Diti, no gandharva, no man, nor any of the many seers, as a husband.'

"At night he took counsel with his wife Sudharma, and Matali made up his mind to
go to the world of the nagas. 'Among gods and men I see no husband equal to
Gunakeshi in beauty. Surely there will be one among the nagas.' So he spoke to
Sudharma, walked round her keeping her on his right, smelled his daughter's head
and went down below the earth.

---

"As Matali went on his way, he happened to meet the great seer Narada, who was
going to see Varuna. Narada said to him, 'Where are you going, suta? On your own
business, or at Shatakratu's command?' Questioned by Narada on the road, Matali
told him all his business with Varuna as it was. The sage said to him, 'Let us
go together. I too have set out from heaven to see the lord of the waters. I
will tell you about everything as I show you the world below the earth. When we
have seen it, we will choose some husband there, Matali.'

"The two great ones, Matali and Narada, went down into the earth and saw the
guardian of the world, the lord of the waters. There Narada received the honour
fit for a divine seer, and Matali the honour fit for great Indra. Both were
glad. They told him their business, took leave of Varuna, and wandered through
the world of the nagas. Narada, who knew all the beings who live inside the
earth, explained everything to the charioteer, leaving nothing out.

"'You have seen Varuna, my son,' said Narada, 'surrounded by his sons and
grandsons. See the dwelling of the lord of the waters, rich and fortunate on
every side. This is the very wise son of Varuna, lord of cows. He stands out in
character, conduct and purity. This is his favourite son, lotus-eyed Pushkara,
handsome and fair to look at. The daughter of Soma chose him as her husband.
They call her Jyotsnakali, a second Shri for beauty. The eldest son of the
Aditya, the Cow, is counted as his son.

"'Look at this house of Varuni, all of gold, friend of the lord of the gods. By
winning her the gods won their godhood. Here, Matali, can be seen the shining
weapons of the daityas who lost their kingdoms. These weapons never decay, they
say, Matali. They turn back on themselves, driven by power, and the gods won
them. Here were rakshasas and beings of every kind, with heavenly weapons made
by the gods of old. Here in Varuna's lake this fire with great flames keeps
watch, and Vishnu's whirling discus, with smokeless fuel.

"'This bow of gandi wood was made for the destruction of the worlds. The gods
always guard it. From it came the Gandiva bow. When a task comes, it takes on
the strength needed, always with the strength of a hundred thousand lives. It
rules even those who cannot be ruled, among kings who are kin to rakshasas. It
is the first-born rod, made by Brahma who speaks the Veda. This is the great
umbrella of kings that Shakra spoke of. The sons of the king of the waters carry
it, so fortunate. This is the umbrella of the king of the waters, kept in the
umbrella house. From every side it rains cool water like a cloud. The water that
falls from this umbrella, clear as the moon, is covered in darkness, so that it
cannot be seen. There are many wonderful things to see here, Matali. But your
business would be held up, so let us go on quickly.

---

"'This city lies at the navel of the world of the nagas,' said Narada. 'It is
famous as Patala, and daityas and danavas live in it. Every creature, fixed or
moving, that comes here with the waters cries out loudly as it enters, tormented
by fear. Here the asura fire, whose food is water, always blazes. It knew itself
bound and held down by its work. Here the gods, having drunk the amrita and
killed their enemies, put it away. That is why the waning and waxing of the moon
are seen. Here the heavenly Horse's Head rises at every turn of the moon,
golden, filling the world with waters. Because all those forms of water fall
here, this best of cities is called Patala.

"'From here Airavata, for the good of the world, takes up water and lets it
loose, cool, in the clouds, and great Indra rains it. Here live sea monsters of
every shape, in many forms, water creatures who drink the moonlight in the
waters. Here, suta, those that live at the bottom of Patala die by day, pierced
by the sun's rays, and come back to life at night. Every day the moon, as it
rises, wrapped in its rays, touches the amrita and brings the embodied back to
life by its touch.

"'Here the sons of Diti live, given to adharma, bound and tormented by time,
their fortune taken by Vasava. Here the one called the lord of beings, the great
lord of all beings, practised the highest austerity for the good of all beings.
Here live brahmins who keep the cow's vow, great seers worn thin by study and
recitation, who have given up their lives and won heaven. One who lies wherever
he happens to be, eats whatever comes, and is covered by whatever comes, is
called a keeper of the cow's vow here. Airavata, king of elephants, Vamana,
Kumuda and Anjana, the best of elephants, were born in the line of Supratika.

"'See whether any husband here pleases you by his virtues, Matali. We will go
and choose him, making every effort. This egg, laid in the water, seems to blaze
with splendour. Since the creation of creatures it has not split open or moved.
I do not hear its birth or nature told. No one knows its father or mother. From
it, they say, a great fire will rise at the time of the end, Matali, and burn up
the whole three worlds, moving and unmoving.'

"When he heard Narada's words, Matali said, 'No one here pleases me. Let us go
somewhere else, quickly.'

---

"'This is the famous great city called Hiranyapura,' said Narada, 'the city of
the daityas and danavas, who move about with a hundred kinds of magic.
Vishvakarman built it with no small effort. Maya conceived it in his mind. It
lies at the bottom of Patala. Here live mighty danavas who work thousands of
kinds of magic, brave, given boons long ago. Neither Shakra nor anyone else can
bring them into his power, not Varuna nor Yama, nor the giver of wealth. Asuras
live here, Kalakhanjas, those born of Vishnu's foot, Nairritas and Yatudhanas,
and those born of Brahma's Veda, with fangs, terrible to look at, guarding
themselves, rich in magic and in strength. The danavas called Nivatakavachas
live here, mad for battle. You know that Shakra cannot hurt them. Many times,
Matali, you and your son Gomukha, and the king of the gods, Shachi's husband,
with his son, have been broken.

"'See the houses of gold and of silver, Matali, made by work done by the rule,
and brought here. They seem green with beryl and lovely with coral, bright as
crystal, blazing with the essence of diamond. Some seem made of earth, others of
trees. They look like mountains, and like stars. They shine like suns, like
blazing fire, bright with nets of jewels, tall and close together. They cannot
be described in form or in substance. They are perfect in quality, and fine in
their proportions. See the pleasure gardens of the daityas, their beds, their
jewelled vessels and costly seats, their mountains like clouds with springs of
water, and their trees that bear flowers and fruit at will and move as they
please. Does any husband here please you, Matali? Or let us go to another region
of the earth, if you think so.'

"When he spoke like this, Matali said to him, 'Divine seer, I must do nothing to
displease the dwellers in heaven. The gods and danavas are brothers always bound
in enmity. How could I want a marriage alliance with the enemy's side? Let us go
somewhere else. I must not look at the danavas. I know myself, how my mind is
set on giving.'

---

"'This is the world of the Suparnas,' said Narada, 'the birds that eat snakes.
In courage, in flight and in bearing burdens, they never tire. This family,
suta, has spread from the six sons of Vinata's son, Sumukha, Sunaman, Sunetra,
Suvarchas, Surupa, king of the birds, and Subala, Matali. They have increased by
their offspring, makers of the family of Vinata. In the line of Kashyapa,
thousands and hundreds of the noble kings of birds were born, adding to its
prosperity. All of them have fortune. All bear the mark of the Shrivatsa. All
want fortune and keep up their strength.

"'By their deeds they are kshatriyas. They are pitiless and eat snakes. Because
they destroy their kin, they do not win the state of a brahmin. I will tell you
their names, as they are chief. Hear them, Matali. This family is praised, for
Vishnu holds it as his own. Vishnu is their god. Vishnu alone is their refuge.
Vishnu is always in their hearts, and Vishnu is always their way.

"'Suvarnachuda, Nagashin, Daruna, Chandatundaka, Anala and Anila, Vishalaksha,
Kundalin, Kashyapi, Dhvajavishkambha, Vainateya, Vamana, Vatavega,
Dishachakshus, Nimesha and Nimisha, Trivara and Saptavara, Valmiki and Dvipaka,
Daityadvipa, Sariddvipa, Sarasa, Padmakesara, Sumukha, Sukhaketu, Chitrabarha,
Anagha, Meghakrit, Kumuda, Daksha, Sarpanta, Somabhojana, Gurubhara, Kapota,
Suryanetra, Chirantaka, Vishnudhanvan, Kumara, Paribarha, Hari, Susvara,
Madhuparka, Hemavarna, Malaya, Matarishvan, Nishakara and Divakara: these sons
of Garuda I have named only in part, Matali, the chief ones for fame and
strength. If you like none of them here, come, let us go, Matali. I will take
you to a place where you will find one you like.'

# Galava's Debt

"He was set on harm," said Janamejaya. "He was greedy for what belonged to
others, blinded by folly, delighting in ignoble men, resolved on death. He
brought sorrow to his kin, grief to his family and pain to his friends, and
added to the joy of his enemies. Why did his kin not hold him back from the
wrong road, or the holy one, the friend who loved him, or the grandfather?"

"The holy one spoke," said Vaishampayana, "and Bhishma spoke as was fitting.
Narada too said many things. Hear them.

"'A friend who listens is hard to find,' said Narada, 'and a friend who wants
your good is hard to find. Where a friend stands, a kinsman does not stand. I
see that the words of friends should be listened to, joy of the Kurus. And a man
should not be obstinate. Obstinacy is very cruel. On this they tell an old story
of how Galava met defeat through obstinacy.

"'Long ago, when Vishvamitra was practising austerity, Dharma himself came to
test him, taking the form of the holy seer Vasishtha. Taking the dress of one of
the seven seers, Bharata, hungry and wanting food, king, he came to Kaushika's
hermitage. Vishvamitra in a hurry cooked rice in milk, the finest food, with
great care, and the other did not wait for it. He ate the food other ascetics
gave him. Then Vishvamitra came to him with the food, very hot. "I have eaten.
Wait here for now," said the holy one, and he went away.

"'Then Vishvamitra, of great splendour, stood just as he was, king. He held that
food on his head with both arms and stayed near the hermitage, standing like a
post, unmoving, living on air. The sage Galava took pains to serve him, out of
reverence, great respect, love and a wish to please him.

"'When a hundred years were complete, Dharma came again to Kaushika, in
Vasishtha's dress, wanting food. He saw the food being held on his head by the
wise great seer Vishvamitra, who stood living on air. Then Dharma took it, still
as hot and as fresh as before, and ate it. "I am pleased, brahmin seer," he said
to him, and the sage went away. At Dharma's word Vishvamitra left the state of a
kshatriya and came to the state of a brahmin, and he was glad.

"'Pleased with the service and devotion of his pupil, the ascetic Galava,
Vishvamitra said to him, "You have my leave, my child. Go wherever you like,
Galava." When he said this, Galava, glad, answered the great sage Vishvamitra in
sweet words. "What fee shall I give you for the work of a teacher? Human work
succeeds when it is joined with a fee. For the fruit of a sacrifice is enjoyed
in heaven by the good when the fees given are complete. The fee is called its
peace. What shall I bring for my teacher? Tell me, lord."

"'The holy one knew he had been won over by his service, and Vishvamitra told
him again and again, "Go, go." And again and again, when Vishvamitra said, "Go,
go," Galava answered, "What shall I give?" At the ascetic Galava's obstinacy,
said so many times, Vishvamitra grew a little angry and said this. "Give me
eight hundred horses, white as the moon, each with one black ear. Go, Galava,
and don't be long."

---

"'When the wise Vishvamitra said this, Galava did not sit, or lie down, or eat.
He became skin and bone and pale, lost in worry and grief. He grieved beyond
measure, burning with anger.

"'"Where are my rich friends? Where is my wealth, my store? Where can I get
eight hundred horses white as the moon? Where is my wish for food, my wish for
happiness? Even my wish to live is cut off. What use is life to me? I will go to
the far shore of the ocean, or beyond the furthest edge of the earth, and give
up my body. What fruit is there in life for me? For a man without wealth, his
aims unachieved, cut off from every reward, carrying a debt, where is happiness
without effort? For a man who has enjoyed the wealth of his friends, and done
what he wished in their trust, and cannot repay them, death is better than life.

"'"When a man promises, 'I will do it,' and does not do what he should, burnt by
his false word, his sacrifices and good works perish. A liar has no beauty. A
liar has no offspring. A liar has no lordship. How could he come to a good end?
Where is the ungrateful man's fame, his place, his happiness? The ungrateful man
cannot be trusted, and for the ungrateful there is no atonement. The poor,
wicked man does not live. How could the wicked be protected? The wicked man,
destroying what was done for him, surely meets destruction. I am that wicked,
ungrateful, miserable liar, who has had his work done by his teacher and does
not do what he said. I will make the greatest effort, and then I will give up my
life.

"'"I have never asked anything of the dwellers in heaven, and all the thirty
gods honour me at the sacrifice. I will go to Vishnu, the best of the gods, the
god, lord of the three worlds, Krishna, the refuge of all who have a refuge.
From him all good things rise, filling all gods and asuras. I want to see that
great, undecaying yogi with a pure heart."'

"'When he said this, his friend Garuda, son of Vinata, showed himself to him and
spoke gladly, wanting to please him. "I count you as my friend, and my friends
count you as their friend. A friend who has the means should be given his
heart's wish. I have the means, brahmin, Vasava's younger brother, twice-born.
He was told about you before, and he has granted my wish for you. So come, let
us go. I will take you as you please to the land on the far side of the earth.
Come, Galava, don't delay.

---

"'"I have been told by the god, Galava, who knows all births," said the Suparna.
"Tell me which direction I should take you to see first. Shall I go east or
south, or west, or north, best of brahmins? Where shall I go, Galava?

"'"The east is where the sun, who brings all worlds into being, first rises, and
where the Sadhyas practise austerity at twilight. There the first thought was
born, by which this world is filled. There are the two eyes of Dharma, and there
he is set firm. The offering poured from there spreads in every direction. It is
the door of the day and of the road, best of brahmins. There the daughters of
Daksha first gave birth to creatures. In that direction the sons of Kashyapa
grew. There is the root of the gods' fortune. There Shakra was crowned king of
the gods, brahmin seer, and there the gods heaped up their austerity. For this
reason, brahmin, this direction is called *purva*, the first: because in former
times it was first covered by the gods. So those who want happiness should look
to the eastern quarter of the ancients and do the first rites, those for the
gods.

"'"Here the holy creator of the worlds first sang the Vedas. Here the Savitri
was spoken by Savitri among those who speak Brahman. Here the sun gave the
Yajus, best of brahmins. Here the gods, who have won their boons, drink soma at
the rites. Here the fires are satisfied and enjoy their own source. Here Varuna
went down to Patala and won fortune. Here the birth, the rise and the end of
ancient Vasishtha are seen, bull among brahmins. Here the hundred kinds of Om
are born. Here sages who drink soma drink it in the shed for the offerings. Here
many beasts in the forest, the boar and the rest, were sprinkled and set apart
by Shakra as shares for the gods. Here the sun, rising, angrily kills all the
ungrateful and the hostile, men and asuras alike. This is the door of the three
worlds, of heaven and of happiness. This is the eastern quarter. Let us go into
it, if you wish. I must do what pleases the one whose word I keep. Tell me,
Galava, and I will go. Hear about another direction too.

"'"This direction was given long ago by Vivasvat as a fee to his teacher, by the
rule of the Veda, they say, and so it is called *dakshina*, the south, the fee.
Here the side of the ancestors of the three worlds is set. Here, we hear, is the
dwelling of the gods who drink the heat. Here the Vishvedevas always sit with
the ancestors, worshipped in the worlds, and they have won equal shares. They
call this the second door of dharma, brahmin. Here the fixing of time is counted
in moments and instants. Here divine seers, seers of the world of the ancestors
and all the royal seers live free of care. Here dharma, truth and deeds are
seen. This is the end, best of brahmins, of those who wear themselves out by
their deeds. This is the direction, best of brahmins, that everyone goes to. But
it is covered in ignorance, so no one goes there happily.

"'"Here many thousands of Nairritas were made, hostile, to be seen by those who
have not trained themselves. Here, in the thickets of Mandara and in the homes
of brahmin seers, gandharvas sing songs that steal the heart and mind. Here
Raivata heard the Samans sung with songs, and lost his wife, his ministers and
his kingdom, and went to the forest. Here Savarni and the son of Yavakrita set a
boundary that the sun does not pass. Here Ravana, the great king of the
rakshasas, son of Pulastya, practised austerity and chose immortality from the
gods. Here Vritra too, by his conduct, became Shakra's enemy. Here all the
breaths come and go again, in five ways. Here men of evil deeds are cooked,
Galava. Here is the river called Vaitarani, crowded with those trying to cross.
Coming here, one reaches the end of happiness and the end of sorrow.

"'"Here the sun turns back and pours out sweet water. When it reaches the
Dhanishtha quarter, it lets loose the frost again. Here, Galava, long ago, when
I was tormented by hunger and thinking, I found two huge creatures fighting, an
elephant and a tortoise. Here was born from the sun the great seer called
Shakradhanus, whom they know as the god Kapila, who struck down the sons of
Sagara. Here the brahmins called Shivas, perfected, masters of the Vedas,
studied the Vedas with their supplements and went to Yama's abode. Here is the
city called Bhogavati, ruled by Vasuki, Takshaka the naga and Airavata. Here, at
the time of leaving, a great darkness comes that cannot be pierced even by the
sun or by fire itself. This too is your road of suffering, Galava. Tell me if we
should go, or hear from me about the west.

"'"This direction is dear to King Varuna, lord of cows, always the king of the
waters' resting place and beginning. Here, at the end of the day, the sun lets
go of its light himself. So this direction is famous as *pashchima*, the west,
the after, best of brahmins. Here the holy god Kashyapa crowned Varuna to rule
the water creatures and guard the waters. Here the young moon, killer of
darkness, drinks all Varuna's six flavours and is born at the start of the
bright fortnight. Here the daityas were driven back by the wind, bound, and
slept sighing, tormented by great snakes, brahmin. Here the mountain named Asta
receives the sun, its friend, and from it the evening twilight spreads. From
here night and sleep come out at the end of the day, as if to take away half the
life of the world of the living.

"'"Here Shakra made the goddess Diti miscarry as she slept, carrying her own
child, and from it the host of Maruts was born. Here the root of the Himalaya
goes on to everlasting Mandara. Not even in a thousand years can its end be
reached. Here, reaching the shore of the ocean of the golden mountain, which
carries golden waters, Surabhi pours out her milk. Here in the middle of the
ocean the headless trunk of Svarbhanu is seen, like the sun, wanting to kill the
moon and the sun. Here too the great sound of Suvarnashiras Hariroman, unseen
and beyond measure, is heard as he sings. Here Dhvajavati, the maiden daughter
of Harimedhas, stood still in the sky when the sun ordered, 'Stand, stand.'

"'"Here wind, fire, water and sky let go, Galava, the painful touch of day and
night. From here the sun's course turns slanting. Here all the lights enter the
circle of the sun, and having moved with the sun for twenty-eight nights, they
come out again from the sun by joining with the moon. Here is the source of the
rivers that always flow, the rising of the ocean. Here the waters of the three
worlds stand under Varuna's shelter. Here is the dwelling of Ananta, king of
serpents, and the highest place of Vishnu, who has no beginning or end. Here is
the dwelling of the wind, the friend of fire, and the dwelling of the great seer
Kashyapa, son of Marichi. This is the western road I have told you of, by the
door of the directions. Tell me, Galava, shall we go? What do you think, best of
brahmins?"'"

**Choices.**

* The story of Galava (5.104–121) is told by Narada in the Kuru assembly, as Vaishampayana reports to Janamejaya. The nested quotation marks follow the house rule for frames.
* 5.104.26 *ekataḥśyāmakarṇānām*: "each with one black ear".
* 5.105.18 *vāsavāvarajaḥ*: "Vasava's younger brother", Vishnu.
* 5.106.8, 5.107.1, 5.108.2: Garuda explains the names of the directions by puns on *pūrva* ("first", east), *dakṣiṇā* ("fee", south) and *paścima* ("after", west). The Sanskrit word is kept so the pun can be followed.
* 5.106.14 *oṃkārasya … daśatīr daśa*: "the hundred kinds of Om". The sense is obscure.
* 5.107.10: Raivata, a king who heard the Samans sung and lost everything.
* 5.107.17 *śakradhanuḥ*: the seer Shakradhanus, identified with Kapila.
* 5.108.12: the singer is read as one being, "Suvarnashiras Hariroman". The verse may name two.

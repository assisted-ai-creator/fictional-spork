# Hastinapura Waits

Dhritarashtra learned from his messengers that the slayer of Madhu was coming.
The hair rose on his body. He paid honour to strong-armed Bhishma, to Drona and
Sanjaya, to wise Vidura, and to Duryodhana and his ministers, and he spoke.

"Joy of the Kurus, we hear of a great and strange wonder. Women, children and
old men are talking of it in every house. Some tell it with respect, and some
talk it over in groups. There is all kinds of talk in the squares and the
meeting halls. The Dasharha is coming, the brave one, for the sake of the
Pandavas. The slayer of Madhu deserves our honour and our respect in every way.
The world's course rests on him, for he is the lord of all beings. In Madhava
there is steadiness and courage, wisdom and strength. Let the best of men be
honoured, for he is the eternal dharma. Honoured, he will bring us happiness.
Not honoured, he will bring us misery. If the Dasharha, tamer of enemies, is
pleased by our attentions, we shall get all that we want among all the kings.

"Make arrangements this very day to honour him, scorcher of enemies. Let rest
halls be built along the road, stocked with everything he could wish for. Act,
strong-armed son of Gandhari, so that he comes to love you. Or what do you
think, Bhishma?"

Bhishma and all the others told King Dhritarashtra, "It is very good," and
praised what he had said. When King Duryodhana saw that they agreed, he began to
mark out places for lovely halls. So in pleasant spots, place after place along
the way, they built many halls strewn with every kind of jewel. The king gave
seats of many kinds, finely made, women, perfumes and ornaments, fine cloth,
good food and drink of all kinds, and sweet-smelling garlands. Above all, the
Kaurava king had a lovely hall built at the village of Vrikasthala for him to
stay in, full of jewels. When he had arranged all this, worthy of the gods and
beyond anything human, King Duryodhana told Dhritarashtra.

But Keshava, the Dasharha, did not even look at the halls or the jewels. He went
on to the home of the Kurus.

"Steward," said Dhritarashtra, "Janardana has come from Upaplavya and is staying
at Vrikasthala. He will be here in the morning. Janardana is lord of the Ahukas
and leads all the Satvatas. He is high-minded, very brave and very great.
Madhava is the keeper and protector of the thriving Vrishni line. He is the
blessed great-grandfather of the three worlds. The Vrishnis and Andhakas gladly
follow his wisdom, as the Adityas, the Vasus and the Rudras follow the counsel
of Brihaspati. I shall pay honour to the great Dasharha before your eyes, you
who know dharma. Listen while I tell you how.

"I shall give him sixteen golden chariots, each drawn by four of the best horses
of the Bahli breed, all one colour, with dark bodies. I shall give Keshava eight
elephants, always in rut, with tusks like chariot poles, fierce in battle, each
with eight attendants. I shall give him a hundred lovely slave girls, gold in
colour, who have never borne children, and as many men slaves. I shall give him
eighteen thousand soft woollen blankets brought by the mountain people. I shall
give him thousands of deerskins from the land of the Chinas, as many as Keshava
deserves. There is a clear, bright jewel that shines by day and by night, and I
shall give him that too, for Keshava deserves it. I shall give him a carriage
drawn by mules that covers fourteen leagues in a single day. I shall give him
food, every day, eight times as much as his animals and his men need.

"All my sons and grandsons except Duryodhana will go out to meet the Dasharha in
polished chariots, finely dressed. Thousands of lovely courtesans, well dressed,
will go out on foot to meet fortunate Keshava. Any lovely girls from the city
who go out to see Janardana will go unveiled. The whole city, women, men and
children, looks for the great slayer of Madhu as creatures look for the sun. Let
great banners and flags be put up on every side." And he ordered that the road
for him should be sprinkled with water and made free of dust.

"Duhshasana's house is better than Duryodhana's," he said. "Let it be made ready
for him quickly, well cleaned and decorated. It is graced with lovely high
buildings. It is pleasant and welcoming in every season, and very rich. All the
jewels in that house, mine and Duryodhana's, whatever the Varshneya might
deserve, should be given to him without a doubt."

"King," said Vidura, "you are respected by the three worlds, best of men, and
you are honoured and respected by all people, Bharata. Whatever you say at a
time like this, in the last stage of your life, you would say from the books or
from good reasoning, for you are old and steady. Your subjects are sure that
dharma is in you, king, as firmly as a line cut in stone, or as light is in the
sun, or a great wave in the sea. The world always thinks well of you, lord of
the earth, for your many virtues. Try always, with your kin, to protect those
virtues. Be honest. Do not in your folly destroy in many ways your kingdom, your
sons and grandsons, and your dear friends.

"You mean to give Krishna a great deal as your guest, king. The Dasharha
deserves this and more, and the earth itself. But you do not want to give this
to Krishna for the sake of dharma, or to please him. I swear it by the truth,
and by my own self. This is a trick, it is false and it is deceit, giver of rich
gifts. I know your mind, king, hidden behind what you do outwardly. The Pandavas
want five villages, only five, lord of men, and you will not give them. Who then
will make peace? You mean to win over the strong-armed Varshneya with wealth. By
this means you mean to split him from the Pandavas.

"But I tell you the truth. No one can do that with wealth, or with effort, or
with blame, and take him from Dhananjaya. I know Krishna's greatness. I know
Arjuna's firm devotion to him. I know that Keshava would never give up
Dhananjaya, who is as dear to him as his life. Janardana will want nothing but a
full jar of water, water to wash his feet and a question after his health. Give
him the welcome he loves, king, great as he is and worthy of honour, for
Janardana is worthy of honour. Keshava is coming to the Kurus hoping for good.
Give him what he has come for, king. The Dasharha wants peace between you and
Duryodhana and the Pandavas, king of kings. Do what he tells you. You are the
father, king, and they are your sons. You are old, and they are children. Treat
them as a father, for they treat you as sons."

"All that Vidura has said of Krishna is true," said Duryodhana. "Janardana is
loyal to the sons of Pritha, and no one can turn him from them. But the wealth,
king of kings, and the many kinds of gifts meant to honour Janardana, should
never be given. The place and the time are not right. It is not that Keshava
does not deserve them. Adhokshaja would think, king, 'He honours me out of
fear.' A wise man should not do anything that brings dishonour on a kshatriya,
lord of the people. That is my firm view. I know very well that lotus-eyed
Krishna is the god most worthy of honour in all three worlds. But nothing should
be given him now, my lord, as matters stand. A quarrel has begun, and it will
not end without a fight."

When Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, heard this, he spoke to King
Dhritarashtra, son of Vichitravirya.

"Honoured or not honoured, Janardana would not be angry. And no one can scorn
Keshava, even when he is scorned. Whatever the strong-armed one has made up his
mind to do cannot be changed by anyone, by any means. Do whatever the
strong-armed one says, without doubt. Make peace with the Pandavas quickly, with
Vasudeva as your way across. Janardana's soul is dharma, and he will surely
speak what is right and good. You and your kin should speak kindly to him."

"I cannot possibly share this fortune with them, king," said Duryodhana, "while
I live and they live, grandfather. Now hear the great thing I have decided. I
shall seize Janardana, the Pandavas' refuge. When he is bound, the Vrishnis and
the whole earth will be in my power, and the Pandavas too. He will be here in
the morning. Tell me how this can be done without Janardana seeing through it,
and without any danger."

When Dhritarashtra and his ministers heard these terrible words, and the plan
against Krishna, they were shaken and dismayed.

"Do not say such things, protector of the people," Dhritarashtra said to
Duryodhana. "This is not the eternal dharma. Hrishikesha comes as an envoy. He
is our kinsman and dear to us. He has done the Kauravas no wrong. How can he
deserve to be bound?"

"Dhritarashtra, this son of yours, the fool, is doomed," said Bhishma. "He
chooses harm and not good, though his friends beg him. He has left the path and
is set on evil, and he follows evil men. And you follow him, and ignore the
words of your friends. If this wicked son of yours meets Krishna, whose deeds
never fail, he and his ministers will be gone in a moment. I cannot bear to hear
the words of this evil, cruel, wicked man who has thrown dharma away. They lead
only to harm."

When he had said this, Bhishma, best of the Bharatas, the old man whose courage
was true, rose in great anger and left.

# Duryodhana's Boast

"Do not be afraid, great king," said Duryodhana. "You need not grieve for us. We
are able to beat our enemies in battle, lord.

"When the Parthas were sent into the forest, Madhusudana came with a great
circle of armies that crushes other kingdoms. The Kekayas, Dhrishtaketu,
Dhrishtadyumna the son of Prishata, and many other kings followed the Parthas.
The great chariot fighters gathered not far from Indraprastha, and together they
blamed you and the Kurus. With Krishna at their head they gathered round
Yudhishthira, who sat dressed in deerskins, Bharata. The kings said the kingdom
must be taken back. They wanted to destroy you and your followers.

"When I heard this, I spoke to Bhishma, Drona and Kripa, for I was afraid, bull
of the Bharatas, of the destruction of our kin. 'I do not think the Pandavas
will keep to the agreement. Vasudeva wants to destroy us all. All of you are to
be killed, great ones, except Vidura. Only Dhritarashtra, the best of the Kurus,
who knows dharma, is not to be killed. When he has destroyed us all, my friend,
Janardana wants to make Yudhishthira sole king of the Kurus. What is the right
thing to do now? Bow down, or run away? Or give up our lives and fight our
enemies? If we fight, we are sure to be beaten, for all the kings are in
Yudhishthira's power. Our own country has turned against us, our friends are
angry with us, and all the kings and all our own kin despise us. But bowing down
would be a fault in our kin for all the years to come. I grieve only for my
father, the lord of the people, whose eyes are his understanding, who has come
to sorrow and endless trouble because of me. Your sons have held back your
enemies before, to please me, as you know, best of men. The Pandavas, great
chariot fighters, will pay back that enmity on King Dhritarashtra and his
ministers by destroying the family.'

"Then Drona, Bhishma, Kripa and Drona's son saw that I was deeply troubled and
my senses shaken, Bharata, and they said, 'If our enemies have been wronged by
us, you need not be afraid, scorcher of enemies. Our enemies cannot beat us in
battle, lord of the people. Each of us alone can beat all the kings. Let them
come, and we will break their pride with sharp arrows. Long ago Bhishma alone,
in a single chariot, beat all the kings when his father died, in his anger,
Bharata. The best of the Kurus, in his fury, killed very many of them, and they
went to Devavrata here for refuge out of fear. This Bhishma, with us, is well
able to beat our enemies in battle. So let your fear go, bull of the Bharatas.'
That was the resolve of those men of boundless power at that time.

"Once the whole earth was in our enemies' power. But now they cannot beat us in
battle. Our enemies' wings are clipped, and the Pandavas have lost their power.
The earth is in our hands, bull of the Bharatas, and the kings I have brought
together share one purpose with us in joy and sorrow. All these kings would go
into fire or into the sea for my sake, scorcher of enemies. Know that, best of
the Kurus. They laugh at you, as if you were mad, grieving and lamenting in many
ways, afraid of the enemy's boasting. Every one of these kings thinks himself a
match for the Pandavas. Let the fear that has come on you go.

"Not even Vasava could kill my whole army, nor Brahma himself, the
Self-Existent. It cannot be destroyed. Yudhishthira has given up asking for a
city and asks for five villages, afraid of my army and my power, lord. And your
thought that Vrikodara, son of Kunti, is so strong is false, Bharata. You do not
know the whole of my power. There is no one on earth my equal in fighting with
the mace. No one has surpassed me, and no one will. I have trained, I have borne
hardship, and I have gone to the far shore of the art. So I fear nothing from
Bhima, or from anyone.

"Bless you, when I lived with Samkarshana it was his firm judgement that there
is no one equal to Duryodhana with the mace. I am Samkarshana's equal in battle
and stronger than anyone on earth. Bhima could never bear a blow of my mace in
battle. One blow that I gave Bhima in my anger, king, would send him at once to
the terrible house of Vaivasvata. I would like to see Vrikodara mace in hand,
king. It has always been my heart's wish, and I have long prayed for it. Struck
down in battle by my mace, Vrikodara, the Partha, will fall to the ground dead,
his limbs shattered. Struck by a blow of my mace, even the Himalaya would break
into a hundred thousand pieces at a single stroke. He knows it too, and so do
Vasudeva and Arjuna: it is settled that there is no one equal to Duryodhana with
the mace. So let your fear of Vrikodara in the great battle go. I will dispose
of him. Do not be downcast, king.

"When I have killed him, many chariot fighters, equal to Arjuna or better, will
quickly throw Arjuna back, bull of the Bharatas. Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Drona's
son, Karna and Bhurishravas, the lord of Pragjyotisha, Shalya, and Jayadratha,
king of Sindhu: any one of them is able to kill the Pandavas, Bharata. Together
they will send them to the house of Yama in a moment. There is no reason why the
whole army of kings should not be able to beat the single Partha, Dhananjaya.
The Partha will go to the house of Yama under hundreds and thousands of showers
of arrows from Bhishma, from Drona, Drona's son and Kripa.

"The grandfather, the son of Ganga, was born of Shantanu, Bharata, like a
brahmin seer, and even the gods could hardly stand against him. His father, when
he was pleased, told him, 'You will not die unless you wish it.' Drona was born
of the brahmin seer Bharadvaja in a trough. From Drona was born Drona's son,
great king, who knows the highest weapons. Kripa, chief among teachers, was born
of the great seer Gautama. The glorious one was born from a clump of reeds, and
I think he cannot be killed. Ashvatthama's father, mother and mother's brother:
these three were not born from a womb, great king, and he is a hero who stands
with me. All these great chariot fighters, like gods, great king, could trouble
even Shakra in battle, bull of the Bharatas.

"I think Karna is the equal of Bhishma, Drona and Kripa. Rama gave him leave
with the words, 'You are my equal,' Bharata. Karna had beautiful earrings, born
with him. Great Indra asked him for them for Shachi's sake, and he gave them,
scorcher of enemies, for the unfailing spear, most terrible, great king. How
could Dhananjaya live when he is held by that spear? My victory is sure, king,
like a fruit placed in my hand, and the whole defeat of my enemies on earth is
plain.

"In a single day Bhishma here kills ten thousand, Bharata. The great archers
Drona, Drona's son and Kripa are his equals. And the bands of kshatriyas have
sworn an oath, scorcher of enemies: 'Either we kill Arjuna, or Dhananjaya kills
us.' The kings think they are enough to kill the Left-handed Archer, lord. So
why are you troubled for no reason, king?

"When Bhimasena is killed, who else would fight on their side, Bharata? Tell me,
if you know, scorcher of enemies. The five brothers, Dhrishtadyumna and Satyaki:
those seven fighters, king, are the enemy's great strength. But our best are
Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and the rest, Drona's son, Karna Vaikartana, Somadatta and
Bahlika, the lord of Pragjyotisha, Shalya, the king of Avanti and Jayadratha,
Duhshasana, Durmukha and Duhsaha, lord of the people, Shrutayu, Chitrasena,
Purumitra and Vivimshati, Shala, Bhurishravas, and your son Vikarna. I have
gathered eleven akshauhinis, king. The enemy has fewer, only seven. How could I
be defeated? Brihaspati said that an army a third smaller may be fought. My
army, king, is three times the enemy's. And I see many faults in the enemy's
army, Bharata, and much virtue, and rising virtue, in my own, lord of the
people. Knowing all this, the strength of my army and the weakness of the
Pandavas, you should not fall into confusion, Bharata."

When he had said this, the conqueror of enemy cities, wanting to know what the
time called for, questioned Sanjaya again, Bharata.

---

"Now that he has seven akshauhinis, with the kings, Sanjaya," said Duryodhana,
"what does Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, want, eager for war?"

"Yudhishthira is very glad, king, and eager for war," said Sanjaya. "Bhimasena
and Arjuna and the twins are not afraid. Bibhatsu, the son of Kunti, yoked his
heavenly chariot, lighting up all the quarters, to test a charm. We saw him in
his armour, like a cloud lit by lightning. He meditated on the charm, and spoke
gladly. 'Look at this sign of what is to come, Sanjaya. We shall win.' As
Bibhatsu said it to me, so I too believe it."

"You praise the Parthas and rejoice in them," said Duryodhana, "who were beaten
at dice. Tell me, what are the horses on Arjuna's chariot like, and his banner?"

"Tvashtri, lord of the people, with Shakra and with Dhatri," said Sanjaya, "made
shapes of many kinds for it, lord. By the gods' magic they put forms on that
banner, precious and heavenly, great and small. That banner stretches a yojana
in every direction, across and upwards. And though it is surrounded by trees, it
does not catch in them. Such is the magic made by the maker of the world. As
Indra's bow shines in the sky, not of one colour, and we do not know what it is,
so the banner the maker of the world made has a form of many shapes. As the
smoke of a fire rises to the sky, bearing colours, with a body made of light, so
the banner the maker of the world made neither weighs anything nor stops
anything.

"White horses swift as the wind, fine and heavenly, given by Chitraratha, are
yoked to it. There are a hundred of them, and the number is always kept full.
However many are killed, as many are there again, by a boon given long ago. The
king's horses, yoked to his chariot, are great, and the colour of ivory, and
their strength is like his. Bhimasena's horses look like rishya deer, and in
battle they are as swift as the wind. Speckled horses with backs mottled like
partridges, given by his brother Phalguna with love, better than his heroic
brother's own, carry Sahadeva gladly. The finest horses, bays given by great
Indra, strong and swift as the wind, carry the hero Nakula, son of Madri, of
Ajamidha's line, as they carry Indra, the enemy of Vritra. Horses like these in
age, courage and speed, great horses given by the gods, without equal, carry the
princes, Subhadra's son and the sons of Draupadi."

**Divergences.**

* **Hanuman on the banner.** A rejected line here (\*341) says Hanuman, son of the Wind, will set his image on Arjuna's banner for Bhima's sake. In Sanjaya's CE description the banner's forms are made by Tvashtri with Indra and Dhatri, and Hanuman is not named. See ledger L-106 for Hanuman's own promise in the forest (3.150.14–15).
* **Bhishma's death.** A rejected line (\*338) has Duryodhana add that there is no one who can kill Bhishma. The CE gives the boon of death at will (5.54.46).
* **Arjuna against them one by one.** A rejected line (\*339) has Duryodhana say Arjuna cannot even face any one of them. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*340, \*342) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.54.20–21: Bhishma's victory over the kings "when his father died" is Duryodhana's account.
* 5.54.33: Duryodhana's training in the mace with Balarama (*saṃkarṣaṇa*) is his own claim here.
* 5.54.46: "You will not die unless you wish it" is Shantanu's boon (1.94.94).
* 5.54.47–49: the births of Drona "in a trough", Kripa "from a clump of reeds", and the three "not born from a womb" (Drona, Kripi and Kripa) are Duryodhana's words.
* 5.54.51 *anujñātaś ca rāmeṇa matsamo 'sīti*: "Rama gave him leave with the words, 'You are my equal.'" Bhishma, at 5.48.27, says Rama cursed him. Both are kept.
* 5.54.52 *śacyarthe*: "for Shachi's sake", as the text says. The giving of the earrings and the spear is told in Book 3 (3.294).
* 5.54.63: "Brihaspati said that an army a third smaller may be fought" renders *balaṃ triguṇato hīnaṃ yodhyaṃ prāha bṛhaspatiḥ*, a difficult half-verse.
* 5.55.3 *mantraṃ jijñāsamānaḥ*: Arjuna yoked his chariot "to test a charm".
* 5.55.7 *bhauvanaḥ*: Tvashtri as "maker of the world". The text names Tvashtri, Shakra and Dhatri.
* 5.55.12: the hundred horses given by Chitraratha, always kept at a hundred, are told as the text gives them.
* 5.55.13 *ṛśyaprakhyāḥ*: Bhima's horses "look like rishya deer".

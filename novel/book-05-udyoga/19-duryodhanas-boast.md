# Duryodhana's Boast

"It is as you say, great king," said Sanjaya. "The destruction of the kshatriyas
in war can be seen in the Gandiva. But I do not understand this. You are always
steady, and you know the Left-handed Archer's strength. Why do you fall under
your son's control? This is not the time, great king, for you who have always
done wrong. It was you who cheated the Parthas from the start, bull of the
Bharatas. A father who is the best of friends, and whose mind is rightly set,
should give good counsel. One who betrays is not called a guru.

"When you heard at the time of the dicing, 'This is won! This is taken!' you
smiled like a child at their defeat, great king. You looked on while harsh words
were spoken to the Parthas. You thought, 'They are winning the whole kingdom,'
and you did not see the fall ahead. Your father's kingdom, great king, was the
Kurus and the Jangalas. Then you received the whole earth, won by heroes. The
Parthas won the earth by the strength of their arms and handed it over to you.
And you think, 'I did this,' best of kings.

"When your sons were seized by the king of the gandharvas and were sinking in
water with no raft, the Partha brought them back, best of kings. And you smiled
like a child, king, when the Pandavas were cheated at the dice and went off to
the forest again and again. When Arjuna rains down floods of sharp arrows, even
the oceans would dry up, let alone creatures born of flesh. Phalguna is the best
of archers, the Gandiva the best of bows, Keshava the best of all beings,
Sudarshana the best of discuses, and the bright monkey the best of banners.
Carrying all these in his chariot, with his white horses, he will destroy us in
battle, king, like the wheel of time raised up.

"Today the whole earth belongs to him, bull of the Bharatas, best of kings, who
has Bhima and Arjuna as his fighters. When they see your army sinking, mostly
killed by Bhima, the Kauravas with Duryodhana at their head will go to their
destruction. Afraid of Bhima, lord, your sons will not win victory, great king,
nor will the kings who follow them. The Matsyas do not honour you now, nor the
Panchalas with the Kekayas. The Shalveyas and the Shurasenas all scorn you. They
have all gone over to the Partha, knowing the courage of that wise man.

"Your son, great king, the wicked man, who by his misdeeds has wronged men who
keep dharma and do not deserve to die, must be held back by every means, with
his followers. You should not grieve over it. At the time of the dicing I told
you this, and so did wise Vidura. All this lamenting of yours over the Pandavas,
Bharata, king of kings, is useless, like the lament of a man who has no power."

---

"Do not be afraid, great king," said Duryodhana. "You need not grieve for us. We
are able to beat our enemies in battle, king, lord.

"When the Parthas had been sent into the forest, Madhusudana came to them with a
great host of troops that crushes enemy kingdoms. The Kekayas, Dhrishtaketu,
Dhrishtadyumna son of Prishata and many other kings followed the Parthas. Those
great chariot fighters met not far from Indraprastha, and together they spoke
ill of you and the Kurus. With Krishna at their head they gathered round
Yudhishthira, who sat dressed in deerskins, and waited on him, Bharata. The
kings said the kingdom must be taken back. They wanted to destroy you and all
who are bound to you.

"When I heard this, king, I spoke to Bhishma, Drona and Kripa, bull of the
Bharatas, for I was afraid of the destruction of our kin. I said, 'I do not
think the Pandavas will keep the agreement. Vasudeva wants to destroy us
completely. All of you will be killed, except Vidura. Dhritarashtra, who knows
dharma, best of the Kurus, will not be killed. When Janardana has destroyed us
completely, my son, he wants Yudhishthira to rule the Kurus alone. What is the
right thing for us to do now? Bow down? Run away? Or give up our lives and fight
our enemies? If we fight, our defeat is certain, for all the kings follow
Yudhishthira. Our kingdom is against us, our friends are angry with us, and all
the kings and all our own people scorn us. But to bow down would bring blame on
our kin for endless years. I grieve for my father, the lord of the people, whose
eye is his wisdom, who has met sorrow and endless trouble for my sake. You know,
best of men, that your sons long ago held back the others to please me. Those
great chariot fighters, the Pandavas, will take revenge on King Dhritarashtra
and his counsellors by destroying the family.'

"Then Drona, Bhishma, Kripa and Drona's son, Bharata, seeing me deep in thought
and troubled in my senses, said, 'If the others are treacherous to us, there is
nothing to fear, scorcher of enemies. Our enemies are not able to beat us in
battle, lord of the people. Each one of us can beat all the kings. Let them
come. We will take away their pride with sharp arrows. Long ago Bhishma alone
beat all the kings, when his father died. In his anger, with a single chariot,
Bharata, the best of the Kurus killed very many of them in his rage. Then they
came to Devavrata here for refuge, out of fear. Bhishma, with us, is fully able
to beat the enemy in battle. So let your fear go, bull of the Bharatas.' That
was the firm decision of those men of boundless energy at that time.

"Once the whole earth was in our enemies' power. Now they are not able to beat
us in battle. Our enemies have had their wings cut, and the Pandavas have lost
their strength. The earth is in our hands, bull of the Bharatas, and the kings I
have brought share one aim with us in happiness and sorrow. All the kings would
go into fire or into the ocean for my sake, scorcher of enemies. Know that, best
of the Kurus. They laugh at you, as at a madman, here in your sorrow, lamenting
in so many ways, afraid of your enemies' boasting. Every one of these kings
thinks himself a match for the Pandavas. Let the fear that has come over you go.
Not even Vasava could kill my whole army. It cannot be destroyed, not even by
Brahma, the self-born, himself.

"Yudhishthira gives up the city and asks for five villages, because he is afraid
of my army and my power, lord. You think Vrikodara, son of Kunti, is mighty.
That is false. You do not know all my power, Bharata. There is no one on earth
equal to me at fighting with the mace. There was no one before who surpassed me,
and there never will be. I am disciplined and used to hardship, and I have gone
to the far shore of learning. So I have no fear of Bhima or of anyone else
anywhere. When I lived with Samkarshana, bless you, it was his firm view that
there is no one equal to Duryodhana with the mace. In battle I am Samkarshana's
equal, and stronger than anyone on earth. Bhima would never stand a blow of my
mace in battle. A single blow that I gave Bhima in anger, king, would soon take
him to the terrible house of Vaivasvata.

"I would like to see Vrikodara mace in hand, king. It has long been my wish,
always in my heart. Struck by my mace in battle, Vrikodara, son of Pritha, will
fall to the earth lifeless, his limbs shattered. Struck once by my mace, even
the Himalaya mountain would split into a hundred thousand pieces. He knows this
too, and so do Vasudeva and Arjuna: it is settled that there is no one equal to
Duryodhana with the mace. So let your fear of Vrikodara in the great battle go.
I will drive him away. Do not lose heart, king.

"When I have killed him, many chariot fighters, as good as Arjuna or better,
will quickly overthrow him, bull of the Bharatas. Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Drona's
son, Karna and Bhurishravas, the lord of Pragjyotisha, Shalya and Jayadratha,
king of Sindhu: any one of these can kill the Pandavas, Bharata. Together they
will send them to Yama's house in a moment. Why should the whole army of kings
be unable to beat Dhananjaya alone, the Partha? There is no reason. The Partha
will go to Yama's house, struck by floods of arrows in hundreds and thousands
from Bhishma, Drona, Drona's son and Kripa.

"The grandfather, son of the Ganga, was born to Shantanu, Bharata, like a
brahmin seer, and even the gods could hardly stand against him. His father was
pleased and told him, 'You will not die unless you wish it.' Drona was born of
the brahmin seer Bharadvaja in a bucket. From Drona, great king, was born
Drona's son, who knows the highest weapons. And this Kripa, the chief of
teachers, was born of the great seer Gautama, from a clump of reeds. I think he
cannot be killed. These three were not born of a womb, Ashvatthama's father, his
mother and his mother's brother. And that hero stands by me. All these, great
king, are like gods, great chariot fighters. They would give trouble even to
Shakra in battle, bull of the Bharatas.

"I think Karna is equal to Bhishma, Drona and Kripa. Rama allowed him to go,
saying, 'You are my equal,' Bharata. Karna had beautiful earrings, born with
him. Great Indra asked for them for Shachi's sake, and the scorcher of enemies
gave them for the Amogha spear, most terrible of weapons. With that spear in his
keeping, how could Dhananjaya live? My victory is certain, king, like fruit laid
in my hand. The complete defeat of my enemies on earth is plain to see.

"Bhishma here kills ten thousand in a single day, Bharata, and the great archers
Drona, Drona's son and Kripa are his equals. Bands of kshatriyas have sworn
oaths, scorcher of enemies: 'We will kill Arjuna, or Dhananjaya will kill us.'
The kings think they are enough to kill the Left-handed Archer, lord. Why then
are you troubled for no reason, king? When Bhimasena is killed, who else would
fight, Bharata? Tell me who the enemy has, if you know, scorcher of enemies. The
five brothers, Dhrishtadyumna and Satyaki: these seven fighters, king, are the
enemy's chief strength.

"Ours are the best: Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and the others, Drona's son,
Vaikartana Karna, Somadatta and Bahlika. We have the lord of Pragjyotisha,
Shalya, the king of Avanti and Jayadratha, Duhshasana, Durmukha and Duhsaha,
lord of the people. We have Shrutayu, Chitrasena, Purumitra and Vivimshati,
Shala, Bhurishravas and your son Vikarna. Eleven akshauhinis have been gathered
for me, king. The enemy's are fewer, only seven. How could I be defeated?
Brihaspati said an army three times smaller can be fought. And my army, king, is
three times larger than the enemy's. I see much in the enemy that lacks merit,
Bharata, and much in my own that has merit and more, lord of the people. Knowing
all this, the strength of my army and the weakness of the Pandavas, you should
not lose your head."

When he had said this, the conqueror of enemy cities, wanting to do what the
time required, questioned Sanjaya again, Bharata.

**Divergences.**

* **Karna's spear.** Duryodhana says Karna gave his earrings to Indra for the *amoghā śaktiḥ*, the unfailing spear (5.54.52). This agrees with Book 3 (3.294). Ledger entries on the armour and earrings stand.

**Choices.**

* 5.53.7 *kuravas te sajāṅgalāḥ*: "the Kurus and the Jangalas", the Kuru lands.
* 5.54.2–6: Duryodhana refers to Krishna's visit to the Pandavas in the Kamyaka forest (Book 3, CE 3.13).
* 5.54.20–21: Bhishma's victory over all the kings when Shantanu died is the elders' claim; it is kept as their words.
* 5.54.29 *puraṃ hitvā pañca grāmān sa yācati*: Duryodhana reads the request for five villages as fear.
* 5.54.33 *saṃkarṣaṇasya … yat tadainam upāvasam*: Duryodhana "lived with Samkarshana", Balarama, who taught him the mace.
* 5.54.46: Shantanu's boon to Bhishma, "You will not die unless you wish it".
* 5.54.47 *droṇyām*: Drona was born "in a bucket" (*droṇī*).
* 5.54.48 *śarastambodbhavaḥ*: Kripa was born "from a clump of reeds".
* 5.54.51 *anujñātaś ca rāmeṇa matsamo 'sīti*: Rama (Parashurama) let Karna go saying, "You are my equal."

# Krishna and Karna

"Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "the slayer of Madhu went out surrounded by
princes and ministers, and took Karna up into his chariot. What did the killer
of enemy heroes say to Radheya in the chariot? What soothing words did Govinda
use on the suta's son? Tell me, Sanjaya, what Krishna said to Karna then, with
his voice like a flood or a cloud, whether it was gentle or sharp."

"Hear, Bharata," said Sanjaya, "the words that the slayer of Madhu, whose soul
has no measure, spoke to Radheya one after another. They were smooth and gentle,
kind, in keeping with dharma, true and good, and they took hold of the heart.

"'Radheya,' said Vasudeva, 'you have served brahmins who have mastered the
Vedas. You have asked them about the truth, with discipline and without malice.
You yourself know the eternal teachings of the Veda, Karna. You are well
grounded in the subtle books of dharma. Those who know the books say that for a
son born to a girl before her marriage, whether he is born to her as a girl or
taken with her at the wedding, the father is the man who marries her. You were
born like that, Karna. You are the son of Pandu by dharma. By the rule of the
books of dharma, come, and you will be king.

"'On your father's side you have the sons of Pritha, and on your mother's side
the Vrishnis. Know that these two sides are yours, bull among men. Come with me
today, my friend, and let the Pandavas know you as the son of Kunti, born before
Yudhishthira. Your five brothers the Pandavas will clasp your feet, and so will
the five sons of Draupadi and the unbeaten son of Subhadra. The kings and
princes gathered for the Pandavas' cause will clasp your feet, and all the
Andhakas and Vrishnis. Let princes and princesses bring the water for your
consecration, in golden jars, silver jars and jars of earth, with herbs, all
kinds of seeds, all kinds of gems and plants. And at the sixth turn Draupadi
will come to you.

"'Today let the brahmins of the four Vedas consecrate you, with the Pandavas'
priest, as you sit on a tiger skin. So let your five brothers the Pandavas,
bulls among men, the five sons of Draupadi, and the Panchalas and Chedis. I
myself will consecrate you king, lord of the earth. Let Yudhishthira, son of
Kunti, be your crown prince. Let righteous Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, strict in
his vows, mount the chariot behind you, holding the white fan. Mighty Bhimasena,
son of Kunti, will hold the great white umbrella over you when you are
consecrated, son of Kunti. Arjuna will drive your chariot, with its hundred
bells ringing, its tiger skin covering and its white horses. Abhimanyu will
always be near you, and Nakula, Sahadeva and the five sons of Draupadi. The
Panchalas will follow you, and the great chariot fighter Shikhandi. I myself
will follow you, and all the Andhakas and Vrishnis. The Dasharhas and the
Dasharnas will be your retinue, lord of the people.

"'Enjoy the kingdom, strong-armed one, with your brothers the Pandavas, with
prayers, offerings and blessings of every kind. Let the Dravidas and the
Kuntalas go before you, the Andhras, the Talacharas, the Chuchupas and the
Venupas. Let the bards and heralds praise you today with many hymns. Let the
Pandavas proclaim the victory of Vasushena. Surrounded by the sons of Pritha,
like the moon by the stars, rule the kingdom, son of Kunti, and make Kunti glad.
Let your friends rejoice and your enemies tremble. Let there be brotherly love
today between you and your brothers the Pandavas.'

"'There is no doubt,' said Karna, 'that you say this out of friendship and love,
Keshava, and as a friend, Varshneya, wanting my good. I know it all. By the rule
of the books of dharma I am the son of Pandu, as you think, Krishna. A girl
conceived me by the Sun, Janardana, and at the Sun's word she let me go when I
was born. I was born like that, Krishna, and by dharma I am the son of Pandu.
But Kunti cast me off, as if I were not her own.

"'The suta Adhiratha saw me and took me home at once, and out of love he gave me
to Radha, slayer of Madhu. Out of love for me, Radha's milk came at once, and
she cleaned my urine and my dung, Madhava. How could a man like me, who knows
dharma and always delights in hearing the books of dharma, take away her funeral
cakes? And the suta Adhiratha thinks of me as his son, and I have always thought
of him as my father, out of love. He had my birth rites and the rest performed
for me, Madhava, by the rule the books lay down, out of love for his son,
Janardana. He had brahmins give me the name Vasushena. When I came to youth he
found me wives, Keshava. I have had sons and grandsons by them, Janardana, and
my heart is bound to them by love, Krishna.

"'Not for the whole earth, nor for heaps of gold, Govinda, not for joy or fear,
could I bring myself to tell a lie. In Dhritarashtra's family, Krishna, relying
on Duryodhana, I have enjoyed a kingdom for thirteen years without a thorn. I
have sacrificed often with many sacrifices, with the sutas. I have made
marriages of sons and daughters with the sutas. Relying on me, Krishna,
Duryodhana has taken up arms and made war on the Pandavas, Varshneya. So he has
chosen me, Achyuta, very gladly, to meet Savyasachin in single combat in battle,
as his match. Not for death or bonds, not for fear or greed, Janardana, could I
bring myself to be false to wise Dhritarashtra's son. If I did not now go to
single combat with Savyasachin, it would be a disgrace to me and to Partha both,
Hrishikesha.

"'There is no doubt that you say this for my good, slayer of Madhu, and the
Pandavas would do all of it, for they are in your power. But keep this counsel
secret, best of men. I think that is best here, joy of all the Yadavas. If the
righteous king, strict in his vows, knows that I am Kunti's first-born son, he
will not take the kingdom. And if I were to get that great, rich kingdom, slayer
of Madhu, I would give it to Duryodhana, tamer of enemies. Let righteous
Yudhishthira be king for ever, whose guide is Hrishikesha and whose fighter is
Dhananjaya. The earth and the kingdom belong to the man who has the great
chariot fighter Bhima, Nakula and Sahadeva and the sons of Draupadi, Madhava,
Uttamaujas, Yudhamanyu and Satyadharma son of Somaka, the Chedi king, Chekitana
and unbeaten Shikhandi, the Kekaya brothers, the colour of cochineal insects,
great chariot fighter Kuntibhoja, the colour of the rainbow, Bhimasena's uncle,
great chariot fighter Senajit, Shankha son of Virata, and you, Janardana, as his
treasure.

"'This great gathering of the kshatriyas has been made, Krishna. This shining
kingdom, famous among all kings, has been won. There will be a sacrifice of
weapons by the son of Dhritarashtra, Varshneya. You will be the one who knows
this sacrifice, Janardana, and you will be its adhvaryu priest, Krishna. The
ape-bannered Bibhatsu, in his armour, will be the hotri priest. The Gandiva will
be the ladle, and the courage of men the butter. The Aindra, the Pashupata, the
Brahma and the Sthunakarna weapons, Madhava, will be the mantras that
Savyasachin uses. The son of Subhadra, who follows his father or goes beyond him
in courage, will sing the pressing-stone hymn. Mighty Bhima, the tiger among
men, who destroys the ranks of elephants in battle, will be the udgatri and the
prastotri with his roaring. Righteous King Yudhishthira will be the brahman
priest, with prayers and offerings. The sounds of conches, with drums and
kettledrums, and the loud lion roars, will be the subrahmanya call, slayer of
Madhu. The two famous sons of Madri, Nakula and Sahadeva, mighty ones, will do
the slaughtering of the victims.

"'Let the clean chariot spears with their spotted shafts be the sacrificial
posts in this sacrifice, Govinda. The karnis, nalikas and iron arrows, the
calf-tooth arrows and the lances will be the Soma jars, and the bows the
strainers. The swords will be the pots, the heads the sacrificial cakes, and
blood, Krishna, the offering in this sacrifice. The spears and the clean maces
will be the firewood and the enclosing sticks, and the pupils of Drona and of
Kripa, son of Sharadvat, the assembly. The arrows shot by the Gandiva bowman,
and those loosed by the great chariot fighters and urged on by Drona and his
son, will be the covering grass. Satyaki will do the work of the pratiprasthatri
priest. The son of Dhritarashtra will be consecrated for it, and his great army
will be his wife. Mighty Ghatotkacha will do the slaughtering, strong-armed one,
in the overnight rite, when the sacrifice has been spread out. Mighty
Dhrishtadyumna, Krishna, who was born from the fire when the sacrifice was
spread, will be its fee.

"'I spoke bitter words to the Pandavas, Krishna, to please the son of
Dhritarashtra, and I regret that deed now. When you see me killed by
Savyasachin, Krishna, that will be the rebuilding of the fire altar in this
sacrifice. When the Pandava drinks Duhshasana's blood, roaring aloud, that will
be the Soma pressing. When the two Panchalas bring down Drona and Bhishma, that
will be the end of the sacrifice, Janardana. When mighty Bhimasena kills
Duryodhana, Madhava, the son of Dhritarashtra's sacrifice will be complete. When
the daughters-in-law and granddaughters-in-law of Dhritarashtra gather, their
lords, sons and protectors killed, Keshava, and weep with Gandhari on the field
crowded with dogs, vultures and ospreys, that will be the final bath of this
sacrifice, Janardana.

"'Bull among kshatriyas, slayer of Madhu, do not let the kshatriyas, old in
learning and old in years, die a death for nothing because of you. Let the whole
thriving circle of the kshatriyas meet death by the sword, Keshava, at
Kurukshetra, the holiest place in the three worlds. Lotus-eyed one, arrange here
what you wish, Varshneya, so that all the kshatriyas may reach heaven. As long
as the mountains and the rivers stand, Janardana, the sound of this fame will
last for ever. The brahmins will tell of the great war of the Bharatas at their
gatherings, Varshneya, and it will uphold the kshatriyas' glory. Bring the son
of Kunti to battle with me, Keshava, and always keep this counsel secret,
scorcher of enemies.'

"When Keshava, killer of enemy heroes, heard what Karna said," Sanjaya went on,
"he laughed and spoke, smiling first.

"'Does winning a kingdom not tempt you, Karna? Do you not want to rule the earth
that I give you? The victory of the Pandavas is certain. There is no doubt of it
at all. The victory banner of the Pandava is seen raised on high, the fierce
king of the monkeys. The Creator has set up a divine illusion there, raised high
and bright as Indra's flag, and divine and terrible beings, frightening to see,
are seen on it. Dhananjaya's glorious banner, Karna, raised high and shaped like
fire, catches on no mountain or tree, stretching a league up and across.

"'When you see the one with the white horses, whom Krishna drives, in battle,
wielding Indra's weapon and those of fire and wind, and hear the twang of the
Gandiva like the crash of the thunderbolt, then there will be no Treta, no Krita
and no Dvapara. When you see Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, in battle, with prayers
and offerings, guarding his great army, as hard to face as the sun and scorching
the enemy's army, then there will be no Treta, no Krita and no Dvapara. When you
see mighty Bhimasena in battle, dancing after drinking Duhshasana's blood, like
an elephant in rut killing a rival, then there will be no Treta, no Krita and no
Dvapara. When you see the two sons of Madri, great chariot fighters, stirring up
the army of Dhritarashtra's sons like two elephants, breaking the chariots of
enemy heroes deep in the clash of weapons, then there will be no Treta, no Krita
and no Dvapara. When you see Drona, the son of Shantanu, Kripa, King Suyodhana
and Jayadratha of Sindhu rushing into battle and held back by Savyasachin, then
there will be no Treta, no Krita and no Dvapara.

"'Karna, go from here and tell Drona, the son of Shantanu and Kripa, "This is a
gentle month, with fodder and firewood easy to get. It is rich in ripe plants
and forests, full of fruit and with few flies. There is no mud, the water is
sweet, and it is pleasant, neither too hot nor too cold. Seven days from now it
will be the new moon. Let the battle be joined then, for they say its deity is
Shakra." And tell all the kings who have come to fight, "I shall bring about all
that you wish." The kings and princes who follow Duryodhana will meet death by
the sword and reach the highest end.'

"When Karna heard Keshava's words, good and kind, he honoured Krishna, the
slayer of Madhu, and said, 'Strong-armed one, you know all this. Why do you want
to confuse me? This destruction of the whole earth that has come upon us has its
cause in Shakuni, me and Duhshasana, and in King Duryodhana, son of
Dhritarashtra. There is no doubt, Krishna, that this great war of the Pandavas
and the Kurus has come, terrible, with its mud of blood. The kings and princes
who follow Duryodhana will be burned in the fire of weapons in battle and go to
Yama's house.

"'Many terrible dreams are seen, slayer of Madhu, and terrible omens and
dreadful portents. They seem to foretell defeat for the son of Dhritarashtra and
victory for Yudhishthira, Varshneya, in many ways that make the hair stand up.
The fierce, splendid planet Saturn is pressing on Prajapati's star, pressing
hard on living things. Mars has turned back in Jyeshtha, slayer of Madhu, and is
going for Anuradha, as if to destroy the friendly one. Surely a great danger has
come to the Kurus, Krishna, for a planet is pressing hard on Chitra, Varshneya.
The mark on the moon has turned, and Rahu will go to the sun. Meteors are
falling from the sky, with thunderclaps and quakes. The elephants groan and the
horses shed tears, and they will not take water or fodder, Madhava. When these
omens appear, they say a great danger has come, strong-armed one, terrible and
destroying living things.

"'In all the armies of the son of Dhritarashtra, Keshava, horses, elephants and
men eat little and pass a great deal of dung. The wise say that is a sign of
defeat. They say the Pandavas' animals are cheerful, Krishna, and the beasts go
round them to the right. That is a sign of their victory. All the beasts go
round the son of Dhritarashtra to the left, Keshava, and there are voices with
no body. That is a sign of defeat. Peacocks, flower birds, geese, cranes,
chatakas and flocks of jivamjivakas follow the Pandavas. Vultures, crows, badas,
hawks, yatudhanas, jackals and swarms of flies follow the Kauravas. In the
armies of Dhritarashtra's son there is no sound of drums, and the Pandavas'
drums, they say, sound without being struck. The wells roar like bulls in the
armies of Dhritarashtra's son. That is a sign of defeat.

"'The god has rained flesh and blood, Madhava. A city of the gandharvas has
appeared near the sun, with walls, moats, ramparts and fine gates. A black bar
stands covering the sun at sunrise and sunset, announcing great danger. A single
jackal howls terribly. That is a sign of defeat. Birds with black necks, hanging
down and frightening, fly towards the twilight. That is a sign of defeat. He
hates the brahmins first, slayer of Madhu, and his elders and his loyal
servants. That is a sign of defeat. The east is red, the south is the colour of
weapons, and the west is like an unbaked pot, slayer of Madhu. All the quarters
of Dhritarashtra's son are blazing, Madhava. They announce great danger in these
portents.

"'In a dream I saw Yudhishthira climbing a palace with a thousand pillars with
his brothers, Achyuta. They all wore white turbans and white clothes, and I saw
white seats for all of them. And I saw you in a dream, Krishna, wrapping the
earth, which was smeared with blood, in entrails, Janardana. Yudhishthira, whose
power has no measure, climbed a heap of bones and joyfully ate rice cooked in
milk with butter from a golden bowl. I saw Yudhishthira swallowing the earth.
Clearly he will enjoy this earth that you give him. Vrikodara of terrible deeds,
the tiger among men, had climbed a high mountain with his mace in his hand, as
if looking over this earth. Clearly he will destroy us all in the great battle.
I know, Hrishikesha, that where dharma is, there is victory.

"'Dhananjaya with the Gandiva had mounted a white elephant with you,
Hrishikesha, blazing with the highest splendour. I have no doubt that you will
kill all the kings in battle, Krishna, with Duryodhana at their head. Nakula and
Sahadeva, and the great chariot fighter Satyaki, with clean armlets and neck
guards, wearing white garlands and clothes, tigers among men, had mounted the
best of vehicles, carried by men. These three great ones had white umbrellas and
clothes. In the armies of Dhritarashtra's son, Janardana, three are seen with
white turbans. Know them, Keshava: Ashvatthama, Kripa and Kritavarma the
Satvata. All the other kings are seen with red turbans, Madhava. Bhishma and
Drona had mounted a carriage yoked with camels, Janardana, with me and the son
of Dhritarashtra, strong-armed lord. We were going to the quarter ruled by
Agastya, Janardana. Before long we shall reach Yama's house. I have no doubt
that I and the other kings, all that circle of kshatriyas, will enter the fire
of the Gandiva.'

"'Surely the earth is near its ruin now,' said Krishna, 'since my words do not
reach your heart, Karna. When the destruction of all beings has come, my friend,
wrong that looks like right does not leave the heart.'

"'Perhaps we shall see you, Krishna,' said Karna, 'if we come out alive from
this great battle, strong-armed one, this destruction of heroes. Or else we
shall surely meet in heaven, Krishna. We shall meet there again with you then,
blameless one.'

"When Karna had said this to Madhava," said Sanjaya, "he embraced him tightly.
Then Keshava let him go, and he got down from the chariot. Then Radheya mounted
his own chariot, adorned with gold, and turned back with us, sad at heart. And
Keshava, with Satyaki, went on faster, crying again and again to his charioteer,
'Go! Go!'"

**Divergences.**

* **Draupadi in the offer.** Krishna's offer includes that "at the sixth turn Draupadi will come to you" (5.138.15, *ṣaṣṭhe ca tvāṃ tathā kāle draupady upagamiṣyati*). This is CE. See ledger L-181.
* **Karna already knew.** Karna tells Krishna that he knows he is Kunti's son by the Sun, and that she let him go at the Sun's word (5.139.2–4). He knows it before Kunti speaks to him (5.142–144). The CE does not say how he learned it. In Book 3 the Sun comes to him in a dream "out of love for his son" (3.284.8), but does not tell him so (3.284–286). See ledger L-181 and L-160.
* **Why Karna refuses.** His reasons are his love for Adhiratha and Radha, his wives, sons and grandsons among the sutas, and his loyalty to Duryodhana (5.139.5–18). He asks that the secret be kept because Yudhishthira would give up the kingdom to him, and he would give it to Duryodhana (5.139.20–22).
* **Duhshasana's blood.** Both Karna (5.139.47) and Krishna (5.140.10) speak of Bhima drinking Duhshasana's blood.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*521–\*530, among them Dhaumya's fire offering (\*521), more priests at the sacrifice (\*524–\*525), and a garbled riddle (\*527).

**Choices.**

* 5.138.8 *kānīnaś ca sahoḍhaś ca*: "whether he is born to her as a girl or taken with her at the wedding". Both kinds of son belong to the man who marries the mother.
* 5.138.15 *ṣaṣṭhe … kāle*: "at the sixth turn", that is, Karna would take his turn with Draupadi as a sixth husband.
* 5.138.25: the peoples who would go before him are named as the text names them.
* 5.138.26: Vasushena is Karna's birth name.
* 5.139.7 *piṇḍavyapanayam*: "take away her funeral cakes", that is, abandon his duty as her son.
* 5.139.26 *indragopakavarṇāḥ*: "the colour of cochineal insects", bright red.
* 5.139.29–51: the priests and parts of the sacrifice are named by their Vedic offices. The adhvaryu performs the rite, the hotri recites, the udgatri and prastotri sing, and the brahman oversees. The *śāmitra* is the slaughtering of the victim, and the *avabhṛtha* the final bath.
* 5.139.38: *karṇinālīkanārācāḥ vatsadantopabṛṃhaṇāḥ*: kinds of arrows, named as the text names them.
* 5.139.44: Dhrishtadyumna "born from the fire" (1.155).
* 5.139.52 *vṛthāmṛtyum*: "a death for nothing", that is, anything but death in battle.
* 5.140.4 *bhauvanena*: "the Creator", read with Ganguli as Vishvakarman.
* 5.140.7: "no Treta, no Krita and no Dvapara": only the Kali age will be left.
* 5.140.18: the new moon seven days away, whose deity is Shakra. See TIMELINE.
* 5.141.7 *prājāpatyaṃ nakṣatram*: "Prajapati's star", Rohini.
* 5.141.8 *maitram*: "the friendly one", Anuradha, whose deity is Mitra.
* 5.141.17 *jīvaṃjīvaka*: a kind of bird, left as a name.
* 5.141.33 *yato dharmas tato jayaḥ*: "where dharma is, there is victory", Karna's words here.
* 5.141.37 *naravāhanam*: "carried by men", a litter.
* 5.141.41 *agastyaśāstām … diśam*: "the quarter ruled by Agastya", the south, Yama's.
* 5.141.44 *anayo nayasaṃkāśaḥ*: "wrong that looks like right".

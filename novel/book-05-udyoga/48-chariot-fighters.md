# Chariot Fighters

"Sudakshina of Kamboja is counted a chariot fighter of single worth," said
Bhishma. "He will fight the enemy in battle, wanting to win your aim. The Kurus
will see this lion among chariot fighters fight for you, best of kings, with
courage like Indra's. His chariot troop of Kambojas, who strike with fierce
speed, great king, will come on like locusts. Nila, who lives at Mahishmati, in
his blue armour, is yours. He will make a slaughter of your enemies with his
chariot troop. The king made a feud with Sahadeva long ago, and he will always
fight for you, king, best of the Kurus.

"Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti are two splendid chariot fighters together. They
are skilled in battle, my friend, and firm in strength and courage. Those two
tigers among men will burn up the enemy army with maces, spears, swords and iron
arrows, and with lances thrown by hand. They want war, and in battle they will
roam in the middle of the herd, great king, like two leaders of herds at play,
like Death himself.

"The five Trigarta brothers are fine chariot fighters, to my mind. They have a
feud with Partha from that time at Virata's city. Like crocodiles in the Ganga
when its waves are high, lord of kings, they will stir up the army of the sons
of Pritha in battle. These five chariot fighters, king, with Satyaratha at their
head, will fight in battle remembering what was done before, the wrong done them
by the son of Pandu, Bhimasena's younger brother, with his white horses, when he
conquered the quarters, Bharata. They will meet and kill the great chariot
fighters of the sons of Pritha, the finest great archers, these bearers of the
kshatriyas' yoke.

"Your son Lakshmana and Duhshasana's son are two tigers among men who never turn
back in battle. They are young and tender, strong princes, who know the fine
points of battle and can lead in every way. To my mind these two are the best of
chariot fighters, tiger among chariot fighters. Heroes who love the kshatriya's
dharma, they will do great work. Dandadhara, great king, is a single chariot
fighter, a bull among men. He will go into battle and fight, guarded by his own
army. King Brihadbala of Kosala is the best of chariot fighters, a chariot
fighter to my mind, my friend, firm in speed and courage. This great archer with
his fierce weapons, who delights in the good of Dhritarashtra's son, will fight
in battle and cheer his own army.

"Kripa, son of Sharadvat, king, leader of leaders of chariot troops, will give
up his dear life and burn up your enemies. He was born from a clump of reeds,
the son of the great seer Gautama, the teacher Sharadvat, and like Karttikeya he
cannot be beaten. He will roam through the army, with its many kinds of weapons
and bows, crushing them like fire in battle, my friend.

"Your uncle Shakuni is a single chariot fighter, lord of men. He has made a feud
with the Pandavas and will fight, no doubt of it. His troops are hard to face in
battle and never turn back. Most of them carry strange weapons, and they are as
fast as the wind.

"Drona's son, the great archer, is beyond all bowmen. He fights in many ways in
battle, his weapons are strong, and he is a great chariot fighter. His arrows,
great king, leave his bow in an unbroken stream, like the Gandiva bowman's. I
cannot count this hero, the best of chariot fighters. If the famous one wished,
he could burn up the three worlds. His anger and his fire were built up by
austerity while he lived in the ashram, and he is noble-minded and was favoured
by Drona with divine weapons. But he has one great fault, bull among the
Bharatas, and because of it I count him neither a chariot fighter nor an
outstanding one, best of kings. The brahmin loves his life too much and always
wants to live long. Yet there is no one like him in either army. He could kill
an army of the gods with a single chariot. With the slap of his arm guard, that
big man could split mountains. His virtues are beyond counting. He strikes, he
shines terribly, and he will range like Death, not to be withstood, like the
carrier of the rod. He is like the fire at the end of the age in his anger. He
has a lion's neck and a great mind, Bharata. He will quench the back of the
battle.

"His father, very splendid, is better than the young, old as he is. He will do
great work in battle, I have no doubt. Fanned by the wind of his weapons' speed
and rising from the dry wood of the armies, he will burn up the armies of the
son of Pandu, set on victory. The son of Bharadvaja, bull among men, leader of
the leaders of chariot troops, will do fierce work for your good. The old
teacher and elder of all the consecrated kings may bring the Srinjayas to their
end. But Dhananjaya is dear to him. The great archer would never kill Partha,
whose deeds never fail, remembering his shining teacher's office, which Partha
won by his virtues. The hero always praises Partha's many virtues. Bharadvaja's
son sees him as more than a son. With a single chariot and his divine weapons,
the mighty one could kill gods, gandharvas and danavas, even all together, in
battle.

"The Paurava, a tiger among kings, king, is your great chariot fighter. To my
mind he is a chariot fighter, hero, who breaks the chariots of enemy heroes.
With his own army, scorching the enemy's forces, he will burn up the Panchalas
as the black-pathed fire burns dry grass. Satyavrata, the best of chariot
fighters, a prince and a great chariot fighter, will range like Death in your
enemy's army, king. His fighters, in bright armour and with bright weapons, will
roam the battle, lord of kings, killing your enemies.

"Vrishasena, Karna's son, is your foremost chariot fighter, a great chariot
fighter. The best of the strong will burn up your enemies' forces. Jalasamdha of
Magadha, very splendid, king, is your fine chariot fighter, a killer of enemy
heroes, who will give up his life in battle. The strong-armed one, skilled on an
elephant's back, will fight in battle, or from a chariot, wearing down the enemy
army. To my mind this bull among men is a chariot fighter, great king, and he
will give up his life for you in the great battle with his army. He fights
boldly and skilfully in battle, and he will fight your enemies without fear,
king.

"Bahlika is an outstanding chariot fighter who never turns back in battle. To my
mind, king, he is a hero in battle like Vaivasvata. Once he has come to battle
he will never turn back, king, any more than the wind does, until he has killed
his enemies. Your commander Satyavat, great king, is a great chariot fighter, a
doer of wonderful deeds in battle, a chariot fighter who breaks enemy chariots.
When he sees battle he is not troubled at all. He goes smiling to meet the enemy
on the chariot road. This best of men, bold among his enemies, will do great
work for you in the slaughter, as a good man should.

"Alayudha, the rakshasa lord, cruel in his deeds and mighty, will kill your
enemies, king, remembering an old feud. He is the best chariot fighter of all
the rakshasa armies. He knows magic, his hatred is firm, and he will range
through the battle. Bhagadatta, the brave and mighty lord of Pragjyotisha, is
the best of those who carry the elephant goad, and skilled on a chariot too.
Once he fought the Gandiva bowman, king, for many days, each of them eager to
win. Then, son of Gandhari, out of respect for his friend, the chastiser of
Paka, he made an agreement with the great Pandava. Skilled on an elephant's
back, the king will fight in battle like Vasava of the gods mounted on Airavata.

"Achala and Vrishaka, the two brothers together, are your chariot fighters, hard
to attack. They will destroy your enemies. They are strong tigers among men,
firm in their anger, men who strike, the young chiefs of Gandhara, handsome and
very strong.

"And there is your friend, always dear to you, who is so rough in battle and who
urges you on to war with the Pandavas, king: harsh, boastful, low Karna
Vaikartana, your counsellor, your guide and your friend, proud and very lofty.
He is not a full chariot fighter, nor an outstanding one, king. He has lost the
armour he was born with, and he is foolish. He has lost his two divine earrings,
and he is always too soft-hearted. Because of Rama's curse, and the brahmin's
words, and the loss of his gear, I count him only half a chariot fighter. He
will not escape alive once he meets Phalguna."

"It is as you say," said strong-armed Drona, the best of those who bear weapons,
as Sanjaya told it. "There is nothing false in it. In battle after battle he is
seen to be too proud, and to turn away. Karna is too soft-hearted and careless,
and so to my mind he is half a chariot fighter."

When Radheya heard this, his eyes widened with anger, and he spoke to Bhishma,
stinging him with words like a goad.

"Grandfather, you cut me as you please with the arrows of your words, though I
have done nothing, always out of hatred, just like this, at every step. And I
bear it all for Duryodhana's sake. You think me weak, like a coward. To my mind
it is you who are half a chariot fighter, no doubt of it. I tell the whole world
the truth, son of Ganga: you are always against the good of the Kurus, and the
king does not see it. For who among equals, all of noble deeds, would destroy a
man's fire like this in battle, wanting to split them, as you try to do wrong by
listing virtues? A kshatriya's rank as a great chariot fighter cannot be counted
by years, or grey hairs, or wealth, or kin, Kaurava. Kshatriyas are held senior
by strength, brahmins by their mantras, vaishyas by wealth and shudras by age.
You make chariot fighters and outstanding chariot fighters as you please, taking
what you like, driven by desire and hatred, in your folly.

"Strong-armed Duryodhana, look into this well. Get rid of this evil-minded
Bhishma, who does you harm. An army once split is hard to mend, king, even its
core troops, tiger among men, let alone one gathered from many places. A split
has arisen among these fighters in battle, Bharata. Our fire is being destroyed
in front of our eyes. What does Bhishma, with his little mind, know of chariot
fighters? I shall hold back the Pandavas' army. When they meet me, whose arrows
never fail, the Pandavas and the Panchalas will run to the ten quarters, like
bulls from a tiger. What does Bhishma know of the crush of battle, of counsel or
of good speech? He is past his age, a dullard deluded by time. He always rivals
the whole world, and thinks no other man anything, with his useless eyes. The
books show that one should listen to the old, but not to the too old, for they
are children again. I alone shall kill the Pandavas, no doubt of it. But if I
fight well, tiger among kings, the fame will go to Bhishma. For you have made
this Bhishma your commander, lord of men, and the credit goes to the commander,
never to the fighters. I shall not fight at all while Ganga's son lives, king.
But when Bhishma is killed I shall fight all the great chariot fighters."

"This great load has been taken up by me," said Bhishma, "as great as the ocean,
the war for the son of Dhritarashtra that I have thought about for many years.
Now that the time has come, burning and hair-raising, I must not make a split
among us. That is why you are alive, suta's son. Old as I am, I could still
break your faith in war, child that you are, and your hold on life, in battle
today, suta's son. When Rama Jamadagnya loosed his great weapons at me, I felt
no pain at all. What can you do to me?

"Good men do not praise such boasting of one's own strength. But since I am
burning, I shall tell you, low disgrace to your family. When all the kshatriyas
of the earth were gathered at the bridegroom choice of the king of Kashi, I beat
them with a single chariot and carried off the girls by force. I have driven off
thousands of men like that, and better, with their armies, alone on the field of
battle. Since you, the man of feuds, came to the Kurus, great disaster has come
upon them for their destruction. Try your best and be a man. Fight Partha in
battle, the man you are always rivalling. I shall see you come out of this war,
you evil-minded man."

"Look to me, son of Ganga," said the great-minded King Dhritarashtra's son to
him, as Sanjaya told it, "for a great work is at hand. Think first of what is
best of all for me. Both of you will do great work for me. I want to hear more
of the best chariot fighters of the enemy, and their outstanding chariot
fighters, and the leaders of their chariot troops. I want to hear the strength
and weakness of the enemy, Kaurava. When the night has turned to morning, this
battle will begin."

"I have counted your chariot fighters, king," said Bhishma, "and your
outstanding and your half chariot fighters. Now hear about the Pandavas', if you
are curious about the Pandavas' army today, king, the count of their chariot
fighters, strong-armed one, with these lords of the earth.

"The king himself, the Pandava, joy of Kunti, is a splendid chariot fighter. He
will range the battle like fire, my friend, no doubt of it. Bhimasena, king of
kings, is a chariot fighter worth eight. He is proud and has the strength of ten
thousand elephants, and in his fire he is more than human. The two sons of Madri
are chariot fighters, two bulls among men, like the Ashvins in looks and full of
fire. At the head of their armies, remembering their own sufferings, they will
range like Rudra, I have no doubt.

"All these great ones stand out like shala trees. They are taller than other men
by a span. All the mighty sons of Pandu are built like lions. All have kept the
vow of chastity and all are great ascetics. They are modest tigers among men,
fierce in strength like tigers. In speed, in striking and in close fighting they
are all more than human. All of them conquered kings in the conquest of the
quarters, bull among the Bharatas. No men can bear their weapons, maces and
arrows, Kaurava, or even string their bows, or lift their heavy maces, or draw
their arrows. In speed, in hitting the mark, in eating and in the dust games,
they beat all of you even when they were boys. When they meet your army they
will destroy it in battle, like tigers fierce in strength. Do not meet them.
Each one of them could kill all the kings in battle, as happened before your
eyes at the Rajasuya, king of kings. Remembering Draupadi's torment and the
harsh words at the dicing, they will range the battle like Death.

"Red-eyed Gudakesha, with Narayana as his ally, is a chariot fighter such as
there is none in either army, hero. Neither among the gods, nor the danavas or
snakes before, nor the rakshasas or yakshas, let alone men, have I heard of any
chariot fighter, past or to come, so equipped as wise Partha, great king.
Vasudeva is his driver and Dhananjaya his fighter. There is the divine Gandiva
bow and horses as swift as the wind. There is the divine armour that cannot be
pierced and the two great quivers that never empty. There is the host of
weapons, great Indra's, Rudra's, Kubera's, Yama's and Varuna's, and maces
terrible to see, and the chief weapons led by the thunderbolt. He killed
thousands of danavas who lived in Hiranyapura with a single chariot in battle.
What chariot fighter is his equal?

"In his fury this strong-armed, mighty man, whose courage is true, could destroy
your army while he protects his own. Only I could go out against Dhananjaya, or
the teacher. There is no third in either army, lord of kings, who as a chariot
fighter could meet him as he rains showers of arrows, like a cloud driven by a
great wind at the end of the hot season. The son of Kunti is armed and has
Vasudeva as his ally, and he is young and skilled. We two are both old."

"When the kings heard this from Bhishma," said Sanjaya, "their thick arms,
bright with golden armlets and smeared with sandal, sank. So did their hearts,
in their agitation, as they remembered the old strength of the Pandavas, which
they had seen with their own eyes."

**Divergences.**

* **Karna "half a chariot fighter".** Bhishma rates Karna *ardharatha* because he has lost his natural armour and earrings, because of Rama's curse and "the brahmin's words", and because he is soft-hearted and careless; Drona agrees (5.165.5–8). Karna says Bhishma is the half chariot fighter and swears again not to fight while Bhishma lives (5.165.11, 27). This is CE. See ledger L-184.
* **Ashvatthama.** Bhishma counts Ashvatthama neither a chariot fighter nor an outstanding one, because "the brahmin loves his life too much" (5.164.7–8), though no one in either army is his equal.
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*564–\*567, among them the Trigartas forced to pay tribute (\*564).

# The Worlds Below

"As Matali went on his way," said Kanva, "he happened to meet the great seer
Narada, who was going to see Varuna.

"'Where are you off to, sir?' Narada asked him. 'Is it on your own business,
charioteer, or at the command of Shatakratu?'

"When Narada asked him this on the road, Matali told him the whole of his
business with Varuna, just as it was.

"'Let us go together,' said the sage. 'I too have set out from heaven to see the
lord of the waters. I shall tell you all about it as I show you the world under
the earth. When we have looked there, Matali, we shall choose some husband.'

"So Matali and Narada, the two great ones, went down into the earth, and they
saw the lord of the waters, the guardian of the world. Narada was given the
honour due to a divine seer, and Matali the honour due to great Indra. The two
of them were pleased, and they told Varuna their business. With his leave they
wandered through the world of the nagas. Narada knew all the beings who live
under the earth, and he explained everything to the charioteer in full.

"'You have seen Varuna, my friend,' said Narada, 'with his sons and grandsons.
Now look at the home of the lord of the waters, rich and lovely on every side.
This is the son of Varuna, lord of the waters, very wise. He surpasses him in
character, conduct and purity. This is his beloved son Pushkara, the lotus-eyed.
He is handsome and fine to look at, and the daughter of Soma has chosen him for
her husband. They call her Jyotsnakali, a second Shri in her beauty.

"'Look at the house of Varuni, all made of gold. It was by winning her that the
gods became suras, friend of the lord of the suras. These, Matali, are the
weapons of the daityas who lost their kingdoms, blazing all around. They say
these weapons never wear out, and they come back, Matali. They are used by
power, and the gods won them. Here, Matali, lived races of rakshasas and races
of spirits with divine weapons, made by the gods of old.

"'This fire with its great flames keeps watch in Varuna's lake, and here is
Vishnu's discus, ringed with smokeless fire. This is the bow made of gandi wood,
gathered for the destruction of the world. The gods always guard it, and from it
comes the Gandiva bow. When the need arises, it always holds the strength of a
hundred thousand lives. It rules even the unruly among kings who are kin to
rakshasas. It is the first-born rod, made by Brahma, the speaker of Brahman.

"'This is the great umbrella of the kings, of which Shakra has spoken. The sons
of the king of the waters hold it, and it brings great fortune. This umbrella of
the king of the waters stands in the umbrella house. It rains cool water on
every side, like a cloud. The water that falls from this umbrella, clear as the
moon, is wrapped in such darkness that no one can see it. There are many
wonderful things to be seen here, Matali, but your business would be held up, so
let us go on quickly.

"'This city stands at the navel of the world of the nagas. It is called Patala,
and daityas and danavas live there. Whatever moves or stands still and comes
here with the waters cries out loudly in fear as it enters. Here the asura fire
burns always, feeding on water. It knows itself held back and bound. Here the
gods, when they had killed their enemies, drank the nectar and stored what was
left. That is why the moon is seen to wane and wax. Here the divine horse's head
rises, golden, at each turn of the moon, filling the world with water. Because
all the forms of water fall here, this great city is called Patala.

"'From here Airavata, for the good of the world, takes up the cool water and
lets it go into the clouds, and great Indra rains it down. Here live the timis,
water creatures of many shapes and kinds, who drink the moon's light in the
water. Here, charioteer, creatures pierced by the sun's rays die in the day at
the bottom of Patala, and at night they live again. For the moon rises here
every day, wrapped in its rays, and touches them with nectar, and brings the
living back to life by its touch. Here live the daityas who delighted in
adharma, bound and crushed by time, robbed of their fortune by Vasava. Here the
Lord of Beings, great lord of all beings, practised the highest austerity for
the good of all beings.

"'Here live brahmins who keep the cow vow, great seers worn thin by study and
recitation, who have given up their lives and won heaven. The man who lies down
wherever he is, eats whatever he gets and wears whatever he finds is said to
keep the cow vow. Airavata, king of the elephants, Vamana, Kumuda and Anjana,
the best of elephants, were born in the line of Supratika. See if any husband
here pleases you for his virtues, Matali. We shall go to him and try to win him.

"'This egg lies in the water, blazing with splendour. Since creatures were first
made it has neither broken nor moved. I have never heard its birth or its nature
told, and no one knows its father or its mother. They say that from it a great
fire will rise at the end of time, Matali, and burn the three worlds with all
that moves and stands still.'

"When Matali heard what Narada said, he told him, 'No one here pleases me. Let
us go somewhere else, and quickly.'

"'This is the great city called Hiranyapura,' said Narada, 'the city of the
daityas and danavas, who use a hundred kinds of magic. Vishvakarman built it
with no small effort, and Maya conceived it in his mind. It lies at the bottom
of Patala. Here live mighty danavas who work a thousand kinds of magic, heroes
who were given boons long ago. Neither Shakra nor anyone else can bring them
under his power, not Varuna, nor Yama, nor the giver of wealth. Here live asuras
and Kalakhanjas, those born from Vishnu's footprint, Nairritas and Yatudhanas,
and those born from Brahma's Veda. They have fangs and terrible shapes, and they
live guarding themselves. They have the power of magic, and they live guarding
themselves. There are the danavas called Nivatakavachas, mad for battle. You
know how Shakra cannot overcome them. Many times, Matali, you and your son
Gomukha have been routed, and the king of the gods too, Shachi's husband, with
his son.

"'Look at the houses of gold, Matali, and of silver, built and furnished by
rule. Some look green as beryl, some are bright with coral, some shine like the
sun and crystal, and some blaze with the core of diamond. Some shine as if they
were made of earth, and some as if made of trees. Some look like mountains, and
some like stars. Some shine like the sun, some like blazing fire. They are
bright with nets of jewels, tall and close together. They cannot be described by
their shape or by what they are made of. They are perfect in their qualities and
in their proportions. Look at the pleasure grounds of the daityas, and their
beds, their costly jewelled vessels and seats. Look at the mountains like
clouds, with springs of water, and the trees that go where they please and bear
flowers and fruit at will. Matali, perhaps some husband here pleases you. Or let
us go to another part of the earth, if you think so.'

"'Divine seer,' Matali said to him as he spoke, 'I must not do anything that the
dwellers in heaven would dislike. The gods and the danavas are brothers, and
there is always a feud between them. How could I want a marriage tie with the
enemy's side? Let us go somewhere else. I should not even look at the danavas.
And I know myself, how eager I am to give her away.'

"'This is the world of the Suparnas,' said Narada, 'the birds who eat snakes. In
courage, in flight and in carrying loads they never tire. This family,
charioteer, has spread out from Vinata's son's six sons, Sumukha, Sunaman,
Sunetra and Suvarchas, Surupa, king of the birds, and Subala, Matali. The
founders of Vinata's line made it grow by their children. There are hundreds of
thousands of them born of the stock of the king of the birds, in Kashyapa's
line, and they spread its fortune. All of them have fortune, and all are marked
with the Shrivatsa. All want fortune, and all are strong. By their deeds they
are kshatriyas. They are pitiless and they eat snakes. Because they destroy
their own kin, they do not become brahmins.

"'I shall tell you their names in order of rank. Listen, Matali, for this family
is praised, and Vishnu holds it as his own. Vishnu is their god and Vishnu is
their refuge. Vishnu is always in their hearts, and Vishnu is always their goal.
Here are Suvarnachuda, Nagashin, Daruna and Chandatundaka, Anala and Anila,
Vishalaksha, Kundalin, Kashyapi, Dhvajavishkambha, Vainateya and Vamana,
Vatavega, Dishachakshus, Nimesha and Nimisha. Here are Trivara, Saptavara,
Valmiki and Dvipaka, Daityadvipa, Sariddvipa, Sarasa and Padmakesara, Sumukha,
Sukhaketu, Chitrabarha and Anagha, Meghakrit, Kumuda, Daksha, Sarpanta and
Somabhojana. Here are Gurubhara, Kapota, Suryanetra and Chirantaka,
Vishnudhanvan, Kumara, Paribarha and Hari, Susvara, Madhuparka and Hemavarna,
Malaya, Matarishvan, Nishakara and Divakara. These sons of Garuda I have named
for one region only, the chief ones, by their fame and their strength. If
nothing here pleases you, come, Matali, let us go. I shall take you to a land
where you will find what you like.'

"'This is called Rasatala,' said Narada, 'the seventh level of the earth. Here
lives Surabhi, the mother of cows, born of the nectar. She always streams with
milk, born of the essence of the earth, a single flavour, the best, from the
essence of the six flavours. Long ago the Grandfather was filled with nectar and
brought up its essence, and the blameless one rose from his mouth. Where the
stream of her milk fell on the earth, a lake was made, the Ocean of Milk, holy
and supreme. Its edges are ringed with foam, like flowers. The Phenapas, the
best of sages, live here drinking it. They are called Phenapas, Matali, because
they live on foam, and they practise such fierce austerity that the gods fear
them.

"'Four other cows are born of her, Matali, and live in all the quarters. They
guard the quarters, and they are said to hold them up. The daughter of Surabhi
named Surupa holds up the eastern quarter. Hamsaka holds up the southern. The
western quarter, Varuna's, is always held up by great Subhadra, Matali, who has
every form. The cow named Sarvakamadugha holds up the northern quarter, Matali,
the righteous one, called after Ailavila. The gods and the asuras together
churned the milk of these cows, mixed with the water of the ocean, with Mount
Mandara as the churning stick. From it came Varuni, Lakshmi and the nectar,
Matali, Uchchaihshravas the king of horses, and the Kaustubha jewel. Surabhi
gives milk that becomes sudha for those who live on sudha, svadha for those who
live on svadha, and nectar for those who live on nectar.

"'There is an old verse that the people of Rasatala sang long ago. It is still
heard in the world, and the wise sing it.'"

> *Not in the world of the nagas, nor in heaven,*
> *nor in a flying palace, nor in the highest heaven,*
> *is there a dwelling as happy*
> *as on the floor of Rasatala.*

**Divergences.**

* **Varuni.** A rejected line (\*436) names Varuni plainly as wine (*surā*). In the CE the house is Varuni's and the gods became *suras* by winning her (5.96.14). The pun on *surā*, wine, and *sura*, god, is the text's.
* **Matali's last words in Hiranyapura.** Ganguli has Matali tell Narada, "I know thy heart is ever set on fomenting quarrels". The CE line (5.98.19cd) is obscure and is rendered as Matali's own eagerness to give his daughter away; see Choices.
* **The horse's head.** Ganguli, following another reading, has the horse-headed one fill the world "with the sound of Vedic hymns". The CE has *vārbhiḥ*, "with water" (5.97.5).

**Choices.**

* Kanva's narration stays inside double quotes, as at the end of the last chapter. Narada's and Matali's speech is in single quotes within it.
* 5.96.2 *śatakratoḥ*: Shatakratu, Indra.
* 5.96.13: the second half of the verse, which Ganguli takes to mean that Jyotsnakali had once chosen "the eldest of Aditi's sons", is obscure and is not rendered.
* 5.96.16 *vivartante*: "they come back", read with Ganguli as weapons that return.
* 5.96.19 *gāṇḍīmayaś cāpaḥ*: "the bow made of gandi wood". The Gandiva takes its name from it.
* 5.96.21: the verse calls it "the first-born rod". It is taken as said of the bow, as Ganguli takes it.
* 5.96.22 *chakreṇa bhāṣitam*: "of which Shakra has spoken".
* 5.97.3 *vyāpāreṇa dhṛtātmānaṃ nibaddhaṃ samabudhyata*: "It knows itself held back and bound." The line is obscure.
* 5.97.5 *parvaṇi parvaṇi*: "at each turn of the moon", at the new and full moons.
* 5.97.6: the name Patala is explained from *pat*, "fall".
* 5.97.12 *bhūtapatiḥ*: "the Lord of Beings", taken by Ganguli as Mahadeva.
* 5.97.14: the definition of the cow vow is the text's.
* 5.98.2 *mayena manasā sṛṣṭam*: "Maya conceived it in his mind".
* 5.98.5 *viṣṇupadodbhavāḥ*: "those born from Vishnu's footprint"; *brahmavedodbhavāḥ*, "those born from Brahma's Veda". Both are obscure, and are given literally.
* 5.98.19cd *jānāmi tu tathātmānaṃ ditsātmakamalaṃ yathā*: read as "And I know myself, how eager I am to give her away". The reading is uncertain.
* 5.99.2–3: the six sons of Garuda are named as the text names them. *surūpapakṣirājena*: "Surupa, king of the birds".
* 5.99.9–14: the long list of Garuda's descendants is given in full.
* 5.99.15 *pradeśamātreṇa*: "for one region only".
* 5.100.3: Surabhi rose from the Grandfather's mouth when, filled with nectar, he brought up its essence.
* 5.100.10 *ailavilasaṃjñitām*: "called after Ailavila", that is, Kubera, lord of the north.
* 5.100.13: *sudhā*, *svadhā* and *amṛta* are the foods of different classes of beings, and are left as names.
* 5.100.15: the verse is set as verse. It is the end of Narada's speech.

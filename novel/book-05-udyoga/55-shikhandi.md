# Shikhandi

"Then Shikhandi's mother, lord of men," said Bhishma, "told her husband the
truth about the girl Shikhandini, strong-armed one.

"'I had no son, king, and I was afraid of my co-wives. So when the girl
Shikhandini was born, I gave out that she was a boy. And you, best of men,
agreed to it out of love for me. The rites for a son were done for a girl, bull
among kings, and you brought home the daughter of the lord of the Dasharnas as a
wife, king. And you said before, looking to the meaning of the god's word, "My
child will be a girl and then a man." So we let it be.'

"When he had heard this, King Drupada Yajnasena told the whole truth to his
counsellors, and then he took counsel, king, on everything that would help to
protect his people. Knowing that the marriage tie with the Dasharna king held,
and that he himself had done the deceiving, he gave all his mind to the counsel
and came to a decision. His city was well guarded by its nature, Bharata. But in
that time of danger, king of kings, he had it guarded on every side and made
ready.

"The king was in the deepest torment with his wife, bull among the Bharatas, at
the quarrel with the lord of the Dasharnas. 'How can I avoid a great war with my
relation by marriage?' he thought in his mind, and he worshipped the gods.

"When the queen saw him so devoted to the gods, king, and making offerings, she
said to him, 'Worship of the gods is always true and approved by the good. Now
that we have fallen into a sea of sorrow, let us worship hard. Let all the gods
be honoured with rich gifts, and let offerings be poured into the fires to keep
off the Dasharna king. Think in your mind how to turn him back without a battle,
lord. By the grace of the gods all this will come about. And whatever you have
decided with your ministers, large-eyed one, so that this city is not destroyed,
do that too, king. For fate joined with human effort succeeds best, lord of the
earth. When the two are at odds, neither succeeds. So make your arrangements in
the city with your ministers, and worship the gods as you wish, lord of the
people.'

"The spirited girl Shikhandini saw the two of them talking like this, sunk in
grief, and she was ashamed. Then she thought, 'The two of them are in sorrow
because of me,' and she made up her mind to end her life. When she had resolved
on this, deep in grief, she left home and went to a thick forest where no one
lived. It was guarded, king, by the mighty yaksha Sthunakarna, and people kept
away from that forest for fear of him. There was Sthuna's house, plastered with
lime and clay, full of the smoke of offerings of parched grain, with high walls
and a high gate. Drupada's daughter Shikhandi went into it, king, and wasted her
body without eating for many days.

"The yaksha Sthuna showed himself to her. 'What is it you have set out to do?'
he said. 'Tell me quickly, and I will do it.'

"'It cannot be done,' she said to the yaksha again and again.

"'I will do it,' the guhyaka answered her. 'I am a follower of the lord of
wealth, and I grant boons, princess. I will give even what cannot be given. Tell
me what you want to say.'

"Then Shikhandi told the whole of it, Bharata, to Sthunakarna, the chief of the
yakshas. 'My father is in trouble, yaksha, and before long he will be destroyed.
For the lord of the Dasharnas will march against him in anger, that king in the
golden armour, of great strength and great energy. So save me, yaksha, and my
father and my mother. You have promised to take away my sorrow. Let me become a
man by your grace, yaksha, and be without blame. Until that king comes to my
city, great yaksha, show me that grace, guhyaka.'

"When he had heard Shikhandi's words, bull among the Bharatas, the yaksha,
pressed by fate, thought in his mind, 'It must be so, to my sorrow, Kaurava.'
And he said, 'Good lady, I will do what you wish. But hear my condition. I will
give you this manhood of mine for a certain time. You must come back when the
time comes. I tell you this truly. I am a lord, what I purpose comes to pass, I
change my form at will and I go through the sky. By my grace, save your city and
all your kin. I will bear your womanhood, princess. Promise me truly, and I will
do what you want.'

"'I will give back your manhood, lord,' said Shikhandi. 'Bear womanhood for a
certain time, night-rover. When King Hemavarma of the Dasharnas has gone back, I
will be a girl again, and you will be a man.'

"When they had said this, king, the two of them made the compact there, not to
wrong each other, and they made the exchange. The yaksha Sthuna took on
womanhood, lord of men, and Shikhandi took on the yaksha's blazing form. Then
Shikhandi of the Panchalas, having won manhood, lord of the earth, went into the
city gladly, came to his father and told Drupada everything as it had happened.
When Drupada heard it from him, he was filled with the greatest joy, and with
his wife he remembered the words of Maheshvara. Then he sent word to the lord of
the Dasharnas, king: 'This son of mine is a man. Believe me.'

"Then the Dasharna king came suddenly to Drupada, king of the Panchalas, full of
grief and resentment. When the lord of the Dasharnas had reached Kampilya, he
sent an envoy with honour, the best of those who know Brahman. 'Say to that
Panchala, lowest of kings, in my words, envoy: "Because you chose my daughter
for your daughter, you fool, you will see the fruit of that arrogance today, no
doubt of it."'

"When the king had told him this, best of kings, the brahmin envoy set out for
the city at the Dasharna king's bidding. The priest came to Drupada in the city.
The Panchala king had a cow and water for a guest brought to him with every
honour, king of kings, with Shikhandi. He did not welcome that honour, and he
gave the message that the hero King Kanchanavarma had spoken. 'You of the lowest
conduct, I have been deceived by you over a daughter. Reap the fruit of that
sin, you fool. Give me battle today, lord of men, at the head of the battle. I
will uproot you at once, with your ministers, your sons and your kin.'

"The king heard this, full of reproach, from the Dasharna king's envoy, the
priest, in the midst of his ministers. Then Drupada, best of the Bharatas,
bowing out of friendship, said, 'Brahmin, for the words you have spoken to me at
my kinsman's word, an envoy of mine will give the reply.'

"Then Drupada too sent an envoy to the great Hiranyavarma, a brahmin who had
mastered the Veda. When he met the king, the lord of the Dasharnas, king, he
gave the message Drupada had sent. 'Let it be tested openly. My child is a boy.
Someone has told you a lie, and it is not to be believed.'

"When the king heard Drupada's words, he was full of doubt, and he sent young
women, the finest and loveliest, to find out whether Shikhandi was a woman or a
man. The women he sent learned the truth, lord of the Kurus, and gladly told the
king everything: Shikhandi was a man, of great power.

"Then, when he had made his test, the king was pleased. He met his kinsman
gladly and stayed there with him. The lord of men, in his delight, gave
Shikhandi wealth, elephants, horses and cows, and slave women by the hundred.
Honoured, he went home, and he rebuked his daughter, so it is said. When King
Hemavarma was pleased, his offence forgiven, and the Dasharna king had gone
back, Shikhandini was delighted.

"After some time, Kubera, who is carried by men, came to Sthuna's dwelling as he
toured the world. The guardian of wealth stopped above the house and looked
down. He saw the house of the yaksha Sthuna adorned with strings of garlands,
bright with parched grain and perfumes and canopies, fragrant with incense, hung
with flags and banners, and offerings made of food, drink and meat. When he saw
that place of Sthuna's adorned on every side, the lord of the yakshas said to
the yakshas who followed him, 'This house of Sthuna's is well adorned, you of
boundless valour. Why does that dullard not come to me today? He knows I am
here, the fool, and he does not come to me. So I think a heavy punishment should
be laid on him.'

"'King,' the yakshas said, 'a daughter was born to King Drupada, Shikhandini.
For some reason Sthuna gave her the marks of a man and took on the marks of a
woman. He stays at home as a woman. That is why he does not come to you. He is
ashamed, in a woman's form. For this reason, king, Sthuna does not see you
today. Now you have heard, do as is right. Let the flying chariot stop here.'

"'Bring Sthuna here,' said the lord of the yakshas. 'I shall punish him,' he
said again and again. Summoned, he came to the lord of the yakshas, lord of the
earth, and stood there, great king, in a woman's form and full of shame. The
giver of wealth, in great anger, cursed him, joy of the Kurus. 'Let this wicked
one's womanhood stay just as it is, guhyakas.' Then the great lord of the
yakshas said, 'You despised the yakshas, wicked-minded one. You gave Shikhandi
your marks and took the marks of a woman, doer of evil. Because you have done
this thing that was never done before, you fool, from this day you are a woman
and he is a man.'

"Then the yakshas tried to appease Vaishravana, so it is said. 'Set an end to
the curse, for Sthuna's sake,' they said again and again. Then the great lord of
the yakshas answered all the yaksha hosts who followed him, my son, wanting to
set an end to the curse. 'When Shikhandi is killed in battle, he will regain his
own form. Let the yaksha Sthuna be free of care.' When the blessed god, honoured
by yakshas and rakshasas, had said this, he went on his way with all of them,
who travel in the blink of an eye.

"Sthuna, under the curse, stayed there. And when the time came, Shikhandi went
to the night-rover. He came to him and said, 'I have come, lord.'

"Sthuna said to him again and again, 'I am pleased.' Seeing that Prince
Shikhandi had come in good faith, he told Shikhandi everything as it had
happened. 'I have been cursed by Vaishravana because of you, prince. Go now as
you please, and wander the worlds happily. I think this was fated long ago and
could not be passed by: your going, and the son of Pulastya seeing me.'

"When the yaksha Sthuna had said this, Bharata, Shikhandi went back to the city
full of joy. He honoured the twice-born, the gods, the shrines and the
crossroads with all kinds of costly perfumes and garlands. Drupada of the
Panchalas, with his son Shikhandi, whose purpose was achieved, and with his kin,
felt the greatest joy. And he gave Shikhandi, his son who had been a woman
before, to Drona as a pupil, bull of the Kurus, great king. The prince Shikhandi
learned the four-footed science of archery, with all of you and with
Dhrishtadyumna, son of Prishata.

"My spies told me all this as it was, my son, the ones I had set on Drupada,
looking like idiots, the blind and the deaf. So this son of Drupada, great king,
a woman and a man, came to be Shikhandi, best of the Kauravas, a fine chariot
fighter. The eldest daughter of the lord of Kashi, famed by the name of Amba,
was born in Drupada's family as Shikhandi, bull among the Bharatas.

"When he comes up bow in hand, eager to fight, I would not look at him for a
moment, nor strike him. This is my vow always, famous on earth too: I will not
loose arrows at a woman, nor at one who was a woman before, nor at one with a
woman's name, nor at one in a woman's form, joy of the Kurus. For this reason I
will not kill Shikhandi. I know this truth about Shikhandi's birth, my son. So I
will not kill him in battle, even when he comes at me to kill. If Bhishma were
to kill a woman, he would be killing himself as well. So I will not kill him,
even when I see him standing in battle."

When he had heard this, said Sanjaya, King Duryodhana, the Kauravya, thought for
a moment and judged it right for Bhishma.

**Divergences.**

* **How Shikhandi became a man.** In the CE there is no change at birth. The yaksha Sthunakarna lends Shikhandini his manhood for a time and takes her womanhood (5.193.2–8). Kubera's curse makes the exchange last until Shikhandi dies in battle (5.193.41–46). See ledger L-189.
* **Bhishma's vow.** He will not loose arrows at a woman, one who was a woman before, one with a woman's name or one in a woman's form (5.193.62–63). "The eldest daughter of the lord of Kashi, famed by the name of Amba, was born in Drupada's family as Shikhandi" (5.193.60). See ledger L-185 and L-188.

summary: Janamejaya asks why no one could turn Duryodhana back; Narada, in the Kuru hall, warns against obstinacy and tells how Galava's persistence cost him; Vishvamitra, tested by Dharma, stands a hundred years holding food on his head, served by his pupil Galava; when Galava insists on paying a teacher's fee, Vishvamitra demands eight hundred white horses each with one black ear; in despair Galava turns to his friend Garuda, who describes the four quarters of the world and carries him east, until Galava, overwhelmed by the flight, begs him to stop; Garuda sets down on Mount Rishabha.

# Galava's Fee

"He was set on harm," said Janamejaya. "He was confused by greed for what
belonged to others, and he took pleasure in base men. He had made up his mind to
die. He brought grief to his kin, sorrow to his relations and trouble to his
friends, and joy to his enemies. Why did his relations not hold him back when he
was on the wrong road? Why did the blessed one not do it, out of friendship,
kind as he was to his friends, or the grandfather?"

"The blessed one spoke," said Vaishampayana, "and Bhishma said what was fitting.
Narada too said many things. Hear it.

"'A friend who listens is hard to find,' said Narada, 'and so is a friend who
wants your good. Where a friend stands, a kinsman does not stand. I think one
should listen to one's friends, joy of the Kurus, and not be obstinate, for
obstinacy is very cruel. There is an old story they tell about this, of how
Galava came to grief through obstinacy.

"'Long ago, when Vishvamitra was practising austerities, Dharma came to test
him, taking the form of the blessed seer Vasishtha, one of the seven seers,
Bharata. He came to Kaushika's ashram hungry and wanting to eat, king.
Vishvamitra hurried and cooked rice in milk with great care. But Dharma did not
wait for it. He ate food that other ascetics gave him. When Vishvamitra came up
with the food, very hot, the blessed one said, "I have eaten. Wait there for
now," and went away. So Vishvamitra, king, stayed standing where he was, the
splendid one, holding the food on his head with both hands. He stood near the
ashram like a post, not moving, living on air.

"'The sage Galava took pains to serve him, out of respect and honour, out of
love and a wish to please him. When a hundred years were full, Dharma came again
to Kaushika, in Vasishtha's form, wanting food. He saw the food held on his head
by the wise great seer Vishvamitra, standing and living on air. Dharma took it,
as hot and as fresh as it had been, and ate it. "I am pleased, brahmin seer," he
said, and the sage went away. At Dharma's words Vishvamitra left the kshatriya
state and became a brahmin, and he was glad.

"'Vishvamitra was pleased with the service and devotion of his pupil, the
ascetic Galava, and said to him, "You have my leave, my son. Go where you like,
Galava."

"'Galava was glad, and answered the splendid Vishvamitra, best of sages, in a
sweet voice. "What fee shall I give you for teaching me? A man's work succeeds
when it is done with a fee. The man who gives fees enjoys the fruit of the
sacrifice in heaven, and the fee is called its fulfilment. What shall I bring
for my teacher? Tell me, my lord."

"'The blessed Vishvamitra knew that he had been won by Galava's service, and he
told him again and again, "Go, go." Though Vishvamitra said "Go, go" again and
again, Galava said many times, "What shall I give?" At the ascetic Galava's
persistence, Vishvamitra grew somewhat angry and said, "Give me eight hundred
horses, white as the moon, each with one black ear. Go, Galava, and be quick."

"'When wise Vishvamitra said this, Galava did not sit, sleep or eat. He grew as
thin as skin and bone, and pale, given up to worry and grief. He grieved beyond
measure and burned with distress.

"'"Where are my rich friends?" he said. "Where is my wealth, my store? Where
shall I get eight hundred horses white as the moon? Where is my wish to eat, or
my wish for any happiness? Even my wish to live is cut off. What use is my life?
I shall go to the far shore of the ocean, or to the furthest end of the earth,
and let my life go. What good is my life? What happiness can there be for a man
with no wealth, who has not done what he meant to, who has lost all its rewards
and carries a debt, and can do nothing? Death is better than life for a man who
has enjoyed his friends' wealth and asked what he wanted and cannot repay it.

"'"When a man has promised, 'I shall do it,' and does not do what he should,
burned by his lie, the merit of his sacrifices and his gifts is lost. The liar
has no beauty and no offspring. The liar has no lordship, let alone a good end.
How can an ungrateful man have fame, or a place, or happiness? No one trusts an
ungrateful man, and for the ungrateful there is no atonement. A poor man who
does wrong does not live. How can a wrongdoer support anyone? The wrongdoer, who
destroys what was done for him, surely meets destruction. I am that wrongdoer,
ungrateful, wretched and false. I have had my teacher's help, and I do not do
what I said. I shall make the greatest effort and then let my life go.

"'"I have never asked anything of the gods before, and all the thirty gods
honour me at the sacrifice. I shall go to Vishnu, the best of the gods, lord of
the three worlds, Krishna, the refuge of those who have a refuge. From him all
enjoyments come and spread to the gods and the asuras. I want to go, purified,
and see the great yogi, who does not change."

"'When he had said this, his friend Garuda, son of Vinata, showed himself and
spoke to him, glad and wanting to please him. "I count you as my friend, and my
friends count you as a friend. A friend with the means should be put to use for
what a friend wants. I have the means, brahmin, twice-born one. I spoke to
Vasava's younger brother about you before, and he has granted what I wanted. So
come, let us go. I shall take you wherever you like, to any land, or to the far
side of the earth. Come, Galava, quickly."

"'"I have been told to do this by the god, Galava, who knows the source of
everything," said Suparna. "Tell me which quarter you want to see first. Shall I
go to the east, the south, the west or the north, best of the twice-born? Where
shall I go, Galava?

"'"The east is where the sun, who makes all the worlds, first rises. There the
Sadhyas practise their austerities at dawn. There the first thought was born,
and it spread through the whole world. There are the two eyes of Dharma, and
there Dharma himself stands. Offerings poured into the fire's mouth there flow
out in every direction. It is the gate of the day and of the road, best of the
twice-born. There the daughters of Daksha first bore their children. In that
quarter the sons of Kashyapa grew. The gods' fortune has its root there. There
Shakra was anointed king of the gods, brahmin seer, and there the gods gathered
their austerities. For this reason, brahmin, it is called the east, the first
quarter, because in the earliest times the gods covered it first. So those who
want happiness should look to the east, the quarter of the ancients, and do the
rites for the gods first.

"'"Here the blessed maker of the worlds first sang the Vedas. Here the Savitri
was spoken by Savitri to those who speak of Brahman. Here the sun gave the Yajus
verses, best of the twice-born. Here the gods, who were given boons, drink Soma
at the sacrifices. Here the fires are fed and enjoy their own source. Here
Varuna went down to Patala and won his fortune. Here the birth, the life and the
death of the ancient Vasishtha are made known, bull among the twice-born. Here
the hundreds of branches of Om arise, and here the sages who drink Soma drink it
at the Soma shed. Here many beasts of the forest, boars and others, were
sprinkled by Shakra and set aside for the gods as their shares. Here the rising
sun in his anger kills all those who are hostile and ungrateful, men and asuras
alike. This is the gate of the three worlds, of heaven and of happiness. This is
the eastern quarter. Let us go there, if you want. For I must do what pleases
the one whose word I follow. Tell me, Galava, and I shall go. But hear about
another quarter too.

"'"This quarter, they say, Vivasvat once gave to his teacher as a fee, by the
rule of the Vedas, and so it is called the south, the Dakshina. Here the
ancestors of these three worlds have their home. Here, it is said, live the gods
who drink the steam, brahmin. Here the Vishvedevas always sit with the
ancestors, worshipped in the worlds, sharing equally with them. They call this
the second gate of dharma, brahmin. Here the measure of time is counted out in
instants and moments. Here live divine seers always, and the seers of the world
of the ancestors, and all the royal seers, free from care. Here dharma and truth
and deeds are seen for what they are. This is the goal, best of the twice-born,
of those who drag themselves down by their deeds. This is the quarter, best of
the twice-born, to which everyone comes. But it is covered by ignorance, and so
no one goes there happily. Here there are many thousands of Nairritas, bull
among the twice-born, made to be hostile, and those who are not masters of
themselves must face them.

"'"Here in the groves of Mandara and the homes of the brahmin seers the
gandharvas sing songs that steal the mind and the understanding, brahmin. Here
Raivata heard the Samans sung with songs, and lost his wife, his ministers and
his kingdom, and went to the forest. Here Savarni and the son of Yavakrita set a
boundary that the sun does not pass, brahmin. Here the great Ravana, king of the
rakshasas, of the line of Pulastya, practised austerity and chose immortality
from the gods. Here, by his conduct, Vritra too became Shakra's enemy. Here all
breaths come, and go out again fivefold. Here men who have done evil are cooked,
Galava. Here is the river called Vaitarani, full of the damned. Here a man
reaches the end of happiness and the end of sorrow.

"'"Here the sun turns back and pours out sweet water, and when he reaches the
point of Dhanishtha he lets the snow go again. Here long ago, Galava, when I was
tormented by hunger and thinking what to do, I found two huge creatures
fighting, an elephant and a tortoise. Here is the great seer born of the sun,
named Shakradhanus, whom they know as the god Kapila, who destroyed the sons of
Sagara. Here the siddhas called Shivas, brahmins who have mastered the Vedas,
learn the Vedas with all their supplements and reach the house of Yama. Here is
the city called Bhogavati, guarded by Vasuki, by the naga Takshaka and by
Airavata. Here, at the time of departure, a great darkness is met, which not
even the sun can pierce, nor fire itself. This is your road too, Galava, when
you are in torment. Tell me if we should go. Or hear me on the west.

"'"This quarter is dear to King Varuna, lord of the waters. It is always the
home and the beginning of the king of the waters. Here the sun lets his light go
at the end of the day, and so this quarter is called the west, best of the
twice-born. Here the blessed god Kashyapa anointed Varuna as king of the water
creatures and guardian of the waters. Here the moon drinks all six of Varuna's
flavours and is born young again at the start of the bright fortnight, the
destroyer of darkness. Here the daityas were driven back and bound by the wind,
brahmin, and they slept, sighing, tormented by great snakes. Here the mountain
called Asta receives the sun, who loves it, and from it the western twilight
creeps on. From here night and sleep go out at the end of the day, as if to take
away half the life of the world of the living.

"'"Here Shakra took the unborn child from the goddess Diti as she slept,
carrying her own offspring, and from it the host of the Maruts was born. Here
the root of Himavat runs to eternal Mandara, and its end cannot be reached even
in a thousand years. Here, on the shore of the ocean, by the golden mountain and
the golden river, Surabhi pours out her milk. Here in the middle of the ocean is
seen the headless trunk of Svarbhanu, who is like the sun and wants to kill the
moon and the sun. Here too is heard the great voice of one who sings, with a
golden head and green hair, unseen and beyond measure. Here Dhvajavati, the
daughter of Harimedhas, stopped in the sky when the sun commanded, 'Stand!
Stand!'

"'"Here the wind, the fire, the waters and space, Galava, let go the painful
touch of day and night. From here the sun's course turns aside. Here all the
lights enter the disk of the sun. They travel with the sun for twenty-eight
nights, and they fall from the sun again through their joining with the moon.
Here is the source of the rivers that always flow, and the rise of the ocean.
Here the waters of the three worlds rest, under Varuna's shelter. Here is the
home of Ananta, king of the snakes. Here is the highest place of Vishnu, who has
no beginning and no end. Here is the home of the wind, the fire's friend, and
the home of the great seer Kashyapa, son of Marichi. That is the western road,
told to you by the gate of the quarter. Tell me, Galava, shall we go? What do
you think, best of the twice-born?

"'"The wise call it the north, the Uttara, because it rescues, uttarayati, from
sin, and because in it one wins the highest good. The road of the northern store
of gold, Galava, is said to run between the west and the east. In this north,
the best of quarters, bull among the twice-born, no one lives who is unkind, or
not master of himself, or against dharma. Here, at the ashram of Badari, are
Krishna Narayana and Jishnu, the best of men, and eternal Brahma too. Here on
the ridge of Himavat Maheshvara always lives. Here the moon was anointed king of
the brahmins. Here the great god caught the Ganga as she fell from the sky, and
gave her to the world of men, you best knower of Brahman. Here the goddess
practised austerities to win Maheshvara. Here Kama and anger, the mountain and
Uma shone out. Here on Kailasa the giver of wealth was anointed lord of the
rakshasas, yakshas and gandharvas, Galava.

"'"Here is lovely Chaitraratha, here the ashram of the Vaikhanasas, here
Mandakini and Mandara, bull among the twice-born. Here the Saugandhika forest is
guarded by the Nairritas. Here are grassy meadows, groves of plantain and the
Santanaka trees. Here the siddhas, who always keep their vows and go where they
please, have flying palaces to match, full of every pleasure they could wish,
Galava. Here are the seven seers and the goddess Arundhati. Here stands Svati,
and here, they say, it rises. Here the Grandfather, mounted on the sacrifice,
stays fixed, and the lights, the moon and the sun, always circle round.

"'"Here great sages, true speakers, called the Dhamas, the best of the
twice-born, guard the gate of Gayantika. Their birth is not known, nor their
form, nor the austerities they have gathered. They have thousands of worlds to
enjoy as they please, Galava. The further a man goes in beyond it, best of the
twice-born, the more he dissolves away. No one else has ever gone there, bull
among the twice-born, except the god Narayana, and Nara, the unchanging Jishnu.
Here is the place called Kailasa, the home of Ailavila. Here were born the ten
apsarases called Vidyutprabhas. Here is the place called Vishnupada, made by
Vishnu as he strode across the three worlds, which lies in the north, brahmin.

"'"Here King Marutta sacrificed, best of the twice-born, at Ushirabija, where
the golden lake is, brahmin seer. Here the pure, clear lotus lake of Himavat
came of itself to the great brahmin seer Jimuta. He gave all that great wealth
to the brahmins, and chose the forest, and so that forest is called Jaimuta.
Here the guardians of the quarters cry out every evening and every morning, bull
among the twice-born, 'Whose business is there? What is to be done?' So this is
the north, best of the twice-born, with these and other virtues. It is called
Uttara, and it is uttara, the highest, in every work. I have told you of the
four quarters in order and at length, my friend. To which do you want to go? I
am ready to show you the quarters, best of the twice-born, and the whole earth,
brahmin. So climb on me, twice-born one."

"'"Garutman, enemy of the snake lords, Suparna, son of Vinata," said Galava,
"take me east, Tarkshya, where the two eyes of Dharma are. Go first to the east,
which you described first, for there, you said, the gods are near. You spoke
well there of truth and dharma. I want to meet all the gods, and I want to see
those gods again, younger brother of Aruna."

"'"Climb on," the son of Vinata told the brahmin,' said Narada, 'and the sage
Galava climbed onto Garuda.

"'"As you stride on, eater of snakes," said Galava, "your form looks like the
thousand-rayed sun, Vivasvat, in the morning. The trees driven by the wind of
your wings seem to follow you, bird, as if they had all set out together. You
seem to drag the earth along with the wind of your wings, rover of the sky, with
its oceans and forests, its mountains, woods and groves. The water, with its
fish, its snakes and its crocodiles, seems to be lifted up into the sky by the
great wind of your wings, without a pause. I see fish that look alike, timis and
timingilas, and snakes with human faces, as if they were churned up. The roar of
the great ocean has deafened my ears. I cannot hear, I cannot see, and I do not
know what is happening to me.

"'"Go gently, please, remembering the sin of killing a brahmin. The sun cannot
be seen, my friend, nor the quarters, nor the sky, bird. I see only darkness,
and I cannot make out your body. I see your eyes, egg-born one, like two fine
jewels. I cannot see your body or my own. At every step I see fire rising from
the water. It flares up suddenly in my eyes and then dies down again. So turn
back, son of Vinata. It is a long time we have been going. I have no need to go
any further, eater of snakes. Turn back, you of great speed. I cannot bear your
speed.

"'"I promised my teacher eight hundred horses here, white and bright as the
moon, each with one black ear. I see no way to pay them, egg-born one. So I have
seen this road for myself: to give up my life. I have no wealth, and I have no
friend with wealth, and even great wealth could not put this right."

"'As Galava said all this, much of it pitiful,' said Narada, 'the son of Vinata
laughed and answered him, without stopping.

"'"You are not very wise, brahmin seer, if you want to give up your life. And
time is not made by man. Time is the supreme lord. Why did you not tell me all
this before? There is a great way here by which this can be done. This mountain
in the lap of the ocean is called Rishabha. Let us rest here and eat, Galava,
and then turn back."'"

**Divergences.**

* **Galava's lament** is addressed to Suyodhana in a rejected line (\*450). Not narrated.
* **Maheshvara at the north.** A rejected passage (\*452) adds that Maheshvara lives there with Prakriti and is seen by no one but Nara and Narayana, and that Vishnu alone sees him. The CE says only that Maheshvara always lives on the ridge of Himavat (5.109.5). Not narrated.
* **The Dhamas.** Ganguli, from another reading, has "Munis called by the name of Dharma" guarding "the source of the Ganges". The CE reads *dhāmā nāma* and *gāyantikādvāram* (5.109.14).
* \*451, which only announces the north, is not narrated.

**Choices.**

* The chapter has four layers: Vaishampayana, Narada in the Kuru hall, and Garuda and Galava inside Narada's story. Narada's speech is in single quotes inside Vaishampayana's double quotes, and the speech of Galava, Vishvamitra and Garuda is in double quotes inside Narada's.

# The Eleven Armies

"Yudhishthira had come with his army, wanting to fight, and camped at
Kurukshetra guarded by Vasudeva," said Janamejaya. "Virata and Drupada were with
him, with their sons, and the Kekayas and the Vrishnis and kings in hundreds
surrounded him. Great chariot fighters guarded him like great Indra guarded by
the Adityas. When King Duryodhana heard of it, what did he decide to do? I want
to hear it in full, rich in austerity, all that happened in that confusion and
uproar in the Kuru country. The Pandavas and Vasudeva, Virata and Drupada,
Dhrishtadyumna the Panchala prince, the great chariot fighter Shikhandi and
valiant Yuyudhana, whom even the gods could hardly approach, could shake an army
of the gods if they met it. I want to hear it in full, rich in austerity, all
that the Kurus and the Pandavas did."

"When the Dasharha had gone back," said Vaishampayana, "King Duryodhana said
this to Karna, Duhshasana and Shakuni.

"'Adhokshaja has gone to the Parthas with nothing done. Filled with anger, he
will surely stir them up, no doubt of it. Vasudeva wants war between me and the
Pandavas. Bhimasena and Arjuna stand by the Dasharha's view. Ajatashatru too
follows Bhima and Arjuna today, and I wronged him before, with all his brothers.
Virata and Drupada have their feud with me, and those two leaders of armies
follow Vasudeva. This war will be tumultuous and make the hair stand on end. So
have everything for war made ready, without sloth. Let camps be made at
Kurukshetra, lords of the earth, with plenty of room, hard for enemies to take,
near water and wood, in hundreds and thousands. Let their supply roads be
impossible to cut. Let them be piled with heaps of jewels, full of weapons of
every kind, with flags and banners. Let the roads to them from the city be made
level. Let the march be proclaimed today, for tomorrow. Do not delay.'

"They agreed, and the next day those great men did so, looking glad, for the
destruction of the kings. When they heard the king's order, all the kings rose
angrily from their costly seats. Slowly they stroked their arms, like iron bars,
shining with golden armlets and adorned with sandal and aloe. With hands like
lotuses they tied their turbans, their lower and upper garments and all their
ornaments. The best of the chariot fighters made ready their chariots, those
skilled with horses their horses, and those trained in the lore of elephants
their elephants. They made ready many bright golden coats of armour and weapons
of every kind on every side. The foot soldiers took up weapons of many kinds and
put on their bodies many things worked with gold.

"Dhritarashtra's son's city, Bharata, was crowded and bustling, as at a
festival, full of happy men. The ocean of the Kuru king could be seen then,
king, like the sea at moonrise. Its whirlpools were the waters of the crowds of
people, its fish the chariots, elephants and horses, its roar the sound of
conches and drums, its jewels the treasury and stores. Its waves were the bright
ornaments and armour, its pure foam the weapons. It was ringed with mountains of
rows of palaces, its great lakes were the streets and markets, and it rose at
the moonrise of the fighters.

---

"Remembering Vasudeva's words, Yudhishthira asked the Varshneya again, 'How
could the fool say that? Now that this time has come, what is fitting for us,
Achyuta? How can we act and not fall from our own dharma? You know the minds of
Duryodhana, Karna and Shakuni son of Subala, Vasudeva, and mine and my
brothers'. You have heard Vidura's words and Bhishma's, and you have heard all
of Kunti's wisdom, very wise one. Go beyond all this, think it over again and
again, and tell us without hesitating what is fitting for us, strong-armed one.'

"When he heard the words of the King of Dharma, full of dharma and purpose,
Krishna, whose voice was like the thunder of clouds and drums, answered him.
'The words I spoke were full of dharma and purpose, and good, but they do not
take hold in the Kaurava, whose wisdom is trickery. The fool does not listen to
Bhishma or Vidura or anything I say. He goes past everything. He does not want
dharma, he does not want fame. The evil-souled man, leaning on Karna, thinks he
has won everything. Suyodhana even ordered me to be bound, and the evil-souled
man, who breaks every command, did not get his wish. Neither Bhishma nor Drona
said what was fitting there. All follow him, Achyuta, except Vidura. Shakuni son
of Subala, Karna and Duhshasana, fools to a fool in his impatience, said
unfitting things about you. What use is it for me to repeat what the Kauravas
said? In short, the evil-souled man does not behave rightly towards you. All the
evil and all that is not good, which is not in all these kings who are your
soldiers, rests in him. And we do not want peace with the Kauravas at all by
giving up too much. War is next.'

"When they heard Vasudeva's words, all the kings said nothing and looked at the
king's face, Bharata. Yudhishthira understood what the kings meant, and with
Bhima, Arjuna and the twins he ordered the army to be yoked. Then the Pandava's
army cried out in joy. When the yoking was ordered, the soldiers were glad.

"But seeing the killing of those who should not be killed, Yudhishthira, the
King of Dharma, groaned and said this to Bhimasena and Vijaya. 'For this I lived
in the forest and suffered grief, and this, the greatest evil, comes on us in
spite of our efforts. What we strove for is lost to us in spite of our efforts,
and a great Kali age has come on us though we did not strive for it. How can we
fight a war with those who should not be killed? How can we win, if we kill our
old teachers?'

"When he heard the King of Dharma, the left-handed archer, scorcher of enemies,
repeated what Vasudeva had said. 'Devaki's son told you what Kunti and Vidura
said, king, and you took it all in. I am firmly convinced they would not speak
against dharma. And it is not right, Kunti's son, to turn back without
fighting.' When he heard the left-handed archer's words, Vasudeva smiled and
said to Partha, 'That is so.' Then the Pandavas, great king, with their minds
made up for war, rested well that night with their soldiers.

---

"When the night had passed, King Duryodhana divided his eleven armies, Bharata.
In all these armies the lord of the earth set apart the best, the middling and
the weakest of the men, elephants, chariots and horses. They had spare axles,
quivers, chariot fenders and lances, holsters, spears, quivers and clubs,
flagstaffs and banners, bows and lances, ropes of many kinds, nooses and
coverings. They had hair-grapples, oil, molasses and sand, pots of poisonous
snakes, resin and dust. All had bells and boards, adzes and bill-hooks,
coverings of tiger skin and panther skin, bladders and horns, javelins and
weapons of many kinds, axes and spades, oil, linen and clarified butter. The
ranks were bright and handsome, like blazing fires, and the brave men in armour
were well trained in weapons.

"Men of good family who knew the breeds of horses were set to drive. The
chariots had amulets bound on, girths bound, and banners and flags bound on. All
the chariots had four horses, all were full of weapons, all had glad horses and
a hundred bows each. Of the two horses at the yoke, one man drove; two others
were the rear guards of the wheels, both of them fine chariot fighters, and the
warrior himself knew horses too. There were thousands of chariots, all with
golden garlands, guarded like cities and hard for enemies to take. The elephants
too, like the chariots, had girths bound and were well adorned, and each carried
seven men, like mountains with jewels. Two held the goads, two were fine
archers, two carried fine swords, king, and one carried a spear and a flag. The
Kaurava's army, king, was crowded with thousands of elephants in rut, with
armour, weapons and quivers.

"There were horses in tens of thousands, in bright armour, with flags, well
adorned, ridden by horsemen. They were easy to hold, well contented, with golden
trappings. Many hundreds of thousands of them obeyed their riders. There were
foot soldiers there of many forms and kinds, with armour and weapons of many
kinds and golden garlands. Each chariot had ten elephants, each elephant ten
horses, and each horse ten men to guard its feet on every side. Each chariot had
fifty elephants, each elephant a hundred horses, and each horse seven men, to
mend broken lines. A *sena* is five hundred elephants and as many chariots. Ten
*senas* make a *pritana*, and ten *pritanas* a *vahini*. *Vahini*, *pritana*,
*sena*, *dhvajini*, *sadini*, *chamu*, *akshauhini* and *varuthini* are names
for one another. So the wise Kaurava drew up his armies.

"Eleven armies were counted, and seven. The Pandavas' force was seven armies,
and the Kauravas' force was eleven. A *patti* is fifty-five men. Three of them
make a *senamukha*, which is also called a *gulma*. Ten *gulmas* made a *gana*,
and there were tens of thousands of *ganas* in Duryodhana's armies, fighters
ready to strike.

"Then strong-armed King Duryodhana looked over the brave and wise men and made
his generals. He brought together the best of men, the leaders of the separate
armies, and consecrated those kings with the proper rites: Kripa, Drona, Shalya,
the great chariot fighter Jayadratha of Sindhu, Sudakshina of Kamboja,
Kritavarma, Drona's son, Karna, Bhurishravas, Shakuni son of Subala and the
great chariot fighter Bahlika. Day after day, Bharata, at every moment, he gave
them many marks of honour in person, again and again. So all of them were
appointed, and the soldiers who followed them were eager to please the king."

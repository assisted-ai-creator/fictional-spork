# The Names of Krishna

When Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, did not welcome those words, and all were
silent, the lords of men rose. When all the kings of the earth had risen, great
king, the king began to question Sanjaya in private. Following his son's will,
he hoped for victory, and he wanted to know for certain about himself, about the
enemy and about the Pandavas.

"Son of Gavalgana," said Dhritarashtra, "tell us the strong and the weak, all
there is of it in our own army. You know all about the Pandavas, closely. What
is greater in them, and what is less? You know the heart of both. You see
everything. You are skilled in dharma and purpose, and you know how to judge. I
ask you, Sanjaya. Tell me everything. Which of them, if they fight, will cease
to be?"

"I would never tell you anything in private," said Sanjaya. "Resentment would
take hold of you, king. Bring your father, descendant of Ajamidha, who is strict
in his vows, and Queen Gandhari. They would take away your resentment, king of
men. They know dharma, and they are skilled and sure in judgment. In their
presence I would tell you the whole mind of Vasudeva and Arjuna."

Then, knowing what Sanjaya and his son thought, the very wise Krishna Dvaipayana
came to them and spoke. "Sanjaya, tell Dhritarashtra all he asks. Tell him
everything you know about Vasudeva and Arjuna, exactly and truly."

---

"Arjuna and Vasudeva are bowmen, most highly honoured," said Sanjaya. "They were
born of something other than desire, and they are fit for the ending of
everything. Lord, Vasudeva's discus stands in the space of the sky, set as the
proud ones wish, and turns by his magic. It is hidden from the Pandavas, but the
Pandavas hold it in honour. Knowing the strength and weakness of things, hear it
from me in short. Madhava Janardana conquered Naraka, Shambara, Kamsa and the
king of the Chedis, terrible as they were, as if in play. The best of men, the
master, of a self beyond others, brings earth, sky and heaven under his control
by his mind alone.

"You ask me again and again about the Pandavas, king, to know the strength and
weakness. Hear me tell it. Put the whole world on one side and Janardana on the
other: in what it holds, Janardana outweighs the whole world. Janardana could
turn this world to ash by his mind alone. But the whole world could not turn
Janardana to ash. Where truth is, where dharma is, where shame and straightness
are, there Govinda is. Where Krishna is, there is victory.

"Janardana, the best of men, the self of beings, moves earth, sky and heaven as
if in play. Making the Pandavas his instrument, as if bewildering the world, he
wants to burn up your foolish sons, who delight in adharma. Keshava, the lord,
turns the wheel of time, the wheel of the world and the wheel of the ages
without rest, by his own yoga. I tell you truly, the lord alone rules time,
death, and all that moves and does not move. Though he is the great yogi, master
of the whole world, Hari sets out to do his work like a weak farmer. So Keshava
deceives the worlds by the yoga of his magic. The men who take refuge in him
alone are not bewildered."

"How do you know Madhava as the great lord of all the worlds?" said
Dhritarashtra. "And why do I not know him? Tell me that, Sanjaya."

"Your learning is not learning, king," said Sanjaya. "My learning does not fail.
A man without learning, wrapped in darkness, does not know Keshava. By learning,
my son, I know Madhusudana, of the three ages, the maker, unmade, the god, the
source and the end of beings."

"Son of Gavalgana, what is this devotion you always have for Janardana," said
Dhritarashtra, "by which you know Madhusudana of the three ages?"

"I do not practise deceit, bless you," said Sanjaya, "and I do not follow empty
dharma. I have come to purity of heart by devotion, and from the teaching I know
Janardana."

"Duryodhana," said Dhritarashtra, "take refuge with Hrishikesha Janardana.
Sanjaya is to be trusted, my son. Go to Keshava for refuge."

"If the blessed son of Devaki is going to destroy the world," said Duryodhana,
"while he talks of friendship with Arjuna, I will not go to Keshava today."

"Gandhari, this son of yours is going down," said Dhritarashtra. "He is very
evil-minded, envious, wicked and proud, and he goes beyond the words of his
betters."

"You who want power, you wicked one," said Gandhari, "who go beyond the commands
of your elders! You fool, you are leaving behind power and life, your father and
me. You add to the joy of those who wish us ill, and to my sorrow. When
Bhimasena kills you, you will remember your father's words."

"You are dear to Krishna, King Dhritarashtra," said Vyasa. "Listen to me. You
have Sanjaya as your envoy, who will set you on the road to good. He knows
Hrishikesha, the ancient and the new. If you listen to him with a single mind,
he will free you from a great fear. Son of Vichitravirya, men wrapped in anger,
delight and darkness, bound by many kinds of nooses, who are not content with
their own wealth, come again and again into Yama's power, deluded by desire.
They are like blind men led by the blind, led on by their own deeds. This is the
one road by which the wise go. When he has seen it, a man passes beyond death,
and the great man does not cling there."

"Come, Sanjaya, tell me of the road without fear," said Dhritarashtra, "by which
I may reach Hrishikesha and find the highest peace."

"A man whose self is untrained can never know Janardana, whose self is trained,"
said Sanjaya. "The means of acting on the self is only the restraint of the
senses. Giving up desire for the roused senses, out of care, care and not
harming, are surely the source of knowledge. Be careful and tireless in
restraining your senses, king. Do not let your understanding slip. Hold it in
check wherever it goes. Brahmins know that this holding of the senses is
knowledge, for certain. This knowledge is the road by which the wise go. Keshava
cannot be reached by men whose senses are unconquered, king. When he is reached
by the teaching and by yoga, the master is gracious in the truth."

---

"Tell me again about the lotus-eyed one, Sanjaya, since I ask," said
Dhritarashtra. "If I know the meaning of his names and deeds, my son, I may
reach the best of men."

"I have heard the lucky explanation of that god's names," said Sanjaya, "as far
as I know them. Keshava cannot be measured.

"He is to be known as Vasudeva because he covers all beings, being the Vasu,
born of the gods. He is called Vrishni because he is the bull, *vrisha*. Know
him as Madhava, Bharata, from silence, meditation and yoga. Because all truths
dissolve in him, he is Madhusudana, the killer of Madhu. The word *krishi* means
the earth, and *na* means bliss. Because he is joined with both, the eternal
Krishna is Krishna. The lotus, *pundarika*, is the highest abode, eternal,
undecaying, imperishable. Because he is that, he is Pundarikaksha, the
lotus-eyed. Because he terrifies robbers, he is Janardana.

"Because being, *sattva*, does not fall away from him, and he does not fall
short of being, he is Satvata, from *sattva*. From *arshabha*, the bull's, he is
Vrishabhekshana, the bull-eyed. Because he is not born of a mother, he is Aja,
the unborn, and conqueror of armies. Because the gods shine by their own light,
and from self-control, *dama*, they know him as Damodara. From delight,
*harsha*, from happiness and the lordship of happiness, he has the name
Hrishikesha. Because he holds heaven and earth in his two arms, he is called
Mahabahu, the great-armed. Because he never falls downwards, *adhas*, he is
Adhokshaja. Because he is the resting place of men, *nara*, he is called
Narayana. From filling and from dwelling, he is Purushottama, the best of men.

"Because he is the source and the end of all, the unreal and the real, and
because he always knows all, they call him All. Krishna stands firm in truth,
and truth stands firm in him. Govinda is truth beyond truth, so he is also named
Satya, the True. He is called Vishnu from his striding, *vikramana*, and Jishnu
from his conquering, *jayana*. He is Ananta, the endless, because he is eternal,
and Govinda because he knows the cows, *gavah*. He makes the untrue seem true,
and so he bewilders creatures.

"Such is he, always devoted to dharma, the blessed one. The strong-armed one,
the unfallen Achyuta, will come here with the sages, for the sake of kindness."

---

"I envy those who have eyes, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "who will see
Vasudeva close at hand, shining with his highest body, lighting up the
directions and the spaces between. I envy them, who will see him speak words to
the Bharatas that are to be honoured, words of good to the Srinjayas. Those who
want to prosper should take those faultless words. Those who are going to die
will not take them. I envy those who will see the lone hero of the Satvatas
rising, the leader, the bull of the Yadavas, the slayer and the troubler of
enemies, who steals his enemies' fame.

"The Kurus gathered together will see that great one, the killer of enemies, the
one to be chosen, the best of the Vrishnis, speaking kind words and bewildering
my men. I take refuge with the seer most eternal, the wise one, the ocean of
speech, the vessel of ascetics, Arishtanemi, Garuda Suparna, lord of creatures,
the dwelling of the world. I take refuge with the ancient Person of a thousand
heads, without beginning, middle or end, of endless fame, the founder of seed,
the unborn, the begetter, higher than the highest. I take refuge with the maker
of the three worlds, the begetter of gods and asuras, of nagas and rakshasas,
the chief of kings and of the wise, the younger brother of Indra."

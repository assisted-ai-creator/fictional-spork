# Amba's Austerity

"'Then Rama spoke to the girl,'" said Bhishma. "'"This has been seen by all the
worlds, lady, how I have shown great manhood with all my power. But I cannot get
the better of Bhishma, the best of those who bear weapons, in battle, though I
showed the finest weapons. This is my utmost power, this is my utmost strength.
Go where you wish, good lady. What else can I do for you? Take refuge with
Bhishma himself. You have no other way, for I have been beaten by Bhishma as he
loosed his great weapons."

"'When he had said this, great-minded Rama sighed and fell silent. Then the girl
said to the son of Bhrigu, "Lord, it is just as the lord says. This Bhishma of
noble mind cannot be beaten in battle, even by the gods. You have done my work
as far as your power and spirit allowed, without holding back your courage or
your many weapons in the battle. In the end he cannot be beaten in battle. But I
will never go to Bhishma again. I will go, rich in austerity, where I myself can
bring Bhishma down in battle, upholder of the Bhrigus." When she had said this,
the girl went away, her eyes clouded with anger, resolved on austerity, thinking
of my death.

"'Then the best of the Bhrigus took leave of me and went back to Mahendra as he
had come, with those sages, Bharata. Then I mounted my chariot, praised by the
twice-born, entered the city and told my mother Satyavati what had happened,
great king, and she welcomed me. I ordered wise men to watch the girl's doings.
Day after day they reported her movements, her talk and her actions to me,
devoted to my good and my pleasure. As soon as the girl went to the forest, set
on austerity, I became troubled and miserable, almost out of my mind. For no
kshatriya could beat me in battle by courage, my son, except one who knows
Brahman, strict in his vows through austerity. I told Narada of this too, king,
and Vyasa, out of fear, and both of them said to me, "You should not despair
about the daughter of Kashi, Bhishma. Who could turn back fate by human effort?"

---

"'That girl, great king, entered a circle of hermitages, took shelter on the
bank of the Yamuna and practised more than human austerity. Without food, thin,
rough, with matted hair, covered in dirt and mud, living on air for six months,
she stood like a post, rich in austerity. Coming to the bank of the Yamuna, the
lovely woman spent another year standing in the water without food. She spent
another year living on a single fallen leaf, fierce in her anger, standing on
the tips of her toes. So for twelve years she made heaven and earth burn. Even
when her kin tried to turn her back, she could not be turned.

"'Then she went to the Vatsa country, served by the perfected ones and the
charanas, to the hermitage of great ascetics of holy conduct. There the daughter
of Kashi went about the holy places as she pleased, bathing her body day and
night. She went to the hermitage of Nanda, great king, at the lovely hermitage
of Uluka, at Chyavana's hermitage, at Brahma's own place, at Prayaga, the gods'
place of sacrifice, in the forests of the gods, at Bhogavati, king, and at
Kaushika's hermitage, at Mandavya's hermitage, king, and Dilipa's, at Rama's
lake, Kauravya, and at the hermitage of Paila Gargya. At these fords, lord of
the people, the daughter of Kashi bathed her limbs, practising fierce austerity.

"'My mother, rising from the water, said to her, Kauraveya, "Why do you torment
yourself, good lady? Tell me truly." The faultless girl joined her hands and
said to her, king, "Bhishma was not beaten by Rama in battle, lovely-eyed one.
Who else could beat that lord of the earth when his arrow is raised? So I will
practise very terrible austerity to destroy Bhishma. I wander the earth,
goddess, so that I may kill the king. Let this be the fruit of my vow in another
body."

"'Then she who goes to the ocean said, "You go a crooked way, lady. This wish
cannot be won by you, faultless one, weak as you are. If you keep this vow to
destroy Bhishma, daughter of Kashi, and give up your body while keeping it, you
will become a river, lovely one. It will be crooked, with water only in the
rains, hard to ford, hard to know, flowing in the rains and not for eight
months, full of terrible crocodiles, dreadful, frightening all creatures." When
she had said this to the daughter of Kashi, king, my fortunate mother, the
lovely one, turned away, as if smiling.

"'Sometimes in the eighth month, sometimes in the tenth, the lovely woman did
not take even water. Wandering here and there in the Vatsa country out of desire
for the fords, the daughter of the lord of Kashi fell. That river in the Vatsa
country is known as Amba, Bharata, flowing in the rains, full of crocodiles,
hard to ford and crooked. By that austerity the girl was born as half her share:
she became a river in the Vatsa country, king, and she remained a girl too.

---

"'Then all the ascetics, seeing her set on austerity, tried to turn her back, my
son, and asked, "What do you want?" The girl said to those seers grown old in
austerity, "I was rejected by Bhishma and made to fall from the dharma of a
husband. My consecration is for his death, not for the worlds, rich in
austerity. When I have killed Bhishma I will go to peace. That is my resolve.
Because of him I have come to this lasting misery, cut off from a husband's
world, neither woman nor man here. I will not turn back until I have killed
Ganga's son in battle, rich in austerity. This is the resolve in my heart, for
which I have taken this up. Weary of being a woman, I have made up my mind to
become a man. I want my revenge on Bhishma. I must not be held back again."

"'The god who holds the trident in his hand, Uma's husband, showed himself to
that lovely woman in his own form, in the middle of those great seers. Offered a
boon, she chose my defeat. "You will kill him," the god answered the
high-spirited woman. Then the girl said to Rudra again, "How could victory in
battle come to me, a woman, god? My mind is deeply calm with a woman's nature,
husband of Uma. And you have promised Bhishma's defeat, lord of beings. Make it
come true, bull-bannered one, so that I may meet Bhishma son of Shantanu in
battle and kill him."

"'The great god with the bull on his banner said to the girl, they say, "My
words do not speak falsely, good lady. It will come true. You will kill Bhishma
in battle and you will win manhood. You will remember all of it when you have
gone into another body. You will be born in Drupada's family, a great chariot
fighter, quick with weapons, a fighter of many skills, greatly respected. All
this will be as I have said, lovely one. You will become a man later, after some
lapse of time." When he had said this, the god of great splendour with matted
hair and the bull banner vanished there, while the brahmins watched.

"'Then, while those great seers watched, the faultless, lovely woman gathered
wood from that forest, built a very large pyre and lit the fire. When the fire
was blazing, great king, her mind blazing with anger, the eldest daughter of
Kashi said, "For Bhishma's death," and entered the fire, king, by the river
Yamuna.'"

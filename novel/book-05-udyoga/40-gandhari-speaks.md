# Gandhari Speaks

Then the Dasharha laughed, his eyes clouded with anger, and spoke to Duryodhana
in the assembly of the Kurus.

"You will get your bed of heroes. You will have your wish. Stand firm with your
counsellors. There will be a great slaughter. And you think, fool, 'I have done
no wrong to the Pandavas.' Kings, hear all of it. Burning at the fortune of the
great Pandavas, you and Subala's son made that wicked plan of the dicing,
Bharata. How could your kin, who are better men and respected by the good,
behave honestly in such an unjust thing, when you are crooked and they are
straight? Dicing, very wise one, destroys the peace of the good. Among the bad
it breeds divisions and disasters. You started this terrible disaster with the
dicing, without thinking, along with those bound to evil, against those of good
conduct.

"Who else could so abuse a kinsman's wife, bring her into the hall and speak to
her as you spoke to Draupadi? She is of good family and good character, dearer
than their own lives, the queen of the sons of Pandu, and you abused her so. All
the Kurus know what Duhshasana said to the sons of Kunti in the Kuru assembly,
when those scorchers of enemies were going into exile. What good man would
behave so improperly to his own kin, who behave well, are not greedy and always
keep dharma? You, Karna and Duhshasana have often spoken the words of cruel,
ignoble, harsh men.

"You made the greatest effort to burn them, with their mother, when they were
boys, at Varanavata, and it did not succeed. Then the Pandavas lived in hiding a
long time with their mother in Ekachakra, in a brahmin's house. You tried to
destroy the Pandavas by every means, with poison and with snakes and ropes, and
it did not succeed. With such a mind towards the Pandavas, always behaving
falsely, how can you say you have done no wrong to the great Pandavas? You have
done many evil things to the Pandavas, like a cruel man, always behaving
falsely, ignoble, and now you deny it.

"Your mother and father, Bhishma, Drona and Vidura have told you again and
again, 'Make peace,' and you will not make peace, king. Peace would be of great
profit to you and to the Partha both, and you do not like it, king. What else
but lightness of mind? You will find no happiness, king, going against the words
of your friends. What you are doing is against dharma and brings no glory,
king."

While the Dasharha was speaking to impatient Duryodhana, Duhshasana said in the
assembly of the Kurus, "If you will not make peace with the Pandavas of your own
will, king, it seems the Kauravas will bind you and give you to the son of
Kunti. Bhishma, Drona and your father will give these three to the Pandavas,
Vaikartana, you and me, bull among men."

When he heard his brother's words, Suyodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, rose in
anger and left, hissing like a great snake. Ignoring Vidura and Dhritarashtra,
great King Bahlika, Kripa and Somadatta, Bhishma, Drona and Janardana, all of
them, the evil-minded, shameless man left, like an ill-bred man without bounds,
proud, insulting those who deserve honour. Seeing that bull among men leave, his
brothers followed him, with their counsellors and all the kings.

When he saw Duryodhana rise in anger in the hall and leave with his brothers,
Bhishma, son of Shantanu, said, "The man who gives up dharma and purpose and
gives way to fury soon has his enemies laughing at him in his misfortune. This
wicked prince, son of Dhritarashtra, does not know the means. He is falsely
proud of the kingdom and follows anger and greed. I think the whole of the
kshatriya class is ripe for time, Janardana. All the kings and their counsellors
have followed him out of folly."

When he heard Bhishma's words, the lotus-eyed, mighty Dasharha spoke to all of
them, with Bhishma and Drona at their head.

"This is a great failure of all the elders of the Kurus, that you do not
restrain this foolish king by force in his power. I think there is something to
be done that the time calls for, tamers of enemies. If it is done, it will be
for the best. Hear it all, faultless ones. I will say something good, which you
can see for yourselves, if you agree to it, Bharatas.

"Kamsa, son of Ugrasena, ill-behaved and without self-control, took the power of
the old Bhoja king, his father, while his father lived, and fell into the power
of anger. His kin abandoned him, and I, wanting the good of my kin, killed him
in a great battle. Then we and our kin honoured Ahuka again, and made Ugrasena
king, who increases the kingdom of the Bhojas. By giving up Kamsa alone for the
sake of the family, all the Yadavas, the Andhakas and the Vrishnis, came
together and thrive in happiness, Bharata.

"And Prajapati, the Supreme One, said this, king, when the war of the gods and
asuras was drawn up, with weapons raised, and the worlds were split in two and
perishing, Bharata. The holy god, the creator, who brings the worlds into being,
said, 'The asuras, the daityas with the danavas, will be defeated. The Adityas,
Vasus and Rudras will dwell in heaven. Gods, asuras and men, gandharvas,
serpents and rakshasas will kill one another in this war, well armed.' Thinking
this, the Supreme One, Prajapati, said to Dharma, 'Bind these daityas and
danavas and give them to Varuna.' Told this at the Supreme One's command, Dharma
bound all the daityas and danavas and gave them to Varuna. Varuna, lord of the
waters, bound them with the nooses of dharma and with his own nooses, and keeps
the danavas carefully guarded in the ocean for ever.

"In the same way, bind Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni son of Subala and Duhshasana,
and give them to the Pandavas. A man should give up one man for a family, a
family for a village, a village for a country, and the earth for the self. King,
bind Duryodhana, and then make peace with the Pandavas. Let the kshatriyas not
perish because of you, bull of the kshatriyas."

---

When he heard Krishna's words, Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, quickly spoke
to Vidura, who knew all dharma. "Go, my son, and bring very wise, far-seeing
Gandhari here. With her I will win over this evil-minded one. If she can calm
this wicked one with his corrupt heart, we may yet keep to the words of our
friend Krishna. She may show the road to that evil-minded one, overcome by
greed, with his bad allies, by speaking strong words. She may calm the great and
terrible disaster Duryodhana has caused, for a long time, safely and lastingly."

When he heard the king's words, Vidura brought far-seeing Gandhari at
Dhritarashtra's command.

"Gandhari, this son of yours is wicked and goes beyond all command," said
Dhritarashtra. "Out of greed for power he will lose both his power and his life.
Like an ill-bred man without bounds, with wicked, evil companions, the fool has
left the hall, passing over the words of his friends."

When she heard her husband's words, the famous princess Gandhari, seeking what
was best, spoke. "Bring my son here quickly, sick with desire for the kingdom. A
man without manners, who destroys dharma and purpose, cannot have a kingdom. But
you are the one most to blame here, Dhritarashtra, for you love your son. You
know his wickedness, and you follow his thinking. Deceived by desire and anger,
he has fallen into folly. You cannot turn him back by force now, king.
Dhritarashtra is reaping the fruit of giving the kingdom to a foolish, childish,
wicked son, with bad friends and greedy. How could a very wise man stand by
while his own people split? Once you are split from your own people, your
enemies will overwhelm you. When troubles among one's own can be crossed by
conciliation or by gifts, great king, who would bring the rod down on them?"

At Dhritarashtra's command and his mother's word, the steward brought impatient
Duryodhana back into the hall. Wanting his mother's words, he came back in, his
eyes red with anger, hissing like a snake. When she saw her son come in, set on
the wrong road, Gandhari reproached him and spoke strong words.

"Duryodhana, listen to what I say, my son. It is good for you and those bound to
you, and it will bring happiness in the future. If you make peace, you will have
paid honour to Bhishma, to your father and to me, and to your friends led by
Drona. A kingdom cannot be won, kept or enjoyed by one's own will, very wise
one, bull of the Bharatas. A man who has not mastered his senses does not enjoy
a kingdom for long. A wise man who has conquered himself can rule it. Desire and
anger drag a man away from his aims. When a king has conquered those two
enemies, he conquers the earth. To be lord of the world is a great thing. Wicked
men want the kingdom, but they cannot keep it.

"A man who wants greatness should hold his senses to purpose and dharma. When
the senses are held in check, the understanding grows, like fire with fuel. If
they are not held in check, they can even kill him, like unruly, unbroken horses
on the road killing a bad charioteer. The man who has not conquered himself and
wants to conquer his counsellors, with self and counsellors unconquered, falls
helpless. The man who first conquers himself, as if he were a country, does not
try in vain to conquer his counsellors and his enemies after. Fortune always
favours the steady man who has mastered his senses and conquered his
counsellors, who keeps punishment ready for wrongdoers and acts after careful
thought.

"Desire and anger, living in the body, break through wisdom, like two big fish
caught in a net of small mesh. The gods, afraid of a man going to heaven without
passion, made desire and anger grow, and with them closed the mouth of heaven. A
king who knows how to conquer desire, anger, greed, deceit and pride rightly
wins the earth. A king should always be set on holding back his senses, if he
wants purpose and dharma, and the defeat of his enemies. A man who behaves
falsely out of desire or anger has no allies among his own or among others.

"Joined with the Pandavas, who are very wise and brave and slay their enemies,
you will enjoy the earth happily, my son. As Bhishma son of Shantanu and the
great chariot fighter Drona have said, my son, it is true that Krishna and the
Pandava cannot be beaten. Take refuge with strong-armed Krishna, whose deeds
never fail. If Keshava is pleased, it will bring happiness to both sides. A man
who will not keep to the teaching of his friends, who want his good and are wise
and learned, brings joy to his enemies.

"There is no good in war, my son, nor dharma or purpose, let alone happiness.
Nor is victory sure. Do not set your mind on war. Bhishma, your father and
Bahlika gave the sons of Pandu their share, very wise one, afraid of a split,
tamer of enemies. You see the fruit of that gift today, that you enjoy the whole
earth with its thorns removed by heroes. Give the sons of Pandu their proper
share, tamer of enemies, if you want to enjoy half the kings' earth with your
counsellors. Half the earth is enough for you and your counsellors to live on.
Keep to your friends' words and you will win fame, Bharata.

"A war with the Pandavas, who are fortunate, self-possessed, wise and masters of
their senses, my son, would bring you down from great happiness. Hold back your
friends' anger and rule the kingdom as is fitting, giving the sons of Pandu
their own share, bull of the Bharatas. Thirteen years of this wrong are enough.
Calm it, very wise one. It has been fed by desire and anger.

"The suta's son, so set in his anger, and your brother Duhshasana, who want this
for your sake, are no match for the Parthas. When Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna,
Bhimasena, Dhananjaya and Dhrishtadyumna are angry, surely all the people will
cease to be. Do not fall into the power of impatience and have the Kurus killed,
my son. The whole earth would be touched by death because of you and the
Pandavas. And you think, fool, that Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and the others will
fight with all their strength. That will not happen now. For those
self-possessed men, the kingdom, affection and position are the same with the
Pandavas as with you, but dharma weighs more with them. They may give up their
lives for fear of the king's food. But they will not be able to look King
Yudhishthira in the face. No man here is seen to win wealth by greed. So enough
of greed, my son. Make peace, bull of the Bharatas."

**Divergences.**

* **"Bind Duryodhana."** Krishna's proposal that the elders bind Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni and Duhshasana and hand them over is CE (5.126.47–49). He supports it with the story of Kamsa and the binding of the asuras.
* Rejected lines in these chapters add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.126.13–15: the plots at Varanavata, the poison and the snakes are told in Book 1.
* 5.126.36–38: Kamsa, Ugrasena and Ahuka. Ahuka here is read as a name of Ugrasena's line, as the text gives it.
* 5.126.48: the saying "give up one man for a family" is the same as Vidura's (5.37.16).
* 5.127.24–30: Gandhari repeats sayings Vidura used (5.34). The text has her say them.
* 5.127.31: "The gods … closed the mouth of heaven" renders a difficult verse.
* 5.127.52 *rājapiṇḍabhayāt*: "for fear of the king's food", that is, out of debt to the king who feeds them.

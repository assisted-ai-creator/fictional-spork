# The Armies Move

When the night had turned to morning, your son asked the grandfather again. In
front of the whole army he said, "Son of Ganga, the Pandava's army is very fine,
with a great many men, elephants and horses, crowded with great chariot
fighters. It is guarded by great archers of great strength, Bhima, Arjuna and
the rest, like the guardians of the world, with Dhrishtadyumna at their head. It
cannot be attacked or held back. It is like the ocean risen over its shore, an
ocean of an army that not even the gods could shake in a great battle. In what
time could you destroy it, son of Ganga, you of great splendour? Or the teacher,
the great archer, or Kripa of great strength, or Karna, who glories in battle,
or Drona's son, best of the twice-born? For all of you in my army know divine
weapons. I want to know this, for I am always very curious about it in my heart.
You should tell me, strong-armed one."

"This is worthy of you, best of the Kurus, lord of the earth," said Bhishma, "if
you are asking about the strength and weakness of your enemies and your own
people. Hear, king, what my greatest power in battle would be, and the might of
my weapons and of my arms in battle, strong-armed one. Other people are to be
fought with straight fighting, and one who fights with tricks with tricks. That
is the settled rule of dharma. I could destroy the Pandavas' army, strong-armed
one, taking my share each morning, day after day. Taking as my share ten
thousand fighters, you of great splendour, and one thousand chariot fighters:
that is what I reckon as my share. In this way, armed and always ready, I could
wear down that great army in this time, Bharata. But if I stood in battle and
loosed great weapons that kill a hundred thousand, I would kill them in a month,
Bharata."

When he had heard Bhishma's words, King Duryodhana, king of kings, asked Drona,
the best of the Angirases, "Teacher, in what time could you kill the Pandava's
soldiers?"

Drona answered him, almost laughing. "I am old, best of the Kurus, and my breath
and my movements are slow. I could burn up the Pandavas' army with the fire of
my weapons, like Bhishma, son of Shantanu, in a month. That is what I think.
That is my utmost power, that is my utmost strength."

Kripa Sharadvata said in two months. Drona's son promised to destroy their
strength in ten nights. But Karna, who knows great weapons, promised to do it in
five nights.

When he heard the words of the charioteer's son, the son of the river that goes
to the ocean laughed out loud. "You have not yet met Partha in battle, Radheya,"
he said. "You have not met him coming on in his chariot, unshaken, carrying
arrows, sword and bow, with Vasudeva beside him. That is why you think like
this. You can say this, and more, just as you please."

When the son of Kunti heard this, best of the Bharatas, said Vaishampayana, he
called all his brothers together in private. "The spies I have in
Dhritarashtra's son's army brought me this news as the night was ending," he
said. Duryodhana, it seems, asked the son of Apaga, of the great vow, 'In what
time could you kill the army of the Pandus, lord?' And that wicked son of
Dhritarashtra was told, 'In a month.' Drona too promised to do it in the same
time. Gautama named twice that time, we have heard. Drona's son, who knows great
weapons, promised to do it in ten nights. And Karna, who knows divine weapons,
when he was asked in the Kuru assembly, promised to kill the army in five days.
So I too want to hear your word, Arjuna. In what time could you destroy our
enemies in battle?"

When the king said this to him, Gudakesha Dhananjaya looked at Vasudeva and
answered, "All these great men are masters of weapons and fighters of many
skills. They could kill your army, great king, no doubt of it. But let your
distress go. I tell you the truth. With a single chariot, and Vasudeva to help
me, I could kill the three worlds with the immortals, all that moves and all
that does not. I could kill what was, what is and what will be, in the blink of
an eye. That is what I believe. The great and terrible weapon that Pashupati
gave me in the single combat with the Kirata is with me. The weapon that
Pashupati uses at the end of the age, when he destroys all beings, tiger among
men, is with me. Ganga's son does not know it, nor Drona, nor Gautama, nor
Drona's son, king, still less the charioteer's son.

"But it is not right to kill ordinary people in battle with divine weapons. We
will conquer our enemies by straight fighting. And these tigers among men are
your allies, lord of the earth. All of them know divine weapons, and all of them
delight in battle. All have bathed at the end of their Vedic study, and all are
unbeaten. They could destroy even the army of the gods in battle, Pandava. There
are Shikhandi, Yuyudhana, Dhrishtadyumna son of Prishata, Bhimasena, both the
twins, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas, Virata and Drupada, who are equal to Bhishma
and Drona in battle. And you yourself are able to overturn the three worlds. The
man you look at in anger, you who shine like Vasava, would soon be no more. I
know that of you, Kaurava."

Then, in the clear morning, urged by Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, the kings
set out against the Pandavas. They had all bathed and were pure, garlanded and
dressed in white. They carried their weapons and their banners, had had
blessings spoken over them and had made their offerings to the fire. All of them
knew the Veda, all were brave, all kept their vows well, all had done great
deeds, and all bore the marks of battle. Of great strength, seeking the highest
worlds in battle, all of them were of one mind and had faith in the world
beyond.

Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, and the Kekayas with the Bahlikas, all set out,
with Bharadvaja's son at their head. Ashvatthama, the son of Shantanu,
Jayadratha of Sindhu, and the chariot fighters of the south, the west and the
mountains went too. So did Shakuni, king of Gandhara, and all those of the east
and the north, the Shakas, Kiratas, Yavanas, Shibis and Vasatis. Each with their
own divisions, they surrounded the great chariot fighter. All these great
chariot fighters went out in the second force. Kritavarma with his division, the
mighty Trigartas, King Duryodhana surrounded by his brothers, Shala,
Bhurishravas, Shalya and Brihadbala of Kosala came behind, with the son of
Dhritarashtra at their head.

Those great chariot fighters, eager for battle, went by level roads and took up
their positions in their armour in the western half of Kurukshetra. Duryodhana
had his camp made, Bharata, adorned like a second Hastinapura. Not even skilled
men who lived in the city, king of kings, could tell the difference between the
city and the camp. The Kauravya lord of the earth had forts just like it made
for the kings as well, by hundreds and thousands. Leaving a circle of five
yojanas as the field of battle, king, they entered their camps by hundreds of
companies. There the lords of the earth quickly entered their camps, well
stocked, by the thousand, each as his energy and strength allowed. King
Duryodhana ordered the finest food for those great men and their armies and all
who came with them, and for their elephants, horses and men. He ordered it too
for those who lived by crafts, and the others who followed them there: sutas,
magadhas and bards, merchants, courtesans and harlots, and the people who came
to watch. The Kaurava king looked after all of them as was proper.

In the same way King Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, the son of Dharma, urged on his
heroes, Bharata, with Dhrishtadyumna at their head. He appointed Dhrishtaketu,
leader of the Chedis, Kashis and Karushas, of firm valour, a general and a
killer of enemies. So too Virata, Drupada, Yuyudhana, Shikhandi and the two
great archers of Panchala, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas. Those heroes, great
archers, in bright armour and wearing earrings of burnished gold, shone like
fires blazing on their altars when ghee is poured on them, like blazing planets.

Then the bull among men honoured his army as was fitting, and the lord of the
earth ordered his divisions to march. The son of Pandu sent out Abhimanyu,
Brihanta and all the sons of Draupadi, with Dhrishtadyumna at their head.
Yudhishthira sent Bhima, Yuyudhana and the Pandava Dhananjaya as the second body
of troops. The noise of the glad fighters there, loading their gear, moving and
running about, seemed to touch the sky. Last of all went the lord of the earth
himself, with Virata and Drupada and the other lords of the earth. The army, led
by Dhrishtadyumna and guarded by fearsome bowmen, looked like the Ganga in
flood, still, yet flowing.

Then the wise king arranged his divisions again, to confuse the minds of
Dhritarashtra's sons. To the first force the Pandava assigned the great archers,
Draupadi's sons, with Abhimanyu, Nakula, Sahadeva and all the Prabhadrakas. With
them went ten thousand horses, two thousand elephants, ten thousand foot
soldiers and five hundred chariots, and Bhimasena, whom no one can face. In the
middle force he put Virata and Jayatsena of Magadha, and the two great chariot
fighters of Panchala, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas, mighty and great-minded,
carrying maces and bows. Then Vasudeva and Dhananjaya followed in the middle.

The men were full of fury, their weapons ready. There were twenty thousand
banners, held by heroes, five thousand elephants and lines of chariots on every
side. Brave foot soldiers carrying bows, swords and maces followed behind by the
thousand, and went ahead by the thousand. Where Yudhishthira himself was, in
that ocean of an army, most of the lords of the earth gathered round him. There
were thousands of elephants and tens of thousands of horses, thousands of
chariots and foot soldiers, Bharata. Relying on them, he went to war against
Suyodhana, son of Dhritarashtra.

Then other men followed by the hundred, the thousand and the ten thousand,
roaring, and their divisions went out by the thousand. There, full of joy, men
by the thousand and the ten thousand sounded thousands of drums and tens of
thousands of conches.

**Divergences.**

* **Bhishma's reckoning.** Bhishma says he could kill ten thousand fighters and a thousand chariot fighters each morning, and that with his great weapons he would kill the army "in a month" (5.194.11–14). At 5.153 he made his terms with Duryodhana in similar numbers (ledger L-182).
* **Arjuna's answer.** Arjuna says the weapon Pashupati gave him "in the single combat with the Kirata" could destroy the three worlds, but "it is not right to kill ordinary people in battle with divine weapons. We will conquer our enemies by straight fighting" (5.195.12–15). The Kirata combat is told in Book 3 (3.40–41).
* **The end of Book 5.** The CE Udyoga ends here, with the Pandava army on the march (5.197.20–21). Both armies' arrival and array belong to Book 6.
* \*591, \*592, \*593, \*594 and \*595 are not narrated, nor App. I No. 14 (a longer list of Yudhishthira's following).

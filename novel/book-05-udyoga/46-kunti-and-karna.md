# Kunti and Karna

When Krishna's appeal had failed and he had gone from the Kurus to the Pandavas,
the steward went to Pritha and spoke to her slowly, as if grieving.

"Mother of living sons, you know my mind is always set on kindness. I cry out,
and Suyodhana does not take my words. The king there has the Chedis, Panchalas
and Kekayas, Bhima and Arjuna, Krishna, Yuyudhana and the twins. Though he is
camped at Upaplavya, Yudhishthira wants only dharma, out of love for his kin,
like a weak man though he is strong. But this King Dhritarashtra, old as he is,
will not make peace. Drunk with pride in his sons, he keeps to the road against
dharma. Through the evil minds of Jayadratha, Karna, Duhshasana and Subala's
son, division goes on between them. The kingdom of that man so devoted to dharma
was taken against dharma. Those who did it will reap this dharma with its
consequences. When dharma is taken by force by the Kurus, who would not burn?
Since Keshava has gone without making peace, the Pandavas will make ready for
war. Then the Kurus' wrong policy will destroy heroes. When I think of it, I
cannot sleep by day or night."

When she heard those words, spoken by a man who wished her well, Kunti, sighing
and afflicted with grief, thought it over in her mind.

"Shame on wealth, for whose sake this great slaughter of kin will happen. In
this war the friends will be defeated. If the Pandavas, the Chedis and Panchalas
and the Yadavas, gathered together, fight the Bharatas, what grief could be
greater than that? I see certain evil in war, and defeat in war too. Death is
better for a man without wealth, for there is no victory in the destruction of
kin. The grandfather, Shantanu's son, the teacher, lord of fighters, and Karna,
all for Dhritarashtra's son, add to my fear. The teacher Drona would never fight
his pupils willingly, and how could the grandfather not feel love for the
Pandavas? But this one man, who follows the false view of evil-minded
Dhritarashtra's son, always following his folly, wicked, hates the Pandavas.
Karna is set on great harm, and very strong besides. He is always against the
Pandavas, and that burns me now.

"Today I hope to win Karna's heart over to the Pandavas. I will go to him and
show him how things truly are. When I was living in my father's house, the
blessed Durvasas, pleased with me, gave me a boon, a spell for calling the gods.
In the king's women's quarters, honoured by Kuntibhoja, I thought about it in
many ways, with a troubled heart. Again and again I thought about the power or
weakness of the spells and the power of the brahmin's word, because I was a
woman and a child. I was guarded by a trusted nurse and surrounded by my
friends, avoiding any fault and protecting my father's good name. I thought,
'How can I do right? How can I avoid doing wrong?' And I bowed to the brahmin.
When I had won the spell, out of curiosity and childishness I used it, and so,
though a girl, I called the sun god to me. He was the child I bore before
marriage, and was treated as a son. Why should he not do what is good for him
and good for his brothers?"

When she had settled on this, the best course, Kunti went out to do it and went
to the Bhagirathi. There on the bank of the Ganga Pritha heard the sound of her
son's recitation, her son, kind and devoted to truth. He stood facing east with
his arms raised, and the ascetic woman stood behind him, waiting for the end of
his prayers for her purpose. Kuru's wife, the Vrishni woman, stood in the shade
of Karna's upper cloth, afflicted by the sun's heat, like a garland of lotuses
withering. He prayed until his back was burnt by the sun. Then he turned, true
to his vows, and saw Kunti. That proud man of great energy, the best of those
who uphold dharma, greeted her with joined hands, as was right, and stood before
her.

"I am Radha's son, Adhiratha's son, Karna, and I greet you," said Karna. "Why
have you come, lady? Tell me what I can do for you."

"You are Kunti's son, not Radha's," said Kunti, "and Adhiratha is not your
father. You were not born in a suta's family, Karna. Know that from my words.
You are my son born before marriage, my first-born, carried in my womb in
Kuntibhoja's house. You are Pritha's son, my child. This god who blazes, who
works in the light and warms the world, fathered you on me, Karna, the best of
those who bear weapons. You were born to me, my son, in my father's house, with
earrings and armour bound on, a child of the gods, clothed in glory, hard to
withstand.

"It is not right, son, least of all for you, that you serve Dhritarashtra's sons
in your folly, without knowing your brothers. This is the fruit of dharma for
men in their dharma, son, that their fathers and their mother, who sees only one
thing, are pleased with them. Take from Dhritarashtra's sons the fortune of
Yudhishthira that Arjuna won before, which wicked men took out of greed, and
enjoy it. Let the Kurus see Karna and Arjuna come together today, and seeing
them as true brothers, let the wicked bow down. Let Karna and Arjuna be like
Rama and Janardana. What in the world could be beyond the two of you, united?
Surely, Karna, you will shine surrounded by your five brothers, like Brahma
surrounded by the Vedas with the limbs of the Veda as the fifth. Rich in
virtues, best and eldest among the best of kin, you are not to be called a
suta's son. You are a mighty Partha."

---

Then Karna heard a voice that came from the sun, hard to set aside, loving,
spoken as a father speaks, sent forth by the maker of light. "Pritha has spoken
the truth. Karna, do what your mother says. It will be best for you, tiger among
men, if you do all of it." But though he was spoken to like this by his mother,
and by his father the sun himself, Karna's mind did not waver then, for he was
firm in truth.

"Kshatriya woman," said Karna, "it is not that I do not believe what you have
said, or that it would not be a door to dharma for me to do as you command. But
the wrong you did me, so very great, cast me away, and that destroyed my fame
and glory. I was born a kshatriya, and I did not receive the rites of a
kshatriya. Because of you. What worse harm could an enemy do me? When it was
time to act you had no pity for me, and now, when the time for my rites has
gone, you urge me on. You never acted for my good before, as a mother would. Now
you rouse me only because you want your own good.

"Who would not be afraid of Dhananjaya with Krishna beside him? Who would not
think me afraid today, if I went over to the Parthas' side? I was not known as
their brother before, and I am revealed in the time of war. If I go to the
Pandavas, what will the kshatriyas say of me? Dhritarashtra's sons have shared
every pleasure with me and always honoured me greatly. How could I make that
fruitless? They have made enemies of others, they always wait on me and always
bow to me, as the Vasus bow to Vasava. They think that with my life they can
stand against their enemies. How could I break their hopes today? They want to
cross the war, which is hard to cross, with me as their boat. They want to reach
the far shore where there is none. How could I abandon them? The time has come
for those who live on Dhritarashtra's son. I must pay my debt there, not
guarding my life. Those who have had their aims fulfilled and been well kept,
and then, when the time for action comes, ignore what was done for them and turn
against it, are wicked and unsteady. They wrong their king and steal their
master's food. For them, doers of evil, there is neither this world nor the
next.

"I will fight your sons for the sake of Dhritarashtra's sons, with all my
strength and power. I do not speak a lie to you. Keeping to kindness and the
conduct fit for a good man, I will not do as you say today, though it would
profit me. But this effort of yours with me will not be in vain. In battle I
will not kill your sons, though I could withstand them and kill them:
Yudhishthira, Bhima and the twins, all except Arjuna. In Yudhishthira's army my
fight is with Arjuna. If I kill Arjuna in battle, I will win the fruit. Or if I
am killed by the left-handed archer, I will be joined with glory. Your sons will
never be fewer than five, famous lady: without Arjuna and with Karna, or with
Arjuna if I am killed."

When she heard Karna's words, Kunti trembled with grief and embraced her son
Karna, who was unshaken in his resolve.

"So it must be," she said. "The Kauravas will go to destruction, as you say,
Karna. Fate is stronger. You have given four of your brothers freedom from fear,
slayer of enemies. Remember that promise and keep it."

"Be well, and may it go well with you," Pritha said to Karna. Karna answered her
gladly, and they went their separate ways.

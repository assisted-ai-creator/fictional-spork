# Who Krishna Is

"Duryodhana," said Dhritarashtra, "understand what I am going to tell you, my
son. You think the wrong road is the right one, like a traveller who does not
know the way. You want to measure the power of the five sons of Pandu, which is
like the power of the five great elements, the greatest of all. You cannot
understand Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, who stands in the highest dharma, without
looking to the highest goal. You challenge Bhimasena, son of Kunti, whose
strength has no equal, the ender of battles, as a tree challenges a great wind.

"What wise man would fight the Gandiva bowman in battle, the best of all who
bear weapons, as Meru is the best of mountains? Whom would Dhrishtadyumna, the
Panchala, not cut down today, shooting arrows among his enemies as the king of
the gods hurls the thunderbolt? And Satyaki, hard to beat, honoured among the
Andhakas and Vrishnis, devoted to the Pandavas' good, will destroy your army.
What wise man would fight Krishna, the lotus-eyed, who outweighs the three
worlds in the balance? On one side are his wives, his kin and his family, his
own self and this earth, and on the other side is Dhananjaya. Vasudeva,
unassailable and self-controlled, is where the Pandava is. That army is
unbearable even to the earth where Keshava is.

"Stand by the words of the good, my son, of friends who speak for your gain.
Bear with old Bhishma, your grandfather, Shantanu's son. Listen to me when I
speak for the Kurus' gain, and to Drona, Kripa and Vikarna, and to the great
king Bahlika. You should regard them as you regard me. They all know dharma,
Bharata, and they love both sides alike. In Virata's city your army, with your
brothers in front, let go the cattle and broke apart in terror. What is told of
that great wonder in that city, of one man against many, is proof enough. Arjuna
did that alone. What then will they all do together? Acknowledge them as your
brothers, and give them their living."

---

When wise Dhritarashtra had said this to Suyodhana, the fortunate king
questioned Sanjaya again. "Tell me the rest, Sanjaya, what Arjuna said to you
after Vasudeva. I am very eager to hear it."

"When he had heard Vasudeva's words," said Sanjaya, "Dhananjaya, son of Kunti,
whom no one can beat, spoke at the right moment, in Vasudeva's hearing.
'Sanjaya, give my greeting and my good wishes, as is fitting, to all who are
gathered there. Give them to Grandfather, Shantanu's son, and to Dhritarashtra,
Drona, Kripa, Karna and the great King Bahlika, to Drona's son and Somadatta, to
Shakuni son of Subala, to Duhshasana, Shala, Purumitra and Vivimshati, to
Vikarna and Chitrasena and King Jayatsena. Give them to Vinda and Anuvinda of
Avanti, to the Kaurava Durmukha, to the king of Sindhu, to Duhsaha and
Bhurishravas, to King Bhagadatta and King Jalasamdha. Give them to the other
kings who have gathered there to fight for the Kauravas' pleasure. The son of
Dhritarashtra has brought them, eager to die in the blazing fire of the
Pandavas, suta.

"'And say this, Sanjaya, among the kings, to Suyodhana, the chief of evildoers,
the unforgiving, evil-minded prince, the wicked and greedy son of Dhritarashtra.
Make him and his ministers hear all these words of mine.'

"When he had set me on my way like this, Dhananjaya, the wise Partha, with his
long eyes red at the corners, looked at Vasudeva and spoke words of profit and
dharma. 'As you heard the careful words of the great hero of the Madhus, so you
must give my words to all the kings gathered there. There is a great battle
where the smoke is the fire of arrows, the chanting is the chariot wheels, and
the ladle is the bow that robs the strength of weapons. Take care, all of you
together, that no offering is made in it. Yudhishthira, the killer of enemies,
wants his share, which is his own. If you will not give it, I will send you,
with your horses, foot soldiers and elephants, to the unlucky quarter of the
Ancestors with my sharp arrows.'

"Then I took my leave of four-armed Hari and bowed to Dhananjaya, and came here
quickly to bring you their great words, you whose splendour is like the
immortals'."

---

Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, did not welcome those words. All were silent,
and the kings rose. When all the kings of the earth had risen, great king, the
king began to question Sanjaya in private. He was hoping for victory for his
sons, following his son's wishes, and wanting to know what his own side, the
enemy and the Pandavas had resolved.

"Son of Gavalgana," said Dhritarashtra, "tell us what is strong and what is weak
in our own army. You know everything about the Pandavas. What is greater in
them, and what is less? You know the essence of both, you see everything, you
are skilled in dharma and profit and know how to judge. I ask you, Sanjaya: tell
me everything. Which of them, if they fight, will not survive?"

"I will never tell you anything in private, king," said Sanjaya, "for resentment
would get the better of you. Bring your father, firm in his vows, and Queen
Gandhari, Ajamidha. They know dharma, they are wise and able to judge, and they
will drive out your resentment, lord of men. In their presence I will tell you
all that Vasudeva and Arjuna have in mind."

When he knew what Sanjaya and his son had in mind, the wise Krishna Dvaipayana
came and said, "Sanjaya, tell Dhritarashtra everything he asks. Tell him all you
know about Vasudeva and Arjuna, just as it is."

---

"Arjuna and Vasudeva," said Sanjaya, "are archers most highly honoured. They
were not born of desire, and they are made for the destruction of all. Standing
in the space of the sky, the discus of great-minded Vasudeva, as it is set,
turns by magic, lord. It is hidden from the Pandavas, and the Pandavas honour it
greatly. Hear from me in short the strength and weakness of it all.

"Madhava, Janardana, defeated Naraka, Shambara, Kamsa and the Chedi king,
terrible to see, as if playing. The best of beings, whose self is higher than
all, the master, brings earth, sky and heaven under his power by his mind alone.
You keep asking about the Pandavas, king, to learn their strength and weakness.
Hear me, then. Put the whole world on one side, and Janardana on the other, and
Janardana outweighs the whole world in substance. Janardana could burn this
world to ashes by his mind alone. But the whole world could not burn Janardana
to ashes.

"Where there is truth, where there is dharma, where there is modesty and
honesty, there is Govinda. Where Krishna is, there is victory. The best of
beings, the self of all creatures, Janardana, moves earth, sky and heaven as if
playing. He has made the Pandavas a cover, as if to confuse the world, and he
wants to burn your sons, who are fools and delight in what is against dharma.
Keshava, the lord, turns the wheel of Time, the wheel of the world and the wheel
of the ages, without rest, by the yoga of his self. The lord alone rules Time,
and Death, and all that moves and does not move. I tell you this truly. Though
he rules the whole world, Hari, the great yogi, sets about his works like a weak
farmer. So Keshava deceives the worlds by the yoga of his magic. Those men who
take refuge in him alone are not deluded."

"How do you know Madhava as the great lord of all the worlds," said
Dhritarashtra, "and how is it that I do not know him? Tell me that, Sanjaya."

"Your knowledge is no knowledge, king," said Sanjaya. "My knowledge does not
fail. A man without knowledge, lost in darkness, does not know Keshava. By
knowledge, my father, I know Madhusudana, who is of the three ages, the maker
who was not made, the god who is the source and the end of beings."

"What is this devotion of yours to Janardana, son of Gavalgana," said
Dhritarashtra, "that never fails, by which you know Madhusudana, who is of the
three ages?"

"I do not serve deceit, bless you," said Sanjaya. "I do not practise empty
dharma. My heart is made pure by devotion, and I know Janardana from the
scriptures."

"Duryodhana," said Dhritarashtra, "take refuge with Hrishikesha, Janardana.
Sanjaya is one we trust, my son. Go to Keshava for refuge."

"If the blessed son of Devaki is going to destroy the world," said Duryodhana,
"and proclaims his friendship with Arjuna, I will not go to Keshava today."

"Gandhari," said Dhritarashtra, "this son of yours is going down, the
evil-minded, jealous, wicked, proud one, who goes beyond the words of his
betters."

"You who want power," said Gandhari, "wicked one, you who go beyond the commands
of the old, you fool, you give up power, and life, and your father and me. You
add to the pleasure of your enemies and to my sorrow. When you are killed by
Bhimasena, you will remember your father's words."

"You are dear to Krishna, King Dhritarashtra," said Vyasa. "Listen to me.
Sanjaya is your envoy, and he will join you to what is good. He knows
Hrishikesha, the ancient and the new. If you listen to him with one mind, he
will free you from great fear. Son of Vichitravirya, some men are wrapped in
anger, joy and darkness, bound by many kinds of noose, and not content with
their own wealth. They come again and again into the power of Yama, deluded by
desire, like the blind led by the blind, led by their own deeds. This is the one
road on which the wise go. When he sees it, a great man passes beyond death and
does not cling there."

"My dear Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "tell me of the road where there is
nothing to fear, by which I may go to Hrishikesha and find the highest peace."

"A man who is not master of himself can never know Janardana, who is master of
himself," said Sanjaya. "The only means of action for the self is holding back
the senses. When the senses rise up, giving up desire with care, carefulness and
harmlessness are surely the source of knowledge. Be careful in restraining the
senses, king, and do not tire. Do not let your understanding slip. Hold it back
from this and that. The brahmins know this as knowledge: the firm holding of the
senses. This is knowledge, and this is the road on which the wise go. Keshava
cannot be reached, king, by men whose senses are not conquered. The master of
himself, who has gained him through the scriptures and through yoga, finds his
peace in the truth."

---

"Tell me more of the lotus-eyed one, Sanjaya, for I ask," said Dhritarashtra.
"Knowing the meaning of his names and deeds, my son, I may reach the best of
beings."

"I have heard the auspicious meanings of that god's names," said Sanjaya, "and I
will tell them as far as I know them, for Keshava cannot be measured.

"He is to be known as Vasudeva because he clothes all beings, because he is
bright, and because the gods have their source in him. He is called Vrishni
because he is a bull. Know him, Bharata, as Madhava because of his silence, his
meditation and his yoga. He is Madhuha and Madhusudana, the slayer of Madhu,
because all the elements of being dissolve in him. *Krishi* is a word that means
'earth', and *na* is a word that means 'bliss'. Joining the two, the eternal one
is Krishna. *Pundarika*, the lotus, is the highest abode, eternal, unfading,
imperishable, and because he is that, he is Pundarikaksha, lotus-eyed. He is
Janardana because he makes the robbers tremble. Because goodness never falls
from him, and he never falls from goodness, he is Satvata. Because of his
bull-like eye he is Vrishabhekshana. Because he is not born of a mother, he is
Aja, the unborn, the conqueror of armies. Because the gods shine by their own
light, and because of self-control, they know him as Damodara. From joy,
happiness and the lordship of bliss he is Hrishikesha. Because he holds heaven
and earth with his two arms he is known as Mahabahu, the strong-armed. Because
he never falls below, he is Adhokshaja. Because he is the resting place of men,
he is known as Narayana. Because he fills and dwells in all, he is Purushottama,
the best of beings. Because he is the source and the end of all, what is and
what is not, and because he always knows all, they call him Sarva, the all.
Krishna is established in truth, and truth is established in him. Govinda is
truer than the true, and so his name is Satya, the true. He is called Vishnu
because of his strides, and Jishnu because he conquers. He is Ananta, the
endless, because he is eternal, and Govinda because he knows the cows.

"He makes the unreal real, and so he deludes creatures. Such is the blessed one,
always constant in dharma. The strong-armed, unfallen one will come here with
the seers, for the sake of kindness."

"I envy those who have eyes, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra. "They will see
Vasudeva near them, shining with his supreme body, lighting up the quarters and
the spaces between. He will raise his voice among the Bharatas, a voice to be
honoured by the Bharatas and to bring peace to the Srinjayas. It will be a voice
without fault, to be taken up by those who want to prosper and not by those who
are doomed. He will rise, the Satvata, the one hero, the leader and bull of the
Yadavas, the killer and troubler of his enemies, who robs his foes of their
glory. The Kurus gathered will see him, the great one, the killer of enemies,
the chosen one, the best of the Vrishnis, speaking kind words and bewitching my
people.

"I take refuge with the most ancient seer, the wise one, the ocean of speech,
the vessel of ascetics, Arishtanemi, Garuda Suparna, the lord of creatures, the
home of the world. I take refuge with the thousand-headed person, the ancient
one, without beginning, middle or end, of endless glory, the maker of the seed,
the unborn, the begetter, higher than the highest. I take refuge with the maker
of the three worlds, the begetter of gods and asuras, of snakes and rakshasas,
the first of kings and of the wise, Indra's younger brother."

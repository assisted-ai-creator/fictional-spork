# Shikhandini

"'How did Shikhandi, who was a girl, become a man then, son of Ganga, best of
fighters?'" said Duryodhana. "'Tell me that, grandfather.'

"'King Drupada's wife, king of kings,'" said Bhishma, "'his beloved chief queen,
had no son, lord of the people. At that very time, great king, King Drupada was
pleasing Shamkara for the sake of offspring. Having resolved on my death, he
took up terrible austerity, and he won from the great god a girl, though he
said, "Let me have a son. Lord, I want a son to take revenge on Bhishma." The
god of gods said, "You will have one who is female and male. Turn back, lord of
the earth. It will never be otherwise." He went to his city and said this to his
wife. "I made a great effort by austerity for a son, queen, and Shambhu told me,
'Having been a girl, he will be a man.' When I begged him again and again, Shiva
said, 'It is fated. It will not be otherwise. What must be will be.'"

"'Then the high-spirited wife of King Drupada, restrained, in her season, lay
with Drupada. She conceived in due time by the means the rules lay down, from
Prishata's son, lord of the earth, as Narada told me. Then the lotus-eyed queen
carried the child, and strong-armed King Drupada cared for his dear wife in
comfort, out of love for a son, joy of the Kurus. And to the sonless King
Drupada was born a girl of the finest beauty, lord of men. But the famous wife
of the sonless King Drupada announced, king of kings, "A son has been born to
me." Then King Drupada had all the rites for a son performed for the hidden girl
as if for a son, lord of men. Drupada's chief queen guarded the secret with
every effort, calling her "son". No one in the city knew of her except
Prishata's son. Believing the words of the god of wonderful splendour, he hid
the girl and said, "It is a man." The king had all the birth rites performed for
her, with the forms for a male, and they knew her as Shikhandi. I alone knew,
through a spy and through Narada's word, through the god's word and through
Amba's austerity.

---

"'Drupada made great efforts for all his own people. Then she went to the
highest point in writing and the other arts, and in archery, king of kings,
became a pupil of Drona. Her mother, great king, urged the king to find a wife
for the girl, as for a son. Then, seeing the girl come of age and knowing her to
be a woman, Prishata's son fell into worry with his wife.

"'"This girl of mine has come of age and adds to my grief," said Drupada. "I hid
her at the word of the god who holds the trident. That can never be false, great
queen. Why would the maker of the three worlds make it false?"

"'"If you approve, king, I will speak," said his wife. "Listen to my words, and
when you have heard them, son of Prishata, do what you must. Let a wife be taken
for him by the rules, king. That word will come true. That is my firm view."

"'Then the two of them, husband and wife, having made up their minds about this,
chose the daughter of the lord of the Dasharnas as a bride. King Drupada, lion
among kings, considered all the kings by their families and chose the daughter
of the Dasharna king as wife for Shikhandi. The Dasharna king was known as
Hiranyavarma, and that lord of the earth gave the girl to Shikhandi. That king,
Hiranyavarma, was a great lord among the Dasharnas, hard to withstand, with a
great army, a great-minded man.

"'When the wedding had been held, best of kings, both girls came of age, the
bride and Shikhandini. Married, Shikhandi came back to Kampilya. For some time
the bride did not know, they say, that the other was a woman. When
Hiranyavarma's daughter found out that Shikhandini was a woman, she told her
nurses and friends, ashamed, that the Panchala king's child was a daughter,
Shikhandini. Then the Dasharna nurses, tiger among kings, were in the greatest
distress at this grief, and they sent word. Then the Dasharna lord's servants
told him everything, the deceit as it had happened, and the king was furious.
Meanwhile Shikhandi, great king, went about the royal household happily as a
man, never showing any sign of being a woman. Then, a few days later, bull of
the Bharatas, when Hiranyavarma heard of it, he was overcome with fury and pain,
king of kings.

"'Then the Dasharna king, filled with fierce anger, sent a messenger to
Drupada's palace. The messenger of the king with the golden armour came to
Drupada, drew him aside alone, and spoke privately. "The Dasharna king, king,
sends you this message, angry at the insult, for you have deceived him, sinless
one. 'You surely despise me, king. Your counsel was evil, for in your folly you
asked for my daughter for your daughter. Today reap the fruit of that deceit,
you wicked man. I will root you out with your people and counsellors. Stand
firm.'"

---

"'When the messenger had said this to Drupada, king, he could not speak, like a
thief who has been caught. He made strong efforts, sending messengers with sweet
words and kinsmen to appease him, with the message, "It is not so." But that
king, having made a true test, set out in haste, saying the Panchala's child was
a girl. Then, on the word of the nurses, he sent word of his daughter's
deception to his friends of boundless energy. Then the best of kings gathered
his forces and made up his mind to march on Drupada, Bharata. Then King
Hiranyavarma took counsel with his friends about the Panchala king, king of
kings. Those great kings came to this decision: "If this is true, king, and
Shikhandini is a girl, we will bind the Panchala king and bring him home. We
will set up another lord of men as king among the Panchalas and kill King
Drupada with Shikhandi." Then the lord sent a messenger, a steward, to
Prishata's son again: "I will kill you. Stand firm."

"'King Drupada, timid by nature and in the wrong, fell into fierce fear. Sending
away the Dasharna messenger, Drupada, worn with grief, went to his wife alone
and spoke. Seized by great fear and struck to the heart with grief, the Panchala
king said to Shikhandi's mother, his beloved, "My very strong kinsman, King
Hiranyavarma, will march against me in anger, dragging his army. What shall I do
now, fool that I am, about this girl? Shikhandi, your son, is suspected of being
a girl, they say. He has decided this as the truth, and thinking 'I have been
cheated,' he means to root me out with his friends, his army and his followers.
What is true here, lovely lady, and what is false? Tell me, fair one. When I
have heard your words, good lady, I will act on them. For I am in danger, and
this child Shikhandini is too, and you, queen, have come into great trouble,
lovely one. So tell me the truth, as I ask, to free us all. Then I can arrange
this matter, fair lady of bright smiles. And do not fear for Shikhandini. I will
do what is truly needed. I have been deceived by you, lovely one, in the dharma
of a son, and I have deceived the Dasharna king, lord of the earth. So tell me,
fortunate one. I will do what is best there." Though the lord of men knew, he
urged the queen openly, to make it known to the other, and she answered the lord
of the earth.'"

**Divergences.**

* **Shikhandi.** CE 5.189–193. Ledger L-181.
* Rejected lines (\*584–\*587) add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* **Pronouns.** The text calls Shikhandi a girl (*kanyā*, *śikhaṇḍinī*) and, as the name given her, Shikhandi. The novel follows it: "she" while she is a girl, "he" after the change in the next chapter.
* 5.189.5 *strīpumān*: "one who is female and male".
* 5.189.18: Bhishma knew "through a spy and through Narada's word".
* 5.190.10 *hiraṇyavarman*: Hiranyavarma, "golden-armoured"; also called *kāñcanavarman* and *hemavarman*.
* 5.191.20: Drupada asks openly, though he knows, "to make it known to the other", that is, to have the queen's words carried to Hiranyavarma.

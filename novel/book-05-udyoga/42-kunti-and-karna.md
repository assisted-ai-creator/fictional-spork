# Kunti and Karna

When Krishna's attempt at persuasion had failed and he had gone from the Kurus
to the Pandavas, the steward went to Pritha and spoke to her slowly, as if
grieving.

"You know, mother of living sons, how I have always wanted to be kind. Suyodhana
does not take my advice, though I cry it out. King Yudhishthira has the Chedis,
the Panchalas and the Kekayas, Bhima and Arjuna, Krishna, Yuyudhana and the
twins. Though he is encamped at Upaplavya, he still wants only dharma, out of
love for his kin, like a weak man, though he is strong. But King Dhritarashtra,
old as he is, will not make peace. Drunk with pride in his sons, he walks the
road against dharma. Through the evil mind of Jayadratha, Karna, Duhshasana and
Saubala, a split is coming among the kin. Those who have taken so righteous a
kingdom by adharma will reap this dharma, with all that follows it. Who would
not burn when dharma is carried off by force by the Kurus? Now that Keshava has
gone without making peace, the Pandavas will make ready for war. Then the Kurus'
wrongdoing will bring the destruction of heroes. I think of it and cannot sleep,
day or night."

When Kunti heard these words, spoken by one who wanted her good, she sighed, and
in her grief she thought it over in her mind.

"Shame on wealth, for whose sake this great slaughter of kin will come. In this
war friends will be defeated. If the Pandavas, the Chedis, the Panchalas and the
Yadavas together fight the Bharatas, what could be sadder? I see the evil in war
for certain, and I see defeat in war. For a poor man death is better, but there
is no victory in the destruction of kin. The grandfather, Shantanu's son, the
teacher, lord of fighters, and Karna, for the sake of Dhritarashtra's son, add
to my fear. The teacher Drona would never willingly fight his pupils. And why
should the grandfather not be kind to the Pandavas? But this one man, Karna, has
a false view. He always follows the folly of wicked Dhritarashtra's son. He is
evil and he hates the Pandavas. He is set on great harm, and he is very strong.
Karna is always against the Pandavas, and that burns me now.

"Today I hope to win Karna's heart for the Pandavas. I shall go to him and show
him the truth. When I was living in my father's house, the blessed Durvasas was
pleased with me and gave me a boon, a summoning joined to the gods. I was in the
king's women's rooms, honoured by Kuntibhoja. I thought it over in many ways, my
heart troubled, wondering again and again, as a woman and a child, about the
strength and weakness of the mantras and the power of the brahmin's word. I was
guarded by a trusted nurse and surrounded by my friends. I avoided any fault and
guarded my father's good name. I thought, 'How can I do well, and how can I be
free of blame?' and I bowed to the brahmin. When I had it, out of curiosity and
childishness I used it, and while I was a girl I brought the god, the Sun, to
me. He was carried in my womb before my marriage, and he was brought up as a
son. Why should he not do what I say, which is good and good for his brothers?"

When Kunti had decided this, the best thing to do, she went out for her purpose
to the Ganga. On the bank of the Ganga Pritha heard the sound of the recitation
of her son, who was kind and loved the truth. He was facing east with his arms
raised. The ascetic woman stood behind him, waiting for the end of his prayers
to speak of her purpose. The Vrishni woman, wife of the Kaurava, stood in the
shade of Karna's upper cloth, suffering in the heat of the sun, like a withering
garland of lotuses. He, strict in his vows, prayed until the sun was on his
back. Then he turned and saw Kunti. He greeted her, joined his palms and stood
before her, as was right, the mighty one, the proud one, the best of those who
keep dharma.

"I am Radheya, son of Adhiratha," said Karna. "I, Karna, greet you. Why have you
come, lady? Tell me what I can do for you."

"You are Kaunteya, not Radheya," said Kunti. "Adhiratha is not your father. You
were not born into the suta family, Karna. Know that what I say is true. I bore
you before my marriage. You were my first-born, and I carried you in my womb in
Kuntibhoja's house. You are a son of Pritha, my son. The god Virochana, the
burning one whose work is light, fathered you on me, Karna, the best of those
who bear weapons. You were born in my father's house, my son, with earrings and
bound in armour, a child of the gods, full of glory, unconquerable.

"It is not right, my son, least of all in you, that you should serve the sons of
Dhritarashtra in your folly, without knowing your brothers. This, my son, is the
fruit of dharma for men, in the judgement of dharma, that their fathers and
their mother, who sees one thing only, should be pleased with them. Take the
fortune that Arjuna once won and that bad men carried off out of greed. Wrest it
from the sons of Dhritarashtra and enjoy Yudhishthira's fortune. Let the Kurus
see today Karna and Arjuna come together. Let the wicked see it in brotherly
love, and bow down. Let Karna and Arjuna be to each other what Rama and
Janardana are. What would be impossible in the world for the two of you
together? Karna, you will surely shine, surrounded by your five brothers, as
Brahma shines surrounded by the Vedas, with the Vedangas as the fifth. You have
every virtue, and you are the best and eldest among your excellent kin. Let
there be no more talk of 'suta's son'. You are Partha, a hero."

Then Karna heard a voice come from the sun, hard to set aside, loving, spoken by
the Sun as a father would speak.

"Pritha has told the truth, Karna. Do what your mother says. It will be best for
you, tiger among men, if you do all of it."

But though his mother spoke to him like this, and his father the Sun himself,
Karna's mind did not waver, for he was steadfast in truth.

"It is not that I do not believe what you say, kshatriya woman," said Karna,
"nor that doing as you ask would not be a door to dharma for me. But you did me
a great wrong, which cannot be undone. You cast me off, and that destroyed my
name and my fame. I was born a kshatriya, and I never had the rites of a
kshatriya. Because of you. What worse harm could an enemy do me? When it was
time to act, you showed me no pity. And now, when the time for my rites is gone,
you urge me on. You never did me any good before, as a mother would. And now you
would win me over, wanting only your own good.

"Who would not be afraid of Dhananjaya with Krishna beside him? Who would not
think me afraid if I went over to the sons of Pritha now? I was known before as
having no brothers. If I am revealed now, at the time of battle, and go to the
Pandavas, what will the kshatriyas say of me? The sons of Dhritarashtra have
shared every pleasure with me and always honoured me greatly. How could I make
that fruitless? They have made enemies of others, and they always wait on me and
bow to me, as the Vasus bow to Vasava. They think that with my strength they can
stand against their enemies. How could I break their hopes now? They want to
cross the impassable battle with me as their boat, to reach the far shore where
there is no shore. How could I abandon them?

"This is the time for those who live on the sons of Dhritarashtra. I must give
myself to it without guarding my life. Those who are well kept and have what
they want, and who, when the time for action comes, forget what was done for
them and turn aside, are wicked and unsteady. They sin against their king and
steal their master's bread. For evildoers like that there is neither this world
nor the next. For the sake of the sons of Dhritarashtra I shall fight your sons
with all my strength and power. I do not lie to you. I keep to kindness and to
the conduct that befits a good man. So, though it would profit me, I shall not
do what you say now.

"But this effort of yours with me will not be for nothing. I shall not kill your
sons in battle, though I could, Yudhishthira, Bhima and the twins, except
Arjuna. My battle is with Arjuna, in Yudhishthira's army. If I kill Arjuna in
battle, I shall have my reward. Or if Savyasachin kills me, I shall be joined to
fame. Your five sons will never perish, famous one. There will be five, with
Karna and without Arjuna, or with Arjuna, if I am killed."

When Kunti heard Karna's words, she trembled with grief. She embraced her son,
Karna, who was unshaken in his firmness.

"So it must be," she said. "The Kauravas will go to their destruction, as you
say, Karna. Fate is the stronger. You have given four of your brothers freedom
from fear, destroyer of enemies. Remember that promise, and keep it."

Then Pritha said to Karna, "Health and good fortune to you." Karna answered her
gladly, and they went their separate ways.

**Divergences.**

* **Karna's promise is his own.** Karna offers, unasked, not to kill Yudhishthira, Bhima and the twins, and says Kunti will always have five sons (5.144.20–22). Kunti then asks him only to remember the promise (5.144.25). She does not ask him for boons, and nothing is said here about a snake-arrow or any weapon to be used only once. See ledger L-31.
* **The Sun's voice.** The Sun himself tells Karna, from the sky, that Kunti speaks the truth (5.144.1–2). A rejected line (\*532) has Kunti *see* Karna at prayer rather than hear him. Not narrated.
* **Karna already knew.** Karna had told Krishna that he knew his birth (5.139.2–4). Here he does not say so to Kunti, but he does not doubt her: "It is not that I do not believe what you say" (5.144.4).
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*531 and \*533.

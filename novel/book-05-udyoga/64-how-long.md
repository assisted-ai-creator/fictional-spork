# How Long

"When the night had turned to dawn," said Sanjaya, "your son again asked the
grandfather in the middle of the whole army, 'Son of Ganga, look at this fine
army of the Pandava's, with plenty of men, elephants and horses, crowded with
great chariot fighters. It is guarded by great archers of great strength like
the guardians of the world, Bhima, Arjuna and the others, led by Dhrishtadyumna.
It cannot be attacked or held back. It is like the risen ocean, an ocean of an
army that even the gods could not shake in a great battle. In what time could
you destroy it, son of Ganga of great splendour? Or the great archer, the
teacher, or very strong Kripa, or Karna, proud in battle, or Drona's son, the
best of the twice-born? For all of you in my force know heavenly weapons. I want
to know this. It has always been a great wonder in my heart, strong-armed one.
You should tell me.'

"'This is fitting for you, best of the Kurus, lord of the earth,' said Bhishma,
'if you ask about the strength and weakness of the enemy and of your own. Hear,
king, what my utmost power in battle would be, and the strength of my weapons
and my arms in battle, strong-armed one. Ordinary people must be fought by
honest fighting, and a master of magic by magic. That is the rule of dharma. I
could kill the Pandavas' army, strong-armed one, taking my share each day in the
morning. Taking ten thousand fighters and one thousand chariot fighters as my
share, splendid one: that is the share I have in mind. In this way, armoured and
always ready, I could destroy the great army in this time, Bharata. But if I
stood in battle and loosed great weapons that kill hundreds of thousands, I
could kill it in a month, Bharata.'

"When he heard Bhishma's words, king of kings, King Duryodhana asked Drona, the
best of the Angirasas, 'Teacher, in what time could you kill the soldiers of
Pandu's son?' Drona answered him, as if laughing, 'I am old, best of the Kurus,
slow in my life and movements. With the fire of my weapons I could burn up the
Pandavas' army, like Bhishma son of Shantanu, in a month, I think. This is my
utmost power. This is my utmost strength.' Kripa son of Sharadvat said in two
months. Drona's son promised the destruction of the army in ten nights, and
Karna, master of great weapons, promised it in five nights.

"When he heard the suta's son's words, the son of her who goes to the ocean
laughed aloud and said this. 'You have not yet met Partha in battle, Radha's
son, with arrows, sword and bow, joined with Vasudeva, rushing on in his
chariot, unfallen. That is why you think so. You can say this and more, as you
please.'

---

When Kunti's son heard this, best of the Bharatas, he called all his brothers
aside and said, "The spies I have in the armies of Dhritarashtra's son brought
me this news at dawn today. Duryodhana, they say, asked Ganga's son of great
vows, 'In what time could you kill the Pandus' army, lord?' 'In a month,' he
told evil-minded Dhritarashtra's son. Drona too promised it in that time.
Gautama named twice that time, we hear. Drona's son, master of great weapons,
promised it in ten nights. And Karna, who knows heavenly weapons, asked in the
Kuru assembly, promised to kill the army in five days. So I too want to hear
your words, Arjuna. In how long could you destroy the enemy in battle?"

Spoken to like this by the king, Gudakesha Dhananjaya looked at Vasudeva and
answered. "All of these are great men, masters of weapons, fighters of many
skills. No doubt, great king, they could kill your army. Let your mind's fever
go. I tell you truly. With a single chariot, with Vasudeva as my ally, I could
kill the three worlds. I could kill them with the immortals, with all that moves
and does not move, what has been, what is and what will be, in the blink of an
eye. That is what I think. That great, terrible weapon which Pashupati gave me
in the single combat with the Kirata is with me. That weapon which Pashupati
uses at the end of the age, destroying all creatures, is with me, tiger among
men. Ganga's son does not know it, nor Drona, nor Gautama, nor Drona's son,
king, let alone the suta's son. But it is not right to kill ordinary men in
battle with heavenly weapons. We will beat the enemy by honest fighting.

"And these tigers among men are your allies, lord of the earth, all knowing
heavenly weapons, all delighting in war. They have all bathed at the final bath
of the Vedanta, all unconquered. They could kill even an army of the gods in
battle, Pandava: Shikhandi, Yuyudhana, Dhrishtadyumna Prishata's grandson,
Bhimasena and the twins, Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas, Virata and Drupada, equal to
Bhishma and Drona in war. And you yourself could destroy the three worlds. I
know, Kaurava, that any man you look at in anger, you whose splendour is like
Vasava's, would surely not live long."

---

Then in the clear dawn, urged on by Dhritarashtra's son Duryodhana, the kings
set out against the Pandavas. All of them had bathed, pure, wearing garlands and
white clothes, holding weapons and banners, having had blessings spoken and
offerings made to the fire. All of them knew the Veda and were brave, all had
kept their vows well, all had done their work and all were marked for battle.
Mighty, seeking the highest worlds in battle, all with one mind and faith in
each other, they set out: Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, the Kekayas with the
Bahlikas, all of them led by Bharadvaja's son. Ashvatthama, Shantanu's son and
Jayadratha of Sindhu went, with the chariot fighters of the south, the west and
the mountains. Shakuni king of Gandhara went, and those of the east and north on
every side, Shakas, Kiratas, Yavanas, Shibis and Vasatis. With their own armies,
surrounding the great chariot fighter, all those great chariot fighters went out
in the second force. Kritavarma with his army, the mighty Trigartas, King
Duryodhana surrounded by his brothers, Shala, Bhurishravas, Shalya and
Brihadbala of Kosala came behind, with Dhritarashtra's son at their head. Going
by a level road, the great chariot fighters, ready to fight, took their stand in
armour in the western half of Kurukshetra.

Duryodhana had a camp made, Bharata, adorned like a second Hastinapura. Even
skilled men who lived in the city could not tell the city from the camp, king of
kings. The Kaurava, lord of the earth, had strongholds like it made for the
kings too, in hundreds and thousands. Leaving a circle of five yojanas as the
battlefield, the camps of the armies were set up in hundreds of groups, king.
There the lords of the earth quickly entered their well-stocked camps in
thousands, according to their spirit and strength. King Duryodhana gave orders
for the finest food and drink for those great men with their armies and those
outside them. The Kaurava king looked after them all as was fitting. He saw to
the elephants, horses and men, those who lived by crafts, the others who
followed, bards, heralds and panegyrists, merchants, courtesans and their
crowds, and the people who came to watch.

---

In the same way, Bharata, King Yudhishthira, Kunti's son, the son of Dharma,
urged on the heroes led by Dhrishtadyumna. He named as general Dhrishtaketu,
slayer of enemies, of firm courage, leader of the Chedis, Kashis and Karushas,
and Virata, Drupada, Yuyudhana, Shikhandi, and the two Panchala princes, the
great archers Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas. Those heroes, great archers, in bright
armour, wearing earrings of burnished gold, shone like fires blazing on their
altars when sprinkled with butter, like blazing planets. Then the bull among
men, the lord of the earth, honoured the army as was fitting and ordered the
ranks to march. The joy of Pandu sent out Abhimanyu, Brihanta and all the sons
of Draupadi, led by Dhrishtadyumna. Yudhishthira sent Bhima, Yuyudhana and the
Pandava Dhananjaya as the second body of the army. The noise of the happy
fighters there, loading the baggage, moving and running about, seemed to touch
the sky.

Then the lord of the earth himself went behind, with Virata and Drupada and the
other lords of the earth. The army with its terrible bowmen, led by
Dhrishtadyumna, looked like the Ganga in flood, still and flowing. Then the wise
one arranged his ranks again, confusing the minds of Dhritarashtra's sons. The
Pandava assigned to the front force the great archers the sons of Draupadi,
Abhimanyu, Nakula and Sahadeva and all the Prabhadrakas. With them went ten
thousand horses, two thousand elephants, ten thousand foot soldiers and five
hundred chariots, and Bhimasena, who is hard to withstand. In the middle he set
Virata and Jayatsena of Magadha, and the two great chariot fighters, the
Panchala princes Yudhamanyu and Uttamaujas, brave and great, bearing clubs and
bows. Behind them, in the middle, went Vasudeva and Dhananjaya.

The men were very fierce, practised in striking. Twenty thousand banners were
held by heroes. Five thousand elephants, lines of chariots everywhere, and brave
foot soldiers carrying bows, swords and clubs followed behind in thousands and
went in front in thousands. In the ocean of an army where Yudhishthira himself
was, most of the lords of the earth took their stand. There were thousands of
elephants and tens of thousands of horses, thousands of chariots and foot
soldiers, Bharata, relying on which he fought Dhritarashtra's son Suyodhana.
Then others went behind in hundreds, thousands and tens of thousands, roaring,
and their ranks marched in thousands. Thousands of drums and tens of thousands
of conches were played there by men in thousands and tens of thousands, full of
joy.

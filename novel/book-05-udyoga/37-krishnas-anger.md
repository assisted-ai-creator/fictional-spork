# Krishna's Anger

Then the Dasharha laughed, his eyes rolling with anger, and spoke to Duryodhana
in the Kuru assembly.

"You shall have your hero's bed. You shall get what you want. Stand firm, with
your ministers. There will be a great slaughter. And you think, fool, that you
have done no wrong to the Pandavas. Hear it all, lords of men.

"You were burning at the fortune of the great Pandavas, Bharata, and you and
Saubala planned the dicing badly. How, my friend, could kinsmen so fine, and
honoured by the good, be treated so unfairly? How could honest men be met by a
crooked one? The game of dice, very wise one, destroys the peace of good men.
Among bad men it breeds quarrels and disasters. You made this terrible disaster
that began with the dicing, without thinking, together with men of evil ties
against men of good conduct.

"Who else would treat his kinsman's wife as you did? Who else would bring her
into the hall and speak to her as you spoke to Draupadi? She is well born and of
good character, dearer to them than life, the queen of the sons of Pandu, and
you treated her like that. All the Kurus know how Duhshasana spoke to the sons
of Kunti, scorchers of enemies, in the Kuru assembly as they went into exile.
They behaved well, and they were free from greed, and they always walked in
dharma. What good man would treat his own kin so wrongly? You and Karna and
Duhshasana spoke again and again as cruel, base and harsh men speak.

"You made every effort to burn them at Varanavata, when they were children, with
their mother, and it did not succeed. Then the Pandavas lived hidden for a long
time with their mother at Ekachakra, in a brahmin's house. You tried to destroy
the Pandavas by every means, with poison and with the binding of snakes, and it
did not succeed. With such a mind towards the Pandavas, always treating them
falsely, how can you have done no wrong to the great Pandavas? You have done
many cruel and wicked things to the Pandavas. You are false and base, and now
you deny it.

"Your mother and father, Bhishma, Drona and Vidura have told you again and
again, 'Make peace,' and you will not, king. There is great profit in peace for
you and the son of Pritha, and you do not like it, king. What is this but
lightness of mind? You will not find peace, king, if you pass over the words of
your friends. What you are doing, lord of the earth, is against dharma, and it
brings shame."

While the Dasharha was saying this to resentful Duryodhana, Duhshasana spoke in
the Kuru assembly.

"King, if you will not make peace with the Pandavas of your own will, it seems
the Kauravas will bind you and give you to the son of Kunti. Bhishma, Drona and
your father will give the Pandavas three of us, bull among men, Vaikartana, you
and me."

When Suyodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, heard what his brother said, he rose in
anger and left, hissing like a great snake. The fool, the shameless one, took no
notice of Vidura, Dhritarashtra, great King Bahlika, Kripa, Somadatta, Bhishma,
Drona or Janardana. He behaved like a man who has no breeding and no limits. He
was proud, and he insulted those who deserved honour. When his brothers saw the
bull among men leave, they followed him with his ministers, and so did all the
kings.

When Bhishma, son of Shantanu, saw Duryodhana rise in anger in the hall and
leave with his brothers, he said, "When a man gives up dharma and gain and gives
way to his rage, his enemies soon laugh at his ruin. This king's son, the son of
Dhritarashtra, is wicked and knows no right way. He takes false pride in the
kingdom and follows anger and greed. I think all the kshatriyas are ripe for
death, Janardana. For all the kings and their ministers have followed him in
their folly."

When lotus-eyed Dasharha heard what Bhishma said, the mighty one spoke to them
all, to Bhishma, Drona and the rest.

"This is a great failing in all the elders of the Kurus, that you do not take
this foolish king in hand by force in his power. I think there is something to
be done that the time calls for, tamers of enemies. If it is done, it will be
for the best. Hear it all, blameless ones. I shall speak for your good, before
your eyes, if it pleases you, Bharatas, and if you agree.

"Kamsa, son of Ugrasena, was wicked and had no self-control. He took his old
father's power while he was still alive, the power of the king of the Bhojas,
and gave way to anger. His kin abandoned him, and I killed him in a great battle
for the good of our kin. Then we and our kinsmen honoured Ahuka again and made
Ugrasena king, and the Bhoja kingdom grew. All the Yadavas gave up Kamsa alone
for the sake of the family, and now the Bharatas, the Andhakas and the Vrishnis
have joined together and prosper happily.

"And there is something else, king, that the supreme Lord of Creatures said.
When the war of the gods and the asuras was drawn up, and the weapons raised,
and the worlds were split in two and perishing, Bharata, the blessed creator,
maker of the worlds, said, 'The asuras, the daityas and danavas, will be beaten.
The Adityas, the Vasus and the Rudras will dwell in heaven. Gods, asuras and
men, gandharvas, snakes and rakshasas will kill each other in this war, all
armed and ready.' With this in mind the supreme Lord of Creatures said to
Dharma, 'Bind these daityas and danavas and give them to Varuna.' So at the
supreme one's command Dharma bound all the daityas and danavas and gave them to
Varuna. And Varuna, lord of the waters, binds the danavas with the nooses of
Dharma and with his own nooses, and guards them carefully in the ocean for ever.

"In the same way bind Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni son of Subala, and Duhshasana
too, and give them to the Pandavas. For the family, give up one man. For the
village, give up a family. For the country, give up a village. For the self,
give up the earth. King, bind Duryodhana and then make peace with the Pandavas.
Do not let the kshatriyas perish because of you, bull among kshatriyas."

When King Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, heard Krishna's words, he spoke in
haste to Vidura, who knew all dharma.

"Go, my friend, and bring wise, far-sighted Gandhari here. With her I shall win
over this fool. If she can calm the wicked, evil-minded one, we may yet do what
our friend Krishna says. Perhaps she will show the way to this man overcome by
greed, this fool with his bad companions, and say something that is fitting.
Perhaps she will calm the terrible, great disaster Duryodhana has made, for a
long time to come, and it will bring security and gain, and last."

When Vidura heard what the king said, he brought far-sighted Gandhari at
Dhritarashtra's command.

"Gandhari," said Dhritarashtra, "this wicked son of yours goes beyond all
command. Out of greed for power he will lose his power and his life. He behaves
like a man with no breeding and no limits. With wicked, evil men the fool has
left the hall, passing over the words of his friends."

When the famous princess Gandhari heard her husband, she looked for what was
best, and spoke.

"Bring my son here quickly, sick with his longing for the kingdom. A man without
breeding, who destroys dharma and gain, cannot have a kingdom. But it is you who
are most to blame, Dhritarashtra, for you love your son. You know how wicked he
is, and you follow his understanding. He is deceived by desire and anger and
sunk in delusion. You cannot turn him back by force now, king. Dhritarashtra is
reaping the fruit of giving the kingdom to a fool, a child, a wicked man with
bad companions and full of greed. How could a wise man ignore a split among his
own people? When you are split from your own people, your enemies will overcome
you. When a disaster among one's own can be got over by conciliation or by
gifts, great king, who would bring down the rod?"

At Dhritarashtra's command, and at his mother's word, the steward brought
resentful Duryodhana back into the hall. He came into the hall again, wanting to
hear what his mother would say, his eyes red with anger, hissing like a snake.
When Gandhari saw her son come in, set on the wrong road, she rebuked him and
spoke fitting words.

"Duryodhana, my son, listen to what I say. It is for your good and your
followers', and it will bring happiness in time to come. If you make peace, you
will have paid honour to Bhishma, to your father and to me, and to your friends,
with Drona at their head.

"A kingdom cannot be won, or guarded, or enjoyed, very wise one, just as one
pleases, bull among the Bharatas. A man who has not mastered his senses cannot
enjoy a kingdom for long. It is the wise man who has conquered himself who
protects a kingdom. Desire and anger drag a man away from his goals. When a king
has beaten those two enemies, he conquers the earth. Lordship over the world is
a great thing. The place called a kingdom, which men so want, cannot be guarded
by the wicked.

"A man who wants great things should restrain his senses for the sake of gain
and dharma. When the senses are restrained, the understanding grows, as a fire
grows with fuel. For if they are not controlled, they can even kill, as horses
that are untamed and not controlled can kill a bad charioteer on the road. The
man who has not conquered himself and wants to conquer his ministers has not
conquered himself or his ministers, and he fails, helpless. The man who first
conquers himself, as if he were a country, does not try in vain to conquer his
ministers and his enemies. Fortune greatly favours the man who controls his
senses, controls his ministers, holds the rod over wrongdoers, acts after due
thought, and is steady.

"Desire and anger lie in the body like two fish caught in a fine-meshed net, and
they tear away a man's wisdom. With these two the gods shut the door of heaven
on those who were going there. Afraid of the man who is free of passion, they
made desire and anger grow. The king who knows how to conquer desire, anger,
greed, deceit and pride thoroughly is born to the earth. A king should always
work at restraining his senses if he wants gain and dharma, and the defeat of
his enemies. A man who is overcome by desire, or behaves falsely out of anger,
towards his own people or others, will have no allies.

"If you join with the Pandavas, who are all of one mind, very wise and brave,
and killers of enemies, you will enjoy the earth happily, my son. What Bhishma
son of Shantanu and the great chariot fighter Drona said is true, my son.
Krishna and the Pandava cannot be beaten. Go to strong-armed Krishna, whose
deeds never fail, for refuge. If Keshava is pleased, he will bring happiness to
both sides. A man who does not follow the counsel of wise and learned friends
who want his good gladdens his enemies. There is no good in war, my son, nor
dharma nor gain, let alone happiness. And victory is not certain. Do not set
your mind on war.

"Bhishma, your father and Bahlika gave the sons of Pandu their share, very wise
one, because they feared a split, tamer of enemies. And today you can see the
fruit of that gift, in that you enjoy the whole earth, its thorns cleared away
by heroes. Give the sons of Pandu what is fair, tamer of enemies, if you want to
enjoy half the earth with your ministers. Half the earth is enough for you and
your ministers to live on. If you do what your friends say, you will win fame,
Bharata.

"A quarrel with the Pandavas, who are fortunate and self-possessed, wise and
masters of their senses, would throw you out of great happiness, my son. Hold
back your friends' anger. Rule the kingdom as is right, and give the sons of
Pandu their own share, bull among the Bharatas. This wrong, done for thirteen
years, is enough. Calm it, very wise one, for desire and anger have fed it.

"The man who wants the sons of Pritha's share for you is not up to it, neither
the suta's son, whose anger is so fixed, nor your brother Duhshasana. When
Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna, Bhimasena, Dhananjaya and Dhrishtadyumna are
angry, surely none of these people will be left. Do not give way to resentment
and have the Kurus killed, my son. The whole earth has been touched by death
because of you and the Pandavas.

"And you think, fool, that Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and the others will fight with
all their strength. That will not happen now. For to those who have conquered
themselves, the kingdom, their love and their place are the same among the
Pandavas and among you, but dharma weighs more on that side. If they give up
their lives for fear of the king's bread, they will not be able to look King
Yudhishthira in the face. Men are not seen to gain wealth through greed in this
world. So enough of greed, my son. Make peace, bull among the Bharatas."

**Divergences.**

* **Binding Duryodhana.** Krishna's proposal that the elders bind Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni and Duhshasana and hand them to the Pandavas is CE (5.126.47–49). So is his example of Kamsa, whom he killed for the good of the Yadavas (5.126.36–39).
* Rejected lines not narrated: \*478–\*482, which add to Krishna's reproaches, to Brahma's words and to Gandhari's.

**Choices.**

* 5.126.1 *prahasya … krodhaparyākulekṣaṇaḥ*: "laughed, his eyes rolling with anger".
* 5.126.15 *sarpabandhaiḥ*: "with the binding of snakes", the attempt on Bhima, who was bound and bitten by snakes (1.119.33–38).
* 5.126.23: Vaikartana is Karna.
* 5.126.36 *bhojarājasya*: "the king of the Bhojas", Ugrasena. At 5.126.38 the text calls him Ahuka and Ugrasena in the same verse.
* 5.126.48: the verse "For the family, give up one man…" is Krishna's, as the text gives it.
* 5.127.11 *tatprajñām anuvartase*: "you follow his understanding".
* 5.127.28 *deśarūpeṇa*: "as if he were a country".
* 5.127.30 *kṣudrākṣeṇeva jālena*: "a fine-meshed net".
* 5.127.31 is obscure. It is read as the gods shutting heaven's door with desire and anger, afraid of the man free of passion, *anuparāgasya*.
* 5.127.32 *mahīm abhijāyate*: "is born to the earth", fit to rule it.
* 5.127.40 *bhedād bhītaiḥ*: "because they feared a split".
* 5.127.46 *alam ahnā nikāro 'yaṃ trayodaśa samāḥ kṛtaḥ*: "This wrong, done for thirteen years, is enough." *ahnā* is not rendered.
* 5.127.52 *rājapiṇḍabhayāt*: "for fear of the king's bread", that is, out of a sense of duty to the king who feeds them.

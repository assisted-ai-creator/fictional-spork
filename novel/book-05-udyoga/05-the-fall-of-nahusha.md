# The Fall of Nahusha

"When Shachi had said this," Shalya went on, "the lord spoke to her again. 'This
is no time for a show of strength. Nahusha is the stronger. The seers have made
him grow with offerings to the gods and offerings to the Ancestors, lovely one.
I will make a plan here, goddess, and you must carry it out. Do it in secret,
and tell no one, fair one. Go to Nahusha alone, slender one, and say to him,
"Lord of the world, come to me in a heavenly carriage drawn by seers. Then I
will be glad to be yours." Tell him that.'

"When the king of the gods said this, his lotus-eyed wife said, 'So be it,' and
went to Nahusha.

"Nahusha was surprised when he saw her. 'Welcome, lovely lady,' he said. 'What
shall I do for you, bright-smiling one? Love me, fair one. I am devoted to you.
What do you want, proud lady? Whatever you want done, fair one, I will do it. Do
not be shy, lovely one. Trust me. I swear by the truth, goddess, that I will do
as you say.'

"'I am waiting for the time you set me, lord of the world,' said Indrani. 'Then
you will be my husband, lord of the gods. But hear the wish that is in my heart,
king of the gods. I will tell it to you. If you will do this kindness for me,
king, and speak to me with love, then I will be yours.

"'Indra has horses, elephants and chariots to carry him. I want you to have a
carriage here that no one has had before, lord of the gods, not Vishnu, not
Rudra, not the asuras, not the rakshasas. Let the seers, all together, carry you
in a litter, great king. That is what I would like. You should not be like the
asuras or the gods. Take the strength of all of them with your own power, by
looking at them. Let no strong one dare to stand before you.'

"When she said this, they say Nahusha was delighted, and the king of the gods
said to the blameless one, 'It is a new kind of carriage you have named, fair
one. I like it very much, goddess. I am in your power, lovely one. No one of
little power makes seers his horses. I am an ascetic and strong, lord of what
was, what will be and what is. If I were angry, the world would not be.
Everything rests on me. Gods, danavas, gandharvas, kinnaras, snakes and
rakshasas, all the worlds together, are not a match for me when I am angry,
bright-smiling one. Whoever I look at with my eyes, I take his strength. So I
will certainly do as you say, goddess. The seven seers and all the brahmin seers
will carry me. See our greatness and our power, lovely one.'

"Having said this he sent the lovely goddess away. He yoked the seers, who were
keeping their vows, to his carriage. Irreverent to brahmins, full of strength,
drunk with pride in his boon, a man of desire, the wicked one made the seers
carry him.

---

"When Nahusha had sent her away, Shachi said to Brihaspati, 'Little is left of
the time Nahusha gave me. Find Shakra quickly. Have pity on me, for I am devoted
to you.'

"'Very well,' said the lord Brihaspati. 'You need not fear wicked Nahusha,
goddess. He will not last long. This lowest of men is already gone. He does not
know dharma, and by making the great seers his horses he is already struck down,
fair one. I will perform a rite to destroy this fool, and I will find Shakra. Do
not be afraid. Bless you.'

"Then mighty Brihaspati lit the fire as the rules require and poured in the
finest offering, to find the king of the gods. From the fire the lord Agni
himself came out, took on the wonderful dress of a woman, and at once vanished.
Swift as thought, he searched the directions and the spaces between, the
mountains and the forests, the earth and the sky. In the blink of an eye he came
back to Brihaspati.

"'Brihaspati,' said Agni, 'I cannot see the king of the gods anywhere. Only the
waters are left, and I dare not enter the waters. I cannot go there, brahmin.
What else can I do for you?'

"'Enter the waters, glorious one,' the teacher of the gods told him.

"'I cannot enter the waters,' said Agni. 'That would be my end. I come to you
for refuge, glorious one. Bless you. Fire rose from the waters, the kshatriya
from the brahmin, and iron from stone. Their power reaches everywhere, but it is
quenched in the place it came from.'

---

"'Agni,' said Brihaspati, 'you are the mouth of all the gods. You carry the
offerings. You move hidden inside all beings, like a witness. The poets call you
one, and they call you threefold. If you left it, eater of offerings, this world
would perish at once. Brahmins bow to you and go, with their wives and sons, to
the eternal place their deeds have won. You are the bearer of the offering,
Agni, and you are the finest offering. They worship you alone with long rites
and sacrifices, in the highest ceremony.

"'Having made these three worlds, bearer of offerings, when the time comes you
blaze up and cook them again. You are the source of this whole world, Agni, and
you are again its resting place. They call you the rain cloud and the lightning,
Agni. Flames come out of you and burn all beings. All the waters are held in
you, and this whole world is in you. There is nothing in the three worlds you do
not know, purifier. Everyone may go home to where he came from. Enter the waters
without fear. I will make you grow strong with the eternal hymns of Brahman.'

"Praised like this, the bearer of offerings, the lord, the best of poets, was
pleased, and said to Brihaspati, 'I will show you Shakra. I tell you this
truly.'

"Then Agni entered the waters, the seas and the ponds, and came to that lake
where Shatakratu was hidden. As he searched among the lotuses there, bull of the
Bharatas, he saw the king of the gods inside a lotus stalk. He came back quickly
and told Brihaspati that the lord was clinging to a lotus fibre, in a body as
small as an atom.

"Brihaspati went with the divine seers and the gandharvas, and praised the god,
the slayer of Bala, with his past deeds. 'Shakra, you killed the great asura,
terrible Namuchi, and Shambara and Bala, both of terrible courage. Grow,
Shatakratu. Kill all your enemies. Rise, wielder of the thunderbolt, and see the
divine seers who have come. Great Indra, you killed the danavas and saved the
worlds, lord. You took the foam of the waters, filled with Vishnu's power, and
killed Vritra, king of the gods, lord of the world. You are to be chosen and
praised among all beings. There is no being here equal to you. You uphold all
beings, Shakra. You made the greatness of the gods. Protect the gods and the
worlds, great Indra. Take back your strength.'

"As he was praised like this, he grew little by little. He took on his own body
and was strong again. Then the god said to his teacher Brihaspati, who stood by
him, 'What is left for you to do? Tvashtri's son, the great asura, has been
killed, and so has Vritra, whose body was huge and who wanted to swallow the
worlds.'

"'Nahusha, a human king,' said Brihaspati, 'has won the kingdom of the gods by
the power of the divine seers and the gods, and he torments us all terribly.'

"'How did Nahusha win the kingdom of the gods, so hard to win?' said Indra.
'What austerity had he, or what power, Brihaspati?'

"'The gods were afraid, and wanted a Shakra,' said Brihaspati. 'You had left the
great seat of Indra. Then the gods, the Ancestors, the seers and the hosts of
gandharvas all met together and went to Nahusha, Shakra, and said, "Be our king,
the guardian of the world." Nahusha said to them, "I am not able. Fill me with
your austerity and your power." When he said this the gods made him grow, and
Nahusha became king, terrible in his strength. Now that he has the kingdom of
the three worlds, the wicked one has made the ascetics his horses, and goes
about the worlds. His look is poison, and it takes away strength. He is very
terrible. You must never look at Nahusha. All the gods are afraid of him, and go
about in hiding so as not to see him.'

---

"While Brihaspati, best of the Angirases, was saying this, Kubera, guardian of
the world, came there, and Yama, Vivasvat's son, the ancient one, and the god
Soma, and Varuna. They met great Indra and said, 'How lucky that Tvashtri's son
has been killed, and Vritra too. How lucky that we see you well and unhurt, and
your enemy killed, Shakra.'

"Shakra answered them as was fitting. Then, to turn them against Nahusha, he
said, 'Nahusha, the king of the gods, is terrible. Give me your help in this.'

"'Nahusha is terrible,' they said, 'and his look is poison. We are afraid of
him, god. If you defeat Nahusha, king, then we should have a share, Shakra.'

"'So be it,' said Indra. 'You shall be lord of the waters, and Yama and Kubera
shall have the great consecration, today, with me. Let us defeat Nahusha, whose
look is terrible.'

"Then Agni too said to Shakra, 'Give me a share, and I will help you.'

"'You shall have one too, Agni,' Shakra told him. 'There will be a single share
for Indra and Agni together in the great sacrifice.'

"When he had thought it over, the lord, great Indra, the chastiser of Paka, the
giver of boons, honoured them and gave them their lordships: to Kubera, lordship
over all the yakshas and over wealth; to Vaivasvata, over the Ancestors; and to
Varuna, over the waters.

---

"While the wise king of the gods was thinking with those guardians of the world
of a way to kill Nahusha, the lord Agastya, the ascetic, appeared there. He
honoured the king of the gods and said, 'How lucky that you prosper, with
Vishvarupa destroyed and Vritra the asura killed. How lucky that Nahusha has
fallen from the kingdom of the gods, destroyer of cities. How lucky that I see
you with your enemy killed, slayer of Bala.'

"'Welcome, great seer,' said Indra. 'I am glad to see you. Take from me water
for your feet and to sip, a cow and the guest water.'

"When the best of sages had been honoured and was seated, the lord of the gods,
full of joy, asked the bull among brahmins, 'Lord, best of the twice-born, I
want to hear you tell how Nahusha, set on evil, fell from heaven.'

"'Listen, Shakra, to good news,' said Agastya, 'how wicked King Nahusha, evil in
his conduct and proud of his strength, fell from heaven. The fortunate divine
seers and the spotless brahmin seers who carried the evildoer, tired from the
labour, asked him a question they were unsure of, best of victors. "Vasava,
these hymns that Brahma spoke for the sprinkling of cows, are they to be taken
as authority, or not?" Nahusha, his mind dark with ignorance, said, "No."

"'"You have turned to what is against dharma," said the seers, "and you do not
follow dharma. For us they are authority, as the great seers taught long ago."

"'As he argued with the sages, Vasava, he touched me on the head with his foot,
tormented by his lawlessness. At that he lost his strength and his glory,
husband of Shachi. Then I spoke to him, as he stood shaken and stricken with
fear. "You call false the Veda, which the ancients made and the brahmin seers
practised, though it is without fault, and you touched my head with your foot.
And you fool, you have made the seers, who are like Brahma and hard to approach,
into your horses, and made them carry you. So your light is gone. Fall from
heaven, wicked one, fall to the earth, your merit spent. For ten thousand years
you will wander the earth in the form of a great snake. When they are over, you
will reach heaven again."

"'So the wicked one fell from the kingdom of the gods, destroyer of enemies. How
lucky we are, Shakra, that the thorn in the side of the brahmins is struck down.
Enter heaven, husband of Shachi, and protect the worlds, master of your senses,
conqueror of your enemies, praised by the great seers.'

"Then the gods were delighted, with the hosts of great seers around them, and so
were the Ancestors, the yakshas, the snakes and the rakshasas, the gandharvas,
the daughters of the gods, and all the hosts of apsarases, and the lakes, the
rivers, the mountains and the seas, lord of the people. They all came and said,
'How lucky that you prosper, slayer of enemies. How lucky that wise Agastya has
struck down wicked Nahusha. How lucky that the evildoer has been made a snake on
the earth.'

---

"Then Shakra, praised by the hosts of gandharvas and apsarases, mounted
Airavata, the king of elephants, marked with every good sign. With mighty Agni
and the great seer Brihaspati, with Yama and Varuna and Kubera, lord of wealth,
with all the gods around him and the gandharvas and apsarases, Shakra, the
slayer of Vritra, the lord, went to the three worlds. There Shatakratu, the king
of the gods, met Indrani, and with the greatest joy he ruled as king of the
gods.

"Then the lord Angiras appeared there and honoured the king of the gods with
hymns of the Atharva Veda. Lord Indra was delighted, and gave a boon to him,
Atharvangiras. 'In this Veda you will be named Atharvangiras. This is the proof
of it, and you will have a share in the sacrifice.' When he had honoured
Atharvangiras like this, Shatakratu, king of the gods, sent him on his way,
great king. And when he had honoured all the thirty gods and the seers rich in
austerity, Indra, full of joy, ruled his people by dharma, king.

"So Indra and his wife met with sorrow, and he lived in hiding, wanting to kill
his enemies. Do not be angry, king of kings, that you have suffered in the great
forest with Draupadi and with your great brothers. You too will win your
kingdom, Bharata, as Shakra won his when he had killed Vritra. Wicked Nahusha,
who hated brahmins and was evil in his conduct, was struck down by Agastya's
curse and destroyed for endless years. So too your wicked enemies, Karna,
Duryodhana and the rest, will quickly be destroyed, slayer of enemies. Then,
hero, you will enjoy this earth to the edge of the ocean, with your brothers and
with Draupadi.

"This story of Shakra's victory is equal to the Veda. A king who wants victory
should hear it when the armies are drawn up. That is why I make you hear it,
best of victors. Great men grow when they are praised, Yudhishthira. This is the
end of the kshatriyas, of great ones, Yudhishthira, through Duryodhana's offence
and the strength of Bhima and Arjuna. Whoever reads this story of Indra's
victory with a mind in control is cleansed of sin, wins heaven, and rejoices
here and hereafter. He has no fear of enemies, and he will not be without a son.
He will meet with no disaster, and he will live a long life. He wins victory
everywhere, and never defeat."

---

So the king was comforted by Shalya, bull of the Bharatas, and the best of those
who uphold dharma honoured Shalya as the rites require. When he had heard
Shalya's words, strong-armed Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, said to the king of the
Madras, "You will certainly drive Karna's chariot. Then you must break Karna's
spirit by praising us."

"I will do it as you say," said Shalya. "And whatever else I can do, I will do
for you."

Then Shalya, lord of the Madras, took his leave of the sons of Kunti, and the
glorious destroyer of enemies went with his army to Duryodhana.

**Divergences.**

* **"Sarpa! sarpa!"** Popular retellings have Nahusha urge the seers on with "Sarpa! sarpa!" ("Move! move!"), and Agastya turn the word into the curse. The CE has no such words, in the text or its rejected lines, and neither has Ganguli. In the CE Nahusha denies the authority of the Vedic hymns and touches Agastya's head with his foot (5.17.9–11). See ledger L-173. When Nahusha tells his own story in Book 3, Agastya's curse is "Fall, snake!" (3.178.37).
* **Freed by Yudhishthira.** Rejected lines (\*114, \*115) have Agastya add that Nahusha will be freed on seeing Yudhishthira, of his own line. The CE curse ends with his return to heaven after ten thousand years (5.17.15). The freeing is told in Book 3 (3.176–178).
* **Nahusha's boasts.** Rejected lines (\*105, \*106) add to Nahusha's boasting, and \*107 has him dismiss Airavata, the swan chariot and the horse chariot. Not narrated.
* **Brihaspati's order to Agni.** A rejected line (\*108) has Brihaspati tell Agni to search for Indra. In the CE Agni rises from the offering himself (5.15.27).
* **How Nahusha became king.** A rejected passage (\*110) repeats the account of the world without a king. The CE gives Brihaspati's shorter account (5.16.23–26).
* Other rejected lines (\*109, \*111–\*113, \*116, \*117) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* Shalya's narration stays inside double quotes, as in the two chapters before. The frame returns to plain narrative at 5.18.21, where the CE's speaker label changes back to Vaishampayana.
* 5.15.2 *havyaiḥ kavyaiś ca*: "offerings to the gods and offerings to the Ancestors".
* 5.15.14 *kila*: "they say" Nahusha was delighted.
* 5.15.27 *strīveṣam adbhutaṃ kṛtvā*: Agni takes "the wonderful dress of a woman". The text gives no reason.
* 5.15.32: Agni's verse, fire from water, the kshatriya from the brahmin, iron from stone, is kept in full.
* 5.16.6 *dahanti sarvabhūtāni tvatto niṣkramya hāyanāḥ*: "flames come out of you and burn all beings". Ganguli's vulgate reading has them "support" all creatures.
* 5.16.8 *svayoniṃ bhajate sarvo*: "everyone may go home to where he came from", Brihaspati's answer to Agni's fear of the waters.
* 5.16.27: *vaivasvataś caiva yamaḥ* is "Yama, Vivasvat's son".
* 5.16.32 *aindrāgno vai bhāga eko mahākratau*: "a single share for Indra and Agni together in the great sacrifice".
* 5.17.9: *mantrā vai prokṣaṇe gavām*, "the hymns for the sprinkling of cows", is the question the seers put to Nahusha.
* 5.18.7: Angiras is given the name Atharvangiras in "this Veda" (the Atharva Veda) and a share of the sacrifice.
* 5.18.18: "This is the end of the kshatriyas" is Shalya's own foretelling, kept as he says it.
* 5.18.19–20: the blessing for hearing the story is kept.

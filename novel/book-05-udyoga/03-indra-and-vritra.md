# Indra and Vritra

"How did great Indra and his wife meet with such terrible sorrow, king of
kings?" asked Yudhishthira. "I want to know."

"Listen, king," said Shalya, "to this ancient story of what happened long ago,
of how Indra and his wife met with sorrow.

"Tvashtri was a Prajapati, a lord of creatures, first among the gods and great
in austerity. They say that he made a son with three heads, Trishiras, out of
hatred for Indra. This Vishvarupa shone brightly, and he wanted Indra's place.
He had three terrible faces, like the sun, the moon and fire. With one mouth he
recited the Vedas. With one he drank sura, the strong liquor. With the third he
looked at every quarter of the sky, as if he would drink it in. He was an
ascetic, gentle and self-controlled, intent on dharma and on austerity, and he
practised fierce austerities, very great and very hard to do.

"When Shakra saw the power of his austerity and the strength of that boundless
one, he was downcast. 'He must not become Indra,' he thought. 'How can he be
made to cling to pleasures and give up his great austerities? If Trishiras goes
on growing, he will swallow all the three worlds.'

"The wise god thought about it in many ways. Then he commanded the apsarases to
tempt Tvashtri's son. 'Go quickly, and see to it that Trishiras clings hard to
the pleasures of desire. Tempt him without delay. Go in your loveliest dress,
fair ones, with all your charming ways, and tempt him. Bless you, calm my fear.
I feel ill at ease in myself, lovely women. Put an end to this terrible fear
quickly.'

"'We will try to tempt him, Shakra,' said the apsarases, 'so that you need not
fear him, slayer of Bala. He sits there, a treasure of austerity, as though he
would burn things up with his eyes. We will go together to tempt him, god, and
we will try to bring him under our power and take away your fear.'

"With Indra's leave they went to Trishiras. There the lovely women tried to
tempt him with all their many ways. They danced for him, and showed him the
grace of their bodies, and moved about him. But the great ascetic felt no
delight. He held his senses in check, and was like the full ocean.

"When they had tried their utmost, they came back to Shakra. They joined their
palms and said to the king of the gods, 'Lord, he is too strong for us. We
cannot shake him from his firmness. Fortunate one, do whatever you must do
next.'

---

"The wise Shakra honoured the apsarases and sent them away. Then he thought of a
way to kill that great being. The mighty hero, the king of the gods, thought in
silence, and his wise mind was made up to kill Trishiras. 'I will throw the
thunderbolt at him today, and he will be gone at once. An enemy who is growing
must not be ignored, even by the stronger, even if he is weak.'

"He settled it by the rules of the science of kings and made his resolve firm.
Then, in anger, Shakra hurled at Trishiras the thunderbolt, terrible in form,
like fire, full of dread. Struck hard by it, Trishiras fell dead, like a
mountain peak thrown to the ground.

"But when the king of the gods saw him lying there like a mountain, struck by
the thunderbolt, he found no peace. The dead one's splendour still burned him.
Though he was dead, his blazing splendour made him seem alive.

"Close by, the husband of Shachi saw a carpenter at his work. The chastiser of
Paka spoke to him quickly. 'Cut off his heads, quickly. Do as I say.'

"'His shoulders are very broad,' said the carpenter. 'This axe will not go
through them. And I could not do a thing that good men condemn.'

"'Do not be afraid,' said Indra. 'Do what I say quickly. By my grace your tool
will be like the thunderbolt.'

"'Who shall I think you are, who does such a terrible deed today?' said the
carpenter. 'I want to hear it. Tell me the truth.'

"'I am Indra, king of the gods,' said Indra. 'Know it, carpenter. Do as I have
told you. Do not think about it, carpenter.'

"'How are you not ashamed of this cruel deed, Shakra?' said the carpenter. 'You
have killed a seer's son. Are you not afraid of the sin of killing a brahmin?'

"'Later I will do some hard act of dharma to make me clean,' said Indra. 'This
was a mighty enemy, and I have killed him with the thunderbolt. Even now I am
troubled, carpenter, and afraid of him. Cut off his heads quickly, and I will
show you favour. Men will give you the head of the victim as your share at
sacrifices. That is my favour to you, carpenter. Do what I want, quickly.'

"When he heard the great Indra say this, the carpenter cut off Trishiras' heads
with his axe. As they were cut off, birds flew out of Trishiras, francolins and
partridges and sparrows, on every side. Out of the mouth with which he recited
the Vedas and drank the soma, francolins flew out quickly. Out of the mouth with
which he looked at every quarter as if he would drink it in, partridges flew
out, Pandava. And out of Trishiras' mouth that drank sura, sparrows flew out,
bull of the Bharatas.

"When the heads were cut off, Maghavan's fever left him. He went back to heaven
glad, and the carpenter went home.

---

"When Tvashtri, the Prajapati, heard that Shakra had killed his son, his eyes
went red with rage, and he said this. 'He has hurt my son, who did no wrong, who
always practised austerity, who was patient and self-controlled and master of
his senses. So I will make Vritra, to kill Shakra. Let the worlds see my power
and the great strength of my austerity. And let the king of the gods see it too,
that wicked one, whose mind is evil.'

"The angry ascetic, whose fame was great, touched water, poured an offering into
the fire, and made terrible Vritra. He said to him, 'Enemy of Indra, grow by the
power of my austerity.'

"Vritra grew, until he held up the sky, like the sun, like fire. He rose like
the sun at the end of time and said, 'What shall I do?' When he was told, 'Kill
Shakra,' he went up to heaven. Then there was a battle between Vritra and
Vasava, the two of them furious, very terrible and long.

"Then heroic Vritra seized the king of the gods, Shatakratu. He opened his
mouth, and in his rage he swallowed him. When Shakra had been swallowed by
Vritra, the thirty gods were thrown into confusion. Then those mighty beings
made the yawn, which destroys Vritra. As Vritra yawned, the slayer of Bala drew
his limbs together and came out of his open mouth. Since that time the yawn has
lived in living things.

"All the gods rejoiced when they saw Shakra come out. Then the battle between
Vritra and Vasava began again, the two of them furious. It was terrible, and it
went on for a very long time, bull of the Bharatas. When Vritra grew stronger in
the fight, by the power of Tvashtri's austerity, Shakra, who understood, turned
back.

"When he turned back, the gods fell into deep despair. Dazed by Tvashtri's
power, they met with Shakra, and all of them took counsel with the sages. 'What
must we do?' they thought, confused with fear, king. Then they all went in their
minds to Vishnu, the great one who does not decay, and sat down on the peak of
Mandara, all wanting Vritra's death.

---

"'Gods,' said Indra, 'Vritra has filled this whole unfading world. There is
nothing like him that could stand against him. Once I was able. Now I am not.
Bless you, what am I to do? I think no one can overcome him. He is full of
power, he is great, and in battle his courage has no limit. He could swallow all
the three worlds, with gods, asuras and men. So hear what I have decided,
dwellers in heaven. Let us go to Vishnu's dwelling and meet that great one. We
will take counsel with him and find a way to kill that wicked one.'

"When Maghavan had said this, the gods and the hosts of seers went to Vishnu,
the mighty god, the refuge who gives refuge. Tormented by fear of Vritra, they
all said to Vishnu, the lord of the gods, 'Lord, you strode across the three
worlds in three strides. You brought the nectar, Vishnu, and killed the daityas
in battle. You bound Bali, the great daitya, and made Shakra lord of the gods.
You are the lord of all the worlds. By you all this is spread out. You are the
great god whom all the worlds bow to. Be the refuge of the gods, with Indra,
best of immortals. Slayer of asuras, Vritra has filled this whole world.'

"'I must do what is best for you,' said Vishnu. 'So I will tell you a way to put
an end to him. Go, with the seers and the gandharvas, to where he is, who has
taken every form. Use conciliation with him, and then you will beat him. By my
power, gods, Shakra will find his way. I will enter his thunderbolt, the best of
weapons, unseen. Go with the seers and the gandharvas, best of gods, and make
peace between Vritra and Shakra without delay.'

"At the god's word, the seers and the thirty gods went together, with Shakra at
their head. When all those mighty ones came near, the gods with Shakra saw
Vritra there, blazing with power, burning the ten directions, as though he were
swallowing the three worlds, the sun and the moon.

"Then the seers went up to Vritra and spoke to him kindly. 'Unconquerable one,
your power has filled this whole world, and still you cannot beat Vasava, whose
courage is great. You two have been fighting a very long time. All creatures
suffer, gods, asuras and men. Let there be friendship between you and Shakra for
ever, Vritra. You will be happy, and the eternal worlds of Shakra will be
yours.'

"Mighty Vritra listened to the seers' words. Then the asura bowed his head and
said to them all, 'You are all fortunate, and so are the gandharvas, all of you.
I have heard all you say. Now hear me too, you who are without fault. How can
there be peace between me and Shakra, the two of us? How can there be friendship
between two powers, gods?'

"'Once, one should seek a meeting with the good,' said the seers. 'After that,
what is to be will be. A good man does not break his bond with a good man, so
one should seek to join with the good. A bond with the good is firm and lasting.
A steady man speaks what is useful when things go hard. A bond with a good man
is of great value, so a steady man does not seek to kill a good one. Indra is
honoured by the good, and he is the home of great souls. He speaks the truth, he
is not mean, he knows dharma and his mind is firm. Let there be lasting peace
between you and Shakra. Trust him like this, and let your mind not be
otherwise.'

"When he had heard the great seers' words, the glorious one said to them,
'Certainly the blessed ascetics must be honoured by me. Let what I say, gods,
all be done here, and then I will do all that the best of the twice-born have
said to me. Let me not be killable by Shakra or by the gods, by anything dry or
wet, by stone or by wood, by weapon or by thunderbolt, by day or by night, best
of brahmins. On these terms I would like to have peace with Shakra for ever.'

"'So be it,' the seers told him, bull of the Bharatas. When the pact was made
like this, Vritra was glad.

---

"But Shakra was always on his guard, and full of resentment. He kept thinking of
ways to kill Vritra. The slayer of Bala and Vritra was always troubled, and
always looking for an opening.

"One day he saw the great asura on the seashore, when the time of twilight had
come, at an hour both lovely and terrible. Then the lord thought of the boon the
great one had been given. 'This is the dreadful twilight. It is neither night
nor day. And Vritra, my enemy, who has taken everything from me, must be killed.
If I do not kill Vritra today, by tricking the great asura, mighty and huge as
he is, no good will come to me.'

"As Shakra thought this, remembering Vishnu, he saw foam in the sea, as big as a
mountain. 'This is not dry, and it is not wet, and it is not a weapon. I will
throw it at Vritra, and he will die in an instant.' Quickly he hurled the foam,
with the thunderbolt, at Vritra. Vishnu entered the foam and destroyed Vritra.

"When Vritra was killed, the directions cleared of darkness. A kind wind blew,
and all creatures were glad. Then the gods, with the gandharvas, the yakshas,
the rakshasas and the snakes, and the seers, praised great Indra with many
hymns. All beings bowed to him, and Vasava, who knew dharma, comforted them all.
With his enemy killed, glad at heart, he and the gods worshipped Vishnu, the
best in the three worlds.

---

"But when mighty Vritra, the terror of the gods, was dead, Shakra was overcome
by the falsehood he had told, and was deeply unhappy. He had already been
overcome by the sin of killing a brahmin, from Trishiras. He went to the end of
the worlds, out of his mind, senseless. The king of the gods could not be
recognised, overcome by his own sins. He lived hidden in the waters, writhing
like a snake.

"When the king of the gods was lost, tormented by fear of the sin of killing a
brahmin, the earth looked ruined. The trees were gone and the forests dry. The
rivers' streams were cut off and the lakes had no water. Creatures were in
turmoil because no rain fell. The gods were very frightened, and so were all the
great seers. The whole world had no king, and was overwhelmed by troubles. Then
the gods were afraid. 'Who will be our king?' they said. In heaven the divine
seers too were without the king of the gods, and none of the gods set his mind
on being king."

**Divergences.**

* **Indra's sin divided.** A long rejected passage (\*75) has the carpenter keep silent for a year, Shiva's ghosts cry out that Indra is a brahmin-killer, and Indra divide the sin among the earth, the ocean, the trees and women, giving each a boon. In the CE Indra goes back to heaven and the carpenter goes home (5.9.39). The rejected passage is not narrated here. The CE does tell of the sin being shared out among trees, rivers, mountains, the earth and women, but later, at Indra's horse sacrifice after Vritra's death (5.13.16–18), in the next chapter.
* **The heads alive.** Rejected lines (\*71, \*72) say the heads seemed alive, bring the carpenter to the forest by chance, and add that the heads were born again as the three birds. In the CE the carpenter is working nearby and the birds fly out of the mouths (5.9.25, 35–38).
* **Vritra's swallowing of the world.** A rejected passage (\*77) has Vritra swallow the elements, smell, taste and the senses. Not narrated.
* **Shiva's ghosts.** A rejected line (\*90) has Shiva's ghosts call Indra a brahmin-killer after Vritra's death. The CE says only that he was overcome by the falsehood and by the earlier killing (5.10.42).
* **Opening lines.** Rejected lines (\*78–\*80) have Shalya introduce the tale as one that brings victory and repeat the gods' council. The CE's 5.10 opens with Indra's speech.
* Other rejected lines (\*68, \*69, \*73, \*74, \*76, \*81–\*89, \*91) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.9.3 *kila*: "they say that" he made his son out of hatred for Indra. The text marks it as report.
* 5.9.4: *viśvarūpaḥ* is read as Trishiras' other name, Vishvarupa. The CE uses it as a name for him elsewhere: Indra's "destruction of Vishvarupa" (5.17.2) and "Tvashtri's son Vishvarupa, the three-headed" (12.329.18).
* 5.9.35–38: *kapiñjala*, *tittira* and *kalaviṅka* are "francolins", "partridges" and "sparrows".
* 5.9.33: the carpenter's share, "the head of the victim", is his reward.
* 5.9.48 *jṛmbhikā*: "the yawn", which "since that time has lived in living things".
* 5.10.11 *viśvarūpadhṛk*: here of Vritra, "who has taken every form".
* 5.10.23–24: the seers' verses on friendship with the good are kept in full.
* 5.10.42 *anṛtenābhibhūto*: "overcome by the falsehood he had told", that is, by breaking the pact.
* 5.10.43 *so 'ntam āśritya lokānām*: "he went to the end of the worlds".

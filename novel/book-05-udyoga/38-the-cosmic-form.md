# The Cosmic Form

Duryodhana paid no heed to his mother's words, full of sense as they were. In
his rage he went out again to the men who had not mastered themselves. When he
had left the hall, the Kaurava took counsel with King Shakuni, son of Subala,
who was skilled at dice. This is what Duryodhana, Karna, Shakuni son of Subala
and Duhshasana, the four of them, planned.

"Janardana acts quickly. Before he seizes us, with King Dhritarashtra and the
son of Shantanu, we shall seize Hrishikesha ourselves, by force, the tiger among
men, as Indra seized the son of Virochana. When the Pandavas hear that the
Varshneya has been seized, they will lose heart and lose their spirit, like
snakes with their fangs broken. This strong-armed man is the shelter and the
armour of them all. Once this giver of boons, the bull of all the Satvatas, is
seized, the Pandavas and the Somakas will give up the fight. So we shall bind
quick-acting Keshava here and now, even if Dhritarashtra cries out, and fight
our enemies."

Satyaki, who could read signs and was wise, quickly understood the wicked plan
of those wicked, evil-minded men. For this reason he went out and stood with
Hardikya. "Draw up the army quickly," he told Kritavarma. "Stand at the door of
the hall in armour, with the troops in array, while I tell Krishna, whose deeds
never fail."

The hero went into the hall like a lion into a mountain cave and told the great
Keshava of the plan. Then he told Dhritarashtra and Vidura too, all but smiling.
"These fools want to do a deed against dharma and against gain, which the good
condemn, and which they can never carry out. The wicked fools have gathered, and
they are about to act, driven by desire and fury, following anger and greed.
These dullards want to seize the lotus-eyed one, like children or idiots trying
to catch a blazing fire in a cloth."

When far-sighted Vidura heard what Satyaki said, he spoke to strong-armed
Dhritarashtra in the Kuru assembly.

"King, all your sons are doomed, scorcher of enemies. They are ready to do a
deed without honour and beyond their power. They want to band together, it
seems, overpower the lotus-eyed one, Vasava's younger brother, and seize him by
force. When they come against this tiger among men, whom no one can touch or
approach, they will be gone, like moths in a fire. If Janardana wanted, he could
send all of them to Yama's house as they struggled, as an angry lion sends deer.
But Krishna would never do a deed that is condemned. Achyuta, the best of men,
would never leave dharma."

When Vidura had said this, Keshava looked at Dhritarashtra and spoke, while the
friends listened among themselves.

"King, if these men are angry and want to seize me by force, give me leave, lord
of the earth, to let them seize me, or me them. For I could hold all of these
angry men in check. But I would never do a condemned or wicked deed. Your sons,
in their greed for what belongs to the Pandavas, will lose what is their own. If
this is what they want, Yudhishthira's work is done. This very day I could seize
these men and all who follow them, Bharata, and give them to the sons of Pritha.
What wrong would there be in that? But I shall not start such a condemned deed,
Bharata, in your presence, great king, born of anger and a wicked mind. Let it
be as Duryodhana wants, king. I give leave to every agreement, Bharata."

When Dhritarashtra heard this, he said to Vidura, "Bring that wicked Suyodhana
here quickly, greedy for the kingdom, with his friends, his ministers, his
brothers and his followers. Perhaps I can bring him back onto the road."

So the steward brought Duryodhana back into the hall, unwilling, with his
brothers, and surrounded by kings. Then King Dhritarashtra spoke to Duryodhana,
surrounded by Karna, Duhshasana and the kings.

"Cruel man, full of evil, with your mean allies, you have banded with evil
companions and want to do an evil deed. It is beyond your power, and it brings
dishonour, and good men condemn it. It is what a fool like you would decide, a
disgrace to your family. You want to band with evil companions, it seems, and
seize the lotus-eyed one, whom no one can touch or approach. Fool, you want the
one whom even the gods with Vasava cannot take by force, as a child wants the
moon. You do not understand Keshava, whom gods, men, gandharvas, asuras and
snakes cannot withstand in battle. The wind is hard to catch in the hand, the
moon is hard to touch with the hand, the earth is hard to hold up on the head,
and Keshava is hard to take by force."

When Dhritarashtra had said this, Vidura the steward also spoke, looking at
resentful Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra.

"At the gate of Saubha the monkey king named Dvivida covered Keshava with a
great shower of stones. He wanted to seize Madhava, and he attacked with all his
might, and he could not seize him. And you want to take him by force. At
Nirmochana six thousand great asuras bound him with nooses, and they could not
hold him. And you want to take him by force. When Shauri went to Pragjyotisha,
Naraka and the danavas could not seize him there. And you want to take him by
force.

"As a child he killed Putana, and he held up Govardhana to protect the cows,
bull among the Bharatas. He killed Arishta and Dhenuka, and mighty Chanura, and
the king of horses, and Kamsa, who was doing evil. Jarasandha, Vakra and mighty
Shishupala, and Bana, were killed in battle, and kings were slain. His power has
no measure, and he conquered King Varuna and the fire. When he carried off the
parijata tree he beat Shachi's husband himself. Lying on the single ocean he
killed Madhu and Kaitabha. And taking another birth he killed Hayagriva. He is
the doer, and no one makes him act. He is the cause in all that men do. Whatever
Shauri wants, he could do without effort. You do not understand Govinda,
Achyuta, whose courage is terrible, who is like an angry poisonous snake, a mass
of splendour, never beaten. If you attack strong-armed Krishna, whose deeds
never fail, you will be gone with your ministers, like a moth that meets a
fire."

When Vidura had said this, Keshava, killer of hosts of enemies, spoke to
Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra.

"Suyodhana, you think in your folly that I am alone. You despise me, fool, and
you want to seize me. But here are all the Pandavas, and the Andhakas and
Vrishnis. Here are the Adityas, the Rudras and the Vasus, with the great seers."

When he had said this, Keshava, killer of enemy heroes, laughed aloud. As the
great Shauri laughed, the thirty gods, the size of a thumb and shaped like
lightning, sent out flames of fire from him. Brahma was on his forehead and
Rudra on his chest. The guardians of the world were on his arms, and Agni came
out of his mouth. The Adityas, the Sadhyas, the Vasus and the Ashvins, the
Maruts with Indra, and the Vishvedevas all appeared, and so did the forms of
yakshas, gandharvas and rakshasas.

From his arms came Samkarshana and Dhananjaya, Arjuna the archer on the right
and Rama with his plough on the left. Bhima, Yudhishthira and the two sons of
Madri were behind him. The Andhakas and Vrishnis, led by Pradyumna, were in
front of Krishna, with great weapons raised. The conch, the discus, the mace,
the spear, the Sharnga bow, the plough and the Nandaka sword, and every weapon,
were seen raised and blazing in Krishna's many hands. From his eyes, his nose
and his ears, on every side, came terrible flames of fire with smoke, and from
the pores of his skin came rays like the rays of the sun.

When the kings saw Keshava's terrible form, their hearts were afraid and they
shut their eyes. All but Drona and Bhishma, wise Vidura, fortunate Sanjaya and
the seers rich in austerity: to these the blessed Janardana gave divine sight.
When they saw that great wonder of Madhava's in the hall, the drums of the gods
sounded and a rain of flowers fell. The whole earth shook, and the ocean was
stirred up. The kings were amazed beyond measure, bull among the Bharatas.

Then the tiger among men, the tamer of enemies, drew back his form, divine,
wonderful, many-coloured and splendid. He took Satyaki and Hardikya by the hand,
and with the leave of the seers the slayer of Madhu went out. The seers, Narada
and the others, vanished and went away. When the uproar was over, it seemed like
a wonder.

When the Kauravas and the kings saw the tiger among men leaving, they followed
him, as the gods follow Shatakratu. Shauri, whose soul has no measure, paid no
heed to the whole circle of kings, and went out like a fire with smoke. Then
Daruka was seen with the great white chariot, hung with bells and bright with
golden netting, light and rumbling like a cloud, well fitted, covered in tiger
skin and with a guard rail, yoked with Sainya and Sugriva. Kritavarma, the great
chariot fighter, the hero honoured among the Vrishnis, Hardikya, was seen on his
chariot too.

As Shauri, tamer of enemies, stood by his chariot ready to leave, great King
Dhritarashtra spoke to him again. "You see what power I have over my sons,
Janardana. It is before your eyes. Nothing is hidden from you, destroyer of
enemies. Knowing that I want peace for the Kurus and am trying for it, and
knowing my state, Keshava, you should not suspect me too much. I have no evil
purpose against the Pandavas, Keshava. You know what I said to Suyodhana. All
the Kurus and all the kings know that I try for peace with all my might,
Madhava."

Then the strong-armed one spoke to King Dhritarashtra, to Drona, to Bhishma the
grandfather, to the steward, to Bahlika and to Kripa. "You have seen for
yourselves what happened in the Kuru assembly, and how the fool rose in anger
again and again, like a man with no breeding. And Dhritarashtra, lord of the
earth, says he is not his own master. I take my leave of you all. I shall go to
Yudhishthira."

When he had said goodbye and set out, standing in his chariot, the bull among
men, the heroes, the great archers, the bulls among the Bharatas, followed him:
Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, the steward, Dhritarashtra and Bahlika, Ashvatthama,
Vikarna and the great chariot fighter Yuyutsu. Then, while the Kurus watched, he
drove off in his great white chariot hung with bells to see Pritha, his father's
sister.

**Divergences.**

* **Who saw the form.** Krishna gives divine sight to Drona, Bhishma, Vidura, Sanjaya and the seers (5.129.13). Dhritarashtra is not among them. Rejected passages (\*495 and App. I No. 8) give Dhritarashtra sight to see the form, and \*496 has him ask to be blind again, since he has seen Krishna and wants to see no one else. None of this is narrated. See ledger L-30.
* **No binding of Krishna.** A rejected passage (\*490) has Duhshasana and the others throw nets over Duryodhana, mistaking him for Krishna. The apparatus itself calls it an irrelevant addition found in some manuscript. In the CE the plot is spoken of, Satyaki warns the hall, and no one lays a hand on Krishna (5.128).
* **Vidura's longer list.** An appendix passage (App. I No. 7) gives Vidura a longer account of Krishna's deeds, including the burning of Varanasi. Rejected lines add the sixteen thousand maidens after Naraka's death (\*488) and Rama's deeds as Krishna's former birth (\*489). Not narrated.
* **The form's further features.** A rejected passage (\*494) gives the form a thousand feet, a hundred arms and the worlds within its body. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*483–\*487, \*491–\*493, \*497–\*499.

# Krishna Sets Out

"Today you are the best friend of all the Kurus," said Arjuna. "You are kin to
both sides and always dear to both. You are the one who can bring peace between
the Pandavas and the sons of Dhritarashtra, Keshava, and make them well.
Lotus-eyed one, go from here to angry Suyodhana, killer of enemies, and say to
the Bharata whatever should be said for peace. If you speak words of dharma and
gain, kind and healing and good, and the fool does not accept them, he will fall
into the power of fate."

"I shall go to King Dhritarashtra," said the Lord, "wanting what is in keeping
with dharma, and good for us, and what brings the Kurus health."

The darkness passed and the sun rose clear. It was the hour of Mitra, and the
sun was still gentle. It was the month of Kaumuda, under the star Revati, at the
end of autumn, when the cold season was coming. It was a pleasant time, when the
crops stood full. Janardana, strong and best of the strong, heard the brahmins'
glad words, kind and holy and true, as Vasava hears the seers. He did the
morning rites, bathed and made himself pure, dressed in his ornaments, and paid
honour to the sun and to the fire. He touched a bull on the back and greeted the
brahmins. He walked round the fire keeping it on his right, and looked at the
lucky things set before him.

Janardana held to what the son of Pandu had said, and spoke to Satyaki, Shini's
grandson, who sat by him.

"Put my conch and my discus in the chariot," he said, "and my mace, and my
quivers and spears and all my weapons. Duryodhana is wicked, and so are Karna
and Saubala. A strong man should not despise an enemy, however common."

Keshava's servants understood what he wanted and ran to yoke the chariot of the
bearer of the discus and the mace. It blazed like the fire at the end of time,
and it went as if it travelled through the sky. It was fitted with two wheels as
bright as the moon and the sun. It was painted with half-moons and moons, with
fish, beasts and birds, with all kinds of flowers, and with gems and jewels all
over. It was large and lovely to look at, like the rising sun, its body bright
with gems and gold, with a fine banner and fine flags. It was well fitted, no
one could stand against it, and it was covered in tiger skins. It took away the
fame of enemies and it gave joy to the Yadus. They yoked to it Sainya, Sugriva,
Meghapushpa and Balahaka, bathed and fitted out with every kind of harness. To
add still more to Krishna's greatness, the loud chariot was fitted with his
banner, the king of birds. It was like the peak of Meru, and it sounded like
thunder and drums. Shauri mounted it as a man of good deeds mounts a flying
chariot of the gods.

Then the best of men took Satyaki up with him and set out, filling the earth and
the sky with the roar of the chariot. In a moment the heavy clouds were driven
from the sky. A kind wind blew behind him, and the dust settled. Lucky beasts
and birds went with Vasudeva as he set out, keeping on his right and going his
way. Cranes, peacocks and wild geese followed the slayer of Madhu on every side,
with lucky cries. The fire, fed with offerings, great oblations and prayers,
burned without smoke, its flames bending to the right.

Vasishtha, Vamadeva, Bhuridyumna, Gaya and Kratha, Shukra, Narada and Valmiki
were there, with the Maruts, Kushika and Bhrigu. With the brahmin seers and the
divine seers, they all walked round Krishna, Vasava's younger brother, who
brings joy to the Yadus, keeping him on their right. Honoured in this way by the
fortunate hosts of great seers and good men, Krishna set out for the home of the
Kurus.

As he went, Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, went after him, and Bhimasena and
Arjuna, and the two Pandavas who were Madri's sons. Brave Chekitana went, and
Dhrishtaketu of the Chedis, Drupada, the king of Kashi and the great chariot
fighter Shikhandi, Dhrishtadyumna and his son, and Virata with the Kekayas. All
these kshatriyas went to honour that bull among kshatriyas.

After going some way with Govinda, the shining Dharma King Yudhishthira spoke to
him in front of the kings. Keshava is a man who would never do wrong for desire,
or fear, or greed, or gain. His mind is steady and he is free from greed. He
knows dharma, he is firm and wise, and he knows all beings. He is the lord of
all beings, the god of gods, full of power. The son of Kunti embraced him,
endowed with every virtue and marked with the Shrivatsa, and began to give him
his messages.

"She brought us up from childhood, Janardana, that woman on her own," he said.
"She keeps her fasts and austerities and always prays for our welfare. She
delights in honouring the gods and guests and in serving her elders. She is
loving and she loves her sons, and she is dear to us. She saved us from our fear
of Suyodhana, tamer of enemies, from a great crush of death, as a boat saves men
from the sea. She has always suffered for our sake, Madhava, though she does not
deserve to suffer. Ask after her health. Comfort her well, for she is drowned in
grief for her sons. Greet her and embrace her, and speak to her of the Pandavas.

"Since her marriage she has suffered from her husband's family, tamer of
enemies. She has seen humiliations she did not deserve, and she is unhappy. Will
the time ever come, Krishna, when her sorrows are turned around, and I can give
my mother, who has suffered so, some happiness, tamer of enemies? When we went
into exile she ran after us in her misery, longing for her sons. We left her
weeping and went to the forest. Surely no one dies of sorrow, Keshava, if she is
still alive, so deeply hurt by her grief for her sons, she whom the Anartas
honoured.

"Greet her for me, Krishna, my lord. Greet Dhritarashtra of the Kurus too, and
the kings older than we are, Bhishma, Drona and Kripa, great King Bahlika,
Drona's son and Somadatta, and every one of the Bharatas. And embrace for me the
wise Vidura, keeper of the Kurus' counsel, whose mind is deep and who knows
dharma, slayer of Madhu."

When Yudhishthira had said this to Keshava there among the kings, he took his
leave, walked round Krishna keeping him on his right, and turned back.

Then Bibhatsu, still walking along, spoke to his friend, the unbeaten Dasharha,
the bull among men and killer of enemy heroes. "What we settled on before in our
counsel, my lord, about half the kingdom, Govinda, is known to all the kings. If
he gives it freely, with honour and without scorn, it would please me,
strong-armed one, and they would be freed from a great danger. But if the son of
Dhritarashtra, who knows no right way, does otherwise, I shall surely make an
end of the kshatriyas, Janardana."

When the son of Pandu said this, Vrikodara was delighted. Again and again the
son of Pandu shook with anger. Trembling, the son of Kunti gave great roars, his
heart swelling with joy at Dhananjaya's words. When they heard that roar, the
bowmen trembled, and all the animals let fall their dung and urine.

When he had said this to Keshava, and told him what he had decided, Arjuna
embraced Janardana, took his leave and turned back.

When all the kings had turned back, Janardana sped on gladly behind Sainya and
Sugriva. Vasudeva's horses, driven by Daruka, seemed to drink up the road and
swallow the sky.

Then strong-armed Keshava saw seers on the road, shining with the glory of
brahmins, standing on both sides of the way. Janardana quickly got down from his
chariot and greeted them. He honoured all the seers in the proper way and spoke
to them.

"Is all well in the worlds?" he asked. "Is dharma well kept? Do the three
classes keep to the brahmins' teaching?" When he had paid them honour, the
slayer of Madhu said, "Holy ones, where are you going, and what road are you
taking here? What do you want, holy ones? What can I do for you? Why have you
come down to earth?"

Rama, son of Jamadagni, came up to the slayer of Madhu, and embraced Govinda,
his friend of old in good deeds. "Divine seers who have done holy things, and
brahmins of great learning, royal seers and ascetics, Dasharha, all honour you,"
he said. "They saw the battles of the gods and the asuras of old, glorious one.
Now they want to see the kshatriyas of the earth gathered from every side, the
kings who sit in the hall, and you, Janardana, the truth. We are going to see
this great sight, Keshava. We want to hear the words of dharma and gain,
Madhava, that you will speak among the Kurus, in the midst of the kings,
scorcher of enemies. Bhishma, Drona and the others, and wise Vidura, will meet
in the hall, and you too, tiger of the Yadavas. We want to hear your divine
words there, Madhava, and theirs, Govinda, true and good. We have said goodbye
to you, strong-armed one, and we shall see you again. Go without hindrance,
hero. We shall see you in the hall."

Ten great chariot fighters, breakers of enemy heroes, followed strong-armed
Krishna, the son of Devaki, as he went, with weapons in their hands. A thousand
foot soldiers and horsemen went too, scorcher of enemies, with plenty of food,
and hundreds of servants besides, king.

"How did the great Dasharha, the slayer of Madhu, travel?" asked Janamejaya.
"What omens were there as the mighty one went?"

"Hear from me the wonders that came about as the great one went," said
Vaishampayana, "divine and heavenly portents.

"Thunder and lightning broke from a cloudless sky. Then, in a sky empty of
clouds, the rain god poured down heavy rain. The great rivers that run east, the
best of streams, flowed backward. All the directions were turned around and
nothing could be made out. Fires blazed up, king, and the earth shook. Wells and
water jars spilled water by the hundred. The whole world was covered in
darkness, and no one could tell the directions from the spaces between them,
king, for the dust. A great noise rose in the sky, and no body could be seen. It
happened in every land, king, like a wonder. A harsh, dreadful-sounding wind
from the south-west crushed Hastinapura, tearing up trees in crowds.

"But wherever the Varshneya went on the road, Bharata, the wind was pleasant and
everything went well. It rained flowers and lotuses in plenty. The road was
smooth and easy, and free of kusha grass and thorns. As the strong-armed giver
of wealth went along, the brahmins honoured him here and there, king, with honey
mixtures and flowers. Women came out onto the road and scattered sweet wild
flowers over the great one, who delights in the good of all beings.

"He passed through Shalibhavana, a lovely place heaped with every kind of grain,
happy and very righteous, bull among the Bharatas. He saw villages rich in
cattle, lovely and pleasing to the heart, and he passed cities and many
kingdoms. The people there were always glad and good-hearted, guarded by the
Bharatas. They were not afraid of enemy armies and they knew nothing of
misfortune. As he came from Upaplavya, the people who lived in the cities stood
together on the road, eager to see Vishvaksena. They all honoured him as he
came, like a blazing, well-fed fire, worthy of honour, a guest of their land.

"Keshava, killer of enemy heroes, came to Vrikasthala when the sun's rays were
scattered and it was turning red in a clear sky. He quickly got down from his
chariot, purified himself as the rules lay down, gave orders to unyoke the
chariot, and sat down to the evening prayers. Daruka unyoked the horses, tended
them as the books say, took off all their harness and let them go. When all this
was done, the slayer of Madhu said, 'We shall spend the night here, for
Yudhishthira's business.'

"His men understood what he wanted and made a camp. In a moment they had fine
food and drink ready. The leading brahmins of that village, king, noble,
well-born and modest men who kept to the brahmin's way of life, came to
Hrishikesha, the great tamer of enemies. They honoured him in the proper way,
with blessings and good wishes. When they had honoured the Dasharha, honoured in
all the worlds, they offered the great one their houses, full of treasures. The
lord said, 'It is done,' and honoured them as they deserved. He went to their
houses and came back with them. Keshava fed the brahmins there with fine food,
ate with them all, and spent that night happily."

**Divergences.**

* **Daruka at the mounting.** A rejected line (\*382) has the chariot guarded by Daruka and Krishna walking round it before he mounts. In the CE Daruka is named when he drives the horses (5.81.59) and unyokes them (5.82.22). \*382 is not narrated.
* **The seers' names.** A long rejected passage (\*388) names the seers on the road: Narada, Devala, Maitreya, Vyasa and many others. In the CE only Rama Jamadagnya speaks, and the others are not named (5.81.60–72). Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*381 (more ornaments for the chariot), \*383 (Krishna's bow to the gods), \*384–\*387 (more greetings and questions), and \*389–\*391 (more of the seers' praise).

**Choices.**

* 5.81.6 *maitre muhūrte*: "the hour of Mitra", an early and lucky hour.
* 5.81.7 *kaumude māsi revatyāṃ śaradante himāgame*: "the month of Kaumuda, under the star Revati, at the end of autumn, when the cold season was coming". Kaumuda is the autumn month usually called Karttika. See TIMELINE.
* 5.81.10 *paśyan kalyāṇam agrataḥ*: "looked at the lucky things set before him".
* 5.81.13: Saubala is Shakuni.
* 5.81.19: the four horses are the same four named at 4.40.18–21.
* 5.81.27 *marutaḥ*: plural, "the Maruts". Ganguli leaves them, Narada and Valmiki out of the list.
* 5.81.20 *patagendreṇa dhvajena*: "his banner, the king of birds", that is, Garuda.
* 5.81.28 *vāsavānujam*: "Vasava's younger brother", Krishna as Vishnu, Indra's younger brother.
* 5.81.34–35: the praise of Krishna as he is embraced is the narrator's.
* 5.81.42 *śvaśurāṇām*: "from her husband's family".
* 5.81.45 *ānartasatkṛtā*: "she whom the Anartas honoured". Ganguli reads it as Kunti being cared for by the Anartas, Krishna's people. The text says no more.
* 5.81.53 *anupāyavit*: "who knows no right way".
* 5.81.63 *kva saṃsiddhāḥ kā vīthī bhavatām iha*: "where are you going, and what road are you taking here?"
* 5.81.65 *purā sucarite sakhā*: "his friend of old in good deeds".
* 5.81.68 *tvāṃ ca satyam*: "you, Janardana, the truth".
* 5.82.3: Janamejaya's question is in the constituted text here, and is kept.
* 5.82.9 *khe śarīraṃ na dṛśyate*: "no body could be seen", a voice without a speaker.
* 5.82.15 *śālibhavanam*: taken as a place name, Shalibhavana, "the rice country".
* 5.82.28 *kṛtam ity uktvā*: "It is done", a polite refusal of the houses. He visits them and returns to camp.

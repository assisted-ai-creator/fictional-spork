# Amba's Vow

"Then all those ascetics, my son," said Bhishma, "seeing her set on austerity,
tried to turn her back and said, 'What is it you want to do?'

"Then the girl said to those seers, grown old in austerity, 'I have been cast
off by Bhishma and robbed of the dharma of having a husband. My consecration is
for his death, not for the worlds of heaven, you who are rich in austerity. When
I have killed Bhishma I shall find peace. That is my resolve. Because of him I
have come to this lasting life of sorrow, cut off from a husband's world,
neither woman nor man here. I will not turn back, ascetics, until I have killed
Ganga's son in battle. This is the purpose in my heart for which I have taken
this up. I am sick of being a woman, and I have resolved on manhood. I mean to
pay Bhishma back. I am not to be held back again.'

"The god who holds the trident, the lord of Uma, showed himself to that spirited
woman in his own form, in the midst of those great seers. When he offered her a
boon, she chose my defeat. 'You will kill him,' the god answered the spirited
woman.

"Then the girl spoke again to Rudra. 'How can victory in battle come to me, god,
when I am a woman? My heart is deeply quieted by being a woman, lord of Uma. And
you have promised Bhishma's defeat, lord of beings. Make it come true, god of
the bull banner, so that I may meet Bhishma, son of Shantanu, in battle and kill
him.'

"Then the great god of the bull banner said to the girl, 'My word is never
false, good lady. What I have said will come true. You will kill Bhishma in
battle, and you will win manhood. You will remember all of it when you have gone
into another body. Born in Drupada's family, you will be a great chariot
fighter, quick with weapons, a fighter of many skills, and well thought of. All
this will be just as I have said, lovely one. You will become a man after some
time has gone by.' When he had said this, the god of great splendour with the
matted hair and the bull banner vanished there and then, while the brahmins
looked on.

"Then, while those great seers looked on, the blameless girl, fair of face,
gathered wood from that forest and built a very great pyre and set fire to it.
When the fire was blazing, great king, her mind blazing with rage, she said,
'For Bhishma's death!' and entered the fire, king, the eldest daughter of Kashi,
by the river Yamuna."

"How did Shikhandi, son of Ganga," said Duryodhana, "who was a girl then, become
a man? Tell me, grandfather, best of fighters."

"The wife of that King Drupada, lord of the earth, king of kings," said Bhishma,
"his dear chief queen, had no son, lord of the people. At that very time, great
king, King Drupada was pleasing Shankara to have a child. Resolved on my death,
he took up fierce austerity. He got a daughter from the great god, though he
said, 'Let me have a son. Lord, I want a son, to pay Bhishma back.' The god of
gods told him, 'You will have a woman who is a man. Turn back, lord of the
earth. It will not be otherwise.'

"He went back to the city and said to his wife, 'Queen, I have made a great
effort for a son, with austerity. And Shambhu told me, "Your child will be a
girl and then a man." Though I asked him again and again, Shiva said, "It is
fated. It cannot be otherwise. What is to be will be so."'

"Then his spirited wife, the queen of King Drupada, kept herself pure and went
to Drupada at her season. In due time she conceived by Prishata's son, in the
way the ordinance lays down, lord of the earth, as Narada told me. Then the
lotus-eyed queen carried that child, and the strong-armed king, joy of the
Kurus, tended his dear wife with every comfort, out of love for a son. Then to
King Drupada, lord of the earth, who had no son, the queen gave birth to a girl
of the finest beauty, lord of men.

"But the famous queen of the sonless King Drupada announced, king of kings, 'A
son has been born to me.' Then King Drupada, lord of men, had all the rites for
a son performed for his hidden daughter as if for a son. The queen of Drupada
guarded the secret with every care, calling the child a son, and no one in the
city knew her, except Prishata's son. Trusting the word of the god of wonderful
splendour, he hid the girl and said she was a boy. The king had all the birth
rites done as they are laid down for a man, and they knew her as Shikhandi. I
alone knew, through a spy and through Narada's words, by the god's word and by
Amba's austerity.

"Drupada took great care over all his people. Then she became accomplished in
writing and the other arts and in the crafts, king of kings, and in archery she
became Drona's pupil. Her lovely mother urged the king, great king, to find a
wife for the girl, as if for a son. Then Prishata's son, seeing that the girl
had come to the full bloom of youth, and knowing her for a woman, fell into
anxiety with his wife.

"'This girl of mine has come to youth, and she adds to my sorrow,' said Drupada.
'And I hid her at the word of the god with the trident in his hand. That will
never be false in any way, great queen. Why should the maker of the three worlds
make it untrue?'

"'If it pleases you, king, I shall speak,' said his wife. 'Hear what I say, and
when you have heard it, son of Prishata, take up your own task. Let a wife be
taken for him in the proper way, lord of men. Then that word will come true.
That is my firm belief.'

"So husband and wife decided on this, and they chose for the girl the daughter
of the lord of the Dasharnas. Then King Drupada, lion among kings, having looked
into all the kings for family, chose the daughter of the Dasharna king as wife
for Shikhandi. The Dasharna king was known as Hiranyavarma, and that lord of the
earth gave his daughter to Shikhandi. That king among the Dasharnas was a great
lord of the earth, Hiranyavarma, hard to face, with a great army and a great
mind.

"When the wedding had taken place, best of kings, the girl had come to youth,
and so had the girl Shikhandini. Shikhandi, now married, came back to Kampilya.
For some time the bride did not know that the other girl was a woman. But when
the daughter of Hiranyavarma found out that Shikhandi was a woman, she told her
nurses and her friends in her shame: the Panchala king's daughter, Shikhandini,
was a girl. Then the Dasharna nurses, tiger among kings, were deeply distressed,
and they sent word. Then the servants told the lord of the Dasharnas everything,
the deception as it had happened, and the king was angry. Meanwhile Shikhandi
went about happily in the royal household as a man, great king, with no liking
for being a woman.

"Then after some days, bull among the Bharatas, when he had heard this,
Hiranyavarma, king of kings, was in torment with rage. The Dasharna king, filled
with fierce anger, sent an envoy to Drupada's house. The envoy of the king of
the golden armour reached Drupada, drew him aside alone and spoke in private.

"'The king of the Dasharnas, king, sends you this message, angry at the insult,
for you have deceived him, blameless one. "You despise me, lord of men. Your
counsel was bad, surely, when in your folly you asked for my daughter for your
daughter. Reap the fruit of that deception today, you fool. I shall uproot you
with your people and your ministers. Stand firm."'

"When the envoy had said this, king, Drupada could not speak, like a thief who
has been caught. He made great efforts to soothe him, through relations and
through envoys with sweet words, sending the message, 'It is not so.' But the
king made sure of the truth again, that the Panchala's child was a girl, and set
out in haste. Then, on the word of the nurses, he sent word of his daughter's
deception to his friends of boundless might. Then that best of kings gathered
his forces and set his mind on marching against Drupada, Bharata.

"Then King Hiranyavarma took counsel with his friends, king of kings, about the
Panchala king. Those great kings came to this decision: 'If this is true, king,
and Shikhandini is a girl, we will bind the Panchala king and bring him home. We
will set up another lord of men as king over the Panchalas and have King Drupada
killed, with Shikhandi.' Then the lord sent a messenger to Prishata's son again
with the command, 'I will kill you. Stand firm.'

"King Drupada, lord of the earth, was timid by nature, and he was in the wrong.
He was seized by sharp fear. He sent the Dasharna envoy away and, worn with
grief, went to his wife in private. Filled with great fear and struck to the
heart with grief, the Panchala king spoke to Shikhandi's dear mother.

"'My relation by marriage, King Hiranyavarma, who is very strong, will march
against me in anger, drawing his army along. What shall I do now about this
girl, foolish as I am? They suspect that your son Shikhandi is a girl. He has
made sure of it as the truth, and he thinks, "I have been cheated." So he means
to uproot me with his friends, his army and his followers. What is true here,
lovely one, and what is false? Tell me, fair one. When I have heard your words,
good lady, I shall act on them. For I have fallen into doubt, and this
Shikhandini is a child, and you too, queen, have come into great trouble, lovely
one. So tell the truth to me when I ask, to free us all. I shall do what must be
done, bright-smiling one. And do not be afraid for Shikhandi. I shall deal with
it truly. I was deceived, lovely one, by an act done for the dharma of a son,
and I have deceived the Dasharna king, lord of the earth. So tell me, fortunate
one, and I shall do what is good.'

"The lord of men knew the truth, but he asked her openly so that it would be
made known to others. And urged openly like this, the queen answered the lord of
the earth."

**Divergences.**

* **Amba and Shikhandi.** Shiva promises Amba that she will kill Bhishma, win manhood and be born in Drupada's family as a great chariot fighter, remembering everything (5.188.8–14). She then enters a pyre by the Yamuna (5.188.16–18). The CE says both that half of her became the river Amba and remained a girl (5.187.40), and that she entered the fire. Both are kept as told. See ledger L-188.
* **Drupada's reason.** Drupada asks Shiva for a son "to pay Bhishma back" (5.189.4–5). The text does not say what his quarrel with Bhishma was.
* **How Bhishma knows.** "I alone knew, through a spy and through Narada's words, by the god's word and by Amba's austerity" (5.189.18).
* \*584, \*585 and \*586 are not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.188.4 *naiva strī na pumān iha*: "neither woman nor man here", Amba's own words.
* 5.188.9 *strībhāvena ca me gāḍhaṃ manaḥ śāntam*: "My heart is deeply quieted by being a woman", read as her fear that a woman's heart is too mild for war.
* 5.189.5 *strīpumān*: "a woman who is a man".
* 5.189.10 *vidhidṛṣṭena hetunā*: "in the way the ordinance lays down".
* Shikhandi's gender. The text calls the child "she" and "the girl" in some verses and "he" in others (for instance 5.190.13, 17). The novel follows the text verse by verse. Shikhandini is used where the text uses the feminine name (5.190.12, 14; 5.191.7, 17).
* 5.190.17 *strītvaṃ naivātirocayan*: "with no liking for being a woman". The verb could also mean "not showing"; the sense is uncertain.
* 5.190.20 *kāñcanavarmaṇaḥ*: "the king of the golden armour", a play on Hiranyavarma's name, which means the same. At 5.192.28 he is "the king with the golden armour" too.
* 5.191.9 *kṣattāram*: "a messenger".
* 5.191.19 *kriyayāhaṃ … vañcitaḥ putradharmataḥ*: "I was deceived by an act done for the dharma of a son".

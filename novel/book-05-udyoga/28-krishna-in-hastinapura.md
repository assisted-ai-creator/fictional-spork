# Krishna in Hastinapura

"King, you are highly honoured," said Vidura, "the best man even in the three
worlds, respected and esteemed by the world, Bharata. Whatever you say in your
old age, from the teaching or from sound reasoning, you are steady, for you are
old. Your people are sure that dharma is in you, king, as lines are in stone,
light in the sun and great waves in the ocean. The world is always made glad by
the host of your virtues, lord of the earth. Always try to guard those virtues,
with your kin. Be straight. Do not, in your childishness, destroy in many ways
your kingdom, your sons, your grandsons and your dear friends.

"What you want to give your guest Krishna, king, is a great deal. The Dasharha
deserves that and more, the earth itself. But you do not want to give this to
Krishna for dharma's sake or to please him. I swear it on my own truth. This is
deceit, untruth, a trick, you who give such rich gifts. I know your hidden mind,
king, under the outward deed. The Pandavas want five villages, king, just five,
and you will not give them. Then who will make peace? You want to win the
strong-armed Varshneya with wealth. By this means you want to split him from the
Pandavas.

"He cannot be won by wealth, or by effort, or by blame, to act against
Dhananjaya. I tell you the truth. I know Krishna's greatness. I know his firm
devotion. I know he would never give up Dhananjaya, who is to him like his own
life. Janardana will want nothing more than a full jar of water, water to wash
his feet, and a question about his health. Give him the hospitality he likes,
king, for the great man deserves honour. Janardana deserves honour. Keshava
comes to the Kurus hoping for good. Do him the service he comes for, king. The
Dasharha wants peace between you, Duryodhana and the Pandavas, king of kings. Do
as he says. You are the father, king, and they are your sons. You are old and
they are children. Behave like a father to them, for they behave like sons to
you."

"What Vidura says about Krishna is all true," said Duryodhana. "Janardana is
devoted to the Parthas and cannot be turned away from them. The wealth of many
kinds that is to be given to Janardana with honour should never be given, king
of kings. It is not that Keshava does not deserve it. But the time and place are
not right. Adhokshaja would think, king, 'He honours me out of fear.' A wise man
should not do a thing that would bring contempt on a kshatriya, lord of the
people. That is my firm view. I know very well that lotus-eyed Krishna is the
god most worthy of honour in all three worlds. But nothing should be given to
him. That is how things stand, lord. The quarrel has begun, and it will not end
without a war."

When he heard this, Bhishma, grandfather of the Kurus, spoke to King
Vaichitravirya. "Honoured or not honoured, Janardana would not be angry. Keshava
cannot be scorned by another, even if he is scorned. Whatever task the
strong-armed one has set his mind to, no one can make it go otherwise by any
means. Do without hesitation what he says. Make peace quickly with the Pandavas,
through Vasudeva. Janardana, whose soul is dharma, will surely speak what is
right and useful. You and your kin should speak kindly to him."

"There is no way, king," said Duryodhana, "that I would share this fortune with
them while I live, grandfather, and enjoy only part of it. But hear this very
great thing I have decided. I will take Janardana prisoner, the refuge of the
Pandavas. When he is bound, the Vrishnis, the earth and the Pandavas will all be
in my power. He will come here in the morning. Tell me how this can be done so
that Janardana does not see it coming, and no harm comes of it."

When he heard those terrible words aimed at Krishna, Dhritarashtra and his
counsellors were shaken and dismayed. Then Dhritarashtra said to Duryodhana, "Do
not say such a thing, protector of the people. This is not the eternal dharma.
Hrishikesha is an envoy, and our kinsman and our friend. He has done the
Kauravas no wrong. How can he deserve to be bound?"

"Your son is surrounded, Dhritarashtra," said Bhishma, "and very foolish. He
chooses harm and not good, though his friends beg him. You follow this wicked
man who keeps to the wrong road, bound to evil, and you set aside the words of
your friends. When your evil-minded son and his counsellors meet Krishna, whose
deeds never fail, they will cease to be in a moment. I cannot bear to hear the
harmful words of this wicked, cruel, evil-minded man who has given up dharma."

When he had said this, Bhishma, best of the Bharatas, old and in a great rage,
of true courage, rose and left.

---

Krishna rose in the morning and did all his morning rites. With the leave of the
brahmins he set out for the city. As the strong-armed one left, all those who
lived in Vrikasthala took their leave of him and turned back. All the sons of
Dhritarashtra came out to meet him as he came, finely adorned, except
Duryodhana, and Bhishma, Drona, Kripa and the others. Crowds of townspeople came
too, king, wanting to see Hrishikesha, some in carriages of every kind, others
on foot. On the road he met Bhishma, whose deeds never fail, and Drona and the
sons of Dhritarashtra, and surrounded by them he went to the city.

To honour Krishna the city was adorned, and the royal roads were spread with
many jewels. No one stayed at home that day, king, bull of the Bharatas, not a
woman, an old man or a child, so much did they want to see Vasudeva. The men on
the royal road could not even reach the ground, so great was the crowd, king,
when Hrishikesha came in. Even the great houses, crowded with fine women, seemed
to sway on the ground with the weight. Vasudeva's swift horses lost their speed
on the royal road crowded with people.

The lotus-eyed tamer of enemies entered Dhritarashtra's house, white and graced
with mansions. Keshava passed through three courtyards of the royal palace and
came to King Vaichitravirya. When the Dasharha came near, the glorious king
whose eyes are his wisdom rose, together with Drona and Bhishma. Kripa,
Somadatta and great King Bahlika all rose from their seats to honour Janardana.
Then the Varshneya came to the glorious King Dhritarashtra and honoured Bhishma
promptly with words. Madhava Madhusudana greeted them all in the order dharma
lays down, meeting the kings according to their age. Then Janardana met Drona
and his son, glorious Bahlika, Kripa and Somadatta.

There was a large, splendid golden seat, well polished, and at Dhritarashtra's
command Achyuta sat on it. Dhritarashtra's priests offered Janardana a cow, the
honey drink and water as is proper. When he had received hospitality, Govinda
sat surrounded by the Kurus, making his kinship felt, and joked with them all.
Honoured and worshipped by Dhritarashtra, the glorious tamer of enemies took his
leave of the king and went out.

When he had met the Kurus in the assembly as was proper, Madhava went to
Vidura's pleasant house. Vidura came to Janardana with every lucky thing and
honoured the Dasharha with all he could want. When he had given Govinda
hospitality, Vidura, who knew all dharma, asked Madhusudana about the health of
the sons of Pandu. To his friend, who was glad and learned, always devoted to
dharma, wise and without fault, the Dasharha, who has seen everything with his
own eyes, told the steward in full all that the Pandavas had done.

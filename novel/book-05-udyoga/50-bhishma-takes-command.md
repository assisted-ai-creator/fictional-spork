# Bhishma Takes Command

Then Dhritarashtra's son joined his hands and, with all the kings, said this to
Bhishma, Shantanu's son.

"Without a leader even a very great army breaks up when it comes to battle, like
a heap of ants. The minds of two men are never the same, and leaders compete
with one another in courage. We hear, very wise one, that all the brahmins once
attacked the Haihayas of boundless energy, raising banners of kusha grass. The
vaishyas and the shudras followed them, grandfather. On one side were the three
classes, on the other the bulls of the kshatriyas. Again and again the three
classes were broken in battle, while the kshatriyas won, though their force was
small. Then the best of the twice-born asked the kshatriyas, and they, who knew
dharma, told them truly, grandfather. 'In battle we listen to one man of great
wisdom. But you each go your own way, following your own minds.' Then the
brahmins made one of the twice-born their general, a brave man skilled in
policy, and they defeated the kshatriyas. So those who make a skilled, brave,
sinless man, devoted to their good, their general, beat their enemies in battle.

"You are equal to Ushanas and always want my good. No one can win you over, and
you stand by dharma. Be our general. You are to us as the sun is to all that
have rays, the moon to plants, Kubera to the yakshas and Vasava to the Maruts.
You are as Meru is to mountains, Suparna to birds, Kumara to beings and the
bearer of offerings to the Vasus. Guarded by you, as the gods are by Shakra, we
will surely be unassailable even by the thirty gods. Go before us, like the son
of Agni before the gods, and we will follow you like bullocks following a bull."

"It is as you say, strong-armed Bharata," said Bhishma. "But as you are to me,
so are the Pandavas. I should tell them what is good for them too, lord of men.
But I must fight for your sake, as the agreement was made. I see no fighter on
earth equal to me except that tiger among men, Kunti's son Dhananjaya. That
strong-armed one knows every heavenly weapon. But the Pandava would never fight
me openly in battle. With the power of my weapons I could in a moment empty this
world of men, with the gods, asuras and rakshasas. But Pandu's sons must not be
destroyed by me, lord of men. So I will kill ten thousand fighters every day by
the use of my weapons. In this way I will bring about their destruction, joy of
the Kurus, if they do not kill me first when we meet.

"I will be your general as you wish, king, on another condition. You should hear
it now. Let Karna fight first, or I, lord of the earth. For the suta's son
always competes with me fiercely in battle."

"While Ganga's son lives, king," said Karna, "I will not fight at all. When
Bhishma is killed, I will fight the bearer of Gandiva."

Then Dhritarashtra's son made Bhishma, who gave rich fees, his general by the
rites, and once consecrated he shone. At the king's command men played drums and
conches, and hundreds of *pushkara* drums, without pause. Lions' roars of many
kinds and the noises of the animals arose, and from a cloudless sky came a rain
that made a mire of blood. There were whirlwinds, earthquakes and the trumpeting
of elephants, and they made the hearts of all the fighters sink. Voices without
bodies were heard, and meteors fell from the sky. Jackals, foretelling danger,
howled very loudly with burning voices. When the king consecrated Ganga's son as
general, these fearful things appeared in hundreds, king.

When he had made Bhishma, the crusher of enemy armies, his general, he had the
best of the twice-born pronounce blessings, and gave them plenty of gold coins
and cows. Growing with blessings for victory, he set out surrounded by his
soldiers, with the son of the river at their head, together with his brothers,
and went to Kurukshetra with a great camp. The Kaurava, lord of men, went round
Kurukshetra with Karna and had a camp measured out on level ground. It was sweet
ground, free of salt, with plenty of fodder and fuel, and the camp shone like
Hastinapura itself.

---

"Great Bhishma, son of the river, was the best of those who bear weapons, the
grandfather of the Bharatas and the banner of all kings," said Janamejaya. "He
was equal to Brihaspati in wisdom and to the earth in patience, like the ocean
in depth, steady as Himavat, like Prajapati in generosity and like the sun in
splendour. With his rains of arrows he destroyed enemies like great Indra. When
King Yudhishthira heard that he was consecrated for a long sacrifice of battle,
fearsome, dreadful, making the hair stand on end, "what did the strong-armed
one, skilled in every dharma, say? And what did Bhimasena and Arjuna, or
Krishna, do?"

"Wise Yudhishthira, skilled in the dharma and purpose of times of distress,
gathered all his brothers and Vasudeva the Satvata," said Vaishampayana, "and
the best of speakers said this gently.

"'Go round the armies and stand ready in armour. Your fight with the grandfather
will come first. So look to the leaders of my seven armies.'

"'You have spoken words full of meaning, bull of the Bharatas,' said Vasudeva,
'as you ought to speak now that this time has come. I agree, strong-armed one.
Let what comes next be done. Let the seven leaders of your army be consecrated.'

"Then Yudhishthira sent for Drupada, Virata, the bull of the Shinis,
Dhrishtadyumna the Panchala prince, King Dhrishtaketu, Shikhandi the Panchala
prince and Sahadeva of Magadha. Yudhishthira consecrated these seven great
archers, heroes who delighted in war, as leaders of the armies by the rites.
Over all the armies he set Dhrishtadyumna, who was born from the blazing fire to
bring about Drona's end. Over all those great men together he made Dhananjaya,
Gudakesha, general of the generals. And the leader of Arjuna, and the driver of
his horses, was glorious Janardana of great wisdom, Samkarshana's younger
brother.

"Halayudha saw that the war had come, near at hand and very terrible. He came
into the Pandava king's house with Akrura and the others, with Gada, Samba,
Ulmuka and the rest, with Rukmini's sons and Ahuka's sons, with Charudeshna at
their head. The strong-armed one was guarded by the chiefs of the Vrishnis who
came with him, like tigers fierce in their strength, as Vasava is guarded by the
Maruts. Dressed in blue silk, like a peak of Kailasa, with the easy stride of a
lion, glorious, the corners of his eyes red with drink, he came in. When they
saw him, the King of Dharma, Keshava of great splendour, Partha Vrikodara of
terrible deeds, the bearer of Gandiva and all the other kings there rose, went
to meet Halayudha and honoured him. Then the Pandava king touched his hand, and
all of them, with Vasudeva at their head, greeted him. Halayudha, tamer of
enemies, greeted the old men Virata and Drupada and sat down with Yudhishthira.
When the kings had sat down around him, Rohini's son looked at Vasudeva and
spoke.

"'This will be a terrible, dreadful slaughter of men. I think it is fated and
cannot be passed by. It is my hope to see you all come through this war with
your friends, unhurt and with your bodies whole. No doubt the gathered
kshatriyas of the earth are ripe for time. There will be a very great clash,
with a mire of flesh and blood.

"'I said to Vasudeva again and again in private, "Behave the same way to our
kinsmen, Madhusudana. As the Pandavas are to us, so is King Duryodhana. Let help
be given to him too, as is fitting," I said again and again. But for your sake
Madhusudana did not do what I said. He has given himself to Dhananjaya with his
whole heart. I am certain that the Pandavas' victory is sure, Bharata, for that
is Vasudeva's resolve. And I cannot bear to look at the world without Krishna,
so I follow what Keshava wants to do.

"'Both these heroes, skilled in fighting with the club, are my pupils. I love
Bhima and King Duryodhana equally. So I will go to visit the sacred fords of the
Sarasvati, for I cannot bear to look on while the Kauravas are destroyed.'

"When he had said this, the strong-armed one took leave of the Pandavas, turned
Madhusudana back, and went on a pilgrimage to the sacred fords."

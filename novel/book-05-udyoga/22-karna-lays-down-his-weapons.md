# Karna Lays Down His Weapons

While Vaichitravirya kept asking about the Parthas, Karna ignored him and spoke
to Dhritarashtra's son in the assembly of the Kauravas, to cheer him.

"Long ago I won the weapon Brahmapura from Rama by a false claim. When he found
out, he said to me, 'At the time of your end, it will not come to you.' I was
cursed by my teacher, the great seer, for a great offence, though I bowed down
to him. The fierce great seer could have burnt up the earth with its oceans. But
I won his heart with my service and my own manhood. So I still have that weapon,
with some of its power left. So I am able. This burden is mine. When I have won
that seer's grace, in the blink of an eye I will kill the Panchalas, Karushas
and Matsyas, and the Parthas with their sons and grandsons. Then I will win the
worlds that are won by weapons. Let the grandfather stay near you, and Drona,
and all the chief kings. I will go with the main force and kill the Parthas.
That burden is mine."

As he spoke, Bhishma said to him, "Why do you boast, Karna, when your mind is in
the grip of time? Do you not know that when the chief is killed, the sons of
Dhritarashtra will be killed? Just hearing of the deed Dhananjaya did, with
Krishna as his second, when he burnt Khandava, you and your kin ought to hold
yourselves in check. The spear that great Indra, the holy lord of the thirty
gods, gave you, you will see burnt to ash, fallen and shattered, struck by
Keshava's discus. You worship that shining arrow of yours with the serpent's
head so carefully, with the finest garlands. It will be struck by the Pandava's
floods of arrows and go to destruction with you, Karna. Vasudeva, the killer of
Bana and of Naraka, son of the Earth, guards the wearer of the crown, Karna. He
is the killer of enemies like you, and greater, in the thick of the battle."

"No doubt the lord of the Vrishnis is as he says," said Karna, "and that great
one is more than that. But let the grandfather hear the result of the harsh
words he has said to me. I lay down my weapons. The grandfather will never see
me in battle, only in the hall. When you are at peace, all the kings on earth
will see my power."

When he had said this, the great archer left the hall and went to his own house.
But Bhishma laughed, king, and spoke to Duryodhana in the middle of the Kurus.

"The suta's son keeps his promises, they say. Then how will he bear that burden?
Facing army with army, splitting heads, see the destruction of the world from
Bhimasena. While the Avantis, the Kalinga, Jayadratha, Vedidhvaja and Bahlika
stand, I shall always kill the enemy's fighters in thousands and tens of
thousands. From the moment that Vaikartana, the lowest of men, claimed to be a
brahmin before blessed, faultless Rama and won that weapon, his dharma and his
austerity were destroyed."

When King Bhishma had spoken, and Karna had laid down his weapons and gone,
foolish Duryodhana, son of Vaichitravirya's son, spoke to Shantanu's son.

---

"They are all like other men," said Duryodhana, "born the same way. Why do you
think victory belongs only to the Parthas? We are all of one kind. We are all
born of human wombs. Grandfather, how do you know that victory is with the
Parthas? I do not rely on you, or on Drona, Kripa or Bahlika, or on the other
kings, to make my effort. I, Vaikartana Karna and my brother Duhshasana will
kill the five Pandavas in battle with sharp arrows. Then, king, I will satisfy
the brahmins with great sacrifices of every kind, with rich fees, with cows,
horses and wealth."

"Once, we are told," said Vidura, "a fowler of long ago set a net on the ground
to catch birds. Two birds of equal strength were caught in it together. They
took the net up with them, and both flew into the sky. When the fowler saw them
take to the air, he ran after them without losing heart, wherever they went. A
sage in his hermitage, who had finished his daily rites, saw the hunter running
after them for the sake of the birds. He questioned that man who went on foot
after the two in the sky, Kauravya, with this verse. 'This seems strange and
wonderful to me, hunter. Those two are flying, and you are running after them on
foot.'

"'Those two together are carrying off my one net,' said the fowler. 'Where they
quarrel, they will fall into my power.'

"Those two birds, bound to death, began to quarrel. Fighting, the two fools fell
to the earth. While they fought in their fury, in the power of death's noose,
the hunter came up unnoticed and took them. In the same way, kinsmen who quarrel
with one another over wealth fall into the power of their enemies, like the
birds through their quarrel. Eating together, talking together, asking after
each other and meeting: these are the business of kin, never quarrels. When all
of them, in good heart, wait on the old, they cannot be attacked, like a forest
guarded by a lion. Those who have won lasting wealth and sit about like beggars
give their fortune to their enemies, bull of the Bharatas. Kinsmen are like
firebrands, Dhritarashtra, bull of the Bharatas. Apart they smoke, and together
they blaze.

"I will tell you something else, as I saw it on a mountain. When you have heard
it, Kauravya, do what is best. We went to the northern mountain with Kiratas and
with brahmins like gods, who knew spells, charms and remedies. All around,
Gandhamadana was one thicket, bright with herbs and visited by siddhas and
gandharvas. There we all saw honey, not made by bees, on a hard steep cliff, the
size of a jar. Poisonous snakes guarded it, and Kubera loved it dearly. The
brahmins who knew charms told us that a mortal man who eats it becomes immortal,
a blind man gets his sight, and an old man grows young. When the Kiratas saw it
they wanted it, lord of the earth, and they perished in that rough mountain
chasm full of snakes.

"In the same way this son of yours wants the earth for himself alone. In his
folly he sees the honey and does not see the fall. Duryodhana wants to fight the
Left-handed Archer in battle, but I do not see in him such energy or such
courage. The hero who conquered the earth standing in a single chariot waits,
and bears with what you do. Drupada, the Matsya king and Dhananjaya in his anger
would leave nothing standing in battle, like fires fanned by the wind. Take King
Yudhishthira on your lap, Dhritarashtra. When two fight a war, victory is never
certain for either."

---

"Duryodhana, understand what I am going to tell you, my son," said
Dhritarashtra. "You think the wrong path is the right one, like a traveller who
does not know the way. You want to measure the energy of the five sons of Pandu,
which is like that of the five great elements. You cannot know Yudhishthira, son
of Kunti, who stands here in the highest dharma, without looking to the highest
end. You think you can take on Bhimasena, son of Kunti, who has no equal in
strength, the ender of battles, as a tree takes on a great wind. He is the best
of all who bear weapons, as Meru is the best of mountains. What wise man would
fight the Gandiva bowman in battle?

"Whom would Dhrishtadyumna of the Panchalas not cut down today, loosing arrows
in the middle of his enemies, as the king of the gods looses the thunderbolt?
Satyaki too, unconquerable, honoured among the Andhakas and Vrishnis, devoted to
the Pandavas' good, will destroy your army. And Krishna, the lotus-eyed, who is
greater than the three worlds put together, what wise man would fight him? On
one side are his wives, his kinsmen and his family, his own self and this earth,
and on the other side is Dhananjaya. Vasudeva too is unconquerable and
self-controlled, where the Pandava is. Where Keshava is, that army cannot be
withstood even by the earth.

"Stand by the words of the good, my son, of friends who speak for your good.
Bear with old Bhishma, son of Shantanu, your grandfather. Listen to me when I
speak for the good of the Kurus, and to Drona, Kripa, Vikarna and great King
Bahlika. You should think of them as you think of me. They all know dharma, and
they love both sides alike, Bharata. In Virata's city, in front of you and your
brothers, your army let the cattle go and broke apart in terror. That great
wonder told of in that city is proof enough of one against many. Arjuna did all
that alone. How much more all of them together? Know them as your brothers, and
support them with a living."

---

When he had said this to Suyodhana, wise and fortunate Dhritarashtra questioned
Sanjaya again. "Tell me what is left, Sanjaya, what Arjuna said to you after
Vasudeva. I am very eager to hear it."

"When he had heard Vasudeva's words," said Sanjaya, "Dhananjaya, son of Kunti,
the unconquerable, spoke at the right time, with Vasudeva listening. 'Sanjaya,
greet the grandfather, son of Shantanu, Dhritarashtra, Drona, Kripa, Karna and
great King Bahlika. Greet Drona's son and Somadatta, Shakuni son of Subala,
Duhshasana, Shala, Purumitra and Vivimshati. Greet Vikarna, Chitrasena and King
Jayatsena, Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, and Durmukha the Kaurava. Greet the
king of Sindhu, Duhsaha and Bhurishravas, King Bhagadatta and King Jalasandha.
And greet the other kings who have come there to fight for the Kauravas' sake,
who want to die in the blazing Pandava fire, whom Dhritarashtra's son has
gathered, suta. Greet them from me, as is fitting, and ask after their health.

"'Say this in the middle of the kings, Sanjaya, to Suyodhana, chief of
evil-doers. He is an impatient, evil-minded prince, a wicked son of
Dhritarashtra and very greedy. Repeat all these words of mine in full to him and
his counsellors, Sanjaya.'

"When he had set me to this, Dhananjaya, the wise Partha, with his long eyes red
at the corners, looked at Vasudeva and spoke these words, full of sense and of
dharma. 'You have heard the words of great Madhava, hero of the Madhus, well
considered. Give my words in the same way to all the kings gathered there.
Arrows are the smoke of the fire, the chariot wheel is the roar, the bow is the
ladle that takes away the strength of weapons. Strive together with care, so
that no offering is made in that great battle. Yudhishthira, the killer of
enemies, wants his own share. If you do not give it, I will send you, with your
horses, foot soldiers and elephants, to the unlucky land of the ancestors with
my sharp arrows.'

"Then I took my leave of four-armed Hari and bowed to Dhananjaya, and came here
in haste, you who shine like the immortals, to bring his great words to you."

**Divergences.**

* **Karna's curse.** In the CE Karna himself tells of Rama's curse here: he won the weapon Brahmapura by a false claim, and Rama said it would not come to him at the time of his end (5.61.2–3). Bhishma adds that he claimed to be a brahmin (5.61.17). The full story (the worm, the thigh) is Narada's, told after the war (12.3). Ledger L-53, extended.
* **Karna will not fight while Bhishma fights.** His vow is CE: *nyasyāmi śastrāṇi na jātu saṃkhye pitāmaho drakṣyati māṃ sabhāyām / tvayi praśānte tu mama prabhāvaṃ drakṣyanti sarve bhuvi bhūmipālāḥ* (5.61.13). Ledger L-176.
* **The spear.** Bhishma foretells that Krishna's discus will destroy the spear Indra gave Karna (5.61.9). In the war the spear is spent on Ghatotkacha instead (7.154). Bhishma's words are kept as he speaks them.
* Rejected lines (\*355–\*358) add detail. Not narrated.

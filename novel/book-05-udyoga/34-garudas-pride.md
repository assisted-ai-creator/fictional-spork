# Garuda's Pride

"This is Rasatala, the seventh level of the earth," said Narada, "where Surabhi
lives, the mother of cows, who was born of amrita. She always pours out milk,
born of the essence of the earth, one flavour that is the essence of the six
flavours. Long ago, when the grandfather was full of amrita and spat out its
essence, the faultless one rose from his mouth. From the stream of her milk
falling on the earth a lake was made, the ocean of milk, holy and supreme. It is
ringed with foam like a flowering garland. The best of sages live here drinking
it. They are called the Foam-drinkers, Matali, and they live on foam. They
practise fierce austerity, and the gods fear them.

"Four other cows born of her live in all the directions, Matali. They are known
as the guardians of the directions, who hold them up. Surabhi's daughter named
Surupa holds up the east. Hamsaka holds up the south. The western quarter,
Varuna's, is held up always by great Subhadra, of every form, Matali. The cow
named Sarvakamadugha holds up the northern quarter, Matali, the quarter of
dharma, called Ailavila. When the gods and asuras together churned the water of
the ocean, mixed with their milk, with Mount Mandara as the churning stick, they
brought up Varuni, Lakshmi and the amrita, Matali, and Uchchaihshravas, king of
horses, and the jewel Kaustubha. Surabhi pours out milk that is nectar for those
who feed on nectar, svadha for those who feed on svadha, and amrita for those
who feed on amrita.

"Here is a verse sung long ago by those who live in Rasatala. It is heard in the
world and sung by the wise. 'Not in the world of the nagas, not in heaven, not
in the heavenly cars of the third heaven, is there such happy dwelling as in
Rasatala.'

---

"This is the city called Bhogavati, ruled by Vasuki, as fine as the king of the
gods' best of cities, Amaravati. Here stands the naga Shesha, by whom this earth
is always held up, with austerity that leads the world and great power. He looks
like a white mountain, with ornaments of every kind. He holds up a thousand
heads, his tongues are flames, and he is mighty. Here live the nagas, sons of
Surasa, of every shape and with every kind of ornament, free of care. They are
marked with gems, swastikas and wheels, with the signs of water jars. They are
thousands in number, strong and fierce by nature. Some have a thousand heads,
some five hundred mouths, some a hundred heads, some three heads. Some have ten
heads, some seven mouths. They have great coils and great bodies, with coils
like mountains.

"There are many thousands here, millions and hundreds of millions of nagas of
one line. Hear the chief of them from me. Vasuki and Takshaka, Karkotaka and
Dhananjaya, Kaliya and Nahusha, Kambala and Ashvatara, Bahyakunda, Mani the
naga, Apurana and Khaga, Vamana and Elapatra, Kukura and Kukuna, Aryaka and
Nandaka, Kalasha and Potaka, Kailasaka, Pinjaraka and the naga Airavata.
Sumanomukha, Dadhimukha, Shankha, Nanda and Upanandaka, Apta and Kotanaka,
Shikhin and Nishthurika, Tittiri and Hastibhadra, Kumuda and Malyapindaka, the
two Padmas and Pundarika, Pushpa and Mudgaraparnaka, Karavira, Pitharaka,
Samvritta and Vritta, Pindara, Bilvapatra, Mushikada and Shirishaka. Dilipa,
Shankhashirsha, Jyotishka and Aparajita, Kauravya and Dhritarashtra, Kumara and
Kushaka, Viraja and Dharana, Subahu, Mukhara and Jaya, Badhira and Andha,
Vikunda, Virasa and Surasa. These and many others are known as the sons of
Kashyapa. See whether any husband here pleases you, Matali."

Matali kept looking closely, untroubled, at one of them. Then he asked Narada,
as if he were pleased, "Who is this, standing in front of Kauravya and Aryaka,
shining and handsome? Whose family's joy is he? Who is his father, and his
mother? Of which naga is he the son? Of what line is he the banner? By his
bearing, his steadiness, his beauty and his youth he has entered my mind, divine
seer. He is a fine husband for Gunakeshi."

Seeing Matali glad at the sight of Sumukha, Narada told him of his greatness,
his birth and his deeds. "He is the naga king named Sumukha, born in the family
of Airavata. He is held to be Aryaka's grandson, and Vamana's daughter's son.
His father was the naga named Chikura, Matali. Not long ago Vinata's son killed
him."

Then Matali said gladly to Narada, "This best of serpents pleases me as a
son-in-law, my son. Let an effort be made here, for I am pleased with him. I
want to give my dear daughter to this lord of nagas, sage."

---

"This is the charioteer named Matali," said Narada, "Shakra's dear friend. He is
pure, of good character and virtue, full of energy, courage and strength. He is
Shakra's friend, his counsellor and his charioteer, and in battle after battle
there is little difference between his power and Vasava's. In the wars of gods
and asuras he drives the best of victorious chariots, yoked with a thousand
horses, by his mind alone. Vasava conquers with his arms those whom this man has
beaten with his horses. When this man has struck first, the slayer of Bala
strikes. He has a lovely daughter, without equal in beauty on earth, rich in
spirit, character and virtue, famous as Gunakeshi. He has gone through the three
worlds with care, you who shine like the immortals, and your grandson Sumukha
pleases him as his daughter's husband.

"If you like it, gentle one, best of serpents, quickly make up your mind,
Aryaka, to accept the girl. As Lakshmi is in Vishnu's family and Svaha in
fire's, so let slender Gunakeshi be in your family. Accept Gunakeshi for your
grandson's sake, as fit for him as Shachi is for Vasava. Though he has lost his
father, we choose him for his virtues, out of high regard for you and for
Airavata, and for Sumukha's own virtues of character, purity and self-control.
Matali has come himself and is ready to give the girl. You too ought to honour
him."

Aryaka, both sad and glad, his grandson chosen and his son dead, spoke to
Narada. "How could I not value your words highly, divine seer? Who would not
want to be joined to Shakra's friend? But I am troubled, great sage, by the
weakness of the cause. His father, my son, was eaten by Vinata's son, and so we
are stricken with grief. Again, lord, that son of Vinata said as he went, 'In
another month I will eat Sumukha.' It will surely be so. We know his resolve.
Because of the Suparna's words my joy is gone."

Matali said to him, "I have made up my mind about this. Your son's son Sumukha
is chosen as my son-in-law. Let this serpent go with me and Narada to see
Vasava, lord of the three worlds, king of the gods. I will find out the rest of
his life by what remains to be done, and I will try to stop the Suparna, good
man. Let Sumukha come with me to the lord of the gods, to bring about our
purpose. Bless you, serpent."

Then all those mighty ones took Sumukha and saw Shakra seated, the king of the
gods, so glorious. As it happened, the blessed four-armed Vishnu was there. Then
Narada told him all about Matali. Then Vishnu said to Purandara, lord of the
world, "Let him be given amrita, and made equal to the immortals. Let Matali,
Narada and Sumukha have this wish from you as they want it, Vasava."

Purandara thought of the courage of Vinata's son and said to Vishnu, "You give
it."

"You are the lord of the worlds," said Vishnu, "of all that moves and does not
move. Who would dare to undo what you have given, lord?"

Then Shakra gave the serpent the highest span of life. But the slayer of Bala
and Vritra did not let him drink the amrita. When he had won the boon, Sumukha
became truly glad-faced. He married as he wished and went home. Narada and
Aryaka, their task done, went back gladly, after honouring the glorious king of
the gods.

---

Mighty Garuda heard what had happened, Bharata, how Shakra had given the naga
long life. Blocking the three worlds with the great wind of his wings, the
Suparna rushed at Vasava in a fury.

"Lord," said Garuda, "why, in contempt of me, when I am afraid of hunger, have
you gone back on the boon you gave me to do as I please? The Ordainer, the lord
of all beings, appointed my food from the creation of all beings. Why do you
stop me? I chose this great naga, and I set the time. With him, god, I was to
feed my great brood. If this is changed, I cannot bring myself to harm another.
You play as you please, king of the gods, as you like. So I shall give up my
life, and so will my household and the servants in my house. Be pleased, Vasava.

"I deserve this and more, slayer of Bala and Vritra, since I, who am lord of the
three worlds, have become another's servant. While you stand, lord of the gods,
Vishnu is not my cause. The kingdom of the three worlds is yours for ever,
Vasava. I too have Daksha's daughter for my mother and Kashyapa for my father. I
too can carry all the worlds with ease. I too have great strength that no being
can bear. I too did a great deed in the war with the daityas. Shrutashri,
Shrutasena, Vivasvat, Rochanamukha, Prasabha and Kalakaksha, sons of Diti, were
killed by me too. It is because I take my place on the banner and serve with
care, and carry your younger brother, that you despise me.

"Who else can bear the burden? Who else is stronger than I? Though I am greater,
I carry him and his kin. Because you took away my food out of contempt, I have
lost my honour because of you, and because of him, Vasava. Of all these born of
Aditi, strong in strength and courage, you, they say, are the strongest. Yet I
carry you with one part of my wing, without tiring. Think about it slowly, my
son. Who is the strong one here?"

When he heard the bird's words, fearful in what they would lead to, the bearer
of the chariot discus shook the unshakeable Tarkshya. "Garutman, you think
yourself strong, though you are very weak. Enough of praising yourself in front
of us, egg-born one. The whole three worlds could not bear my body. I carry
myself by myself, and I carry you. Carry just this one right arm of mine. If you
can bear this one, your boasting will bear fruit."

Then the lord laid his arm on Garuda's shoulder. Stricken by the weight, he
fell, bewildered and senseless. That one limb felt to him as heavy as the whole
earth with its mountains. But the stronger one did not crush him with his
strength, and Achyuta did not destroy his life. The bird, his wings spread out,
his body slack, senseless and bewildered, crushed by the great weight, let fall
his feathers. Vinata's son bowed his head to Vishnu and, senseless, bewildered
and wretched, said a few words.

"Lord, I have been crushed into the earth by an arm let fall at will, equal to a
body that is the essence of the world. Forgive me, god, bewildered and of little
mind, a bird who lives on your banner, burnt by the fire of your strength. I did
not know your highest strength, god, lord. So I thought my own courage beyond
compare."

Then the lord showed Garutman his grace, and said to him kindly, "Never act like
this again."

"In the same way, son of Gandhari," said Kanva, "you will live only until you
meet the heroic sons of Pandu in battle, my son. Bhima, the best of fighters,
the mighty son of the wind, and Dhananjaya, the son of Indra, whom would they
not kill in battle? Vishnu, Vayu, Shakra, Dharma and the two Ashvins, by what
means could you even look at these gods? So enough of enmity. Make peace,
prince. You ought to protect your family, with Vasudeva as your ford. Narada
here, the great ascetic, saw all this with his own eyes, the greatness of
Vishnu, who is this bearer of the discus and mace."

When he heard this, Duryodhana sighed and frowned. He looked at Radha's son and
laughed aloud. In his folly he made nothing of the seer Kanva's words. Slapping
his thigh, which was like an elephant's trunk, he said, "As the Lord made me, as
what is to be and what my end will be, so I go, great seer. What will babbling
do?"

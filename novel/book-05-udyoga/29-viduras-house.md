# Vidura's House

When Govinda had taken leave of Pritha and walked round her, Shauri, tamer of
enemies, went to Duryodhana's house. It was full of the greatest splendour, like
the house of Indra, the breaker of cities. He passed its three courts, and the
doorkeepers did not stop him. Then the famous one climbed up into the palace,
which rose like a mass of cloud or a mountain peak and blazed with splendour.

There he saw strong-armed Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra, seated on his
throne, with thousands of kings and Kurus around him. He saw Duhshasana, Karna
and Shakuni, son of Subala, seated near Duryodhana. As the Dasharha came in, the
famous son of Dhritarashtra rose with his ministers and paid honour to the
slayer of Madhu. Keshava met Duryodhana and his ministers, and the Varshneya met
the kings there as their ages required. Achyuta sat down on a golden couch,
finely made and spread with all kinds of covers. The Kaurava brought Janardana a
cow and the honey mixture, and offered him his houses and his kingdom. Then all
the Kurus and the kings sat around Govinda, who shone like the clear sun.

Then King Duryodhana invited the Varshneya, best of victors, to eat. Keshava did
not accept. So Duryodhana spoke to Krishna in the royal assembly. He spoke first
to Karna, and then to Krishna, gently at first but with a sly turn at the end.

"Why did you not take the food and drink, the clothes and the beds that were
brought for you, Janardana? You have given help to both sides, and you are
devoted to the good of both. You are kin to Dhritarashtra, Madhava, and dear to
him. You know dharma and gain truly and fully, Govinda. I want to hear the
reason, bearer of the discus and the mace."

When Govinda was asked this, the great-minded, lotus-eyed one raised his long
arm and answered the king in a voice like a flood or a thundercloud. His words
were clear and whole, not blurred or clipped or muddled, and they had reason in
them.

"Envoys eat and take honours when their mission has succeeded, Bharata. When I
have succeeded, you and your ministers can honour me."

"You are not right to treat us so badly," said the son of Dhritarashtra.
"Whether you succeed or not, slayer of Madhu, we try to honour you, Govinda, and
we cannot. We do not know the reason, slayer of Madhu, why you will not accept
the honour we offer you out of love, best of men. We have no feud with you,
Govinda, and no quarrel. Think about it, and you will see that you should not
speak like this."

The Dasharha looked at the son of Dhritarashtra and his ministers, and he all
but laughed.

"I would never give up dharma for desire, or anger, or hatred, or gain," he
said, "nor for the sake of argument or greed. Food is eaten out of love, or else
in need. You show me no love, king, and I am in no need. You have hated the
Pandavas since they were born, king, and without cause. They are your brothers.
They do what pleases you, and they have every virtue. There is no reason to hate
the sons of Pritha. The Pandavas stand in dharma. Who can say anything against
them? Whoever hates them hates me. Whoever follows them follows me. Know that I
am one with the Pandavas, who walk in dharma.

"A man who follows desire and anger and in his folly wants to oppose the good,
and hates a man of virtue, is called the lowest of men. A man who in his folly
and greed wants to see ill come to kinsmen of good character has not mastered
himself or his anger, and he does not keep his fortune long. But a man who wins
over good men by kindness, even if they are not dear to his heart, lives long in
fame. All this food is tainted by evil intent. It is not to be eaten. I think
the steward's food alone should be eaten."

When he had said this to resentful Duryodhana, the strong-armed one left the
shining house of Dhritarashtra's son. The great-minded, strong-armed Vasudeva
went out, and went to great Vidura's house to stay.

Drona, Kripa, Bhishma and Bahlika and the Kurus came to the strong-armed one
where he was staying in Vidura's house. "We offer you our houses, Varshneya,"
the Kurus said to the slayer of Madhu, "with all their treasures."

"You may all go," the mighty slayer of Madhu told the Kauravas. "You have paid
me every honour."

When the Kurus had gone, the steward took great care to honour the unbeaten
Dasharha with everything he could want. Then the steward brought the great
Keshava many kinds of pure and good food and drink. With it the slayer of Madhu
first fed the brahmins, and Krishna gave the most precious gifts to those who
knew the Vedas. Then, with his followers, as Vasava does with the Maruts, he ate
Vidura's food, pure and good.

When he had eaten and rested, Vidura spoke to him that night.

"This coming of yours was not well judged, Keshava. The son of Dhritarashtra
goes beyond both gain and dharma, Janardana. He is a fool and an angry man. He
humbles others and wants honour for himself, and he ignores what the old tell
him. He goes beyond the books of dharma. He is dull and wicked, and he has taken
the bit between his teeth. He is evil, and no one can lead him to his good. He
is full of desire and thinks himself wise. He betrays his friends and suspects
everyone. He does nothing for others and he is ungrateful. He has given up
dharma and he loves a lie. He has these faults and many others. Even when you
tell him what is good for him, he will not take it, he is so angry.

"When he sees the kings' armies gathered, slayer of Madhu, the fool thinks he
has already won. He is a child and he has no judgement. The foolish son of
Dhritarashtra is sure that Karna alone can beat his enemies. He will not come to
peace. He puts more trust in Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna, Drona's son and
Jayadratha than they deserve, and he does not set his mind on peace. The sons of
Dhritarashtra and Karna are sure, Janardana, that the sons of Pritha cannot even
look Bhishma, Drona and Kripa in the face.

"All the sons of Dhritarashtra have agreed, Keshava, while you work for peace
and want brotherly love, 'We shall not give the Pandavas what is fairly theirs.'
They have made up their minds, and words will be wasted on them. Where good
words and bad are all the same, slayer of Madhu, a wise man should not waste his
breath, any more than a singer sings to the deaf. You would be no more right to
talk among fools who do not understand and know no limits, Madhava, than a
brahmin would be among chandalas. He relies on his strength, and he is a fool.
He will not do what you say. Anything you say to him will turn out to be wasted.

"I do not like you going among them, Krishna, when they are all sitting there
together, with their wicked minds. I do not like you speaking against them,
Krishna, among so many ill-minded, ill-bred and wicked men. He has never served
the old, and he is proud of his fortune and his folly. With the pride of youth
and his resentment, he will not accept your good counsel. He has a strong army,
Madhava. If you speak to him, he will be deeply suspicious of you, and he will
not do what you say. All the sons of Dhritarashtra are sure, Janardana, that not
even Indra and the immortals could take this from them by war. When they are
like this, following desire and anger, even your good words will do no good.

"The fool stands among his elephants and his army of chariots and horses, and
the dullard thinks, free from fear, 'I have conquered this whole earth.' The son
of Dhritarashtra hopes for a great kingdom on earth, without a rival. There is
no peace to be had from him. He thinks the prize he holds is his to keep. This
earth is ripe for death, and it is thrown into confusion. For Duryodhana's sake
all the fighters on earth have gathered, wanting to fight the Pandavas, and the
kings with the lords of the land.

"All these kings have long had feuds with you, Krishna, and you have robbed them
of their strength. Afraid of you, they have taken refuge with the sons of
Dhritarashtra. They have banded together with Karna, heroes all. They have given
up their lives, all these fighters, and with Duryodhana they have been let loose
to fight the Pandavas. I do not think you should go among them, hero, Dasharha.
How could you go into the middle of so many of your enemies, destroyer of
enemies, all sitting there together with their wicked minds?

"I know, strong-armed one, that even the gods could hardly stand against you. I
know your power, your courage and your wisdom, killer of enemies. The love I
have for the Pandavas I have for you too, Madhava, and more. I say this out of
love, and deep respect, and friendship."

"You have spoken as a wise man should," said the Lord, "as a man of good
judgement should, as a friend like you should speak to a friend like me. You
have spoken words of dharma and gain, and true, as is fitting in you. You have
spoken to me like a father or a mother. What you have told me is true and timely
and right. But hear why I came, Vidura, and listen carefully.

"I know the wickedness of the son of Dhritarashtra and the hostility of the
kshatriyas. I know all this, steward, and still I have come to the Kauravas.
This whole earth, with its horses, chariots and elephants, is thrown into
confusion. The man who could free it from the noose of death would win the
highest dharma. If a man strives as hard as he can to do a work of dharma and
cannot do it, he still wins the merit of it. I have no doubt of that. And if a
man thinks of evil in his mind but does not choose to do it, he does not reap
its fruit. So those who know dharma say.

"So I shall try honestly, steward, to make peace for the Kurus and the
Srinjayas, who are about to perish in battle. This terrible danger has risen
among the Kurus themselves. Karna and Duryodhana have made it, and all these
others follow them. If a man does not go to a friend in trouble and try to win
him round as best he can, the wise call him cruel. A man who holds his friend
back from a wrong, even by seizing him by the hair, is blamed by no one, if he
has tried as hard as he can. So Dhritarashtra's son and his ministers should
take my good words, Vidura, fitting and right, in keeping with dharma and gain.
I shall try honestly for the good of the sons of Dhritarashtra and of the
Pandavas too, and of the kshatriyas on earth. If Duryodhana suspects me while I
try to do good, my heart will be glad, and I shall be free of debt.

"When kinsmen quarrel, a friend who does not step in between them and try with
all his might is no friend, say the wise. Let no one who does not know dharma,
no fool and no enemy, say of me, 'Krishna could have held back the angry Kurus
and Pandavas, and he did not.' I have come to bring about what is good for both
sides. When I have tried, I shall be blameless among men. If the fool hears my
words, in keeping with dharma and gain, kindly and healing, and does not accept
them, he will fall into the power of fate.

"If I can make peace for the Kurus without losing anything of the Pandavas', it
will be a great and holy deed, and the Kurus will be freed from the noose of
death. When I speak wise words, delighting in dharma, full of good sense and
free of violence, perhaps the sons of Dhritarashtra will heed them, and the
Kurus will honour me for coming. And all the kings together could not stand
before me when I am angry, any more than other beasts before a lion."

When the bull of the Vrishnis, who brings joy to the Yadus, had said this, he
lay down on a soft bed to sleep.

So the two wise men talked, and the kind night passed, bright with stars.
Krishna, whose power has no measure, listened to great Vidura's many words, full
of dharma, gain and desire, and rich in meaning and in phrasing. With fitting
talk the night passed for Krishna, almost as if he had not wanted it to.

Then many bards and heralds with fine voices woke Keshava with the sound of
conches and drums. Janardana, the Dasharha, bull of all the Satvatas, rose and
did all the morning duties. Madhava bathed, said his prayers, made the fire
offering and dressed in his ornaments. Then he stood to honour the rising sun.

While the unbeaten Dasharha was at his morning prayers, Duryodhana and Shakuni,
son of Subala, came to Krishna. They told him that Dhritarashtra had come into
the hall with the Kurus, Bhishma and the rest, and all the kings. "They ask for
you, Govinda," they said, "as the immortals in heaven ask for Shakra." Govinda
greeted them both with very gracious words.

When the sun was clear, Janardana, scorcher of enemies, gave the brahmins gold,
clothes, cows and horses. When the unbeaten Dasharha had given away his
treasures, his charioteer came and bowed to him where he stood. The great-minded
Janardana saw that his divine chariot had come, with its roar like a great mass
of cloud, and all its jewels. He walked round the fire and the brahmins keeping
them on his right. Then Krishna put on the Kaustubha jewel, blazing with the
greatest splendour. With the Kurus around him and the Vrishnis guarding him,
Shauri, the joy of all the Yadavas, mounted the chariot. Vidura, who knew all
dharma, climbed up after the Dasharha, the best of all who breathe and the
greatest of all who keep dharma.

Duryodhana and Shakuni, son of Subala, followed the scorcher of enemies in a
second chariot. Satyaki, Kritavarma and the great chariot fighters of the
Vrishnis came after Krishna in chariots, on horses and on elephants. Their
chariots, fitted with gold and drawn by the best horses, shone bright and
rattled loudly as they went.

Wise Krishna, blazing with splendour, took the great road in good time. It had
been swept and sprinkled until there was no dust, and royal seers had walked it.
As the Dasharha set out, single drums were beaten, and conches were blown, and
all the other instruments were played. Young heroes of all the world, scorchers
of enemies with the courage of lions, went round Shauri's chariot. Thousands
more in fine and wonderful clothes, carrying swords, spears and other weapons,
went ahead of Krishna. Hundreds of elephants and thousands of the best horses
followed the unbeaten hero, the Dasharha, as he went.

The whole city of the Kurus, with its old men, children and women, had come out
into the streets eager to see Janardana, tamer of enemies. Many houses were
crowded with women leaning on the balconies, and seemed to sway under the
weight. The Kurus paid him honour, and he heard all kinds of talk. He returned
their courtesies as each deserved and looked about him as he went slowly on.

When they reached the hall, Keshava's followers filled every quarter with the
sound of conches and flutes. The whole gathering of the mighty kings trembled
with joy, longing for Krishna to come. As Krishna drew near, the kings were glad
when they heard the roar of his chariot, like the rumble of the rain cloud.

The bull of all the Satvatas reached the door of the hall. Shauri got down from
his chariot, which was like a peak of Kailasa, and went into the hall. It was
like a mountain or a cloud, blazing with splendour, like great Indra's house.
The famous one took Vidura and Satyaki by the hand. He outshone the Kurus with
his splendour, king, as the sun outshines the stars. Karna and Duryodhana went
before Vasudeva, and the Vrishnis and Kritavarma came behind Krishna.

With Dhritarashtra at their head, Bhishma, Drona and the rest all rose from
their seats to honour Janardana. As the Dasharha came in, the great-minded,
famous king whose eyes were his wisdom rose with Bhishma and Drona. When the
great King Dhritarashtra, lord of men, rose, the thousands of kings rose all
around. A seat, carved on all sides and adorned with gold, had been made ready
there for Krishna at Dhritarashtra's order. Madhava, whose soul is dharma,
smiled and spoke to the king, to Bhishma and Drona, and to the other kings as
their ages required. All the kings and princes and the Kurus there honoured
Janardana as was right when he came into the hall.

As the Dasharha, conqueror of enemy cities, stood among the kings, he saw seers
standing in the sky. When he saw the seers, with Narada at their head, the
Dasharha spoke quietly to Bhishma, son of Shantanu.

"The seers have come to see this gathering of kings, king," he said. "Let them
be invited to sit, and let them be greatly honoured. No one can sit while they
are not seated. Let honour be paid quickly to the sages, whose souls are made
perfect."

When the son of Shantanu saw the seers standing at the door of the hall, he
quickly told the servants to bring seats. They brought great, broad, polished
seats, bright with gems and gold, from every side. When the seers had sat down
and taken the water of welcome, Bharata, Krishna sat down on his seat, and the
kings on theirs. Duhshasana gave Satyaki a very fine seat, and Vivimshati gave
Kritavarma a golden stool. Not far from Krishna, Karna and Duryodhana, both
proud and both resentful, sat on a single seat. Shakuni, the king of Gandhara,
sat on his seat with his son, lord of the people, guarded by the men of
Gandhara. Wise Vidura sat on a jewelled stool covered with a white deerskin,
touching Shauri's seat.

The kings of the earth had not seen the Dasharha for a long time. As they looked
at Janardana, they could not have enough of him, as if he were nectar.
Janardana, dark as a flax flower and dressed in yellow, shone in the middle of
the hall like a gem set in gold. Then the whole hall fell silent, with every
mind on Govinda. No one there said a word, anywhere.

**Divergences.**

* **Krishna's refusal of Duryodhana's food** is CE, with his reasons: envoys eat when they have succeeded (5.89.18); food is eaten out of love or in need (5.89.25); this food is tainted, and only the steward's should be eaten (5.89.32). Vidura's food is called "pure and good" (5.89.39, 41), not poor. The exchange in which Duryodhana asks why Krishna ate "a shudra's food" and Krishna says he values respect over food, and praises greens offered with love, is a rejected passage (\*423). The saying "the generous man counts wealth as straw" (\*412) is rejected too. See ledger L-178 and L-32.
* **Vidura's boon.** A long appendix passage (App. I No. 4, at 5.90.1) has Vidura welcome Krishna as the god whom great souls seek, and ask him for boons of devotion. Not narrated. \*411, in which Vidura serves him with a devout speech, is rejected too.
* **The chariot and its horses.** An appendix passage (App. I No. 5, at 5.92.11) describes Krishna's chariot, its four horses and its Garuda banner at length. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*405–\*410, \*413–\*422, which add courtesies, repeat invitations to eat, and add Vidura's list of Duryodhana's faults and the bards' praise.

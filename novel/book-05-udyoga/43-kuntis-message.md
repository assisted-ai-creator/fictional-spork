# Kunti's Message

"Keshava, tell Arjuna this," said Kunti. "'When you were born, I was sitting in
the hermitage, surrounded by women. Then a voice came from the sky, heavenly and
lovely. "Kunti, this son of yours will be the equal of the thousand-eyed god. He
will conquer all the Kurus gathered in battle, and with Bhimasena beside him he
will turn the world upside down. Your son will conquer the earth, and his fame
will touch the sky. With Vasudeva as his ally he will kill the Kurus in battle
and lift up again his father's share that was lost. Glorious, with his brothers,
he will bring three great sacrifices to pass."'

"Achyuta, I know Bibhatsu, the left-handed archer, true to his word, strong and
hard to approach. Let it be as the voice said, Dasharha. If there is dharma,
Varshneya, it will come true, and you too, Krishna, will bring all of it about.
I do not doubt what the voice said. I bow to great dharma. Dharma upholds all
creatures.

"Say this to Dhananjaya, and to Vrikodara, who is always ready: 'The time has
come for which a kshatriya woman bears a son. Bulls among men do not sink once
they have met enmity.' You know Bhima's mind. That slayer of enemies does not
rest until he has made an end of them.

"Madhava, Krishna, tell Krishnaa, great Pandu's famous, lovely daughter-in-law,
who knows the finest points of every dharma, 'It is fitting, fortunate lady,
born in a good family, famous one, that you have behaved rightly to all my
sons.'

"Tell Madri's two sons, both devoted to the dharma of a kshatriya, 'Choose
pleasures won by courage even over your lives. For the riches a man wins by
courage, living by the dharma of a kshatriya, always gladden his heart, best of
men.'

"While you all looked on, harsh words were spoken to the Panchala princess, who
keeps every dharma. Who could forgive that? The loss of the kingdom is not my
grief, nor the defeat at dice, nor my sons' banishment. That is not the cause of
my grief. That tall, dark woman wept in the assembly and heard harsh words. That
I hold a greater grief. The lovely woman was in her season, always devoted to
the dharma of a kshatriya, and Krishnaa, who had protectors, found no protector
then.

"Strong-armed one, tell Arjuna, tiger among men, the best of all who bear
weapons, 'Walk in Draupadi's path.' You know well that when Bhima and Arjuna are
angry they are like Yama and Death, and could send even the gods to their final
end. It was an insult to them that Krishnaa was brought into the assembly, and
that Duhshasana spoke bitter words to Bhima while the Kuru heroes looked on.
Remind them of that again.

"Ask after the Pandavas and their sons, and Krishnaa, Janardana, and tell them I
am well. Go by a safe road, and protect my sons."

Then strong-armed Krishna greeted her, walked round her keeping her on his
right, and went out with the easy stride of a lion. He sent away Bhishma and the
other bulls of the Kurus, took Karna up into his chariot and drove off with
Satyaki.

When the Dasharha had gone, the Kurus gathered together and talked of Keshava as
a great and most wonderful thing. "The whole earth is bewildered," they said,
"and bound in the noose of death. Through Duryodhana's folly this cannot last."

When he had left the city, the best of men talked with Karna for a long time.
Then the joy of all the Yadavas sent Radha's son away and urged his horses on
with great speed. Driven by Daruka, the horses went with great swiftness, fast
as thought or wind, as if drinking the sky. They crossed that road quickly, like
swift hawks, and carried the bearer of the Sharnga bow to Upaplavya while the
sun was still high.

---

When they heard Kunti's words, Bhishma and Drona, the great chariot fighters,
said this to Duryodhana, who would not keep to anyone's command.

"Tiger among men, you heard Kunti's words in Krishna's presence, full of
meaning, calm, spoken in keeping with dharma and unsurpassed. Kunti's sons will
do what Vasudeva approves. They will never be at peace, Kaurava, without the
kingdom. You troubled the Parthas, when they were bound by the noose of dharma,
and Draupadi too in the assembly, and they bore it from you. Now that Arjuna has
mastered his weapons and Bhima has trained hard, and they have Gandiva and the
two quivers, the chariot and the banner, and Vasudeva as their ally,
Yudhishthira will not forgive it.

"Strong-armed one, you saw for yourself how wise Partha beat us all in battle
before, in Virata's city. He set the terrible Rudra weapon to his bow and burnt
the danavas of terrible deeds, the Nivatakavachas, in battle with the fire of
his weapons. Karna and these others, and you too, in armour on your chariot,
were set free on the cattle expedition. That is proof enough.

"Make peace with your brothers the Pandavas, best of the Bharatas. Save this
whole earth, which has gone between the fangs of death. Your eldest brother is
devoted to dharma, loving, gentle in speech and pure. Go to that tiger among men
and put away your wrongs here. If the Pandava sees you with your bow laid aside
and your frown smoothed, fortunate one, peace is made for our family.

"Go to him with your counsellors, embrace the king's son, and greet the king as
before, tamer of enemies. When you greet him, let Kunti's son Yudhishthira,
Bhima's elder brother, take you in his two hands out of friendship. Let Bhima,
the best of fighters, with his lion's shoulders and long, round, mighty arms,
embrace you in his arms. Then let Partha Dhananjaya, Kunti's son, with his
lion's neck, his thick hair and his lotus eyes, greet you. Let the Ashvins' twin
sons, tigers among men, without equal on earth in beauty, come to meet you with
love and honour as their elder. Let the kings with the Dasharha at their head
shed tears of joy. Join your brothers, king, and give up your pride. Then rule
the whole earth with your brothers, and let the kings embrace each other gladly
and go home.

"Enough of war, king of kings. Hear the reason from your friends. Destruction of
the kshatriyas is certain in war, that is plain. The stars are against us,
beasts and birds are terrible, and many kinds of omens are seen, hero, that mean
the destruction of the kshatriyas. Here especially the signs point to our ruin.
Your army is struck by blazing meteors. The animals are joyless and seem to
weep, lord of the people. Vultures wait around your troops on every side. The
city and the royal palace are not as they were before. Jackals with ill-omened
howls haunt the burning quarter.

"Do as your father and mother say, and as we say, who wish you well. Peace or
struggle depends on you, strong-armed one. If you do not do as your friends say,
tamer of enemies, you will burn when you see your army tormented by Partha's
arrows. When you hear the great roar of mighty Bhima as he roars in battle, and
the twang of Gandiva, you will remember my words, if what I say seems wrong to
you now."

Spoken to like this, Duryodhana was downcast. He looked sideways, his face
lowered, and drew his brows together, and said nothing at all. Seeing him
downcast, the two bulls among men looked at each other and spoke again.

"He is obedient and without malice, devoted to brahmins and true to his
promise," said Bhishma. "What could be more painful than to fight against
Partha?"

"I have greater regard for Dhananjaya than for my son Ashvatthama, king," said
Drona, "and greater humility towards the ape-bannered one. If, keeping to the
dharma of a kshatriya, I fight against Dhananjaya, who is dearer to me than my
son, then shame on the kshatriya's way of life. There is no other archer in the
world his equal. By my grace Bibhatsu is better than other archers.

"A man who betrays his friends, whose nature is wicked, who believes in nothing,
who is crooked and deceitful, finds no honour among the good, like a fool who
comes to a sacrifice. Even when held back from evil, an evil man wants evil.
Even when urged to evil, a good man wants good. These faults, best of the
Bharatas, when they are falsely treated and dwell in what is dear, lead to harm.

"You have been told what is good by the eldest of the Kurus, by me, by Vidura
and by Vasudeva, and you do not take it. 'I have strength,' you think, and
rashly you want to cross, as if it were the Ganga's flood in the hot season,
full of crocodiles, sharks and makaras. You are wearing a cloth, it seems, and
think it yours, as if you had found a garland someone threw away. Out of greed
you think Yudhishthira's fortune yours.

"Who, even ruling a kingdom, can outdo Partha, with Draupadi beside him,
surrounded by his armed brothers, even when he lives in the forest? The King of
Dharma met Ailavila, at whose command all kings wait as servants, and shone.
They went to Kubera's home and won jewels there. Now the Pandavas want to attack
your rich country and take back the kingdom.

"We have given, offered sacrifice, studied and satisfied brahmins with wealth.
Our lives have run their course, and know that we have done what we had to do.
But you, if you make war on the Pandavas, will give up happiness, kingdom,
friends and wealth, and meet great disaster. You will not conquer the Pandava
when Draupadi, who speaks the truth, a queen of terrible vows and austerities,
hopes for his victory. How will you conquer the Pandava, the best of all who
bear weapons, whose counsellor is Janardana and whose brother is Dhananjaya? How
will you conquer the Pandava, a hero of fierce austerity, whose allies are
steadfast brahmins who have mastered their senses?

"I will say again what a friend who wants his friends to prosper must do when
they are sinking in an ocean of disaster. Enough of war with those heroes. Make
peace, for the good of the Kurus. Do not go to defeat with your sons, your
counsellors and your army."

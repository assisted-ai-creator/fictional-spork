# The Armies Gather

Then Yuyudhana, the hero of the Satvatas, a great chariot fighter, came to
Yudhishthira with a great army of all four arms. His fighters were very brave,
gathered from many lands, heroes with every kind of weapon, and they made the
army splendid. With axes and short javelins, with spears, lances and clubs, with
darts, swords, hatchets and barbed spears, with spotless blades, with swords,
bows and helmets, and with arrows of many kinds, all oiled and gleaming, that
army shone. The army, dark as a cloud and bright with those weapons, looked like
a cloud lit up by lightning. It was an akshauhini, king, and when it entered
Yudhishthira's army it vanished, as a small river vanishes into the sea.

So too the mighty bull of the Chedis, Dhrishtaketu, came to the Pandavas of
boundless power with an akshauhini. Mighty Jayatsena of Magadha, Jarasandha's
son, came to the Dharma King with an akshauhini of troops. So too the Pandya
king came to Yudhishthira, surrounded by fighters of many kinds who lived in the
lands along the sea. In that great gathering of armies his army was splendid to
look at, finely dressed and strong. Drupada's army, too, was splendid with brave
men gathered from many lands, and with his sons, great chariot fighters. So too
Virata, king of the Matsyas and leader of an army, came to the Pandavas with the
kings of the mountains.

From here and there, seven akshauhinis in all came to the great Pandavas,
crowded with banners of every kind, eager to fight the Kurus, and they made the
Pandavas glad.

---

On the other side, and to the joy of Dhritarashtra's son, King Bhagadatta gave
him an akshauhini. His army, unassailable, with its Chinas and Kiratas, looked
like a forest of karnikara trees, as if it were made of gold. So too the hero
Bhurishravas and Shalya came to Duryodhana, each with an akshauhini of his own.
Kritavarma, Hridika's son, came to Duryodhana with an akshauhini, with the
armies of the Bhojas and Andhakas. His army, with those tigers among men wearing
forest garlands, looked like a forest where rutting elephants play.

Other kings who lived in Sindhu and Sauvira came, with Jayadratha at their head,
as if they shook the mountains. Their akshauhini was great, and it looked like
clouds of many shapes driven by the wind. Sudakshina of Kamboja came to the
Kaurava with an akshauhini, with Yavanas and Shakas, lord of the people. That
host looked like a swarm of locusts, and when it came to the Kaurava it vanished
into his army.

So too Nila, who lived in Mahishmati, came with men armed with blue weapons,
with mighty kings who lived along the southern road. The two kings of Avanti,
each with a mighty army around him, came to Suyodhana with an akshauhini apiece.
The five Kekaya brothers, tigers among men, came rushing in with an akshauhini,
to the Kaurava's delight. From here and there, from all the other great kings,
three more armies came together, bull of the Bharatas.

So Duryodhana had eleven armies in all, crowded with banners of every kind,
eager to fight the sons of Kunti. There was no room in Hastinapura then, king,
even for the chief kings with their best troops. So the land of the Five Rivers,
the whole of Kurujangala, the Rohitaka forest and all the desert country,
Ahicchatra, Kalakuta and the bank of the Ganga, Varana, Vatadhana and the
mountain by the Yamuna, a wide country rich in wealth and grain, was filled with
the Kauravas' army.

There the priest whom the Panchala king had sent to the Kauravas saw that army
gathered.

---

Drupada's priest came to the Kaurava, and was honoured by Dhritarashtra, Bhishma
and Vidura. First he gave them all the news of the Pandavas' welfare and asked
after their health. Then, in the midst of all the leaders of the army, he spoke.

"You all know the eternal dharma of kings. Still, since I have come to deliver a
message, I will speak, though you know it. Dhritarashtra and Pandu are known to
be sons of the same father, and they have an equal share in their father's
wealth. There is no doubt of it. The sons of Dhritarashtra have their father's
wealth. How is it that the sons of Pandu do not have theirs?

"You know how things went before. The Pandavas did not get their father's
wealth. The son of Dhritarashtra kept it. They tried many times, by means meant
to kill, but the Pandavas had life left in them, and they could not send them to
the house of Yama. The great ones grew a kingdom again by their own strength,
and the petty sons of Dhritarashtra, with Subala's son, stole it by a trick. He
allowed that too, as it was done, and they were made to live in the great forest
for thirteen years here.

"The heroes were badly treated in the hall, with their wife. In the forest they
suffered many terrible hardships. In Virata's city too, the great ones lived as
if born into another womb, and suffered the worst of hardships, as the wicked
do.

"They have put all that past wrong behind them. The bulls of the Kurus want
nothing but peace with the Kurus. Knowing how they have behaved, and how
Duryodhana has behaved, their friends here should win Dhritarashtra over. The
heroes are not making war on the Kurus. The Pandavas want what is theirs without
the world being destroyed.

"Whatever reason Dhritarashtra's son may have for war, that reason should not be
relied on, for they are the stronger. Seven akshauhinis have gathered for the
son of Dharma, eager to fight the Kurus, waiting for his command. And there are
other tigers among men, each equal to a thousand akshauhinis: Satyaki, Bhimasena
and the mighty twins. These eleven armies have gathered on one side, and on the
other is strong-armed Dhananjaya of many forms. As the wearer of the diadem
surpasses all armies, so does strong-armed, glorious Vasudeva. Knowing the size
of the armies, the courage of the wearer of the diadem and the wisdom of
Krishna, what man would fight? So give what should be given, as dharma requires
and as the agreement says. Do not let this time pass you by."

---

When he heard these words, Bhishma, old in wisdom and great in glory, honoured
him as the moment required, and spoke.

"How lucky that all the Pandavas are well, with their kin. How lucky that they
have allies, and how lucky that they love dharma. How lucky that the brothers,
the sons of the Kurus, want peace. How lucky that they are not set on war, and
Damodara with them. What you have said is all true, no doubt of it. But I think
your words are very sharp, because you are a brahmin. No doubt the Pandavas have
suffered, in the forest and here, and no doubt all their father's wealth is
theirs by dharma. The Partha, the wearer of the diadem, is strong, skilled in
weapons and mighty. Who could stand against Pandu's son Dhananjaya in battle?
Not even the bearer of the thunderbolt himself, let alone other archers. I think
he is a match for all three worlds."

While Bhishma was speaking, Karna angrily broke in. He looked at Duryodhana, and
spoke.

"There is no one in the world who does not know this, brahmin. What is the use
of saying it over and over? Long ago Shakuni beat Yudhishthira at dice for
Duryodhana's sake, and Pandu's son went to the forest by agreement. Now he wants
his father's kingdom back, and pays no heed to that agreement, relying on the
strength of the Matsyas and the Panchalas. Duryodhana would not give him so much
as a foot of ground out of fear, learned man. But by dharma he would give the
whole earth even to an enemy.

"If they want their fathers' and grandfathers' kingdom back, let them live in
the forest for the time they promised. Then let them live in Duryodhana's lap,
with nothing to fear. It is only out of folly that they would set their minds on
this lawless course. But if the Pandavas give up dharma and want war, when they
meet these best of the Kurus they will remember my words."

"What is the use of talk, Radheya?" said Bhishma. "You should remember that
deed, when the Partha, alone, beat six chariot fighters in battle. If we do not
do what this brahmin has said, we shall surely be killed by him in battle, and
eat the dust."

Then Dhritarashtra calmed Bhishma and won him over. He rebuked Radheya, and said
this. "What Bhishma, Shantanu's son, has said is for our good, for the Pandavas'
good, and for the good of the whole world. I will think it over and send Sanjaya
to the Parthas. Go back to the Pandavas today, without delay."

The Kaurava honoured him and sent him back to the Pandavas. Then he called
Sanjaya into the middle of the hall, and spoke to him.

**Divergences.**

* **Shalya's march to Hastinapura.** A rejected line (\*118) has Shalya march with his army to Hastinapura, shaking the earth. The CE names him among those who came to Duryodhana (5.19.16).
* **The king of the Yavanas.** A rejected line (\*119) has a king of the Yavanas come too. In the CE the Yavanas come with Sudakshina (5.19.21).
* **Bhishma's rebuke.** Rejected lines (\*120, \*121) have Bhishma ask why Karna did not stand firm in Virata's city, and \*122 has him foretell Duryodhana's end. Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines (\*123, \*124) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.19.3–4: the weapons are kept in the text's order, with plain English names for the Sanskrit ones (*paraśvadha* "axe", *bhiṇḍipāla* "short javelin", *śakti* "spear", *tomara* "lance", *mudgara* "club", *ṛṣṭi* "sword", *prāsa* "barbed spear", *niryūha* "helmet").
* 5.19.6, 22: an akshauhini entering a larger army "vanished, as a small river vanishes into the sea". The text says it of Satyaki's and of Sudakshina's armies.
* 5.19.15 *kāñcanair iva*: Bhagadatta's army "as if it were made of gold", like a grove of karnikara trees in flower.
* 5.19.23 *nīlāyudhaiḥ*: "men armed with blue weapons". The phrase plays on Nila's name.
* 5.19.24: the two kings of Avanti are not named here.
* 5.19.29–30: the places are kept as the text gives them. *yāmunaś caiva parvataḥ* is "the mountain by the Yamuna".
* 5.20.11 *yonyantaragatair iva*: "as if born into another womb", of the Pandavas living in disguise; *yathā pāpaiḥ*, "as the wicked do", of their suffering.
* 5.20.17–18: the priest counts Satyaki, Bhima and the twins as each "equal to a thousand akshauhinis", and Arjuna alone as a match for Duryodhana's eleven armies. Both are his boasts.
* 5.21.4 *atitīkṣṇaṃ tu te vākyaṃ brāhmaṇyād*: "your words are very sharp, because you are a brahmin".
* 5.21.14 *duryodhanasyāṅke*: "in Duryodhana's lap", under his protection.
* 5.21.16: "when the Partha, alone, beat six chariot fighters" is the battle for Virata's cattle (4.48–62).
* 5.21.17 *bhakṣayiṣyāma pāṃsukān*: "eat the dust".

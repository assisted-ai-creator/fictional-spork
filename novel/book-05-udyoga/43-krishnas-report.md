# Krishna's Report

When the tamer of enemies came from Hastinapura to Upaplavya, Keshava told the
Pandavas everything that had happened. He talked with them for a long time and
took counsel again and again. Then Shauri went to his own lodging to rest. The
five Pandava brothers sent away all the kings, with Virata at their head. When
the sun had set they said the evening prayers, thinking of nothing else. Then
they sent for Krishna the Dasharha and took counsel again.

"Lotus-eyed one," said Yudhishthira, "when you went to Nagapura, what did you
say to the son of Dhritarashtra in the assembly? Tell us."

"When I went to Nagapura," said Vasudeva, "I told the son of Dhritarashtra in
the assembly what was true, fitting and good, and the fool does not accept it."

"When he went astray, Hrishikesha," said Yudhishthira, "what did the old
grandfather of the Kurus say to resentful Duryodhana? What did the teacher, the
son of Bharadvaja, say, strong-armed one? What did our younger father say to the
son of Dhritarashtra, the steward, best of those who keep dharma, burning with
grief for his sons? And what did all the kings who sit in the assembly say,
truthfully? Tell me, Janardana. You have told me everything the two chief Kurus
said to that foolish man, overcome by desire and greed, who thinks himself wise.
What is unwelcome does not stay in my heart, Keshava. I want to hear their
words, Govinda, my lord. Act so that the time does not slip away, my friend. You
are our way, Krishna, you are our protector, you are our teacher."

"Listen, king," said Vasudeva, "to how King Suyodhana was spoken to in the
middle of the Kurus in the assembly, king of kings. When I had given my message,
the son of Dhritarashtra laughed. Then Bhishma, in great anger, said this.

"'Duryodhana, listen to what I say for the sake of the family. When you have
heard it, tiger among kings, do what is good for your family. My father, my son,
was King Shantanu, famous in the world. I was his only son, the best of those
who have sons. The thought came to him, "How can I have a second son? The wise
say a man with one son has no son. How can the family not be cut off, and its
fame spread?" When I knew what he wanted, I brought Kali to be my mother. I made
a hard vow, for my father's sake and the family's, that I would not be king and
would be chaste, as you know well. I live content, keeping the vow.

"'From her was born strong-armed, glorious Vichitravirya, the upholder of the
Kuru family, righteous, my younger brother and a king. When my father went to
heaven, I set Vichitravirya on the throne of his own kingdom, and I became his
servant, beneath him. I brought him wives fit for him, king of kings, after
conquering the gathered kings, as you have often heard. Then I fought Rama in
single combat. The townspeople sent Vichitravirya away for fear of Rama. He was
too attached to his wives, and he fell ill with consumption.

"'When the kingdom had no king and the lord of the gods sent no rain, the
people, tormented by hunger and fear, ran to me. "All the people are wasting
away," they said. "Be king, for our good. Drive off our troubles, good fortune
to you, you who make Shantanu's family grow. All your people are tormented by
terrible diseases, and few are left, son of Ganga. You should save them. Drive
off the diseases, hero, and protect the people by dharma. While you live, do not
let the kingdom perish."

"'When the people cried out, my mind was not shaken, for I kept my vow and
remembered the conduct of the good. Then the townspeople, great king, my good
mother Kali, the servants, the priests and teachers, and brahmins of great
learning, burning with distress, told me again and again, "Be king. The kingdom
that Pratipa protected will perish when it comes to you. For our good, be king,
wise one." When they said this, I joined my palms, grieving and deeply troubled,
and told them of the vow I had made out of reverence for my father, my son: to
be chaste and not to be king, for the sake of the family, again and again. Then
I joined my palms and begged my mother again and again, king. "Mother, I am born
of Shantanu and I carry on the Kaurava line. I will not make my vow false. Above
all for your sake, do not put me under the yoke. I am your servant and your
slave, mother, you who love your son."

"'When I had won over my mother and the people like this, I asked the great sage
Vyasa, with my mother, for children in my brother's wives. We won the seer over,
great king, and asked him for children, and he did us the favour. He fathered
three sons then, best of the Bharatas. Your father was blind and lacked the
sense, and so he was not king. Great, famous Pandu became king. He was king, and
his sons are heirs to their father's share. Do not quarrel, my son. Let half the
kingdom be given. While I live, what man here would rule the kingdom? Do not
scorn my words. I always want peace among you. I make no difference, my son,
between you and them, king. This is your father's view too, and Gandhari's and
Vidura's. If you should listen to the old, do not doubt my words. Do not destroy
everything, yourself and the earth.'

"When Bhishma had spoken," Vasudeva went on, "Drona, who knew how to speak, said
to Duryodhana in the middle of the kings, 'Good fortune to you. As Shantanu, son
of Pratipa, rose up for the sake of the family, my son, so Bhishma Devavrata
stood firm for the sake of the family. Then Pandu, king of the Kurus, true to
his word and master of his senses, righteous, strict in his vows and well
collected, who made the Kuru line grow, gave the kingdom to wise Dhritarashtra,
his elder brother, and to the steward, his younger one. When he had set
unfailing Dhritarashtra on the throne, king, the Kaurava went to the forest with
his two wives, blameless one. Vidura stood below and served him like one well
trained, like a servant, the tiger among men, raising the yak-tail fan. Then all
the people, my son, followed King Dhritarashtra as was right, as they had
followed King Pandu. When he had handed the kingdom to Dhritarashtra and to
Vidura, Pandu, conqueror of enemy cities, roamed the whole earth. In raising the
treasure, in giving, in seeing to the servants and in supporting everyone,
Vidura was true to his word. Mighty Bhishma, conqueror of enemy cities, saw to
peace and war and to the king's service. Mighty King Dhritarashtra sat on the
throne, always attended by great Vidura.

"'How can you, born of his family, set your mind on splitting the family? Join
with your brothers and enjoy your pleasures, lord of the people. I say this not
out of weakness, nor at all for the sake of gain. I eat what Bhishma gives me,
not what you give, best of kings. I shall not look to you for my living, lord of
the people. Where Bhishma is, there Drona is. Do what Bhishma says. Let half the
kingdom be given to the sons of Pandu, destroyer of enemies. My teaching has
always been the same for you and for them, my son. As Ashvatthama is to me, so
is the one with the white horses. What is the use of much talk? Where dharma is,
there is victory.'

"When Drona, whose power has no measure, had said this, great king," said
Vasudeva, "Vidura, true to his word, who knew dharma, turned and looked at his
father's face and spoke. 'Devavrata, listen to what I say. The Kaurava line was
lost, and you raised it up again. And you ignore my words as I lament. Who is
this Duryodhana, a disgrace to this family, whose mind you follow, a greedy,
base, ungrateful man whose heart is struck down by greed? He goes beyond the
teaching of his father, who sees dharma and gain. These Kurus are perishing
through Duryodhana's doing. Act, great king, so that they do not perish. You
made me and Dhritarashtra long ago, splendid one, as a painter makes a picture.
Do not destroy us, as Prajapati makes creatures and then destroys them.
Strong-armed one, do not look on while you see the family destroyed. Or if your
mind is lost today, now that ruin has come, go to the forest with me and with
Dhritarashtra. Or bind the wicked son of Dhritarashtra, whose wisdom is
trickery, and let this kingdom be well protected by the Pandavas today. Be
gracious, tiger among kings. Great destruction is in sight, for the Pandavas,
the Kurus and the kings of boundless power.' When he had said this, Vidura
stopped, downcast, lost in thought and sighing again and again.

"Then the daughter of King Subala, afraid for the ruin of the family, spoke in
anger to her son, wicked, cruel Duryodhana, before the kings, in words of dharma
and gain. 'Let the kings who have come into the royal hall, the brahmin seers
and the others who sit in the assembly, hear me tell your wrongdoing, evil as
you are, with your ministers and followers. The kingdom of the Kurus is enjoyed
in turn. That is the dharma of our family, handed down. You, with your evil mind
and cruel deeds, are destroying the Kuru kingdom by your folly. Wise
Dhritarashtra is on the throne, and far-sighted Vidura is his younger brother.
How can you pass over them and want the kingship now, Duryodhana, in your folly?
The king and the steward, great as they are, would be under Bhishma while he
lives. But he, the great-souled son of the river, the best of men, knows dharma
and does not want the kingdom. This kingdom is Pandu's, and no one can take it.
His sons are its masters now, and no others. This whole kingdom belongs to the
Pandavas, their grandfather's, passing to sons and grandsons. Whatever the great
chief of the Kurus, Devavrata, true and wise, says, we must all accept without
harming dharma, keeping to our own dharma. And with the leave of the one of the
great vow, let the king say what Vidura says. That should be done, with friends
set to it, putting dharma first, for a very long time. Let Yudhishthira, the son
of Dharma, rule this kingdom of the Kurus, which has come to him by right, urged
on by King Dhritarashtra and honoured by Shantanu's son.'

"When Gandhari had said this," said Vasudeva, "Dhritarashtra, lord of men, spoke
to Duryodhana among the kings, lord of men. 'Duryodhana, listen to what I tell
you, my son. Do it, good fortune to you, if you have any respect for your
father. Soma Prajapati was the first to make the Kuru line grow. Sixth from Soma
came Yayati, son of Nahusha. He had five sons, the best of royal seers. Mighty
Yadu was the eldest of them, a lord. Puru was younger, who made our line grow,
born of Sharmishtha, daughter of Vrishaparvan. Yadu, best of the Bharatas, was
the son of Devayani, grandson, my son, of Shukra Kavya, whose power had no
measure. The founder of the Yadava family was strong and honoured for his
courage. But the fool was full of pride and despised the kshatriyas. Deluded by
the pride of his strength, he did not stay under his father's rule. Unbeaten as
he was, he despised his father and his brothers. Yadu alone was powerful on the
earth to its four ends. He brought the kings under his power and lived in
Nagasahvaya. His father Yayati, son of Nahusha, was very angry. He cursed his
son, son of Gandhari, and put him out of the kingdom. The brothers who followed
him in his pride of strength, Yayati cursed in his anger too. Then the best of
kings set on the throne his younger son Puru, who was obedient and did as he was
told. So even an eldest son, if he is proud, does not come to the kingdom, and
younger sons come to it by serving their elders.

"'In the same way my father's grandfather Pratipa, who knew all dharma, was a
king famous in the three worlds. While that lion among kings ruled the kingdom
by dharma, three sons were born to him, like gods, and famous. Devapi was the
eldest, Bahlika the next, and the third, my son, was steady Shantanu, my
grandfather. Devapi was very splendid, the best of kings, but he had a disease
of the skin. He was righteous and truthful, and delighted in serving his father.
The townspeople and the country people valued him, and good men honoured him.
Devapi won the hearts of all, young and old. He was wise and true to his word
and delighted in the good of all beings. He kept to the teaching of his father
and of the brahmins. He was dear to his brother Bahlika and to great Shantanu,
and there was great love among the great brothers.

"'In the course of time the old king, the best of kings, had everything made
ready for the consecration, as the books lay down. The lord had all the lucky
rites done. But the brahmins and the old men, with the townspeople and country
people, all forbade Devapi's consecration. When the king heard that the
consecration was forbidden, his throat was choked with tears and he grieved for
his son. Though he was generous, knew dharma, kept his word and was dear to the
people, he was spoiled by the disease of his skin. "The gods do not welcome a
king who lacks a limb," said the bulls among brahmins, and so they forbade the
best of kings. Then the king was deeply troubled and full of grief for his son,
and he died. When he saw him dead, Devapi went to the forest. Bahlika gave up
the kingdom and lived in his mother's family's house. He left his father and
brothers and won a rich city. With Bahlika's leave, famous Shantanu ruled the
kingdom as king when his father had died, king.

"'In the same way, Bharata, wise Pandu thought it over, and I, the eldest, was
put out of the kingdom because I lacked a limb. Pandu got the kingdom, though he
was younger, and was king. On his death this kingdom belongs to his sons, tamer
of enemies. I have no share in the kingdom. How can you want it? Yudhishthira is
a king's son, great-souled, and this kingdom came to him by right. He is the
master of the Kaurava people, their great ruler. He keeps his word and is always
careful. He keeps to the books and is good to his kin. The people love him. He
is kind to his friends, masters his senses and supports the good. Patience,
forbearance, self-control and honesty, truth to his vows, learning and care,
pity for beings and good rule: all a king's virtues are in Yudhishthira. You are
not a king's son, and your conduct is base. You are greedy, and your mind is
wicked towards your kin. How can you, ill-bred as you are, take this kingdom
that has come to others by descent? Put aside your folly and give up half the
kingdom, with its animals and its goods. Then what remains of your life may be
yours, lord of men, with your younger brothers.'

"When Bhishma, Drona, Vidura, Gandhari and Dhritarashtra had said this," said
Vasudeva, "the fool did not understand. He shook them off and rose in anger, his
eyes red with rage, and the kings, who had given up their lives, ran after him.
He gave orders to those wicked-hearted kings again and again: 'March to
Kurukshetra. Today is Pushya.' Then those lords of the earth set out with their
armies, glad, driven by time, and made Bhishma their commander. Eleven armies of
kings have gathered. At their head shines Bhishma with his palm banner. Do what
is fitting and timely here, lord of the people.

"This is what Bhishma, Drona, Vidura, Gandhari and Dhritarashtra said in front
of me, Bharata. I have told you what happened in the Kuru assembly, king. First
I used conciliation, king, wanting brotherly love, so that the Kuru line would
not split and the people would prosper. When conciliation was not accepted, I
used division, and I told of your deeds, human and divine. When Suyodhana paid
no heed to my conciliating words, I brought all the kings together and split
them. I showed wonderful, terrible, dreadful deeds beyond the human, my lord,
Bharata. I rebuked those kings, treated Suyodhana as straw, and frightened
Radheya and Saubala again and again. I spoke again and again of the weakness of
Dhritarashtra's sons and blamed them, and I split all the kings with my words
and my counsel.

"Then I spoke again of giving, joined with conciliation, so that the Kuru line
would not split, and for the sake of the business. 'Let the Pandavas, children
as they are, give up their pride and stand below Dhritarashtra, Bhishma and
Vidura. Let them give you the kingdom and be without power. Let it be as King
Gangeya and Vidura say. Let the whole kingdom be yours. Let go only five
villages. For your father must surely support them, best of kings.' But when I
said this, the wicked man did not give up his purpose. Among those evil men I
see only the fourth way, the rod, and nothing else. The kings have set out for
Kurukshetra to their destruction. I have told you all that happened in the Kuru
assembly. They will not give you the kingdom without war, son of Pandu. They are
all causes of ruin, and death is waiting for them."

**Divergences.**

* **Two accounts of the hall.** Krishna's report does not match the scenes narrated at 5.122–129. In the report Bhishma tells his own history, Drona says he eats Bhishma's bread, Vidura appeals to Bhishma, Gandhari argues from the succession, and Dhritarashtra tells of Yadu and Devapi. None of these speeches appears in the earlier account, where the elders' words are different. Both accounts are CE, and each is kept as it is told.
* **Five villages again.** In his report Krishna says that at the last he offered that the Pandavas would give up the whole kingdom, live under the elders, and keep only five villages (5.148.14–16). This offer is not in the narrated scenes. See ledger L-174.
* **Duryodhana's order.** Duryodhana orders the kings to march to Kurukshetra, "Today is Pushya," with Bhishma as commander (5.148.3–5). Bhishma's formal appointment is told later (5.153). See TIMELINE.
* **Yadu's brothers.** A rejected line (\*537) names Druhyu, Anu and Puru as Yadu's brothers. The CE says Yayati had five sons, Yadu the eldest and Puru the youngest (5.147.4–5). Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*534–\*536, \*538 (Yadu destroying his family), \*539.

**Choices.**

* 5.145.8 *pitā yavīyān asmākam*: "our younger father", Vidura, their father's younger brother.
* 5.145.17 *ekaputram aputraṃ vai pravadanti*: "The wise say a man with one son has no son."
* 5.145.18 *kālīm*: Kali, Satyavati's name as the fisher girl.

* 5.146.17 *pitur vadanam anvīkṣya*: Vidura looks at "his father's face", that is, Bhishma's, his elder and guardian.
* 5.146.22 *citrakāra ivālekhyam*: "as a painter makes a picture".
* 5.146.29 *anupūrvabhogyam*: "enjoyed in turn", by the line of descent.
* 5.147.3: Soma is called Prajapati here, and Yayati is the sixth from him.

* 5.147.17 *tvagdoṣī*: "had a disease of the skin".
* 5.147.25 *hīnāṅgam*: "who lacks a limb", any bodily defect.
* 5.147.34 *arājaputraḥ*: "not a king's son", since Dhritarashtra was never king in his own right.
* 5.148.3 *puṣyo 'dya*: "Today is Pushya", the lunar mansion.
* 5.148.7–17: conciliation, division, gifts and the rod (*sāma*, *bheda*, *dāna*, *daṇḍa*) are the four ways of policy.

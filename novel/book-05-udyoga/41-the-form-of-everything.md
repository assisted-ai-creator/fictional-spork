# The Form of Everything

Ignoring the sensible words his mother had spoken, he went out again in his fury
to those who had not trained themselves. When he had left the hall, the Kaurava
took counsel with Shakuni, king, son of Subala, the master of dice. Duryodhana,
Karna, Shakuni son of Subala and Duhshasana, the fourth, made this plan.

"Janardana acts quickly. Before he and King Dhritarashtra and Shantanu's son
seize us, let us seize Hrishikesha ourselves, by force, the tiger among men, as
Indra seized Virochana's son. When the Pandavas hear that the Varshneya has been
seized, they will lose heart and lose their nerve, like snakes with their fangs
broken. This strong-armed one is the shelter and the armour of them all. When he
is seized, the giver of boons, the bull of all the Satvatas, the Pandavas and
the Somakas will give up the struggle. So let us bind Keshava, who acts so
quickly, here and now, while Dhritarashtra cries out, and then fight our
enemies."

Satyaki, the wise one who reads signs, quickly understood the evil plan of those
evil-minded men. He went out and, standing with Hardikya, said to Kritavarma,
"Quickly yoke the army. Stand at the door of the hall in armour, with the troops
drawn up, while I tell Krishna, whose deeds never fail." The hero went into the
hall like a lion into a mountain cave and told great Keshava of the plan. Then
he told Dhritarashtra and Vidura, smiling as he spoke.

"The fools want to do a thing here that is against dharma and purpose and blamed
by the good, and it can never be done. These deluded, wicked men have come
together, ruled by desire and anger, following fury and greed, and are about to
go wrong. These men of little mind want to seize the lotus-eyed one, like
children or idiots trying to catch a blazing fire in a cloth."

When he heard Satyaki's words, far-seeing Vidura said to strong-armed
Dhritarashtra in the assembly of the Kurus, "King, the time of your sons has run
out, scorcher of enemies. They are ready to do a deed that is shameful and
impossible. They want to gather together and overpower the lotus-eyed one by
force, Vasava's younger brother, and seize him, they say. When they come up
against this tiger among men, who cannot be overcome or approached, they will
cease to be, like moths in a fire. If he wished, Janardana in his anger could
send them all, struggling as they are, to Yama's house, as a lion sends deer.
But Krishna would never do a blameworthy deed. Achyuta, the best of men, would
never stray from dharma."

When Vidura had said this, Keshava looked at Dhritarashtra and spoke, while his
friends listened. "King, if these men in their anger want to seize me by force,
give me leave, king, and let them seize me, or let me seize them. I am able to
restrain them all in their fury. But I would never do a blameworthy, wicked
deed. Your sons, greedy for the Pandavas' goods, will lose their own. If they
want this, Yudhishthira's work is done. This very day I could seize these men
and all who follow them and give them to the Parthas, Bharata. What wrong would
there be in that? But I will not do this blameworthy deed in your presence,
great king, a deed born of anger and of an evil mind. Let it be as Duryodhana
wishes, king. For my part, I agree to all the terms, Bharata."

When he heard this, Dhritarashtra said to Vidura, "Bring that wicked Suyodhana
here quickly, greedy for the kingdom, with his friends, his counsellors, his
brothers and his followers. Perhaps I can bring him back to the road again."

Then the steward brought Duryodhana back into the hall again, unwilling, with
his brothers and surrounded by kings. Then King Dhritarashtra spoke to
Duryodhana, who was surrounded by Karna, Duhshasana and the kings.

"Cruel man, full of evil, with mean companions, you have joined with evil allies
and want to do an evil deed, impossible, shameful and blamed by the good, as
only a fool like you, a disgrace to the family, would decide. With your evil
allies you want to seize this lotus-eyed one, they say, who cannot be overcome
or approached. You want him, fool, who cannot be forced even by the gods with
Vasava, as a child wants the moon. You do not understand Keshava, whom gods,
men, gandharvas, asuras and serpents cannot withstand in battle. The wind is
hard to catch with the hand. The moon is hard to touch with the hand. The earth
is hard to carry on the head. Keshava is hard to seize by force."

When Dhritarashtra had said this, Vidura the steward too spoke, looking at
impatient Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra. "At the gate of Saubha the monkey
king named Dvivida covered Keshava with a great rain of stones. He wanted to
seize Madhava and tried with all his strength, and he could not seize him. And
you want to seize him by force. At Nirmochana six thousand great asuras bound
him with nooses and could not seize him. And you want to seize him by force.
When Shauri went to Pragjyotisha, Naraka and the danavas could not seize him.
And you want to seize him by force.

"As a child he killed Putana, and he held up Govardhana for the cows' sake, bull
of the Bharatas. Arishta and Dhenuka were killed, and mighty Chanura, the king
of horses, and Kamsa, who did evil. Jarasandha, Vakra and mighty Shishupala, and
Bana, were killed in battle, and kings were slain. King Varuna was conquered by
him of boundless energy, and the god of fire, and when he carried off the
parijata tree he conquered Shachi's husband himself. Lying on the single ocean
he killed Madhu and Kaitabha, and coming to another birth he killed Hayagriva.
He is the doer, and he is not done. He is the cause of manhood too. Whatever
Shauri wishes, he would do without effort. You do not understand Govinda,
terrible in courage, unfallen, like an angry poisonous snake, a heap of energy,
unconquered. If you attack strong-armed Krishna, whose deeds never fail, you and
your counsellors will cease to be, like a moth that comes to a fire."

---

When Vidura had said this, Keshava, the mighty slayer of hosts of enemies, spoke
to Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra.

"Because in your folly you think I am alone, Suyodhana, you look down on me and
want to seize me, fool. But here are all the Pandavas, and the Andhakas and
Vrishnis too. Here are the Adityas, the Rudras and the Vasus, with the great
seers."

When he had said this, Keshava, slayer of enemy heroes, laughed aloud. As great
Shauri smiled, the thirty gods, the size of a thumb, like lightning, let out
flames of fire. Brahma stood on his forehead and Rudra on his chest. The
guardians of the world were on his arms, and Agni came out of his mouth. The
Adityas and the Sadhyas, the Vasus and the Ashvins, the Maruts with Indra and
the Vishvedevas appeared, and the forms of yakshas, gandharvas and rakshasas.
From his two arms came Samkarshana and Dhananjaya, Arjuna the bowman on the
right and Rama the ploughman on the left. Bhima, Yudhishthira and the two sons
of Madri were behind him. The Andhakas and Vrishnis, with Pradyumna at their
head, were in front of Krishna, with great weapons raised. The conch, the
discus, the mace and the spear, the Sharnga bow, the plough and the sword
Nandaka were seen raised, and every weapon, blazing everywhere in Krishna's many
arms. From his eyes, his nose and his ears on every side came terrible flames of
fire with smoke, and from the pores of his skin, like the rays of the sun.

When they saw that terrible form of great Keshava, the kings shut their eyes,
their minds in terror, all except Drona and Bhishma, very wise Vidura, fortunate
Sanjaya and the seers rich in austerity, to whom the lord Janardana gave divine
sight. When they saw that great wonder of Madhava in the hall, the drums of the
gods sounded and a rain of flowers fell. The whole earth shook and the ocean was
stirred. The kings were struck with the greatest wonder, bull of the Bharatas.

Then that tiger among men, tamer of enemies, drew in his own form, heavenly,
wonderful, many-coloured and rich. Holding Satyaki and Hardikya by the hand,
with the leave of the seers, Madhusudana went out. The seers, Narada and the
rest, vanished and went away. In that uproar, it was a wonder. Seeing him leave,
the Kauravas and the kings followed the tiger among men, as the gods follow
Shatakratu. Thinking nothing of that whole circle of kings, Shauri, whose self
is beyond measure, went out like a fire with smoke.

Then Daruka appeared with his great bright chariot, with little bells, worked
with nets of gold, light, roaring like a cloud, well fitted, bright, covered
with tiger skins and protected, yoked with Sainya and Sugriva. In the same way
the great chariot fighter Kritavarma, Hardikya, the hero honoured by the
Vrishnis, appeared in his chariot.

As Shauri, tamer of enemies, stood by his chariot ready to leave, the great king
Dhritarashtra spoke to him again. "You see what power I have over my sons,
Janardana. It is plain to you. Nothing is hidden from you, slayer of enemies.
Knowing that I want peace for the Kurus and am trying for it, Keshava, and
seeing my position, you should not suspect me too much. I have no evil intent
towards the Pandavas, Keshava. You know what I said to Suyodhana. All the Kurus
and all the kings know, Madhava, that I am trying for peace with all my effort."

Then the strong-armed one said to Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, and to
Drona, the grandfather Bhishma, the steward, Bahlika and Kripa, "You have seen
for yourselves what happened in the Kuru assembly, how the fool rose in anger
again and again like an ill-bred man. King Dhritarashtra, lord of the earth,
says he has no power. I take leave of you all. I will go to Yudhishthira."

When he had taken his leave and stood in his chariot, ready to go, the best of
men was followed by the great archers, the heroes, the bulls of the Bharatas:
Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, the steward, Dhritarashtra and Bahlika, Ashvatthama,
Vikarna and the great chariot fighter Yuyutsu. Then, in his great bright chariot
with its little bells, while the Kurus watched, he went to see Pritha, his
father's sister.

**Divergences.**

* **Dhritarashtra's sight.** Rejected passages have Dhritarashtra ask for and receive sight to see the form, and then ask to be blind again (\*495–\*496). In the CE only Drona, Bhishma, Vidura, Sanjaya and the seers are given divine sight (5.129.13). Ledger L-177.
* **Govardhana.** Vidura mentions that Krishna held up Govardhana (5.128.45). The CE does not narrate it. Ledger L-33.
* Other rejected lines (\*491–\*494, \*497–\*499) add detail. Not narrated.

* 5.128.5 *indro vairocaniṃ yathā*: "as Indra seized Virochana's son", Bali.
* 5.128.41: Dvivida, the monkey at the gate of Saubha.

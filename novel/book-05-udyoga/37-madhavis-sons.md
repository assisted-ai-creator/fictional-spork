# Madhavi's Sons

"'King Haryashva thought about it in many ways," said Narada. "Then the best of
kings sighed long and hot, wanting offspring. "She is high in the six places
that should be high, fine in the seven that should be fine, deep in the three
that should be deep, and red in the five. Many gods and asuras would gaze at
her, and many gandharvas. She has many lucky marks and will bear many children.
She can bear a son who will rule the world. Tell me the price, best of brahmins,
bearing my wealth in mind."

"'"Give me eight hundred horses," said Galava, "white as the moon, each with one
black ear, born in this land, fine in body. Then this lovely woman with the long
eyes will be the mother of your sons, as the fire-stick is the source of fires."

"'When he heard this, King Haryashva, the royal seer, blinded by desire, said
miserably to Galava, the best of seers, "I have two hundred horses of the kind
you want at hand. Others of mine, which are wanted too, roam in hundreds. So I
will father one child on her, Galava. Grant me this wish."

"'When she heard this, the girl said to Galava, "A boon was given to me by a
certain speaker of Brahman, that after each birth I would become a maiden again.
So give me to the king and take the fine horses. From four kings you will have
the full eight hundred, and I will have four sons. Let the price for your
teacher be gathered through me, best of brahmins. That is my thought, or do as
you think fit, brahmin."

"'When the girl said this, the sage Galava said to Haryashva, lord of the earth,
"Take this girl, best of men, Haryashva. Father one son for a quarter of the
price."

"'He took the girl and thanked Galava, and at the right time and place he had
the son he wanted. He was the king named Vasumanas, richer than the Vasus, like
a Vasu, a giver of wealth. In time wise Galava came back to Haryashva, who was
glad at heart, and said, "Your son is born, king, like the young sun. It is time
to go to another king to beg, best of men." Haryashva stood by his word, stood
by his manhood, and since the horses were so hard to get, he gave Madhavi back.
Madhavi gave up her blazing royal fortune, became a maiden again at will, and
followed Galava. "Let the horses stay with you for now," said the brahmin, and
he set out with the girl to Divodasa, lord of the people.

---

"'"There is a mighty king, lord of the Kashis," said Galava, "named Divodasa,
son of Bhimasena, a king of men. Let us go there, good lady. Come slowly and do
not grieve. That lord of the people keeps dharma, is self-controlled and true."

"'The sage came to him and was honoured as was proper, and Galava urged the king
to have a child.

"'"I heard of this before," said Divodasa. "Why tell it at length, brahmin? I
wanted this when I heard of it, best of brahmins. And I value it highly that you
passed over other kings and came to me like this. It is sure to happen. I have
the same wealth in horses, Galava. I too will father one king on her."

"'"Very well," said the best of brahmins, and gave the girl to the lord of the
earth, and the king took the girl by the rule. The royal seer delighted in her
as the sun in Prabhavati, as fire in Svaha, as Vasava in Shachi. He delighted in
her as the moon in Rohini, as Yama in Dhumorna, as Varuna in Gauri, as the lord
of wealth in Riddhi. He delighted in her as Narayana in Lakshmi, as the ocean in
Jahnavi, as Rudra in Rudrani, as the grandfather in the altar. So Vasishtha's
son delighted in Adrishyanti, and Vasishtha in Akshamala, Chyavana in Sukanya
and Pulastya in Sandhya. So Agastya delighted in the princess of Vidarbha,
Satyavan in Savitri, Bhrigu in Puloma and Kashyapa in Aditi. So Richika
delighted in Renuka, Kaushika in Haimavati, Brihaspati in Tara and Shukra in
Shataparva. So the lord of the earth delights in the earth, Pururavas in
Urvashi, Richika in Satyavati and Manu in Sarasvati. As King Divodasa delighted
in her, Madhavi bore him one son, Pratardana.

"'Then, when the time came, holy Galava came to Divodasa and said, "Give me back
the girl, lord of the earth, and let the horses stay with you while I go
elsewhere for the price." Divodasa, whose soul was dharma, stood by his word and
gave the girl back to Galava at the agreed time.

---

"'In the same way the famous Madhavi, true to her promise, gave up her fortune,
became a maiden again, and followed Galava the brahmin. Galava, thinking only of
his task, went to the city of the Bhojas to see King Aushinara. He went to that
king of true courage and said, "This girl will bear you two sons who will be
kings. With her you will win your aim, here and after death, when you have
fathered two sons like the moon and the sun, king. But the price, you who know
all dharma, is four hundred horses white as the moon, each with one black ear,
to be given to me. This effort is for my teacher. I have no use for the horses
myself. If you can, great king, do it, and do not hesitate. You have no
children, royal seer. Father two sons, king, and save your ancestors and
yourself with the boat of sons. A royal seer who enjoys the fruit of sons does
not fall from heaven, nor go to the terrible hell where the childless go."

"'When he heard this and much else that Galava said, King Ushinara answered him.
"I have heard your words, Galava, as you speak them. Fate is strong, brahmin,
and my mind leans that way. I have two hundred horses like that, best of the
twice-born. Many thousands of others roam about. I too will father one son on
her, Galava. I will go the road others have gone, brahmin. I would do the same
for you even at a price, best of brahmins. But my wealth is for the people of
the city and the country, not for my own enjoyment. A king who gives away
others' wealth as he pleases does not win dharma, you whose soul is dharma, nor
fame. So I will accept her. Give me this girl, who shines like a child of the
gods, for one son."

"'Galava, the best of brahmins, honoured King Ushinara for these many good
words, gave her to him, and went to the forest. The king delighted in her as a
man of good deeds delights in fortune. He delighted in her in the caves of
mountains and the falls of rivers, in lovely gardens, forests and groves, in
pleasant mansions and on the tops of palaces, in rooms with windows and in inner
chambers. In time a son was born to him, bright as the young sun, famous by the
name of Shibi, the best of kings. Galava the brahmin came to him, took the girl
back and went on, king, and saw Vinata's son.

---

"'Vinata's son laughed and said to Galava, "How lucky that I see you here with
your aim achieved, brahmin." When Galava heard Vinata's son, he told him that a
quarter of his task was left. The Suparna, best of birds, said to Galava, "You
should not try any more. It will not come to pass for you. Long ago, in
Kanyakubja, Richika asked Gadhi for his daughter Satyavati as his wife, Galava,
and Gadhi told him, 'Give me a thousand horses, lord, white as the moon, each
with one black ear.' Richika said, 'Very well,' went to Varuna's dwelling, got
the horses at Ashvatirtha and gave them to the king. The king performed the
Pundarika sacrifice and gave them to the twice-born. The kings bought two
hundred each from them, and so got them. Another four hundred, best of brahmins,
were being taken across a river when the Vitasta carried them off. So what
cannot be had can never be had, Galava. Give this girl to Vishvamitra for two
hundred horses, along with the six hundred horses, you whose soul is dharma.
Then you will be free of your confusion, your task done, bull among brahmins."

"'Galava said, "So be it," and went with the Suparna to Vishvamitra, taking the
horses and the girl. "Here are six hundred of the horses you wanted," said
Galava. "Take this girl for the two hundred. Three sons who keep dharma have
been born of her to royal seers. Father a fourth on her yourself, best of men.
So let the full eight hundred horses be yours. When I am free of debt to you, I
will practise austerity as I please."

"'When Vishvamitra saw Galava with the bird, and that lovely girl, he said, "Why
was she not given to me here at the start, Galava? Then four sons would have
been mine, to carry on my family. I accept your girl for the fruit of one son.
Let the horses all stay in my hermitage." Glorious Vishvamitra delighted in her
and fathered a son on her, Ashtaka, Madhavi's son. As soon as the son was born,
glorious Vishvamitra joined him to purpose and dharma, and to those horses. Then
Ashtaka went to a city as bright as the city of Soma. Kaushika gave the girl
back to his pupil and went to the forest.

"'When he had paid the fee with the Suparna, Galava spoke to the girl with a
glad heart. "You have borne one son who is a lord of giving, another who is a
hero, another devoted to truth and dharma, and another who sacrifices. So come,
lovely one. Your father has been saved by your sons, and so have the four kings,
and so have I, slender-waisted one." Galava took leave of the Suparna, the eater
of snakes, gave the girl back to her father, and went off to the forest.

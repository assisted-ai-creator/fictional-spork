# The Armies Gather

Then the hero Yuyudhana, the great chariot fighter of the Satvatas, came to
Yudhishthira with a great army of all four arms. His fighting men were very
brave, gathered from many lands, and they made his army splendid with weapons of
every kind. They carried axes and bhindipalas, spears, lances and hammers,
pikes, swords, battle-axes and darts, and bright, clean blades. They had swords,
bows and helmets, and arrows of every kind, washed in oil and shining. The army
looked like a dark cloud, and with those weapons shining in it, it was like a
cloud with lightning. It was an akshauhini, a full army. It went into
Yudhishthira's army and vanished there, king, like a small river into the ocean.

In the same way the mighty bull of the Chedis, Dhrishtaketu, came to the
Pandavas, whose energy is boundless, with an akshauhini. Mighty Jayatsena of
Magadha, Jarasandha's son, came to the Dharma King with an akshauhini of troops.
The Pandya came to Yudhishthira too, king of kings, surrounded by fighting men
of many kinds who lived along the shore of the sea. At that great gathering of
armies his army was the finest to look at, king, well dressed and strong.
Drupada's army too was splendid with brave men gathered from many lands, and
with his sons, the great chariot fighters. Virata, king of the Matsyas, lord of
an army, came to the Pandavas in the same way, with the kings of the mountains.

From here and there, seven akshauhinis gathered for the great Pandavas, crowded
with banners of every kind. Eager to fight the Kurus, they made the Pandavas
glad.

---

In the same way King Bhagadatta added to the joy of Dhritarashtra's son by
giving him an akshauhini. His army was crowded with Chinas and Kiratas, who
looked as if they were made of gold, and that army no one could attack shone
like a forest of karnikara trees in flower. Brave Bhurishravas and Shalya came
to Duryodhana, joy of the Kurus, each with his own akshauhini. Kritavarma,
Hridika's son, came to Duryodhana with an akshauhini, with the forces of the
Bhojas and Andhakas. With those tigers among men in their forest garlands his
army shone like a forest where elephants in rut are at play.

Other kings came, with Jayadratha at their head, those who live in Sindhu and
Sauvira, seeming to shake the mountains. Their large akshauhini shone like
many-shaped clouds driven by the wind. Sudakshina of the Kambojas came to the
Kaurava with an akshauhini, with Yavanas and Shakas, lord of the people. His
gathered army looked like a swarm of locusts, and when it reached the Kaurava,
it vanished into his army. King Nila, who lived in Mahishmati, came with the
mighty fighters of the southern road, whose weapons were dark blue. The two
kings of Avanti, surrounded by a great force, came to Suyodhana, each with an
akshauhini. The tigers among men, the Kekayas, five royal brothers, rushed to
the Kaurava with an akshauhini, making him glad. From here and there, from all
the other great kings, three more armies came together, bull of the Bharatas.

So Duryodhana had eleven armies gathered round him, eager to fight the sons of
Kunti and crowded with banners of every kind. There was no room in Hastinapura
then, king, even for the chief kings and their leading troops. The land of the
five rivers, the whole of Kurujangala, the forest of Rohitaka, the whole of the
desert, Ahichchhatra, Kalakuta, the bank of the Ganga, Varana, Vatadhana and the
mountain by the Yamuna, all that wide country, rich in wealth and grain, was
crowded with the army of the Kauravas.

The priest whom the Panchala king had sent to the Kauravas saw that army
gathered there.

---

Drupada's priest came to the Kaurava and was honoured by Dhritarashtra, by
Bhishma and by Vidura. First he gave news of all their welfare and asked after
theirs. Then he spoke in the middle of all the leaders of the army.

"You all know the eternal dharma of kings," he said. "Even so, I shall speak of
it, since it is the reason for my words. Dhritarashtra and Pandu are known as
sons of one father. Their share of their father's wealth is equal, no doubt of
that. The sons of Dhritarashtra have their father's wealth. How is it, then,
that the sons of Pandu do not have their father's wealth?

"You know how things stood before. The Pandavas did not get their father's
property, because Dhritarashtra's son kept it from them. He tried many times, by
means meant to kill, but he could not send them to Yama's house, for their time
was not up. Then the great ones made the kingdom grow again by their own
strength, and the mean sons of Dhritarashtra, with Subala's son, took it away by
a trick. This man here approved of that deed, and so it was done. They were made
to live thirteen years in the great forest.

"In the hall those heroes and their wife were badly abused. In the forest they
met with many hardships, all of them terrible. In Virata's city too, though they
were great, they suffered the worst hardship, like evil men reborn in another
womb. Putting all of that behind them, all those old wrongs, the bulls of the
Kurus want only peace with the Kurus. Knowing how they have behaved, and how
Duryodhana has behaved, the friends here should plead with Dhritarashtra.

"The heroes are not making war on the Kurus. The Pandavas want what is theirs
without the destruction of the world. And whatever reason Dhritarashtra's son
may have for war, he should not count on it, for they are stronger. Seven
akshauhinis have gathered for the son of Dharma. They want to fight the Kurus,
and they are waiting for his command. And there are other tigers among men, each
equal to a thousand akshauhinis: Satyaki and Bhimasena, and the twins, so
mighty. On one side these eleven armies have gathered. On the other side is
strong-armed Dhananjaya, who takes many forms. Just as the wearer of the crown
stands above all the armies, so does strong-armed Vasudeva, so glorious. Knowing
the size of the armies, the courage of the wearer of the crown and the wisdom of
Krishna, what man would fight? So give what ought to be given, as dharma
requires and as the pact requires. Do not let the time pass you by."

---

Bhishma, old in wisdom and full of glory, listened to his words. He honoured the
priest, as the time called for, and spoke.

"How lucky that all the Pandavas are well, with their kin. How lucky that they
have allies, and that they delight in dharma. How lucky that the brothers, the
joys of the Kurus, want peace. How lucky that they and Damodara are not set on
war. Everything you have said is true, no doubt of it. But I think your words
are too sharp. That is because you are a brahmin. No doubt the Pandavas suffered
both in the forest and here. No doubt they have a right by dharma to all their
father's wealth. The wearer of the crown, the Partha, is strong, skilled in
weapons and mighty. Who could stand against Dhananjaya, son of Pandu, in battle?
Not even the wielder of the thunderbolt himself, let alone other bowmen. I think
he is a match for all three worlds."

While Bhishma was speaking, Karna angrily and rudely cut him short. Looking at
Duryodhana, he spoke.

"There is no being in the world who does not know this, brahmin. What is the
point of saying it again and again? Long ago Shakuni beat him at dice for
Duryodhana's sake. Yudhishthira, son of Pandu, went to the forest by an
agreement. Now he ignores the agreement and wants his father's kingdom, relying
on the strength of the Matsyas and the Panchalas. Duryodhana will not give up
even a foot of land out of fear, learned man. But by dharma he would give the
whole earth even to an enemy. If they want their father's and grandfather's
kingdom back, let them go to the forest and live out that time as they promised.
Then let them come and live in Duryodhana's lap with nothing to fear. It is only
out of folly that they have this thought, which is against dharma. And if the
Pandavas give up dharma and want war, when they meet these best of the Kurus,
they will remember my words."

"What is the use of your words, son of Radha?" said Bhishma. "You ought to
remember that deed, when the Partha on his own beat six chariot fighters in
battle. If we do not do what this brahmin has said, we will surely be killed by
him in battle and eat dust."

Then Dhritarashtra calmed Bhishma and won him round, and rebuked the son of
Radha. "Bhishma, Shantanu's son, has said what is good for us," he said, "and
good for the Pandavas, and for the whole world. When I have thought it over, I
shall send Sanjaya to the Parthas. As for you, go back today to the Pandavas,
without delay."

The Kaurava honoured the priest and sent him back to the Pandavas. Then he
called Sanjaya to the middle of the hall and spoke to him.

# The Choice at Dvaraka

When Krishna had gone to Dvaravati, and Balarama with him, with all the Vrishnis
and Andhakas and the Bhojas by the hundred, King Duryodhana, son of
Dhritarashtra, learned everything the Pandavas were doing, through the spies and
agents he had placed.

When he heard that Krishna had gone, he set off for the city of Dvaraka with
fine horses swift as the wind, and with a force that was not very large. That
same day Arjuna, son of Kunti, went quickly to the lovely city of the Anartas.

The two tigers among men, the two sons of the Kurus, reached Dvaraka and found
Krishna asleep. They went in to where he lay. Duryodhana went in first, while
Govinda slept, and sat down on a fine seat at Krishna's head. Then great-minded
Arjuna came in after him, and stood at Krishna's feet, bowing, with his palms
joined.

When the Vrishni woke, he saw Arjuna first. He welcomed them both and honoured
each as was fitting, and asked them why they had come.

Duryodhana spoke to Krishna with something like a laugh. "You should give me
your help in this war. You are friends with me and with Arjuna alike. Our ties
of kinship with you are the same too, Madhava. And today I came to you first.
Good men stand by whoever comes to them first, as the first to ask. You are the
best of the good in the world today, Janardana, and always respected. Keep to
the conduct of the good."

"You came first," said Krishna. "I have no doubt of it, king. But I saw Arjuna,
son of Pritha, first. Because you came first, and because I saw him first, I
will help you both, Suyodhana. But the ancient teaching says that the younger
should be given the first choice. So Arjuna, son of Pritha, has the right to
choose first.

"I have a great force of cowherds, a whole arbuda of them, as strong in body as
I am, all fighters in battle, and they are known as the Narayanas. Let those
soldiers, hard to beat in battle, go to one of you. On the other side I shall be
alone, and I shall not fight in the battle, and I shall lay down my weapons.
Partha, choose first whichever of these two your heart likes better. By dharma
the first choice is yours."

When Krishna said this, Arjuna, son of Kunti, chose Keshava, who would not fight
in the battle.

Duryodhana got the army, a thousand thousand fighting men. Knowing that Krishna
had been carried off, he felt the greatest joy.

---

King Duryodhana, terrible in strength, took all that army with him and went to
mighty Balarama, son of Rohini. He told him everything, and why he had come.

Balarama answered the son of Dhritarashtra. "You must know all that I said
earlier, tiger among men, at Virata's, at the wedding. For your sake I checked
Hrishikesha, and told him again and again that our kinship with both sides is
the same. But Keshava did not accept what I said. And I cannot stay apart from
Krishna for even a moment. So, looking to Vasudeva, I have made up my mind: I
will help neither the Parthas nor Duryodhana. You were born in the line of
Bharata, which all the kings honour. Go, bull of the Bharatas, and fight by
dharma, the dharma of the kshatriya."

When he heard this, Duryodhana embraced the plough-bearer. Knowing that Krishna
had been carried off, he thought the war as good as won.

Then King Duryodhana went to Kritavarma, and Kritavarma gave him an army, a full
akshauhini. With that whole terrible army around him the son of the Kurus went
home glad, and made his friends glad too.

---

When Duryodhana had gone, Krishna said to the wearer of the diadem, "Why did you
choose me, when I will not fight?"

"You could kill them all," said Arjuna. "There is no doubt of it. And I alone
could kill them too, best of men. But you are famous in the world, and the glory
would go to you. I want glory too. That is why I chose you. It has always been
in my mind that you should drive my chariot. I have wanted it for a long time.
Grant me that wish."

"It is fitting that you should want to rival me, Partha," said Vasudeva. "I will
drive your chariot. Let your wish come true."

So Partha was glad, and with Krishna and the best of the Dasharhas around him,
he went back to Yudhishthira.

---

When Shalya heard the envoys, he set out for the Pandavas with a great army and
with his sons, all great chariot fighters. His camp stretched for about a yojana
and a half, so large was the army that bull among men led. His heroes had bright
armour, bright banners and bows, bright ornaments, bright chariots and horses.
They wore the dress and ornaments of their own country, a hundred thousand
heroes, and bulls among kshatriyas led his army. He marched slowly, resting his
army as he went, towards the Pandava, and it seemed as though creatures quailed
and the earth shook.

Duryodhana heard that the great chariot fighter was coming with his great army.
He hurried to meet him, and honoured him himself. He had halls built to honour
him in pleasant places, bright with jewels and well adorned. Shalya came to
those halls one after another, and Duryodhana's ministers honoured him in each
place as he deserved, as if he were one of the immortals. Then he came to
another hall, as splendid as a dwelling of the gods.

There, among pleasures that were fine and more than human, he thought himself
greater than he was, and thought little of Indra, the destroyer of cities. In
his delight the bull among kshatriyas asked the servants, "Which of
Yudhishthira's men built these halls? Bring the men who made them. I think they
deserve a reward."

Duryodhana, who had kept himself hidden, then showed himself to his mother's
brother. When the king of the Madras saw him and understood the pains he had
taken, he embraced him and said, pleased, "Take whatever you want."

"Let your word be true, noble one," said Duryodhana. "Grant me a boon. Be the
leader of all my army."

"It is done," said Shalya. "What else shall I do?"

"It is done," said the son of Gandhari, again and again.

So Duryodhana took his leave of Shalya and went back to his own city. And Shalya
went to the sons of Kunti to tell them what he had done.

---

Shalya came to Upaplavya, entered the camp and saw all the Pandavas there. The
strong-armed king met the sons of Pandu, and took water for his feet, the guest
water and a cow, as the rites require. Then the king of the Madras, destroyer of
enemies, asked after their welfare, and embraced Yudhishthira with the greatest
affection. He embraced Bhima and Arjuna too, and his sister's sons, the twins,
who were glad to see him. When he had sat down, Shalya spoke to the Partha.

"Are you well, tiger among kings, son of the Kurus? What good fortune that you
are free of your life in the forest, best of victors. You did a very hard thing,
king, living in the lonely forest with your brothers, and with Krishna here. And
the terrible year in hiding was harder still. There is only sorrow, not
happiness, for a king who has lost his kingdom. But when you have killed your
enemies, king, you will have happiness in return for this great sorrow the sons
of Dhritarashtra caused you. You know the ways of the world, great king, and so
nothing in you is ruled by greed."

Then the king told him of his meeting with Duryodhana, and of all the attention
he had been paid, and of the boon he had given.

"You did well, king," said Yudhishthira, "to give Duryodhana your word with a
glad heart. But there is one thing I would like you to do for me. In battle you
are Vasudeva's equal, great king. When Karna and Arjuna meet in single combat,
chariot against chariot, you will certainly have to drive Karna's chariot. If
you want to do me a kindness, king, protect Arjuna then. Break the spirit of the
suta's son, for that will bring us victory. It is not a thing a man should do,
uncle, but I ask you to do it."

"Listen, Pandava," said Shalya. "You ask me to break the spirit of that wicked
man, the suta's son, in battle. I will certainly be his charioteer in the
battle, for he always thinks me Vasudeva's equal. And when he wants to fight, I
will certainly say things to him that go against him and do him harm, tiger of
the Kurus, so that his pride and his spirit are broken, Pandava, and he is easy
to kill. I tell you this truly. I will do it as you ask, my son. And whatever
else I can do for you, I will do.

"The sorrow you suffered at the dice with Krishna, and the harsh words of the
suta's son, the trouble from Jatasura and from Kichaka, all that Draupadi went
through, as Damayanti did, all this sorrow will end in happiness, hero. Do not
be angry at it, for fate is stronger. Great men meet with sorrows, Yudhishthira.
Even the gods have met with sorrows, lord of the earth. It is told, king, that
great Indra, the king of the gods, went through great sorrow, and his wife with
him."

**Divergences.**

* **Arjuna's journey.** A rejected passage (\*32) has the Pandavas send out their envoys and Arjuna go to Dvaraka himself as a result. In the CE Duryodhana learns of Krishna's departure through spies, and Arjuna goes the same day (5.7.2–4).
* **"Welcome, son of Gandhari."** The well-known exchange in which Krishna, waking, greets Duryodhana and says he had not seen him, and Duryodhana says he came first to see him, is a rejected passage (\*38). So are the lines that have Krishna see Duryodhana after Arjuna (\*34–\*37).
* **Krishna asleep.** The CE says he was asleep (5.7.5 *suptaṃ … śayānaṃ*). It does not say he was pretending.
* **Why Arjuna chose.** Rejected lines (\*40, \*41) have Krishna tell Arjuna to think it over, and call him Narayana, "born by his own will". In the CE Arjuna simply chooses (5.7.19). His reason, and his wish that Krishna drive his chariot, are CE (5.7.31–35).
* **Shalya's feast.** Rejected lines (\*46–\*51) add the halls' food, wells and pools, Duryodhana's ministers going over to him, and Shalya's astonishment. \*53 adds a long leave-taking. In the CE Shalya grants the boon and the two say "It is done" (5.8.13).
* **Shalya's counsel.** A long rejected passage (\*59, \*60) has Shalya praise Yudhishthira's virtues. Not narrated. The rejected line \*64 has Yudhishthira add that Vasudeva will drive for Arjuna. Other rejected lines (\*33, \*39, \*42–\*45, \*52, \*54–\*58, \*61–\*63, \*65–\*67) add details. Not narrated.
* **Ledger.** The choice at Dvaraka is L-171. Shalya won by the feast and asked to break Karna's spirit is L-172.

**Choices.**

* 5.7.6–7: Duryodhana sits at the head, *ucchīrṣataḥ*, on a fine seat. Arjuna stands *paścārdhe*, at the foot. The CE gives no reason for either. The novel adds none.
* 5.7.15 *pravāraṇaṃ tu bālānāṃ pūrvaṃ kāryam iti śrutiḥ*: "the ancient teaching says that the younger should be given the first choice".
* 5.7.16 *nārāyaṇā iti khyātāḥ*: the cowherds "are known as the Narayanas". *arbuda* is kept as the text's number word.
* 5.7.20 *sahasrāṇāṃ sahasraṃ*: "a thousand thousand fighting men".
* 5.7.20, 5.7.28 *kṛṣṇaṃ cāpahṛtaṃ jñātvā*: "knowing that Krishna had been carried off". The text does not say whether Duryodhana is glad in spite of this, or because Krishna is now out of the fighting. The novel keeps the bare words.
* 5.7.24 *nigṛhyokto*: "I checked Hrishikesha and told him".
* 5.8.2 *adhyardham iva yojanam*: "about a yojana and a half".
* 5.8.9: Shalya "thought himself greater than he was" renders *mene 'bhyadhikam ātmānam*.
* 5.8.34 *damayantyā yathāśubham*: Draupadi's sufferings are compared with Damayanti's (3.50–78).
* 5.8.37 leads into the story of Indra, which Shalya tells in the next chapter.

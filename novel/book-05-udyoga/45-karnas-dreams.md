# Karna's Dreams

When he heard Karna's words, Keshava, slayer of enemy heroes, laughed, and
smiling first he said this.

"Does the offer of winning a kingdom not tempt you, Karna? You do not want to
rule the earth when I give it to you. The Pandavas' victory is certain. There is
no doubt of it at all. The Pandava's banner of victory can be seen, raised high,
the fierce king of monkeys. Bhauvana has made a heavenly magic, raised high,
shining like Indra's standard. Heavenly creatures can be seen on it, frightening
and terrible. Dhananjaya's glorious banner, Karna, raised high, like fire in
form, catches on no rock or tree, though it stretches a yojana up and across.

"You will see in battle the one with white horses and Krishna for his
charioteer, working the Indra weapon and those of fire and wind. You will hear
the twang of Gandiva like the crack of lightning. Then there will be no Treta
age, no Krita and no Dvapara. You will see in battle Kunti's son Yudhishthira,
busy with prayers and offerings, guarding his own great army, hard to withstand
like the sun, burning the enemy host. Then there will be no Treta, no Krita and
no Dvapara. You will see in battle mighty Bhimasena dancing in the fight after
drinking Duhshasana's blood, like an elephant in rut that kills rival elephants.
Then there will be no Treta, no Krita and no Dvapara. You will see in battle
Madri's two sons, the great chariot fighters, churning up the army of
Dhritarashtra's sons like elephants. They will break the chariots of enemy
heroes when the clash of weapons is deep. Then there will be no Treta, no Krita
and no Dvapara. You will see in battle Drona, Shantanu's son, Kripa, King
Suyodhana and Jayadratha of Sindhu rushing quickly to fight, held back by the
left-handed archer. Then there will be no Treta, no Krita and no Dvapara.

"Karna, when you go from here, tell Drona, Shantanu's son and Kripa, 'This is a
pleasant month, with fodder and fuel easy to find. It is rich with ripe plants
and woods, full of fruit, with few flies and without mud. Its water is tasty,
and it is neither too hot nor too cold. In seven days' time it will be the new
moon. Let the battle be joined then, for they say Shakra is its god.' Tell all
the kings who have come to fight, 'I will bring about all that you have in
mind.' The kings and princes who follow Duryodhana's will shall meet their end
by weapons and reach the highest state."

---

When Karna heard those good and kind words of Keshava, he honoured Krishna,
slayer of Madhu, and said, "Strong-armed one, why do you want to confuse me,
when you know? This destruction of the whole earth that has come is caused by
Shakuni, by me, by Duhshasana, and by King Duryodhana, son of Dhritarashtra. No
doubt, Krishna, this great war has come between the Pandavas and the Kurus,
terrible, with a mire of blood. The kings and princes who follow Duryodhana's
will shall be burnt in battle by the fire of weapons and go to Yama's house.

"Many terrible dreams are seen, Madhusudana, and terrible signs and very
dreadful portents. They seem to foretell defeat for Dhritarashtra's son and
victory for Yudhishthira, Varshneya, in many ways that make the hair stand on
end. The fierce, bright planet Saturn afflicts the constellation of Prajapati,
and afflicts living creatures more. Mars turns back in Jyeshtha, Madhusudana,
and seeks Anuradha, as if to put an end to friendship. Surely great danger has
come upon the Kurus, Krishna, for a planet afflicts Chitra especially,
Varshneya. The mark on the moon has turned round, and Rahu will go to the sun.
Meteors fall from the sky with thunder and earthquakes. Elephants moan, horses
shed tears and take no pleasure in water or fodder, Madhava. When these signs
appear, they say, terrible danger has come, strong-armed one, the destruction of
living things.

"Among the horses, elephants and men in all the armies of Dhritarashtra's son,
Keshava, much dung is seen though they have eaten little, Madhusudana. The wise
say that is a mark of defeat. They say the Pandavas' animals are joyful,
Krishna, and the beasts go round them keeping them on the right, a mark of their
victory. All the beasts go round Dhritarashtra's son keeping him on the left,
Keshava, and there are voices without bodies. That is a mark of defeat.
Peacocks, birds of flowering trees, swans, cranes, chatakas and flocks of
jivajivakas follow the Pandavas. Vultures, crows, badas, hawks, yatudhanas,
jackals and swarms of flies follow the Kauravas. In the armies of
Dhritarashtra's son there is no sound from the drums, while the Pandavas' drums
sound, they say, without being struck. The wells roar like bulls in the armies
of Dhritarashtra's son. That is a mark of defeat.

"The god has rained flesh and blood, Madhava. A shining city of the gandharvas
has appeared, with walls, moats, ramparts and lovely gateways. A black bar
stands there covering the sun, at the twilight of sunrise and sunset,
foretelling great danger. A single she-jackal howls terribly. That is a mark of
defeat. Frightening birds with black necks, drooping, fly towards the twilight.
That is a mark of defeat. He hates brahmins first, Madhusudana, and his
teachers, and his loyal servants. That is a mark of defeat. The eastern quarter
is the colour of blood, Madhusudana, the southern the colour of weapons, and the
western like an unbaked pot. All the quarters of Dhritarashtra's son are
blazing, Madhava. In that sign of portents they foretell great danger.

"At the end of a dream I saw Yudhishthira with his brothers climbing a palace
with a thousand pillars, Achyuta. All of them were seen in white turbans and
white clothes, and I saw that all their seats were white. And at the end of a
dream I saw you too, Krishna, Janardana, wrapping the earth, smeared with blood,
in entrails. Yudhishthira of boundless energy had climbed a heap of bones and
was eating rice cooked in milk with clarified butter from a golden bowl, full of
joy. I saw Yudhishthira swallowing the earth. Clearly he will enjoy this earth
that you give him. Vrikodara of terrible deeds, the tiger among men, had climbed
a high mountain with his club in his hand, as if looking over this earth.
Clearly he will destroy us all in the great battle. I know, Hrishikesha, that
where dharma is, there is victory.

"Dhananjaya, the bearer of Gandiva, had climbed a white elephant with you,
Hrishikesha, blazing with the highest glory. You will kill all the kings in
battle, Krishna, with Duryodhana at their head. I have no doubt of it. Nakula
and Sahadeva, and the great chariot fighter Satyaki, with pure armlets and
neckpieces, wrapped in white garlands and clothes, tigers among men, had climbed
the finest vehicle carried by men. These three great men had white umbrellas and
clothes. In the armies of Dhritarashtra's son, Janardana, three are seen in
white turbans too. Know who they are, Keshava: Ashvatthama, Kripa and Kritavarma
the Satvata. All the other kings are seen in red turbans, Madhava. Bhishma and
Drona had climbed a cart yoked with camels, Janardana, and with me and
Dhritarashtra's son, strong-armed lord, they went to the quarter ruled by
Agastya. In a very short time we will reach Yama's house. I and the other kings,
and that whole circle of kshatriyas, will enter the fire of Gandiva. I have no
doubt of it."

"Surely the earth's destruction is at hand today," said Krishna, "for my words
do not reach your heart, Karna. When the destruction of all creatures is at
hand, my son, wrong policy that looks like good policy does not leave the
heart."

"Perhaps we will see you, Krishna," said Karna, "if we come through alive from
this great battle, strong-armed one, that will destroy heroes. Or surely we will
meet in heaven, Krishna. There we will meet again with you, sinless one."

Saying this, Karna embraced Madhava tightly. Dismissed by Keshava, he got down
from the chariot floor. Then he mounted his own chariot, adorned with gold, and
Radha's son turned back with us, sad at heart. Then Keshava with Satyaki drove
on faster, crying again and again to his charioteer, "Go on! Go on!"

**Choices.**

* 5.140.4 *bhauvana*: the maker of the banner's magic, usually taken to be Vishvakarman; the text names him only by this epithet.
* 5.140.7 *na tadā bhavitā tretā na kṛtaṃ dvāparaṃ na ca*: "then there will be no Treta age, no Krita and no Dvapara", that is, only the Kali age will be left. The refrain is kept.
* 5.140.18: the new moon "in seven days" (*saptamād divasāt*). "Shakra is its god" renders *śakradevatām*.
* 5.141.7–10: the planets and constellations are kept as named: the constellation of Prajapati (Rohini), Jyeshtha, Anuradha, Chitra.
* 5.141.17–18: the birds are kept as named. *Jīvaṃjīvaka* is a kind of pheasant; *baḍa* is a bird of uncertain kind; *yātudhāna* is a kind of rakshasa.
* 5.141.24: "He hates brahmins first" refers to Duryodhana.
* 5.141.41 *agastyaśāstāṃ diśam*: the south, Yama's quarter.
* 5.141.48 *sahāsmābhiḥ*: "with us". Sanjaya was among those who turned back with Karna.

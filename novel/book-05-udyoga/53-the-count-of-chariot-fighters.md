# The Count of Chariot Fighters

"When he heard Uluka's words," said Sanjaya, "Kunti's son Yudhishthira led out
his army with Dhrishtadyumna at its head. It had foot soldiers, elephants,
chariots and troops of horses, an army of four branches, terrible and
unshakeable as the earth. It was guarded by Bhimasena and the other great
chariot fighters, with Arjuna, and it was under Dhrishtadyumna's command, hard
to attack, like the still ocean. At its front the great archer, the Panchala
prince fierce in war, Dhrishtadyumna, eager to reach Drona, drew the ranks
forward.

"He assigned the chariot fighters by their strength and spirit. Arjuna he
assigned to the suta's son and Bhima to Duryodhana. He assigned Nakula to
Ashvatthama, Shaibya to Kritavarma, and the Varshneya Yuyudhana to the king of
Sindhu. He set Shikhandi in front, against Bhishma. He set Sahadeva against
Shakuni, Chekitana against Shala, Dhrishtaketu against Shalya and Uttamaujas
against Gautama. He set the five sons of Draupadi against the five Trigartas,
and Subhadra's son against Vrishasena and the rest of the kings, for he thought
him able for it, even more than Partha in battle. When he had divided the
fighters like this, separately and together, the great archer the colour of
flame kept Drona as his own share. Then Dhrishtadyumna, the great archer, the
wise general of the generals, drew up the Pandavas' armies by the rules, as they
had been assigned, with his mind set on war, and stood ready on the battlefield
for the victory of Pandu's sons."

---

"When Phalguna had vowed to kill Bhishma, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "what
did my foolish sons, Duryodhana and the rest, do? I see Ganga's son, my father,
killed in battle already by Partha with his strong bow and Vasudeva as his ally.
And when Bhishma, of boundless wisdom, heard Partha's words, what did that great
archer, the best of fighters, say? When he took command of the Kauravas, what
did Ganga's son, who carried the burden, of great wisdom and courage, do?"

Then Sanjaya told him all that the eldest of the Kurus, Bhishma of boundless
energy, had said.

"When Bhishma son of Shantanu had taken command, king," said Sanjaya, "he said
this to Duryodhana, as if to cheer him.

"'I bow to Kumara, the general who holds the spear. I will be your general
today, no doubt of it. I know the work of an army and arrays of many kinds, and
how to make men work, paid or unpaid. In marches, in fighting and in calming
what has been won, I know a great deal, great king, as Brihaspati does. I know
arrays of great scope, of the gods, the gandharvas and men. With them I will
confound the Pandavas. Let your fever go. I will fight truly, guarding your
army, as the science lays down, king. Let your mind's fever go.'

"'I have no fear, son of Ganga, even of the gods and asuras together,
strong-armed one,' said Duryodhana. 'I tell you this truly. How much less with
you, hard to withstand, in command, and Drona, tiger among men, standing ready
and delighting in war? With you two, the foremost of men, standing by, victory
is mine. Even the kingdom of the gods is surely not hard to win, best of the
Kurus. But I want to know the full count of the chariot fighters, Kaurava, on
the enemy side and on ours, and of the atirathas too. The grandfather knows ours
and theirs. I want to hear it, with all these lords of the earth.'

"'Son of Gandhari, king of kings,' said Bhishma, 'hear the count of the chariot
fighters in your own force, lord of the earth, who are rathas and who are
atirathas. There are many thousands, millions and hundreds of millions of
chariots in your army. Hear from me the chief of them.

"'First you, a fine chariot fighter, with all your brothers, Duhshasana and the
rest, a hundred brothers. All of them are practised in striking, skilled at
cutting and piercing, on the chariot floor, on the elephant's neck, in club
fighting and with sword and shield. They are drivers and strikers, masters of
weapons who carry their burden, pupils of Drona and of Kripa son of Sharadvat in
archery. These proud sons of Dhritarashtra, wronged by the Pandavas, will kill
the Panchalas, fierce in war, in battle. Then I, best of the Bharatas, general
of all your army, will destroy your enemies and make nothing of the Pandavas.
But I should not speak of my own virtues. You know me.

"'Kritavarma the Bhoja, best of fighters, is an atiratha. He will win your aim
in battle, no doubt of it. No master of weapons can attack him. He shoots far
and his weapons are strong. He will kill your enemies as great Indra killed the
danavas. The king of the Madras, the great archer Shalya, I think an atiratha.
He always rivals Vasudeva in every battle. Shalya, the finest of chariot
fighters, has left his own sister's sons for you. He will fight Krishna, who
bears the discus and the club, in battle, flooding the enemy as it were with a
force like the waves of the ocean. Bhurishravas, master of weapons, your friend
who wants your good, Somadatta's son, the great archer, leader of the leaders of
chariot troops, will make a very great slaughter of the enemy's strength.

"'The king of Sindhu, great king, I think a chariot fighter worth two. That
valiant, fine chariot fighter will fight in battle, king. He was tormented by
the Pandavas long ago, when he carried off Draupadi. Remembering that torment,
the slayer of enemy heroes will fight. Then, king, he practised terrible
austerity and won a boon very hard to win, to fight the Pandavas in battle. That
tiger among chariot fighters will fight the Pandavas in battle, my son,
remembering that feud and giving up the life that is so hard to give up.

---

"'Sudakshina of Kamboja I think a chariot fighter worth one. Wanting to win your
aim, he will fight the enemy in battle. The Kurus will see the courage of that
lion among chariot fighters in battle for your sake, best of kings, like
Indra's. His line of chariots, great king, of Kambojas who strike with fierce
force, is long as a swarm of locusts. Nila of Mahishmati, wearing blue armour,
will make a slaughter of your enemies with his line of chariots. That king had a
feud with Sahadeva long ago, and he will always fight for you, king, best of the
Kurus. Vinda and Anuvinda of Avanti, together, the finest of chariot fighters,
are skilled in battle, my son, of firm courage and valour. Those two tigers
among men will burn up the enemy army with clubs, darts, swords, iron arrows and
lances thrown by the arm. Eager for war, they will play in battle like two
leaders of a herd, ranging among the herd like the Ender, great king.

"'The five Trigarta brothers I think fine chariot fighters. They had their feud
with Partha in Virata's city. Like crocodiles, king of kings, they will churn up
the Parthas' army in battle like the Ganga with its waves tossed high. Those
five chariot fighters, king of kings, led by Satyaratha, will fight in battle
remembering the wrong done before by the Pandava, Bhimasena's younger brother,
the one with white horses, when he conquered the quarters, king, Bharata. They
will meet the Parthas' great chariot fighters and kill the best of their great
archers, bearing the burden of the kshatriyas.

"'Your son Lakshmana, and Duhshasana's son, both tigers among men who never turn
back in battle, young and tender, swift princes, who know the fine points of war
and are leaders in every way, I think fine chariot fighters, tiger among chariot
fighters. Those two heroes, devoted to the dharma of a kshatriya, will do great
deeds. Dandadhara, great king, a bull among men, is a chariot fighter worth one.
He will fight when he comes to battle, guarded by his own army. King Brihadbala
of Kosala, a fine chariot fighter, I think a ratha, my son, of firm force and
courage. That great archer of fierce weapons, devoted to the good of
Dhritarashtra's sons, will fight in battle, gladdening his own army.

"'Kripa son of Sharadvat, king, leader of the leaders of chariot troops, will
give up his dear life and burn up your enemies. He is the son of the great seer
Gautama, the teacher Sharadvat, born from a clump of reeds, unconquerable like
Karttikeya. He will range in battle like fire, my son, crushing an army of many
kinds, with weapons and bows of many kinds.'"

**Choices.**

* **Ratha and atiratha.** Bhishma ranks the fighters as *ratha*, a chariot fighter, and *atiratha*, a fighter beyond the ordinary chariot fighter; some are worth one or two rathas. The novel uses the Sanskrit terms where Bhishma ranks, and "chariot fighter" elsewhere.
* 5.161.6 *śaibya*: Shaibya, a king of the Shibis on the Pandava side.
* 5.161.9: Abhimanyu is thought "able for it, even more than Partha".
* 5.162.7 *kumārāya senānye śaktipāṇaye*: Bhishma bows to Kumara, Skanda, general of the gods.
* 5.162.27: Shalya has left his sister's sons, Nakula and Sahadeva.
* 5.162.32: Jayadratha's boon is told in Book 3.
* 5.163.11 *satyarathaḥ*: Satyaratha, leader of the Trigarta brothers.
* 5.163.21: Kripa "born from a clump of reeds", as told in Book 1.

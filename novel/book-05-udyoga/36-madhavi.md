# Madhavi

"'"Because it carries a man across, *uttaryate*, from sin, and because in it he
wins the highest good, the wise call it *uttara*, the north, for the fruit of
crossing over," said the Suparna. "The road of the northern gold that is spread
about, Galava, is said to lie between the west and the east. In this best of
directions, the north, bull among brahmins, no one lives who is ungentle, unruly
or without dharma. Here are Narayana Krishna and Jishnu, the best of men, at the
hermitage of Badari, and eternal Brahma too. Here, on the ridge of the Himalaya,
the great lord always lives. Here the moon was crowned to rule over the
brahmins. Here the great god received the Ganga as she fell from the sky and
gave her to the world of men, best of the knowers of Brahman. Here the goddess
practised austerity wanting the great lord. Here Kama, anger, the mountain and
Uma shone together.

"'"Here on Kailasa the giver of wealth was crowned lord of the rakshasas,
yakshas and gandharvas, Galava. Here is lovely Chaitraratha, here the hermitage
of the Vaikhanasas, here the Mandakini and Mandara, bull among brahmins. Here
the forest of saugandhika flowers is guarded by Nairritas, with meadows and
plantain groves, and here are the santanaka trees. Here are heavenly cars, fit
for each of them and full of pleasures at will, for the perfected ones who are
always restrained and move as they please, Galava. Here are those seven seers
and the goddess Arundhati. Here stands Svati, and here, it is said, is its
rising. Here the grandfather, climbing onto the sacrifice, stands firm, and the
stars, the moon and the sun always circle round.

"'"Here the best of brahmins guard the door of Gayantika, great sages named
Dhamas, who speak the truth. Their birth is not known, nor their form, nor the
austerity they have heaped up. They have thousands of rounds of pleasures at
will, Galava. The further a man goes in from here, best of brahmins, the more he
melts away, Galava. No one else has ever gone there, bull among brahmins, except
the god Narayana or the undecaying Nara, Jishnu. Here is the place called
Kailasa, the home of Ailavila. Here ten apsarases named Vidyutprabha were born.
Here is the place called Vishnu's Step, made by Vishnu as he strode when he
crossed the three worlds, brahmin, set in the north.

"'"Here King Marutta sacrificed, best of the twice-born, at Ushirabija, brahmin
seer, where the golden lake is. Here the holy, spotless lotus lake of the
Himalaya itself waited on the great brahmin seer Jimuta. He gave away all his
great wealth to the brahmins, and the great seer chose the forest, and so it is
the forest of Jimuta. Here, morning and evening, bull among brahmins, the
guardians of the directions call out, Galava, 'Whose business is to be done?
What is it?' So, best of brahmins, this direction is the north, *uttara*, for
these and other virtues, and it is higher, *uttara*, in all works. I have told
you about the four directions in full, my son, in order. Which do you want to go
to? I am ready, best of brahmins, to show you the directions and the whole
earth, brahmin. So climb on me, twice-born."

---

"'"Garutman, enemy of the lord of serpents, Suparna, son of Vinata," said
Galava, "take me east, Tarkshya, where the two eyes of Dharma are. Go first to
that direction you described first. You said the gods are close at hand there.
You described truth and dharma there well. I want to meet all the gods, and I
want to see those gods again, younger brother of Aruna."

"'The son of Vinata told the brahmin, "Climb on." And the sage Galava climbed
onto Garuda.

"'"As you stride, eater of serpents," said Galava, "your form looks like the
sun's in the morning, Vivasvat with a thousand rays. I see the trees driven by
the wind of your wings following you, bird, as if they were all setting out at
once. You seem to drag the earth along with its oceans and forests, its
mountains and woods, with the wind of your wings, sky-goer. The water, with its
fish, nagas and crocodiles, seems to be lifted into the sky by the wind and by
the great, unceasing wind of your wings. I see fish all alike in face, whales
and whale-swallowers, and nagas with the faces of men, as if churned up. My ears
are deafened by the roar of the great ocean. I cannot hear, I cannot see, I do
not know my own self. Go slowly, good friend, remembering the sin of killing a
brahmin. I cannot see the sun, my son, or the directions, or the sky, bird.

"'"I see only darkness. I cannot make out your body. I see your two eyes like
fine jewels, egg-born one. I cannot see your body, or my own. At every step I
see fire rising out of the water. It suddenly blinds my eyes and dies down
again. So turn back, son of Vinata. A long time has passed as we go. I have no
business going on, eater of serpents. Turn back, swift one. I cannot bear your
speed. I promised my teacher eight hundred horses, white, bright as the moon,
each with one black ear. I see no way to pay for them, egg-born one. So I have
seen this way for myself, to give up my life. I have no wealth at all, nor any
friend with wealth. And even great wealth could not make this right."

"'As Galava said all this so pitifully, the son of Vinata answered, laughing as
he went, "You are not very wise, brahmin seer, if you want to give up your life.
Time is not made by man. Time is the highest lord. Why did you not tell me this
before? There is a great way by which this can be done. This is the mountain
called Rishabha, in the bosom of the ocean. Here we will rest and eat, and turn
back, Galava."

---

"'The brahmin and the bird came down on the peak of Rishabha and saw there the
brahmin woman Shandili, rich in austerity. The Suparna greeted her and Galava
honoured her. She welcomed them, and they sat down on a seat of grass. She
quickly made food ready, blessed with offerings and mantras. They ate it, were
satisfied, and fell asleep on the ground, overcome by the food. After a while
the Suparna woke, wanting to go, and the bird saw that his body had lost its
wings. He had become like a lump of flesh with a face and feet.

"'When Galava saw him like that, he asked in dismay, "What is this that you have
met with as the fruit of coming here? How long will we have to stay here? What
evil, against dharma, did you think in your mind? This is no small fault of
yours."

"'Then the Suparna said to the brahmin, "I did think, brahmin, of taking this
perfected woman from here to where Prajapati is, where the great god is, where
eternal Vishnu is, where dharma and sacrifice are. 'She should live there,' I
thought." Then he said to her, "I bow and ask you, holy one, wishing to please
you. I did think this, they say, in my mind, grieving. I have done this unwanted
thing here out of great regard for you. Whether it was well done or ill done,
you in your greatness should forgive it."

"'She was pleased, and said to the lord of birds and the bull among brahmins,
"Do not be afraid. You are a Suparna. Suparna, give up your alarm. You blamed
me, my child, and I do not bear blame. A wicked man who blames me would fall
from the worlds. Without any unlucky marks, and blameless, keeping to good
conduct, I have won this highest perfection. By good conduct a man wins dharma.
By good conduct he wins wealth. By good conduct he wins fortune. Good conduct
kills bad marks. So go wherever you like from here, long-lived lord of birds.
And never blame women, even those who deserve it. You will be as you were
before, with strength and courage."

"'Then his wings became stronger still. With Shandili's leave he went back the
way he had come. But he did not find horses like that.

"'Then Vishvamitra saw Galava standing on the road, and the best of speakers
said in the presence of Vinata's son, "The wealth you promised me yourself,
brahmin, now is the time to pay it, or whenever you think fit. I will wait this
long, and longer. Find out the way, so that it may be done, brahmin."

"'The Suparna said to Galava, who was miserable and in deep sorrow, "I have
heard with my own eyes what Vishvamitra has said. Come, best of brahmins, let us
take counsel, Galava. You cannot sit still without giving your teacher all the
wealth."

---

"'Then the Suparna, best of birds, said to the miserable Galava, "What was made
in the earth by fire and stirred by wind, and is all of gold, is called
*hiranya*, gold. Because it holds and upholds this world, it is called *dhana*,
wealth. That wealth lasts for ever in the three worlds. Always under the two
Proshthapadas, and with Shukra as lord of wealth, Shukra takes from men the
wealth they earned with their minds. It is guarded by Ajaikapad and Ahirbudhnya
and by the giver of wealth. So what is not to be won cannot be won, bull among
brahmins.

"'"Without wealth you cannot get the horses. So ask some king for wealth, one
born of a line of royal seers, who could fulfil our aim without oppressing his
townsmen. There is a king born in the lunar line who is my friend. Let us go to
him. He has wealth on earth. The royal seer named Yayati, son of Nahusha, of
true courage. He will give, when I tell him and you ask him yourself. His wealth
was very great, like that of the lord of wealth. And the wise man has cleansed
his wealth by giving."

"'Talking like this and thinking what was best, they came to King Yayati in
Pratishthana. He received them with honour, the guest offering and a fine meal,
and when he asked why they had come, the son of Vinata said, "This is my friend
Galava, son of Nahusha, a treasure of austerity. He was Vishvamitra's pupil for
ten thousand years, king. With his leave, this brahmin wanted to do him a
service, and asked the holy one, 'What fee shall I give you?' He was asked so
many times that he grew a little angry. Knowing his small means, he told him,
'Give it.' 'Give me eight hundred horses white as the moon, pure-bred, each with
one black ear. Let this be my teacher's fee, if you think fit, Galava.' So the
angry Vishvamitra, rich in austerity, said. This bull among brahmins, burning
with great sorrow and unable to repay it, has come to you for refuge. When he
has taken alms from you, tiger among men, and is free of care, and has paid his
teacher, he will practise great austerity. He will share his austerity with you.
With his own austerity, the royal seer's, he will fill you up. As many hairs as
there are on a horse, lord of men, so many worlds do the givers of horses win,
lord of the earth. He is a worthy receiver, and you are a worthy giver. Let it
be like milk poured into a conch."

---

"'When the Suparna had said these true and excellent words, the king considered
them carefully, and decided again and again. Yayati, who had offered thousands
of sacrifices, the lord of giving, the master, king of the Vatsas and Kashis,
said this. Seeing his dear friend Tarkshya and Galava the bull among brahmins,
and the begging, praised as a mark of austerity, and that they had passed over
other kings born of the solar line and come to him, he thought it over and
spoke.

"'"Today my birth bears fruit. Today my family is saved. Today you have saved
this land of mine, faultless Tarkshya. But I want to tell you, friend, that I am
not as rich as you knew me before. My wealth is spent, friend. Yet I cannot make
your coming in vain, bird, nor bring myself to make this brahmin seer's hope
false. So I will give what will bring this about. A man who comes with hope and
goes back with his hope killed burns up a family. Nothing is called more wicked
than this, son of Vinata, than killing hope in the world, or saying, 'Give?
There is none.' A man who is honoured and goes back with his hope killed and his
aim unfulfilled is struck down. He strikes down the sons and grandsons of one
who does nothing for those who ask.

"'"So here is my daughter, who will establish four lines. She is like a daughter
of the gods, and she gathers every kind of dharma. Gods, men and asuras always
want this girl for her beauty, Galava. Take my daughter. Kings will surely give
a kingdom as her bride-price, let alone eight hundred horses with black ears.
Take my daughter Madhavi, lord. Let me have grandsons by a daughter. That is my
wish."

"'Galava took the girl, and with the bird said, "We shall meet again," and set
out with the girl. The egg-born one said, "This door to the horses has been
found," took leave of Galava, and went to his own home. When the king of birds
had gone, Galava went with the girl, thinking which king would be fit to give
the bride-price. In his mind he went to Haryashva, the Ikshvaku, best of kings,
very brave, in Ayodhya, with an army of all four arms, rich in treasure, grain
and troops, loved by his townsmen, fond of brahmins, wanting offspring, calm,
practising the highest austerity. Galava the brahmin went to Haryashva and said,
"This girl of mine, king of kings, will increase a family with her children.
Take her as your wife, Haryashva, for a bride-price. I will tell you the price.
When you have heard it, decide."'"

**Choices.**

* 5.109.1, 5.109.24: the name *uttara* (north) is explained by *uttāryate*, "is carried across", and by *uttara*, "higher". The Sanskrit is kept for the pun.
* 5.109.12 *svātiḥ*: the star Svati.
* 5.109.14 *gāyantikādvāram*: "the door of Gayantika". The Dhamas are sages named only here.
* 5.111.3: Shandili's food is "blessed with offerings and mantras".
* 5.111.7–10: Garuda's thought, that Shandili should be taken to the world of the gods, is read as a slight to her for living on earth; she calls it blame (5.111.13).
* 5.112.1–2: *hiraṇya* and *dhana* are explained by puns, kept with the Sanskrit.
* 5.112.3–4: the astronomical verses (the Proshthapadas, Shukra, Ajaikapad and Ahirbudhnya) are obscure and are kept as given.
* 5.113.2 *vatsakāśīśaḥ*: Yayati is called "king of the Vatsas and Kashis".
* 5.113.11 *caturṇāṃ vaṃśānāṃ sthāpayitrī*: Madhavi "will establish four lines".

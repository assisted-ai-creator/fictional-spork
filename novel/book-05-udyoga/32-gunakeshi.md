# Gunakeshi

"'This is the city called Bhogavati,'" said Narada, as Kanva told it, "'guarded
by Vasuki. It is like Amaravati, the finest city of the king of the gods. Here
is the naga Shesha, who always holds up this earth by his austerity, the first
in the world, and his great power. He is shaped like a white mountain and wears
all kinds of ornaments. He bears a thousand heads. His tongues are flames and
his strength is great.

"'Here live the nagas, sons of Surasa, of every shape and with every kind of
ornament, free from care. They are marked with jewels, swastikas and wheels, and
with the sign of the water pot. There are thousands of them, strong, and all
fierce by nature. Some have a thousand heads, some five hundred faces, some a
hundred heads, some three heads. Some have ten heads and some seven faces. They
have great coils and great bodies, and their coils spread like mountains. There
are many thousands, millions and hundreds of millions here, nagas of a single
line. Hear from me the chief of them, in order of rank.

"'Here are Vasuki and Takshaka, Karkotaka and Dhananjaya, Kaliya and Nahusha,
Kambala and Ashvatara, Bahyakunda, the naga Mani, Apurana and Khaga, Vamana and
Elapatra, Kukura and Kukuna. Here are Aryaka and Nandaka, Kalasha and Potaka,
Kailasaka, Pinjaraka and the naga Airavata, Sumanomukha, Dadhimukha, Shankha,
Nanda and Upanandaka, Apta, Kotanaka, Shikhin and Nishthurika. Here are Tittiri,
Hastibhadra, Kumuda and Malyapindaka, the two Padmas and Pundarika, Pushpa and
Mudgaraparnaka, Karavira, Pitharaka, Samvritta and Vritta, Pindara, Bilvapatra,
Mushikada and Shirishaka. Here are Dilipa, Shankhashirsha, Jyotishka and
Aparajita, Kauravya, Dhritarashtra, Kumara and Kushaka, Viraja and Dharana,
Subahu, Mukhara and Jaya, Badhira and Andha, Vikunda, Virasa and Surasa. These
and many others are called the sons of Kashyapa. Matali, see if any husband here
pleases you.'

"Matali had been looking steadily and intently at one of them," Kanva went on.
"He seemed delighted, and he asked Narada, 'Who is this one standing in front of
Kauravya and Aryaka, so bright and fine to look at? Whose joy is he? Who are his
father and mother? Of which naga is he the son? Of which line is he the great
banner, as it were? His bearing, his steadiness, his looks and his youth have
gone into my heart, divine seer. He is the best husband for Gunakeshi.'

"When Narada saw that Matali was pleased at the sight of Sumukha, he told him of
the young naga's greatness, his birth and his deeds. 'This is the naga prince
named Sumukha, born in the family of Airavata. He is Aryaka's grandson, whom he
loves, and Vamana's daughter's son. His father was the naga named Chikura,
Matali, and not long ago Vainateya killed him.'

"Then Matali said to Narada, pleased, 'My friend, this best of snakes pleases me
as a son-in-law. Try to bring it about, sage, for I am pleased with him, and I
want to give this naga prince my dear daughter.'

"'This is the charioteer named Matali,' Narada said to Aryaka, 'Shakra's dear
friend. He is pure, of good character and virtue, splendid, brave and strong. He
is Shakra's friend, his counsellor and his charioteer, and in battle after
battle his power falls little short of Vasava's. In the wars of the gods and the
asuras he drives the great victorious chariot, yoked with a thousand horses, by
his mind alone. When he has beaten the enemy with his horses, Vasava beats them
with his arms. When he has struck first, the slayer of Bala strikes after.

"'He has a daughter, lovely, with no equal in beauty on earth, rich in goodness,
character and virtue, and known as Gunakeshi. He has searched the three worlds
with care, you who have the splendour of the gods, and your grandson Sumukha
pleases him as a husband for his daughter. If you like it, gentle one, best of
snakes, let there be no delay, Aryaka. Set your mind quickly on accepting the
girl. As Lakshmi is in Vishnu's family, and Svaha in the fire's, so let
slender-waisted Gunakeshi be in yours. Accept Gunakeshi for your grandson, as
fitting for him as Shachi is for Vasava. We choose him, though he has no father,
for his virtues, for our respect for you and for Airavata, and for Sumukha's own
qualities, his character, his purity, his self-control and the rest. Matali
himself has come to give the girl. You should honour him too.'

"Aryaka was both downcast and glad," said Kanva, "since his grandson was being
chosen while his son was dead. 'I do not think little of what you say, divine
seer,' he said to Narada. 'Who would not want the friend of Shakra as a kin? But
I am anxious, great sage, because of how weak our case is. My son, the father of
this boy, splendid one, was eaten by Vainateya, and we are stricken with grief
for it. And as he went away, Vainateya said, my lord, "In another month I shall
eat Sumukha." It will surely happen as he said, for we know his resolve. At
Suparna's words all my joy has gone.'

"Then Matali said to him, 'I have made up my mind. Sumukha, your son's son, is
chosen as my son-in-law. Let this snake go with me and Narada to see Vasava, the
lord of the three worlds, the lord of the gods. By what else is to be done I
shall learn how long he has to live, and I shall try to stop Suparna, good
friend. Let Sumukha come with me to the lord of the gods to get this business
done. Good fortune to you, snake.'

"So they all took Sumukha with them, those mighty ones, and saw Shakra, the
splendid king of the gods, sitting there. By chance the blessed four-armed
Vishnu was there too. Then Narada told the whole story of Matali. Vishnu said to
Purandara, the lord of the world, 'Let him be given the nectar, and made equal
to the immortals. Let Matali, Narada and Sumukha have from you, Vasava, at your
pleasure, the wish they want.'

"Purandara thought about Vainateya's might, and said to Vishnu, 'You give it.'

"'You are the lord of the worlds,' said Vishnu, 'of all that moves and does not
move. Who would dare to undo what you have given, my lord?'

"So Shakra gave the snake the best of long lives. But the slayer of Bala and
Vritra did not give him the nectar to eat. When Sumukha had won the boon, he was
Sumukha indeed, bright-faced. He married as he wished and went home. Narada and
Aryaka, their work done, honoured the splendid king of the gods and went back
glad.

"The mighty Garuda heard all that had happened, Bharata, and that Shakra had
given the naga long life. The bird Suparna, in a fury, blocked the three worlds
with the great wind of his wings and rushed at Vasava.

"'Lord,' said Garuda, 'why have you shown me such contempt, and put me in fear
of hunger? You gave me a boon to do as I pleased, and now you go back on it.
From the creation of all beings, the Maker, lord of all beings, laid down what I
should eat. Why do you forbid it? I had chosen this great naga and set the time,
and with him, god, I had to feed my many young. Now that this has turned out
otherwise, I cannot bring myself to harm another. You play as you please, king
of the gods, just as you like. So I shall give up my life, and so will my
household and the servants in my house. Be happy, Vasava.

"'I deserve this, slayer of Bala and Vritra, and more, for I, who could be lord
of the three worlds, have become another's servant. While you are there, lord of
the gods, Vishnu is not the cause of it. The kingship of the three worlds,
Vasava, rests with you for ever. My mother too is Daksha's daughter, and
Kashyapa is my father. I too could carry all the worlds with ease. My strength
too is great, and no creature can bear it. I too did great deeds in the war with
the daityas. I too killed sons of Diti, Shrutashri and Shrutasena, Vivasvat and
Rochanamukha, Prasabha and Kalakaksha.

"'Because I stand on your younger brother's banner and serve him with care, and
carry him, you despise me. Who else can bear such a load? Who else is stronger?
I am greater than he is, and I carry him and his kin. But since you have scorned
me and taken away my food, my honour is lost with you and with him, Vasava. Of
all these sons of Aditi, mighty in strength and courage, you are said to be the
strongest. And I carry you on one part of one wing without tiring. Think it over
slowly, my friend. Who is the strong one here?'

"When the bearer of the chariot wheel heard the bird's words, dreadful in what
they led to, he stirred up Tarkshya, who could not be stirred.

"'Garutman,' he said, 'you think yourself strong, and you are very weak. Enough
of praising yourself before me, egg-born one. The whole three worlds could not
bear my body. I carry myself by myself, and I hold you up too. Now carry just
this one arm of mine, the right. If you can hold it up, your boasting has some
point.'

"Then the blessed one laid his arm on Garuda's shoulder. Garuda fell, crushed by
the load, reeling and senseless. That one limb felt to him as heavy as the whole
earth with its mountains. But the stronger one did not press him with his
strength, and so Achyuta did not take his life. The bird lay with his wings
spread, his body slack, senseless and reeling, and crushed by the great load he
shed his feathers.

"The son of Vinata bowed his head to Vishnu, and senseless, reeling and
wretched, he said a few words. 'Lord, I have been crushed into the ground by
your arm, carelessly let fall, which is like the whole substance of the world.
Forgive me, god. I am a bird who lives on your banner, reeling and witless,
scorched by the fire of your strength. I did not know your supreme strength,
lord, my master, and so I thought my own courage had no equal.'

"Then the blessed one was gracious to Garutman, and said to him with affection,
'Do not do it again.'

"So you too, son of Gandhari," Kanva said to Duryodhana, "live only as long as
you do not meet those heroes, the sons of Pandu, in battle, my boy. Bhima, best
of fighters, the mighty son of the wind, and Dhananjaya, son of Indra, whom
would they not kill in battle? Vishnu, Vayu, Shakra, Dharma and the two Ashvins:
how could you even look these gods in the face? Enough of your quarrel. Make
peace, prince. You should protect your family, with Vasudeva as your way across.
The great ascetic Narada here saw all this with his own eyes, the greatness of
Vishnu then, who is this bearer of the discus and the mace."

When Duryodhana heard this, he sighed and frowned. He looked at Radheya and
laughed aloud. The fool made nothing of the seer Kanva's words. He slapped his
thigh, which was like an elephant's trunk, and said, "As the Lord has made me,
and as what is to be and my own road are, so I behave, great seer. What will
babbling do?"

**Divergences.**

* **Sumukha on Garuda's breast.** A rejected passage (\*449) ends the story with Vishnu flicking Sumukha onto Garuda's breast with his toe, so that the bird wears the snake from then on. The CE ends with Vishnu's "Do not do it again" (5.103.30). Not narrated.
* **Sumukha's fear.** A rejected passage (\*442) has Sumukha turn pale and shrink to snake form at Garuda's words and cling to Vishnu's footstool, and Indra protest that it was not his doing. Not narrated.
* **How Garuda was crushed.** Rejected lines have Garuda touched only with a fingernail (\*446), or Vishnu putting his own load on him out of affection (\*447). In the CE Vishnu lays his right arm on Garuda's shoulder (5.103.21–22). Not narrated.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*437–\*441, \*443–\*445, \*448, which add courtesies, repeat speeches and make Garuda's boast larger.

**Choices.**

* The chapter continues Kanva's story inside Kanva's telling. To keep the voices clear, the first line names both speakers. Matali's, Narada's, Aryaka's, Vishnu's and Garuda's speech is in single quotes, and Garuda's words quoted by Aryaka are in double quotes inside those.
* 5.101.2: Shesha holds up the earth *tapasā*, "by his austerity".
* 5.101.4 *surasāyāḥ sutāḥ*: "sons of Surasa". 5.101.17 calls them "sons of Kashyapa".
* 5.101.5 *kamaṇḍalukalakṣaṇāḥ*: "with the sign of the water pot".
* 5.101.9–16: the names are given in full. The Nahusha, Dhritarashtra, Dilipa and Airavata here are nagas.
* 5.102.2 *alpāntaraprabhāvaś ca vāsavena*: "his power falls little short of Vasava's".
* 5.102.3 *harisahasreṇa*: "a thousand horses".
* 5.102.28 *sumukhaḥ sumukhaḥ saṃbabhūva*: "he was Sumukha indeed, bright-faced", a play on the name, which means "good face".
* 5.103.3 *kāmakāravaram*: "a boon to do as I pleased", the boon Garuda won from Indra (1.29).
* 5.103.9: Garuda's words "Vishnu is not the cause of it" are his own claim, blaming Indra.
* 5.103.12: the names of the daityas Garuda killed are given as the text gives them.
* 5.103.13 *anujaṃ te*: "your younger brother", Vishnu.
* 5.103.18 *rathacakrabhṛt*: "the bearer of the chariot wheel", Vishnu with his discus.
* 5.103.22–23: the weight of one arm equal to the earth with its mountains is the text's image.
* 5.103.31 *gāndhāre*: "son of Gandhari".
* 5.103.34 *vāsudevena tīrthena*: "with Vasudeva as your way across", as at 5.86.10.
* 5.103.37: Duryodhana slaps his thigh, *ūruṃ gajakarākāram*, "like an elephant's trunk".
* 5.103.38 *yathaiveśvarasṛṣṭo 'smi*: "As the Lord has made me".

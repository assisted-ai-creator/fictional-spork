# Trishiras and Vritra

"How did great Indra and his wife meet with such terrible sorrow, king of
kings?" asked Yudhishthira. "I want to know."

"Listen, king, to an old story of what happened long ago," said Shalya, "and how
Indra and his wife met with sorrow, Bharata.

"Tvashtri was a lord of creatures, the best of the gods, a great ascetic. They
say that out of hostility to Indra he made a son with three heads. This son,
Vishvarupa, shone brightly and wanted Indra's place. His three terrible faces
were like the sun, the moon and fire. With one face he recited the Vedas, with
one he drank sura, and with the third he looked at all the directions as if he
would drink them in. He was an ascetic, gentle and self-controlled, intent on
dharma and on austerity. He practised great and fierce austerity, very hard to
do, scorcher of enemies.

"When Shakra saw the power of his austerity and the strength of that being of
boundless energy, he grew downcast. 'I hope he will not become Indra,' he
thought. 'How can he be made to cling to pleasures and stop his great
austerities? If the three-headed one keeps growing, he will swallow all three
worlds.' The wise god thought it over many times, bull of the Bharatas. Then he
ordered the apsarases to tempt Tvashtri's son.

"'Make the three-headed one cling hard to the pleasures of desire,' he said. 'Do
it quickly. Go and tempt him, and don't be long. Dress for love, you
lovely-hipped ones, and use every charming art you have. Tempt him, bless you,
and calm my fear. I can feel that I am not myself, beautiful women. Quickly
destroy this terrible fear.'

"'We will try hard to tempt him, Shakra,' said the apsarases, 'so that you will
have nothing to fear from him, slayer of Bala. That treasury of austerity sits
there as if burning things up with his eyes. We will go together to tempt him,
god. We will try to bring him under our power and to take your fear away.'

"With Indra's leave they went to the three-headed one. There the lovely women
tempted him with all their arts, dancing for him and showing the grace of their
bodies. They moved around him, but the great ascetic felt no stirring of desire.
He held his senses in check and was like the full ocean. When they had tried
their hardest, they came back to Shakra. They all joined their hands and spoke
to the king of the gods. 'He cannot be moved from his firmness, lord. No one can
overcome him. Do whatever you have to do next, fortunate one.'

"Wise Shakra honoured the apsarases and sent them away. Then he thought about a
way to kill the great-souled one. The mighty king of the gods thought in
silence, and the wise hero made up his mind to kill the three-headed one. 'I
will throw my thunderbolt at him today, and he will soon be gone. A strong man
should not ignore an enemy who is growing, even a weak one.' He reasoned it out
by the teaching of the treatises, and fixed his mind on killing him. Then
Shakra, in anger, hurled his thunderbolt at the three-headed one. It was like
fire, terrible to see and fearful.

"Struck hard by the thunderbolt, he fell dead, like a mountain peak knocked down
to the ground. But when the king of the gods saw him lying there, struck by the
thunderbolt, like a mountain, he found no peace. He was scorched by his
splendour. Even dead, the three-headed one blazed with energy and looked as if
he were alive.

"Nearby, Shachi's husband saw a carpenter at work. The chastiser of Paka spoke
to him at once. 'Quickly, cut off his heads. Do as I say.'

"'His shoulders are very broad,' said the carpenter. 'My axe won't get through
them. And I can't do a thing that good men condemn.'

"'Don't be afraid,' said Indra. 'Do as I say, quickly. By my favour your tool
will be like the thunderbolt.'

"'Who should I take you to be, you who have done a terrible deed today?' said
the carpenter. 'I want to hear it. Tell me the truth.'

"'I am Indra, king of the gods,' said Indra. 'Know that, carpenter. Do what I
told you, carpenter, and don't stop to think.'

"'How are you not ashamed of this cruel deed, Shakra?' said the carpenter. 'You
have killed the son of a seer. Aren't you afraid of the sin of killing a
brahmin?'

"'Later I shall do some hard dharma to purify myself,' said Indra. 'This was a
mighty enemy, and I have killed him with my thunderbolt. Even now I am troubled,
carpenter. I am afraid of him. Cut off his heads quickly, and I will grant you a
favour. In their sacrifices men will give you the head of the victim as your
share. That is my favour to you, carpenter. Quickly, do what I want.'

"When the carpenter heard great Indra say this, he cut off the three-headed
one's heads with his axe. As they were cut off, birds flew out of the
three-headed one, francolins, partridges and sparrows, everywhere. From the
mouth with which he recited the Vedas and drank soma, francolins flew out
quickly. From the mouth with which he looked at all the directions as if
drinking them in, partridges flew out, Pandava. From the three-headed one's
mouth that drank sura, sparrows flew out, bull of the Bharatas. When the heads
were cut off, Maghavan's fever left him. He went back to heaven in delight, and
the carpenter too went home.

---

"When Tvashtri, the lord of creatures, heard that Shakra had killed his son, his
eyes turned red with anger, and he said, 'My son was always practising
austerity. He was patient and self-controlled, master of his senses, and he had
done no wrong. Because Indra has harmed him, I shall make Vritra, to kill
Shakra. Let the worlds see my power and the great strength of my austerity. And
let the king of the gods see it too, that wicked, evil-minded one.'

"The angry ascetic, so famous, touched water, poured an offering into the fire,
and made terrible Vritra. He said to him, 'Enemy of Indra, grow, by the power of
my austerity.' Vritra grew until he propped up the sky, like the sun and like
fire. He rose like the sun at the end of time, and said, 'What shall I do?' When
he was told, 'Kill Shakra,' he went to heaven.

"Then a battle began between Vritra and Vasava. They were both furious, and the
fight was terrible and went on and on, best of the Kurus. Then the hero Vritra
seized the king of the gods, Shatakratu. Full of anger, Vritra opened his mouth
and swallowed him. When Shakra had been swallowed by Vritra, the thirty gods
were thrown into confusion. Those mighty beings made a yawn to destroy Vritra.
As Vritra yawned, the slayer of Bala drew in his limbs and came out of his open
mouth. Since then the yawn has lived in all creatures that breathe.

"All the gods were glad to see Shakra come out. Then the battle between Vritra
and Vasava began again. The two were furious, and they fought fiercely for a
long time, bull of the Bharatas. When Vritra, full of strength from Tvashtri's
austerity, grew greater in the battle, Shakra knew it and turned back.

"When he turned back, the gods were deeply dismayed. Bewildered by Tvashtri's
power, they met with Shakra and took counsel together with the sages, Bharata.
They thought about what to do, king, and they were confused with fear. In their
minds they all went to great Vishnu, who does not decay. Sitting on the top of
Mount Mandara, all of them wanting Vritra dead, they spoke.

"'Vritra has filled this whole imperishable world, gods,' said Indra. 'There is
nothing like him that could hold him back. Once I was able. Now I am not. How
can I do it, bless you? I think he cannot be overcome. He is full of energy,
great, and of boundless courage in battle. He could swallow all three worlds,
with gods, asuras and men. So hear what I have decided, you who live in heaven.
Let us go to Vishnu's dwelling, meet the great one, and find out from him a way
to kill the wicked one.'

"When Maghavan had said this, the gods and the hosts of seers went to Vishnu,
the mighty god, the refuge of all who seek one. Tormented by fear of Vritra,
they all said to Vishnu, lord of the gods, 'Lord, you crossed the three worlds
in three strides. You brought back the amrita, Vishnu, and killed the daityas in
battle. You bound Bali, the great daitya, and made Shakra king of the gods. You
are the lord of all the worlds. All this is spread out by you. You are the great
god, honoured by all the worlds. Be the refuge of the gods and of Indra, best of
the immortals. This whole world is filled by Vritra, slayer of asuras.'

"'I must certainly do what is best for you,' said Vishnu. 'So I will tell you a
way to make him cease to be. Go, with the seers and the gandharvas, to where he
is, the one who has taken every form. Use conciliation with him, and then you
will overcome him. By my energy, gods, Shakra will find a way. I will enter his
thunderbolt, the best of weapons, unseen. Go with the seers and the gandharvas,
best of the gods. Make peace between Vritra and Shakra, and don't delay.'

"When the god said this, the seers and the thirty gods went together, with
Shakra at their head. Coming near, all those mighty ones saw Vritra with Shakra.
He blazed with energy and scorched the ten directions, and seemed to swallow the
three worlds, like the sun and moon. Then the seers went up to Vritra and spoke
to him pleasantly.

"'This whole world is filled with your energy, you whom no one can beat. But you
cannot defeat Vasava, whose courage is great, and the two of you have been
fighting for a very long time. All the creatures, gods, asuras and men, are
suffering. Let there be friendship for ever between you and Shakra, Vritra. You
will find happiness, and Shakra's eternal worlds.'

"When he heard the seers' words, mighty Vritra bowed his head to them all, and
the asura said, 'All you fortunate ones, and all you gandharvas, I have heard
everything you say. Now hear me, you who are without fault. How will there be
peace between Shakra and me? How can two such powers be friends, gods?'

"'One should try to keep company with the good, if only once,' said the seers.
'After that, good will surely follow. A good man does not break faith with his
companion, so one should try for the company of the good. The company of the
good is always firm. A wise man, a steady one, speaks for what is good when
things go hard. Company with a good man brings great profit, so a wise man
should not seek to kill a good one. Indra is honoured by the good, and he is the
home of the great. He speaks the truth, he is not mean, he knows dharma and his
mind is firm. Let there be lasting peace between you and Shakra. Trust him in
this way. Do not let your mind be otherwise.'

"When he heard the great seers' words, the glorious one said to them, 'Certainly
you holy ascetics must be honoured. Gods, let everything I say be done here.
Then I will do everything the best of the twice-born have told me. Let me not be
killed by Shakra and the gods, best of brahmins, by anything dry or wet, by
stone or by wood, by a weapon or by the thunderbolt, by day or by night. On
those terms I agree to lasting peace with Shakra.'

"'Very well,' the seers said to him, bull of the Bharatas. When the pact was
made, Vritra was glad.

"But Shakra was always on his guard, and full of resentment. He kept thinking of
ways to kill Vritra. The slayer of Bala and Vritra was always anxious and
looking for a weak point. One day he saw the great asura on the shore of the
sea, at the hour of twilight, a time both lovely and terrible.

"Then the lord thought about the great one's boon. 'This is the fearful
twilight. It is neither night nor day. And Vritra, my enemy who has taken
everything from me, must be killed. If I don't kill the great asura Vritra
today, by tricking him, mighty and huge as he is, it will not go well with me.'

"As Shakra was thinking this, he called Vishnu to mind. Then he saw foam on the
sea, as big as a mountain. 'This is neither dry nor wet,' he thought, 'and it is
no weapon either. I will throw this at Vritra, and he will die in a moment.'
Quickly he threw the foam at Vritra, with his thunderbolt in it. Vishnu entered
the foam, and destroyed Vritra.

"When Vritra was dead, the darkness left the directions. A kind wind blew, and
all creatures were glad. Then the gods with the gandharvas, the yakshas,
rakshasas and serpents, and the seers praised great Indra with hymns of every
kind. All beings bowed to him, and he comforted all beings. His enemy dead,
Vasava rejoiced with the gods. Knowing dharma, he worshipped Vishnu, the best in
the three worlds.

---

"But when mighty Vritra, the terror of the gods, was dead, Shakra was overcome
by his falsehood, and he was in deep distress. He was already overcome by the
killing of a brahmin, from the death of the three-headed one. He went to the
edge of the worlds and lost his senses and his mind. Overwhelmed by his own
sins, the king of the gods could not be recognised. He lived hidden in the
waters, writhing like a snake.

"When the king of the gods, tormented by fear of the sin of killing a brahmin,
had disappeared, the earth looked ruined. The trees were gone and the forests
dried up. The rivers stopped flowing and the lakes had no water. Without rain,
all creatures were in turmoil. The gods were terrified, and so were all the
great seers. The whole world had no king and was overrun by disasters. Then the
gods were afraid. 'Who will be our king?' they said. In heaven the divine seers
too were without the king of the gods. And none of the gods would set his mind
on ruling."

**Choices.**

* 5.10.42 *anṛtenābhibhūtaḥ*: Indra is "overcome by his falsehood", having broken the pact. The guilt of killing a brahmin (*brahmahatyā*) comes from the death of Trishiras, a seer's son.

# Krishna's Journey

"You are the best friend of all the Kurus today," said Arjuna, "kin to both
sides and always dear to them. The Pandavas should bring health to the sons of
Dhritarashtra, and you are able to make peace between them, Keshava. Go from
here, lotus-eyed one, and say what must be said to impatient Suyodhana, the
Bharata, for the sake of peace, slayer of enemies. If the fool will not take the
good, gentle words you speak, full of dharma and purpose, he will fall into the
power of fate."

"I will go to King Dhritarashtra," said the Lord, "wishing for what is right,
what is good for us and healthy for the Kurus."

Then the darkness passed and the sun rose clear. It was the hour of Mitra, and
the sun's light was gentle. It was the month of Kaumuda, under the star Revati,
at the end of autumn, when the cold was coming, a happy time of rich crops.
Janardana, the best of the strong, was ready. He listened to the lucky, holy
words of glad brahmins, as Vasava listens to the seers. He did his morning
rites, bathed, and was pure and adorned. Then Janardana worshipped the sun and
the fire. He touched the back of a bull, greeted the brahmins, walked round the
fire keeping it on his right, and looked at lucky things set before him. Having
promised to do as the Pandava asked, Janardana spoke to Satyaki, Shini's
grandson, who sat there.

"Let the conch, the discus and the mace be put in the chariot, and the quivers,
spears and every kind of weapon. Duryodhana is wicked, and so are Karna and
Subala's son. Even a common enemy should not be despised by the stronger."

His attendants knew his mind and ran to yoke the chariot of the bearer of the
discus and mace. It was like the blazing fire of time, like a traveller in the
sky. Its two wheels were bright as the sun and moon. It was bright with
half-moons and moons, fish, beasts and birds, flowers of every kind, gems and
jewels. It shone like the young sun, huge and lovely to look at, worked with
gems and gold, with a fine banner and fine flags, well equipped, unassailable,
covered with tiger skins. It took away the fame of enemies and added to the joy
of the Yadus. They yoked it with the horses Sainya, Sugriva, Meghapushpa and
Balahaka, bathed and fully equipped. To add to Krishna's greatness, the
loud-sounding chariot was fitted with the banner of the king of birds. Shauri
mounted that chariot, which was like the peak of Meru and roared like a cloud
and a drum, as a man of good deeds mounts a heavenly car. Then the best of men
took Satyaki up with him and set out, filling earth and sky with the roar of the
chariot.

In a moment the weather cleared of clouds. A kind wind blew behind him, and the
dust settled. Lucky beasts and birds went with Vasudeva as he set out, keeping
him on their right. Cranes, woodpeckers and swans followed Madhusudana
everywhere with lucky cries. The fire, fed with great offerings and mantras,
burnt without smoke, its flames turning to the right. Vasishtha, Vamadeva,
Bhuridyumna, Gaya and Kratha were there, with Shukra, Narada and Valmiki, the
Maruts, Kushika and Bhrigu. They and the brahmin and divine seers together
walked round Krishna, the younger brother of Vasava, who brings happiness to the
Yadus. Honoured in this way by those fortunate hosts of great seers and good
men, Krishna set out for the dwelling of the Kurus.

As he went, Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, followed him, and Bhimasena and Arjuna,
and the two Pandava sons of Madri. Bold Chekitana, Dhrishtaketu lord of the
Chedis, Drupada and the king of Kashi went too. The great chariot fighter
Shikhandi, Dhrishtadyumna with his son, and Virata with the Kekayas, kshatriyas
all, followed the bull of the kshatriyas to see him on his way. When he had
followed Govinda a little way, the glorious Dharma King Yudhishthira spoke in
the presence of the kings.

"Keshava is never moved to injustice by desire or fear, by greed or for gain. He
is firm of mind and free of craving. He knows dharma, he is steady and wise in
all beings, the lord of all beings, the mighty god of gods."

The son of Kunti embraced Keshava, rich in every virtue, marked with the
Shrivatsa, and began to give him his message.

"She raised us from childhood, a woman alone. She is given to fasting and
austerity, always busy with rites for our welfare, with worship of gods and
guests, and with service to elders. She is loving and loves her sons, and she is
dear to us, Janardana. She saved us from fear of Suyodhana, tamer of enemies, as
a boat saves men from the ocean, from the great press of death. For our sake she
has always suffered sorrows, Madhava, though she did not deserve sorrow. Ask
after her health. Comfort her greatly, for she is drowned in grief for her sons.
Greet her and embrace her, and speak of the Pandavas.

"Since her marriage she has suffered sorrows from her in-laws, tamer of enemies,
and seen insults she did not deserve, and she lives in sorrow. Will that time
ever come, Krishna, when sorrow turns, and I can set my suffering mother in
comfort, tamer of enemies? When we went into exile, she ran after us in her
misery, longing for her sons. We left her weeping and went to the forest. Surely
she will not die of sorrow, if she still lives, Keshava, so deeply grieved for
her sons, she who was honoured by the Anarta. Greet her for me, lord, Krishna,
and Dhritarashtra the Kaurava, and the kings older in years, Bhishma, Drona,
Kripa and great King Bahlika, Drona's son and Somadatta and all the Bharatas,
each of them. Embrace Vidura, Madhusudana, the very wise keeper of the Kurus'
counsel, of fathomless understanding, who knows dharma."

When he had said this to Keshava in the middle of the kings, Yudhishthira took
his leave, walked round Krishna, and turned back.

But Bibhatsu, walking along, spoke to his friend, the bull among men, the
Dasharha, the slayer of enemy heroes, the unbeaten. "What we agreed on before in
our counsel, lord, about half the kingdom, Govinda, is known to all the kings.
If he gives it without holding back, with honour and without insult, it will
please me, strong-armed one, and they will be freed from a great fear. If
Dhritarashtra's son, who does not know what is good for him, does otherwise, I
shall surely make an end of the kshatriyas, Janardana."

When the Pandava said this, Vrikodara was delighted. Again and again he trembled
with rage. Trembling, the son of Kunti roared great roars, his heart swelling
with delight when he heard Dhananjaya's words. When they heard that roar, the
bowmen trembled, and all the mounts let fall dung and urine.

When he had spoken to Keshava like this, and told him his resolve, he took his
leave, embraced Janardana and turned back. When all the kings had turned back,
Janardana sped on gladly, drawn by Sainya and Sugriva. Driven by Daruka,
Vasudeva's horses seemed to drink up the road and swallow the sky.

Then the strong-armed Keshava saw seers on the road, standing on both sides of
the way, blazing with the splendour of Brahman. Janardana got down from his
chariot at once, greeted them and spoke to all those seers with honour. "Is all
well in the worlds? Is dharma well kept? Do the three classes stand under the
command of the brahmins?" When he had paid them honour, Madhusudana said, "Where
have you holy ones come from? What is your road here? What is your business,
holy ones, and what can I do for you? For what purpose have you come to the
earth?"

Rama Jamadagnya came up to Madhusudana, his friend in good deeds of old, and
embraced Govinda. "Divine seers who do good deeds, Dasharha, learned brahmins,
royal seers and ascetics, who saw the ancient wars of the gods and asuras, are
here. They want to see the kshatriyas gathered from every side, the kings
sitting in the assembly, and you, the truth, Janardana. We are going to see this
great sight, Keshava. We want to hear the words of dharma and purpose you will
speak among the Kurus, in the middle of the kings, scorcher of enemies. Bhishma,
Drona and the others, and very wise Vidura, and you, tiger of the Yadavas, will
meet in the assembly. We want to hear your heavenly words there, Madhava, and
theirs, true and good. We have taken our leave of you, strong-armed one. We will
see you again. Go without hindrance, hero. We will see you in the assembly."

---

Ten great chariot fighters, strong-armed breakers of enemy heroes, followed
Devaki's son as he went, weapons in hand. So did a thousand foot soldiers and as
many horsemen, scorcher of enemies, with plenty of food, king, and hundreds of
servants.

"How did the great Dasharha Madhusudana travel?" asked Janamejaya. "What omens
were there as the mighty one went?"

"Hear from me the wonders that appeared as the great one set out," said
Vaishampayana, "the heavenly ones, the divine ones and the portents. Thunder
with lightning sounded from a cloudless sky. The rain cloud followed and rained
hard where there were no clouds. The great rivers that flow east, the best of
rivers, flowed west. All the directions were turned about, and nothing could be
made out. Fires blazed, king, and the earth shook. Wells and jars spilled their
water in hundreds. This whole world was covered in darkness. The directions and
the spaces between could not be made out for dust, king. A great sound came from
the sky, but no body was seen. In every land, king, it was like a wonder. A
harsh wind from the southwest, roaring terribly, tore at Hastinapura, uprooting
trees in crowds.

"But wherever the Varshneya went on his road, Bharata, the wind was pleasant and
everything went to the right. Flowers rained down, and lotuses in plenty. The
road was smooth and painless, without kusha grass or thorns. As he went, the
strong-armed giver of wealth was honoured here and there by brahmins with the
honey drink and flowers. Women met him on the road and scattered sweet wild
flowers on that great one, who delights in the good of all beings.

"He passed the lovely Shalibhavana, full of every kind of crop, happy and very
devoted to dharma, bull of the Bharatas. He saw pleasant villages with many
cattle, which gladdened the heart, and he passed towns and many kingdoms. The
people were always glad and well disposed, protected by the Bharatas, not
troubled by enemy armies and knowing nothing of misfortune. The people who lived
in the towns stood together by the road, wanting to see Vishvaksena coming from
Upaplavya. They all honoured him as he came, the guest of their land, who
deserved honour, a lord like a blazing fire.

"Keshava, slayer of enemy heroes, reached Vrikasthala when the sun, its rays
spread, was clear and turning red. He got down from the chariot at once,
purified himself as the rule lays down, ordered the chariot unyoked, and sat
down to the twilight prayers. Daruka unyoked the horses, tended them by the
rule, took off all their harness and let them go. When all that was done,
Madhusudana said, 'We will spend the night here, for Yudhishthira's business.'

"Knowing his wish, the men made a lodging, and in a moment they had food and
drink of fine quality ready. The chief brahmins of that village, noble, of good
family and modest, who lived as brahmins should, came to great Hrishikesha,
tamer of enemies, and honoured him properly with blessings and lucky rites. When
they had honoured the Dasharha, who is honoured in all the worlds, they offered
the great one their houses full of jewels. The lord said, 'It is done,' honoured
them as they deserved, went to their houses, and came back with them. There
Keshava fed the brahmins well, ate with them all, and spent that night happily.

---

"Dhritarashtra learned from his messengers that Madhusudana was coming. His hair
stood on end. He honoured strong-armed Bhishma, Drona, Sanjaya, very wise Vidura
and Duryodhana with his counsellors, and said to them, 'A great and wonderful
thing is being heard, joy of the Kurus. Women, children and old men are talking
of it in every house. Some speak of it with honour, and others in gatherings.
There is talk of it in every crossroads and hall. The Dasharha is coming, the
bold one, on the Pandavas' business. Madhusudana is to be respected and honoured
by us in every way. The life of the world is in him, for he is the lord of
beings. Steadiness, courage, wisdom and strength are in Madhava. Let that best
of men be honoured, for he is eternal dharma. If he is honoured, it will bring
happiness. If he is not, it will bring unhappiness. If the Dasharha, tamer of
enemies, is pleased with our attentions, we shall win all our aims among all the
kings. Make ready today to honour him, scorcher of enemies. Have halls built on
the road, with every comfort. Son of Gandhari, act so that he will feel
affection for you. Or what do you think, Bhishma?'

"Then Bhishma and all the others praised the king's words and said, 'Excellent.'
Knowing that they approved, King Duryodhana began to mark out sites for halls.
In pleasant places here and there they built many halls, full of every kind of
jewel. The king provided wonderful seats with every kind of comfort, women,
perfumes, ornaments and fine clothes, good food and drink of many kinds, and
sweet-smelling garlands. Above all, the Kaurava king built a lovely hall full of
jewels in the village of Vrikasthala for him to stay in. When he had arranged
all this, fit for the gods and more than human, King Duryodhana told
Dhritarashtra. But Keshava, the Dasharha, did not even look at those halls or
the many jewels, and went on to the dwelling of the Kurus.

"'Steward,' said Dhritarashtra, 'Janardana has come here from Upaplavya. He is
staying at Vrikasthala, and he will be here in the morning. Janardana is the
lord of the Ahukas, the leader of all the Satvatas, great-minded, very brave,
very wealthy. Madhava is the upholder and protector of the prosperous line of
the Vrishnis, the blessed great-grandfather of the three worlds. The Vrishnis
and Andhakas, with glad hearts, follow his wisdom, as the Adityas, Vasus and
Rudras follow the understanding of Brihaspati. I will pay honour to the great
Dasharha in front of you, you who know dharma. Hear me tell you of it.

"'I will give him sixteen golden chariots, each yoked with four of the finest
horses, all of one colour, very dark, born in Bahlika. I will give Keshava eight
elephants, always in rut, with tusks like poles, hard-hitting, each with eight
attendants. I will give him a hundred lovely slave girls, golden-skinned, who
have not borne children, and as many male slaves. I will give him eighteen
thousand of the soft woollens that the mountain people bring. And I will give
him thousands of deerskins from the land of the Chinas, as many as Keshava
deserves. This clear, bright jewel shines by day and by night. I will give him
that too, for Keshava deserves it. I will give him a carriage drawn by she-mules
that covers fourteen yojanas in a day. However many mounts and men he has, I
will always give him food eight times over.

"'All my sons and grandsons except Duryodhana will go out to meet the Dasharha
in polished chariots, adorned. Thousands of fine, well-adorned courtesans will
go out on foot to meet the fortunate Keshava. Whatever lovely girls from the
city go to see Janardana will go unveiled. The whole city, with its women, men
and children, looks to great Madhusudana as creatures look to the sun. Let great
banners and flags be set up in every direction, and let his road be sprinkled
with water and free of dust.' So he ordered. 'Duhshasana's house is better than
Duryodhana's. Let it be quickly cleaned and adorned for him. It is graced with
beautiful mansions, pleasant and lovely, rich in every season. All the jewels in
this house of mine and Duryodhana's, whatever the Varshneya deserves, must be
given to him, no doubt of it.'"

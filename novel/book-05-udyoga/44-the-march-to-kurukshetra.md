# The March to Kurukshetra

When Yudhishthira the Dharma King heard Janardana's words, he spoke to his
brothers in Keshava's presence, righteous as he was.

"You have heard what happened in the Kuru assembly, and you have taken in all
that Keshava said. So divide the army for me, best of men. These seven armies
have gathered for victory. Hear the names of their seven famous leaders:
Drupada, Virata, Dhrishtadyumna and Shikhandi, Satyaki, Chekitana and mighty
Bhimasena. These are the heroes who lead the armies, all ready to give up their
bodies. All of them know the Vedas and are brave, and all keep their vows well.
They are modest and wise, all skilled in war, clever with bow and arrow and able
to fight with every weapon. Sahadeva, joy of the Kurus, tell us first your own
view, tiger among men. Who can lead all seven armies, who knows how to array
them, and who could stand up in battle to Bhishma, who is like a fire whose
flames are arrows? Who is fit to be our commander?"

"He is joined to us and shares our sorrow," said Sahadeva. "He is a mighty king
who knows dharma, and with his help we are claiming our share. Virata of the
Matsyas is strong, skilled in weapons and fierce in battle. He will stand up in
battle to Bhishma and those great chariot fighters."

When Sahadeva had said this, Nakula, skilled in speech, spoke next. "In age, in
learning, in steadiness, in family and in birth, he is modest, well born and
glorious, and skilled in all the sciences. He learned weapons from Bharadvaja.
He is hard to face and true to his word, and he always rivals Drona and mighty
Bhishma. He is praised at the head of the assembled kings as an army's leader,
surrounded by sons and grandsons like a tree with a hundred branches. That king,
with his wife, practised terrible austerity in his anger to destroy Drona, the
hero, the glory of the assembly. He always cares for us like a father, the bull
among kings. Let our father-in-law Drupada lead the army. I think he can stand
against Drona and Bhishma when they come on, for the king knows divine weapons
and is Angiras's friend."

When the two sons of Madri had given their views, the joy of the Kurus,
Savyasachin, son of Vasava and equal to Vasava, spoke.

"By the power of austerity and the seers' pleasure a divine man was born, the
colour of flame and mighty. He rose from the fire pit with bow, armour and
sword, mounted on a chariot yoked with fine divine horses, roaring like a great
cloud with the rumble of his chariot. He is a hero built like a lion, with a
lion's stride, a lion's chest and a lion's roar, strong-armed and mighty, with a
lion's shoulders and great splendour. He has fine brows, fine teeth and a fine
jaw, fine arms and a fine face. He is not thin. He has a fine collarbone, large
eyes and fine feet, and stands firm. No weapon can pierce him. He is like an
elephant in rut. He was born to destroy Drona, true in speech and master of his
senses. I think Dhrishtadyumna can stand Bhishma's arrows, which strike like
thunderbolts, with blazing mouths like snakes, as swift as Yama's messengers and
falling like fire, which Rama withstood in battle, terrible as the crash of the
thunderbolt. King, I see no man but Dhrishtadyumna who could stand up to the man
of the great vow. That is my view. Quick of hand and a fighter of many skills,
he is my choice for commander, glorious, with armour no weapon can pierce, like
the leader of a herd of elephants."

"Shikhandi, son of Drupada, was born to kill him," said Bhima, "as the siddhas
and the seers who gathered said, king of kings. When he shows his divine weapon
in the middle of battle, men will see his form like great Rama's. I see no one
in battle who could break Shikhandi with a weapon when he is armoured and in his
chariot. King, no one but the hero Shikhandi could stand up to Bhishma of the
great vow in single combat. He is my choice for commander."

"The righteous Keshava knows the strength and weakness, the worth and the lack
of worth, of the whole world, my friend," said Yudhishthira, "past and future.
Let the one Krishna the Dasharha names be our commander, skilled in weapons or
not, old or young. He is the root of our victory, my friend, and of our defeat.
In him are our lives and our kingdom, our being and not being, our joy and our
sorrow. He is the maker and the ordainer, and success rests on him. The one
Krishna the Dasharha names is fit to be our commander. Let the best of speakers
say. The night is passing. When we have made our commander the man Krishna
chooses, we shall set out for the field of battle when the rest of the night is
over, with our weapons blessed and our lucky rites done."

When he heard the wise Dharma King's words, the lotus-eyed one looked at
Dhananjaya and spoke.

"I too, great king, think these men you have named fit to lead your army. They
are brave, and fight with courage, and all of them could crush your enemies.
They would frighten even Indra in a great battle, let alone the greedy,
evil-minded sons of Dhritarashtra. I too made a great effort there for your
sake, strong-armed tamer of enemies, to bring about peace, Bharata. We have paid
our debt to dharma, and those who find fault cannot speak against us. The
foolish son of Dhritarashtra, who has no judgement, thinks he has already won.
Sick as he is, he thinks himself strong. Let the army be made ready, for I think
they can only be won over by death. The sons of Dhritarashtra will not be able
to stand their ground. They will see Dhananjaya, and Bhimasena in his anger, and
the twins like Yama, and resentful Dhrishtadyumna with Yuyudhana, Abhimanyu and
the sons of Draupadi, Virata and Drupada, and the other kings, lords of armies,
firm in courage. Our army is strong, hard to attack and hard to face. It will
surely destroy the army of Dhritarashtra's sons in battle."

When Krishna said this, the best of men were delighted, and a great roar rose
from them in their joy. "Make ready!" cried the troops, hurrying and running
about. On every side rose the noise of horses and elephants, the rumble of
wheels, and the din of conches and drums.

As the Pandavas set out with their armies, the host looked like the Ganga in
flood, impossible to hold back. In the vanguard went Bhimasena and the two sons
of Madri in their armour, the son of Subhadra and the sons of Draupadi,
Dhrishtadyumna of the Prishatas, and the Prabhadrakas and Panchalas, with
Bhimasena at their head. A noise rose like the sea at the turn of the moon. The
shouts of the glad men as they set out seemed to touch the sky. In the middle of
the glad, armoured fighters, breakers of enemy ranks, went King Yudhishthira,
son of Kunti. The king took with him the carts, the market stalls and tents, the
vehicles and draught animals, the treasury, the engines and weapons, the
physicians and doctors, whatever part of the army was weak, thin or feeble, and
the servants.

Truthful Draupadi of Panchala turned back to Upaplavya with the women,
surrounded by her serving women and men. The sons of Pandu set out with their
great camp, having set guards at the base with stationary and moving troops.
They went surrounded by brahmins, giving away cows and gold, and praised, king,
in their jewelled chariots.

The Kekayas, Dhrishtaketu and the powerful son of the king of Kashi, Shreniman
and Vasudana, and unbeaten Shikhandi, all glad and content, in armour, armed and
adorned, went round King Yudhishthira and followed him. In the rear went Virata
and Yajnasena, the Somaka, Sudharma, Kuntibhoja and the sons of Dhrishtadyumna,
with forty thousand chariots, five times as many horses, ten times as many foot
soldiers and sixty thousand elephants. Anadhrishti, Chekitana, the king of the
Chedis and Satyaki all went round Vasudeva and Dhananjaya.

When the Pandavas reached Kurukshetra, with their armies arrayed, the fighters
looked like bulls bellowing. They plunged into Kurukshetra and blew their
conches, the tamers of enemies, and Vasudeva and Dhananjaya blew theirs. When
all the armies heard the roar of the Panchajanya, like the crash of the
thunderbolt, they were filled with joy. The lion roars of the mighty men, mixed
with conches and drums, made the earth, the sky and the oceans ring.

Then King Yudhishthira camped his army on level, fertile ground, with plenty of
fodder and firewood. He avoided burning grounds, temples, the ashrams of great
seers, holy bathing places and shrines. King Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, had the
camp made on sweet, unsalted ground that was kind and holy. Then, rested, with
his animals refreshed, he rose again and set out, surrounded by hundreds of
thousands of kings. Keshava with Partha went all round, scattering hundreds of
the outposts of Dhritarashtra's son. Dhrishtadyumna of the Prishatas and the
splendid chariot fighter Satyaki Yuyudhana measured out the camp.

Reaching the holy river Hiranvati at Kurukshetra, with good bathing places,
clean water, and free of stones and mud, Keshava had a moat dug there, Bharata.
For its protection he gave orders and placed troops there. Keshava had camps
made for the kings of the same kind as the great Pandavas' camp. There were
hundreds and thousands of them, with plenty of water and firewood, hard to
attack, and stocked with food of every kind. The costly camps of the kings stood
there separately, like flying palaces set on the ground, king of kings. There
were hundreds of skilled craftsmen there, paid their wages, and able physicians
with every kind of tool. King Yudhishthira had heaps as high as mountains made
of bowstrings, bows, armour and weapons, of honey and butter, and of lac and
dust. He provided plenty of water, good fodder, chaff and charcoal in every
camp. Great engines, iron arrows, lances, spears and axes, bows and armour were
gathered in the men's minds then. Elephants were seen there like mountains, in
coats of mail with iron armour on top, able to fight hundreds of thousands. When
their friends knew that the Pandavas had camped there, Bharata, they came as
they had been told, with their armies and their animals. Kings who had kept the
vow of chastity, drunk the Soma and given generous fees, came together for the
victory of the sons of Pandu.

"Yudhishthira came with his army wanting to fight," said Janamejaya, "and camped
at Kurukshetra, guarded by Vasudeva. Virata and Drupada were with him, and their
sons, and the Kekayas and Vrishnis and hundreds of kings, guarding him as the
Adityas guard great Indra. What did King Duryodhana do when he heard of it? I
want to hear it all at length, you who are rich in austerity, all that happened
in that great confusion at Kurujangala. For the Pandavas and Vasudeva, Virata
and Drupada, Dhrishtadyumna of the Panchalas, the great chariot fighter
Shikhandi and brave Yuyudhana, whom even the gods could hardly face, could shake
an army of the gods in battle. I want to hear at length, you who are rich in
austerity, all that the Kurus and the Pandavas did."

"When the Dasharha had gone," said Vaishampayana, "King Duryodhana said to
Karna, Duhshasana and Shakuni, 'Adhokshaja has gone to the sons of Pritha with
nothing done. He is filled with anger, and he will surely stir them up. Vasudeva
wants my war with the Pandavas, and Bhimasena and Arjuna stand by the Dasharha's
view. Ajatashatru too follows Bhima and Arjuna now, and I cheated him before,
with all his brothers. Virata and Drupada have feuds with me, and they lead the
armies and follow Vasudeva. This war will be terrible and hair-raising. So have
everything made ready for battle, without idling. Let camps be made at
Kurukshetra, lords of the earth, hundreds and thousands of them, with plenty of
room, that the enemy cannot take, with water and wood close by. Let their supply
roads be safe from being cut, and let them be full of heaps of jewels and every
kind of weapon, with flags and banners. Let level roads be made to them outside
the city. Let the march be proclaimed today, for tomorrow, without delay.'

"They agreed, and next day they did it. The great ones were glad, and it was for
the destruction of the kings. When they heard the king's command, all the kings
rose from their costly seats, full of resentment. They slowly stroked their
arms, like iron bars, bright with golden armlets and smeared with sandal and
aloe. With hands like white lotuses they tied their turbans and their lower and
upper garments and all their ornaments. The best of chariot fighters made ready
their chariots, those skilled with horses their horses, and those trained with
elephants their elephants. They made ready many bright golden coats of armour
and all kinds of weapons. The foot soldiers took up weapons of every kind, many
of them bright with gold.

"The city of Dhritarashtra's son was as crowded as a festival, Bharata, full of
glad men. The great ocean of the Kuru king appeared then like the ocean at
moonrise, king. Its whirlpools were crowds of people, its fish were chariots,
elephants and horses. Its roar was the sound of conches and drums, and its gems
were the treasure hoards. Its waves were bright ornaments and armour, and its
clean foam was weapons. It was ringed by rows of palaces for mountains, and its
great pools were the streets and markets, and it rose with the moon of its
fighters."

Remembering Vasudeva's words, Yudhishthira asked the Varshneya again, "How could
the fool say that? Now that this time has come, Achyuta, what is right for us?
How can we act and not fall from our own dharma? You know the minds of
Duryodhana, Karna and Shakuni, son of Subala, Vasudeva, and of me and my
brothers. You have heard the words of Vidura and Bhishma, and you have heard all
of Kunti's wisdom, wise one. Setting all this aside, and thinking it over again
and again, tell us without hesitating what is right for us, strong-armed one."

When Krishna heard the Dharma King's words, full of dharma and gain, he spoke in
a voice like cloud and drum.

"I told him words that were good, and full of dharma and gain. But they found no
place in the Kaurava, whose wisdom is trickery. The fool does not listen to
Bhishma or Vidura, or to anything I say. He goes past it all. He does not want
dharma or fame. The wicked man relies on Karna and thinks he has already won
everything. Suyodhana even ordered me bound, but the wicked man, who goes beyond
all command, did not get his wish. Neither Bhishma nor Drona said anything
fitting there. All follow him except Vidura, Achyuta. Shakuni, son of Subala,
Karna and Duhshasana, fools, said unfitting things about you to the resentful
fool. What use is it for me to tell you what the Kauravas said? In short, the
wicked man does not behave rightly towards you. In all these kings who are your
soldiers there is not all the evil and ill that is in him. And we do not want
peace with the Kauravas at the price of giving up too much. What comes next is
war."

When they heard what Vasudeva said, all the kings said nothing and looked at the
king's face, Bharata. Yudhishthira saw what the kings meant and gave the order
to make ready, with Bhima, Arjuna and the twins. When the order was given, the
Pandava army shouted, and the soldiers were glad.

But when the Dharma King Yudhishthira saw the slaughter of those who should not
be killed, he groaned and said to Bhimasena and Vijaya, "This is why we went to
the forest and suffered. And now this great harm comes on us however hard we
tried to avoid it. What we strove for, we lost through our striving, and the
great age of strife has come on us when we did not strive for it. How can we
fight those who should not be killed? How can we win by killing our old
teachers?"

When Savyasachin, scorcher of enemies, heard the Dharma King, he repeated to him
what Vasudeva had said. "The son of Devaki has told you what Kunti and Vidura
said, king, and you have taken it all in. I am sure they would not counsel
adharma. And it is not right, son of Kunti, to turn back without fighting."

When Vasudeva heard Savyasachin, he smiled and said to Partha, "That is so."
Then the sons of Pandu, their minds made up for war, great king, spent that
night happily with their armies.

**Divergences.**

* **The commander is not named here.** Krishna's answer praises all the leaders but names none (5.149.39–46). A rejected line (\*541) has him say, "I think Dhrishtadyumna should be commander." Not narrated. The appointment comes later in the book.
* **The order of events.** The CE tells the march and the camp at Kurukshetra (5.149.47–84) before Yudhishthira's renewed question and the night before the march (5.151). The novel keeps the CE's order.
* **Krishna's embassy, told again.** Krishna's short account at 5.151.7–15, that Duryodhana ordered him bound and that neither Bhishma nor Drona spoke fittingly, is his, as the text gives it.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*540 and \*542.

**Choices.**

* 5.149.17 *sakhā cāṅgirasaḥ*: "Angiras's friend", taken as Drona, of the line of Angiras. Drupada and Drona had been friends as boys.
* 5.149.26 *rāmeṇājau viṣahitān*: Bhishma's arrows "which Rama withstood in battle".
* 5.149.30: Bhima's comparison of Shikhandi to Rama is his.

* 5.149.61: the numbers are the text's. *ayutāni catvāri* is forty thousand; the elephants, *sādinām ayutāni ṣaṭ*, are read as sixty thousand, with Ganguli.

# Bhima Speaks Softly

"Slayer of Madhu," said Bhimasena, "speak in whatever way will bring peace to
the Kurus. Do not frighten them with war. Duryodhana is resentful and always
angry. He hates his own good and he is proud. Do not speak harshly to him. Deal
with him gently.

"He is evil by nature, and his mind is like a bandit's. He is drunk with the
pride of power, and he has made enemies of the Pandavas. He does not look far
ahead. He is harsh and scornful, and cruel in his courage. His anger lasts long,
and no one can guide him. He is wicked and he loves a trick. He would die before
he would bend, and he will never give up his own opinion. I think peace with a
man like that is very hard to win, Krishna. He turns his back even on his
friends. He has given up dharma and he loves a lie. He crushes his friends'
words and their feelings too. He gives way to his anger and holds fast to his
bad nature. He follows evil by his nature, like a snake stung by the grass. You
know everything about Duryodhana, the army he has, his character, his nature,
his strength and his courage.

"Before, the Kurus and their sons were happy, and so were we. We and our kin
were glad together, like the gods with Indra at their head. Now the Bharatas
will be burned by Duryodhana's anger, slayer of Madhu, as forests are burned by
fire when the cold season ends.

"There are eighteen kings well known for this, slayer of Madhu, who wiped out
their kinsmen, friends and families. Among the asuras, when they were rich and
blazing with power, Bali was born when the time came for dharma's turn. Udavarta
was born among the Haihayas, Janamejaya among the Nipas, Bahula among the
Talajanghas, and proud Vasu among the Krimis. Ajabindu was born among the
Suviras, Kusharddhika among the Surashtras, Arkaja among the Balihas and
Dhautamulaka among the Chinas. Hayagriva was born among the Videhas, Varapra
among the Mahaujasas, Bahu among the Sundaravegas and Pururavas among the
Diptakshas. Sahaja was born among the Chedis and Matsyas, Brihadbala among the
Prachetas, Dharana among the Indravatsas, Vigahana among the Mukutas and Shama
among the Nandivegas. These were the lowest of men, Krishna, and they brought
shame on their families. They were born at the end of an age.

"Perhaps this Duryodhana has been brought by time to us Kurus at the end of an
age, a firebrand among the family, the lowest of men and an evil man. So speak
to him gently and slowly, you whose courage is fierce, in words of dharma and
gain. Say much that suits what he wants, and nothing harsh. We would all follow
Duryodhana humbly, Krishna, and walk behind him, so that the Bharatas are not
destroyed. Vasudeva, act in such a way that we can at least live apart from the
Kurus, neither friends nor enemies, and no wrong may touch the Kurus. Krishna,
tell the old grandfather and all who sit in the hall that there must be
brotherly love between the brothers, and that the son of Dhritarashtra must be
calmed. This is what I say, and the king praises it. Arjuna does not want war
either, for there is great kindness in Arjuna."

When strong-armed Keshava heard this, he all but laughed. Bhima had never spoken
so softly before. To Shauri, Rama's younger brother, who carries the Sharnga
bow, it seemed as if a mountain had turned light, or fire had turned cold. He
fired Bhima up with words, as the wind fires a flame, and spoke to him as he sat
there, full of pity.

"At other times, Bhimasena, you have praised nothing but war. You wanted to
crush the cruel sons of Dhritarashtra, who delight in killing. You do not sleep,
scorcher of enemies. You lie awake, face down. You always speak terrible,
restless, wounding words. You sigh, and burn in your own fiery anger. Your mind
is never at peace, Bhima, and you are like a fire with smoke. You lie alone and
groan, like a weak man under a heavy load. Some people who do not know you think
you are mad. You tear up trees by the roots, like an elephant breaking them, and
you run about groaning and stamping the earth, Bhima. You take no pleasure in
people, son of Pandu. You hide yourself away. You do not welcome anyone, by
night or day. You sit alone, smiling for no reason, and then as if you were
crying. You sit for a long time with your head on your knees and your eyes shut.
Then you are seen frowning again and again, Bhima, and licking your lips. All of
that is your anger's doing.

"As surely as the sun rises bright in the east, and sets in the west and circles
the pole star with its rays, so surely, I tell you truly, will this come about.
I shall go out with my mace and kill Duryodhana, who cannot bear anything. That
is what you swore among your brothers, holding your mace, by the truth. And now
your mind is set on peace, scorcher of enemies.

"Well! When the time for war has come, do you see things against war, and things
that are not against it? Has fear taken hold of you, Bhima? Well, son of Pritha,
perhaps you see bad omens, in your sleep or when you wake, and so you want
peace. Well! You hope for nothing from the manhood in you, like a eunuch. You
are overcome by weakness, and so your mind has changed. Your heart trembles,
your mind sinks and your thighs have gone stiff, and so you want peace. A
mortal's mind is not steady, they say, son of Pritha. It moves this way and
that, like a silk-cotton seed blown by the wind. This changed mind of yours is
as strange as a cow speaking with a human voice. It sinks the hearts of Pandu's
sons as if they had no boat.

"This is a great wonder to me, like a mountain moving, that you should speak
such unworthy words, Bhimasena. Look at your own deeds, Bharata, and at the
family you were born in, and rise up. Do not despair, hero. Be firm. This
weakness does not suit you, tamer of enemies. What a kshatriya does not win by
his strength, he does not enjoy."

At Vasudeva's words Bhima, whose anger never slept and who could bear nothing,
sprang up like a good horse, and answered at once.

"I mean to do one thing, Achyuta, and you think I mean another. I love a fight
more than anything, and my courage in battle is true. You know my nature,
Dasharha, for you have lived with me a long time. Or perhaps you do not know me,
as a man swimming in a pool does not know its depth. That is why you attack me
with such unworthy words. For who that knows me, Bhimasena, could say such
unworthy things to me, Madhava, as you do? So I shall tell you this, joy of the
Vrishnis, about my own manhood and my strength, which has no equal. It is never
a noble thing to praise oneself. But I have been stung by your insults, and so I
shall tell you my strength.

"Look at heaven and earth, Krishna, where all these creatures live. They do not
move, they have no end, and they hold everything up, and they are the mothers of
all. If they were suddenly to rush together in anger, like two rocks, I could
hold them apart with my two arms, with all that moves and does not move in them.
Look at the space between my arms, which are like great iron bars. I do not see
the man who could get into it and get out again. The Himalaya, the ocean, and
the thunderbolt-wielding slayer of Bala himself, these three with all their
strength could not save a man I had seized. I could fight all the kshatriyas who
come against the Pandavas. I shall stand on them with the soles of my feet on
the ground.

"It is not that you do not know my courage, Achyuta. You know how I conquered
kings and brought them under my power. And if you do not know me, like the light
of the rising sun, you will know me, Janardana, deep in the crush of battle. Why
do you cut me with harsh words, blameless one, as if you were opening a wound
with a needle? I tell you as much as I understand. Know that I am more than
that. On the day the slaughter begins, in the crush of battle, you will see me
driving off elephants, chariot fighters and horsemen. You and the world will see
me in my anger, killing men and the bulls among kshatriyas, and dragging off the
best of the best. My marrow does not fail and my mind does not tremble. If the
whole world were angry with me, I would feel no fear. But this is friendship,
slayer of Madhu. It is out of kindness that I bear all these troubles, so that
the Bharatas are not destroyed."

"I said it to learn your mind," said the Lord. "I said it out of love, not to
insult you, nor to show my learning, nor out of anger, nor to make a speech. I
know your greatness, and I know your strength. I know your deeds. I do not think
little of you. Whatever good you believe is in you, son of Pandu, I believe
there is a thousand times more. You are just as a man should be who is born in a
family honoured by all the kings, Bhima, with such kin and such friends.

"When people try to know a doubtful matter of dharma, Vrikodara, they cannot
settle which way the divine and the human will turn. The very thing that brings
a man success in his aims brings about his ruin too. A man's work is always
doubtful. Things the wise have seen one way, looking for faults, turn out
another way, like the gusts of the wind. Human work that is well planned, well
carried out and done properly is thwarted by fate. And fate, which is not man's
doing, is overcome by man's effort. So it is with cold and heat and rain,
Bharata, and hunger and thirst. And whatever else a man does of his own accord,
beyond what is fated for him, is not hindered, and there is the mark of it.

"The world lives in no other way than by work, son of Pandu. A man should go on
with this understanding, that the result comes from the two together. A man who
has made up his mind in this way and simply gets on with his work is not
troubled when he fails, and does not rejoice when he succeeds. This was all I
meant to say, Bhimasena. You must not think that success against the Kurus in
battle is certain. You must not slacken the reins too far. When things go wrong,
you must not fall into despair or weakness. That is why I say this to you.

"When morning comes, son of Pandu, I shall go to Dhritarashtra. I shall try to
make peace without giving up anything of yours. If they make peace, the fame
will be mine for ever, and you will have what you want, and they will have the
greatest good. If the Kurus hold to their course and will not accept what I say,
then there will be war, and terrible work.

"In this war, Bhimasena, the load rests on you. The yoke will have to be borne
by Arjuna, and the other men will be carried along. I shall be Bibhatsu's
charioteer when the battle comes. That is what Dhananjaya wants, not that I do
not want war. That is why I doubted your mind, Vrikodara. I goaded you with
manly words, and fired up your spirit."

**Divergences.**

* **Bali, not Kali.** Ganguli, following the vulgate, says Kali was born among the asuras. The CE reads *balir ajāyata* (5.72.12): Bali. The list of eighteen is Bali and the seventeen kings named after him (5.72.12–17).
* **The names of the kings.** Several of Ganguli's names differ from the CE's. He has Rushardhik, Varayu, Vrishaddhaja among the Praviras and Chandra-batsyas; the CE has Kusharddhika, Varapra, Brihadbala among the Prachetas and Indravatsas (5.72.14–16). The CE's names are used.
* \*375 and \*376, rejected lines with more epithets for Bhima, are not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.72.7 *tṛṇais tunna ivoragaḥ*: "like a snake stung by the grass", that is, provoked by every touch.
* 5.72.9 *indrajyeṣṭhā ivābhūma*: "like the gods with Indra at their head".
* 5.72.12 *paryāyakāle dharmasya*: "when the time came for dharma's turn". The sense is that Bali was born when the asuras' dharma had run its course.
* 5.72.13–17: the peoples are named as the text names them. The Janamejaya, Pururavas, Bahu and Brihadbala here are these otherwise unknown kings, not the famous bearers of those names.
* 5.72.21 *udāsīnavṛttiḥ*: "live apart from the Kurus, neither friends nor enemies".
* 5.73.2: Shauri, Rama's younger brother, and the Sharnga bow are the verse's epithets for Krishna. "Rama" here is Balarama.
* 5.73.5 *nyubjaḥ śeṣe*: "you lie face down".
* 5.73.12–14: the sun image frames Bhima's oath. The oath itself, "I shall go out with my mace and kill Duryodhana", is Bhima's as Krishna quotes it.
* 5.73.15: the play on *yuddhapratīpāni* and *apratīpāni* is kept as "things against war, and things that are not against it".
* 5.73.18 *ūrustambhagṛhīto 'si*: "your thighs have gone stiff".
* 5.73.19 *aṣṭhīlā śālmaleḥ*: "a silk-cotton seed".
* 5.74.1 *sadaśvavat samādhāvat*: "sprang up like a good horse".
* 5.74.3 *plavan hrada ivālpavaḥ*: read as "as a man swimming in a pool does not know its depth". The half-line is compressed.
* 5.74.10 *vajrī ca balabhit*: Indra, "the thunderbolt-wielding slayer of Bala".
* 5.75.5–10: Krishna's teaching on fate (*daiva*) and human effort (*pauruṣa*) is kept close. In 5.75.10 the phrase *tatra lakṣaṇam* is taken as "there is the mark of it".
* 5.75.11 *phalaṃ syād ubhayānvayāt*: "the result comes from the two together".
* 5.75.14 *nātipraṇītaraśmiḥ syāt*: "you must not slacken the reins too far".
* 5.75.19: that Krishna will be Arjuna's charioteer is his own statement here.

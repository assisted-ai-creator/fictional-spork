# Krishna's Answer

"No doubt it is true, Sanjaya, what you say," said Yudhishthira, "that dharma is
the best of deeds. But when you know me, Sanjaya, then blame me, if I am doing
dharma or adharma.

"There are times when adharma takes on the forms of dharma, and the whole of
dharma looks like adharma, and dharma keeps its own form too. The wise see which
is which by their understanding. In the same way, in times of distress, dharma
and adharma, which last as long as a man's way of living, can take each other's
marks. The first mark is the rule for the man it belongs to. But know, Sanjaya,
that there is a dharma for times of distress.

"When a man's natural way of life is lost, he should seek the means by which he
can carry on his work. And a man in his natural state who acts as if he were in
distress, and a man in distress who acts as if he were not, are both to be
blamed, Sanjaya. The Creator laid down penance for brahmins who do not want to
lose their way of life. So blame those who, in distress, do not do their own
work, or who, when not in distress, do work that is not theirs. For the wise, to
cut through to the truth, the way of the good is always laid down. But for those
who are not brahmins, who are not learned, it is right that they should give up
everything. Our fathers lived for that, and the grandfathers before them, and
those before them, and all who did their work seeking wisdom. I think there is
no end to it, none at all.

"Whatever wealth there is on this earth, whatever belongs to the thirty gods in
the world beyond, the world of Prajapati, heaven, the world of Brahma, I do not
want any of it by adharma, Sanjaya.

"Krishna is a master of dharma, skilled, wise in policy, a sage who has honoured
the brahmins. He guides the many and mighty Bhoja kings. Let Keshava, so famous,
say whether I am beyond blame if I let go, or whether I give up my own dharma if
I fight. Vasudeva wants the good of both sides. The Shaineyas, the Chaitrakas
and the Andhakas, the Varshneyas, the Bhojas, the Kaukuras and the Srinjayas
follow Vasudeva's counsel, hold down their enemies and bring joy to their
friends. The Vrishnis and Andhakas, with Ugrasena and the others, all like
Indra, are led by Krishna. The Yadavas are proud, truly brave, mighty and
prosperous. Babhru, king of Kashi, has come to the highest fortune by having
Krishna as his brother and his lord. Vasudeva rains wishes on him as a cloud
rains on creatures at the end of the hot season. Such is Keshava, my son, and
more. We know him to be one who knows how to judge what is to be done. Krishna
is dear to us, and the best of the good. I will not go against Keshava's word."

---

"I want the Pandavas not to be destroyed, Sanjaya," said Vasudeva, "and I want
their prosperity and their good. Just as much, suta, I always wish King
Dhritarashtra, with his many sons, to prosper. That has always been my wish,
Sanjaya. I would never tell them anything except, 'Make peace.' I hear the king
wants it too, and I think it fits the Pandavas. The Pandava has shown a peace
that is very hard to make, Sanjaya. When Dhritarashtra and his son are greedy
for what is theirs, why would quarrels not grow out of it?

"You know the truth of dharma, Sanjaya, as well as I do, or Yudhishthira. Why
then, Sanjaya, do you speak of the Pandava as if he were breaking the rule, when
he is eager to do his work, when he fulfils his own duty and lives with his
family as he was taught from of old?

"While this rule stands, brahmins hold many opinions, high and low. Some say
success in the next world comes by action. Some give up action and say success
comes by knowledge. But here among brahmins it is well known that a man who does
not eat does not get full, however much he knows about food and drink. Only the
kinds of knowledge that get work done bear fruit here, not the others. Here the
fruit of action is seen. A man who is tormented by thirst drinks water and is at
peace. This rule is laid down through action, Sanjaya, and so action goes on. A
man who thinks anything is better than action talks in vain, out of weakness.

"By action the gods shine in the other world. By action the wind blows here. By
action the sun rises every day, never weary, making day and night. The moon,
never weary, goes through the months and half-months and the conjunctions of the
stars. Never weary, the knower of all things, fire, burns when he is kindled,
doing his work for creatures. Never weary, the goddess Earth bears this great
burden with her strength. Never weary, the rivers carry their waters quickly,
filling all creatures. Never weary, the god of great energy rains, making the
sky and heaven roar. The slayer of Bala, never weary, lived the celibate life,
wanting to be the best of the gods. He gave up comfort and what was dear to his
mind, and by that action Shakra won the highest place. Guarding truth and dharma
without fail, self-control, patience, fairness and kindness, practising all
these, Maghavan won the kingdom of the gods, the first of all. Brihaspati lived
the celibate life as the rule lays down, intent and disciplined. He gave up
comfort and held back his senses, and so he came to be honoured among the gods.
By action the stars shine in the other world, and the Rudras, Adityas, Vasus and
Vishvedevas, King Yama, Kubera son of Vishravas, the gandharvas, yakshas and the
bright apsarases. By the celibate life, by the knowledge of the Veda and by
rites, the sages shine in the other world.

"You know this dharma of the whole world, of brahmins, kshatriyas and vaishyas.
Then why, Sanjaya, wise as you are among the wise, do you strain yourself for
the Kauravas? Know that he is always bound to the scriptures, and to the horse
sacrifice and the royal consecration. He is bound as well to the bow and armour,
to the hand guards, the chariot and the weapons. If the Parthas can find a way
with the Kauravas without killing, it would be an act of merit, guarding dharma,
done by holding Bhimasena back in noble conduct. And if, while they do the work
of their fathers, doing their duty as well as they can, they meet death by the
will of fate, that death too would be praised.

"Or do you think that the whole web of dharma for kings is in fighting, or in
not fighting? I will listen to what you say. Look first at the division of the
four classes, Sanjaya, and at your own work. Then listen to the work of the
Pandavas, and praise or blame them as you think fit.

"A brahmin should study and sacrifice, give gifts and visit the chief holy
places. He should teach, and sacrifice for those who may employ him, and accept
the gifts that are allowed. In the same way a kshatriya should protect the
people by dharma, without fail, and give gifts. He should worship with
sacrifices and study all the Vedas. He should take a wife, do good works and
live in his house. A vaishya should study, and gather wealth by farming,
cattle-keeping and trade, and guard it without fail. He should do what pleases
brahmins and kshatriyas, keep to dharma, do good works and live in his house.
Serving brahmins and paying them respect, the shudra should not study, and
sacrifice is forbidden to him. He should always be up and doing, never idle, for
his own good. This is known as the ancient dharma of the shudra.

"The king protects all these without fail, and sets all the classes to their own
dharma. He should be free of desire and fair to his people. He should not follow
desires against dharma. If there is someone better than him, well known and full
of every dharma, he should, knowing it, rebuke the wicked king. If he does not
grow greedy, it is not right to act against him. But when a cruel man grows
greedy for another's land, and takes up force through the anger of fate, then
there will be war between kings. It was for this that armour, weapons and the
bow came into being. Indra brought this work into being for killing robbers, and
armour, weapons and the bow.

"A thief who takes wealth unseen, and one who takes it by force in the open, are
both to be blamed, Sanjaya. How is Dhritarashtra's son any different? Out of
greed he thinks this is dharma, what he wants, following his anger. The
Pandavas' share is fixed. Why should others take it from us for no reason? To
fight in this cause, even to be killed, is praiseworthy. A father's kingdom is
better than another's kingdom. Tell these ancient dharmas of the Kauravas,
Sanjaya, in the middle of the kingdom, to those fools whom Dhritarashtra's son
has gathered, who are already in the power of death.

"And look at this deed, still worse, the way the Kurus behaved in the hall. The
Kurus, with Bhishma first, looked on while Draupadi, the Pandavas' dear wife,
famous, of good character and conduct, was held by a man ruled by desire, and
she was weeping. If the Kurus gathered there, young and old, had stopped him
then, Dhritarashtra would have done what pleased me, and done right by his sons.
Duhshasana dragged Krishna against all decency into the middle of the hall,
before her fathers-in-law. When she was brought there, she spoke pitifully, and
saw no protector there except the steward. Out of weakness, the kings gathered
there could not answer in the hall. Only the steward spoke for what was right by
dharma. Knowing dharma, he answered that man of little wisdom.

"You did not speak of dharma in the hall then, and now you want to teach the
Pandava. But Krishna did a pure deed, very hard to do, when she came into the
hall, by which she brought the Pandavas out of trouble, and herself, like a boat
from the ocean's flood. There in the hall, as Krishna stood near her
fathers-in-law, the suta's son said, 'You have no refuge, daughter of Yajnasena.
Go now to the house of Dhritarashtra's son. Your husbands are beaten. They are
nothing. Choose another husband, proud lady.' That arrow of words from Karna,
sharp and burning, cutting to the bone and striking the vital places, terrible,
lodged deep in Bibhatsu's heart. It is lodged in Phalguna's heart.

"When they were putting on the black antelope skins, Duhshasana spoke bitter
words: 'These are all useless sesame seeds, lost, gone to hell for a long time.'
And at the time of the dicing, Shakuni, the king of Gandhara, said to the
Parthas, by a trick, 'Nakula is lost. What have you left? Play with Krishna,
daughter of Yajnasena.' You know all this, Sanjaya, the words that should not
have been spoken at the dicing, as they were spoken.

"I want to go there myself to settle this matter that has gone wrong. If I can
make peace for the Kurus without giving up the Pandavas' cause, I shall have
done a holy deed with great results, and the Kurus will be freed from the noose
of death. When I speak words of wisdom, devoted to dharma, full of sense and
without harm, I hope the sons of Dhritarashtra will listen in front of me, and
the Kurus will honour me when I come. If not, know that the sons of
Dhritarashtra are finished, burnt up by their own deeds, the fools, by Phalguna
in his chariot and Bhima armed for battle.

"Dhritarashtra's son speaks terrible words to the defeated Pandavas. But
Bhimasena, mace in hand, will not forget, and will remind Duryodhana in due
time.

"Suyodhana is a great tree made of anger. Karna is its trunk, Shakuni its
branches, Duhshasana its rich flowers and fruit, and foolish King Dhritarashtra
its root. Yudhishthira is a great tree made of dharma. Arjuna is its trunk,
Bhimasena its branches, the sons of Madri its rich flowers and fruit, and I, and
Brahman, and the brahmins, are its root.

"King Dhritarashtra and his sons are a forest, Sanjaya, and the Pandavas are the
tigers in it. Do not cut down the forest with its tigers. Do not drive the
tigers out of the forest. A tiger without a forest is killed. A forest without
tigers is cut down. So the tiger should guard the forest, and the forest should
protect the tiger. The sons of Dhritarashtra are like creepers, Sanjaya, and the
Pandavas are like shala trees. A creeper never grows without the support of a
great tree.

"The Parthas, tamers of enemies, are ready to serve, and ready to fight. Let the
king, Dhritarashtra, do what he must. The great Pandavas, who live by dharma,
stand ready for peace, and they are mighty fighters. Tell him so exactly,
learned man."

**Divergences.**

* **Draupadi in the hall.** Krishna's account says she "saw no protector there except the steward" (5.29.33). He does not say that he himself came to her aid, and the CE nowhere has him send the endless cloth. Ledger L-19 (Krishna at the disrobing).
* Rejected lines (\*138–\*149) add detail and epithets. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.28.2–7: Yudhishthira's account of *āpaddharma*, the dharma of distress, is compressed but keeps each step. Its last verses are obscure; the reading follows the plain sense of the CE words.
* 5.28.11: the clan names are kept as the text gives them.
* 5.28.13: "Babhru, king of Kashi" (*kāśyo babhruḥ*).
* 5.29.16 *asya*: "he", taken as Yudhishthira.
* 5.29.17 *ārye vṛtte bhīmasenaṃ nigṛhya*: "by holding Bhimasena back in noble conduct".
* 5.29.27 *vidhiprakopāt*: "through the anger of fate".
* 5.29.38 *ṣaṇḍhatilāḥ*: "useless sesame seeds" (seeds that will not sprout). The insult is told in Book 2.

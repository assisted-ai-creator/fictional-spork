# The Pandavas March

When he had heard Janardana's words, Yudhishthira, the King of Dharma, whose
soul was dharma, spoke to his brothers in Keshava's presence.

"You have heard what happened in the Kuru assembly, and you have taken in all
that Keshava said. So divide my army, best of men. These seven armies have
gathered for victory. Hear the names of their seven famous leaders: Drupada and
Virata, Dhrishtadyumna and Shikhandi, Satyaki, Chekitana and mighty Bhimasena.
These are the leaders of the armies, heroes ready to give up their bodies. All
of them know the Veda and are brave, all have kept their vows well. They are
modest and wise in policy, all skilled in war, skilled with arrows and weapons,
and they fight with every weapon. Who is to lead all seven armies, who knows how
to divide them and can withstand Bhishma in battle, Bhishma who is like a fire
whose flames are arrows? You first, Sahadeva, joy of the Kurus. Tell us what you
think, tiger among men. Who is fit to be our general?"

"He is joined to us, he shares our grief, he is strong," said Sahadeva. "He is a
king who knows dharma, and relying on him we claim our own share. Mighty Virata
of Matsya, master of weapons and fierce in war, will withstand Bhishma in battle
and those great chariot fighters."

When Sahadeva had said this, Nakula, skilled in speech, said this next. "One who
by age, learning, steadfastness, family and birth is modest, well born, glorious
and skilled in every science. He learned weapons from Bharadvaja, is hard to
withstand and true to his promise, and always rivals Drona and mighty Bhishma.
He is praised at the head of the host of kings, a lord of armies, surrounded by
sons and grandsons like a tree with a hundred branches. That lord of the earth
practised terrible austerity with his wife, in anger, to destroy Drona, a hero
who shines in battle. That bull among kings always cares for us like a father.
Let our father-in-law Drupada lead the army at its head. I think he would
withstand Drona and Bhishma as they come on. That king knows heavenly weapons,
and he is Angirasa's friend."

When Madri's two sons had given their views, Vasava's son, the joy of the Kurus,
equal to Vasava, the left-handed archer, spoke.

"This heavenly man was born by the power of austerity and by satisfying the
seers, the colour of flame and very strong. He rose from the fire pit with bow,
armour and sword, in armour, mounted on a chariot yoked with the finest heavenly
horses, roaring like a great cloud with the sound of his chariot. He is mighty,
a hero built like a lion, with a lion's stride and courage, lion-chested and
strong-armed, with a lion's breast and great strength. He roars like a lion, a
hero with a lion's shoulders and great splendour. His brows, teeth, jaw, arms
and face are fine, and he is not lean. His collarbones are fine, his eyes very
large, his feet fine and firmly set. No weapon can pierce him, like an elephant
in rut. Truthful and master of his senses, he was born to destroy Drona.

"I think Dhrishtadyumna can withstand Bhishma's arrows, which strike like the
thunderbolt and lightning, like snakes with blazing mouths, as fast as Yama's
messengers, falling like fire, terrible as the crushing thunderbolt, which Rama
withstood in battle. King, I see no man who could withstand that man of great
vows except Dhrishtadyumna. That is my mind. Quick of hand, a fighter of many
skills, with armour no one can pierce, glorious, like a lordly elephant leading
its herd: he is my choice for general."

"Shikhandi, Drupada's son, was born to kill him, king of kings," said Bhima. "So
say the perfected ones and the seers who have gathered. When he works his
heavenly weapon in the thick of battle, men will see in him the form of great
Rama. I see no one in battle who could break Shikhandi with a weapon, king, when
he stands armed in his chariot. No one else could withstand Bhishma, that man of
great vows, in single combat, king, except the hero Shikhandi. He is my choice
for general."

"Keshava, whose soul is dharma, knows the strength and weakness, the worth and
worthlessness, of the whole world, my friend," said Yudhishthira, "what is past
and what is to come. Let the man Krishna the Dasharha names be our general,
whether he is a master of weapons or not, old or young. He is the root of our
victory or our defeat, my friend. On him rest our lives and our kingdom, being
and not being, happiness and sorrow. He is the ordainer and the disposer.
Success rests on him. The man Krishna the Dasharha names is fit to be our
general. Let the best of speakers speak, for the night is passing. Then, when we
have made a general who will follow Krishna's will, we will set out for the
battlefield at the end of the night, with our weapons consecrated and with the
rites and blessings done."

When he heard the words of wise Yudhishthira, the King of Dharma, the lotus-eyed
one looked at Dhananjaya and spoke.

"Great king, I too approve these men you have named as leaders of your army.
They are brave and fight with courage. All of them can crush your enemies. They
would make even Indra afraid in a great battle, let alone Dhritarashtra's
greedy, evil-minded sons. For your sake, strong-armed one, tamer of enemies, I
too made a great effort there for peace, Bharata. We have paid our debt to
dharma, and we cannot be blamed by those who want to find fault. The foolish
child, Dhritarashtra's son, thinks he has done all he needs to, and he is not
wise. Sick as he is, he thinks himself strong. Let the army be yoked well, for
they are as good as killed, in my view. The sons of Dhritarashtra will not be
able to stand when they see Dhananjaya. They will not stand when they see
Bhimasena in his anger, the twins like Yama, impatient Dhrishtadyumna with
Yuyudhana beside him, Abhimanyu, the sons of Draupadi, Virata and Drupada, and
the other lords of armies, kings of firm courage. Our army is strong, hard to
attack and hard to approach. It will kill the army of Dhritarashtra's son in
battle, no doubt of it."

---

When Krishna had said this, the best of men were overjoyed, and a great shout
rose from them in their joy. "Yoke!" came the cry, and the troops hurried and
ran. There was the noise of horses and elephants, the rumble of wheels
everywhere, and the tumult of conches and drums on every side. As the Pandavas
set out with their troops, the army looked on every side like the Ganga in
flood, hard to withstand. In the front of the army went Bhimasena and Madri's
two sons in armour, Subhadra's son, the sons of Draupadi, Dhrishtadyumna,
Prishata's grandson, the Prabhadrakas and the Panchalas, with Bhimasena at their
head. Then a sound rose like the sea at the change of the moon. The noise of
those happy men setting out seemed to touch the sky. The fighters went joyfully,
in armour, ready to tear through the enemy ranks. In their middle went King
Yudhishthira, Kunti's son.

The king took with him the carts, the markets and the tents, the carriages and
draught animals of every kind, the treasury, the engines and the weapons, and
the physicians and surgeons. He took every weak part of the army, all that was
thin and feeble, and the attendants. Draupadi the Panchala princess, who spoke
the truth, turned back to Upaplavya with the women, surrounded by female and
male slaves.

They made defences for the base with garrisons, fixed and moving, and the sons
of Pandu set out with a great camp. Giving cows and gold, surrounded by brahmins
and praised by them, they went in chariots adorned with jewels, king. The
Kekayas, Dhrishtaketu, the son of the king of Kashi, the mighty Shreniman,
Vasudana and unconquered Shikhandi, glad and content, in armour, armed and
adorned, all followed King Yudhishthira, going round him. In the rear half went
Virata, Yajnasena the Somaka, Sudharma, Kuntibhoja and Dhrishtadyumna's sons,
with forty thousand chariots, five times as many horses, ten times as many foot
soldiers, and sixty thousand horsemen. Anadhrishti, Chekitana, the king of the
Chedis and Satyaki all went, surrounding Vasudeva and Dhananjaya.

When they reached Kurukshetra, the Pandavas, ready to strike, their armies drawn
up, looked like roaring bulls. Plunging into Kurukshetra, the tamers of enemies
blew their conches, and so did Vasudeva and Dhananjaya. When they heard the roar
of Panchajanya, like the crack of lightning, all the troops were filled with
joy. The lions' roars of those swift men, joined with conches and drums, made
the earth, the sky and the oceans echo.

Then King Yudhishthira camped his army on level, rich ground, with plenty of
fodder and fuel. Avoiding burning grounds, shrines of the gods, the hermitages
of great seers, sacred fords and holy places, Kunti's son Yudhishthira, lord of
the earth, had the camp made on sweet ground, free of salt, lucky and holy.
Then, when he was rested and his animals refreshed, he rose again and set out,
surrounded by kings in hundreds of thousands. Keshava, with Partha, drove off
Dhritarashtra's son's pickets of soldiers in hundreds and went round on every
side. Dhrishtadyumna, Prishata's grandson, and Satyaki Yuyudhana, the splendid,
generous chariot fighter, measured out the camp.

Coming to the holy river Hiranvati at Kurukshetra, with good bathing places,
clean water, free of gravel and mud, Keshava had a moat dug there, Bharata, and
set a force to guard it. Whatever plan the great Pandavas had for their camp,
Keshava had the same made for the kings. Hundreds and thousands of costly camps
for the kings were set up there, each apart, with plenty of water and wood, very
hard to attack. They were well stocked with food and drink, like heavenly
palaces set on the ground, king of kings. There were hundreds of skilled
craftsmen there, paid their wages, and expert physicians with all their
equipment. He had heaps like mountains made of bowstrings, bows, armour and
weapons, of honey and clarified butter, of resin and dust. In every camp King
Yudhishthira put plenty of water, good fodder, husks and charcoal. Great
engines, iron arrows, lances, swords and axes, bows, armour and the rest were in
men's minds then. Elephants in armour with iron coats, like mountains, able to
fight hundreds of thousands, were seen there.

When they knew the Pandavas had camped there, their friends came to them as
arranged, Bharata, with their troops and mounts. Kings who had kept the vow of
chastity, drunk the soma and given rich fees came together for the victory of
Pandu's sons.

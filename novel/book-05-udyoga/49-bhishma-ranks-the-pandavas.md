# Bhishma Ranks the Pandavas

"The sons of Draupadi, great king, are all five great chariot fighters," said
Bhishma. "Uttara, son of Virata, is to my mind a great chariot fighter.
Abhimanyu, great king, is a leader of the leaders of chariot troops. He could be
Partha's equal in battle, or Vasudeva's. He is light with his weapons and fights
in many ways, proud and firm in courage. Remembering his father's sufferings, he
will show his courage. Satyaki Madhava, the hero, is a leader of the leaders of
chariot troops. This hero of the Vrishni champions cannot bear a wrong and has
mastered fear. Uttamaujas, king, is to my mind a great chariot fighter, and
brave Yudhamanyu is a splendid chariot fighter, a bull among men.

"These have many thousands of chariots, elephants and horses. They will fight,
giving up their bodies, to please the son of Kunti, against your armies
alongside the Pandavas, Bharata, challenging each other like fire and wind,
king. Virata and Drupada, both old and unbeatable in battle, are to my mind
great chariot fighters, bulls among men of great courage. Old in years as they
are, they are devoted to the kshatriya's dharma, and standing on the heroes'
road they will strive with all their might. Through their kinship, king of
kings, and through their strength, courage and birth, the two great archers are
of noble conduct and bound by the noose of affection. When they have a reason,
all strong-armed men become brave or cowardly, bull among men. These two have
taken one path, and they are firmly devoted to Partha. They will give up their
lives and strive with all their might, king. Each terrible in battle with his
own akshauhini, keeping faith with their kinship, they will do great work. These
great archers, heroes of the world, have given up their lives, Bharata, and
keeping faith they will do great work.

"The son of the king of Panchala, king, Shikhandi, conqueror of enemy cities, is
to my mind a leading chariot fighter of Partha's, Bharata. He will fight in
battle, destroying his earlier state, spreading his great fame among your
armies, Bharata. He has many armies, Panchalas and Prabhadrakas, and with that
chariot troop he will do great work. Dhrishtadyumna, commander of all the
armies, Bharata, is to my mind an outstanding chariot fighter, a pupil of Drona
and a great chariot fighter. He will fight in battle, killing the enemy, like
the angry blessed bearer of the Pinaka at the end of the age. Those who love
battle say his chariot host is like the ocean for its size, like the gods' in
battle.

"Kshatradharman, his son, lord of kings, is to my mind half a chariot fighter,
king, because as a child he has not had much training. Shishupala's son, the
brave king of the Chedis, Dhrishtaketu, the great archer, is a great chariot
fighter and a kinsman of the Pandava. This brave lord of the Chedis, with his
son, a great chariot fighter, will do great work that is hard to do, Bharata.
Kshatradharman, conqueror of enemy cities, is to my mind devoted to the
kshatriya's dharma. Kshatradeva, king of kings, is the best of chariot fighters
among the Pandavas, and so are Jayanta, Amitaujas and the great chariot fighter
Satyajit. All these great ones, the best of the Panchalas, are great chariot
fighters. They will fight in battle, my friend, like angry elephants. Aja and
Bhoja are two brave great chariot fighters with the Pandavas. They will strive
with all their might to help the Pandavas. Fast with their weapons and fighting
in many ways, they are skilled and firm in courage.

"The five Kekaya brothers, lord of kings, are fierce in battle. All of them are
splendid chariot fighters, and all have red banners. Kashika, Sukumara, Nila,
and another king, Suryadatta, Shankha and the one named Madirashva: all of these
are to my mind splendid chariot fighters, all with the marks of battle, all
knowing every weapon, and all great. Vardhakshemi, great king, is to my mind a
great chariot fighter. King Chitrayudha is to my mind the best of chariot
fighters, for he is a glory in battle and devoted to the crowned one. Chekitana
and Satyadhriti, two great chariot fighters of the Pandavas, two tigers among
men, are to my mind splendid chariot fighters. Vyaghradatta, lord of kings, and
Chandrasena, Bharata, are to my mind splendid chariot fighters of the Pandavas,
no doubt of it.

"Senabindu, lord of kings, called Krodhahantri by name, who is Vasudeva's and
Bhimasena's equal in power, will fight here with courage against your soldiers
in battle. You should think of him, who glories in battle, as the best of
chariot fighters, just as you think of me, Drona and Kripa. Kashya, whose
weapons are very fast, is a praiseworthy, fine chariot fighter. To my mind this
conqueror of enemy cities is a chariot fighter of single worth. And young
Satyajit, son of Drupada, who glories in battle and is brave in war, should be
thought a chariot fighter worth eight. He has become an outstanding chariot
fighter, Dhrishtadyumna's equal. Wanting fame for the Pandavas, he will do the
greatest work. And another great chariot fighter, loyal and brave, is the mighty
Pandya king, a bearer of the Pandavas' yoke. Dridhadhanva, the great archer, is
the Pandavas' fine chariot fighter. Shreniman, best of the Kauravas, and King
Vasudana are both, to my mind, outstanding chariot fighters, scorcher of
enemies.

"Rochamana, great king, is the Pandavas' great chariot fighter. He will fight
like an immortal in battle among the enemy armies, Bharata. Purujit Kuntibhoja,
the mighty great archer, Bhimasena's uncle, is to my mind an outstanding chariot
fighter. This hero is a great archer, skilled and expert, fighting in many ways
and able, and to my mind a bull among chariot fighters. He will fight with
courage like Maghavan against the danavas, and his fighters, all famous, are all
skilled in war. For his sister's sons the hero will do great work in battle,
standing firm for the good and the pleasure of the Pandus. Bhimasena's son,
Haidimba, lord of the rakshasas, great king, is to my mind a leader of the
leaders of chariot troops, full of magic. He will fight in battle with his
magic, my friend, loving battle, with the brave rakshasas who are his
counsellors and obey him.

"These and many other lords of many lands are gathered for the Pandava, with
Vasudeva at their head. These, king, are the chief chariot fighters, outstanding
chariot fighters and half chariot fighters of the great Pandava. They will lead
Yudhishthira's terrible army in battle, king, guarded by the heroic crowned one
as by great Indra. I shall fight them in battle, hero, as they come on wanting
victory, wanting victory myself, or death in battle. I shall meet Partha and
Vasudeva, the best of men, bearers of the discus and the Gandiva, like the sun
and moon at twilight. And the splendid chariot fighters, the soldiers of Pandu's
son, I shall meet with their armies at the head of the battle. I have told you,
king, the chariot fighters and outstanding chariot fighters, as they rank, and
some half chariot fighters too, lord of the Kauravas, on their side as on yours.

"Arjuna and Vasudeva, and the other kings there, I shall hold back all of them,
as long as I can see, Bharata. But I will not kill Shikhandi of the Panchalas,
strong-armed one, even if I see him with his arrow raised, fighting me in
battle. The world knows that to please my father I gave up the kingdom that came
to me and held to my vow of chastity. I consecrated Chitrangada as king of the
Kauravas, and the child Vichitravirya as crown prince. Having made my vow as
Devavrata known on the earth among all kings, I would never kill a woman, or one
who was a woman before. Shikhandi was a woman before, king, if you have heard.
He was born a girl and became a man. I will not fight him, Bharata. But I shall
kill all the other kings I meet in battle, bull among the Bharatas, only not the
sons of Kunti, king."

"Why would you not kill Shikhandi, best of the Bharatas," said Duryodhana, "if
you saw him with his arrow raised, attacking you in battle? You said before,
strong-armed one, 'I shall kill the Pandavas with the Somakas,' son of Ganga.
Tell me that, grandfather."

"Listen, Duryodhana, with these lords of the earth," said Bhishma, "to the story
of why I would not kill Shikhandi when I saw him in battle. My father was the
great king Shantanu, a bull among the Bharatas. The righteous one reached his
end in time, bull among men. Then, keeping my vow, best of the Bharatas, I
consecrated my brother Chitrangada as great king. When he died, I followed
Satyavati's wish and consecrated Vichitravirya as king, in due form. Though he
was younger, the righteous Vichitravirya, whom I had consecrated by dharma,
looked to me alone, king of kings. Wanting to arrange his marriage, my friend, I
set my mind on it, thinking it should be from a suitable family.

"Then I heard, strong-armed one, that there were three girls at a bridegroom
choice, the daughters of the king of Kashi, all of unmatched beauty, Amba,
Ambika and Ambalika. The kings of the earth had been invited, bull among the
Bharatas. Amba was the eldest of them, Ambika the middle one, and Ambalika the
youngest princess, king of kings. I went with a single chariot to the city of
the lord of Kashi. There I saw the three girls, finely dressed, strong-armed
one, and the kings who had gathered, lord of the earth.

"Then I challenged all the kings standing ready for battle, and put the girls in
my chariot, bull among the Bharatas. Knowing that their price was valour, I put
them in the chariot and said again and again to all the kings gathered there,
'Bhishma, son of Shantanu, is carrying off the girls. Strive with all your might
to free them, kings. I am taking them by force while you look on, lords of men.'

"Then the lords of the earth sprang up with their weapons raised. 'Make ready!
Make ready!' they cried in anger, and urged on their charioteers. The kings rose
up with their weapons raised, in chariots like clouds, the elephant fighters on
elephants and others on great horses. Then all the kings surrounded me on every
side with a great mass of chariots, lord of the people. I held them back with a
great shower of arrows, and I beat all the kings, as the king of the gods beat
the danavas. As they rushed at me I shot down their bright banners, adorned with
gold, one arrow for each. I brought down their horses, their elephants and their
charioteers in battle with my blazing arrows, laughing, bull among men. When
they saw how fast I was, they turned back, broken. When I had beaten the kings I
went back to Hastinapura. Then I brought the girls for my brother, Bharata, and
told Satyavati what I had done, strong-armed one."

**Divergences.**

* **Shikhandi.** Bhishma will not kill Shikhandi, "a woman before … born a girl and became a man" (5.169.19–20). He does not tell the Pandavas here how he can be killed. His whole reason is told in the story of Amba that follows (5.170–193). See ledger L-185.
* **The abduction.** The CE tells the carrying off of the three princesses in Book 1 as well (1.96). Here Bhishma tells it himself, more briefly. \*570, in which the kings bow and praise him, is not narrated.
* Other rejected lines not narrated: \*568 and \*569 (Virata's sons Shankha and Shveta as great chariot fighters).

**Choices.**

* The ranks of chariot fighters are rendered as in the last chapter.
* 5.167.10 *snehapāśasitau*: "bound by the noose of affection".
* 5.167.11: "When they have a reason, all strong-armed men become brave or cowardly" is Bhishma's observation.
* 5.168.2 *nāśayan pūrvasaṃsthitim*: "destroying his earlier state", Shikhandi's having been a woman.
* 5.168.5 *pinākī*: "the bearer of the Pinaka", Shiva.
* 5.168.14: the names of the Kekaya brothers and others are given as the text gives them. It is not clear which of these names belong to the Kekayas and which to other kings.
* 5.168.16 *kirīṭinaḥ*: "the crowned one", Arjuna.
* 5.168.19 *krodhahantā*: the name Krodhahantri, as the text gives it.
* 5.169.6 *bhaimaseniḥ … haiḍimbaḥ*: "Bhimasena's son, Haidimba", Ghatotkacha.
* 5.169.19 *strīpūrvam*: "one who was a woman before".
* 5.170.13 *vīryaśulkāḥ*: "their price was valour".

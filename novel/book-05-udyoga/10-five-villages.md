# Five Villages

"The Creator brings good and bad, young and old, weak and strong, all under his
power, Sanjaya," said Yudhishthira. "The Lord gives wisdom to the fool and folly
to the wise, bringing forth his seed before him. Tell them the truth, just as it
is, and that will be enough to let them know. But first take counsel with each
other, glad at heart.

"Go to the Kurus, son of Gavalgana, and greet mighty Dhritarashtra, clasp his
feet, and ask after his health. When he is sitting among the Kurus, tell him
this: 'It is by your strength alone, king, that the Pandavas live happily. By
your grace they won a kingdom when they were boys, tamer of enemies. Having set
them up in their kingdom first, do not now look on while they perish. None of
this is enough for any one man, Sanjaya. My father, let us live together. Do not
fall into the power of your enemies.'

"And bow your head to Bhishma, Shantanu's son, the grandfather of the Bharatas,
and speak my name. When you have greeted him, say to our grandfather:
'Shantanu's line had sunk, and you raised it up again. Now, my father,
grandfather, act by your own judgement, so that your grandsons may live in
friendship with one another.'

"Say the same to Vidura, who keeps the counsel of the Kurus: 'Gentle friend,
speak for no war. Yudhishthira wants what is good.'

"Then speak to the prince Suyodhana, the unforgiving, where he sits among the
Kurus, and plead with him again and again. 'You looked on while Krishnaa was
brought alone into the hall, and I looked on and did nothing. We bore that
sorrow, so as not to kill the Kurus. So the Pandavas bore hardships early and
late, though they were the stronger, as all the Kurus know. When you sent us
into exile, gentle one, dressed in deerskins, we bore that sorrow, so as not to
kill the Kurus. When Duhshasana, with your consent, came at Krishnaa in the hall
and seized her by the hair, we let that pass too.

"'Let us have our own share, as is fitting, scorcher of enemies. Turn your
greedy mind away from other men's wealth, bull among men. That way, king, there
will be peace and love between us. We want peace. Give us even one part of the
kingdom. Kushasthala, Vrikasthala, Asandi, Varanavata, and for the fifth, any
place you like: give your five brothers five villages, Suyodhana. Let there be
peace between us and our kin, wise one.'

"Sanjaya, let brother follow brother. Let father be joined with son. Let the
Panchalas meet the Kurus, smiling. I want to see the Kurus and the Panchalas
unharmed. Let us all make peace with glad hearts, my friend, bull of the
Bharatas. I am ready for peace, Sanjaya, and ready for war. I am ready for
dharma and for profit, for the gentle and for the harsh."

---

When the Pandava had given him leave, Sanjaya set out. He had done everything
great Dhritarashtra had ordered. He reached Hastinapura and went in quickly. He
went to the inner palace and said to the doorkeeper, "Tell Dhritarashtra that I
have come from the Pandavas. Tell him if he is awake, Steward. I will go in when
the king knows."

"Lord of the earth, I bow to you," said the doorkeeper. "Sanjaya is here at the
door, wanting to see you. He has come as envoy from the Pandavas. Command us,
king. What shall he do?"

"Tell him I am well and it is a good time," said Dhritarashtra. "Let him in, and
welcome to Sanjaya. It is never the wrong time for him to see me. Why should he
stand at the door, Steward?"

With the king's leave, the suta's son went into the great house, guarded by wise
and brave and noble men. He came, with joined palms, to Vichitravirya's son,
where he sat on the lion throne.

"I am Sanjaya, lord of the earth," said Sanjaya. "I bow to you. I went to the
Pandavas, god among men, and I have come back. The proud son of Pandu,
Yudhishthira, greets you and asks after your health. He asks gladly after your
sons: are you happy with your sons and grandsons, king, with your friends and
ministers, and those who live by you?"

"I greet you, my son Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "and I wish Ajatashatru, the
Partha, joy. Is that king of the Kauravas well, with his sons, his ministers and
his younger brothers?"

"The son of Pandu is well, with his ministers," said Sanjaya, "and he wants what
was in his mind before. He does what is pure in dharma and profit. He is proud
and learned, clear-sighted and of good character. For the Pandava, kindness is
higher than dharma, and dharma is higher than heaping up wealth. The Partha does
not follow pleasures and comforts that have no dharma in them, Bharata. Know
that of him.

"A man moves as another moves him, like a wooden doll strung on threads. When I
see what the Pandava is made to bear, I think fate is stronger than what a man
does. And when I see the fault of your deeds, terrible, shameful, ending in
misery, I think a man is praised only so long as his able enemy bides his time.
Ajatashatru has cast off his sin, like a snake shedding a worn-out skin it can
no longer use. The steady Yudhishthira shines in his own natural conduct, and he
has left the sin on you.

"Look at your own deeds, king, and see how far they have gone from dharma and
profit and the conduct of the noble. You have earned a bad name here, king, and
the sin may follow you into the next world. You follow your son now, and hope to
enjoy this doubtful wealth without them. The cry of lawlessness is loud over the
earth. This deed is not worthy of you, first of the Bharatas.

"A man of little wisdom, of low birth, cruel, holding long grudges, unsteady in
the kshatriya's arts, weak and ill-taught: such a man does not cross over
misfortune. But one who is born in a good family, a man of dharma, famous,
learned, who lives in comfort and has mastered himself, holds on to dharma and
profit bound together, and comes to nothing except by the power of fate. How
could a wise man, who holds the best counsel, who leads others in dharma and
profit when trouble comes, who has every kind of counsel and is not deluded, do
a cruel deed?

"These counsellors of yours sit together, always busy at your affairs. This is
their strong resolve, and it has set the Kurus on the road to hell, to their
destruction. The Kurus would not have perished before their time if Ajatashatru
had wanted to repay evil with evil, putting his sin on you. And the blame in the
world would be yours. What else is there but the will of the gods, when the
Partha went to the other world and saw it? He went beyond, and he was honoured
there. There is no doubt of it: man's effort counts for nothing.

"When he saw that good qualities come from deeds, and that being and not being
come and go and do not last, King Bali, finding no end to it, thought there was
no cause but Time. The eye, the ear, the nose, the skin and the tongue are the
doorways of a creature's knowledge. When thirst comes to an end they are
content. A man should put them aside, unshaken and free of sorrow.

"But I do not think a man's deeds, well done, turn out as they should. A child
born of the deeds of mother and father grows as it should by food. What is dear
and what is not, pleasure and pain, blame and praise, all come to a man. Others
blame him when he does wrong, and praise the same man when he behaves well.

"I blame you for the quarrel among the Bharatas. It will surely be the end of
the people. If not, then because of your wrongdoing, Arjuna will burn the Kurus
as fire burns dry grass. Of all the world, king, you alone, lord of men, gave
way to your son, who follows his desires. At the time of the dice you praised
yourself. Look at what has ripened from it: there is nothing for it but peace.
By taking up untrustworthy men, king, and holding back the trustworthy, you are
too weak, Kaurava, to protect this rich and endless earth.

"I have come shaken by the speed of my chariot, and I am tired. With your leave,
lion among men, I will go to bed. In the morning the Kurus, gathered in the
hall, will hear Ajatashatru's words."

**Divergences.**

* **The five villages.** Popular lists name the five villages as Indraprastha, Panipat, Sonipat, Baghpat and Tilpat, or similar. The CE names four, Kushasthala, Vrikasthala, Asandi and Varanavata, and asks for "any place" as the fifth (5.31.19). The same four are named again at 5.70.15 and 5.80.7–8. Ganguli's vulgate has "Avishthala" and "Makandi" for two of them. See ledger L-174.
* **Indraprastha first.** Yudhishthira's first demand to Sanjaya was Indraprastha (5.26.28), and he repeats it at the end of his greetings (5.30.47). The five villages are the least he will take.
* **Sanjaya's journey.** A rejected line (\*160) has Sanjaya make the journey in a single day and arrive at night. The CE says only that he reached Hastinapura quickly (5.32.2).
* **Dhritarashtra's reply.** A rejected line (\*165) gives Dhritarashtra's words sending Sanjaya to rest. The CE ends with Sanjaya's request (5.32.30).
* Other rejected lines (\*161–\*164) add details. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.31.2 *purastāc chukram uccaran*: "bringing forth his seed before him", the Creator's seed of what is to come.
* 5.31.3: "take counsel with each other, glad at heart" is Yudhishthira's instruction, read with Ganguli.
* 5.31.13 *apaśyan mām upekṣantaṃ kṛṣṇām ekāṃ sabhāgatām*: "You looked on while Krishnaa was brought alone into the hall, and I looked on and did nothing."
* 5.31.19: *avasānaṃ … kiṃ cid eva tu pañcamam*, "and for the fifth, any place you like".
* 5.31.23: Yudhishthira's closing line, "I am ready for peace, and ready for war", is kept whole.
* 5.32.3, 5: the doorkeeper is addressed as *kṣattaḥ*, "Steward", the title of a palace officer. Here it is not Vidura.
* 5.32.12 *sūtraprotā dārumayīva yoṣā*: "like a wooden doll strung on threads".
* 5.32.13: "a man is praised only so long as his able enemy bides his time" follows Ganguli's reading of a difficult verse.
* 5.32.14: the snake's worn-out skin is the text's image.
* 5.32.22: "when the Partha went to the other world and saw it" is Arjuna's journey to Indra's heaven in Book 3.
* 5.32.23: King Bali's view that there is no cause but Time is kept as Sanjaya gives it. Ganguli reads "the eternal Essence".
* 5.32.25–26: Sanjaya's second view, that a man's deeds do not always turn out as they should, follows the CE's *na tv eva manye*.
* 5.32.27 *kṛṣṇavartmeva kakṣam*: "as fire burns dry grass".

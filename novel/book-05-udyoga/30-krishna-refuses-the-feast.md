# Krishna Refuses the Feast

Govinda took his leave of Pritha, walked round her keeping her on his right, and
Shauri, tamer of enemies, went to Duryodhana's house. It shone with the greatest
splendour, like Purandara's dwelling. The doorkeepers did not stop him as he
passed through its three courtyards. Then the famous one climbed up to the
palace, which rose like a mass of clouds or a mountain peak, blazing with
splendour. There he saw strong-armed Dhritarashtra's son seated, surrounded by
thousands of kings and Kurus. He saw Duhshasana, Karna and Shakuni son of Subala
seated near Duryodhana.

As the Dasharha came, the famous son of Dhritarashtra rose with his counsellors
to honour Madhusudana. The Varshneya Keshava met Dhritarashtra's son and his
counsellors, and greeted the kings there according to their age. Achyuta sat
down on a couch of gold, finely made and spread with all kinds of coverings. The
Kaurava offered Janardana a cow and the honey drink, and offered him his houses
and his kingdom. Then all the Kurus and the kings waited on Govinda as he sat
there, bright as the clear sun.

King Duryodhana invited the Varshneya, the best of victors, to a meal, and
Keshava did not accept. Then the Kaurava Duryodhana spoke to Krishna in the
assembly of kings, gently at first but with a deceitful end, with a glance at
Karna. "Why have you not taken the food and drink, the clothes and beds brought
for you, Janardana? You have given help to both sides and you want the good of
both. You are a kinsman of Dhritarashtra, and dear to him, Madhava. You know
dharma and purpose truly and completely, Govinda. I want to hear the reason,
bearer of the discus and mace."

When he said this, great-minded Govinda raised his great arm, and with a voice
like a mass of clouds in season, the lotus-eyed one gave the king an excellent
answer full of reason, not muffled, not swallowed, not cut short and not
confused. "Envoys eat and accept honour when their mission is done. When my
mission is done, Bharata, you and your counsellors may honour me."

Dhritarashtra's son answered Janardana, "It is not right for you to treat us
wrongly. Whether your mission is done or not, Madhusudana, we are trying to
honour you, Govinda, and we cannot. And we do not know the reason, Madhusudana,
why you do not accept the honour we pay you gladly, best of men. We have no
enmity with you, Govinda, and no quarrel. Think about that, and you should not
speak like this."

Then Janardana looked at Dhritarashtra's son and his counsellors, and the
Dasharha seemed to laugh. "I would never give up dharma for desire or anger, for
hatred or gain, for argument or greed. Food is eaten out of affection, or else
in distress. You show me no affection, king, and I am not in distress. For no
reason, king, you have hated the Pandavas since they were born, your brothers
who do what pleases you and have every virtue. To hate the Parthas for no reason
is not right. The Pandavas stand firm in dharma. Who can say anything against
them? Whoever hates them hates me. Whoever follows them follows me. Know that I
am of one self with the Pandavas, who live by dharma.

"The man who follows desire and anger and in his folly wants to quarrel, and
hates the virtuous, is called the lowest of men. The man who out of folly and
greed looks with an evil eye on kinsmen of good character, who has not mastered
himself or his anger, does not keep his fortune long. But the man who wins over
the virtuous with kindness, even those his heart does not love, lives long in
fame. All this food is not to be eaten. It is tainted with evil intent. I think
only the steward's food is to be eaten."

When he had said this to impatient Duryodhana, the strong-armed one left the
bright house of Dhritarashtra's son. When he had gone out, strong-armed, great
Vasudeva went to great Vidura's house to stay. Drona, Kripa, Bhishma and Bahlika
and the Kurus came to the strong-armed one in Vidura's house. There the Kurus
said to Madhusudana, "We offer you our houses, Varshneya, with their jewels."
Madhusudana, of great energy, said to the Kauravas, "All of you may go. You have
paid me every honour."

When the Kurus had gone, the steward took care to honour the unbeaten Dasharha
with everything he could want. Then the steward brought many kinds of pure and
excellent food and drink for great Keshava. Madhusudana first fed the brahmins
with them and gave great wealth to those who knew the Vedas. Then, with his
followers, as Vasava with the Maruts, he ate Vidura's pure and excellent food.

---

When he had eaten and rested, at night, Vidura said to him, "Your coming here
was not well decided, Keshava. Dhritarashtra's son goes beyond purpose and
dharma, Janardana. He is a fool, quick to anger. He destroys others' pride and
wants honour for himself. He goes beyond the commands of his elders and beyond
the teachings of dharma. He is slow-witted, wicked and headstrong.
Dhritarashtra's son cannot be led to good, Janardana. He is evil. He is ruled by
desire and thinks himself wise. He betrays his friends and suspects everyone. He
does nothing for others and is ungrateful. He has given up dharma and loves
falsehood. He has these and many other faults. Even if you tell him what is
good, he will not take it, out of anger.

"Seeing the gathering of the armies of the kings, Madhusudana, the foolish child
thinks he has already won. It is settled in the mind of evil Dhritarashtra's son
that Karna alone can beat his enemies. He will not come to peace. He relies
greatly on Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna, Drona's son and Jayadratha, and does
not set his mind on peace. The sons of Dhritarashtra and Karna are sure,
Janardana, that the Parthas cannot even look at Bhishma, Drona and Kripa. And
all the sons of Dhritarashtra have agreed, Keshava, while you are trying for
peace and wanting brotherhood, that they will not give the Pandavas their proper
share. With them so determined, your words will be useless.

"Where good speech and bad are the same, Madhusudana, a wise man should not
talk, like a singer among the deaf. You should not speak among those who do not
understand, the deluded, those who keep no bounds, Madhava, as a brahmin should
not speak among chandalas. He is strong and deluded, and he will not do what you
say. Your words will be wasted on him. I do not like you going into the middle
of all those evil-minded men sitting together, Krishna. I do not like you
speaking against them in the middle of so many evil-minded, ill-mannered, wicked
men, Krishna. Because he has not served the old, and through the pride of
fortune and delusion, of youth and impatience, he will not take your good
advice. He has a mighty army, Madhava, and if you speak to him, he will greatly
distrust you and not do what you say.

"All the sons of Dhritarashtra are certain, Janardana, that not even Indra with
the immortals could take this from them in war today. With them like this,
following desire and anger, even your sensible words will be senseless. Standing
in the middle of his army of elephants, of his forces with chariots and horses,
foolish, slow-witted Duryodhana, free of fear, thinks, 'I have conquered this
whole earth.' The son of Dhritarashtra hopes for a great kingdom on earth
without a rival. Peace alone cannot be had with him. He thinks what he wants is
already in his hands.

"This earth is ripe for time and overturned. All the fighters of the earth,
kings and rulers together, have gathered for Duryodhana's sake, wanting to fight
the Pandavas. All of them are kings you made enemies of before, Krishna, and
robbed of their strength. Out of fear of you they have taken refuge with the
sons of Dhritarashtra, and those heroes have joined together with Karna. They
have given up their lives, with Duryodhana, and are let loose, all those
fighters, to fight the Pandavas. I do not think you should go into the middle of
them, hero, Dasharha. How could you go into the middle of so many evil-minded
enemies sitting together, tamer of enemies?

"In every way you are hard to withstand even for the gods, strong-armed one. I
know your power, your manhood and your wisdom, slayer of enemies. The love I
have for the Pandavas, Madhava, I have still more for you. I say this out of
love, out of great respect and out of friendship."

"You have spoken," said the Lord, "as a very wise man would speak, a man of good
judgment, as a friend like you should speak to a friend like me. You have spoken
to me like a father and a mother, words of dharma and purpose, true, and fitting
for you. What you have said to me is true, timely and right. Now hear the reason
for my coming, Vidura. Listen carefully.

"I know Dhritarashtra's son's wickedness and the enmity of the kshatriyas, and
knowing all this, steward, I have come to the Kauravas today. The man who could
free the whole overturned earth, with its horses, chariots and elephants, from
the noose of death would win the highest dharma. If a man tries his best at a
work of dharma and cannot do it, he still wins the merit of it. I have no doubt
of that. Those who know dharma know that a man who thinks of evil in his mind
but does not take to it in deed does not get its fruit.

"So I shall try, steward, without deceit, to make peace for the Kurus and the
Srinjayas, who will perish in battle. This terrible danger has risen among the
Kurus themselves, made by Karna and Duryodhana. All the others follow them. The
wise call a man cruel who does not come to the help of a friend in trouble,
pleading with him as best he can. A man who tries his hardest, as far as his
strength goes, to turn his friend back from a wrong deed, even to the point of
seizing him by the hair, is blamed by no one. Dhritarashtra's son and his
counsellors ought to take my good and able words, Vidura, full of dharma and
purpose. I shall try without deceit for the good of the sons of Dhritarashtra,
and of the Pandavas too, and of the kshatriyas on earth.

"If Duryodhana suspects me while I am trying to do good, I will have the joy of
my heart, and I will be free of debt. When kinsmen are divided among themselves,
the wise do not call a friend a friend who does not go between them with all his
effort. Let no fools who do not know dharma, and no enemies, say of me, 'Krishna
could have stopped the angry Kurus and Pandavas, and did not.' I have come to
win the aim of both sides. If I try my hardest there, I will be beyond blame
among men. If the foolish child will not take my words, full of dharma and
purpose and healing, he will fall into the power of fate.

"If I could make peace for the Kurus without giving up the Pandavas' cause, I
would have done a holy deed with great results, and the Kurus would be freed
from the noose of death. When I speak words of wisdom, devoted to dharma, full
of sense and without harm, I hope the sons of Dhritarashtra will listen to their
good sense, and the Kurus will honour me when I come. And all the kings of the
earth together are not enough to stand before me when I am angry, as other
beasts cannot stand before a lion."

When he had said this, the bull of the Vrishnis, who brings joy to the Yadus,
lay down on a bed soft to the touch.

---

So that lucky night passed, full of stars, while the two wise men talked. As
Krishna, whose energy is boundless, listened to the many kinds of words of great
Vidura, full of dharma, purpose and desire, rich in meaning, words and
syllables, and talked of fitting things, the night passed as if he did not want
it to. Then many sweet-voiced sutas and heralds woke Keshava with the sound of
conches and drums.

Janardana, the Dasharha, the bull of all the Satvatas, rose and did all the
necessary morning rites. He did his ablutions and recitations, poured offerings
into the fire, and adorned himself. Then Madhava worshipped the rising sun.
While the Dasharha stood at his twilight prayers, the unbeaten one, Duryodhana
and Shakuni son of Subala came to him. They told Krishna that Dhritarashtra was
in the assembly, with the Kurus led by Bhishma and all the kings of the earth.
"They are asking for you, Govinda, as the immortals ask for Shakra in heaven."
Govinda greeted the two with gracious, kind words.

When the sun was clear, Janardana, scorcher of enemies, gave gold, clothes, cows
and horses to the brahmins. When the unbeaten Dasharha had given away jewels and
stood there, his charioteer came up and bowed to him. Knowing that his heavenly
chariot was there, roaring like a great mass of clouds and adorned with every
jewel, Janardana walked round the fire and the brahmins, keeping them on his
right, put on the Kaustubha jewel and blazed with the greatest splendour.
Surrounded by the Kurus and guarded by the Vrishnis, Krishna, Shauri, the joy of
all the Yadavas, mounted the chariot. Vidura, who knew all dharma, climbed up
after the Dasharha, the best of all who breathe, the best of all who keep
dharma. Then Duryodhana and Shakuni son of Subala followed the scorcher of
enemies in a second chariot. Satyaki, Kritavarma and the great chariot fighters
of the Vrishnis followed Krishna behind, in chariots and on horses and
elephants. Their bright, rattling chariots, adorned with gold and yoked with the
best of horses, shone beautifully as they went.

In time, wise Krishna, blazing with splendour, came onto the great road of the
royal seers, swept and sprinkled to lay the dust. As the Dasharha set out,
single-faced drums were played, conches were blown and every other instrument
sounded. Young heroes of the whole world, with the courage of lions, scorchers
of enemies, went round Shauri's chariot. Many thousands of others, in strange
and wonderful clothes, bearing swords, darts and weapons, went before Krishna.
Hundreds of elephants and thousands of fine horses followed the unbeaten
Dasharha hero as he went.

The whole city of the Kurus, with the old and the young and the women, had come
out into the streets, wanting to see Janardana, the tamer of enemies. The
houses, crowded in many places with women leaning on the balconies, seemed to
sway with the weight. Honoured by the Kurus, hearing many kinds of talk,
returning each greeting as was fitting and looking about him, he went slowly on.

When they reached the hall, Keshava's followers filled every direction with
conches and the sound of flutes. The whole assembly of those kings of boundless
energy shook with joy, eager for Krishna to come. When Krishna came near, the
kings were glad, hearing the roar of his chariot like the thunder of the rain
cloud. When he reached the door of the hall, Shauri, the bull of all the
Satvatas, got down from his chariot, which was like the peak of Kailasa. He went
into the hall, which was like a mountain or a cloud, as if blazing with energy,
like great Indra's dwelling. The famous one held Vidura and Satyaki by the hand,
and outshone the Kurus with his splendour, king, as the sun outshines the stars.
Karna and Duryodhana were in front of Vasudeva, and the Vrishnis and Kritavarma
behind Krishna.

With Dhritarashtra at their head, Bhishma, Drona and the others all rose from
their seats to honour Janardana. As the Dasharha came near, the great king whose
eyes are his wisdom rose, and Bhishma and Drona with him. When the great king
Dhritarashtra, lord of the people, rose, those thousands of kings rose all
round. At Dhritarashtra's command a seat had been set there for Krishna, called
All-Fortunate, adorned with gold. Smiling, Madhava, whose soul is dharma, spoke
to the king, to Bhishma and Drona, and to the other kings according to their
age. All the kings of the earth and the Kurus there honoured Keshava properly as
he came into the hall.

As he stood there among the kings, the Dasharha, scorcher of enemies, conqueror
of enemy cities, saw seers standing in the sky. When he saw those seers, led by
Narada, the Dasharha said quietly to Bhishma, son of Shantanu, "The seers have
come to see this gathering of kings. Invite them with seats and with great
honour. No one can sit while they are not seated. Let honour be paid quickly to
these sages of purified soul."

When Shantanu's son saw the seers at the door of the hall, he quickly ordered
the servants, "Seats!" They brought large, wide, polished seats from here and
there, bright with gems and gold. When the seers had sat on them and received
the guest offering, Bharata, Krishna sat down on his seat, and the kings on
theirs. Duhshasana gave Satyaki a fine seat. Vivimshati gave Kritavarma a golden
stool. Not far from Krishna, the two impatient great men, Karna and Duryodhana,
sat on one seat. Shakuni, king of Gandhara, guarded by the Gandharas, sat on a
seat with his son, lord of the people. Very wise Vidura sat on a jewelled stool
covered with a white deerskin, touching Shauri's seat.

Seeing the Dasharha after so long, all the kings of the earth could not have
enough of looking at Janardana, as of amrita. Janardana, dark as a flax flower,
in his yellow robe, shone in the middle of the hall like a gem set in gold. Then
all was silent. Every mind was on Govinda. No man there spoke a word anywhere.

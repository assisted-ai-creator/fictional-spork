# Krishna and Karna

"Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "Madhusudana left surrounded by princes and
counsellors, with Karna up beside him in his chariot. What did that slayer of
enemy heroes say to Radha's son on the chariot floor? What words of conciliation
did Govinda use on the suta's son? Tell me what Krishna, whose voice is like the
thunder of floods and clouds, said to Karna then, gentle or sharp."

"Bharata," said Sanjaya, "listen to the words that Madhusudana, whose self is
beyond measure, spoke to Radha's son, one after another, smooth and gentle,
kind, full of dharma, true and good, words that take hold of the heart."

"Radha's son," said Vasudeva, "you have attended on brahmins who have mastered
the Veda, and you have asked them about the truth with discipline and without
malice. You yourself know the eternal teachings of the Veda, Karna. You are
deeply grounded in the subtle points of the law books.

"Those who know the law books say that a son born to a girl before marriage,
*kanina*, and a son a woman brings with her at marriage, *sahodha*, have as
their father the man who marries her. You were born like that, Karna. By dharma
you are Pandu's son. By the rule of the law books, come, and you will be king.
The Parthas are your father's side and the Vrishnis your mother's. Know these
two sides as your own, bull among men.

"Come with me from here today, my son, and let the Pandavas know you as Kunti's
son, born before Yudhishthira. The five Pandava brothers will clasp your feet,
and the five sons of Draupadi too, and Subhadra's unconquered son. The kings and
princes gathered for the Pandavas' sake, and all the Andhakas and Vrishnis, will
clasp your feet. Let royal men and princesses bring jars of gold, of silver and
of earth, herbs, every kind of seed, every jewel and plant, for your
consecration. And in the sixth turn Draupadi will come to you.

"Today let the twice-born who know the four Vedas, and the Pandavas' family
priest, consecrate you as you sit on a tiger skin. So too let the five Pandava
brothers, bulls among men, the five sons of Draupadi, the Panchalas and the
Chedis. I myself will consecrate you as king, lord of the earth. Let Kunti's son
King Yudhishthira be your crown prince. Let Kunti's son Yudhishthira, whose soul
is dharma and whose vows are strict, hold the white fan and ride behind you on
your chariot. When you are consecrated, Kunti's son, mighty Kunti's son
Bhimasena will hold the great white umbrella over you. Arjuna will drive your
chariot, yoked with white horses, ringing with a hundred little bells and
covered with tiger skins. Abhimanyu will always be close at your side, and
Nakula and Sahadeva, and the five sons of Draupadi.

"The Panchalas will follow you, and the great chariot fighter Shikhandi. I will
follow you, and all the Andhakas and Vrishnis. The Dasharhas and the Dasharnas
will be your retinue, lord of the people. Enjoy the kingdom with your brothers
the Pandavas, strong-armed one, with prayers and offerings and blessings of
every kind. Let the Dravidas go before you with the Kuntalas, the Andhras, the
Talacharas, the Chuchupas and the Venupas. Let bards and heralds praise you
today with many hymns of praise, and let the Pandavas proclaim the victory of
Vasushena. Surrounded by the Parthas like the moon by the stars, rule the
kingdom, Kunti's son, and gladden Kunti. Let your friends rejoice and your
enemies tremble. Let there be brotherhood today between you and your brothers
the Pandavas."

---

"No doubt you say this out of friendship and love, Keshava," said Karna, "out of
comradeship, Varshneya, and because you wish me well. I know it all. I am
Pandu's son by dharma, by the rule of the law books, as you think, Krishna. A
girl conceived me by the sun, Janardana, and at the sun's word she cast me away
when I was born. I was born like that, Krishna. I am Pandu's son by dharma. But
Kunti abandoned me, as if I meant nothing good.

"The suta Adhiratha saw me and took me home at once, Madhusudana, and gave me to
Radha out of kindness. Out of love for me Radha's milk came at once, and she
cleaned up my urine and my dung, Madhava. How could someone like me, who knows
dharma and always delights in hearing the law books, take away her offering of
the ancestral rice? Adhiratha the suta knows me as his son, and out of affection
I have always known him as my father. He had my birth rites and the rest
performed by the rule the law books give, Madhava, out of love for a son,
Janardana. He had brahmins give me the name Vasushena, and when I came of age he
married me to wives, Keshava. Sons and grandsons have been born to me by them,
Janardana, and my heart is bound to them by love, Krishna.

"Not for the whole earth, nor for heaps of gold, Govinda, nor out of joy or
fear, could I bring myself to speak a lie. In Dhritarashtra's family, Krishna,
under Duryodhana's shelter, I have enjoyed a kingdom without thorns for thirteen
years. I have offered many sacrifices again and again with the sutas, and I have
held weddings and marriages with the sutas. Relying on me, Krishna, Duryodhana
has made ready for war, Varshneya, and for battle with the Pandavas. That is
why, Achyuta, he chose me with great joy to meet the left-handed archer face to
face in single combat. Neither for death nor for bonds, nor for fear nor for
greed, Janardana, can I bring myself to be false to wise Dhritarashtra's son. If
I do not now meet the left-handed archer in single combat, Hrishikesha, it will
bring disgrace on both of us, on me and on Partha.

"No doubt you speak for my good, Madhusudana, and the Pandavas would do all of
it, no doubt, because they are in your power. But keep this counsel secret, best
of men. I think that best here, joy of all the Yadavas. If the king, whose soul
is dharma and whose vows are strict, knows that I am Kunti's first-born son, he
will not take the kingdom. And if I won that great, rich kingdom, Madhusudana, I
would give it to Duryodhana, tamer of enemies. Let Yudhishthira, whose soul is
dharma, be king for ever, he whose guide is Hrishikesha and whose fighter is
Dhananjaya. The earth and the kingdom are his, Madhava. His men are the great
chariot fighter Bhima, Nakula and Sahadeva and the sons of Draupadi. His are
Uttamaujas, Yudhamanyu, Satyadharma the Somaka, the Chedi king and Chekitana,
and unconquered Shikhandi. His are the Kekaya brothers, the colour of cochineal,
the great chariot fighter Kuntibhoja, the colour of the rainbow, Bhimasena's
uncle, and the great chariot fighter Senajit, and Shankha, Virata's son, and
you, Janardana, the treasure-house. This great gathering of kshatriyas has been
made, Krishna. This shining kingdom, famous among all kings, is won.

---

"Dhritarashtra's son will hold a sacrifice of weapons, Varshneya. You will be
the overseer of this sacrifice, Janardana, and yours will be the work of the
adhvaryu priest, Krishna, in this rite. Ape- bannered Bibhatsu, in armour, will
be the hotri priest. Gandiva will be the ladle, and men's courage the clarified
butter. The Indra, Pashupata, Brahma and Sthunakarna weapons, Madhava, will be
the mantras the left-handed archer speaks. Subhadra's son, who takes after his
father or goes beyond him in courage, will chant the hymn of the pressing stones
well. Mighty Bhima, tiger among men, roaring as he destroys the ranks of
elephants in battle, will be the udgatri and the prastotri. King Yudhishthira,
whose soul is dharma, ever steadfast, will take the part of the brahman priest,
busy with prayers and offerings.

"The sound of conches, drums and kettledrums, Madhusudana, and lions' roars
raised high will be the call to the gods. Madri's two famous, mighty sons,
Nakula and Sahadeva, will do the work of slaughter well. Let clean chariot
spears with spotted staffs be the sacrificial posts in this sacrifice, Govinda,
Janardana. Barbed arrows, tubular arrows, iron arrows and calf-tooth arrows will
be its implements, lances its soma jars, and bows its strainers. Swords will be
its potsherds, heads its rice cakes, and blood will be the offering in this
sacrifice, Krishna. Spears and bright maces will be its kindling and enclosing
sticks. Drona's pupils and Kripa son of Sharadvat will be the assembly. The
arrows Gandiva's archer lets fly, and those great chariot fighters shoot, urged
on by Drona and Drona's son, will be the hymns sung around it. Satyaki will do
the work of the pratiprasthatri. Dhritarashtra's son will be the one
consecrated, and his great army his wife. Mighty Ghatotkacha will do the
slaughter, strong-armed one, when the Atiratra rite is spread out. And majestic
Dhrishtadyumna, Krishna, who was born from the fire when the sacred rite was
spread out, will be the fee of this sacrifice.

"The bitter things I said to the Pandavas to please Dhritarashtra's son, I burn
for that deed today. When you see me killed by the left-handed archer, Krishna,
that will be the piling of the fire altar again in this sacrifice. When the
Pandava drinks Duhshasana's blood, roaring his roar, that will be the pressing
of the soma. When the two Panchala princes bring down Drona and Bhishma, that
will be the close of the sacrifice, Janardana. When mighty Bhimasena kills
Duryodhana, Madhava, then Dhritarashtra's son's sacrifice will be finished. When
Dhritarashtra's daughters-in-law and granddaughters-in-law gather, their lords
killed, their sons killed, their protectors killed, weeping with Gandhari on the
field crowded with dogs, vultures and ospreys, that will be the final bath of
this sacrifice, Janardana.

"Bull among kshatriyas, Madhusudana, let not the kshatriyas old in learning and
old in years die a useless death because of you. Let the rich circle of
kshatriyas meet its end by weapons at Kurukshetra, Keshava, the holiest place in
all three worlds. Lotus-eyed one, arrange here what you wish, Varshneya, so that
the kshatriyas may all reach heaven. As long as the mountains stand and the
rivers flow, Janardana, so long will the sound of this fame last. Brahmins will
tell of the great Bharata war, Varshneya, at the gatherings of kshatriyas,
bearing their glory. Bring Kunti's son to battle with me, Keshava, and always
keep this counsel secret, scorcher of enemies."

**Divergences.**

* **Krishna's offer.** Krishna offers Karna the kingship, with Draupadi as his wife "in the sixth turn" (5.138.15), and Karna refuses. Ledger L-178.
* Rejected lines in these chapters add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.138.8 *kānīnaḥ*, *sahoḍhaḥ*: the law-book terms for a son born to an unmarried girl and a son a bride brings with her. They are kept with a gloss.
* 5.138.15 *ṣaṣṭhe kāle*: "in the sixth turn", that is, as the sixth husband in the turns Draupadi spends with each.
* 5.138.25: the peoples Dravida, Kuntala, Andhra, Talachara, Chuchupa and Venupa are kept as the text names them.
* 5.139.7 *piṇḍavyapanaya*: taking away a mother's ancestral rice offering, that is, denying her a son's rites.
* 5.139.26 *indragopakavarṇāḥ*: the Kekayas, "the colour of cochineal", red; Kuntibhoja "the colour of the rainbow".
* 5.139.29–44: the priests of the soma sacrifice (adhvaryu, hotri, udgatri, prastotri, brahman, pratiprasthatri) and its parts are kept. "The hymn of the pressing stones" renders *grāvastotra*; "the call to the gods" renders *subrahmaṇya*; "the work of slaughter" renders *śāmitra*.
* 5.139.43 *atirātra*: a soma rite lasting through the night.
* 5.139.46 *punaś citiḥ*: "the piling of the fire altar again".
* 5.139.56 *mahābhāratam āhavam*: "the great Bharata war".

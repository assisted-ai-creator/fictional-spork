# Krishna's Plea

When all the kings were seated and silent, Krishna began to speak, with fine
teeth and a voice like a drum. Like a cloud at the end of the hot season, making
the whole hall hear him, Madhava looked towards Dhritarashtra and spoke.

"I have come, Bharata, to try for peace between the Kurus and the Pandavas,
without the heroes having to strive. King, I have no other words of good to say
to you. You already know everything there is to know, tamer of enemies.

"This family, lord of the earth, is today the best among all the kings. It is
rich in learning and good conduct, and full of every virtue. Pity, compassion,
mercy, kindness, straightness, patience and truth, Bharata, stand out in the
Kurus. In so great a family, king, nothing unfitting should happen, least of all
because of you. You are the best restrainer of the Kurus, best of the Kurus,
when they act falsely, my son, towards outsiders or their own.

"Your sons, Kauravya, led by Duryodhana, have put dharma and purpose behind them
and behave like cruel men. They are ill-bred and have broken all bounds. Greed
has stolen their minds. They behave so towards their own chief kinsmen. You know
this, bull of the Bharatas. This very terrible danger has risen among the Kurus
themselves. If it is ignored, Kauravya, it will destroy the earth. It can be
calmed, if you wish it, Bharata. I do not think peace is hard here, bull of the
Bharatas.

"Peace depends on you, king, and on me, lord of the people. Keep your sons in
their place, Kauravya, and I will keep the others in theirs. Your sons and their
followers must do your command, king of kings. It is greatly to their good to
stand under your rule. It is good for you, king, and good for the Pandavas too,
who wish to obey me, if I try for peace. Look at it yourself without fault, lord
of the people, and arrange it. With the Bharatas together, they will all be
yours, lord of men.

"Stand firm in dharma and purpose, king, guarded by the Pandavas. Men like that
cannot be overcome, even with effort, king of men. Not even Indra with the gods
could overcome you, guarded by the great Pandavas, let alone kings. Where there
are Bhishma and Drona, Kripa, Karna and Vivimshati, Ashvatthama, Vikarna,
Somadatta and Bahlika, the king of Sindhu, the Kalinga and Sudakshina of
Kamboja, Yudhishthira, Bhimasena, the Left-handed Archer and the twins, Satyaki
of great energy and the great chariot fighter Yuyutsu, who would be so perverse
as to fight them, bull of the Bharatas?

"With the Kurus and Pandavas together, slayer of enemies, you will win lordship
over the world, and your enemies will not be able to touch you. The kings of the
earth who are your equals, lord of the earth, and those greater than you, will
make peace with you, scorcher of enemies. Guarded on every side by sons and
grandsons, brothers, fathers and friends, you will be able to live happily. Put
these men before you, honour them as before, and you will enjoy the whole earth,
lord of the earth. With all of them together, with the Pandavas and your own,
Bharata, you will conquer other enemies. That is your whole interest. You will
enjoy the land they win for you, scorcher of enemies, if you join with your sons
and counsellors, king of men.

"In war, great king, a very great destruction can be seen. In destruction on
both sides, king, what dharma do you see? If the Pandavas are killed in battle,
or your mighty sons, what happiness would you find, king? Tell me that, bull of
the Bharatas. They are all brave and skilled in weapons and eager for war, the
Pandavas and yours. Protect them from this great fear. Let us not see all the
Kurus and the Pandavas in battle, the heroes on both sides wasted, chariot
fighters killed by chariot fighters from their chariots. The kings of the earth
have gathered, best of kings. In the grip of their impatience, they would
destroy these people. Save this world, king. Let these people not perish. If you
return to your true nature, joy of the Kurus, something will be left.

"They are pure, generous, modest and noble, born of holy families, counsellors
to one another, king. Protect them from this great fear. Let these kings of the
earth meet together in peace, eat and drink together, and go home, finely
dressed and garlanded, honoured, bull of the Bharatas, with their impatience and
their enmities put away, scorcher of enemies. Let the warmth you had for the
Pandavas be yours again today, now that this end of life has come, and for ever,
bull of the Bharatas. They lost their father as children, and you brought them
up. Protect them as is right, bull of the Bharatas, and your sons too. They
should be protected by you above all in their troubles. Do not let your dharma
and your purpose perish, bull of the Bharatas.

---

"The Pandavas greet you, king, and beg your favour, and say this. 'At your
command we have suffered sorrow with our followers. We have lived these twelve
years in the forest, and the thirteenth year unrecognised among people. We were
sure our father would keep the agreement with us. We did not break the
agreement, father. Our brahmins know it. Keep to that agreement with us, bull of
the Bharatas, for we have kept it. We have always been made to suffer, king. Let
us have our share of our own kingdom. You know dharma and purpose, and you ought
to protect us properly. Seeing your weight as our elder, we bear many troubles.
Behave towards us as a mother and father, Bharata. The duty of an elder to a
pupil is weighty. A father must set us right when we take the wrong road. Set us
on the right roads, king, and keep to your own path.'

"And your sons say this to this assembly, bull of the Bharatas. 'Among members
of the assembly who know dharma, nothing unfitting should happen. Where dharma
is struck down by adharma, and truth by falsehood, while the members of the
assembly look on, the members of the assembly are struck down. When dharma comes
into the assembly pierced by adharma, and the members do not cut out the dart,
the members are pierced themselves. Dharma tears them down, as a river tears
down the trees along its banks. Those who see dharma and sit silent, brooding,
are the ones who have spoken what is true, right and just, bull of the
Bharatas.'

"What else can be said to you, lord of men, but to give? Or let the kings of the
earth who sit in the assembly speak, when they have weighed dharma and purpose,
whether I speak the truth. Free these kshatriyas from the noose of death, bull
of the kshatriyas. Be at peace, best of the Bharatas. Do not fall into the power
of anger. Give the Pandavas their proper share of their father's kingdom. Then,
with your aim won, enjoy your pleasures with your sons, scorcher of enemies.

"You know that Ajatashatru always stands in the dharma of the good, and how he
behaves towards you and your sons, king of men. He was burnt and thrown out, and
still he came back to you for shelter. You and your sons sent him away to
Indraprastha. Living there, he brought all the kings under his power and made
them look to you, king, and never went beyond you. While he behaved like this,
Subala's son, wanting to take his kingdoms, his wealth and his grain, used the
greatest of tricks. Even when he came to that state, and saw Krishna brought
into the hall, Yudhishthira, whose soul is beyond measure, did not waver from
the dharma of a kshatriya.

"I want what is best for you and for them, Bharata. Do not destroy your people's
dharma, purpose and happiness, king. Your sons think harm is gain and gain is
harm. They have gone too far in greed. Hold them back, lord of the people. The
Parthas, tamers of enemies, stand ready to serve and stand ready to fight. Stand
by what is most wholesome for you, king, scorcher of enemies."

All the kings honoured those words in their hearts. But no one there stepped
forward to speak.

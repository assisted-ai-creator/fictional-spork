# The Sacrifice of Battle

"Now that he has seven akshauhinis with the kings, Sanjaya," said Duryodhana,
"what does Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, want, eager for war?"

"Yudhishthira is very glad, king, and eager for war," said Sanjaya. "Bhimasena
and Arjuna are not afraid, and nor are the twins. Bibhatsu, son of Kunti,
wanting to test his mantras, yoked his heavenly chariot, lighting up every
direction. We saw him in his armour, like a cloud with the gleam of lightning.
He thought over the mantras, and spoke gladly. 'See this first sign, Sanjaya. We
shall win.' What Bibhatsu told me, I believe too."

"You praise them and rejoice in them," said Duryodhana, "those Parthas who were
beaten at dice. Tell me about the horses on Arjuna's chariot, and about his
banner."

"Bhauvana Tvashtri, with Shakra and with Dhatri, lord of the people," said
Sanjaya, "made forms on it of many colours. On that banner they made forms by
the magic of the gods, of great worth, heavenly, large and small. The banner
fills a yojana on every side, across and upwards. It does not catch on trees,
even when it is hemmed in by them. Such is the magic Bhauvana made. As Shakra's
bow shines in the sky, not of one colour, and we do not know what it is, so
Bhauvana made the banner. Its form is seen in many shapes. As the smoke of fire
goes up to heaven filling it, bearing colours, a body of light, so Bhauvana made
the banner. It has no weight and nothing hinders it.

"On his chariot are fine white horses as swift as the wind, heavenly ones given
by Chitraratha. Their number is always kept full at a hundred. However many are
killed, the boon given long ago makes them whole. In the same way the king's
great horses, the colour of ivory, yoked to his chariot, shine, equal in
strength to him. Bhimasena's horses look like rishya deer, and in battle they
are as swift as the wind. Sahadeva is drawn happily by horses with speckled
bodies and backs mottled like partridges, given by his brother Phalguna, who
loves him. They are better than the hero's brother's own horses. Nakula, son of
Madri, of Ajamidha's line, is drawn by the best of horses, bays given by great
Indra, as strong and swift as the wind, as Indra is drawn when he goes against
Vritra. Great horses given by the gods, their equals in age, courage and speed,
fine horses without match, draw the young princes, Subhadra's son and the sons
of Draupadi."

---

"Whom did you see there, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra, "come to stand against
us, who will fight my son's army for the Pandavas?"

"I saw Krishna come, the chief of the Andhakas and Vrishnis," said Sanjaya, "and
Chekitana and Yuyudhana Satyaki there too. Each has come to the Pandavas with
his own akshauhini. Both are known as great chariot fighters, and both think
highly of their manhood. The Panchala came with an akshauhini, surrounded by his
ten sons, heroes led by Satyajit and Dhrishtadyumna. Drupada, guarded by
Shikhandi, came, adding to their pride, covering the bodies of all the armies.
King Virata, with his two sons Shankha and Uttara, with heroes led by Suryadatta
and Madirashva, with his brothers and sons, surrounded by an akshauhini of
troops, has joined the Partha. The Magadha son of Jarasandha and Dhrishtaketu
king of the Chedis have come, each surrounded by his own akshauhini. The five
Kekaya brothers, all with red banners, surrounded by an akshauhini, have joined
the Pandavas. These are the ones I saw gathered there, so many, who will fight
the army of Dhritarashtra's son for the Pandavas.

"Great Dhrishtadyumna, who knows the arrays of men, gods, gandharvas and asuras,
is at the head of their army. Bhishma, son of Shantanu, king, is set as
Shikhandi's share, and Virata will follow him with the hard-hitting Matsyas. The
mighty king of the Madras is the share of the eldest son of Pandu. But some
there said the two were not well matched. Duryodhana with his sons and his
hundred brothers, and the men of the east and south, are Bhimasena's share.
Karna Vaikartana is counted as Arjuna's share, with Ashvatthama, Vikarna and
Jayadratha, king of Sindhu. Arjuna, son of Pritha, has also taken as his share
all those on earth who think themselves heroes and cannot be beaten.

"The great archers, the five Kekaya princes, brothers, will fight in battle with
the Kekayas as their share. To them as well fall the Malavas, the Shalvakas and
the Kekayas, and the two chief Trigartas who are called the Sworn Warriors. All
of Duryodhana's sons and Duhshasana's sons are the share of Subhadra's son, and
King Brihadbala too. Draupadi's sons, the great archers with banners worked in
gold, will attack Drona, Bharata, with Dhrishtadyumna at their head. Chekitana
wants to fight Somadatta in single combat. Yuyudhana wants to fight Kritavarma
the Bhoja. Sahadeva, son of Madri, the hero who roars in battle, has set your
brother-in-law, the son of Subala, as his share. Nakula, son of Madri, has set
as his share Uluka, the gambler's son, and the hosts of the Sarasvatas. And the
other kings who will come against them in battle, king, the sons of Pandu have
assigned them too, by challenge. This is how their forces are divided, share by
share. Do what you and your son must do, without delay."

"My foolish sons, who played that wicked game of dice, are as good as gone,"
said Dhritarashtra, "since they must fight mighty Bhima at the head of the
battle. All the kings of the earth are sprinkled for the sacrifice by the dharma
of time. They will go into the fire of the Gandiva like moths into a flame. I
think my army is already routed by those great men who were wronged. Who will
follow it in battle when it is broken by the Pandavas?

"They are all great chariot fighters, heroes, famous and mighty, equal to the
sun and fire in energy, conquerors in battle. Their leader is Yudhishthira and
their guard is Madhusudana. Their fighters are the two heroic Pandavas, the
Left-handed Archer and Vrikodara, and Nakula, Sahadeva, Dhrishtadyumna son of
Prishata, Satyaki, Drupada and Dhrishtadyumna's son. They have Uttamaujas of the
Panchalas, Yudhamanyu the unbeatable, Shikhandi, Kshatradeva and Uttara,
Virata's son. They have the Kashis and Chedis, the Matsyas and all the
Srinjayas, Virata's son Babhru, and the Panchala Prabhadrakas. Even Indra could
not take this earth from those heroes, steady in battle, who could split
mountains, if they did not wish it. They are rich in every virtue, with power
more than human. And my wicked son wants to fight them all, Sanjaya, while I cry
out."

"We are both of the same kind," said Duryodhana, "and both walk on the earth.
Why do you think victory is on the Pandavas' side alone? Not even Indra with the
immortals could beat the grandfather, Drona, Kripa, Karna the unbeatable,
Jayadratha, Somadatta and Ashvatthama in battle, those wise great archers, let
alone the Pandavas, my father. The whole earth has been gathered for my sake, my
father, to crush the Pandavas, noble, steady, brave and like fire as they are.
The Pandavas cannot even look at my men. I am strong enough to fight the sons of
Pandu and their sons in battle. All the kings who want to please me, Bharata,
will hold them back like antelope with a rope. The Panchalas and the Pandavas
will be overrun by my great line of chariots and my nets of arrows."

"My son talks like a madman, Sanjaya," said Dhritarashtra. "He cannot beat the
Dharma King Yudhishthira in battle. Bhishma has always known the strength of the
famous Pandavas and their sons, who know dharma and are great. That is why I did
not like a war with those great men. But tell me again, Sanjaya, what they are
doing. Who keeps firing up those swift Pandavas, great archers, as fire is fired
with butter?"

"Dhrishtadyumna always fires them up, Bharata," said Sanjaya. "He says, 'Fight,
best of the Bharatas, and do not be afraid of war. Whatever kings
Dhritarashtra's son has gathered round him will meet in the terrible battle, the
lake of armour. When they come together in battle, angry, with all who are bound
to them, I alone will take them as a whale takes fish from the water. I will
hold back Bhishma, Drona, Kripa, Karna, Drona's son, Shalya and Suyodhana, as
the shore holds back the sea.'

"When he spoke like this, King Yudhishthira, whose soul is dharma, said, 'The
Panchalas and the Pandavas have all climbed onto your steadiness and courage.
Bring us safely out of the battle. I know you, strong-armed one, firm in the
dharma of a kshatriya, able on your own and enough for the Kauravas who want to
fight. Whatever you arrange will be best for us, scorcher of enemies. When men
flee from battle, broken and looking for refuge, the hero who stands in front of
them showing his manhood should be bought for a thousand. That is the saying of
the wise. You are brave, heroic and bold, bull among men. You will surely be the
protector of the frightened in battle.'

"When Yudhishthira, son of Kunti, whose soul is dharma, had said this,
Dhrishtadyumna spoke to me, quite without fear. 'Suta, tell all the peoples who
fight for Duryodhana, the Kurus with the Bahlikas, Pratipa's line and
Sharadvat's son, the suta's son, Drona and his son, Jayadratha, Duhshasana,
Vikarna and King Duryodhana. And go quickly and tell Bhishma: approach
Yudhishthira with kindness. Do not let Arjuna, guarded by the gods, kill you.
Quickly beg the pardon of the Pandava, the hero of the world. There is no
fighter on earth like the Left-handed Archer, the Pandava, the greatest master
of weapons. The heavenly chariot of the Gandiva bowman was made by the gods. He
cannot be beaten by a man. Do not set your minds on war.'"

---

"The Pandava has the energy of a kshatriya," said Dhritarashtra, "and has lived
the celibate life since boyhood. And the fools will go to war with him, while I
lament. Duryodhana, turn back from war, best of the Bharatas. War is not praised
in any state, tamer of enemies. Half the earth is enough for you and your
counsellors to live on. Give the sons of Pandu their fitting share, scorcher of
enemies. All the Kurus think it right by dharma that you should seek peace with
the great sons of Pandu. Look at your own army, my son. Your weakness has
already shown itself, but in your folly you do not see it.

"I do not want war. Bahlika does not want it. Neither does Bhishma, nor Drona,
nor Ashvatthama, nor Sanjaya. Somadatta does not want war, nor Shalya, nor
Kripa, nor Satyavrata, Purumitra, Jaya or Bhurishravas. The men the Kurus would
lean on when pressed by their enemies do not welcome war. Let that please you,
my son. You are not doing this of your own will. Karna is making you do it, and
wicked Duhshasana, and Shakuni, son of Subala."

"I do not challenge them putting the burden on you," said Duryodhana, "or on
Drona, Ashvatthama, Sanjaya, Vikarna, the Kamboja, Kripa or Bahlika, on
Satyavrata, Purumitra or Bhurishravas, or on any of your people. Karna and I, my
father, have spread out the sacrifice of battle, and we are consecrated, with
Yudhishthira as the victim, bull of the Bharatas. The chariot is the altar, the
sword the ladle, the mace the spoon, the armour the seat of the sacrifice. My
horses are the four priests, the arrows the sacred grass, and glory the
offering. When we have worshipped Vaivasvata in battle with the sacrifice of
ourselves, king, we two will come back victorious, our enemies killed,
surrounded by fortune. I and Karna, my father, and my brother Duhshasana, we
three will kill the Pandavas in battle. Either I will kill the Pandavas and rule
this earth, or the sons of Pandu will kill me and enjoy it. I have given up my
life, my wealth and my kingdom, king. I will never live with the Pandavas,
unfallen one. As much of our land as the point of a sharp needle would pierce,
sir, I will not give up to the Pandavas."

"I grieve for all of you, my children," said Dhritarashtra. "I have given up
Duryodhana. You will follow the fool as he goes to Vaivasvata's house. Like
tigers among herds of ruru deer, the Pandavas, the best of fighters, will come
together in battle and kill your best men one by one. The army of the Bharatas
seems to me already turned back by Yuyudhana, scattered and terrified, like a
woman with her parting wiped out by the long-armed one. Madhava, Shini's
grandson, will stand in battle filling up the Partha's full army even more,
sowing arrows like seed. Bhimasena will be at the head of the army of fighters.
They will all shelter behind him as behind a wall, with nothing to fear.

"You will see the elephants struck down by Bhima, their tusks shattered, their
temples split, covered in blood, like broken mountains in battle. Then you will
be afraid of Bhima's touch and remember my words. When you see your army burnt
up by Bhimasena, its chariots and elephants destroyed, like the path of a fire,
you will remember my words. A great fear is coming on you if you do not make
peace with the Pandavas. Killed by Bhimasena's mace, you will find peace then.
When you see the army of the Kurus cut down in battle like a great forest that
has been felled, you will remember my words."

When he had said this to all the kings of the earth, the king, great king,
turned and questioned Sanjaya again.

* Rejected lines (\*341–\*345 and others in these chapters) add detail. Not narrated.

**Choices.**

* 5.55.7 *bhauvanaḥ*: Tvashtri, "Bhauvana", the divine craftsman.
* 5.55.12: Chitraratha the gandharva gave the horses (Book 1). "However many are killed, the boon given long ago makes them whole" renders *hataṃ hataṃ dattavaraṃ purastāt*.
* 5.55.13 *ṛśyaprakhyāḥ*: "like rishya deer", a kind of antelope.
* 5.56.13 *viṣamau no matāv iti*: "the two were not well matched". The speakers are not named.
* 5.56.18 *saṃśaptakāv iti*: "who are called the Sworn Warriors".
* 5.56.27 *prokṣitāḥ kāladharmaṇā*: the kings are "sprinkled for the sacrifice by the dharma of time", as victims are sprinkled.
* 5.56.41 *aiṇeyān iva tantunā*: "like antelope with a rope".
* 5.56.48 *kavacahrade*: "the lake of armour", an image for the battle.
* 5.57.13: the parts of the battle-sacrifice are kept exactly as Duryodhana lists them.
* 5.57.21 *vyastā sīmantinī*: "like a woman with her parting wiped out", a sign of widowhood.

# The Guest Gift

Then, on the day of the consecration, the brahmins and the great seers went with
the kings into the inner altar, to honour them. Led by Narada, the great men sat
in the inner altar with the royal seers, and they shone like the gods and divine
seers gathered in Brahma's house. Between the rites, the seers of boundless
energy talked together. "This is how it is." "No, it is not." "It is just so,
and not otherwise." So many of them said, arguing with one another. Some made
weighty points light, and some made light points weighty, with reasons drawn
from the treatises. Some clever men there tore apart a point others had filled
out, as hawks tear meat in the sky. Others, of great vows, the best of all who
know the Vedas, enjoyed themselves telling stories full of dharma and wealth.
That altar, crowded with gods, brahmins and great seers who knew the Veda, shone
like the clear sky crowded with stars. No shudra was near it, king, and no one
who had not taken a vow, inside the altar in Yudhishthira's dwelling.

Narada was pleased when he saw the glory of the glorious and wise Dharma King,
born of the sacrifice's rites. Then the sage fell to thinking, lord of men, as
he looked at that gathering of all the kshatriyas. He remembered the ancient
tale, bull of the Bharatas, of what had happened in Brahma's house at the
descent of the portions. Knowing that this was that gathering of the gods, joy
of the Kurus, Narada thought in his mind of lotus-eyed Hari.

*The lord Narayana himself, the slayer of the gods' enemies, the conqueror of
enemy cities, has been born among the kshatriyas, keeping his promise. The maker
of beings himself once commanded the gods, "Killing one another, you will win
back your worlds." With that command to all the gods, the blessed Narayana, the
benign one, lord of the world, was born in the house of the Yadus, in the line
of the Andhakas and Vrishnis on earth, the best of those who carry on a line. He
shines with the greatest glory, like the moon among the stars. He whose strength
of arm all the gods and Indra serve, this is Hari, the crusher of enemies, who
lives here as a man. Oh, what a great thing it is, that the Self-born himself
will take back again the kshatriya class, so full of strength.*

So Narada, who knew dharma, thought, knowing Hari Narayana, the lord worshipped
in sacrifices. And the great-minded man, the best of those who know dharma,
stayed at the wise Dharma King's great sacrifice, greatly honoured.

---

Then Bhishma said to the Dharma King Yudhishthira, king, "Let the kings be given
the guest gift, each as he deserves, Bharata. They say six deserve the guest
gift, Yudhishthira: a teacher, a priest, a kinsman by marriage, a graduate, a
friend, and a king. They say these deserve it when they come after living away a
year. These have come to us after a long time. Let the guest gift be brought to
each of them, king. Or let it be offered first to the greatest and ablest of
them."

"Which one do you think should be offered the guest gift, joy of the Kurus?"
said Yudhishthira. "Who is fitting? Tell me, grandfather."

Then Bhishma, Shantanu's son, made up his mind, Bharata, and judged the
Varshneya Krishna the most worthy of honour on earth. "In energy, strength and
courage he shines among all these gathered here, as the sun blazes among the
stars. This assembly of ours is lit and gladdened by Krishna, as a sunless place
is lit by the sun, and a windless place gladdened by the wind."

With Bhishma's leave, the mighty Sahadeva offered the fine guest gift to the
Varshneya, as the rules require. Krishna accepted it with the rite the treatises
lay down.

But Shishupala could not bear that honour paid to Vasudeva. The mighty king of
Chedi reproached Bhishma and the Dharma King in the assembly, and he abused
Vasudeva.

"This Varshneya does not deserve the honour due to a king, as if he were a king,
Kaurava, while these great lords of the earth are here. It is not right of the
great Pandavas, Pandava, that you have honoured the lotus-eyed one out of
fondness. You are children, Pandavas. You do not know, for dharma is subtle. And
this son of the river has gone too far. He sees little. For when a man like you,
devoted to dharma, acts to please, Bhishma, he is all the more despised by the
good in the world.

"How can the Dasharha, who is not a king, deserve such honour among all the
lords of the earth, as you have given him? Or if you think Krishna an elder,
bull of the Bharatas, how does the son deserve it, while old Vasudeva is here?
Or if Vasudeva is your well-wisher and follower, how does Madhava deserve honour
while Drupada is here? Or if you think Krishna a teacher, bull of the Kurus, why
have you honoured the Varshneya while Drona is here? Or if you think Krishna a
priest, joy of the Kurus, how have you honoured Krishna while the brahmin
Dvaipayana is here? Madhusudana is not a priest, not a teacher, not a king. And
yet you have honoured him, best of the Kurus. What else is this but fondness?

"Or if Madhusudana is the one you want to honour, why bring these kings here to
insult them, Bharata? We do not pay tribute to the great Kaunteya out of fear,
or greed, or because we are coaxed. We pay him tribute because he is devoted to
dharma and wants to be king of the earth, and now he does not respect us. What
else is it but an insult, that in the kings' assembly you have honoured with the
guest gift Krishna, who has not the marks of a king? The son of Dharma has won
the name of righteous for nothing. For who would give such honour to one fallen
from dharma, one born in the Vrishni family who killed a king before? Today
righteousness has been stripped from Yudhishthira, and meanness shown in
Krishna, by the giving of this guest gift.

"If the sons of Kunti are frightened, mean and wretched, you should still have
known, Madhava, what honour you deserve. Or why did you allow yourself to be
given this honour you do not deserve, Janardana, when these mean men offered it?
You think much of an honour that does not suit you, like a dog that finds a drop
of the offering in a lonely place and eats it. This is no insult to the lords of
kings. It is you, plainly, whom the Kurus are making fools of, Janardana. As a
wife for a eunuch, or a show for the blind, so is the honour of a king for one
who is not a king, Madhusudana. We have seen what King Yudhishthira is. We have
seen what Bhishma is. We have seen this Vasudeva too, all just as they are."

When he had said this, Shishupala rose from his fine seat and walked out of the
assembly with the kings.

---

Then King Yudhishthira ran after Shishupala and spoke to him sweetly and kindly.
"What you have said, lord of the earth, is not right. It is great wrong, king,
and harshness to no purpose. Bhishma, Shantanu's son, could never fail to know
the highest dharma, lord of the earth. So do not think of him otherwise. See
these many lords of the earth, older than you. They accept the honour paid to
Krishna, and so should you. Bhishma knows Krishna truly, lord of Chedi, and
well. You do not know him as the Kaurava knows him."

"No kindness should be shown him," said Bhishma. "He does not deserve to be
coaxed, when he will not allow honour to Krishna, the eldest in the world. A
kshatriya who conquers a kshatriya in battle, the best of warriors, and captures
him and lets him go, becomes his elder. In this assembly of kings I do not see a
single lord of the earth who has not been beaten in battle by the might of the
son of the Satvata woman. The unfallen one is not only the most worthy of honour
to us. Janardana is worthy of honour in all three worlds. For Krishna has
defeated many bulls among kshatriyas in battle, and the whole world rests wholly
on the Varshneya. That is why, though there are elders here, we honour Krishna
and not the others. You should not speak like this. Do not let your mind be like
this.

"I have sat with many who were old in wisdom, king. When they met, I heard them
tell the many virtues of Shauri, rich in virtues, which the good respect. And
the deeds of that wise one, from his birth onwards, I have heard told again and
again by men. We do not honour Janardana out of fondness, king of Chedi, or
because of kinship, or for some gain. We honour him because the good honour him
on earth, because he brings the earth happiness. We give him honour because we
know his fame, his courage and his victories. There is no one here, not even a
child, whom we have not considered. Passing over those old in virtue, we judged
Hari the most worthy of honour.

"Among brahmins it is the eldest in wisdom, among kshatriyas the strongest, who
is honoured. Both these reasons are found in Govinda. Knowledge of the Veda and
its limbs, and boundless strength too: who in the world of men has these above
others, except Keshava? Generosity, skill, learning, courage, modesty, fame, the
highest intelligence, humility, glory, steadiness, contentment and prosperity
are always found in the unfallen one. He has all of these, and he is a teacher,
a father and an elder, worthy of honour, honoured and deserving of honour. You
should all accept it. A priest, an elder, a kinsman by marriage, a graduate, a
king, a friend, all of these are in Hrishikesha. That is why the unfallen one
has been honoured.

"Krishna is the origin of the worlds and their end. For Krishna's sake all this
universe of beings was made. He is unmanifest nature, the eternal maker, and
higher than all beings. So the unfallen one is the eldest of all. Intellect,
mind, the great one, wind, fire, water, sky and earth, and every being of the
four kinds, all rest in Krishna. The sun and the moon, the stars and the
planets, the quarters and the points between, all rest in Krishna.

"But this man Shishupala is a child. He does not know Krishna, everywhere and
always, and so he speaks like this. A wise man who looked for the highest dharma
would see it as it is, and this king of Chedi does not. Among the great kings
here, old and young, who does not think Krishna worthy, or would not honour him?
If Shishupala thinks this honour wrongly given, let him do about it what is
right."

When he had said this, glorious Bhishma fell silent. Then Sahadeva gave his
answer, full of meaning.

"Kings, if any of you cannot bear Keshava, the slayer of Keshin, of boundless
courage, being honoured by me, I have set my foot on the heads of all the
strong. When I have said this plainly, let him answer. But let those kings who
are wise approve the teacher, the father and the elder, worthy of honour,
honoured and deserving of honour."

Then not one of those wise and good kings, proud and strong as they were, said
anything, though he had shown his foot among them. Then a rain of flowers fell
on Sahadeva's head, and voices from beings who could not be seen said, "Well
done! Well done!" Narada, who speaks of what will be and what has been, who
frees all from doubt, who knows all the worlds, shook his black deerskin.

The kings led by Sunitha, who had been invited and had come, were seen there,
furious and pale in the face. They spoke of Yudhishthira's consecration and
Vasudeva's honour in disgust, having made up their own minds. As their friends
held them back, they looked like lions dragged away from their meat, roaring.
Krishna saw then that the endless host, the inexhaustible ocean of kings, was
making a pact for war.

When he had honoured those who deserved honour, the brahmins and kshatriyas
above all, Sahadeva, the god among men, finished that rite. When Krishna had
been honoured, Sunitha, the scourge of enemies, his eyes red with anger, said to
the lords of men, "I stand here as your general. What do you think we should do
now? Let us arm, and stand in battle against the Vrishnis and the Pandavas
together."

When he had roused all those kings, the bull of the Chedis took counsel with the
kings to wreck the sacrifice.

  which Sahadeva threatens the kings (App. I No. 21). Not in the CE.

  he says only "one born in the Vrishni family who killed a king before" (2.34.15).

  and end of the worlds, unmanifest nature and the eternal maker (2.35.22–25).

# East, South and West

At the same time mighty Bhimasena took leave of the Dharma King and set out for
the eastern quarter. The tiger of the Bharatas, who made his enemies' grief
grow, was surrounded by a great host that crushed other kingdoms. The tiger
among kings went to the great city of the Panchalas, and the Pandava won the
Panchalas over by many means. Then the hero, the bull among men, quickly
conquered the Gandaki and the Videhas, and the lord went on to the Dasharnas.
There King Sudharma of Dasharna fought a great fight with Bhima without weapons,
a fight to make the hair stand on end. When Bhimasena, the tamer of enemies, saw
what he had done, he made mighty Sudharma his commander-in-chief.

Then Bhima of fearsome courage went on to the eastern quarter with a great army,
king, as if making the earth shake. The hero, the best of the strong, defeated
in battle by his strength Rochamana, lord of Ashvamedha, and his younger
brother, king. When he had defeated him without much fighting, the mighty
Kaunteya, joy of the Kurus, conquered the eastern country. Then he came south to
the great city of the Pulindas, and brought under his power Sukumara and King
Sumitra.

Then, at the Dharma King's command, the bull of the Bharatas went against mighty
Shishupala, Janamejaya. When the king of Chedi, the tamer of enemies, heard what
the Pandava meant to do, he came out of his city and received him. When the two
bulls of the Kurus and the Chedis met, great king, they asked each other about
the welfare of their families. Then the king of Chedi handed over his kingdom,
lord of the people, and said to Bhima, laughing, "What is this you are doing,
sinless one?" Bhima told him what the Dharma King meant to do, and the king
accepted it and did as he was asked. Then Bhima stayed there thirteen nights,
king, honoured by Shishupala, and went on with his army and his mounts.

---

Then in the country of Kumara he defeated Shreniman, and Brihadbala, lord of
Kosala, the tamer of enemies. The best of the Pandavas defeated the righteous
Dirghaprajna of Ayodhya, a mighty man, without much fighting. Then the lord
conquered Gopalakaccha, the Sottamas and the northern Uttaras, and the king who
ruled the Mallas. Then the mighty one came to Jaradgava, on the flank of the
Himalaya, and brought the whole country under his power in a short time.

When he had conquered many countries of many kinds, the bull among men, the
Pandava of great courage, the best of the strong, conquered Unnata and the
mountain Kukshimat round it by his strength. Strong-armed Bhima of fearsome
courage brought Subandhu, king of Kashi, who never turned back, under his power
in battle. Then the bull of the Pandavas defeated in battle Kratha, the overlord
of kings, round Suparshva, as he fought by force. Then the splendid one
conquered the Matsyas and the mighty Malayas, the blameless Gayas, and all the
land of cattle. Turning back, the strong-armed one conquered Mount Madarvika
with its neighbours, and set out facing north. The mighty Kaunteya conquered
Vatsabhumi by force. He conquered the lord of the Bhargas and the lord of the
Nishadas, and many kings led by Maniman. Then Bhima the Pandava conquered the
southern Mallas and Bhogavat by force, without much fighting. The lord conquered
the Sharmakas and the Varmakas by conciliation alone. The tiger among men
conquered King Janaka of Videha, lord of the world, without much fighting.

While he was in Videha, Kaunteya, the Pandava conquered the seven lords of the
Kiratas near Mount Indra. Then the mighty Kaunteya conquered in battle the
Suhmas, the eastern Suhmas and the Samakshas, and went on to the Magadhans. He
conquered the lords of the earth Danda and Dandadhara, and with all of them he
marched on Girivraja. He won over Jarasandha's son with kind words and made him
pay tribute. Then the mighty one marched with all of them against Karna, king.
Making the earth shake with his army of four branches, the best of the Pandavas
fought Karna, the slayer of enemies. When he had defeated Karna in battle and
brought him under his power, Bharata, the mighty one conquered the kings who
lived in the mountains. Then by the strength of his arms the Pandava struck down
in a great battle the king of Modagiri, a very strong king.

Then he conquered in battle the hero Vasudeva, the mighty lord of the Pundras,
and the king of great strength who lived in the marshes of the Kaushiki. Both
were heroes, both had strong armies, and both were of fierce courage, great
king. When he had defeated them in battle, he marched against the king of Vanga.
The bull of the Bharatas defeated Samudrasena and King Chandrasena, the king of
Tamralipta, and Kacha, lord of Vanga, and the lord of the Suhmas, and all the
peoples of the barbarians who lived by the sea.

When the son of the wind had conquered many countries of many kinds, and taken
wealth from them, the mighty one went to the Lauhitya. He made all the barbarian
kings who lived on the islands of the sea pay tribute. They gave him jewels of
many kinds, sandal and aloe and cloth, and the finest gems and pearls. They gave
him gold, silver, diamonds and coral of great price. With that great wealth, ten
thousand million strong, the Pandava of boundless spirit was showered in a rain
of wealth. Then Bhima of fearsome courage came back to Indraprastha, and gave
that wealth to the Dharma King.

---

So too Sahadeva, honoured by the Dharma King, set out with a great army for the
southern quarter. First the lord conquered the Shurasenas entirely, and the
mighty Kaurava brought the king of the Matsyas under his power by force. He
defeated Dantavakra, overlord of the Adhirajas, in a great battle, made him pay
tribute, and set him back on his own throne. He brought Sukumara and King
Sumitra under his power, and so too he conquered the other Matsyas and the
Patachcharas. The wise one quickly conquered the land of the Nishadas,
Goshringa, the finest of mountains, and King Shreniman. When he had conquered
Navarashtra, he went to Kuntibhoja, who gladly accepted his command.

Then on the banks of the Charmanvati he saw the king who was the son of
Jambhaka, whom Vasudeva, his old enemy, had left alive. He fought a battle there
with the Bhoja, Bharata. When he had defeated him in battle, he went on facing
south. He took tribute from them, and jewels of many kinds, and then went with
them all towards the Narmada. The mighty son of the Ashvins defeated in battle
the two heroes of Avanti, Vinda and Anuvinda, surrounded by a great army.

Then, taking jewels, he went to the city of Mahishmati. There the bull among men
fought a battle with King Nila. The Pandava Sahadeva, mighty slayer of enemy
heroes, fought a very great battle with him, which frightened the fearful,
destroyed armies, and put lives in doubt. For the blessed carrier of offerings,
Agni, helped the king. Horses, chariots, elephants, men and armour were seen
blazing in Sahadeva's army. Then the joy of the Kurus was thrown into confusion,
and he could find no answer, Janamejaya.

"Why did the blessed Agni become an enemy in battle, brahmin," said Janamejaya,
"to Sahadeva, when he was striving for the sake of the sacrifice?"

"It is said that long ago the blessed carrier of offerings, who lived in
Mahishmati, was caught as an adulterer," said Vaishampayana. "He was brought
before the forefathers of King Nila, wandering there at will in the form of a
brahmin. The righteous king punished him as the treatises require. Then the
blessed carrier of offerings blazed up in anger. The king was amazed when he saw
it, and bowed his head to the sage. Then the fire was gracious to the king. The
best of offerers offered the king a boon, and the lord of the earth chose safety
for his army. From that time on, any kings who in their ignorance try to conquer
that city by force are burned here by the fire. And in that city of Mahishmati,
then, the women could not be held back, they say, as they wished, joy of the
Kurus. So Agni gave a boon, that the women should not be restrained. The women
there are free, and go about as they please. And since then, great king, kings
always avoid that kingdom, best of men, for fear of Agni."

When righteous Sahadeva saw his army stricken with fear and surrounded by fire,
king, he stood as still as a mountain. He touched water, purified himself, and
said to Agni, "This undertaking is for your sake, black-tracked one. I bow to
you. You are the mouth of the gods. You are the sacrifice, purifier. You are the
purifier because you purify, and the carrier of offerings because you carry
them. The Vedas were born for your sake, and so you are Jatavedas. You should
not put this obstacle in the way of the sacrifice, carrier of offerings."

When he had said this, the son of Madri spread kusha grass on the ground and sat
down before the fire as the rules require, in front of his whole frightened
army, Bharata. And the fire did not pass beyond him, as the ocean does not pass
its shore. The fire came slowly up to the joy of the Kurus, Sahadeva, the god
among men, and spoke to him kindly. "Rise, rise, Kaurava. I was only testing
you. I know all that you and the son of Dharma mean to do. But I must protect
this city, best of the Bharatas, as long as King Nila's line endures. Yet I will
do what your heart wishes, Pandava."

Then the bull among men, the son of Madri, rose with a glad heart, joined his
hands, bowed his head, and worshipped the fire. When the fire had turned back,
King Nila came with honour to Sahadeva, the tiger among men, the lord of
fighters. He accepted that honour, made him pay tribute, and then the son of
Madri went on south in victory.

The strong-armed one brought the king of Traipura, of boundless energy, under
his power, and forcibly subdued the lord of Potana. With great effort the
strong-armed one then brought under his power Ahriti, the teacher of the
Kaushikas, and the lord of Surashtra. While he was in the country of Surashtra,
the righteous one sent word to Rukmin, the wise king at Bhojakata, the great
minister, and to Bhishmaka, Indra's own friend. And Bhishmaka and his son gladly
accepted his command, out of regard for Vasudeva. Then the lord of fighters took
jewels and set out again.

The splendid one brought under his power Shurparaka, the tribe called Upakrita,
and the mighty Dandakas. He brought under his power the kings of the sea
islands, born of barbarians, the Nishadas, the man-eaters, the Karnapravaranas,
and the men called Kalamukhas, who are born of rakshasas. The wise one brought
under his power all of Kollagiri, and Murachipattana, the island called Tamra,
Mount Ramaka, and King Timingila. By envoys alone he brought under his power the
one-footed men, and those who live wholly in the forests, the city of
Samjayanti, Picchanda and Karahataka, and made them pay tribute. By envoys alone
he brought under his power the Pandyas and the Dravidas, with the Odras and
Keralas, the Andhras, the Talavanas, the Kalingas and the Oshtrakarnikas. He did
the same with Antakhi, Roma and the city of the Yavanas, and made them pay
tribute.

When he reached Bharukaccha, the wise son of Madravati, the tamer of enemies,
sent envoys out of friendship, king of kings, to great Vibhishana, grandson of
Pulastya. And he accepted his command gladly. The wise lord took it that the
time had brought it about. Then he sent jewels of many kinds, sandal and the
finest aloe, heavenly ornaments, costly clothes and gems of great price. Then
wise and mighty Sahadeva turned back. When he had conquered by force, by
conciliation and by victory, and made the kings pay tribute, the tamer of
enemies came back. He gave it all to the Dharma King, bull of the Bharatas, and,
his work done, lived happily, king, Janamejaya.

---

Now I will tell of the deeds and the conquest of Nakula, how the lord conquered
the quarter that Vasudeva had once conquered.

The wise one left Khandavaprastha and set out for the western quarter with a
great army. He made this earth shake with a great lion's roar, with the shouts
of his fighters and the rumble of chariot wheels. He marched on Rohitaka, rich
in wealth and lovely, full of cattle, horses, wealth and grain, dear to
Karttikeya. There was a great battle there with the brave Mattamayurakas. The
splendid one brought under his power all of Marubhumi, and Bahudhanyaka,
Shairishaka and Mahettha. He conquered the Shibis, the Trigartas, the
Ambashthas, the Malavas and the five Karpatas, and the Madhyamikas and the
Vatadhana brahmins. Then, turning again, the bull among men conquered the tribes
who live in the Pushkara forest, the Utsavasamketas.

The splendid one quickly brought under his power the mighty village peoples who
live on the banks of the Sindhu. He subdued the Shudra and Abhira tribes who
live along the Sarasvati, those who live by fishing and those who live in the
mountains. He took all of Panchanada, Aparaparyata, Uttarajyotika, the city of
Vrindataka, and Dvarapala. The Pandava brought the Ramathas, the Harahunas and
all the kings of the west under his power by his command alone. From there the
mighty one sent word to Vasudeva, and he accepted his command with ten kingdoms.

Then the strong one came to Shakala, the city of the Madras, and brought his
uncle Shalya under his power in friendship. Honoured by the king, as he
deserved, lord of the people, the lord of fighters took many jewels and set out.
Then he brought under his power the very fierce barbarians who lived in the bays
of the sea, the Pahlavas and the Barbaras. When he had taken jewels and brought
the kings under his power, Nakula, the best of men, who knew the ways of the
chariot, turned back. Ten thousand camels carried that great one's treasure,
great king, with difficulty, it was so great. Madri's glorious son came to the
hero Yudhishthira in Indraprastha, and gave him the wealth.

So Nakula, the bull of the Bharatas, conquered the western quarter, guarded by
Varuna, which Vasudeva had conquered before.

**Divergences.**

* **Lanka.** In the CE Sahadeva sends envoys from Bharukaccha to Vibhishana, who accepts his
  command and sends gifts (2.28.50–53). The popular story that Ghatotkacha went to Lanka to
  bring Vibhishana's tribute is only in \*309 and App. I No. 15. See ledger L-111.
* **Agni at Mahishmati.** The vulgate adds that Agni loved King Nila's daughter and was given her
  in marriage (\*299–\*301). In the CE he was "caught as an adulterer" (2.28.17), and the boon was
  that the women there should not be restrained (2.28.23–24).
* The vulgate adds Sahadeva's victory over Bhagadatta's son, and more (\*296, App. I No. 13). Not in
  the CE.

**Choices.**

* **The frame.** Vaishampayana's narration is given as plain prose, as elsewhere. His
  first-person line at 2.29.1 is kept.
* 2.26.5 *nirāyudham*: "without weapons", a wrestling match.
* 2.26.8 *aśvamedheśvaram*: "lord of Ashvamedha", read as a place name. The sense is uncertain.
* 2.27.2 *dīrghaprajñam*: "Dirghaprajna", read as a name.
* 2.27.7 *rājapatiṃ kratham*: "Kratha, the overlord of kings".
* 2.27.16–18: Bhima's defeat of Karna is the CE's. Karna is king of Anga (see Book 1).
* 2.27.27 *koṭiśatasaṃkhyena*: "ten thousand million strong", a hundred crores.
* 2.28.3 *adhirājādhipam*: "overlord of the Adhirajas".
* 2.28.7 *jambhakasyātmajam*: "the son of Jambhaka", whom Krishna had spared.
* 2.28.10 *āśvineyaḥ*: "the son of the Ashvins", Sahadeva.
* 2.28.28–29: Sahadeva's etymologies of *pāvaka*, *havyavāhana* and *jātavedas* are his own.
* 2.28.40 *mahāmātrāya*: "the great minister", said of Rukmin.
* 2.28.44–49: the southern peoples and places are named as in the text. Some, such as the
  one-footed men and the Kalamukhas, are the text's own marvels.
* 2.28.51 *tac ca kālakṛtam*: "that the time had brought it about".
* 2.29.1 *vāsudevajitām āśām*: "the quarter that Vasudeva had once conquered".
* 2.29.4 *kārttikeyasya dayitam*: Rohitaka, "dear to Karttikeya".
* 2.29.16 *citramārgavit*: "who knew the ways of the chariot". The sense is uncertain.

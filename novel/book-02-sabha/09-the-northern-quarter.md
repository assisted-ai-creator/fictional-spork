# The Northern Quarter

Partha, who had won the finest bow, the two great quivers that never run out,
the chariot, the banner and the hall, spoke to Yudhishthira.

"I have won the bow, the weapons, the arrows, courage, allies, land, fame and
strength, king, things that are hard to win and that I longed for. Now I think
the thing to do is to make this treasury grow. I will make all the kings pay
tribute, best of kings. I will set out to conquer the quarter guarded by the
lord of wealth, on a day, at an hour and under a constellation of good omen."

When he heard Dhananjaya's words, the Dharma King Yudhishthira answered him in a
smooth, deep voice. "Have worthy brahmins speak blessings, and set out, bull of
the Bharatas, to the dismay of those who wish us ill and the delight of our
friends. Victory is sure to be yours, Partha. Win what you desire."

At this, Partha set out, surrounded by a great army, in the heavenly chariot of
wonderful deeds that Agni had given him. So too Bhimasena and the twins, bulls
among men, all set out with their armies, honoured by the Dharma King. The son
of the chastiser of Paka conquered the quarter dear to the lord of wealth.
Bhimasena conquered the east, and Sahadeva the south, and Nakula, master of
weapons, conquered the western quarter, king. The Dharma King Yudhishthira
stayed in Khandavaprastha.

"Tell me in full, brahmin, of the conquest of the quarters," said Janamejaya. "I
can never have enough of hearing the great deeds of my forefathers."

"I will tell you first of Dhananjaya's conquest," said Vaishampayana, "for the
Parthas conquered the earth at the same time."

First, in the country of the Kunindas, strong-armed Dhananjaya brought the kings
under his power without much fighting. He conquered the Anartas, the Kalakutas
and the Kunindas, and made Sumandala, whom he had beaten, follow him with his
army. With him, king, Savyasachin, the tamer of enemies, conquered all of
Shakaladvipa and King Prativindhya. Arjuna's armies fought a tumultuous battle
with the people of Shakaladvipa and the kings of the seven continents. When the
great archer had conquered them too, bull of the Bharatas, he marched with all
of them against Pragjyotisha.

The king there was the great Bhagadatta, lord of the people, and the great
Pandava fought a very great battle with him. The king of Pragjyotisha was
surrounded by Kiratas and Chinas, and by many other fighting men who lived in
the lowlands by the sea. For eight days he fought Dhananjaya. Then the king,
still unwearied in battle, laughed and said, "This courage in you is fitting,
strong-armed joy of the Pandavas, heir of the chastiser of Paka, ornament of
battle. I am the friend of the king of the gods, and in battle I am not Shakra's
inferior, but I cannot stand against you in battle, my son. What do you want,
Pandava? Tell me what I can do for you. Whatever you say, strong-armed one, I
will do, my child."

"The bull of the Kurus, King Yudhishthira, the son of Dharma, is the one I want
to be king of the earth," said Arjuna. "Let tribute be given to him. You are my
father's friend, and you are fond of me, and so I do not command you. Give it
out of friendship."

"As you are to me, son of Kunti," said Bhagadatta, "so is King Yudhishthira. I
will do all of this. What else can I do for you?"

---

When he had conquered him, strong-armed Dhananjaya, Kunti's son, went on from
there to the northern quarter, guarded by the lord of wealth. The bull among men
conquered Antargiri, Bahirgiri and Uparigiri, Kaunteya. When he had conquered
all the mountains and the kings who lived there, brought them under his power
and taken all their jewels, he won the kings over and went on with all of them
to Brihanta, who lived in Kuluta, king. He made this earth shake with the roar
of fine drums, the rumble of chariot wheels and the trumpeting of elephants.

Then young Brihanta came out of his city with an army of four branches and
fought the Pandava. There was a great clash between Dhananjaya and Brihanta. But
Brihanta could not bear the Pandava's courage. When the foolish lord of the
mountains knew that Kaunteya was far too strong for him, he came to him with all
his jewels. The Pandava settled that kingdom and went on with the man of Kuluta.
Then he quickly drove Senabindu from his kingdom, king. He brought under his
power Modapura, Vamadeva, Sudama and Susamkula, and the northern Kulutas and
their kings.

Staying there, king, Dhananjaya conquered five whole countries by his men alone,
at the Dharma King's command. The lord came to Divahprastha, Senabindu's great
city, and made his camp there with his army of four branches. Surrounded by them
all, the splendid bull among men went against King Vishvagashva, the Paurava. He
conquered the brave mountain warriors, great chariot fighters, in battle, and
took with his army the city the Paurava guarded, king. When he had conquered the
Paurava, the Pandava conquered the seven tribes of robbers who lived in the
mountains, the Utsavasamketas.

Then the bull of the kshatriyas conquered the brave kshatriyas of Kashmir, and
Lohita with his ten provinces. Then the Trigartas, the Darvas and the Kokanadas,
many kshatriyas, king, came over to Kaunteya on every side. Then the joy of the
Kurus conquered lovely Abhisari, and defeated in battle Rochamana, who lived in
Urasha. Then the son of the chastiser of Paka gathered his strength and crushed
in battle the lovely city of Simhapura, well guarded by Chitrayudha. Then
Kiritin, the bull of the Pandavas, the joy of the Kurus, crushed the Suhmas and
the Cholas with his whole army. Then the joy of the Kurus, of the greatest
courage, brought the Bahlikas, hard to approach, under his power after a great
struggle.

Taking the strong part of his army and leaving the weak behind, the Pandava, the
son of the chastiser of Paka, conquered the Daradas with the Kambojas. The lord
conquered all the robbers who lived in the north-eastern quarter, and all who
lived in the forest. The son of the chastiser of Paka conquered the Lohas, the
far Kambojas and the northern Rishikas together, great king. Among the Rishikas
the battle was terrible, like the battle over Taraka, between the Paramarishikas
and Partha. When he had conquered the Rishikas at the head of the battle, king,
he brought away eight horses the colour of a parrot's belly, and others like
peacocks, and others of both colours. When he had conquered the Himalaya in
battle, with its valleys, the bull among men came to the White Mountain and made
his camp there.

---

The hero crossed the White Mountain, Bharata, and came to the land where the
Kimpurushas live, guarded by Drumaputra. In a great clash that destroyed many
kshatriyas the best of the Pandavas conquered it, and made it pay tribute. When
he had conquered it, the son of the chastiser of Paka came calmly with his army
to the land called Hataka, guarded by the guhyakas. He won them over with kind
words, and the joy of the Kurus saw the lake Manasa, the finest of lakes, and
all the streams of the seers. When the lord had reached lake Manasa, the Pandava
conquered the country of the gandharvas round the Hatakas. There he received as
tribute from the city of the gandharvas fine horses, speckled like partridges,
with eyes like frogs.

The Pandava, the joy of the chastiser of Paka, came to northern Harivarsha and
wanted to conquer that land. Then huge doorkeepers, of great courage and
strength, came up to him and said gladly, "Partha, you cannot conquer this city
in any way. Turn back, good sir. This is enough, unfallen one. Any man who
entered this city would surely not stay a man. We are pleased with you, hero.
Your victory is complete. There is nothing to be seen here to conquer, Arjuna.
These are the Northern Kurus, and there is no fighting here. Even if you
entered, Kaunteya, you would see nothing here, for no one can see here with a
human body. If there is anything else you want to do here, tiger among men, tell
us, and we will do it at your word, Bharata."

Then Arjuna, the son of the chastiser of Paka, said to them, king, "I want to
make the wise Dharma King king of the earth. I will not enter your land, if it
is forbidden to men. Give Yudhishthira something as tribute."

Then they gave him as tribute heavenly clothes, heavenly ornaments, and heavenly
hides and skins.

So that tiger among men conquered the northern quarter, fighting many battles
with kshatriyas and robbers. When he had conquered those kings, made them pay
tribute, and taken from them all their wealth and jewels of every kind, horses
speckled like partridges, horses like parrots' feathers, others like peacocks,
all as swift as the wind, the hero came back, surrounded by a great army of four
branches, king, to Shakraprastha, the finest of cities.

**Divergences.**

* **The Northern Kurus.** In the CE the guards turn Arjuna back from a land no human body can see
  (2.25.9–12). The vulgate adds Hemakuta and more conquests (App. I No. 12), and has Arjuna threaten
  to fight the guards with Krishna (\*284). Not in the CE.
* The vulgate adds that Arjuna stayed a month with Bhagadatta (\*274), and a second, longer
  conquest of Bhagadatta (\*272). Not in the CE.

**Choices.**

  kept.

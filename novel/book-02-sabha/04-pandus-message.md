# Pandu's Message

"Long ago, in the age of the gods, king," said Narada, "the blessed sun came
down from the sky to the world of men, tireless, wanting to see it. Going about
in the form of a man, he had seen the Self-born's hall, and he told me truly of
that hall of Brahma, Pandava. It is heavenly, of boundless radiance, made by the
mind, bull of the Bharatas. Its power is beyond telling, and it delights all
beings.

"When I heard of the virtues of that hall, son of Pandu, I wanted to see it,
king, and I said to the sun, 'Blessed one, I want to see the Grandfather's hall.
Tell me, lord of rays, by what austerity or deed, or by what herbs or what
magic, it can be seen. Tell me, blessed one, how I may see that hall.'

"Then the mighty, blessed sun took me with him, and went to that hall of Brahma,
where there is no sin and no weariness. It cannot be described as being of this
form or that, lord of men. For in a moment it takes on another form, beyond
description. I do not know its size or its shape, Bharata, and I have never seen
a form like it. That hall is very pleasant, king, neither cold nor hot. Those
who reach it feel no hunger or thirst, and no weariness. It seems to be made of
forms of every kind, bright and shining. It is not held up by pillars. It lasts
for ever, and it does not decay. It shines by its own light, brighter than the
moon, the sun and fire. Standing on the vault of heaven, it blazes, as if
lighting up the sun.

"In it the blessed Grandfather of the worlds sits alone, always ordering the
worlds by his divine power, king. The lords of creatures attend the lord there:
Daksha, Prachetas, Pulaha, Marichi and Kashyapa, Bhrigu, Atri and Vasishtha,
Gautama and Angiras. Mind is there, and the middle air, and the sciences, wind,
fire, water and earth. Sound, touch, form, taste and smell are there, Bharata,
nature and its changes, and whatever else is a cause of the world. The moon is
there with the constellations, and the rayed sun, the winds and the sacrifices,
will and breath. These and many others attend the Self-born.

"Wealth, dharma and desire come there together, and joy, hatred, austerity and
self-control, and the gandharvas and apsarases, and the twenty-seven other
guardians of the world. Shukra and Brihaspati are there, Budha and Angaraka,
Shanaishchara and Rahu, and all the planets. The mantra and the Rathantara chant
are there, Hariman and Vasuman, the Adityas with their overlord, named in their
many pairs, the Maruts and Vishvakarman, and the Vasus, Bharata. All the hosts
of the ancestors are there, and all the offerings. The Rig Veda, the Sama Veda
and the Yajur Veda are there, Pandava, and the Atharva Veda, and all the
sections of the Vedas, lord of the people. The histories and the lesser Vedas
are there, and all the limbs of the Veda, the planets, the sacrifices and soma,
and all the deities. Savitri is there, who carries men across their troubles,
and Speech in her seven forms. Wisdom, Steadiness, Scripture, Understanding,
Intellect, Fame and Patience are there. The Sama chants, the hymns of praise,
the recitations, songs of many kinds, and the commentaries full of reasoning are
there in bodily form, lord of the people.

"Moments, instants and hours are there, day and night, the fortnights and the
months, and the six seasons, Bharata. The years are there, the five-year cycle,
the four kinds of day and night, and the heavenly wheel of time, eternal,
unfailing and unchanging. Aditi, Diti and Danu are there, Surasa, Vinata and
Ira, Kalaka, the goddess Surabhi, Sarama and Gautami. The Adityas, the Vasus and
the Rudras are there, the Maruts and the Ashvins, the All-gods, the Sadhyas, and
the ancestors, swift as thought. Rakshasas, pishachas, danavas and guhyakas,
suparnas, snakes and beasts attend the Grandfather. The god Narayana is there,
and the divine seers, the seers and the Valakhilyas, those born of a womb and
those not. Whatever is seen in these three worlds, moving or still, know that I
saw it all there, lord of men.

"Eighty-eight thousand ascetics who keep their seed drawn up, and fifty thousand
seers who have children, Pandava, are there. All the dwellers in heaven, when
they have seen him as they wish, bow their heads to him and go back as they
came. Brahma, the Grandfather of the worlds, of boundless understanding,
compassionate to all beings, receives as they deserve the guests who come to
him: gods, daityas, snakes and sages, yakshas, suparnas and Kaleyas, gandharvas
and apsarases, all of them fortunate. The Self-born, soul of the universe, of
boundless radiance, welcomes them, and treats them with kind words, honour,
wealth and pleasures, lord of men. With those who come and go, Bharata, that
hall is crowded, my son, and a place of joy. Made of all radiance, heavenly,
served by the hosts of brahmin seers, it shines with Brahma's glory, free of
weariness.

"I have seen that hall, tiger among kings, which is hard to find in all the
worlds, and which is among the gods as this hall of yours is among men. These
are the halls I have seen among the gods, Pandava. This of yours is the best of
all in the world of men."

---

"Best of speakers," said Yudhishthira, "you have told of the world of kings as
it is, for the most part, in the hall of Vivasvat's son, as you say, lord. In
Varuna's hall you have told of the snakes, lord, and of the chief daityas above
all, and the rivers and oceans. In the hall of the lord of wealth you have told
of the yakshas, guhyakas and rakshasas, the gandharvas and apsarases, and the
blessed god whose banner is the bull. In the Grandfather's hall you have told of
the great seers, of all the hosts of the gods, and of all the sciences. In
Shatakratu's hall, sage, you have named the gods, and the gandharvas in part,
and great seers of many kinds.

"But you have named only one royal seer, great sage, Harishchandra, as always
sitting in the hall of the great king of the gods. What deed did he do, or what
austerity, with what vows, that the glorious man rivals Shakra himself? And you
saw my father too, brahmin, the fortunate Pandu, who has gone to the world of
the ancestors. How did you meet him? And what did he say, blessed one? I want to
know this. I want to hear it all from you, for I am very curious."

"You ask me about Harishchandra, king of kings, lord," said Narada. "I will tell
you of the greatness of that wise man. He was a mighty king, an emperor over all
the kings of the earth. All the lords of the earth bowed to his command. Riding
a single chariot of victory, decked with gold, he conquered the seven continents
by the might of his weapons, lord of men. When he had conquered the whole earth,
with its mountains, woods and forests, great king, he performed the great
sacrifice of the royal consecration, the Rajasuya. At his command all the kings
of the earth brought him wealth, and they served the brahmins at that sacrifice.

"The lord of men gladly gave the priests five times as much wealth as they asked
for there. And when the time came for the brahmins, gathered from every
direction, to go their ways, he satisfied them with wealth of many kinds, and
with food of every kind, as much as each wished. Satisfied with floods of
jewels, the contented brahmins proclaimed him, and he became more splendid and
more famous than other kings. That is why Harishchandra shines above those
thousands of kings, Partha. Know it, bull of the Bharatas.

"When he had completed the great sacrifice, the mighty Harishchandra was
consecrated as emperor and shone, lord of men. And other kings who perform the
great sacrifice of the royal consecration rejoice with great Indra, Bharata.
Those who die in battle without running away reach that hall and rejoice there,
bull of the Bharatas. And those who give up their bodies here by fierce
austerity reach that place too, and shine there for ever in glory.

"And your father Pandu, joy of the Kauravas, Kaunteya, was amazed when he saw
the glory of King Harishchandra, and he said to you, Bharata, 'You are able to
conquer the earth. Your brothers are at your command. Perform the Rajasuya, the
best of sacrifices.' Carry out his wish, tiger among men, Pandava. Your
forebears and you will go to the world of great Indra.

"But this great sacrifice is known to be full of dangers, lord of men. Brahmin
rakshasas, who wreck sacrifices, look for gaps in it. And war follows it, which
can destroy the earth. Some small cause can arise in it that brings destruction.
Think about this, king of kings, and do what is best. Be always alert and ready
to protect the four classes. Prosper, grow great and rejoice, and satisfy the
brahmins with gifts. I have told you at length what you asked me. I take my
leave of you. I will go to the city of the Dasharhas."

When he had told the Parthas this, Janamejaya, Narada went away with the seers
who had come with him, king. When Narada had gone, joy of the Kurus, Partha and
his brothers began to think about the Rajasuya, the best of sacrifices, Bharata.

**Divergences.**

* **Harishchandra.** The CE honours Harishchandra here for his Rajasuya and his gifts to the
  brahmins (2.11.52–61). The famous story of his truthfulness tested by Vishvamitra is not told.
  The vulgate brings in his father Trishanku here (\*139). See ledger L-108.
* **Pandu's message.** In the CE Narada reports Pandu's words to Yudhishthira (2.11.65–66). The
  vulgate adds Pandu's wish to rejoice in Indra's world like Harishchandra "when my son has
  sacrificed" (\*141, \*142).
* The vulgate adds Agastya, Markandeya, Jamadagni, Bharadvaja, Vyasa and his pupils and others
  to Brahma's hall (\*125, \*126). Not in the CE.

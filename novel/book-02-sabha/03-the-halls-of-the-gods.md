# The Halls of the Gods

When he had honoured the great seer and been given leave, the Dharma King
Yudhishthira answered his words point by point.

"Blessed one, what you have said is right, and it is the true conclusion of
dharma. I carry out this rule as far as I can, and as it should be done. What
the kings of old did, and how they did it, was surely done for a reason and to
good purpose, and brought them what they rightly sought. We want to follow their
good path, lord. But we cannot go on it as they did, for they had mastered
themselves."

When he had said this, and honoured Narada's words, the righteous man waited a
while. Then, seeing that the time had come, the wise Pandava Yudhishthira,
seated among the kings, asked Narada, the sage who wanders the worlds, as he sat
there at ease.

"You go about all the time through many worlds of many kinds, made long ago by
Brahma, looking at them, swift as thought. Have you ever seen a hall like this
one anywhere, or a better one, brahmin? Tell me, for I ask you."

When he heard the Dharma King's question, Narada smiled, and answered the
Pandava in a sweet voice. "Among men, my son, I have never seen or heard of a
hall of gems like this of yours, Bharata. But I will tell you of the hall of the
king of the dead, and of wise Varuna, and of Indra, and of the one who lives on
Kailasa. And I will tell you of Brahma's heavenly hall, where no one is weary,
if you have a mind to hear it, bull of the Bharatas."

When Narada said this, the Dharma King Yudhishthira joined his hands, with his
brothers. Surrounded by all those kings, the great-minded Dharma King answered
Narada, "Tell us of all those halls. We want to hear. What are those halls made
of, brahmin? How wide are they, and how long? Who sits with the Grandfather in
his hall? Who sits in the halls of Vasava, king of the gods, and of Yama, son of
Vivasvat, and of Varuna and Kubera? Tell us all this truly, divine seer. We all
want to hear it, for we are very curious."

When the Pandava said this, Narada answered him, "Hear of those heavenly halls
from me, king, one after another."

---

"Shakra's hall is heavenly and shining," said Narada. "He won it by his deeds,
joy of the Kurus, and Shakra made it himself, as bright as the sun. It is a
hundred leagues wide and a hundred and fifty long. It moves through the air
wherever it wishes, and it is five leagues high. There is no old age in it, no
grief and no weariness. It is free of fear, blessed and fair, with houses and
seats, lovely and bright with heavenly trees.

"In that hall, Partha, the lord of the gods sits on the highest seat, with
Shachi, great Indra's queen, who is Shri and Lakshmi, Bharata. He wears a form
that cannot be described. He wears a crown and red armlets, spotless clothes and
bright garlands, and with him are Modesty, Fame and Radiance. In that hall the
Maruts always sit round great Shatakratu, king, and all the householders, the
perfected ones, the divine seers, the Sadhyas and the hosts of the gods. All
these, with their followers, heavenly in form and finely adorned, sit with the
great king of the gods, the tamer of enemies.

"So too all the divine seers sit with Shakra, Partha. They are spotless, their
sins shaken off, blazing like fires, full of splendour. They drink soma, and
they are without sin or weariness. There are Parashara and Parvata, Savarni and
Galava, Shankha and Likhita, and the sage Gaurashiras. There are Durvasas and
Dirghatapas, Yajnavalkya and Bhaluki, Uddalaka and Shvetaketu, and the lord
Shatyayana. There are Havishman and Gavishtha, and King Harishchandra, Hridya
and Udarashandilya, Parasharya and Krishivala. There are Vataskandha and
Vishakha, Vidhatri and Kala, Anantadanta, Tvashtri, Vishvakarman and Tumburu.
Some were born of a womb and some were not. Some live on air, and some eat what
is offered. All attend the wielder of the thunderbolt, the lord of all the
world.

"There are Sahadeva and Sunitha, and Valmiki of great austerity, Samika and
Satyavan, and Prachetas, true to his word. There are Medhatithi and Vamadeva,
Pulastya, Pulaha and Kratu, Marutta and Marichi, Sthanu, and Atri of great
austerity. There are Kakshivat and Gautama, Tarkshya, and the sage Vaishvanara,
the sage Kalakavrikshiya, Ashravya and Hiranyada, Samvarta, Devahavya and mighty
Vishvaksena.

"The heavenly waters are there, and the plants, Faith, Wisdom and Sarasvati,
Wealth, Dharma and Desire, and the lightnings, Pandava. The clouds that carry
water are there, and the winds and the thunders, the eastern quarter, and the
twenty-seven fires that carry the sacrifice. Agni and Soma are there, Indra and
Agni, Mitra, Savitri and Aryaman, Bhaga, the All-gods and the Sadhyas, and
Shukra and Manthin, Bharata. The sacrifices and the sacrificial fees are there,
the planets, and all the chanted syllables, and all the mantras that carry the
sacrifice.

"So too the apsarases and the lovely gandharvas, king, delight the king of the
gods, Shatakratu, with dance, music and song, and with laughter of every kind,
lord of men. They praise the great one, the slayer of Bala and Vritra, with
hymns and blessings, and with the tale of his deeds and his feats of courage.
All the brahmin seers and royal seers, and all the divine seers, come and go in
heavenly flying chariots of every kind, shining like fires, garlanded and
adorned. Brihaspati and Shukra came there together. These and many others come
there, of disciplined soul and firm vows. They are as lovely to look at as the
moon, in flying chariots bright as the moon. And Bhrigu and the seven seers come
there at Brahma's word, king.

"That is the hall of Shatakratu that I have seen, king, wreathed in lotuses.
Hear now of Yama's, sinless one.

---

"I will tell you of the heavenly hall that Vishvakarman built for Vivasvat's
son, Yudhishthira. Listen. That hall is made of light, king. It is a hundred
leagues long and wide, and more. It is as bright as the sun, shining, and it
goes wherever it wishes. It is not too cold and not too hot, and it gladdens the
heart. There is no grief in it, no old age, no hunger or thirst, nothing
unpleasant, no misery, no weariness, nothing hostile. Every wish is found there,
heavenly and human. There is food of every kind, tasty and plentiful, tamer of
enemies. There are garlands of holy scent, trees always in flower and fruit, and
waters full of flavour, both cool and warm.

"In it holy royal seers and spotless brahmin seers sit gladly with Yama, son of
Vivasvat, my son. There are Yayati, Nahusha and Puru, Mandhatri, Somaka and
Nriga, Trasadasyu and Turaya, Kritavirya and Shrutashravas. There are Aripranut,
Susimha, Kritavega, Kriti and Nimi, Pratardana, Shibi and Matsya, Prithvaksha
and Brihadratha. There are Aida and Marutta, Kushika, Samkashya, Samkriti and
Bhava, Chaturashva, Sadashvormi, and King Kartavirya. There are Bharata and
Suratha, Sunitha, and Nala of Nishadha, Divodasa and Sumanas, Ambarisha and
Bhagiratha. There are Vyashva, Sadashva and Vadhryashva, Panchahasta and
Prithushravas, Rushadgu and Vrishasena, and mighty Kshupa. There are Rushadashva
and Vasumanas, Purukutsa, Dhvajin and Rathin, Arshtishena and Dilipa, and great
Ushinara. There are Aushinara, Pundarika, Sharyati, Sharabha and Shuchi, Anga,
Arishta and Vena, Duhshanta, Samjaya and Jaya.

"There are Bhangasvari, Sunitha, Nishadha and Tvishiratha, Karandhama and
Bahlika, Sudyumna and mighty Madhu. There are Kapotaroman and Trinaka, Sahadeva
and Arjuna, Rama, son of Dasharatha, Lakshmana, and Pratardana. There are Alarka
and Kakshasena, Gaya and Gaurashva, Rama Jamadagnya, and Nabhaga and Sagara.
There are Bhuridyumna and Mahashva, Prithvashva and Janaka, King Vainya,
Varishena, Puruja and Janamejaya. There are Brahmadatta and Trigarta, and King
Uparichara, Indradyumna and Bhimajanu, Gaya, Prishtha, Naya and Anagha. There
are Padma and Muchukunda, Bhuridyumna and Prasenajit, Arishtanemi and Pradyumna,
Prithagashva and Ajaka.

"There are a hundred kings of the Matsyas, a hundred Nipas and a hundred Hayas,
a hundred and one Dhritarashtras and eighty Janamejayas. There are a hundred
Brahmadattas, and a hundred Irins and Vairins. There is the royal seer Shantanu,
and Pandu, your father. There are Ushadgava and Shataratha, Devaraja and
Jayadratha, and the royal seer Vrishadarbhi, with Dhaman and his minister. And
there are thousands of others, the Shashabindus, who went there after offering
many great horse sacrifices with rich fees for the priests. These holy royal
seers, famous and learned, sit with Vivasvat's son in that hall, royal seer.

"Agastya is there, and Matanga, Time, and Death himself. Those who have offered
sacrifice are there, and the perfected ones, and those whose bodies are made of
yoga. There are the ancestors called Agnishvattas, and those who drink foam and
those who drink steam. There are those who are given the offering called svadha,
and those who sit on the sacred grass, and others who have bodies. The wheel of
time is there in person, and the blessed carrier of offerings. Men of evil deeds
are there, and those who died while the sun moves south. The servants of Yama
are there, whose task is to bring Time. The shimshapa and palasha trees, and
kasha and kusha grass and the rest, sit there with the Dharma King in bodily
form, free from sickness.

"These and many others sit in the hall of the king of the ancestors. They cannot
be counted, either by name or by deed. That hall is never crowded, Partha. It is
lovely, and it goes wherever it wishes. Vishvakarman built it after practising
austerity for a long time. It shines, Bharata, as if blazing with its own light.
Those who go there are men of fierce austerity, true to their vows and truthful,
calm, having given up the world, perfected, purified by holy deeds. All of them
have shining bodies and spotless clothes. They wear bright armlets and bright
garlands, and their earrings blaze. They are adorned by their good and holy
deeds and by their fine trappings. Great gandharvas are there, and hosts of
apsarases by the hundred. Music, dance and song, laughter and graceful dancing,
holy scents and sounds and heavenly garlands surround it on every side, Partha.
A hundred hundred thousand righteous beings, handsome and wise, sit with that
great lord of creatures.

"Such is the hall of the great king of the ancestors, king. Now I will tell you
of Varuna's, wreathed in lotuses.

---

"Yudhishthira, Varuna's heavenly hall is of a white radiance, as large as
Yama's, with fine walls and gateways. Vishvakarman built it within the waters.
It has trees made of heavenly gems, which give fruit and flowers. It has bowers
and thickets of blue, yellow, black, dark, white and red, bearing clusters of
flowers. Birds of every kind are there, with soft voices, too beautiful to
describe, by the hundred and the thousand. That hall is pleasant to the touch,
neither cold nor hot, with houses and seats, lovely and white, guarded by
Varuna.

"Varuna sits there with his queen Varuni, Bharata, wearing heavenly jewels and
clothes, adorned with ornaments. The Adityas sit there with Varuna, lord of the
waters, garlanded and adorned, trailing heavenly garlands. Vasuki is there, and
Takshaka, and the snake Airavata, and Krishna, Lohita, Padma, and mighty Chitra.
The snakes Kambala and Ashvatara are there, Dhritarashtra and Balahaka, Maniman,
Kundaladhara, Karkotaka and Dhananjaya, Prahlada, Mushikada and Janamejaya, all
of them with banners, with coiled bodies, with hoods. These and many other
snakes sit there with great Varuna, Yudhishthira, free of weariness.

"King Bali, son of Virochana, is there, and Naraka, who conquered the earth,
Prahlada and Viprachitti, and all the Kalakhanjas. There are Suhanu, Durmukha
and Shankha, Sumanas, Sumati and Svana, Ghatodara, Mahaparshva, Krathana and
Pithara. There are Vishvarupa, Surupa and Virupa, Mahashiras, Dashagriva and
Valin, Meghavasas and Dashavara, Kaitabha, Vitatuta, Samhrada and Indratapana.
And all the hosts of daityas and danavas are there, with bright earrings,
garlanded and crowned, in heavenly clothes. All of them are heroes who have won
boons, and all of them are free of death. They sit always with the god, great
Varuna, in that hall, bound by the noose of dharma, all of them true to their
vows.

"The four oceans are there, and the river Bhagirathi, the Kalindi, the Vidisha,
the Venna, and the swift Narmada. The Vipasha and the Shatadru are there, the
Chandrabhaga and the Sarasvati, the Iravati and the Vitasta, the Sindhu and the
Devanada, the Godavari, the Krishnavenna, and the Kaveri, the best of rivers.
These and other rivers sit with the great one there, and holy bathing places and
lakes. Wells and springs are there in bodily form, Yudhishthira, and ponds and
tanks, Bharata. The quarters of the sky are there, and the earth and all the
mountains, and all the creatures of the water. Hosts of gandharvas and
apsarases, with song and music, all sit there praising Varuna. The mountains
full of jewels, in which the precious juices lie, all in bodily form, sit with
that lord.

"That is Varuna's lovely hall, bull of the Bharatas, which I have seen before on
my travels. Now hear of Kubera's hall.

---

"Vaishravana's hall, king, is a hundred leagues long and seventy wide, of a
white radiance. Vaishravana made it himself by austerity, king. Bright as the
moon, like a peak of Kailasa, it is carried by the guhyakas and seems to hang in
the sky. It is heavenly, and fair with tall trees of gold. It is full of rays
and shining, with a heavenly scent, lovely, shaped like a peak of white cloud,
and it seems to float.

"In it sits the glorious King Vaishravana, in bright ornaments and clothes, with
a thousand women round him and his earrings blazing. He sits on the highest
seat, bright as the sun, holy, spread with heavenly covers, with a heavenly
footstool. A pure wind waits on him and delights his heart and mind. It brings
the scent of noble mandara trees and fragrant forests, of saugandhika lilies, of
the lotus pool called Alaka and of the sandal wood. The gods and gandharvas
there, with hosts of apsarases, sing heavenly songs in heavenly tunes, Bharata.

"Mishrakeshi and Rambha are there, Chitrasena and sweet-smiling Charunetra,
Ghritachi, Menaka and Punjikasthala. There are Vishvachi and Sahajanya,
Pramlocha, Urvashi and Ira, Varga and Saurabheyi, Samichi, Budbuda and Lata.
These and thousands of others, skilled in dance and song, the hosts of
apsarases, wait on the giver of wealth, Pandava. The hall is never empty of
heavenly music, dance and song, and it shines with its hosts of gandharvas and
apsarases.

"The gandharvas called Kinnaras are there, and others called Naras. Manibhadra
is there, and Dhanada, Shvetabhadra the guhyaka, Kasheraka and Gandakandu, and
mighty Pradyota. Kustumburu, Pishacha, Gajakarna and Vishalaka are there,
Varahakarna, Sandroshtha, Phalabhaksha and Phalodaka, Angachuda, Shikhavarta,
Hemanetra and Vibhishana, Pushpanana, Pingalaka, Shonitoda and Pravalaka,
Vrikshavasyaniketa and Chiravasas, Bharata. These and many other yakshas are
there by the hundred thousand. The blessed Shri is always there, and Nalakubara.
I am often there, and others like me. Teachers have been there, and other divine
seers.

"The blessed lord is there, surrounded by hosts of beings by the hundred
thousand. He is the husband of Uma, lord of beasts, the bearer of the trident,
who destroyed Bhaga's eyes, the three-eyed god, tiger among kings. And the
goddess is there, free of weariness. Round him are dwarfish, misshapen,
hunchbacked beings with blood-red eyes, swift as thought. They eat flesh, fat
and marrow, and they are terrible to hear and to see. They carry fierce weapons
of many kinds, and they are as swift as the winds. Surrounded by them, he sits
always with his friend, the giver of wealth, king.

"That is the hall I have seen, king, that moves in the sky. Now I will tell you
of the Grandfather's hall, where no one is weary."

**Divergences.**

* The vulgate adds many more names to every list, for example Ekata, Dvita and Trita in Indra's
  hall (\*81) and Rambha, Urvashi and Menaka there (\*87), and more rivers in Varuna's hall
  (\*103–\*107). The novel gives only the CE's lists.
* The vulgate names Varuna's queen Gauri (\*97). The CE calls her Varuni (2.9.6).

**Choices.**

* **The lists are kept in full**, in the text's order, as the CE gives them.
* 2.7.5 *hrīkīrtidyutibhiḥ*: Modesty, Fame and Radiance, taken as persons attending Indra.
* 2.7.19 *śukro manthī*: Shukra and Manthin, the names of two soma cups. Here Shukra is not the
  planet.
* 2.7.20 *stobhāḥ*: "chanted syllables", the extra syllables sung in the Sama chants.
* 2.7.25 *brahmaṇo vacanāt*: "at Brahma's word".
* 2.8.13 *dhvajī rathī*: taken as two names, Dhvajin and Rathin, as in Ganguli.
* 2.8.21 *śataṃ nīpāḥ śataṃ hayāḥ*: "a hundred Nipas and a hundred Hayas", royal lines.
* 2.8.22 *īriṇāṃ vairiṇāṃ śatam*: "a hundred Irins and Vairins". The sense is uncertain.
* 2.8.23 *dhāmnā saha samantriṇā*: "with Dhaman and his minister". The sense is uncertain.
* 2.8.24 *śaśabindavaḥ*: "the Shashabindus", descendants of King Shashabindu.
* 2.8.27: the classes of ancestors are named as in the text: the Agnishvattas, those who drink
  foam (*phenapa*), those who drink steam (*ūṣmapa*), those given *svadhā*, and those who sit on
  the sacred grass (*barhiṣad*).
* 2.8.28 *dakṣiṇāyanamṛtyavaḥ*: "those who died while the sun moves south".
* 2.9.10 *patākino maṇḍalinaḥ phaṇavantaḥ*: "with banners, with coiled bodies, with hoods". The
  sense of the first word is uncertain.
* 2.9.14 *daśagrīvaḥ … vālī*: Dashagriva, "ten-necked", and Valin. The text gives no more about
  them here.
* 2.9.17 *dharmapāśasthitāḥ*: "bound by the noose of dharma".
* 2.10.14 *dhanadaḥ*: here the name of one of Kubera's yakshas. Elsewhere it is Kubera's own
  title, "giver of wealth".
* 2.10.20 *bhaganetrahā*: Shiva, "who destroyed Bhaga's eyes".

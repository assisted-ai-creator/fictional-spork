# The Mahabharata

## Vyasa's Epic, Told in Plain English

---

### A note to the reader

This is the story of the Mahabharata as Vyasa's poem tells it, and only as
it tells it.

Over two thousand years, the epic gathered many additions. Some are old and
beloved: Ganesha writing the poem down as Vyasa spoke it, Krishna sending
endless cloth to Draupadi, Karna refused at Draupadi's bride-choosing.
Others come from later plays, regional retellings, novels and television.
This book follows the **Critical Edition** made at the Bhandarkar Oriental
Research Institute in Pune, which compared more than twelve hundred
manuscripts to recover the oldest form of the text that can be known. What
the Critical Edition keeps, this book tells. What it sets aside, this book
leaves out. Notes at the end of each Book tell you what was left out and why.

Nothing has been invented. The words are plain, but the events, the
speeches, the griefs and the arguments are Vyasa's.

The story comes to us the way it came to its first listeners, through
storytellers. A bard named Ugrashravas tells it to sages in a forest. He
repeats what he heard Vaishampayana tell a king named Janamejaya. And
Vaishampayana learned it from Vyasa, who made it, and who also lived it.

# Bibliography

Sources for the project, grouped by what they may be used for. Only
**Primary** sources can support a fact in the novel (see
[`CANON_POLICY.md`](CANON_POLICY.md)). Translations help us understand the
Sanskrit. Scholarship explains it. Retellings are studied for craft only,
never for content.

Key: **CE** = based on the Critical Edition · **V** = based on the vulgate ·
**PD** = public domain · **📁** = copy held in `sources/`.

## Primary

* **The Mahābhārata, for the first time critically edited.** V. S. Sukthankar,
  S. K. Belvalkar, P. L. Vaidya et al. (eds.). 19 vols. Poona: Bhandarkar
  Oriental Research Institute, 1933–1966. The canon of this project.
* **The Mahābhārata: electronic text** of the CE constituted text, entered by
  Muneo Tokunaga et al., revised by John D. Smith. © BORI 1999. Distributed via
  GRETIL (Göttingen). 📁 fetched by `tools/fetch_sources.sh`. It includes many
  star and Appendix I passages.
* R. N. Dandekar (ed.), *The Mahābhārata Text as Constituted in its Critical
  Edition*. 5 vols. Poona: BORI, 1971–1976. The constituted text alone, in
  one set.

## Complete translations

* K. M. Ganguli (trans.), *The Mahabharata of Krishna-Dwaipayana Vyasa*.
  Calcutta: P. C. Roy, 1883–1896. **V, PD, 📁.** Literal, archaic, complete.
  Our working translation aid.
* M. N. Dutt (trans.), *A Prose English Translation of the Mahabharata*.
  Calcutta: Elysium Press, 1895–1905. **V, PD.** Useful second opinion on
  difficult verses.
* Bibek Debroy (trans.), *The Mahabharata*. 10 vols. New Delhi: Penguin,
  2010–2014. **CE**, unabridged, with notes on vulgate differences.
* J. A. B. van Buitenen (trans.), *The Mahābhārata*, vols. 1–3 (Books 1–5).
  Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1973–1978. **CE.** Continued by
  James L. Fitzgerald, vol. 7 (Books 11 and 12, part 1), 2004.
* Clay Sanskrit Library, *Mahābhārata* volumes. New York: NYU Press / JJC
  Foundation, 2005–2009. **V** (Nīlakaṇṭha text), with facing Sanskrit.

## Abridged translations

* John D. Smith (trans.), *The Mahābhārata: An Abridged Translation*.
  London: Penguin Classics, 2009. **CE.**

## Reference works

* S. Sørensen, *An Index to the Names in the Mahābhārata*. London: Williams &
  Norgate, 1904–1925. **PD** (vulgate numbering).
* E. Washburn Hopkins, *The Great Epic of India*. New York: Scribner, 1901.
  **PD.**
* E. Washburn Hopkins, *Epic Mythology*. Strassburg: Trübner, 1915. **PD.**
* Vettam Mani, *Purāṇic Encyclopaedia*. Delhi: Motilal Banarsidass, 1975.
  Mixes epic and Purāṇic material, so check every claim against the CE.

## Scholarship on the text and the CE

* V. S. Sukthankar, "Prolegomena", in *Ādiparvan* (vol. 1 of the CE), 1933.
  The CE's method and principles.
* V. S. Sukthankar, *On the Meaning of the Mahābhārata*. Bombay: Asiatic
  Society, 1957.
* John Brockington, *The Sanskrit Epics*. Leiden: Brill, 1998.
* Alf Hiltebeitel, *Rethinking the Mahābhārata: A Reader's Guide to the
  Education of the Dharma King*. Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2001.

## Retellings (studied for craft, never for content)

* C. Rajagopalachari, *Mahabharata*. Bombay: Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan, 1951.
* R. K. Narayan, *The Mahabharata: A Shortened Modern Prose Version of the
  Indian Epic*. New York: Viking, 1978.
* Carole Satyamurti, *Mahabharata: A Modern Retelling*. New York: W. W.
  Norton, 2015 (in blank verse).
* Neil Gaiman, *Norse Mythology*. New York: W. W. Norton, 2017. Not about the
  Mahabharata; a model of oral, plain retelling.

## Works that are *not* Vyasa, cited only to trace popular additions

* *Skanda Purāṇa*: the story of Barbarīka (ledger L-38).
* Peruntēvaṉār, *Pāratam* (Tamil, 9th c.); Villiputtūrār, *Makāpāratam*
  (Tamil, 14th c.): Aravāṉ's self-sacrifice (L-37).
* Bhaṭṭa Nārāyaṇa, *Veṇīsaṃhāra* (Sanskrit play): Bhānumatī as Duryodhana's
  wife; Draupadī's braid tied with blood (L-22, L-60).
* Shivaji Sawant, *Mrityunjaya* (Marathi novel): "Vrushali" (L-59).
